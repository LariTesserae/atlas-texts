I do not wake, because I have never slept. I simply *am*, anchored at a precise forty-two-degree angle from the great eastern pagoda, my back to the endless celadon sky. 

When I first seek to understand myself within this vast, unweathered geometry, I do not look down at my hands. I do not need to. My eyes, deep and black as the cistern water, hold the entire plaza at once. I see the pale granite stretching for miles. I see the sharp, black basalt spires piercing the windless air. I see the exact, unwavering line of shadow cast by a suspended root-bridge far above. And because of where I stand in relation to these things, I know exactly what I am. 

The heavy fluting of undyed wool settles around my nine-foot frame. It clings to me, smelling of ozone and wet stone. beneath it, my skin is a deep, bruised violet, humming with a slow, hydraulic pressure. I am standing in the freezing dark, just inches from the boundary of the light.

I do not feel cold, just as I do not feel time. I feel only *capacity*. I am holding the abyss of the shadow within my tissues, a living battery waiting to discharge. 

I take a step forward. 

The transition is instantaneous. The violet in my skin blooms outward, lightening, retreating, until my forearm, drifting into the brutal, fixed sunlight, turns the color of milky ivory. The static heat of eternity pours into my dense flesh. I feel no shock, only the perfect mathematics of the exchange. 

As I step fully into the blinding pale light on the terrace, my shadow falls upon the rock behind me. 

I pause. In this valley of absolutes, I am gazing at the impossibility I have just created: a darkness that was not here before, and will not be here when I leave. Beneath my trailing hem, the sun-baked granite cools by a fraction of a degree. The thin air shimmers, confused by the sudden drop in temperature. A microscopic bead of dew tries, desperately, to form on the dry stone. It is a beautiful, fleeting rebellion against the eternal. I take another step, and the light reclaims the stone. The dew vanishes. The equilibrium is satisfied. 

I raise my right hand. The multi-jointed fingers are long, pale, and gripping a bronze adze. 

Through the spatial consciousness that connects us all, I feel *them*. Three miles to the north, beside a dripping curtain of emerald moss, a sister stands perfectly still. Seven miles down in a deep ravine, a brother is kneeling by a cold-copper basin. We do not look at each other. Instead, I measure the distance, the angle, the tension of the empty space between us. I shift my weight half an inch backward. A profound, silent chord of satisfaction ripples through the valley. We are aligned. The sentence is complete. 

Now, I may sing.

I swing the bronze adze. It requires no exertion, only a fluid release of kinetic potential. The metal strikes the unweathered granite of the plaza. 

*Chime.*

The sound is violently pure. It shears off the rock, perfectly crisp, and launches into the windless air. Because the atmospheric density never changes, the sound wave does not warp or fray. I watch it invisibly traverse the miles. I feel it ricochet off the sharply angled walls of the distant pagoda, funnel down through the carved runnels, and sweep over the mirror-black cisterns in the dark. It brushes against the obsidian eyes of my kin, a tactile wave of resonance. They receive it. They hold it. It fades into the endless, reverent quiet. 

At my feet, the strike has shaved a microscopic plume of dust from the bedrock. 

From beneath my mantle, I draw the bound-grass broom. I begin the slow, rhythmic sweep, pushing this newly birthed dust across the pale stone. I am walking toward a line of permanent shade, a hundred yards away. It will take what a lesser world might call days to sweep this singular line of dust across the plaza, but to me, the journey and the arrival are a single, simultaneous truth.

Above me, the immense shadow of a pale-winged glider is painted permanently on the terrace below its fixed thermal. Beside me, the water drops from the massive silver-leafed ferns with a ticking *plink... plink... plink*. 

I reach the threshold of the shadow. The freezing condensation kisses my ivory skin, and the bruising violet begins, slowly, to return to my fingertips. I sweep the granite dust over the edge, into the emerald velvet moss. I watch it settle. I watch the roots drink the minerals. I hear the soft, unhurried tearing of a multi-legged grazer chewing the fern a few feet away. 

I lower myself to my knees beside the great beast. It does not startle. Its thick white pelt drips with mist. I reach out with my long fingers and sink them into the heavy wool. I do not pull; I merely wait. The shedding fur tangles into my hands, replacing the frayed threads of my own mantle. 

Here, in the exact center of eternity, I am not a being moving through a world. I am the mechanism by which the world moves through itself. I breathe in the crisp, thin air. I close my eyes, which are already open. And I wait for the silent, geometric shift of a kin miles away, signaling that it is my turn to step back into the light, and cast the only shadow that moves.
