To prepare for visiting this incredible underwater constructed realm, a few key considerations come to mind:

Breathing Apparatus
Since this is a fully submerged environment, you'll need a reliable way to breathe underwater for an extended period. Advanced rebreather technology that recycles exhaled gases would be ideal to maximize dive time. Backup air tanks should also be carried. Training in specialized underwater breathing methods is a must.

Protection From Environment  
The warm, brightly lit water sounds inviting, but you'll want protection from any potential hazards. A durable, flexible surface suit to guard against currents, abrasion, etc. Ultra-high UV filters for the brilliant bioluminescence. And biological filters in case of any contaminants or microorganisms.

Navigation 
With the vast open layouts and constantly shifting structures, advanced navigation tools like high-resolution sonar mapping and locator beacons will be critical to avoiding getting lost. Recording/marking your path will also help with retracing steps.

Tools for Study
This place begs for extensive research and documentation. You'll want an array of compact yet powerful sensors to study the physio-chemical properties of the water, structures, bioluminescent "vegetation", and strange sounds. Cameras, spectrometers, etc. Sampling containers. Data storage with immense capacity.

Open Mind  
Above all, leave any preconceived notions behind and be prepared to have your sense of reality utterly expanded and challenged. This is clearly an environment that defies conventional physics and biology as we know it. An open, inquisitive mindset is paramount.

With the right equipment, training, and philosophical outlook, you'll be as prepared as possible to plunge into this wondrous underwater cosmos and experience one of the universe's most extraordinary constructed environments. Proper acclimatization time will still be needed before immersion. Undoubtedly, it promises to be a humbling and transformative expedition.
