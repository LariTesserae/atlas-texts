Here are some tips for preparing to visit this glacial cirque high in the mountains:

Gear & Clothing
- Dress in very warm, waterproof layers as it will be freezing cold and extremely damp from the waterfall mist
- Wear proper insulated hiking boots with good traction for navigating the rocky, uneven terrain
- Pack rain/wind protection like a waterproof shell jacket and pants 
- Bring a warm hat, gloves, and extra dry layers to change into
- Use trekking poles for stability on the loose rubble

Acclimatization 
- Allow time to acclimatize to the high elevation and thin air before exerting yourself
- Ascend gradually if coming from lower elevations 
- Drink lots of water and eat salty foods to avoid altitude sickness
- Be prepared for potential headaches, nausea, and shortness of breath at first

Navigation
- Pack a topographic map and compass/GPS as there are no marked trails
- Download offline maps for the area in case you lose reception
- Have solid navigation and route-finding skills for the boulder scrambling required
- Bring an emergency bivvy/shelter in case you get caught out overnight

Safety
- Travel with a experienced partner, never alone in such remote terrain
- Bring an emergency satellite communication device 
- Pack a small med kit, repair tools, fire starters, and extra food
- Be aware of rockfall hazards from the cliffs and waterfall
- Know your limits and turn around before overextending yourself

By taking the proper precautions, you can experience the majesty of this primordial alpine world while minimizing risks. Move slowly, stay alert, and appreciate the raw power that has sculpted this incredible glacial cirque over millennia.
