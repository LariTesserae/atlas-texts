To prepare for visiting this hauntingly beautiful and desolate salt flat realm, there are several key considerations:

Environmental Protection:
- Wear soft-soled shoes or boots with excellent traction to avoid damaging the delicate salt crust underfoot. The crunchy surface is very fragile.
- Carry all supplies and trash in and out to prevent any litter or pollution in this pristine environment.
- Stay on established paths when possible to minimize erosion and impact.
- Do not remove any artifacts or disturb ancient remnants you may encounter.

Gear and Supplies:
- Pack ample water and salty snacks/electrolyte drinks - the dry air can cause rapid dehydration.
- Wear breathable, moisture-wicking layers as the salt flats can get very hot during the day even with the dim illumination.
- Sunblock, sunglasses, and broad-brimmed hats are essential for protection from the harsh reflected glare off the salt surfaces.
- Trekking poles or staffs may be handy for extra stability on the uneven terrain. 
- Compass/GPS for navigation in this featureless landscape where it's easy to lose one's bearings.

Logistics:
- Study maps to identify any hazards like areas prone to thin crusts, dissolution pits, or salt water seeps.
- Time your visit for when the dim illumination will allow the best views/photography opportunities.
- Bring equipment like cameras, tripods, drones if you wish to capture the stark beauty and unique formations.

Mindset:
- Be prepared for complete silence and stillness - no wildlife, running water, or man-made sounds. Embrace the profound quiet.
- Maintain a sense of wonder and respect for the ancient geological forces that shaped this austere, alien landscape over millennia.
- Stay alert for subtle shifts in light, shadow, and mirages - your senses will be heightened in the hushed mineral realm.

By coming prepared yet treading lightly, you can fully immerse yourself in this timeless salt flat realm - a surreal, mesmerizing expanse of earth sculpted by mineral, wind, and the ceaseless march of epochal change.
