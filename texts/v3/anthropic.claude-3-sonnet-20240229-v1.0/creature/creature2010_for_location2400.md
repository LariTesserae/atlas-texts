In the high reaches of that ancient, wind-scoured plateau, where stone mazes lie half-buried under epochs of time, life persists in strange and wondrous forms. Here are some of the primordial denizens that dwell within that mineral realm:

Radiolaria
These microscopic, single-celled creatures resemble intricate, glassine sculptures. Their bodies are composed of fusilli-like spirals and latticeworks of silica, formed into geometric perfection over eons of evolution. Drifting through the underground aquifers on cytoplasmic pseudopods, they filter nutrients from the mineral-rich waters. Their fossils, millions of years old, can be found embedded in the surrounding rock strata.

Lithophytes
Plantlike but descended from fungal ancestors, the lithophytes consist of fleshy, tubular stalks that extrude from cracks and fissures in the stone. At their tips, they bear bioluminescent fruiting bodies that glow with a pale green radiance. These light sources attract tiny, arthropodal grazers which disperse the lithophytes' spores.

Antrovores  
Sluggish, wormlike creatures encased in rocky armor plates, the antrovores ingest stone itself. Potent acids in their guts dissolve and metabolize the minerals, allowing them to derive sustenance from the very bedrock over lapses of deep time. Their steady consumption and excretion gradually reshape the underground environs into new caves and tunnels.

Xenoforms
Defying conventional biological categories, the xenoforms are self-replicating inorganic structures, more akin to liquid crystals than carbon-based life. They spread across mineral surfaces in iridescent, kaleidoscopic patterns, their molecular chains zipping apart and rezipping in new arrangements perpetually. Guided by indecipherable programming, they construct intricate, fractal architectures from the raw elements around them.

Such enigmatic beings fill the shadowy, stone labyrinths - inscrutable survivors shaped by the remorseless grinding of ages in this atavistic, near-timeless world entombed in rock.
