In the vast living metropolis of the ancient eroded plateau, a myriad of plant-based inhabitants thrive in a symphony of symbiosis and complexity. Here are some of the denizens that call this verdant realm home:

Myco-Neural Networks: Vast interconnected mycelial colonies of luminous fungi form the neural pathways of this ecosystem. Their glowing filaments pulse with information, coordinating growth, repair, and resource distribution across unimaginable distances. They are the unseen intelligences that govern the rhythms of this living city.

Arboreal Titans: Towering woody trunks twist and fuse together into living archways and vaulted ceilings. Their branches have become habitats unto themselves, hosting epiphytic gardens, micro-ponds, and specialized frond arrays that capture light, wind, and atmospheric gases. The deep, slow breath of these vegetative skyscrapers can be felt in the perpetual creaks and groans that echo through the branches.

Filter Mosses: Thick, spongy mats of finely branched mosses act as the lungs of this system, absorbing and purifying the humid air. Their densely packed phyllids create micro-environments that host micro-faunal decomposers vital to nutrient cycling.

Hydro-Vascular Networks: Systems of roots, conductive tissues, and specialized vascular plants form the circulatory system, channeling and purifying water and nutrients. Aquatic micro-habitats teem with anaerobic root cultures that extract minerals and generate metabolic energy.

Thermo-Metabolic Nodes: In hollows and cavities, dense tangles of fungi and thermophilic bacterial colonies break down woody debris, generating heat and releasing nutrients in highly concentrated composting masses. These are the furnaces that power pockets of accelerated growth.

Epiphytic Gardens: The branches host entire micro-ecosystems of smaller plants and fungi. Ferns, mosses, lichens, and epiphytic orchids find specialized niches, accessing nutrients, water and light through the living architecture around them.

Sessile Faunal Relics: Vestiges of the mobile animal life that once roamed this world remain in isolated pockets - sponges filter-feeding on organic detritus that washes through the branches, coral-like colonial invertebrates encrusting woody surfaces, and strange, primitive arthropods tending symbiotic fungal gardens in rotted hollows.

While still composed of individual species, all inhabitants function as a seamlessly integrated whole in this ultimate culmination of life's grand vegetative experiment - a vast, self-sustaining urban ecology of pure photosynthetic life.
