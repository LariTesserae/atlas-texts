In this lush, artificially cultivated ecosystem, a variety of lifeforms thrive amidst the melding of nature and technology. Here are some of the denizens that might inhabit this unique realm:

Arboreal Maintenance Corps: Agile and nimble humanoid figures clad in flexible, reinforced biomesh suits. They nimbly navigate the towering botanical structures, performing pruning, grafting, and adjustments to the complex framework with specialized tools. Their suits are equipped with integrated oxygen recyclers and nutrient dispensers to sustain the plants.

Myco-Drones: Spore-dispersing robotic drones the size of large insects flit through the underbrush, seeding new fungal colonies and monitoring the health of existing mycological specimens. Their bodies are covered in a fibrous, fungal-like material that allows them to camouflage seamlessly.  

Therapsid Cultivators: Lumbering, four-legged semi-sentient creatures whose bodies are symbiotic composites of flesh and machine. They lumber along the pathways, shedding a trail of nutrient-rich biomatter to fertilize the soil. Heat vents along their backs provide warmth for incubating plant seedlings embedded in their carapace-like shells.

Pollen Vectors: Shimmering, iridescent insectoid creatures no larger than a human hand. They flutter from flower to flower, guided by integrated pheromone sensors, facilitating cross-pollination. Delicate, wire-like appendages allow them to extract and combine genetic material for controlled hybridization.

Phytorematic Custodians: Towering, multi-appendaged robotic sentries that stoically guard the perimeter. Arrays of sensors and high-intensity light projectors allow them to detect and purge airborne contaminants and invasive organisms that could threaten the ecosystem's balance.

This verdant domain is teeming with life, an intricate symbiosis of biological and technological entities all playing their part in maintaining the delicate equilibrium of this manufactured biosphere. Nature and machine have fused into an integrated, self-sustaining habitat.
