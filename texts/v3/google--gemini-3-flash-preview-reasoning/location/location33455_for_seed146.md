This is the **Interior of the Great Cleave**, a subterranean cavity so immense that its ceiling is lost to a permanent, heat-shimmering haze. It is a world defined by the absence of the sun and the presence of a heavy, unmoving warmth that radiates directly from the floor.

### The Terrain of Sharp Edges
The ground is not soil, but a vast, fractured expanse of obsidian and dark basalt. Because there is no water to smooth the stone and no wind to grind it down, every feature remains as sharp as the moment it was birthed by the crust’s shifting. The floor is a mosaic of "razor-flats"—kilometers-wide sheets of black volcanic glass, cracked into geometric patterns. Between these flats are ridges of jagged, un-weathered stone that rise like frozen waves, their peaks sharp enough to sever leather. 

There is no moisture here. The "seas" of this world are vast basins filled with an ultra-fine, frictionless mineral dust. This dust behaves like a liquid, pooling in the lowlands, yet it is bone-dry. If you were to step into it, you would sink instantly into a warm, suffocating silence.

### The Atmosphere and Scale
The air is thick, pressurized by the weight of the miles of rock above, and remarkably still. It carries a scent of scorched flint and dry sulfur. The scale is terrifying; the horizon does not exist, replaced instead by a gradual fading into a charcoal-colored gloom. The "weather" here is not rain or wind, but slow, vertical plumes of heat—thermals that rise from the deep magma-veins below the floor, carrying dust particles that hang suspended for centuries, never settling, never moving horizontally.

### The Abundant, Silent Fauna
Though there is no green life—no leaves to rustle, no moss to cushion a step—the Great Cleave is crowded. The density of presence is high, but it is a silent population.

The fauna here are "Lithovores" and "Heat-Drinkers." Giant, slow-moving entities with silicate shells, resembling trilobites the size of small houses, crawl across the razor-flats. They move with an imperceptible slowness, scraping the mineral deposits from the glass with rhythmic, grinding mandibles. In the air, vast "Manta-Drifters"—creatures made of thin, leathery membranes—hang motionless, suspended on the permanent heat plumes. They do not fly; they simply exist in the stack, layered one above the other in the thousands, filtering the heavy air for microscopic spores of sulfur-fungi.

Everything here is sightless. There is no light to see by, so the creatures navigate via thermal mapping and tremors. They are nearly silent, producing only low-frequency vibrations that hum through the soles of one's feet.

### The Scattered Marks of Hand
Man-made (or sapient-made) structures are rare but striking. They are not cities, but "Way-Markers"—monolithic stacks of obsidian slabs piled high without mortar. These cairns are the only things that break the natural geometry of the glass flats. 

Beside some of these stacks, one might find primitive tools: heavy hammers made of dense ore lashed to bone handles with dried sinew. There are no wheels, no complex machines, and no evidence of fire—for fire requires oxygen and fuel that this dry, mineral world does not provide in abundance. Instead, the inhabitants use the natural heat of the floor to soften resins and shape their tools. The "buildings" are simple, low-slung shelters carved directly into the obsidian ridges, designed to shield the occupants not from weather, but from the crushing weight of the silence and the proximity of the drifting beasts.

### The Rule of Equilibrium
The most striking feature of the Cleave is its stasis. It is a world in a state of perfect, warm equilibrium. There are no seasons, no day-night cycles, and no tides. The temperature never fluctuates more than a fraction of a degree. A stone placed on a ledge will stay there for a million years, undisturbed by wind or rot. 

Time feels irrelevant here. The landscape is "young" in its sharpness, yet it feels ancient in its stillness. It is a fresh wound in the earth that has refused to heal or scar, preserved forever in the warm, dark, and airless quiet. The only sound is the occasional, tectonic *crack* of a glass sheet settling—a sound that carries for leagues, echoing off the invisible ceiling, before the world returns to its heavy, breathless rest.
