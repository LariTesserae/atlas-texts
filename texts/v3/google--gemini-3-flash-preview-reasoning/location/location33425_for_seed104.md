This is a world that has forgotten the concept of a horizon. It is a place of **The High Silt**, a realm where the atmosphere has reached a state of terminal saturation. Imagine an ocean that refuses to fall, a sea that has climbed the sky and stayed there, suspended by ancient, broken physics. 

### The Medium and the Light
You do not breathe air here; you breathe a warm, viscous suspension—a "brine-mist" so thick it offers buoyancy, yet so clear in its own strange way that it transmits a dim, amber radiance from a sun that hasn't been seen directly in a million years. The light is 1.4—a perpetual, gloaming dusk. It doesn't cast shadows; it pools in the hollows of the terrain like spilled honey. The temperature is a steady, feverish 2.3, a humid warmth that feels like the inside of a living throat.

### The Terrain: The Ancient Shelves
The ground beneath this atmospheric ocean is not jagged or young. It is 2.7 eroded—a state of "Final Smoothness." These are the continental plateaus of a dying planet, lifted so high they scrape the edge of the vacuum, yet they are as flat and polished as river stones. There are no crags, no peaks, no sharp edges. The mountains have been worn down into gargantuan, gently sloping mounds that span thousands of miles. 

The material is a dark, non-porous basalt, slick with a microscopic film of bioluminescent slime. Walking—or rather, drifting—across these plains feels like moving across the back of a sleeping god. Because the scale is 2.6 (vast), the weather patterns are internal to the liquid-air itself; massive, slow-moving thermal plumes rise from the ground, carrying rafts of vegetation upward in a "seasonal" cycle that takes decades to complete.

### The Vegetation: The Green Drown
Despite the dim light, the world is lush (1.8). The plants do not have bark or wood; they are made of translucent, fibrous bladders and weeping, emerald veils. They grow in dense, suffocating clusters (2.4 density), creating forests that are more like sponges than groves. 

Long, hair-like fronds, kilometers in length, drift upward from the basalt floor, tethered by root-bulbs that have dissolved the rock over eons. These "Sky-Kelp" forests are so thick that they dampen all movement. To move through them is to be brushed by a thousand velvet fingers. They are quiet, absorbing all vibration, leaving the world in a state of 0.5 sound—a muffled, underwater silence broken only by the occasional low-frequency thrum of the planet’s crust.

### The Built: The Ghost Anchors
Humanity, or whatever preceded this state, is a ghost here (0.8). You will occasionally find a "Ghost Anchor"—a single, monolithic spire of a white, ceramic material that resists all erosion. These are not cities, but perhaps needles meant to stitch the atmosphere to the earth. They are scattered, solitary, and hollow. Inside, there is no technology (0.5), only the calcified remains of biological systems—pipes made of bone, valves made of dried membranes. They serve no purpose now but as perches for the drifting flora.

### The Weirdness: The Breakdown
The reality here is 2.8—highly unstable. Because the water and the air have become one, the laws of fluid dynamics have begun to bleed into the laws of logic. 
*   **Time-Lensing:** In certain dense pockets of vegetation, time slows. You can watch a single drop of heavier brine fall for three days, its descent tracing a slow, elegant spiral.
*   **Non-Euclidean Currents:** You may swim in a straight line for miles, only to find yourself looking at your own back. The space here is folded by the weight of the suspended ocean.
*   **Mirage-Mass:** The water-air occasionally crystallizes into "hard light" structures—brief, translucent geometries that look like buildings or faces, only to dissolve back into warm mist when touched.

### The Fauna: The Solitary Greats
Life is rare (0.6). There are no swarms of fish or flocks of birds. Instead, there are the **Loomers**. These are gargantuan, jellyfish-like entities the size of small moons, drifting in the upper reaches of the brine-mist. They are translucent and nearly invisible, evidenced only by the way the stars (if they could be seen) would wobble behind their mass. They do not hunt; they filter the ambient heat of the world. They are the only things that make sound—a groan that takes an hour to finish, vibrating through your bones rather than your ears.

### The Dynamic
The world is active (2.4), but its pace is geological. The "weather" consists of massive migrations of salinity. A "storm" is a wall of high-density water, a literal river in the sky, a hundred miles wide, that moves through the mist with the force of a slow-motion avalanche. When these currents hit the ancient, smooth plateaus, they create "Vortex Gardens"—swirling nurseries of vegetation that bloom, die, and are recycled into the brine in a matter of centuries.

Everything here is settling. The world is a warm, wet, silent room that is slowly filling with its own history. It is a place of profound peace and terrifying scale, where the difference between drowning and breathing has been eroded away by time.
