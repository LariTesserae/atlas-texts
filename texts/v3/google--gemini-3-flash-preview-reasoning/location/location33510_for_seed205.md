To stand on the surface of the Great Xylem is to forget that stone and soil ever existed. 

The horizon here does not crumble into dust or rise in jagged granite peaks; instead, it curves in a vast, weathered expanse of silver-grey cellulose and furrowed lignin. This is a continent-sized organism, an upland plateau of living fiber situated so high in the atmosphere that the clouds do not float above the land, but scrape against it, snagging like wool on the jagged, miles-wide ridges of its bark.

The heat is a physical weight—a dry, radiating fever generated by the planet-scale metabolism occurring beneath your feet. It is 2.8/3 intensity, a relentless noon that smells of baked sap, ozone, and fermenting nectar. Because the elevation is high, the air is thin and sharp, yet it carries the stifling warmth of a greenhouse. 

### The Substrate and Scarcity
The terrain is a study in "Sparse Totality." While the environment is 100% vegetation—every cliff is a petrified stem, every valley a hollowed-out vascular tube—the actual "content" of the landscape is spread thin. You can walk for days across a flat, sun-bleached plain of dormant moss-fleece without encountering a single landmark. The scale is so vast that the geography feels empty. A "mountain" here is simply a terminal bud, a dormant node of growth three miles high, smoothed by eons of atmospheric erosion into a dome of polished amber.

Water is a rare, precious event. There are no oceans, only the "Vein-Runs." These are deep, vertical canyons that plunge miles into the interior of the plant. During the intermittent rains, these canyons roar with the sound of rushing sap and rainwater, but for most of the cycle, they are merely damp, moss-coated fissures where travelers must climb down to lick the condensation from the massive, pulsing walls.

### The Deafening Ecology
The silence one might expect from a sparse desert is absent here. The landscape is deafening. The sound is a relentless, multi-layered cacophagus: the high-pitched shriek of wind whistling through miles of hollow trichome-hairs, the rhythmic *thrum-thud* of the world-plant’s deep-tissue capillary pumps, and, most dominantly, the fauna.

The animals are overwhelming in number, though often hidden in the microscopic furrows of the bark. They are "chatter-vines" and "click-beetles" the size of ground-cars, and "shriek-wasps" whose wings create a permanent harmonic drone in the air. You do not see them often in the vast open spaces, but you hear them everywhere—a trillion invisible lungs and carapaces vibrating in the heat. To speak to a companion, one must shout.

### The Marks of the Inhabitants
Scattered across this wooden wasteland are the "Splinters"—the built remains of a primitive culture. These are not cities, but solitary, wind-scoured hovels carved directly into the dead-wood of the ridges. 

The technology is strictly manual and bio-extractive. There are no metals. The inhabitants use "Glass-Leaf" as cutting edges—shards of silica-heavy foliage that can be honed to a molecular sharpness. Their ropes are twisted vascular fibers; their light sources are translucent bladders filled with bioluminescent fluid harvested from the "Glow-Galls" that grow in the shade of the stem-mountains. These people are transients, moving between the damp fissures, leaving behind only faint marks: a series of hand-holds cut into a vertical trunk, or a circle of charred wood where a fire of resin-bricks once burned.

### The Rules of the World
In this place, the "weather" is biological. A "storm" might not be rain, but a "Pollen-Gale"—a golden obscuration of the sun as a distant part of the continent releases its reproductive spores, coating everything in a thick, suffocating dust for weeks. 

The erosion here is "established." The world doesn't feel new or chaotic; it feels ancient and weathered. The great canyons have been smoothed by wind for ten thousand years. The plant itself moves at a pace that mimics plate tectonics—a slow, creaking expansion that can trigger "Bark-Quakes," where sections of the landscape the size of cities shear off and fall into the sun-baked abyss below.

You are a parasite on a god. You walk across a skin that does not know you are there, under a sun that wants to bake you into the fibers, accompanied by a scream of life that never stops to breathe. It is a world of infinite wood, shimmering heat, and the lonely, scattered debris of those few who try to call its vast, vibrating surface a home.
