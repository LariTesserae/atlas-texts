The air does not carry the scent of rain, nor the damp rot of a swamp. Instead, it smells of scorched cinnamon, pulverized stone, and the sharp, alkaline sting of dry resin. This is a world of the **Xero-Marrow**: a subterranean expanse the size of a continent, where the concept of a "liquid" is an ancient myth. Here, life has bypassed the need for hydration, evolving instead into a hyper-active, thrumming ecology of dry-cell biology and mineral synthesis.

### The Substrate and the Growth
There is no floor, only the **Great Thicket**. Imagine a forest where the trees do not grow from soil, but are themselves the soil, the architecture, and the atmosphere. Huge, calcified boles—thick as cathedral naves—crisscross the void in a chaotic, three-dimensional lattice. These are not made of wood as we know it, but of a stiff, fibrous silica-lignin that is harder than oak and as dry as bone.

The vegetation is total. It fills the space until the "horizon" is merely a blur of intersecting amber-colored ribs and pale, papery leaves that rattle like sheet metal in the wind. Because the erosion is "emerging," the landscape feels embryonic, as if it were still being knit together by a frantic, invisible weaver. At any moment, a new spire of crystalline thorn-growth might erupt from a pillar, piercing the air with the sound of a shattering mountain, adding a new bridge to the mess of the world.

### The Sound and the Turbulence
The silence of a cave is absent here. The environment is **deafening**. The sound is a constant, tectonic roar—the collective friction of billions of dry leaves grinding against one another, the rhythmic snapping of expanding fibers, and the high-pitched shriek of wind being forced through narrow, organic flutes.

It is a turbulent world. The landscape is not static; it is a slow-motion storm of biological construction. Great sections of the Thicket collapse under their own weight, turning into fine, grey flour that chokes the lower reaches, only to be immediately reclaimed by the fast-growing "Scavenger-Vines" that weave the wreckage back into the ceiling. The heat is a constant, radiating warmth, generated by the sheer kinetic energy of all this growth and the friction of the wind.

### The Fauna
If the plants are the bones of this world, the fauna is its blood. The density of animal life is overwhelming. They are not soft-bodied; they are creatures of chitin, scale, and dry silk. 

*   **Pulse-Moths** with wingspans the size of skiffs beat the air in rhythmic unison, their wings coated in a bioluminescent dust that provides the "normal light" of the realm—a diffused, flickering amber glow.
*   **Needle-Sworms**—clouds of trillion-strong micro-fauna—flow through the gaps in the vegetation like rivers of living smoke, scouring the pillars for mineral deposits.
*   **Striders** with spindly, multi-jointed legs miles long pick their way through the lattice, their bodies vibrating at frequencies that can shatter the very pillars they walk upon.

The animals do not drink. They "breathe" the fine mineral dust, processing the dry chemistry of the air into energy. The competition for space is so fierce that the animals are often fused to the plants—a lizard-thing might spend half its life as a stationary knot on a branch, only to detach and scuttle away when the branch begins to crack.

### The Built and the Primitive
Humanity, or what passes for it here, exists in the cracks. Their presence is **scattered** and **primitive**. You might find a "Loom-Village"—a cluster of dwellings woven directly into the swaying canopy of a Great Thicket branch using hand-stripped fibers and resin-glue. 

There is no metal. Fire is a terrifying, forbidden god, for the entire world is a tinderbox. Instead, the inhabitants use **manual craft** to shape the world. They use saws made of serrated obsidian-leaves to carve out hollows in the pillars. They use gravity-weighted looms to weave the silk of the Pulse-Moths into tough, leather-like clothing. Their "tech" is a mastery of tension and friction; they travel the vertical reaches using complex systems of counter-weighted pulleys and vine-swings.

Signs of their passage are faint: a handprint burned into a resin-slick with a hot stone, a bridge of braided hair spanning a mile-wide chasm, or a solitary prayer-totem carved into a dying pillar.

### The Atmosphere of the Embryo
Despite the scale, the world feels claustrophobic because of its **density**. There is no "up" or "down," only "closer to the core" or "closer to the crust." The light is a steady, warm orange, filtered through leagues of translucent amber leaves. 

It is a place of violent, beautiful becoming. It feels like being inside a heart as it beats, or inside a kiln as the pottery is firing. The rules are simple: grow, weave, or be ground into dust. The air is never still, the noise never ceases, and the thirst is a holy state of being. You are standing in the middle of a biological machine that is building itself out of nothing, fueled by heat and the deafening song of its own frantic expansion.
