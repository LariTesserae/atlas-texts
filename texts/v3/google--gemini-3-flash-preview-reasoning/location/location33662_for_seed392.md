The world is a vast, hand-wrought basin of fired clay and woven reeds, extending toward a horizon that never curves, only pales into a white, shadowless glare. There is no stone here that was not shaped by a palm, no soil that was not carried in a basket and laid flat. It is a total construction, a regional-scale artifact of monumental effort and primitive design.

The ground is a geometric expanse of terracotta tiles, each the size of a courtyard, fitted together with sharp, uneroded precision. The seams between them are crisp, lacking the soft rounding of age or the debris of wind-blown dust. This is a young world, recently finished or perhaps caught in the moment of its completion, yet it is utterly still. There is no wind to carry seeds, and no rain to wear down the hard, orange edges of the work.

Water exists here as a shallow, pervasive presence. It does not flow; it occupies. It sits in the low-lying grooves between tiles and gathers in wide, circular depressions carved into the clay. It is a "wet" world not of storms, but of constant, heavy perspiration. The air is a thick, hot soup of suspended moisture, a condensing atmosphere that clings to the skin like a warm damp cloth. Drops form on the undersides of the woven bridges and hang there, trembling but never falling. The water is clear, warm as blood, and perfectly reflective, turning the ground into a double-image of the blinding white sky.

The vegetation is lush but strangely spaced. Out of pre-drilled apertures in the terracotta floors, enormous, fleshy stalks erupt. These are not wild things; they appear planted, or perhaps summoned. They have the broad, waxy leaves of tropical ferns but the structural rigidity of columns. They are a deep, saturated green, standing in high-contrast clusters against the pale clay. Because there is no wind and the dynamic of the world is frozen, the leaves do not rustle. They are static, heavy fans of chlorophyll, providing shade that is just as bright as the light, as the atmosphere scatters the glare into every corner.

Technologically, the world is a masterpiece of the basic. There are no machines, no humming wires, no moving parts. Everything is built from the materials of the hearth: baked earth, cured hide, braided grass, and notched wood. Massive, multi-story scaffolds made of lashed bamboo rise at wide intervals, serving as towers for non-existent sentries. These structures are intricate, showing the marks of flint knives and hand-turned drills, yet they achieve a scale that suggests a valley-sized city, even if the inhabitants are nowhere to be seen.

The fauna is abundant but behaves with an unsettling, statuesque grace. Large, pale amphibians with skin like wet porcelain sit on the edges of the water-grooves. They do not hunt; they simply exist, their throats pulsing in a slow, rhythmic swell that produces no sound. Insects with wings of stained glass hover over the stagnant pools, their flight so stable they look like ornaments suspended on invisible wires. There is a sense of a high-density life force that has no need for the frantic energy of survival.

Reality here suffers from a quiet, shimmering breakdown. Because the light is so bright and the atmosphere so thick, depth perception begins to fail. Distant towers seem to overlap with nearby leaves. The horizon is not a line, but a smudge of heat-haze where the terracotta tiles seem to float upward into the white sky. If you were to walk for miles, the temperature would not drop, the sun would not move, and the sharp edges of the bricks would not show a single chip of wear. It is a permanent noon in a brand-new world that has already decided it will never change.

The sound is a heavy, ringing silence. The only noise is the internal sound of one’s own pulse and the occasional, wet *thwack* of an amphibian shifting its weight on a tile—a sound that carries for miles across the flat, open basin, unhindered by wind or the rustle of trees. It is a quiet so profound it feels architectural, as if the silence itself was one of the materials used to build the place. 

Everything is made. Everything is new. Everything is still. It is a lush, sweltering gallery of primitive geometry, drowning in its own bright, humid breath.
