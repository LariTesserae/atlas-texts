It is not a planet with a forest; it is a planet that is a forest. Or more accurately, it is a planet that is a single, embryonic tissue, mid-expansion. 

At this scale, the concept of "ground" is a vestigial memory. There is no stone, no tectonic plate, no volcanic heart. There is only the **Root-Core**, a pressurized ball of super-condensed cellulose at the center of the world, and the **Lignin-Shell** that radiates outward for thousands of miles. 

The atmosphere is a permanent, cool grey-green dimness. Because the vegetation occupies nearly 98% of the available volume, the "air" consists only of the narrow, twisting capillaries between massive, mile-thick stalks and the underside of continental-scale leaves. It is a world of perpetual twilight, illuminated only by the faint, bioluminescent pulse of sap moving through translucent bark. The light is a 1.3—a bruised, underwater glow that never brightens, regardless of the rotation of the distant star.

The moisture is absolute. Water does not fall as rain; it *exudes*. The humidity is so high that the air itself feels like a soft, cool sponge. Every surface—from the towering vertical trunks to the smallest capillary filament—is slick with a nutrient-rich phlegm. In the "valleys" (which are merely the spaces where three or more gargantuan limbs converge), the water pools into vertical marshes, held in place by surface tension and the dense, mat-like weave of moss-analogs. These are weeping landscapes. Every inch of the world is crying, a constant, cool perspiration that smells of ozone and crushed mint.

The sound is the most overwhelming feature. At 2.9, it is not a sound you hear; it is a sound you feel in your marrow. It is the **Great Hydraulic Roar**. Because the world is growing at an embryonic pace—expanding, knitting, and hardening simultaneously—the movement of fluid is violent. Imagine the sound of a trillion liters of water being forced through high-pressure pipes, combined with the low-frequency groan of wood the size of mountain ranges snapping and resetting. It is a deafening, wet thrumming that never ceases. It is the sound of a heartbeat if the heart were the size of a moon.

There are no animals here. There is no room for them, and no ecological niche for a consumer when the producer is so total. The "tech" of this world is hidden in the precision of the growth. Upon close inspection, the venation of the leaves does not follow the chaotic branching of Earth-plants; it follows the cold, hyper-efficient geometry of a processor. The plant-mass is embedded with organic nanostructures—crystalline lattices within the cell walls that govern light-harvesting and structural integrity. It is an engineered biosphere that has discarded its creators, a biological machine currently in its "emerging" phase. The erosion is 0.1 because nothing has had time to wear down. The edges of the thorns are molecularly sharp; the ridges of the bark are pristine. The world is being printed, layer by layer, from the inside out.

The terrain is a vertical labyrinth. To move through it is to climb through the inner workings of a gargantuan, wet clock. There is no horizon, only the receding perspective of infinite pillars and the terrifyingly vast overhead canopy that serves as the world’s sky. Occasionally, a "Gasp-Vent" opens—a miles-wide stomata that exhales a plume of frigid oxygen and mist into the upper reaches, creating localized weather systems of frost and falling sleet.

The temperature remains a steady, bracing cool. The plant-mass acts as a perfect heat sink, regulating the entire planet to the exact degree required for the rapid synthesis of new lignin. It feels like the air inside a cellar or a cathedral—damp, still, and heavy with the weight of the structure above.

This is a world of "pre-formation." It is a planetary embryo. It is dense, packed so tightly that a falling object would not hit the ground for days, instead bouncing from leaf to moss-shelf to vine. It is a loud, wet, dark cradle of life that has no need for anything other than itself. It is a masterpiece of biological engineering, growing in the dark, screaming as it expands.
