The space exists within a vertical capillary, a narrow, soaring flue tucked between the folds of something much larger. It is a pocket of extreme verticality, a mile-deep rift compressed into the width of a human thumb. To enter it is to experience the vertigo of a mountain range within the intimacy of a throat.

There is no natural earth here. Every surface is **built**. The walls are a dense, frantic lattice of hand-lashed bamboo, woven reeds, and sun-baked clay tiles, all stacked and spiraling upward in a dizzying, precarious architecture. It is a total construction, a hollow monument made of millions of individual primitive efforts. Because the **erosion is 0.0**, the edges of these materials are unnervingly sharp; the clay is still wet and fingerprint-smudged, the reeds are green and sappy, and the knots in the twine haven’t yet settled into their permanent tension. The world is being birthed and occupied simultaneously.

The **temperature** is a punishing, wet heat, a tropical kiln that never cools. This heat draws moisture out of the very materials of the walls, creating a **condensing atmosphere** where the air feels like a warm, saturated sponge. Water doesn’t just fall; it weeps. It beads on the underside of every hand-carved rung and runs in frantic, hot rivulets down the vertical weaving. The sound is a **constant, rhythmic thrum**—the roar of a hidden boiler, the hissing of steam escaping through reed-pipes, and the wet *slap-slap-slap* of water hitting the saturated foliage below.

**Vegetation** is hyper-dense and aggressive, thriving in the dim, amber light. Translucent ferns with stalks like glass and broad, fleshy leaves the color of bruised plums erupt from the gaps in the woven walls. They grow with a visible, shuddering speed, their tendrils wrapping around the primitive scaffolds even as the builders—whoever they are—try to lash new layers of clay. The plants are the primary structural support, their roots acting as living rebar within the un-eroded mud.

This is where **reality breaks**. Because the scale is so small yet the elevation so extreme, gravity behaves like a liquid. In certain pockets of the flue, the steam doesn't rise; it pools in heavy, glowing spheres that drift upward only when nudged by a loud sound. The verticality is so intense that the "bottom" of the rift is invisible, lost in a shimmering haze of heat-distortion, while the "top" appears to be a blindingly bright needle-eye of white light that never gets closer, no matter how much one climbs.

**Fauna** is nearly non-existent, save for the occasional "Glass-Strider"—a creature that looks like a cluster of wet needles, vibrating at a frequency that matches the roar of the steam. They move through the dense vegetation not by walking, but by flickering, disappearing and reappearing an inch higher as if the frame-rate of the world is stuttering.

The **tech** level is stubbornly manual. There are no gears, only levers made of green wood; no electricity, only the dim phosphorescence of crushed moss smeared into the clay. The environment is a testament to an impossible, frantic labor—a trillion tiny hand-strokes meant to build a mountain inside a crack. It is a claustrophobic cathedral of wet fiber and boiling mist, a place that is being finished at the same moment it is being imagined, loud and hot and perfectly, impossibly full.
