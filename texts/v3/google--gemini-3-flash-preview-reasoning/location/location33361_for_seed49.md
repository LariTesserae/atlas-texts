The world is a singular, suffocating achievement: a planetary-scale urbanity known as the Plenum. 

Here, the concept of a "horizon" has been erased. The elevation is low—a flat, endless basin of hyper-dense infrastructure—but the verticality is so compressed and layered that one feels buried rather than grounded. There is no soil, no silt, no loam. The "crust" of the world is a multi-layered tectonic plate of obsidian-slick alloys, conductive ceramics, and translucent glass that has been weathered into smooth, river-stone curves by eons of friction and moisture.

It is a warm, sweltering world. The temperature sits at a constant, humid fever-pitch, generated by the friction of a trillion moving parts and the heat-bleed of reactors buried miles below the surface. The atmosphere is not just air; it is a thick, saturated suspension. Water is everywhere, though it never falls as distinct rain. Instead, it weeps. Every vertical surface—the soaring pylons of folded-space transit, the jagged honeycombs of the residential hives—glistens with a permanent, heavy condensation. Droplets coalesce on cold-fusion conduits and drip rhythmically into the "gutters," which are actually glowing canals of recycled coolant and runoff that lace the landscape like a glowing vascular system.

There is a total absence of green. Biology has been replaced by a frantic, mineral-based ecology. In the shadows of the massive conduits, instead of moss, there are vast carpets of piezoelectric crystals that flicker with pale, internal light when stepped upon. Instead of trees, there are "spires"—towers of self-organizing silicate that grow in fractal patterns, drawing energy from the ambient electromagnetic roar of the city. These mineral growths are the only thing resembling "vegetation," and they are as hard as diamond and warm to the touch.

The density is absolute. There is no negative space in the Plenum. Every cubic meter is occupied by a wire, a pipe, a dwelling, or a machine. The architecture is "beyond modern," operating on principles of non-Euclidean geometry; a building may appear small from the exterior but contain kilometers of folded interior space. The structures are not built so much as they are secreted and eroded. The city feels "established," like a reef that has grown over millions of years, its sharp edges blunted by the constant flow of traffic and the abrasive quality of the humid air.

The sound is a physical weight. It is a deafening, oceanic roar composed of a billion simultaneous frequencies: the low-frequency thrum of the planet-core generators, the scream of sub-atomic transit shuttles, and the rhythmic, percussive clatter of the "fauna." 

The fauna are abundant, though they are not flesh and blood. They are lithic and mechanical scavengers—hexapedal skittering things made of brass and carbon-fiber, long-necked "siphons" that cling to the weeping walls to drink the mineral-rich condensation, and swarms of tiny, bioluminescent drones that move like starlings through the canyons of the city. These creatures act as the city’s immune system and its janitors, recycling every scrap of waste with a frantic, insectoid energy.

The world is in a state of hyperactive turbulence. Nothing stays static for long. The Plenum is a "bazaar" of matter and energy. Entire sectors of the city may reconfigure themselves in an afternoon—buildings sliding on massive lubricant tracks to make way for new thermal vents, or sky-bridges dissolving into nanoscopic mist to reform elsewhere. It is a chaotic, shifting puzzle where the only constant is the heat, the damp, and the noise.

It is a place of grand, claustrophobic beauty. You stand on a walkway made of translucent jade-glass, peering down through a hundred transparent layers of city, watching the lights of a million lives pulse through the steam. The air tastes of ozone and wet copper. Above you, the sky is not a sky, but a shimmering ceiling of heat-shielding and atmospheric processors, glowing with the dull, steady light of an artificial sun that never sets, casting everything in the sharp, high-contrast clarity of a perpetual noon. You are inside a machine the size of a planet, and it is breathing.
