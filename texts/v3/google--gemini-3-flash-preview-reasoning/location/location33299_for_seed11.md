The world is not a solid floor, but a series of vertical residua. Imagine a mountain range ground down by a billion years of relentless, screaming wind until only the hardest marrow of the earth remains: slender, kilometer-high needles of violet-grey basalt, spaced so far apart that to look from one to the next is to stare into a hazy, shimmering abyss.

This is the **Oul-Rha**, the Sounding Pillars.

### The Terrain and Erosion
The scale is vast and terrifyingly hollow. The density of the world is near zero; it is a landscape of "between." The pillars are the only "somewhere" in a vast "nowhere." Because the erosion is ancient—reaching its stable, final state eons ago—there are no loose rocks, no scree slopes, and no jagged edges. Every surface has been polished by the air into obsidian-smooth curves or rhythmic, fluted grooves that look like the interior of a flute. 

### The Atmosphere and Sound
The air is warm, thick, and perpetually damp, though it rarely rains in drops. Instead, the moisture exists as high-speed ribbons of mist that wrap around the pillars like wet silk. 

The defining characteristic of the Oul-Rha is the **Roar**. The dynamic state of the environment is a 2.9—a permanent, hyper-turbulent atmospheric river that screams through the gaps between the pillars. It is a deafening, physical presence. The sound is not a whistle, but a subterranean thrum combined with a tectonic shriek, a cacophony so loud it can be felt in the marrow of one’s teeth. The air doesn't just move; it boils. It creates standing waves of pressure that can crush a soft object or suspend a heavy stone in mid-air.

### The Lush Ecology
Despite the violence of the wind, the pillars are suffocatingly lush. Evolution has favored the flexible and the adhesive. The vegetation is a carpet of deep, emerald-colored "velvet-moss" and rope-like lianas that wrap hundreds of times around the stone spires to anchor themselves. 

There are "Sail-Ferns" with perforated leaves that allow the gale to pass through without tearing the stem, and "Vomiting Orchids" that capture moisture from the screaming mists and store it in pressurized bladders, occasionally spraying it out in fine, cooling clouds. The green is so intense it looks black in the shadows of the fluted stone. It is a vertical jungle, clinging for dear life to the only stability it has ever known.

### The Built and The Tech
The presence of "civilization" is a ghost. On the leeward side of the widest pillars, protected from the direct brunt of the Roar, one might find a "Thread-Nest." These are the scattered remains of human or near-human habitation: small, circular platforms made of woven liana-fiber and bone, pegged into the basalt with hand-carved stone pitons. 

Technology is strictly primitive and functional. There is no metal; the wind would vibrate it until it shattered or heated to the point of melting. Instead, there are tools of polished bone, heavy stone weights used for stabilizing bridges, and intricate knot-work. The inhabitants communicate not through speech—which is impossible in the Roar—but through a complex language of tactile tugs on shared safety lines and high-contrast hand signals.

### Fauna and Weirdness
Life is rare and strange. The most common inhabitant is the "Sky-Ray," a flat, leathery creature the size of a house that "surfs" the turbulence. They don't fly so much as they are played by the wind like kites, their bodies vibrating to create counter-harmonics that allow them to remain stationary in a 200-mile-per-hour gust. 

There is a fundamental strangeness to the physics here. Occasionally, the turbulence becomes so rhythmic that the moisture in the air crystallizes into "sound-ice"—geometric shapes of frozen mist that don't fall, but drift upward, defying gravity as they are caught in the vertical updrafts of the pillars. These crystals glow with a faint, bioluminescent friction, turning the deafening storms into a kaleidoscope of dancing, freezing light.

### The Rule of the World
In the Oul-Rha, to be disconnected is to cease to exist. Everything—the moss, the ruins, the rare animals—is tethered. The world is a masterpiece of tension. It is an ancient, empty cathedral of wind where the only music is a scream, and the only path is up or down the smooth, green-slicked needles of the earth’s last standing bones.
