The atmosphere here is not air, nor is it quite water; it is a pressurized, hyper-saturated suspended brine, a thick teal gloaming that possesses the weight of a deep ocean but the churning mobility of a storm cell. You do not breathe it so much as your membranes exchange ions with it. It is warm, bordering on hot—a feverish, medicinal heat that smells of crushed mint and scorched copper.

The sound is the first thing that breaks you. It is a relentless, panoramic roar, a physical weight that vibrates the marrow in your bones. It is the sound of a thousand cataracts falling into a hollow mountain, layered with the rhythmic, tectonic shrieking of massive industrial pistons. The noise doesn't travel through the medium; it *is* the medium.

Below, the terrain is a jagged, adolescent nightmare of black obsidian and raw silicate. The land has no memory of stillness. It is "young" in the sense of a fresh wound—sharp-edged ridges, needle-point spires, and deep, geometric fissures that have not yet been smoothed by the violent currents. These obsidian basins are low-lying, forming a vast, regional cradle for the heavy fluid.

Rising from these razor-sharp cracks is a vegetation so aggressive and dense it borders on the claustrophobic. These are not trees, but gargantuan, translucent stalks of vascular kelp-glass, pulsing with a dim, bioluminescent chartreuse. They grow in shivering thickets, their fronds broad as sails, snapping and whipping in the turbulent flow. The growth is hyper-accelerated; you can see the vines spiraling upward, wrapping around anything stationary with a wet, muscular tension.

Interspliced with this emerald chaos are the machines. They are scattered, solitary giants—huge, cast-iron refineries and brass-fitted pumping stations that seem to have been dropped into the basin only yesterday. Their surfaces are pristine, un-pitted by rust, their edges lethal. They are active. Massive flywheels, forty feet across, spin with such velocity that they create cavitation bubbles in the green brine, glowing with white light. Piston-rods thick as redwoods slam into cyclopean cylinders, venting plumes of superheated steam that vanish instantly into the roiling currents. There is no one tending them. There are no animals to flee from their heat. There are only the plants, which weave their tendrils through the gears, sacrificed and crushed in a constant cycle of mangled pulp and regrowth.

Reality here is beginning to fray at the seams of the industrial and the organic. The perspective of the valley distorts; the horizon line occasionally loops, showing the underside of the obsidian seabed arching across the dim, watery sky. Gravity is localized and fickle. In one pocket, the brine flows upward in a spiral toward a humming copper turbine; in another, the vegetation grows in non-Euclidean angles, folding into itself until it disappears.

The light is a bruised, perpetual twilight. The sun is a myth filtered through miles of churning liquid and canopy, reduced to a flickering, sickly glow. The only true illumination comes from the sparks of clashing metal and the faint, rhythmic strobing of the plants as they react to the deafening acoustic pressure.

It is a world of frantic, purposeless productivity. The machines scream and labor to process a medium that is infinite, while the lush, mindless flora attempts to swallow the machines whole. Everything is moving, everything is loud, and everything is new—a raw, high-tech jungle drowning in its own violent birth. There is no silence here, and no stillness. There is only the Great Hiss, the thrum of the engine, and the wet, green dark.
