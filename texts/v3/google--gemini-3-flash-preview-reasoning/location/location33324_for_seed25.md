The world here is a vast, lightless basin, a low-slung depression in the crust of the earth where the air has a weight to it. It is a place of permanent twilight, bordering on total obsidian darkness, cooled by the slow, rhythmic inhalation of the planet. There is no sun here, or if there is, its influence is reduced to a theoretical memory, filtered through leagues of overhead shadow.

The terrain is a study in "established" architecture—not built, but worn. The ground is composed of ancient, water-smoothed basalt and slick, sedimentary shelf-rock. It feels finished, as if the geological processes of upheaval and jaggedness ended eons ago, leaving behind a world of rounded edges and polished hollows. Because the elevation is so low, moisture collects here in a pervasive, heavy dampness. It does not rain in sheets; instead, the walls weep. A fine, cold condensation coats every surface, turning the mineral floor into a black mirror. Occasionally, the topography allows for shallow, stagnant pools or sluggish, oil-thick rivulets that navigate the gentle slopes, but there is no roaring river—only the quiet, persistent soak of a world that never truly dries.

There is not a single leaf, stem, or blade of grass in this expanse. The biological vacuum where plants should be is filled by a mineral sterility that is, paradoxically, teeming with life. In the absence of vegetation, the ecology has skipped a kingdom. The "trees" here are calcified spires, towers of mineral deposits built up by millions of years of dripping water, smoothed by the passage of countless bodies.

The density of this place is its most suffocating quality. It is a "saturated" environment, but the saturation is not of wood or stone, but of flesh, chitin, and breath. The fauna is overwhelming. 

You cannot see the ground because the ground is covered in a carpet of living things. They are layered, three or four deep, a shifting, undulating crust of life that occupies every square inch of the basin. These are not animals as we generally categorize them; they are strange, multi-limbed, eyeless things, ranging from the size of a finger to the size of a siege engine. They move with a collective, frantic purpose, crawling over and under one another in a perpetual, roiling motion. Some are armored in slick, wet plates that clack like stones in a tumbler; others are soft, translucent, and pulsing with internal fluids that cast a faint, cold phosphorescence, though not enough to truly illuminate the dark.

The sound is the most punishing feature of the landscape. It is a physical force. With millions upon millions of creatures occupying a confined, low-lying scale, the noise is a deafening, dissonant roar. It is the sound of a billion legs scraping against polished basalt; the wet, rhythmic slapping of soft bellies against the condensation; the high-frequency chittering of a swarm that never sleeps; and the deep, sub-bass thrumming of the larger leviathans that drift through the press like icebergs in a crowded sea. The sound does not stop. It vibrates in the chest cavity, making the damp air feel as though it is trembling.

The "rules" of this world are governed by touch and vibration. In the absolute darkness, the concept of a "view" is non-existent. The creatures interact through a complex, chaotic language of tremors. The dynamic is one of constant, grinding motion. There is a sense of a food chain, but it is not a hunt; it is a consumption of proximity. The creatures seem to absorb minerals from the weeping walls or feast on the endless, churning biomass of their own kind, a self-sustaining engine of meat and vibration.

It is a cool, damp, screaming world. It feels like the inside of a throat. It is a place where the geology is ancient and tired, but the biology is hyper-active and claustrophobic. You stand in the dark, and you do not feel the wind; you feel the displacement of air from a million moving parts, and you hear the sound of a world that has replaced the silence of the stones with a frantic, eternal heartbeat.
