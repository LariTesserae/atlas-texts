In this place, the sky is not a void but a weight. You are standing on the **Amniotic Heights**, a gargantuan plateau of raw, unweathered obsidian and basalt that sits miles above the planetary crust, yet is entirely drowned. The water here is not a thin liquid; it is a warm, saline soup, viscous and heavy, held against the mountain peaks by a localized gravity so intense it feels like being perpetually hugged by a giant.

The light is the color of bruised plums and deep moss. The sun is a pale, smudged memory filtered through leagues of overhead brine. Because the light is so dim, the world is illuminated from within. 

### The Terrain: The Raw Maw
The ground beneath you has no concept of a curve. This is an infant landscape, geologically speaking—erosion has not yet begun its slow work of sanding down the world. The ridges of the plateau are razor-sharp, crystalline, and jagged. There is no sand, no silt, and no soil. The "earth" is a series of interlocking geometric plates of black glass and cooling volcanic teeth. To walk—or rather, to drift-clamber across it—is to risk being cut by a mountain that hasn't yet learned how to be soft.

### The Vegetation: The Woven Green
Despite the darkness, the flora is overwhelming. Lacking sunlight, the vegetation feeds on the warmth of the rock and the minerals in the thick water. Huge, fleshy fronds the size of ship sails grow in dense, swaying clusters. They are deep indigo and translucent emerald, pulsing with a faint, internal rhythmic glow. These plants are not soft; they are rubbery and tough, designed to withstand the crushing pressure of the heights. They grow in vertical forests that stretch hundreds of feet upward, their roots wrapping like muscular fingers around the sharp obsidian spikes of the terrain.

### The Built World: The Woven Metropolis
Spread across this jagged high-country is a settlement of staggering complexity but primitive means. It is a city of knots. Thousands of bulbous, basket-like dwellings are tethered to the obsidian spires by ropes of braided kelp. There is no metal here, no fire, and no masonry. Every structure is a triumph of manual craft—woven from the husks of giant seed-pods and reinforced with the bones of rare, leviathan-scale creatures that occasionally drift down from the shallower reaches.

The "urban" sprawl is dense. The pods are stacked like clusters of grapes, connected by swinging bridges of vine and hand-carved stone platforms. Because the water is so buoyant, the city is three-dimensional; "streets" are vertical shafts between the woven pods, and "plazas" are open pockets of water where the bioluminescent algae is encouraged to grow in thick, glowing clouds.

### The Atmosphere and Sound
The world moves at the speed of a dream. Because the water is so thick and the temperature so consistently warm, there is no wind and very little current. Objects fallen from a hand do not drop; they drift, swaying like autumn leaves in a heavy fog.

Sound travels far but loses its sharp edges. You hear the low-frequency *thrum* of the planet’s crust—a deep, tectonic vibration that serves as the world’s heartbeat. Occasionally, there is the muffled *clack-clack* of a stone tool hitting a shell, or the rhythmic *swish* of a weaver pulling a vine through a loom. Beyond that, it is a world of silence. The rare fauna—pale, translucent things with many trailing filaments—move like ghosts through the kelp-forests, making no sound at all.

### The Rules of the World
In the Amniotic Heights, time is measured not by days, but by the slow accretion of mineral deposits on the woven walls of the city. To live here is to live in a state of perpetual suspension. 

Technology is limited to what can be achieved with a sharp rock and a clever knot. There is a deep, communal focus on maintenance; the city is constantly being re-woven, as the sharp, un-eroded edges of the mountain slowly saw through the tethers. It is a civilization of stabilizers, holding their breath in a warm, dark, and beautiful cradle of glass and water. 

Nothing is old here except the water. The rocks are new, the city is a work in progress, and the history of the place is still being written in the patterns of the weave. You are standing at the beginning of a world that has decided to take its time starting.
