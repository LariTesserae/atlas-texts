The world is a drowned cathedral of bone-white geometry, a vast, hollowed-out lattice that was once designed but has long since forgotten its purpose. There is no earth here, no soil, no tectonic grinding of stone. Everything—the ground beneath the currents, the pillars that rise into the dark, the archways that span leagues—is constructed. It is a world of "Total Built" substrate, an infinite, skeletal megastructure of an unknown, non-metallic ceramic that feels like petrified ivory to the touch.

It is deep. The water here is not a surface; it is the entirety of the atmosphere, a heavy, frigid, and saline medium that presses inward with the weight of an ocean. Yet, this place exists at a strange "High Elevation" within the water column, a plateau of structures suspended far above a lightless, crushing abyss, though the sky above is equally unreachable. The light is a dim, pervasive indigo, filtering down from somewhere far above, though it never carries the warmth of a sun. It is a cold, perpetual twilight.

The architecture is ancient beyond comprehension. The edges of the great geometric cubes and sprawling honeycombed plazas have been rounded by eons of "Ancient Erosion." The water has polished the hard, white surfaces until they are slick as glass. There are no tools here, no wires, no humming reactors. The technology that built this place has vanished, leaving only the shell behind—a "No Tech" graveyard of monumental proportions. 

Despite the lack of machinery, the environment is intensely "Turbulent." The water does not sit still. Great, invisible rivers of current—subsurface storms—tear through the lattice. These currents are chaotic and hyperactive, shifting direction without warning, carrying clouds of fine, white silt eroded from the structures. One moment, the water is a calm, freezing suspension; the next, it is a violent, rushing gale that threatens to sweep anything not anchored into the void.

There is no vegetation. Not a single strand of kelp or coat of moss clings to the white ribs of the world. The "No Vegetation" parameter renders the landscape stark and monochromatic—a study in white, grey, and deep blue. 

Yet, life is "Abundant." In the absence of plants, the fauna has become the ecology. The structures are populated by pale, translucent organisms that have adapted to the turbulence. Schools of thin, ribbon-like creatures, several meters long, weave through the geometric holes, their bodies undulating to stay stable in the shifting currents. Tiny, bioluminescent crustaceans, like floating sparks, cling to the leeward side of the great pillars, feeding on the microscopic debris kicked up by the erosion. There are larger, slower things, too—massive, gelatinous drifters that act as living filters, anchored to the white floors by thick, fleshy stalks, swaying violently in the underwater storms.

The sound is a paradox. It is "Mostly Quiet," but it is a silence filled with vibration. You do not hear the water so much as you feel it thrumming through the ceramic floor. The turbulence produces a low-frequency moan as the currents whistle through the billions of regular, manufactured apertures in the lattice. It is the sound of a flute the size of a continent, played by a chaotic wind.

The scale is "Open" and "Sparse." You can see for miles through the clear, cold brine, watching the white grid recede into the blue haze. The presences here—the clusters of life or the particularly massive architectural features—are scattered. There is a great deal of "emptiness" between the ribs of the world, a sense of being inside a gargantuan birdcage that has been dropped into a stormy sea. 

There are no rules here but the current. The world is a masterpiece of a dead civilization, now serving as a mere reef for eyeless, drifting things. It is a place of frantic motion and ancient stillness, a built world that has become a wild, cold, and beautiful wilderness.
