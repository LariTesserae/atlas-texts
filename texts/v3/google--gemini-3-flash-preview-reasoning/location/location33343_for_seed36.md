The descent into the Chlorotic Well is not a journey through rock, but a passage through light and pulp. 

At this depth—miles below the world’s true crust—the air is a physical weight, thick with the scent of crushed sugar and overheated sap. The temperature is a constant, unrelenting fever, a dry heat that nonetheless leaves a film of condensation on every surface. It is a world of stagnant brilliance. Above, the sky is not a sky but a singular, blinding aperture of white light, so intense that the shadows it casts are sharp as razors, cutting across a landscape that refuses to move.

The floor of the basin is not composed of earth or stone. There is no geology here, only biology in a state of embryonic arrest. The "ground" is a matted, trembling substrate of pale green lichen and tightly woven, translucent vines, each one as thick as a human torso. These vines are not aged; they possess the raw, tender texture of new shoots, yet they are rigid, supporting the weight of a regional ecosystem. This is a landscape under construction that has forgotten how to finish. Thousands of flower buds, each the size of a carriage, sit perched on the cusp of blooming, their petals perpetually unfurling but never open, frozen in the moment of their birth. The world here is "emerging," a raw, succulent proto-continent that has achieved a perfect, terrifying equilibrium.

Water exists as a secondary thought, a ghost in the machine of the green. It does not flow in rivers; instead, it beads. Enormous globes of distilled moisture, heavy with the weight of the humid air, cling to the undersides of the giant leaves. Occasionally, one will lose its grip, falling in slow motion through the hot, still air to shatter against the moss with a sound like a muffled bell. These are the only interruptions to the silence. 

The density of life is claustrophobic. Every cubic meter of space is occupied by something living—fibrous struts, veined membranes, or the vast, arched canopies of umbrella-trees that never drop a leaf. The scale is immense; the basin stretches for miles, a valley of emerald and bone-white, yet there is no horizon, only the next layer of vertical forest rising to meet the blinding light. 

Dotted throughout this verdant pressure-cooker are the Remains. They are the only things that suggest a history, though they offer no explanation. They are scattered, singular structures: a lone, windowless tower grown from calcified wood; a series of low, stepped platforms made of a material that looks like sun-bleached bone but feels like heartwood. There are no tools here, no scorched earth, no sign of industry. These structures seem to have been secreted by the plants themselves, or perhaps left behind by a civilization that learned to build with cells instead of bricks. They are empty, silent, and slowly being swallowed by the unmoving surge of the moss.

There is almost no movement. The wind does not reach this depth. The fauna is nearly non-existent, save for the occasional "Glass-Strider"—a spindly, six-legged creature with a body of clear chitin that moves with a glacial, rhythmic deliberation. It does not eat; it seems to simply exist as an extension of the heat and the light, a slow-moving crystal in a forest of static jade.

The sound of the place is a heavy, pressurized quiet. It is the sound of a lung held at full capacity. The air is so saturated with oxygen and floral pheromones that it tastes metallic, a sharp tang of ozone and nectar. 

In the Chlorotic Well, time has no teeth. The light never dims, the heat never breaks, and the plants never die. It is a kingdom of the perpetual Now—a raw, embryonic paradise that has attained the stillness of a tomb. Nothing is being destroyed, but nothing is truly being born. It is simply there, brilliant and breathless, beneath the weight of the world.
