The horizon does not recede; it ascends. This is the interior of the Great Mandate, a world-scale lattice where the distinction between a machine and a tree has been eroded by aeons of synthesis. It is a dry, cool, and blindingly bright cathedral of industry that breathes without air and drinks without water.

Here, the ground is not soil, but a densely woven mat of copper-veined peat and calcified circuitry. Everything has been built. Every square inch of the landscape is a product of deliberate assembly, yet it has grown over itself so many times that it possesses the weathered, gnarled dignity of an ancient forest. Imagine a city the size of a continent, then imagine that city turned into a garden, then imagine that garden dying of thirst for a thousand years while its mechanical heart continues to beat.

The light is a constant, shadowless glare, descending from a sky that is less an atmosphere and more a solid crystalline ceiling, miles above, through which a white sun pours its unfiltered fury. Despite the brightness, the air—if it can be called that—is perpetually cool, chilled by the massive heat-sinks of the deep-root processors. It feels like the inside of a refrigerated server room, crisp and sterile.

There is no water. The concept of liquid is anathema to the Mandate. Instead, the "vegetation"—colossal, rigid structures of fibrous carbon and silicon—sustains itself on a diet of fine mineral dust and ionised gas. The leaves are not soft; they are razor-thin sheets of amber and mica, translucent and stiff, clicking against one another in a windless environment. They don't sway; they vibrate at frequencies that suggest a distant, humming power grid.

The scale is oppressive. To walk through a "glade" is to walk between pillars of corrugated bark that are four hundred feet thick, their tops lost in a haze of golden light. These trunks are riddled with brass apertures and rhythmic, hissing valves. Occasionally, one might see a piston the size of a cathedral spire slowly plunging into the heart of a "tree," a silent, industrial pulse that keeps the sap—a viscous, non-aqueous ferrofluid—circulating through the world-grid.

The sounds are minimal and haunting. There is no birdsong, no rustle of wind, no splashing of streams. Instead, there is the *thrum*. It is a low-frequency vibration that one feels in the marrow of the bone rather than hears with the ear. Every few hours, a sound like a giant’s cello string snapping echoes across the plains—the sound of a tectonic-scale tension wire adjusting its grip on the landscape.

As you move deeper, reality begins to fray. The established laws of geometry, having been subjected to the Mandate’s industrial optimization for too long, have started to loop. You may walk in a straight line for miles, passing the same rhythmic arrangement of gear-flowers and obsidian shrubs, only to find that the light is now coming from beneath your feet. The paths do not merely lead forward; they lead into different states of density. In some sectors, the "forest" becomes so thick that the architecture overlaps, two buildings occupying the same physical space, their walls shimmering and phasing through one another like a mirage.

Fauna is nearly non-existent, and where it does appear, it is terrifyingly specialized. You might glimpse a "Threader"—a creature of spindly, telescopic limbs and glass eyes, scurrying silently along a high-tension cable to prune a malfunctioning sensor-leaf. They are not animals so much as mobile maintenance subroutines, devoid of hunger or fear.

This is a world of stagnant activity. It is constantly working, grinding, and growing, yet it never changes. It is an industrial ecosystem in a state of perfect, parched equilibrium. The erosion here is not caused by rain or wind, but by the slow, grinding friction of gear against fiber, the way a diamond-toothed vine slowly saws through a steel support beam over the course of a century. It is a landscape of beautiful, terrifying permanence, where the sun never sets, the water never falls, and the Great Mandate continues its silent, infinite calculation.
