The air here does not move, yet it screams. It is a world of the **Grand Tinnitus**, a perpetual, deafening roar that lacks a source. The sound is thick, a low-frequency tectonic grind layered with a high, crystalline shimmer that vibrates the marrow of the bones. It is not the sound of wind—for there is no wind—but rather the sound of the vacuum of space pressing against the thin, freezing membrane of the sky.

This is a landscape of impossible verticality, a region of **Zenith Shards**. Massive, blunt-nosed pillars of basalt and obsidian rise miles into the dim, violet-grey light. They do not taper like mountains; they are uniform in thickness, ancient and weathered until their surfaces are as smooth as river stones, despite their gargantuan scale. These pillars are separated by vast, terrifying voids of empty air—miles of nothingness where the only presence is the sound and the cold.

The temperature is a constant, biting chill that never fluctuates. It is cold enough to turn breath into immediate frost, but the frost does not fall. It hangs in the air, suspended by a reality that has forgotten how to let things drop.

**Water** exists here as a ghostly, parasitic passenger. It does not rain. Instead, moisture manifests as "Heavy Dew"—thick, gelatinous globs of water that cling to the undersides of floating debris or the vertical faces of the Shards. These pockets of dampness are the only reprieve from the desiccating cold. Occasionally, a river of liquid air or super-cooled mist will be seen spanning the gap between two pillars, perfectly horizontal and motionless, like a bridge of glass. It does not flow; it simply *is*.

**Vegetation** is a rare, desperate occurrence. There are no trees or grasses. Instead, the "Skin-Lichen" grows in the few places where the Heavy Dew gathers. It is a pale, translucent organism, more like a fungus than a plant, that pulses with a faint, internal rhythm. It leeches minerals directly from the obsidian stone. In the deep cracks of the Shards, one might find "Glass-Reeds"—brittle, transparent stalks that grow toward the loudest source of the sound, vibrating so intensely they appear blurred to the eye.

**The Ecology** is sparse to the point of extinction. The rare fauna are "Static-Moths"—large, wingless creatures with spindly, multi-jointed legs that allow them to cling to the vertical faces. They do not move for decades. They feed by extending long, capillary tongues into the suspended moisture globs. Because the world is **Never Changing**, these creatures do not seem to age or die in a traditional sense; they are more like biological statues, locked in a frozen equilibrium with the humming air.

**Reality** here has suffered a graceful, permanent breakdown. Gravity functions not as a downward pull, but as a series of localized, intersecting planes. In one pocket of space, "down" might be toward the face of a Shard; ten feet away, "down" might cease to exist entirely. Light behaves similarly. It does not travel in straight lines but curves in soft, predictable arcs, creating "Light-Lenses" in the empty air where one can see the back of their own head or a distorted view of a Shard that is actually fifty miles away. These anomalies are not chaotic; they are established, ancient, and fixed. They are as much a part of the terrain as the stone itself.

There are **Built** elements, though they are so old and so alien they are almost indistinguishable from the geology. These are "The Markers"—solitary, floating rings of a heavy, non-metallic substance that hang in the voids between Shards. They are not machines. They have no moving parts, no power source, and no apparent function. They are simply there, etched with faint, geometric grooves that have been smoothed by eons of exposure to the vibrating air. They suggest a presence that once understood this static, screaming height, but that presence has been gone for longer than the Shards have stood.

The **Scale** is overwhelming. Standing on the edge of a Shard, one looks out over a valley of nothingness that could swallow a hundred cities. The dim light makes distances hard to judge; a neighboring pillar might be a mile away or twenty, its grey bulk looming through the violet haze like the rib of a dead god.

Everything is in a state of **Frozen Equilibrium**. If you were to throw a stone into the void, it would not fall; it would travel in a straight line at a constant speed until it hit a Light-Lens and began to orbit a pocket of nothingness forever. There is no decay, no growth, no wind, and no silence. There is only the cold, the height, the damp stone, and the eternal, roaring hum of a universe that has reached its final, unmoving form.
