The space is a pressurized pocket located kilometers beneath a crust of desiccated silicate. It is not a cave formed by water, for water has no history here; it is a rift of recent violence, a jagged splintering of the deep rock that has not yet had its edges dulled by time. The walls are composed of raw, unweathered hematite and dark, glass-flecked basalt, rising in sharp, geometric shears that converge thirty meters overhead like the teeth of a trap.

It is a warm, airless vault, though "airless" is a misnomer. The volume is filled not with gas, but with a high-density suspension of ionized dust that behaves with the heavy fluidity of a deep-sea trench. This dust is the medium of existence here. It is bone-dry, a non-aqueous soup of minerals that tastes of copper and burnt hair.

In the center of this cathedral-sized rift sits the Engine.

It is a scattered, skeletal thing of rusted iron and brass, a work of industrial intent that seems to have grown into the rock rather than being built upon it. Massive, exposed pistons—each the size of a redwood trunk—plunge rhythmically into the floor, though there is no visible purpose to their labor. They do not pump fluid; they grind. The sound is a flat, unrelenting roar, a mechanical thunder that never fluctuates in pitch or tempo. Because the sound never changes, it has become a property of the geography, as fundamental as gravity. It is a loud, vibrating stasis.

Life has crowded into this thrumming pressure-cooker. The density of presences is suffocating. Clinging to the sharp edges of the basalt are the "vegetation": sparse, calcified fans of white mineral lace that do not photosynthesize, but rather crystallize out of the dust-heavy atmosphere. They are brittle and motionless, more like ornate scars than plants.

The fauna, however, are frantic. Shimmering, multi-legged organisms—half-flesh, half-clockwork—swarm in the narrow gaps between the engine parts and the jagged walls. They are the size of dogs but move with the jerky, high-frequency agitation of hummingbirds. They have no eyes; they perceive the world through the vibrations of the Engine. They feed on the friction heat and the static discharge of the pistons, their carapaces clashing together in a rhythmic clicking that is buried under the Engine’s roar. They are so numerous that they form a living carpet, a layer of twitching, metallic bodies that coat every horizontal surface.

The light is a dim, bruised purple, emanating from the friction-burns where the machine’s parts rub against the raw stone. There are no shadows, only varying degrees of murk.

Nothing here evolves, grows, or decays. The Engine does not rust further; the fauna do not age; the sharp rocks do not crumble. It is a moment of industrial violence frozen in a state of perfect, loud equilibrium. The heat is constant, a dry fever that radiates from the floor. A single artifact—a heavy, brass-bound ledger—lies on a shelf of rock near one of the pistons. Its pages are not paper, but thin sheets of hammered lead, and they are blank, for in this place, there is no news to report. The cycle is closed. The machine grinds, the dust swirls, and the creatures shiver in a permanent, unchanging ecstasy of noise.
