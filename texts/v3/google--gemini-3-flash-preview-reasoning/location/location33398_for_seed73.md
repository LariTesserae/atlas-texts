Here, the sky does not begin above the mountains; the mountains have outgrown the sky. 

You are standing on the Hemitropics, a series of vertical basalt massifs that rise so far into the upper atmosphere that the air should be thin and freezing. Instead, it is a pressurized, sweltering greenhouse. The heat is a physical weight, a constant 40 degrees Celsius that never breaks, held in place by a shimmering, invisible lattice of post-Newtonian force fields. 

The terrain is a study in verticality. These are not jagged, young peaks, but ancient, fluted columns of stone, weathered smooth by eons of weeping water. The erosion is "established"—the stone has long ago found the shape it wants to be. Huge, circular galleries have been bored through the mountains by the slow passage of water and time, creating wind-tunnels that hum with a deep, permanent bass note.

### The Water and the Atmosphere
Water is the dominant architect here. It does not rain in the traditional sense; rather, the air is so saturated that it simply collapses into liquid whenever it touches a surface. The rock faces are perpetually slick, draped in a "skin" of running water that flows upward as often as it flows down, guided by the localized gravity-warps of the Anchors. 

Everything is damp. The mosses underfoot are like sodden sponges, and the air smells of crushed ginger, ozone, and wet earth. A thick, pearlescent mist hangs in the gaps between the spires, moving with the agonizing slowness of a glacier. It takes hours for a single cloud-bank to drift across your field of vision, revealing and then concealing the terrifying depths below.

### The Vegetation and Density
The flora is aggressively lush. Because the sun is filtered through the atmospheric stabilization fields, the light is a constant, shadowless glow, like the inside of a soft-box. This has triggered a riot of growth. Ferns the size of sails hang from the cliffs, their fronds heavy with condensing moisture. Orchids with translucent, vein-streaked petals grow in clusters so dense they resemble mounds of raw meat. 

The density is claustrophobic yet vertical. There is no open ground; there is only the rock, the clinging roots, and the thickets of giant, waxy succulents that groan under their own weight. Everything is layered. A single square meter of cliff-face might host forty different species of moss, lichen, and micro-flora, all vibrating slightly in the ambient acoustic hum.

### The Tech and the Built
The "built" elements are subtle, almost invisible to the unpracticed eye. There are no cities, only "Nodes." You might find a single, seamless spire of a material that looks like frozen quicksilver—a stabilizer—embedded into the side of a cliff. These objects are silent, yet they are the reason you can breathe at this altitude. They manage the physics of the zone, stitching together gravity and pressure with a precision that makes modern engineering look like stone tools.

Occasionally, you find a walkway: a ribbon of solidified light that feels like warm glass underfoot, stretching across a three-kilometer drop to the next massif. These structures are old, weathered by the humid air, yet they never decay. They are "beyond" tech—they are woven into the fundamental constants of this valley.

### Fauna and Sound
The world is loud. It is a cacophony of biological and mechanical sound. Huge, six-winged insects, the size of hawks, drone through the mist with a sound like low-flying aircraft. Below them, in the dense thickets, "Boom-Toads" let out periodic, rhythmic pulses of sound that act as a sonar in the thick fog. 

The fauna is abundant and slow-moving. Giant, herbivorous crustaceans with shells like polished jade crawl vertically up the mossy walls, their many legs clicking against the stone. They move with extreme patience, taking an entire day to travel a hundred yards. In the air, there are drifters—gelatinous, gas-filled organisms that look like aerial jellyfish, trailing long, stinging tendrils through the humid air to catch micro-insects.

### The Rules of the World
Time feels different here. Because the dynamics are so slow—the clouds barely move, the heat never breaks, the light never dims—a sense of profound stasis takes hold. It is a high-energy environment trapped in a low-energy tempo. 

The fundamental rule of this landscape is **Vertical Saturation**. Everything—water, life, technology—is obsessed with the vertical axis. To move horizontally is a feat of engineering; to move vertically is the natural state of being. The heat and the noise create a fever-dream atmosphere, a place where the mountains seem to be breathing, slowly, in a long, wet, multi-thousand-year cycle. You are an insect crawling on the flank of a sleeping, sweating god.
