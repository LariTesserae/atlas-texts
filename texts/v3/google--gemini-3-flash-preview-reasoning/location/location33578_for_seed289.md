Deep within the primary crust, far beneath the reach of weather or tectonic shifting, exists the **Lumen-Cyst**. It is a space no larger than a cathedral’s pulpit, a secret geode-pocket embedded in a layer of raw, unweathered basalt. 

The first sensation is a blinding, white-out glare. Despite the depth, the chamber is saturated with a light so intense (2.8/3) it feels structural, as if the photons themselves are helping to hold up the ceiling. There is no source—no sun, no glowing moss—just a pervasive, sourceless brilliance reflecting off every facet of the raw geology.

The geology here is embryonic (0.2/3 erosion). The rocks are not rounded or smoothed by time; they are terrifyingly sharp, crystalline, and jagged, having just crystallized from the deep heat but never having felt the touch of wind or moving water. They look like frozen explosions of glass. This is a landscape under construction that has somehow been caught in a state of permanent stasis (0.0/3 dynamic). Nothing evolves here. Nothing decays. The jagged edges of a quartz-spur are as sharp today as they were ten thousand years ago, and they will remain so forever.

The air is cool and heavy, tasting of ozone and wet stone. Moisture (0.7/3) exists only as a thin, silver film of condensation that clings to the underside of the rock-spurs, never dripping, held in place by surface tension and the absolute lack of vibration or air movement. 

The space is impossibly packed (2.8/3 density). It is a "saturated" environment where there is no negative space. Between the needle-like rock formations, the floor and walls are crowded with "the occupants." These are the fauna (1.6/3)—thousands of translucent, chitinous organisms shaped like multi-faceted dice. They do not move. They are stacked one on top of the other, wedged into every crevice, filling the room until the chamber feels like a jar full of marbles. They don't breathe in a way that involves movement; they seem to exist in a state of suspended biological intensity.

Vegetation (1.4/3) is rare but startling. Occasional stalks of a pale, calcified weed poke through the gaps between the dice-creatures. These plants are not green; they are the color of bone, and their leaves are brittle, frozen sheets of mineral. They don't grow upward so much as they crystallize outward, adding to the density of the room.

Scattered throughout this micro-wilderness are the "Built" elements (0.9/3). These are not ruins, but rather singular, inexplicable artifacts. A single, perfectly smooth cube of a dark, unknown alloy sits wedged between two jagged rocks. A series of tiny, microscopic stairs have been carved into the side of a quartz pillar, leading nowhere. There is no technology here—no wires, no screens, no power—just these lonely, biological-adjacent markers of a presence that was once here, or perhaps is still here, hidden in the glare.

The sound is the most overwhelming feature (2.1/3). In this frozen, unchanging room, there is a constant, deafening roar. It is not the sound of wind or water, but a high-pitched, harmonic resonance—the sound of the light hitting the crystals, or perhaps the collective, unmoving hum of the thousands of dice-creatures. It is a static, unchanging scream that never wavers in volume or pitch.

The Lumen-Cyst is a paradox of a world. It is a place of "becoming" that has already finished. It is a crowded, loud, and blindingly bright closet buried in the dark silence of the earth. It is a landscape that defies the passage of time by being so tightly packed with matter and light that there is simply no room for change to occur. You do not visit the Lumen-Cyst; you are merely pressed against it, an intruder in a perfect, eternal jam of crystal and bone.
