The world is a succession of gargantuan, wind-polished mesas, each the size of a subcontinent, suspended above a floor that no eye has ever seen. These are the High Shelves. They are composed of a deep, resonant basalt that has been weathered into soft, flowing curves over eons of dry erosion. There is no sand here, only the solid, unyielding bone of the planet, smoothed until it reflects the blinding brilliance of the sky like a dark mirror.

The light is the dominant force. It is white, piercing, and relentless, originating from a sun that seems too large for the firmament. Because the air is thin and devoid of moisture, the light does not scatter; it falls in sharp, geometric slabs. Shadows are not dark patches, but crisp, cold voids. Despite the intensity of the radiance, the temperature remains stubbornly cool—a crisp, biting chill that preserves the landscape in a state of perpetual, frozen freshness.

Water is an alien concept here. There are no clouds, no ice, no dew. Instead, the "lushness" of the world manifests in the *Litho-mosses* and *Glass-ferns*. In the vast, shallow basins atop the mesas, vegetation grows with a ferocity that defies the aridity. These are not soft, aqueous plants, but complex structures of spun silica and mineral fiber. They appear as rolling meadows of deep violet, ochre, and iridescent silver. To the eye, they look like plush velvet or thick, succulent carpets; to the touch, they are dry, brittle, and chime like wind-chimes. They do not drink; they feed on the torrential light and the static electricity generated by the high-altitude winds.

Density is distributed in a lonely, rhythmic pattern. A traveler might walk for days across a barren, obsidian-slick plateau—a space so vast that the curvature of the world is visible in every direction—before encountering a "Grovetide." When life appears, it appears all at once: a square mile of dense, shoulder-high crystalline fronds, swaying in a silent, synchronized dance. Then, just as suddenly, the life ends, and the emptiness resumes.

The sound of the High Shelves is a profound, heavy silence. Because the air is so dry, it carries little resonance. The only noise is the occasional *tink-tink-tink* of the Glass-ferns brushing against one another, or the low, subsonic hum of the wind passing through the fluted canyons carved into the mesa edges. It is a sound like a distant organ note, held for a century.

Fauna is nearly non-existent, and what does exist is slow and strange. Occasionally, one might see a *Shimmer-wing*—a creature that is more kite than bird, a flat pane of translucent membrane that catches the thermal updrafts. They do not flap; they adjust their surface tension to rise or fall, drifting between the mesas like stray scraps of cellophane. They have no mouths, for there is nothing to eat but the light.

The world is active, but its pace is geological. The "weather" consists of massive thermal tides—vast volumes of cool air shifting from one side of the continent-mesa to the other, creating visible ripples in the Litho-mosses. There is a sense of a world in perfect, ancient balance. Nothing is being built; nothing is being destroyed. The erosion has already done its work, leaving behind a skeleton of absolute beauty. 

Standing on the edge of a Shelf, one looks out across a gap that could hold an entire mountain range, seeing only the distant, hazy silhouette of the next plateau. The scale is so immense that it humbles the very idea of travel. It is a cathedral of stone and light, dry as a sun-bleached bone, yet vibrating with the slow, crystalline pulse of a life that needs no water to bloom.
