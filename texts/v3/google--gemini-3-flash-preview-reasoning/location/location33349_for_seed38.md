This is the **Aethel-Veldt**, a continental plateau suspended so high in the atmosphere that the air below it is a bruised purple void, yet the Veldt itself is a heavy, sodden kingdom of emerald twilight. 

### The Substrate of Time
There is no stone here, and no soil in the geological sense. The ground is a compressed, miles-deep peat of "Marrow-Moss"—a biological tectonic plate that has settled into a state of terminal stability. To walk here is to walk upon the back of a sleeping god made of cellulose. The surface is soft, yielding with a rhythmic, hydraulic sigh that takes minutes to rebound. 

The landscape is not flat, but "eroded" in the way a very old sponge is eroded. There are "The Knees"—massive, rounded humps the size of cities, which were once perhaps mountains of wood, now worn down by eons of weeping condensation into smooth, moss-slicked domes. They are the oldest things in existence, having reached a final, unchangeable geometry.

### The Weeping Atmosphere
The air is a warm, suspended ocean. It does not rain in drops; rather, the atmosphere simply decides to become liquid at random intervals. A fine, heavy mist clings to everything, turning the light into a dim, diffused jade. Water is the primary architect here. It seeps from the "sky-canopy"—a ceiling of translucent, mile-wide leaves that catch the sun far above—and trickles down the gargantuan boles of the *World-Vines*. 

Every surface is "weeping." The vertical faces of the Knees are draped in "Lace-Falls," slow-moving sheets of water that move with the viscosity of nectar, silent and shimmering. There are no crashing waterfalls; there is only the sound of a billion simultaneous drips, a white noise so pervasive it becomes a form of silence.

### The Architecture of the Green
Vegetation is not a feature of the landscape; it *is* the landscape. There is no space that is not occupied by a living cell. The "trees" are not individuals but protrusions of a single, planet-wide root system. Some vines are so thick they have their own internal weather systems, with moisture condensing inside their hollow cores to form subterranean rivers.

In the dim light, the flora does not bother with showy flowers. Instead, it expresses itself through texture and bioluminescence. There are "Lung-Ferns" that expand and contract over the course of a day, filtering the heavy air. There are "Glass-Palms" that are entirely transparent, acting as fiber-optic cables to funnel what little light reaches the depths down into the root-mat.

### The Scattered Marks
The "built" elements are ghosts. They are the "Ribs"—great, calcified arches of a material that is neither bone nor wood, but something in between. They stand in clusters on the crests of the Knees, like the skeletons of cathedrals that were grown rather than built. There are no doors, no windows, no tools. These are the remains of "The Slow-Folders," a vanished presence that perhaps didn't live *in* the world, but guided its growth. 

Occasionally, one finds a "Well-Stone"—a perfectly circular depression lined with a non-reactive, pearl-like substance. These wells collect the purest condensation, and around them, the moss grows in intricate, geometric fractals that suggest a long-forgotten intent. There is no machinery, no metal, no spark of fire. The only "tech" is the sophisticated, slow-motion alchemy of the plants themselves.

### The Fauna of the Mists
Life here moves with the patience of a glacier. The most common creatures are the "Siphon-Whales," translucent, gas-filled bladders the size of houses that drift through the humid air on gossamer fins. They feed on the airborne spores, moving so slowly that moss grows on their undersides.

On the ground, there are "Thrum-Beetles"—heavy, soft-shelled grazers that vibrate at a frequency that keeps the water from settling on their carapaces. Their low hum is the only consistent sound in the Veldt, a deep, resonant bass note that vibrates in the marrow of one's bones. There are no predators, at least not in the traditional sense. There are only "Vines that Wait," which might take a decade to slowly coil around a stationary object.

### The Rules of the World
Time in the Aethel-Veldt is not measured in hours, but in the "Pulse." Every few months, the temperature rises by a few degrees, the mist thickens into a literal soup, and the entire continent of moss glows with a faint, rhythmic phosphorescence. This is the world breathing.

Gravity feels different here—not weaker, but muffled, as if the density of the life around you is pulling back. It is a place of absolute peace and terrifying permanence. Nothing has changed here for a million years, and nothing will change for a million more. The erosion is finished. The growth is total. The world is full.
