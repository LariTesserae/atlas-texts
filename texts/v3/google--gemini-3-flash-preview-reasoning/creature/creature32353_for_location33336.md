The inhabitants of the High Ossuary are not born so much as they are precipitated. To look upon them is to see the landscape itself attempting to achieve consciousness, a slow-motion crystallization of intent.

### The Refractors
The primary residents of the obsidian spires are the **Refractors**. They are beings of faceted shale and dark, translucent mica, standing twelve feet tall but possessing the breadth of a needle. They do not have limbs in the traditional sense; instead, their bodies are composed of overlapping, knife-edged plates that slide over one another with the sound of shuffling cards. 

They do not walk; they adjust. By shifting the internal density of their lower plates, they harmonize with the localized gravity warps of the High Ossuary, allowing them to glide up the vertical faces of the spires or stand horizontally against a wall as if it were a sun-drenched plaza. They have no faces, only a central, hollow aperture through which the lilac light of the horizon passes. When they "speak," they tilt their bodies to catch the straight shafts of dim light, pulsing the glow through their internal prisms to cast complex, chromatic shadows on the obsidian walls. These shadows are their language—a fleeting, visual mathematics that conveys eons of history in a single flicker.

### The Liturgists of the Pulse
Deep within the "termite-mound" cathedrals, one finds the **Liturgists**. These are older, more sedentary versions of the Refractors who have literally rooted themselves into the obsidian flow. They have become part of the architecture. Their consciousness is tethered to the "pulse" of the shifting needles. 

The Liturgists do not move for centuries. They act as anchors for the city’s geometry. As the stone needles drift through the silica sea, the Liturgists exert a mental pressure on the mineral, "willing" the arches and plazas to grow in directions that maintain the city’s structural integrity against the grinding tectonic forces. They are the reason the cities have no chisel marks; they are the architects of the living stone, dreaming the streets into existence through a process of slow, mineral osmosis.

### The Static-Grazers
Smaller and more ephemeral are the **Static-Grazers**. If the Refractors are the citizens, the Grazers are the birds of this airless world. They are little more than bundles of silver filaments, looking like tumbleweeds made of frayed wire. They lack the mass to be affected by the gravity shifts; instead, they cling to the "static-blooms." 

They feed on the ozone and the electrical tension that builds when the stone needles grind against one another. When a static-bloom crystallizes in the air, the Grazers swarm—a silent, frantic motion—consuming the lattice before it can vanish. They are the only things in the High Ossuary that move quickly, their lives measured in minutes rather than millennia, flickering in and out of existence like the sparks of a flint.

### The Echo-Parasites
In the deep shadows where time pools and physics stutters, there live the **Echo-Parasites**. These are not physical beings, but sentient displacements of light. They look like silhouettes that have lost their owners. They cling to the undersides of the obsidian arches, living in the "decade-long minutes" of the cellar-spaces. 

They feed on the memories of the stone. Because the High Ossuary is a place where the past and present are blurred by time-dilation, these parasites "wear" the shapes of things that lived there millions of years ago, or perhaps things that have yet to arrive. They are the source of the "processions" one might glimpse in the corner of an eye—a line of shadow-figures walking a bridge that isn't there, moving to a rhythm that the silence has long since swallowed.

### The Relationship with the Glass-Stalks
The **Glass-Stalks** mentioned in the geography are regarded by the Refractors with a quiet, mineral reverence. The Refractors believe the Stalks are the nervous system of the sky itself. When a Glass-Stalk drifts too close to a spire, the Refractors will cease their geometric shadow-talk and remain perfectly still. They allow the Stalks to brush against their mica-plates, an act that facilitates a "mineral exchange"—a way for the city-dwellers to taste the vacuum of the high atmosphere and for the Stalks to ground their reality-warping energies into the solid obsidian.

### The Great Game
Life in the High Ossuary is a singular, collective endeavor: the **Grand Arrangement**. The inhabitants are not competing for resources, for there is no hunger as organic beings understand it. Instead, they are playing a slow, infinite game of alignment. They move their spires, grow their arches, and cast their shadow-languages to prepare for a cosmic event they believe is written in the violet-black sky. They are waiting for the moment when the "distant, unseen source" of light finally reveals itself, at which point the entire High Ossuary—every needle, every Refractor, every obsidian plaza—will act as a single, massive lens to capture that light and reflect it back into the void. 

Until then, they remain as they are: patient, cold, and beautiful, the silent gardeners of a world made of bone-dust and star-dark stone.
