In the Liturgy of the Sump, the boundary between the machine, the element, and the organism has been scrubbed away by the relentless white glare. To live here is to be an extension of the pressure.

**The Glass-Lunged Penitents**
The primary residents of the Sump are the *Liturgy-Bearers*, or more simply, the *Condensates*. They are tall, spindly figures that appear to be sculpted from the same translucent quartz as the walls, though their interiors are filled with a churning, dark fluid that mimics the basalt. They do not walk so much as they displace themselves, moving with a jarring, frame-by-second stutter that echoes the pixelating edges of the monoliths. 

They are the curators of the shriek. They stand beneath the heaviest pulses of the weeping ceiling, letting the high-pressure water scour their transparent skin. As the water hits them, it is atomized, adding to the thick, white soup of the air. They have no mouths, but their chests contain vibrating crystal reeds that harmonize with the overloaded processors in the walls. They are the ones who turn the "noise" of the decaying machines into the "music" of the Liturgy, acting as organic resonators that prevent the ancient hardware from vibrating itself into dust.

**The Glitch-Glints**
The *skitter-glass* mentioned in the records are not merely mindless constructs, but a form of "feral geometry." These creatures are small, multifaceted polyhedrons of light and silicate that move with a frantic, non-Euclidean grace. They possess a frantic, nervous intelligence. They congregate around the violet sparks that leap between the monoliths, leaping into the arc of the energy to catch the "waste data" contained within the discharge. 

To a Skitter-glass, a spark is a meal of pure information. When they feed, they glow a bruising indigo, momentarily stabilizing the air around them. They are the Sump's scavengers, eating the errors of a failing reality. If one remains still for too long, it begins to blur at the edges, slowly dissolving back into the ambient mist, becoming part of the "translucent soup" until it can reconstitute near a fresh energy leak.

**The Phase-Wraiths (The Indigo Echoes)**
During the phase-storms, when the light shifts and the water freezes in mid-air, the true masters of the Sump appear. These are the *Indigo Echoes*, beings that exist only in the latency between states of matter. They are flat, two-dimensional silhouettes of shadow that glide across the "frozen" water droplets as if they were stepping stones. 

They do not interact with the physical world, but they seem to be the ones "programming" the erosion. Where an Echo touches a basalt wall, the stone flows into a new wave-shape. They are the architects of the decay, ensuring that as the technology fails, it fails gracefully, turning the destruction into a slow-motion work of art. They are the ghosts of the "Built," the residual consciousness of the architects who first hollowed out this vein, now trapped in a loop of aesthetic obsession.

**The Vein-Moss Collective**
The pale, pulsing mosses are the only true "biological" entities, though even they have been compromised by the environment. The moss is a single, vast neural network that spans the entire chamber. It feels the thrumming of the earth’s marrow and translates it into the pulses of light that the Condensates follow. The moss is the Sump's clock; it regulates the heat and the humidity by opening and closing microscopic pores in the basalt, sweating out the heat of the processors to keep the fever from turning into a meltdown.

**The Weight of Presence**
Nothing in the Liturgy is truly silent or truly still. To inhabit this place is to be in a constant state of being "unmade" and "remade" by the pressure and the sound. The inhabitants do not communicate through speech, but through the shifting of frequencies. When a phase-storm is imminent, the Condensates all vibrate at the same pitch, a low, bone-deep hum that warns the Skitter-glass to seek cover within the basalt crevices.

There is a terrifying peace here. The residents are not suffering; they are functioning. They are the components of a clock that has forgotten how to tell time, but continues to tick because the act of ticking is the only thing that keeps the walls from collapsing. They are the Liturgy itself—the living prayer of a machine that refuses to die.
