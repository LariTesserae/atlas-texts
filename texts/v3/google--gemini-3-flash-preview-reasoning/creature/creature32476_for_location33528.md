The inhabitants of the Sun-Cistern are not merely residents; they are extensions of the glare, life-forms that have traded the frantic pulse of biology for the slow, structural integrity of minerals. 

### The Aureates (The Glare-Walkers)
The ones who left the grinding stones and the obsidian shards are the **Aureates**. They are tall, agonizingly thin beings, standing three meters high but weighing no more than a child. Their bodies are composed of a translucent, semi-crystalline lattice that mimics the "frozen lightning" of the succulents. They do not have skin so much as a pressurized membrane that contains a slow-moving, luminous ichor. 

They do not walk; they shift. An Aureate may spend three days mid-stride, their elongated limbs trembling with the effort of pushing through the "weight" of the 2.9 light. To them, the light is not a medium through which they move, but a thick, golden syrup that must be waded through. 

They have no mouths, for they do not eat. They "inhale" the scouring brightness through the pores of their crystalline limbs. The grinding stones are used to pulverize the bleached limestone into a fine, reactive dust, which they apply to their joints to prevent the "stiffness of time"—a condition where an inhabitant becomes so still they literally fuse to the floor of the cistern, becoming a permanent part of the geology.

Their eyes are not lenses, but deep, light-drinking pits of obsidian, identical to the shards they drive into the silt. They use these shards to calculate the "thickness" of the day. When the light reaches its maximum density, the Aureates gather in the center, perfectly still, their bodies vibrating at a frequency that matches the low hum of the wind, entering a state of collective, waking hibernation.

### The Mercury-Slicks
The flat-bodied lizards—the **Mercury-Slicks**—are the scavengers of the cistern's rare moisture. While the Aureates ignore the sapphire-colored water pockets, the Slicks treat them as sacred sites. 

Their skin is a marvel of evolutionary optics; it is perfectly reflective. By shimmering their scales, they can bounce the 2.9 light away from their internal organs, effectively creating a small, mobile shadow beneath their own bellies. This "shadow-pocket" is the only place in the cistern where true darkness exists. They use these tiny patches of shade to cool the sapphire water beads before drinking them, as the water, if left to the sun, becomes hot enough to scald their delicate interiors.

They move with a sudden, terrifying fluidness that defies the cistern’s stasis. One moment, a Slick is a statue of polished opal; the next, it is a blur of silver, a literal ripple in the air. They communicate through flashes of light, tilting their bodies to signal to one another across the void, a silent language of mirrors.

### The Vitreous Insects (The Glass-Wings)
The six-winged insects are the cistern's only source of sound, though it is a sound felt rather than heard. These are the **Vitreous**, creatures whose wings are so thin they are essentially panes of living glass. They do not fly by flapping; they ride the thermal pulses of the "sleeping animal" heat. 

The Vitreous are the gardeners of the crystalline succulents. They carry microscopic grains of salt and mineral from the sweating walls to the plants, acting as a slow-motion pollination system. When they gather in clusters over the damp spots, their wings catch the light and refract it, creating tiny, dancing rainbows that are the only breaks in the monochromatic white of the bowl. 

### The Presence of the "Visitors"
The Aureates are not indigenous to the scrubland above. They are pilgrims who descended into the bowl eons ago and forgot the way out, or perhaps found the surface’s shadows too violent to bear. 

There is a profound sense of *patience* in their culture. They are waiting for the light to become so heavy that it finally collapses the cistern further into the earth. They believe that the 2.9 radiance is a physical door, and if they can remain still enough, and reflective enough, the light will eventually permeate them entirely, turning them from things that *live* in the light to things that *are* the light.

In this place, death is not a rot, but a final crystallization. When an inhabitant ceases to move, they don't decay; they simply stop being translucent. They turn opaque, white, and matte, eventually eroding into the same pale silt that holds the memory of their last, thousand-year-old footprint.
