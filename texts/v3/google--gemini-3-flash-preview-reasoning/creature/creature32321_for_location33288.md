The inhabitants of this vertical, unfinished realm are as much a part of the machinery as they are a part of the stone. They are not merely living *on* the cliffs; they are the nervous system of the factories, the midwives to the emerging rock.

### The Lithe-Walkers
The primary residents—if they can be called that—are the **Lithe-Walkers**, beings who have long since surrendered the concept of a horizontal life. They are terrifyingly tall and unnervingly thin, their limbs elongated by generations of reaching across chasms. Their skin is the color of the slate sky, a matte grey that feels like cured leather, toughened to resist the abrasive grit of the basalt and the biting chill of the ozone.

They do not walk so much as they flow across the industrial lattice. Their hands and feet have developed elongated, hook-like digits, with palms that secrete a tacky, resinous sweat, allowing them to cling to wet obsidian or rusted iron even when gravity decides to pull sideways. They move with the same agonizing slowness as the great brass fans, a rhythmic, deliberate pace that suggests they are tuned to a different frequency of time. To a Lithe-Walker, a fall is not a sudden catastrophe but a slow, drifting descent through the gloom, a voyage they might navigate for hours before catching a lower catwalk.

Their faces are minimal—small, recessed nostrils to filter the peat-scented mist and large, lidless eyes that glow with a faint, internal bioluminescence, tuned to see the "impossible colors" cast by the flickering bulbs. They do not speak in words; they communicate through low-frequency hums that mimic the thrum of the factories, vibrating their chests to send signals through the metal pipes they lean against.

### The Hinge-Keepers
Deep within the soot-stained factories dwell the **Hinge-Keepers**. If the Lithe-Walkers are the climbers, the Keepers are the anchors. They are shorter, squat, and heavily armored in plates of scavenged brass and obsidian. They are the ones who bolt the factories to the rising rock. Their work is eternal; as the mountains extrude and the geometry of the world shifts, they must constantly loosen and retighten the massive iron bolts, ensuring the city doesn't snap like a twig as the basalt grows beneath it.

The Keepers have a symbiotic relationship with the violet-veined moss. They pack it under their armor for warmth, and in return, their sweat—rich in metallic minerals from the factory dust—feeds the plant. They are the only ones who seem to understand the fraying logic of the place. They carry heavy, leaden weights on chains, not for defense, but to constantly test the local direction of gravity, adjusting their gait as the "down" pull shifts ten degrees to the left or begins to spiral.

### The Scintillating Sails
Higher up, in the reaches where the slate sky meets the factory spires, live the **Sail-Moths**. These are not insects in any traditional sense, but drifting, ethereal filter-feeders. Their wings, translucent and vast, act as living solar panels, soaking up the erratic, non-natural spectrum of the industrial lights. 

They are the shepherds of the mist. As they drift, their amber-glowing abdomens pulse in time with the mountains’ tectonic groans. It is believed they feed on the ozone itself, their bodies crackling with tiny, harmless arcs of electricity. When a Sail-Moth dies, it doesn't fall; it becomes a static fixture, its body locking in mid-air as a permanent, glowing buoy around which the Lithe-Walkers build new catwalks.

### The Lingerers (The Detached)
The most unsettling inhabitants are not biological at all. They are the **Lingerers**—the shadows that have successfully detached from their owners. Because reality here is unfinished and the logic is thin, a shadow that stays detached for more than a few minutes begins to develop a two-dimensional agency. 

These dark, flat silhouettes glide along the obsidian faces and the sides of the iron pipes. They have no mass, but they have a presence. They mimic the movements of the living, often trailing a Lithe-Walker for miles, a silent, dark echo of a body that moved past that spot an hour ago. The inhabitants ignore them, treating them like a weather pattern or a trick of the light, even when a Lingerer tries to "wear" a pool of water clinging to the underside of a bridge.

### The Heartbeat
Everything here—from the smallest six-legged scavenger to the massive, soot-caked Hinge-Keeper—exists in a state of **The Patient Watch**. They are waiting for the world to finish being born. They do not build for a future, but for the *immediate present*, a constant adjustment to a geology that is still cooling, still emerging. They are the custodians of a skeletal reality, living in the gaps between the ribs of a god that hasn't yet taken its first breath.
