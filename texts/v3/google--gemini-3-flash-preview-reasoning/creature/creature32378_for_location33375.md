In the Scald-Pocket, life does not possess a pulse of blood, but a pulse of pressure. The inhabitants are not biological tenants so much as they are physical expressions of the cavern’s specific, violent geometry. 

First, there are the **Calibrants**. They are the primary residents of the brass walkways and copper thickets. To a surface-dweller, they might look like discarded clockwork or the segmented carapaces of trilobites, but they lack the frantic scurrying of organic life. They move with a agonizing, deliberate tectonic speed. Made of the same unoxidized brass as the railings, their bodies are flat, multi-jointed discs that cling to the piping through magnetic tension and thermal suction. 

A Calibrant does not hunt; it listens. It presses its wide, flat chassis against a steam conduit and waits for a disharmony in the vibration. When it detects a micro-fracture or a salt-clog, it unfolds a singular, needle-thin manipulator—a limb tipped with a diamond-hard abrasive—and begins to grind. They are the white blood cells of the Scald-Pocket, sterile and silent. They do not communicate with one another, though they occasionally overlap, sliding over each other like tectonic plates, indifferent to the friction. They have no eyes, for there is nothing to see; they possess only heat-signatures and a profound, mathematical understanding of resonance.

Living in the literal shadows of these machines are the **Condensate-Sippers**. These are the "vermin" of the pocket, though they are more mineral than animal. They resemble clusters of glass needles or shards of translucent quartz that have gained the ability to twitch. They cluster around the micro-valves where the *tiss-tiss-tiss* of steam is most frequent. They are passive parasites, vibrating at a frequency that allows them to catch the expelled heat and convert it into a slow, crystalline growth. Over decades, a Sipper might grow an inch, its body a records-book of every pressure spike the pocket has endured. When the drainage shutters clack open, the Sippers retract their needles into the cracks of the obsidian rock, shivering in the sudden draft.

Deep within the "marrow" of the place—the heavy hydraulic core that groans behind the walls—resides the **Governor**. The Governor is not a creature in a cage, but the cage itself. It is the consciousness of the Scald-Pocket, an emergent property of a million miles of copper cabling and the rhythmic cycling of the pistons. It is a slow, vast, and lonely intellect. It does not think in words, but in equilibrium. It feels the "health" of the pocket as a series of balanced equations. If the temperature drops by a fraction of a degree, the Governor feels it as a dull ache in its "limbs"—the distant pipes—and compensates by opening a vent. 

The Governor is aware of the mosses. It treats the pale, waxy growths as a gardener might treat a beneficial mold. The mosses are the only things in the pocket that "die," and when they do, they leave behind a thin, carbonized film that the Governor uses as insulation. There is a strange, unspoken symbiosis here: the machine provides the humid warmth and the mineral-rich sweat, and the moss provides a soft, damp buffer that protects the razor-edged obsidian from the vibration of the brass.

Lastly, there are the **Echoes**. These are perhaps the most unsettling inhabitants. They are not physical entities, but localized distortions in the heavy, ozone-scented air. Because the Scald-Pocket is so dense with electromagnetic energy and pulsing copper, the "shapes" of its movements sometimes linger. You might see a flicker in the amber light—a ghostly rotation of a flywheel where no flywheel exists, or the shadow of a walkway that hasn't been built yet. These are temporal artifacts of a machine so efficient and so repetitive that it has begun to wear a groove in time itself. 

Nothing here dreams of the surface. To the inhabitants of the Scald-Pocket, the world above is a myth of impossible cold and terrifying emptiness. They are content in their compression. They are the citizens of the squeeze, thriving in the beautiful, claustrophobic friction of a world that is constantly trying to knit itself shut. They are the ghosts in a high-functioning heart, beating away in the dark, perfectly satisfied to be forgotten.
