To inhabit the Cold Atrium is not to reside within a space, but to be a single cell within a gargantuan, slow-motion lung. The inhabitants are not tenants; they are components, each one a specialized instrument in a symphony of survival.

### The Vein-Keepers (The Pulse-Wardens)
The primary "citizens" are the plants themselves, but they are not uniform. The most vital are the **Pulse-Wardens**, the massive, bulbous junctions where the root-fiber "streets" meet. These are the hearts of the Atrium. They are translucent, melon-sized nodes that throb with a deep, visceral violet light. Inside, one can see the violent churning of nutrient-slurry—a thick, greenish-gold bile that carries the mountain’s minerals to the highest serrated leaves. 

The Wardens "speak" through pressure. When the mountain wind screams through the cleft, the Wardens contract, tightening the structural fibers of the walls to prevent the Atrium from shattering. They are the administrators of tension, deciding which corridor receives the warmth of the sap and which must be allowed to go brittle and dark to save the whole.

### The Alabaster Pruners
The spindle-legged mites mentioned as mere dots are actually the **Alabaster Pruners**, the Atrium’s immune system and maintenance crew. They are the size of a human hand, though they weigh no more than a dried leaf. Their bodies are made of a white, chitinous substance that mimics the frost needles perfectly. 

They move with a terrifying, jerky precision, skittering across the vertical faces of the pitcher-structures. Their purpose is singular: to detect "stillness." In a world of aggressive growth, anything that stops moving is dying. The Pruners find the necrotic spots—the yellowing edge of a leaf or a frozen vein—and systematically dissolve it with acidic secretions before the rot can spread. They are the reason the Atrium always looks "young." They eat the old age of the city.

### The Velvet Shiverers
In the hollows of the obsidian-dark balconies live the **Velvet Shiverers**—the thick-furred moths. They have evolved beyond the need for flight, as the air is too thin to support their heavy, insulated bodies. Instead, they function as living thermal batteries. 

They huddle in clusters of hundreds within the "buildings," their wings vibrating at a frequency so high it creates a localized pocket of warmth. This heat is not for their own comfort; it is a service. They shelter the **Spore-Hearts**, the delicate, glowing blue nodules that serve as the Atrium’s "seeds." The moths are the gardeners of the next generation, trading their warmth for the sweet, intoxicating nectar that seeps from the shelf-fungi. When a Shiverer dies, it does not fall; it is slowly absorbed by the floor, its fur becoming part of the moss-carpet.

### The Mist-Drinkers
Floating in the "chilled steam" of the upper canopy are the **Mist-Drinkers**. These are not animals, nor strictly plants, but drifting, jellyfish-like bladders of gossamer-thin cellulose. They have no limbs, only long, trailing filaments that catch the silver mist. 

They are the harvesters of the sky. As they soak up the humidity, they grow heavy and sink, eventually docking with the waxy gutters of the "fountains." They plug themselves into the central bladders, discharging their filtered water like biological batteries, then shrivel into light, airy husks to be carried back up by the rising drafts of the mountain cleft.

### The Ghost-Hum
There is a final inhabitant, one that has no body: the **Resonance**. The "humming pressure" of the air is not just a geographical quirk; it is a biological byproduct. The billions of fine hairs on the leaves act as aeolian harps. As the thin air passes over them, it creates a low, constant thrum that keeps the sap from stagnating. 

This sound is the collective consciousness of the Atrium. It is a slow, rhythmic intelligence that remembers the shape of the mountain before the plants arrived. If you were to stand in the center of a root-fiber street, you would feel the Resonance in your marrow—a feeling that you are not being watched, but that you are being *processed*. You are a foreign body in a perfect machine, and the Atrium is already deciding whether you are a nutrient to be absorbed or a necrotic cell to be pruned.
