The inhabitants of Still-Drip are not merely residents; they are the final, living iteration of the mineral itself. To a surface-dweller, they would be invisible, appearing only as thin, translucent flakes of mica or slightly thicker shims of wet slate wedged between the masonry. 

They are the **Vibratory Hives**, a species of lithic-biological hybrids that have surrendered the frantic energy of "life" for the rhythmic endurance of the stone.

### Anatomy of the Still
A citizen of Still-Drip is roughly the size of a fingernail clipping and no thicker than a human hair. Their bodies are composed of flexible, silken membranes stretched over a skeletal frame of needle-fine calcium carbonate. They possess no eyes, for light in the fissure is a trick of the moisture, not a source of information. Instead, their entire dorsal surface is a single, hyper-sensitive tympanum—a drumhead skin that translates the city’s deafening percussion into a high-resolution map of their surroundings.

They do not walk; they resonate. By shifting the tension in their silica-strand muscles, they can harmonize with the specific frequency of a nearby resonator. This causes them to slide across the slick mineral film of the walls, moving with the liquid grace of a bead of mercury. When they stop, they become indistinguishable from the wet schist, their bodies cooling to the exact micro-degree of the rock beneath them.

### The Architects of Percussion
The "unseen architects" mentioned in the city’s lore are these creatures, working in a time-scale that borders on the geological. A single "building" project—the placement of a hollowed-out bird bone found in the silt or a specifically shaped flake of shale—may take a generation to complete. 

They do not use tools. They use the water. By standing in the path of a weeping vein for decades, a group of inhabitants can subtly divert the flow, using their own bodies to erode a new channel or to deposit calcified salts in a specific shape. They are the sculptors of the Groaning Spires, not by chipping away with force, but by guiding the mountain’s slow, liquid blood to pool and harden in precise geometries.

### The Diet of Stasis
They do not hunt the salt-grain arthropods that scuttle across the bridges; to the inhabitants, those creatures move with a chaotic, ugly speed that is beneath notice. Instead, the people of Still-Drip feed through osmosis. They press their ventral membranes against the "oily sheen" of the mineral-rich slime, absorbing the raw chemical energy of the earth’s slow decay. They are consumers of time and pressure, requiring so little caloric intake that a single drop of nutrient-rich seepage can sustain a family for a century.

### The Culture of the Metronome
Their society is governed by the **Great Rhythms**. In a world of permanent darkness, time is not measured by the sun, but by the polyrhythmic drumming of the water. They communicate by striking their own resonant bodies against the slate, adding their own brief, sharp "clicks" to the city’s thunderous white noise. 

There are no leaders, only **Tuners**. These are the eldest inhabitants, individuals who have grown so large and heavy with mineral deposits that they can no longer move. They wedge themselves into the primary resonators—the largest shale plates—and act as living dampers. By adjusting their grip on the stone, they subtly alter the pitch and timing of the city’s heartbeat, signaling shifts in barometric pressure or the arrival of a new mineral vein.

### The End of All Things
Nothing is born in Still-Drip because there is no biological imperative for growth. Reproduction is a rare, seismic event. When a pocket of air is pushed through the fissure by the heaving of the earth, it carries with it microscopic spores of fungal logic. The inhabitants catch these, incorporating the new information into their lattice-work bodies.

When an inhabitant "dies," they do not rot. They simply stop moving. The water, ever-present and ever-patient, begins to deposit calcite over their still form. Within a few hundred years, the individual is entirely subsumed, becoming a new brick in a wall or a reinforcement in a bridge. The City of Still-Drip is built from the ossified remains of its own history. 

They are a people who have won the war against entropy. They have reached the "final form"—a state where life and stone are so perfectly entwined that the difference no longer matters. They wait, vibrating in the dark, listening to the mountain scream, perfectly content to be the echo.
