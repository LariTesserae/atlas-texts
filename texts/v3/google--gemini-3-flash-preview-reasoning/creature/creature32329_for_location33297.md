In this horizontal world, life has abandoned the frantic pursuit of the sun. There is no need to reach upward when the warmth is everywhere—held in the heavy air and radiated from the weeping stone. The inhabitants are not competitors, but residents of a long, slow afternoon.

### The Chorus of the Silt (The Bellows-Kin)
The low, rhythmic thrumming that defines the atmosphere originates from the **Silt-Bellows**. These are soft-bodied, translucent creatures the size of a fist that dwell in the interface between the wet clay and the shallow pools. They are little more than a pair of complex, pulsating lungs wrapped in a thin, iridescent membrane. 

They do not hunt; they filter the mineral-rich "sweat" of the earth through their skin. The thrumming is their primary metabolic function—a collective vibration that prevents the silt around them from settling into a solid, suffocating tomb. To look at a pool is to see a thousand tiny, rhythmic ripples, each marking the position of a Bellows-Kin submerged in the mud. Their drone is the sound of the planet breathing through a million tiny throats.

### The Lithodipterans (The Glass-Crackers)
The sharp, metallic clicks that echo for miles belong to the **Lithodipterans**, the only creatures that move with any semblance of intent. They resemble broad, flat beetles, their carapaces forged from the same dark basalt and copper-colored minerals as the landscape. They are virtually indistinguishable from the stones they rest upon, often remaining motionless for weeks at a time.

Their "click" is a specialized survival mechanism. As the shallow pools evaporate under the golden sun, a thin, brittle crust of mineral salt forms over the water, sealing off the oxygen and nutrients. The Lithodipteran uses a heavy, hammer-like appendage on its forebody to strike the crust, shattering the crystal sheet to keep the water "open." The sound is a dinner bell for the landscape; when a click rings out, the slow-moving life nearby begins a days-long migration toward the newly broken water.

### The Mantle-Treaders
The "clouds" that drift across the mud are the **Mantle-Treaders**. These are the giants of the terminal epoch—beasts that can span ten meters in width but rise no higher than a man’s knee. Their bodies are vast, undulating sheets of leathery, slate-gray skin, textured like the grain of ancient wood. They have no limbs, only a muscular underside that ripples with the glacial patience of a tectonic plate.

The Mantle-Treaders are the gardeners of the horizontalities. As they flow over the basalt swells, they scrape away the excess algae and mineral deposits, keeping the stone "pores" open so the earth can continue to weep. They move so slowly that the copper-colored succulents often take root in the folds of their skin, riding the behemoths for decades. To a Treader, a century is a short walk; a millennium is a life well-spent. 

### The Copper-Vein Succulents
The vegetation is less a plant and more a biological crystal. The **Copper-Vein Succulents** do not have roots in the traditional sense; instead, they have acidic filaments that etch themselves into the basalt. Their leaves are thick, cold to the touch, and filled with a viscous, purple fluid that stores both heat and minerals. 

They grow in radial patterns, forming perfect circles that look like bruised ornaments placed upon the stone. They do not flower. Instead, every fifty years, they release a single, heavy seed that doesn't fly on the wind (for there is no wind) but simply rolls down the gentle swell of a hummock until it finds a new crack to call home.

### The Apex of Stillness
There is no predation here in the way we understand it. There is no chase, no struggle. When a Mantle-Treader finally stops moving—after perhaps ten thousand years—it does not rot. It is simply reclaimed. The Bellows-Kin migrate into its softening folds; the Lithodipterans break the mineral shells that form over its eyes; the Copper-Vein plants lace their filaments through its cooling leather.

In this place, the ultimate inhabitant is the **Atmosphere** itself. The vapor is so thick, so saturated with the biological hum of the plain, that it feels sentient. It carries the history of the epoch in its scent—the smell of warm salt, ancient stone, and the slow, inevitable peace of a world that has nowhere left to go, and is perfectly content to stay there.
