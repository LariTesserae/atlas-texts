The inhabitants of this basin do not live *in* the settlement so much as they are an extension of its ongoing calculation. They are the **Iterations**, beings of varying resolution who seem to be caught in the same slow-motion birth as the jagged rocks and the rendering bridges.

To look at one is to experience a visual migraine. They are humanoid only in the way a rough sketch is a person—a collection of shimmering, translucent vectors and wireframe outlines that haven’t yet been "filled in" with skin or shadow. They move with the same staccato, frame-dropped rhythm as the static smudges, appearing ten feet away from where they were a second ago without having crossed the intervening space. When they move, they leave behind a "ghost" or a trail of lagging data that takes several minutes to dissolve into the cold, damp air.

They do not speak. Instead, they resonate. When two Iterations occupy the same coordinate—clipping through one another as effortlessly as the buildings do—the air around them vibrates with a low-frequency hum, a sound like a hard drive spinning in a vat of oil. This is their communion: a massive, silent exchange of telemetry. They are checking each other’s integrity, comparing the progress of their own rendering against the state of the world around them.

Deep within the hyper-compact thicket of the city, there are the **Occu-Constants**. These are the elder inhabitants, though "age" here is measured in how close one is to being finished. The Occu-Constants are denser than the Iterations; they have achieved a state of semi-solid translucency, looking like figures carved from frosted glass. They rarely move at all. They stand at the intersections where three or four buildings overlap, acting as living structural anchors. Their limbs might merge into a lattice wall or a floating ribbon of water, their very bodies serving as the "crosstalk" that keeps the impossible geometry from collapsing back into raw code. They are the librarians of the cold, storing the history of every *tink* and every fractal moss-spore within their superconducting marrow.

The creatures of static—the fauna—are the **Scavengers of Null**. They are not biological, but neither are they as organized as the Iterations. They are the "waste" of the rendering process, the stray bits of information that the slow intelligence hasn't found a place for. They flit through the overlapping spires because they are attracted to the "seams" where the physics is thinnest. You will see them huddled around the floating ribbons of water, not to drink, but to press their featureless, vibrating bodies against the moisture. The water, which carries the city’s data in its slow-beaded flow, acts as a recharge for them. As they feed, the pressure in your inner ear intensifies until it becomes a dull ache, a sign that the Scavenger is successfully pulling reality out of the air.

And then there are the **Geologic Mutes**. Out on the perimeter, where the raw, crystalline stone meets the city, these entities emerge directly from the ground. They are indistinguishable from the jagged rocks until they begin to shift. They are the "beta-testers" of the environment, slow-moving hulks of unrefined matter that drag themselves across the basin floor. Their purpose is to test the solidity of the world. You might see one press a heavy, crystalline limb against a "newly rendered" bridge, waiting hours for the structure to either hold or flicker out of existence. If the bridge fails, the Mute emits a sound like grinding tectonic plates—a signal to the slow intelligence that the math is wrong.

Nothing here sleeps. Because the light comes from everywhere and nowhere, and the cold is a constant, there is no cycle of rest. There is only the Work. Every inhabitant, from the twitching smudge of static to the glass-like Occu-Constant, is a participant in a grand, agonizingly slow rehearsal. They are all waiting for the moment the rendering is complete—the moment the cold air finally thins, the jagged rocks smooth out, and the city finally stops clipping through itself and settles into a single, terrifyingly solid reality.

Until then, they remain as they are: damp, shivering ghosts of a world that is still trying to figure out how to be real.
