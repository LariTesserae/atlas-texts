The inhabitants of the Calyx-Spires do not consider themselves "citizens" so much as they are **The Attuned**. To live here is to be a component in a slow-motion engine, a soft-bodied gear synchronized to the hydraulic rhythm of the sap.

### The Sap-Chirurgeons (The Attuned)
The people are long-limbed and move with a deliberate, languid grace, as if they are perpetually wading through the thick, mint-colored light. Their skin possesses a translucent, pearlescent quality, often tinged with the same pale green as the canopy above, a result of generations spent under the chlorophyll-filtered sun.

They do not wear clothes of woven fiber, but of **Lignin-Silk**—a fabric harvested from the inner cambium of the Spires, cured in resin until it is as flexible as leather but as durable as copper. Their movements are silent; they wear soft-soled boots tipped with hooks for navigating the verticality of the bark-walls.

A resident of the Spires rarely speaks above a whisper. Their language is one of "Resonance." They communicate through a complex system of rhythmic tapping on the brass pipes that lace the city. By striking a pipe with a copper ring, a dweller can send a vibration through the entire Spire’s vascular system, a message that can be "felt" by anyone touching a railing or floor-plate blocks away.

### The Sphygnomancers (The Pressure-Keepers)
The elite among them are the **Sphygnomancers**, the pulse-readers. They are the ones who reside closest to the "Heart-Valves" near the base of each Spire. They do not look for mechanical failure with their eyes; they press their ears to the bark and listen for the "vocalizations" of the plant. 

A Sphygnomancer can tell by the pitch of a steam-hiss whether a Spire is thirsty or if the pressure in the northern bracket-fungi is reaching a critical, explosive threshold. They are healers who use wrenches and grease instead of herbs and bandages. When a Spire "shudders," the Sphygnomancers are the first to react, adjusting massive wooden pendulums to redistribute the weight before the botanical monolith cracks under its own immense gravity.

### The Ecology of the Mites
The **Clockwork Mites** are more than just pests or pets; they are the primary janitors of the district’s soul. They are roughly the size of a human hand, with iridescent carapaces that shift from emerald to bronze. While they are biological creatures, the Attuned have "augmented" them over centuries. Tiny brass gears are fused to their carapaces, and their mandibles have been tipped with tempered steel.

There is a profound, wordless bond between the Mites and the Attuned. A resident might sit for hours on a fungal street, allowing a Mite to crawl over their lignin-silk sleeves, cleaning off the soot from the steam-vents. In return, the people provide the Mites with "Mineral-Licks"—small cubes of refined copper and salt that keep the beetles' chitinous shells strong.

### The Mist-Waiters
Life revolves around the **Ghost Mist**. When the first tendrils of white vapor begin to curl into the gulfs between the towers, the "active silence" of the district breaks. This is the only time the inhabitants emerge in numbers.

They gather on the bracket-fungi balconies, not to socialize, but to perform the **Rite of the Cup**. As the copper condensation-catchers click open, the inhabitants stand perfectly still, letting the mist coat their skin and clothes. They believe the mist carries the "memory" of the other Spires—those lost in the airy gulfs beyond the known nine. They breathe it in deeply, a communal inhalation that powers their lungs just as it powers the steam-turbines.

### The Philosophy of the Slow
There is no concept of "progress" in the Calyx-Spires. To the inhabitants, the world is already perfect; it only needs to be maintained. They do not build new structures; they "encourage" the Spires to grow into the shapes they need, a process that takes decades. A new room is not hammered together; it is budded.

Death, for the Attuned, is a literal homecoming. When a resident's pulse finally slows to a stop, they are not buried in the ground—for there is no ground. Instead, they are placed within a "Graft-Cradle" in the Spire's deep interior. Over the course of a year, the bark slowly envelopes them, drawing their minerals back into the vascular system. 

The people do not believe they die; they simply become the pressure. They become the hum in the pipes, the shudder in the wood, and the steady, industrial heartbeat that keeps the Nine Spires standing against the emptiness.
