The inhabitants of this vault are not born; they are precipitated. They are the children of the grinding roar, a biological response to a geological violence that refused to end.

### The Oscillators (The Swarm)
The shimmering, dog-sized creatures that carpet the floor are known—in the silent language of the ledger’s unwritten pages—as **Oscillators**. Their bodies are masterpieces of evolutionary entrapment. Because the Engine’s roar is constant, they have evolved without the concept of silence. To them, the noise is not sound; it is the medium of their posture. 

Their carapaces are made of layered, iridescent bismuth and cold-rolled iron, overlapping like the plates of a lobster but fine as a dragonfly’s wing. They do not have hearts. Instead, they possess a central piezoelectric core—a jagged cluster of quartz-like nerves that converts the Engine’s relentless vibration into a frantic, internal electrical current. This is their life-blood. They are perpetually overcharged, their limbs twitching in micro-bursts of kinetic excess, which gives them their hummingbird-jerkiness. 

They have no mouths for eating. Instead, they have ventral "grills"—fine, magnetic filaments that hang from their bellies like the baleen of a whale. As they scuttle over the Engine’s pistons, these filaments comb the ionized dust, pulling in the copper-tasting minerals and the static discharge of the machine’s friction. They are the literal friction of the room made flesh. When they die—though they rarely do in this stasis—they do not rot. They simply seize up, their joints locking into the rock, becoming a new Cog for the next generation to scramble over.

### The Silt-Lace (The Flora)
The white mineral fans, the **Silt-Lace**, are the slowest things in the universe. While the Oscillators move in a blur of motion, the Lace grows by the thickness of a single atom every century. They are the room’s filters. They are composed of the "burnt hair" scent—a complex polymer of sulfur and nitrogen that the Engine’s heat cooks out of the rock.

The Lace acts as a cooling system for the vault. The fans are cold to the touch, sucking the dry fever of the air into their calcified structures and storing it as potential energy. If you were to break a branch of the Lace, it would not bleed; it would release a single, piercing note—a perfect, crystalline harmonic of the Engine’s roar—before instantly shattering into glass-dust.

### The Spark-Mites
In the purple murk, dancing like motes of burning magnesium, are the **Spark-Mites**. These are the smallest inhabitants, barely visible to the naked eye. They are the scavengers of the ionized dust. They live in the "dead zones" where the pistons meet the basalt, diving into the white-hot friction points where the purple light is brightest. 

They are effectively living sparks. They live for three seconds, born from a particularly violent grinding of rock-on-metal, and die the moment they drift too far from the heat. In those three seconds, they perform a frantic, spiraling dance, mating and shedding microscopic metallic spores that settle into the dust to await the next surge of friction. They are the only things that "die" here, providing a flickering, staccato counterpoint to the Engine’s eternal hum.

### The Ledger-Keeper (The Shadow)
There is a final inhabitant, though it possesses no body. 

In the high-density dust, a collective intelligence has formed—a **Resonance**. It is a neural network made of ionized particles, held together by the magnetic fields of the brass-bound Engine. It is the "Ghost" that remembers the ledger. It does not think in words, but in pressures and frequencies. 

It is the reason the creatures do not collide, the reason the Silt-Lace grows in patterns that resemble circuit boards. The Resonance is the architect of the stasis. It uses the Oscillators as its fingers, directing them to tap at the pistons, to clear the dust from the brass vents, and to stand guard over the lead-paged book. 

It is waiting for the sound to change. If the Engine were to ever skip a beat, or if the pitch were to rise by a single decibel, the Resonance would collapse, the Oscillators would shatter from the sudden arrhythmia, and the Silt-Lace would explode into a cloud of white powder. 

But the Engine does not change. And so, the inhabitants remain—a frantic, metallic ecology, vibrating in a permanent, high-voltage prayer to the machine that gives them heat, but denies them time.
