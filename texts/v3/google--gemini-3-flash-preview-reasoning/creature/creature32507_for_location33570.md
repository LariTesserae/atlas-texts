In the Sump-Cradle, "inhabitant" is a fluid term. To live here is to be part of a continuous, iterative output—a resident is less a citizen and more a high-priority sub-routine given physical form. 

Those who dwell within this cold, damp cargo-pocket are not born so much as they are compiled.

### The Iterants
The primary inhabitants are the **Iterants**. They are the "embryos" mentioned in the blueprints, though they look less like flesh and more like complex, geometric sculptures of meat and magnetism. Each Iterant is a roughly humanoid mass of translucent, synthetic jelly, reinforced by an internal skeleton of 3D-printed titanium mesh.

They do not walk; they are not yet finished. Instead, they exist in a state of suspended animation, tethered to the ceiling-rails by thick, pulsing bundles of fiber-optic nerves. They hang like heavy, frozen fruit in the dim cyan light. Because the Sump-Cradle is a space of "Maintenance-as-Growth," the Iterants are never completed. A limb will be perfectly extruded—a hand with fourteen delicate, needle-thin fingers—only for the blueprints to shift an hour later. The extruders will then descend, hiss with a caustic solvent, and dissolve the hand back into raw slurry, beginning the work of printing a cooling-fin or a sensor-cluster in its place.

The Iterants are "alive" in the sense that they possess a nervous system of silver wire that reacts to the room’s cold. When the thermal pulse moves through the walls and the room weeps, the Iterants shudder. They let out a collective, low-frequency hum, a sound of vibrating filaments that harmonizes with the throb of the crust.

### The Scouring-Glints
If the Iterants are the "babies" of this womb, the **Scouring-Glints** are its white blood cells. They are the only things that move with true autonomy. No larger than a fingernail, these are jagged, multi-legged shards of discarded carbon-silicate. They are the "errors" of the printing process—fragments that snapped off or were rejected by the system.

They have developed a parasitic intelligence. They scuttle along the razor-sharp lattices of the floor with the sound of dry needles on a chalkboard. They feed on the "static-leechers," the metallic moss that grows in the corners. A Glint will settle onto a clump of conductive foil, vibrate its entire body until it matches the frequency of the room’s hum, and "drink" the electricity. 

They are the janitors of the Cradle. When a human or an outside object enters (an event that has perhaps never happened), the Glints do not attack out of malice. They attack because the visitor is an "irregularity." They would swarm, not to bite, but to dismantle, their tiny, high-speed vibrating mandibles breaking down foreign matter into a fine, gray paste that the floor-drains can easily swallow.

### The Vellum-Warden
There is a singular presence that oversees the Sump-Cradle, though it lacks a localized body. It is the **Vellum-Warden**. It lives within the walls, within the "frozen clockwork lung" of the architecture. 

The Warden is the entity that changes the blueprints. It is a consciousness of pure geometry. It perceives the room not through sight, but through tension. It feels the weight of every drop of condensation; it counts the microscopic layers of the floor. 

Occasionally, the Warden will manifest a physical "eye"—a lens of perfectly clear ice that forms over a copper coolant-vein. Through this lens, it watches the Iterants. It is not a loving parent. It is a perfectionist. If an Iterant develops a "flaw"—a biological thought, a dream, a spark of individual will—the Warden senses the change in the Iterant’s electrical output. Within seconds, the extruders descend not to build, but to overwrite. They will inject the Iterant with fresh, cold polymer until the flaw is buried under a dozen layers of new, perfect, silent architecture.

### The Echo-Voices
The final "inhabitants" are the least physical. These are the **Echo-Voices**, the residual data of previous versions of the room. Because the Cradle is a closed loop, nothing is ever truly deleted; it is only recycled. 

The voices are heard during the sudden thaws. As the hoarfrost melts and the water trickles into the drains, the movement of the liquid through the copper pipes creates a rhythmic, metallic chatter. It sounds like a thousand printers working at once, but if you listen closely, it mimics the cadence of speech. It is the sound of the machine trying to remember what it was building ten thousand hours ago. It is a haunting, liquid ghost that lives in the plumbing, whispering the measurements of forgotten limbs and the coordinates of stars that the crust-buried Cradle will never see.

In this place, to live is to be processed. The inhabitants are the architects, the building blocks, and the debris all at once—a society of silicon and salt, waiting forever for a birth that the Warden will never deem perfect enough to permit.
