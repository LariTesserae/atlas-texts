The inhabitants of the Glass-Reach Plateau do not live *on* the landscape so much as they are an extension of its frantic, weeping chemistry. They are the **Pale-Handed**, a species whose existence is a breathless sprint against the erosion of their own world.

### The Pale-Handed (The Architects of Flux)
To look at one is to see a creature composed of frosted glass and tension. They possess the elongated, twitching grace of a lemur, but their skin is a translucent, chitinous membrane that reveals the rhythmic pulsing of pale blue ichor beneath. They have six limbs: two powerful legs tipped with hooked, obsidian-hard talons for gripping the wet basalt, and four highly articulated arms ending in needle-thin fingers.

These fingers are their primary sensory organs. In a world of blinding, white-out light, their eyes have recessed into tiny, vestigial pits shielded by nictitating membranes the color of milk. They do not see shapes; they see *vibrations*. They feel the "thrum-thrum" of the mountain as a map. 

They are biologically incapable of stillness. A Pale-Handed individual at rest would likely crystallize and shatter in the brutal cold. Consequently, their metabolism is a furnace. They feed on the iron-grey lichen, scraping it from the rock with specialized, rasping tongues, and they filter the saturated air through gill-slits in their chests, extracting the minerals trapped in the ice-mist. They do not sleep in the way we understand it; they enter a "standing trance" for minutes at a time, their four hands continuing to weave silk even as their consciousness dims.

### The Mountain-Spiders (The Silk-Yielders)
The "silk" that forms Casket-Town is not a product of the builders, but of the **Litho-Arachnids** that dwell within the porous depths of the basalt. These are not predators, but massive, slow-moving scavengers that resemble living boulders. They possess no eyes and no legs, moving instead like gastropods on a single, muscular foot. 

As they move through the internal, water-logged veins of the plateau, they secrete a thick, vitreous resin to stabilize the crumbling rock behind them. The Pale-Handed "farm" this resin by inserting bone-flutes into the weeping pores of the mountain, drawing the silk out in long, shimmering ribbons before it hardens. It is a symbiotic relationship of theft: the builders provide the spiders with scraps of lichen and mineral-salt, and in return, they harvest the building blocks of their vertical hive.

### The Glass-Gnat Swarms
In the spaces between the pods, where the light is most intense, the air is thick with **Glass-Gnats**. These are not insects in the biological sense, but self-replicating mineral shards buoyed by the "kinetic frenzy" of the plateau’s atmosphere. They follow the Pale-Handed, attracted to the heat of their bodies. They act as a secondary skin, settling on the silk bridges and the builders' backs, their crystalline bodies refracting the light so intensely that the Pale-Handed often appear to be wreathed in white fire. 

### The Shadow-Strays
The most unsettling inhabitants are the **Shadow-Strays**. Because the physics of light is warped by the saturated air, the shadows cast by the Pale-Handed often linger for minutes after the creature has climbed away. Over centuries, some of these "lagging images" have seemingly achieved a haunting, semi-permanent autonomy. 

They are thin, two-dimensional stains of darkness that slide across the basalt, independent of any light source. They do not interact with the physical world, but they follow the rhythmic *thrum-thrum* of the mountain. The Pale-Handed ignore them entirely, but a visitor might find themselves followed by a dozen silent, dark silhouettes that mimic their movements with a five-second delay—a ghost-trail of one’s own presence in a land that refuses to let anything remain static.

### The Culture of the Moment
There is no history in Casket-Town, only the *current*. The Pale-Handed have no names, no art that isn't functional, and no concept of "permanent home." A pod is a temporary lung, a place to huddle against the cold for an hour before the mountain’s weeping forces its dismantling. 

They communicate through a complex language of percussion. By snapping their chitinous finger-joints against the slate-reinforced walls of their pods, they send signals through the silk bridges. The city is a giant instrument, a web of clicks, taps, and resonant hums that tell the hive where the rock is thinning or where the water-hair is thickest.

In the center of this "anthill-bazaar," life is a beautiful, terrifying desperation. To live on the Glass-Reach is to be a weaver in a world that is constantly being unspun, a silent, multi-limbed dancer on a shelf of black glass, standing in a blinding light that never reveals the truth, only the motion.
