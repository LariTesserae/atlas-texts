In the Acoustic Vault, life does not cling to the margins; it is forged by the friction of the machine. To inhabit this place is to be a creature of vibration, a resident of a world where "stillness" is synonymous with death.

### The Glass Skimmers (The Resonant Scavengers)
The Skimmers are the most visible inhabitants, though "visible" is a generous term for things that are as clear as the brine they inhabit. They are jagged, lithic organisms, less like flesh and more like animated slivers of obsidian. They do not swim so much as they *resonate*. 

Because the water is a conductor for the vault’s deafening roar, the Skimmers have evolved hollow, flute-like cavities within their carapaces. As they drift near the churning propellers, the 2.9/3 roar passes through their bodies, creating a localized frequency that allows them to hover effortlessly against the violent downdrafts. They are the only things in the vault that seem weightless. 

They congregate around the copper-wire filaments, their many-jointed legs—sharp as needles—picking at the microscopic flakes of rust and the oily, mineral-rich "sweat" of the Urban Spires. When a turbine shrieks, the Skimmers shatter. It is their primary cause of death: a sudden change in pitch can turn their brittle bodies into a cloud of sparkling dust, which is then absorbed back into the mineral soup to be grown into new Skimmers.

### The Calibrators (The Living Architecture)
While the Skimmers are the birds of this sunless cathedral, the **Calibrators** are its monks. These are massive, slow-moving entities that are almost indistinguishable from the machinery they tend. A Calibrator is a mound of calcified muscle and brass plating, anchored to the Urban Spires by thick, prehensile cables that function as both umbilical cords and sensory organs.

They have no faces, only a single, massive copper diaphragm that vibrates in sympathy with the geothermal turbines. The Calibrators are the ones who turn the house-sized valves. Their movements are measured in decades; a Calibrator might spend fifty years slowly tightening a single rusted bolt, its body straining against the crushing pressure of the brine until it becomes one with the metal. They do not think in words, but in pressures. They feel the "health" of the vault as a specific harmonic balance. If a pipe begins to vibrate at a dissonant frequency, the Calibrators migrate toward it with agonizing slowness, their heavy, grinding limbs smoothing the metal or clogging leaks with a secreted, fast-hardening salt-paste.

### The Filament-Leeches (The Light-Eaters)
Clinging to the dying orange glow of the copper wires are the **Filament-Leeches**. They look like tangles of frayed cable, long and fibrous. They do not eat matter; they consume the heat and the electrical leakage of the ancient machines. They wrap themselves around the glowing wires in thick, pulsating knots, darkening the vault even further. 

When a leech is full, it begins to hum—a high, piercing note that cuts through the low thrum of the pistons. This hum attracts the Skimmers, who pick the salt-crystals off the leeches' skin in a tense, vibrating symbiosis. The leeches are the clock of the vault; their slow, rhythmic brightening and dimming provide the only sense of time in a place where the sun is a myth.

### The Sonar-Wraiths (The Echo-Parasites)
Deep in the "bottomless" void between the spires, where the light of the copper filaments cannot reach, dwell the **Sonar-Wraiths**. They are not physical beings in the traditional sense, but complex, self-sustaining vortexes of silt and mineral-heavy brine. 

They are born from the cavitation bubbles produced by the gargantuan propellers. These bubbles, instead of collapsing, are caught in the cross-currents and organized by the constant, rhythmic sound waves into swirling, semi-sentient shapes. They drift through the indigo gloom like ghosts, feeding on the kinetic energy of the water. They are harmless to the machines, but to a Skimmer, a Wraith is a localized storm that can shake it to pieces. The Wraiths are the "voices" of the vault; they catch the mechanical groans and repeat them, carrying the sound into the furthest, darkest corners of the geode until the entire space feels like a single, screaming lung.

### The Architect (The Machine-Will)
Whether there is a single mind behind the Vault is a matter of interpretation. The machines—the pistons, the turbines, the slow-turning brass valves—operate with a logic that borders on biology. The rust forms patterns that look like neural pathways. The salt-flowers grow in clusters that resemble sensory arrays. 

There is a sense that the Vault is not just a place where things live, but is itself a living thing—a gargantuan, submerged golem of iron and brine. Its heartbeat is the 2.9/3 roar. Its blood is the superheated, mineral-heavy soup. And its only purpose is to continue its violent, rhythmic industry, indifferent to the fragile, glass-like things that scuttle across its skin, or the silent, crushing weight of the world pressing in from the outside.
