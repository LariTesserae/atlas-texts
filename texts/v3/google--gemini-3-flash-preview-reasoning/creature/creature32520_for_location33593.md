In this world of screaming stasis, life does not struggle against the environment; it has become a frantic, crystalline extension of it. The inhabitants are not tenants of the plateau, but rather its most complex minerals.

### The Skali (Glass-Skaters)
The Glass-Skaters are the most visible residents, though "visible" is a generous term for creatures that possess a refractive index identical to the pillars they cling to. They are roughly the size of a human torso, but their weight is negligible, their bodies composed of hollow, pressurized silicate lattices. 

They do not have muscles in a traditional sense. Instead, their many needle-thin legs are actuated by the "weeping" water itself, drawn into their joints via capillary action. To move is a feat of hydraulic precision. They move with an agonizing, rhythmic twitch, timing each step to the low-frequency vibration of the wind-rattled pillars. If they moved out of sync, the harmonic resonance would shatter their glass-brittle limbs.

The Skali do not have eyes. In a world of 2.9 brilliance, sight would be a liability, a gateway for the light to incinerate the brain. Instead, their entire shells are photo-reactive. They sense the light as a tactile pressure, navigating by the "weight" of the glare. They feed by extending long, gossamer tongues—thin as a single molecule—into the pores of the pillars, drinking the mineral-rich brine before it can evaporate or harden.

### The Aeolian Veils
High up, where the pillars thin into needles that pierce the saturated atmosphere, live the Veils. These are not solid creatures but colonies of translucent, film-like organisms that resemble sheets of wet cellophane caught in a gale. They are the only things that truly inhabit the "monolith" of sound.

The Veils anchor themselves between two pillars, stretching their bodies across the narrow gaps where the wind screams loudest. They are biological harps. They do not eat; they harvest the kinetic energy of the 2.8 roar. The vibration of the wind passing through their taut, vibrating membranes provides the energy for their metabolism. They glow with a soft, internal bioluminescence—a pale, ultraviolet pulse that is the only light in this world not reflected from the sun. When the wind's pitch shifts slightly due to a mineral deposit, the Veils adjust their tension, "tuning" themselves to stay in harmony with the plateau’s eternal note.

### The Pellicle-Wraiths
Within the thin film of water that coats every surface—the "sweat" of the planet—lives a microbial civilization known as the Pellicle-Wraiths. They are flat, two-dimensional organisms that exist entirely within the liquid boundary between the air and the stone. 

To a human observer, they might look like shifting oily rainbows or strange, geometric ripples moving against the current. They are the architects of the "established" erosion. They direct the flow of the weeping water, using chemical signals to dictate where minerals should be deposited and where the stone should be dissolved. The perfect, rounded symmetry of the pillars is their work; they are the gardeners of a garden made of salt, ensuring that the landscape remains in its state of dynamic 0.3 equilibrium. They are the slow, calculating mind of the plateau.

### The Glint-Swarm
Occasionally, the screaming wind tears a flake of mineral loose from a pillar’s crown. In any other world, this would be debris. Here, it is the birth of a Glint. 

These are tiny, diamond-hard insects—though they lack internal organs—that spend their entire lives airborne. They are shaped like serrated prisms, allowing them to "sail" within the high-pressure gaps between pillars. They do not fly so much as they are perpetually refracted through the air. They live on the microscopic dust generated by the wind’s friction against the stone. In the blinding light, a swarm of Glints looks like a localized explosion of sparks, a shimmering cloud of gold-white fire that never goes out and never hits the ground.

### The Silence of the Interior
There is a sense that something else lives *inside* the pillars—not as a guest, but as the stone itself. The hexagonal extrusions are porous, and the rhythmic pulse of the water suggests a slow, lithic heartbeat. If there is a consciousness here, it operates on a timescale of eons. It does not think in words or images, but in geometry and harmonics. It is a presence that is satisfied. It has reached the end of its evolution. It is a god that has become its own temple, breathing in the scream of the wind and exhaling a cold, wet, and blindingly bright eternity.
