In the Vertical Sump-Garden No. 14, life does not simply inhabit the machine; it has been recruited by it. Over the eons of slow, wet decay, the line between a biological cell and a circuit trace has blurred into a singular, wet ecology of survival. 

To look into the Sump is to see a hierarchy of dwellers, each calibrated to the rhythm of the thrumming engine.

### The Polishers (The Micro-Fauna)
Living in the very pores of the rusted brass are the **Galvanic Mites**. These are tiny, translucent crustaceans no larger than a grain of sand, with shells that flicker with a faint, static charge. They are the base of the Sump’s economy. They do not eat organic matter; they eat friction. They cluster in the joints of the great gears and the hinges of the buckling catwalks, consuming the heat and the microscopic filings of metal produced by the machine’s movement. 

Where the Mites congregate, the machine runs smoothly. They are an accidental maintenance crew, their tiny, vibrating bodies acting as a living lubricant. Without them, the Sump would have seized and turned cold a thousand years ago.

### The Rhythmic Schooling (The Chrome-Darters)
The middle-waters are dominated by the **Chrome-Darters**, fish that look like quicksilver needles. They lack eyes—eyes are useless in the shifting, silt-heavy amber haze—and instead possess long, sensitive whiskers that sense the "magnetic music" of the shaft. 

They are obsessed with the *thrum*. When the great piston fires miles below, the Darters align themselves in perfect, geometric grids, their bodies vibrating in sympathy with the shockwave. They feed on the "Rust-Snow"—the flakes of oxidized iron that drift down from the upper reaches. Their digestive tracts are essentially small blast furnaces, smelting the rust back into the heavy plates of their own shimmering skin.

### The Ambushers (The Cable-Stranglers)
In the shadows of the massive, house-sized gears, where the amber light of the lamps cannot reach, live the **Cable-Stranglers**. These are the apex predators of the Sump, looking less like animals and more like lengths of frayed, high-voltage wiring. 

A Strangler will anchor its tail to a steam pipe and let its long, multi-stranded body drift into the current. To a passing Darter, the creature looks like just another piece of industrial debris—a loose copper lead swaying in the brine. When prey touches a strand, the Strangler delivers a massive electrolytic jolt, parching the victim’s nervous system before drawing it into a "mouth" that is essentially a grinding, biological turbine.

### The Great Lung (The Siphon-Bellows)
Floating in the vast, open center of the shaft are the **Siphon-Bellows**. These are gargantuan, jelly-like organisms, thirty feet across, resembling translucent, rib-caged umbrellas. They are the Sump’s filtration system. 

They move by slowly contracting their bells, mimicking the action of a manual pump. As they drift vertically—up to the cooler heights by day, down to the boiling depths by night—they pull gallons of brine through their porous bodies. They filter out the heavy toxins and the oily ribbons leaking from the lamps, concentrating the heat into a glowing, internal core. At night, a Siphon-Bellows looks like a floating lantern, a pulsing orange heart drifting through the bruised purple dark.

### The Caretaker (The Ossified Custodian)
There is only one of its kind: the **Custodian**. It is not a creature of flesh, nor is it purely a machine. It is a derelict repair-drone that has been "colonized" by the green moss and the fiber-optic vines. 

The Custodian moves with a slow, agonizing shriek of metal, hauling its rusted, multi-limbed frame along the iron ribs of the shaft. It no longer knows its original purpose. Instead, it has become a gardener. It uses its delicate, pincer-like manipulators to prune the gear-kelp away from vital valves and to transplant the pulsing flower-bulbs to areas where the amber light is brightest. 

The Custodian is covered in a thick carpet of emerald moss; it is a walking hill of velvet and brass. The fish do not fear it, and the Strangler eels give it a wide berth. It is the silent priest of the Sump, wandering the miles of the shaft, ensuring that the breakdown of the world remains beautiful.

### The Symbiotic Law
Everything here knows the Law of the Sump: **The Machine is the Mother.** If a Darter grows too large, it is heavy enough to trigger a pressure plate that releases a burst of fresh nutrients. If the moss grows too thick, it insulates the pipes, keeping the water at the precise, womb-like temperature required for the bulbs to bloom. 

It is a world of glorious, functional decay. The dwellers of No. 14 do not wish to escape the shaft, for the void outside is cold and silent. Here, there is heat, there is rhythm, and there is the endless, metallic taste of a home that is slowly, beautifully falling apart.
