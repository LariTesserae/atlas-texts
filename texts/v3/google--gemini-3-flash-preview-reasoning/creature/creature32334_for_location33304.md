The inhabitants of the Amniotic Heights are not merely residents of the terrain; they are its nervous system. To live here is to be a creature of the "Long-Hold," shaped by a world that wants to crush you and a ground that wants to flay you.

### The Inhabitants: The Cirri-Weavers
The dominant species of the Heights, the **Cirri**, are masterpieces of high-pressure biology. They are vertically oriented, standing nearly seven feet tall, yet they possess a startling, fluid lack of rigidity. Their bodies are composed of a dense, non-Newtonian musculature—soft to the touch when drifting, but turning hard as oak the moment the gravity attempts to compress their organs.

Their skin is the color of a clouded opal, translucent enough to reveal the slow, rhythmic pulse of their bioluminescent circulatory system—a soft, golden heat that glows through their chests. They have no hair; instead, their heads and shoulders are draped in a "mantle" of sensitive, ribbon-like filaments. These filaments are their primary sensory organs, tasting the salinity of the water and feeling the tectonic thrums of the plateau long before the tremors arrive.

The Cirri have four arms. The upper pair is long and powerful, used for "swimming" through the viscous soup, while the lower pair is smaller, equipped with dozens of needle-thin, multi-jointed fingers. These are the tools of the Knot. A Cirrus can tie a complex hitch in a kelp-vine with their eyes closed, feeling the tension of the fiber as if it were an extension of their own nerves.

### The Culture of the Stabilizers
Because the obsidian peaks are constantly sawing through the city’s tethers, the Cirri are a people of **Perpetual Maintenance**. There is no concept of "finished" in their language. To build a house is to begin a conversation with the mountain that will last for generations.

Society is organized into "Cords." A Cord is a group of families responsible for a specific cluster of pods and the tethers that hold them. They move through the vertical streets in a slow, coordinated ballet, checking the fraying edges of the kelp-ropes, applying thick, resinous pastes to the obsidian to blunt its edges, and re-weaving the basket-walls.

They do not speak with sound as we know it. In the thick, heavy water, vocal cords would be inefficient. Instead, they "Thrum." By vibrating their dense chest cavities, they produce low-frequency pulses that can be felt in the marrow of one’s bones. A greeting is a gentle, resonant purr; a warning is a sharp, percussive crack that ripples through the water like a physical blow.

### The Fauna: The Translucent Ghosts
Sharing the Woven Green are the **Glass-Gliders**, creatures that look like a cross between a manta ray and a lace curtain. They are entirely transparent, save for their eyes, which are large, silver orbs designed to catch the faintest glimmer of bioluminescence. They drift between the ship-sail fronds, cleaning the algae from the city’s tethers in exchange for the scraps of seed-pod husks discarded by the Cirri.

Deeper in the crevices of the Raw Maw live the **Lithos-Crabs**. These are the scavengers of the heights, creatures with shells made of the same obsidian as the ground. They are the only things "hard" enough to ignore the razor-edges of the plateau. The Cirri hunt them rarely, using their shells to create the few "hard" tools they possess—scrapers, needles, and the occasional ceremonial blade.

### The Silent Elders: The Mineralized
The most revered members of the Cirri do not live in the pods. When a Weaver reaches a great age and their internal light begins to dim, they perform the **Final Tethering**. They descend to the Raw Maw, find a jagged spire of basalt, and wrap themselves in a cocoon of mineral-rich kelp.

Over decades, the slow accretion of minerals from the saline soup turns them into living statues. These "Elders" become part of the mountain itself, their bodies hardening into beautiful, calcified formations. The city is often tethered to these ancestors; the living are literally held aloft by the strength of those who came before. The Cirri visit these sites to press their filaments against the stone, "listening" to the faint, geologic heartbeat of the Elders, seeking wisdom in the slow, rhythmic vibrations of the crust.

### The Philosophy of the Weighted
To an outsider, the Amniotic Heights would feel like a prison of pressure and darkness. To the Cirri, it is the only place where one can truly be "held." They pity the idea of a world with "thin" air, where objects fall and break, and where the sky is an empty void. 

They believe they live in the womb of the world. Each knot they tie, each glow-plant they tend, and each thrum they share is a way of saying: *We are not drifting away. We are here. We are held.* They are a civilization that has mastered the art of staying put in a universe that is always trying to pull things apart.
