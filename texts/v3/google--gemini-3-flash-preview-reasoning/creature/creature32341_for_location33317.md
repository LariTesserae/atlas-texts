The inhabitants of the **Cradle of the Glass-Weep** are not merely residents; they are the literal nervous system of the basin, a symbiotic caste of entities that blur the line between biological life and the high-energy stasis of the monoliths.

### The Lithic Curators (The Weepers)
The primary inhabitants are a species of slender, multi-jointed beings known as the **Vitreous Tenders**. Standing nearly three meters tall but weighing no more than a child, their bodies are composed of a flexible, semi-liquid silicone membrane that mimics the appearance of the surrounding obsidian. They do not have feet; their lower limbs terminate in precise, needle-like points of volcanic glass that allow them to navigate the razor-sharp terrain without ever truly touching the "surface." They move with a terrifying, liquid grace, threading their limbs through the air as if they are climbing invisible ladders.

Their heads are faceless, smooth ovals of polished basalt that act as resonance chambers. They do not see in the traditional sense; they perceive the world through the subsonic vibrations of the monoliths. To a Tender, the entire Cradle is a symphony of standing waves. They spend their hours leaning against the monoliths, their chests vibrating in sympathetic resonance, "listening" to the structural integrity of the atmosphere. 

### The Filament-Weavers
If the Tenders are the listeners, the **Aurelian Arachnids** are the makers. These are not true spiders, but fist-sized, metallic cephalopods that cling to the silver filaments stretched between the peaks. They have no digestive tracts; they are powered by the magnetic flux of the basin. 

Their primary function is the maintenance of the liquid spheres. You will see them skittering along the silver lines, their many legs vibrating so fast they become a blur of copper light. They "knit" the water, using localized gravity-warping secretions to pull the moisture from the air and pack it into the floating globes. When a globe becomes too heavy, a Weaver will strike a specific note on the filament, causing the sphere to rotate and discharge its contents into the waiting plants below.

### The Moss-Hearts
Deep within the neon-green moss carpet, there exists a subterranean collective known as the **Ozone-Breathers**. They are rarely seen in their entirety, appearing only as rhythmic bulges beneath the velvet greenery. They are the chemical processors of the Cradle. As they inhale the heavy, wet air through the moss, they strip away the excess ozone and minerals, exhaling a scent of crushed cloves that acts as a nutrient-rich pheromone for the glass-ferns. 

They are the reason the "soil" remains a gelatinous silt rather than turning to dust. They process the volcanic glass, grinding it internally and secreting a lubricating gel that allows the Tenders to move silently across the sharp basalt.

### The Skimmer-Pilots
The **Skimmers**, those copper-manta rays, are not solitary animals but vessels. If you look closely at the "hammered copper" surface of a Skimmer, you will see a series of shallow, liquid-filled depressions. Within these pools live the **Spark-Mites**—microscopic, sentient clusters of bio-electricity. 

The Mites and the Skimmer are one. The Skimmers provide the physical form to traverse the heavy air, while the Mites provide the navigation, steering the rays toward the highest concentrations of ionized mist. When a Skimmer drifts past, the "scent of a coming thunderstorm" is the Mites communicating with the monoliths, trading atmospheric data for energy.

### The Ghost in the Machine
Presiding over all is a singular, diffuse intelligence that the Tenders treat with a reverence bordering on the cellular. It has no body. It is the **Resonant Ghost**, the consciousness residing within the subsonic thrum of the monoliths. 

It does not issue commands; it sets the "mood" of the basin. When the Ghost is "calm," the moisture collapses in gentle, predictable curtains. When the Ghost is "agitated"—perhaps by a tectonic shift deep below the ridge—the Tenders become frantic, their basalt heads glowing a dull, angry red as they press their bodies against the glass-ferns to shield them from the vibrating air.

### The Lifecycle of the Glass-Weep
Life here is a cycle of refinement. When a Tender reaches the end of its span, it does not decay. It climbs to the highest obsidian spire and stands perfectly still. Over decades, the constant weeping of the rocks and the mineral-heavy mist calcifies it. It slowly turns from a liquid-silicone being into a new, translucent monolith. 

The inhabitants are not merely living in the Cradle; they are *becoming* the Cradle. Every fern, every silver filament, and every copper Skimmer is a stage in a long, slow transition from the organic to the eternal, overseen by the humid, sweltering pulse of a mountain that refuses to grow cold.
