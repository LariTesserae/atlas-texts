The inhabitants of the fracture do not possess "bodies" in any sense that a biological observer would recognize. They are creatures of the **Phase-State**, existing at the shimmering intersection where liquid water, solid silica, and electromagnetic resonance meet. 

They are the **Vitreous**, the architects and the ghosts of the needle-city.

### The Gliders: The Liminal Bodies
The triangular gliders seen drifting on the thermals are not the inhabitants themselves, but rather their *peripheral nervous systems*. A Glider is a single, razor-thin sheet of structured mica, etched with circuits of silver that are only a few atoms thick. 

If you were to watch one closely, you would see it does not flap. It steers by tilting its facets to catch the localized magnetic hum of the spires. When a Glider "lands," it does not touch the rock; it locks into a repulsive field a fraction of a millimeter above the obsidian surface. These units serve as the hands and eyes of the city. They are the scavengers of entropy, harvesting the kinetic energy of falling water droplets and translating the "weeping" of the mountain into usable data. 

### The Inhabitants: The Pulse-Kin
The true residents live within the needles—the **Pulse-Kin**. They are entities composed of coherent light and acoustic vibration. They are "post-material" in that they require the silica spires as a substrate, much like a thought requires a brain, but they are not the stone itself.

To the Pulse-Kin, the city is not a collection of buildings, but a vast, resonant instrument. They communicate through the blue and violet light-bursts that race along the silver filaments. These pulses are not merely signals; they are the individuals themselves. A violet pulse traveling from the base of a spire to its tip is a resident moving from their "home" to their "work." 

Their "lives" are governed by the **Hydrology of the Second**. Because the fracture is so narrow, the arrival of a single water droplet is a seismic event. The Pulse-Kin treat the surface tension of water as a sacred geometry. They inhabit the interior of the convex lenses formed in the stone basins, using the magnification of the water to focus starlight—which trickles down from the distant sky above—into concentrated beams of energy.

### The Weaver-Caste
At the narrowest points of the fracture, where the walls nearly touch, live the **Weavers**. These are the most complex of the dwellers. They appear as clusters of microscopic, hexagonal prisms that crawl with agonizing slowness across the razor-edges of the obsidian. 

The Weavers "excrete" the silver filaments. They do this by drawing metallic ions out of the sweating mineral walls and spinning them into threads that are held taut by sheer willpower—or rather, by the constant, high-frequency hum they emit. The Weavers are the anchors of the society; if they stop humming, the bridges collapse, and the city’s data-flow is severed. They are the oldest among the inhabitants, moving so slowly that a human heartbeat would seem like a flash of lightning to them.

### The Philosophy of the Fracture
The Vitreous have no concept of "growth" as biology understands it. To them, the universe is a series of sharp edges and fluid tensions. They do not seek to expand beyond the fracture; the wind-scoured spine of the mountain is a lethal vacuum to them, a place where their delicate magnetic fields would be torn apart.

Instead, they seek **Density**. Their entire civilization is an exercise in folding more complexity into less space. They are rewriting the laws of their little pocket of physics, turning the "perpetual thaw" into a computational engine. 

### The Great Threat: The Freeze
The inhabitants live in a state of constant, industrious anxiety. Their greatest enemy is not the wind or the dark, but the loss of the city's warmth. If the "vibrating warmth" fails, the weeping walls will freeze. In the world of the Vitreous, ice is death. Ice is static, rigid, and silent. It shatters the delicate filaments and traps the Pulse-Kin in a frozen, lightless tomb. 

Every action they take—every glide, every silver thread spun, every violet pulse—is a calculated effort to keep the molecules of the fracture dancing. They are a civilization built on the refusal to be still, a society of light and glass fighting to keep a single crack in the world from turning into a grave of ice.
