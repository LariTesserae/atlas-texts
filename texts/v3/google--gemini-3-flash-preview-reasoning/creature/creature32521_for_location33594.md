The people of the Cleft—the **Lithos-kin** or the **Silt-treaders**—are a people who have been physically and temperamentally compressed by their environment. They move with a strange, fluid economy of motion, their bodies having long since adapted to the verticality of their world.

### Physiology of the Inhabitants
They are lean and long-limbed, possessing an almost prehensile strength in their fingers and toes. Their skin is the color of wet river-clay—a pale, translucent grey that reveals the faint, blue tracery of veins beneath. Because they live in a world of constant moisture and low light, their skin has a waxy, hydrophobic quality; water beads and rolls off their shoulders as it does the basalt walls. 

Their eyes are their most striking feature: oversized and deeply set, with irises the color of tarnished silver. They do not blink often, as the humidity keeps their membranes perpetually moist. They are built for the "crush," comfortable in spaces that would trigger claustrophobia in a surface-dweller, and they possess a vestibular system so refined that they can sleep soundly on a four-inch ledge without the fear of falling.

### The Social Fabric: The Collective Murmur
Privacy is a concept unknown to the Cleft-dwellers. Because one man’s roof is another man’s porch, the inhabitants live in a state of enforced intimacy. However, this has not resulted in a loud or chaotic society. Instead, they have developed a culture of profound quietude. 

They speak in a melodic, low-register shorthand—a language of hums, glottal clicks, and soft fricatives that does not travel far. To a stranger, a crowded market-ledge would sound like nothing more than the rustle of wind through dry leaves. They move in a perpetual "wet-walk," a sliding gait that minimizes the scrape of callous against stone, ensuring that the only dominant sounds remain the rhythmic *tink-tink* of the weeping walls.

### The Order of the Silt and Spire
The society is organized by altitude. 
*   **The Marsh-Keepers:** Those who live at the lowest levels, near the black silt floor, are the laborers of the tide. They are the most muscular, tasked with the constant dredging of the marsh to ensure the "lung" of the Cleft continues to breathe. They wear heavy aprons of cured crustacean-hide and are the only ones who truly know the secrets of the floor-marsh.
*   **The Gutter-Smiths:** The middle-dwellers, who maintain the dizzying network of log-channels and cisterns. They are the engineers of the Cleft, diverting the weeping water away from the hearths and into communal basins.
*   **The Rim-Watchers:** Those who live near the very top, where the fish-bladder windows tremble most violently. They are the scouts who watch the "ribbon of sky" and signal the approach of the mist-cycles with low-toned bone whistles.

### The Spectral Fauna
Sharing the verticality of the Cleft are the **Glass-Crabs**—long-limbed, spindly crustaceans with shells as clear as the basalt is dark. They act as the Cleft’s scavengers, picking the moss clean of debris and moving with a terrifying, silent speed across the ceilings of the dwellings. The inhabitants treat them with a distant reverence; they are never eaten, as it is believed they "clean the breath" of the stone.

The flightless birds, known as **Silt-Hoppers**, are small, charcoal-feathered creatures with oversized, padded feet. They do not sing. They communicate through rhythmic thumping on the hollowed-out logs of the gutters, their "knocking" serving as a background percussion to the dripping water. They are the pets and companions of the children, who carry them in papooses of braided root.

### The Cycle of the Mist
When the mist descends and the "thickening" begins, the inhabitants undergo a ritualistic shift. This is the only time the Cleft feels hurried. They emerge from their boxy cells in a synchronized ballet, their hands moving with practiced grace to tighten leather awnings and seal the fish-bladder windows. 

There is a communal tension during these hours; the mist is not just water, but a sensory veil that cuts them off even from their neighbors. In the Spire, the "Cantors" gather. They are the elders who sit at the base of the carved basalt pillar, humming in a frequency that resonates through the stone. It is a form of sonogram-navigation; by feeling the vibration of the stone, they can "see" the stability of the Cleft, ensuring that the young basalt isn't shifting under the weight of the saturation.

### Diet and Economy
They eat what the damp provides. Their diet is centered on the **Bloat-Moss**, which is harvested, pressed into nutrient-dense cakes, and fermented in stone jars. The lichen is scraped and dried into a salty, rubbery jerky. For protein, they harvest "Silt-Worms" from the marsh floor—long, translucent annelids that taste of mineral and cold salt.

There is no currency. Instead, the Cleft operates on a "Debt of Gravity." If you live above someone, you owe them the maintenance of your floor (their ceiling). If you live below, you owe them the stability of the foundations. Life in the Cleft is a continuous, manual negotiation with the earth, conducted by a people who have forgotten the horizontal world and found a cramped, silvered kind of peace in the vertical.
