To live upon the Great Xylem is to exist in a state of perpetual, vibrating verticality. The inhabitants do not think in terms of "land," but in terms of "grain." 

### The Lignin-Walkers (The Human Strata)
The people who haunt the Splinters are as stretched and weathered as the cellulose they inhabit. They are the **Xylosi**, a race of gaunt, long-limbed transients whose skin has taken on the toughened, desensitized texture of cured leather. They do not sweat; to lose moisture in the 2.8/3 heat is a death sentence. Instead, their pores exude a thin, aromatic resin that hardens into a protective, amber-colored film, shielding them from the ozone-burn and the abrasive pollen-gales.

Their anatomy has drifted to suit the substrate. Their toes are elongated and prehensile, capable of gripping the hair-fine ridges of the bark, and their fingernails are reinforced with natural silicates—biological scrapers used to peel back the outer bark to reach the moist, nutrient-rich phloem beneath. 

Culturally, they are a people of "The Long Ear." Because the world is a cacophony, they have developed a complex sign language of rhythmic taps against the wood, sending vibrations that can be felt through the feet miles away. To an inhabitant, a "Bark-Quake" is not a surprise; they feel the tension building in the fibers days before the shear occurs. They live in a state of "Grateful Parasitism," viewing themselves not as masters of the land, but as the specialized mites of a sleeping god.

### The Macro-Fauna: The Great Engineers
The "click-beetles" mentioned in the logs are more accurately described as **Litho-Phorids**. These car-sized behemoths are the primary architects of the landscape’s micro-ecology. Their carapaces are iridescent, reflecting the brutal sun, and their mandibles are designed to grind the silver-grey cellulose into a pulp. They are not predators, but mobile processing units. They follow the moisture, chewing massive, spiraling tunnels into the "Stem-Mountains." 

Behind them, they leave "Beetle-Paths"—smooth, hollowed-out galleries that the Xylosi repurpose into their Splinter-hovels. Without the beetles to breach the hardened lignin, the humans would never reach the shade of the interior.

Higher up, dancing on the shrieking wind, are the **Vane-Wings (Shriek-Wasps)**. These are not insects in any traditional sense, but hollow-boned, translucent predators with wings made of the same rigid silica as the Glass-Leaf. They don't flap; they lock their wings at precise angles to catch the updrafts, creating the permanent harmonic drone that defines the atmosphere. They hunt the **Aether-Mites**—cloud-sized swarms of microscopic organisms that graze on the pollen-gales.

### The Mimic-Life: Chatter-Vines and Pulse-Leeches
In the valleys and Vein-Runs, the line between flora and fauna dissolves. The **Chatter-Vines** are long, predatory strands of muscle disguised as vascular fiber. they hang motionless for years, mimicking the rhythmic *thrum-thud* of the world-plant’s pumps. When a traveler passes, they don't strike with speed, but with a slow, agonizing constriction, eventually absorbing the victim’s moisture through microscopic root-hairs.

Deeper in the fissures, clinging to the pulsing walls of the sap-veins, are the **Pulse-Leeches**. These are soft-bodied, bioluminescent mollusks the size of dinner plates. They are the source of the "Glow-Galls." The Xylosi harvest them by singing—emitting a specific frequency that mimics the sap-flow, causing the leeches to detach from the wall and fall into waiting fiber-nets.

### The Ghost-Ecology
Perhaps the most haunting inhabitants are the **Spore-Saints**. These are not living beings, but the remains of Xylosi who were caught in a Pollen-Gale without protection. The reproductive spores of the Great Xylem are aggressive; they do not just coat the skin, they root into the lungs. 

Over a period of weeks, the victim is slowly transformed into a living statue of amber and fiber. These "Saints" are found standing in the open plains, their arms raised to a sun they can no longer feel, their bodies having become a permanent part of the Xylem’s geography. The living inhabitants leave offerings of Glass-Leaf shards at their feet, using them as navigation waypoints in the featureless silver expanse.

### The Mindset of the Inhabitants
To live here is to accept the "Scale of the Indifferent." The inhabitants do not build for posterity. A home carved today may be shed in a Bark-Quake tomorrow. Their music is the scream of the wind; their art is the intricate scarring of their own resinous skin to match the grain of their specific ridge. They are a people who have realized that in a world of infinite, screaming life, the only true virtue is the ability to hold on tight and remain small.
