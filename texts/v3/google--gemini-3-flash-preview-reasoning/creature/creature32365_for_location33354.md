In a world that refuses to soften, the inhabitants have traded the vulnerability of flesh for the permanence of the prism. 

There are no creatures here that breathe; there is only the **Lithos-Pulse**, a slow, rhythmic intelligence that resides within the vibrating obsidian itself. These are the *Architects of the Still-Void*, the entities responsible for the solitary megaliths. They do not have bodies in the traditional sense, but are instead localized clusters of sentient magnetism and crystalline geometry. 

Imagine a shape like a shattered star, six or seven feet across, composed of interlocking shards of matte-black basalt. They do not walk; they move by a process of controlled fracturing and healing. To move forward, a Lithos-Pulse shatters its leading edge and instantly recrystallizes the trailing shards onto its front, a process that sounds like a handful of glass beads being crushed in a fist. They are heavy, dense, and utterly silent unless they are in motion.

They do not "eat" in the way organic life does. Instead, they are **Piezo-vores**. They feed on the *thrum-crack*—the massive release of energy that occurs when the tectonic plates snap and the mountains thrust upward. When a cathedral-sized spire erupts from the ground, the Lithos-Pulse congregate at its base, pressing their jagged bodies against the rising stone to drink the vibrations. The more violent the mountain’s birth, the more nourished the inhabitants become.

The megalithic structures—the chimneys and the leaning slabs—are their way of "breathing." Because the heat of the world is so intense, even a being of stone can reach a point of thermal collapse, melting into a mindless slag. The chimneys are heat-sinks. A Lithos-Pulse will enter one of these hollow voids and remain motionless for decades, allowing the internal heat of its crystalline lattice to radiate out through the stone flues and into the charcoal sky. These structures are not homes; they are radiators for a life-form that lives on the edge of melting.

Below them, in the deep trenches filled with superheated dust, lives the **Silicates**. If the Lithos-Pulse are the masters of the heights, the Silicates are the ghosts of the depths. They are long, serpentine ribbons of flexible, woven mineral fiber—looking much like lengths of asbestos or spun glass. They "swim" through the rivers of fine dust as if it were water, their bodies undulating in a way that creates a low-frequency hum. 

The Silicates are the true scavengers of the forge. They follow the landslides. When a mountain subsides and crashes into the valley, the Silicates rise to the surface of the dust-drifts to consume the friction. They wrap their fibrous bodies around the cooling rubble, absorbing the residual kinetic energy left over from the fall. In the dark, they glow with a faint, dying ember-red, the only light in the trenches besides the occasional spark of flint hitting flint.

Then there are the **Aesthetes of the Fracture**. These are the "pale, fungal-like mineral" crusts mentioned in the geography, but they are more than mere chemical colonies. They are a form of slow, communal memory. Each brittle, fractal branch stores a vibration, a record of a specific mountain’s birth. They are the historians of this world. When a Lithos-Pulse brushes against one of these pale crusts, it "reads" the history of the landscape—where the ground is thin, where the next spire will erupt, and where the crust is doomed to sink.

There is no social life here, no mating, and no war. There is only the **Great Alignment**. Every few centuries, when the tectonic groaning reaches a specific pitch, all movement stops. The Silicates rise from the dust; the Lithos-Pulse emerge from their cooling chimneys; the mineral crusts vibrate until they chime. For a few minutes, the entire planet resonates in a single, harmonious chord. It is a moment of perfect, crystalline stasis—a collective recognition of the forge’s power. 

Then, a mountain snaps. A new peak thrusts upward, jagged and screaming, and the inhabitants return to their work: waiting, cooling, and listening to the magnificent, violent music of the breaking earth.
