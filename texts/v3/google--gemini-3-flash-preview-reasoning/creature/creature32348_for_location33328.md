The inhabitants of the Sun-Cleft are the **Chord-weavers**, a race of beings that seem less like biological entities and more like the physical manifestations of the sound that saturates their world. 

To look upon a Weaver is to see a creature of exquisite, brittle geometry. They are elongated and spindly, rarely taller than a splinter, with limbs that possess the tensile strength of piano wire and the translucence of stained glass. Their bodies are not covered in skin, but in a layered tegument of "living lacquer"—a hard, resonant resin that they excrete and polish against the obsidian walls. In the pitiless gold light, they shimmer with the same orange and violet hues as the Aolian Wicks, making them nearly invisible when they are still.

They are a people of stillness and sudden, blurring speed. Because the atmosphere is a "white roar," the Weavers have no need for ears; their entire skeletal structure is a sensory organ. Their bones are hollow, fluted tubes of silica that vibrate in sympathy with the Cleft’s screams. They do not "hear" the wind; they feel it as a constant, internal heat, a map of pressure and pitch that tells them exactly where every bridge, wick, and neighbor is located. 

### The Low Density of Presence
The city feels empty because the Weavers are a "slow" people. In a world of perpetual agitation, they have evolved a paradoxical metabolism. They spend vast stretches of time—years, sometimes decades—in a state of **Harmonic Stasis**. They hook their hooked, needle-like claws into the weave of their orb-huts and allow their internal vibrations to sync perfectly with the frequency of their specific district. In this state, they do not eat or move; they simply exist as a part of the city’s music. 

A "populated" plaza might contain a hundred Weavers, but to a visitor, it would look like a collection of decorative, crystalline statues. They only "awaken" when the external wind shifts—when a new note enters the Cleft. Then, they move with a terrifying, jerky grace, their joints clicking like castanets, to repair a frayed silk-line or to "retune" a sagging bridge by tightening its knots.

### The Art of the Knot
The Weavers are obsessive architects. Their culture is built entirely on the tension of the thread. They do not use tools; they spin dust-silk from glands in their abdomens and manipulate it with four multi-jointed fingers that end in microscopic hooks. 

To a Weaver, a knot is a prayer, a record, and a stabilizer. Each "Hanging District" is tuned to a different chord. If the wind increases, the city risks shattering from its own resonance. The inhabitants must constantly adjust the tension of the catwalks and the density of the orb-huts to ensure the city doesn’t vibrate itself into dust. They are not just citizens; they are the luthiers of their own habitat, constantly tuning the city to survive the gale.

### The Skimmers and the Hollow Cattle
The "skimmers" mentioned in passing are the Weavers' primary companions and source of supplemental energy. These paper-thin, leaf-like organisms are essentially living solar sails. The Weavers "herd" them using long poles made of wick-filaments, guiding the skimmers into high-velocity wind channels where the friction generates a static charge. The Weavers then "harvest" this charge by touching the skimmers, absorbing the raw electrical energy into their own resonant marrow.

There are also the **Dust-Mites of the Wick**, tiny, spherical scavengers that look like rolling burrs. They tumble through the "forest," cleaning the ash-soil and polishing the base of the Aolian Wicks. The Weavers treat them with a distant, structural respect, as one might treat a honeybee—essential, but fundamentally part of the background machinery.

### Communication: The Touch-Singing
Communication between Weavers is a tactile, percussive affair. They do not have vocal cords. Instead, they communicate by tapping their needle-like digits against the silk floors of their huts or by rubbing their serrated forearms together (stridulation). 

When two Weavers meet on a swaying catwalk, they press their foreheads together—the thinnest part of their resinous shells—and allow their internal vibrations to merge. This is a "merging of chords," a telepathic exchange of information where memories and intent are passed as complex harmonic ripples. To them, a lie is impossible; you cannot fake the frequency of your own bones.

### The Silent Watchers
The scarcity of the Weavers gives the Sun-Cleft its peculiar atmosphere of an "abandoned cathedral." You may walk through a plaza of a thousand huts and see no one, yet you are being watched. A Weaver in stasis sees everything through the vibration of the floor beneath your feet. They are a civilization that has transcended the need for bustle. They are content to be the ghost in their own machine, waiting for the wind to change, surviving on light and the eternal, beautiful violence of the roar.
