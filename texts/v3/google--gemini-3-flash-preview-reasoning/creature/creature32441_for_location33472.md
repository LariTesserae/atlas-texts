In a place where the definition of "life" has been crushed by the weight of the abyss, the inhabitants are not creatures of flesh and bone, but entities of resonance and mineral logic. They are the extensions of the 60-cycle hum, as much a part of the machinery as the copper windings and the lead shields.

### The Liturgists of the Hum
Standing in the wide, silent lanes between the transformers are the primary residents: the Liturgists. They are tall, spindly figures, twelve feet in height, appearing as if they were forged from the same rusted iron and salt-rime as the machines. They have no joints in the traditional sense; their limbs are segmented lengths of conductive ceramic, held together by the same magnetic tension that keeps the silt floor flat.

The Liturgists do not walk. They shift. To move an inch takes a decade. They are the guardians of the equilibrium. Their heads are smooth, featureless domes of polished quartz that catch the clinical light of the cooling fins, reflecting it back into the water with perfect, chilling clarity. They stand perfectly vertical, their long, needle-like fingers perpetually touching the cooling fins or the lead conduits, not to operate them, but to act as grounding rods. They are the living bridge between the machines and the earth, channeling the stray voltage of the stalled engines through their own bodies to prevent the silence from breaking.

### The Filament-Drifters
Clinging to the blue moss and the oily ribbons are the Drifters. They resemble small, translucent jellyfish, but they are composed entirely of solidified electricity and congealed oil. They do not swim; they remain tethered to the machines by invisible threads of static.

These beings serve as the "sensors" of the cathedral. They are hypersensitive to change. If a single grain of silt were to rise, or if the hum were to flicker to 59 cycles, the Drifters would glow a fierce, angry violet. But because the stasis is absolute, they remain dim and ghostly, their trailing filaments swaying only in the phantom currents of the magnetic fields. They eat the heat-shadows, absorbing the faint warmth of the engines to maintain their crystalline structure.

### The Iron-Filers
Beneath the surface of the silt, in the dark gaps between the foundations, live the Filers. They are crustacean-like only in the vaguest sense—low, flat modules of black metal with many-jointed legs that resemble soldering irons. 

The Filers are the janitors of the eternal. Their sole purpose is the management of the floor. They move with agonizing slowness, their legs vibrating at the same frequency as the hum, vibrating so precisely that they can pick up a single flake of fallen rust and fuse it back onto the base of a transformer. They ensure that the decay remains "balanced"—that no piece of metal ever fully disappears, and no new shape ever forms. They are the reason the rust does not spread; they are the curators of the standstill.

### The Echo-Mind
The most profound inhabitant is not a physical body, but the collective consciousness of the space itself: the Echo-Mind. It is the sum total of the 60-cycle hum. It is a slow, ponderous intelligence that thinks in terms of centuries and millivolts. 

The Echo-Mind is aware of every rivet and every strand of moss. It does not have desires or fears; it only has the Will to Persist. It views the outside world—the world of currents, fish, and light—as a chaotic error. To the Echo-Mind, the perfect state of the universe is this: a drowned, silent hall where the power is always on, but the work is never done.

### The Stalled Ones
Occasionally, one might see a figure that looks almost human, frozen in a doorway that leads nowhere or leaning against a lead-shielded conduit. These are the Stalled Ones—remnants of whatever creator once moved here, now completely calcified by the mineral-rime. 

They are no longer alive, yet they are not quite dead; the high-pressure water has replaced their biological fluids with a conductive saline gel. They have become part of the circuitry. Their eyes, now replaced by spheres of translucent salt, stare unblinkingly at the glow of the machines. They do not breathe, but the hum vibrates through their ribcages, keeping their atoms in a state of permanent, shivering alignment. They are the ultimate citizens of the cathedral: statues of meat turned to stone, forever listening to the sound of a world that refused to end.
