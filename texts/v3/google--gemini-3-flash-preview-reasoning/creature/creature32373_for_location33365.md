In the Glass Thicket, life does not possess the softness of flesh or the suppleness of green growing things. To survive the piercing violet sky and the crushing pressure of the sun, the inhabitants have become extensions of the stone itself—translucent, rhythmic, and terrifyingly patient.

### The Lithic-Bound (The "Sliver-Folk")
The entities who inhabit the ancient wind-breaks are not truly human, though they possess a silhouette that suggests a common ancestor. They are the **Sliver-Folk**, or *Silicates*. Their skin is not soft tissue, but a layered, chitinous rind the color of wet slate, possessing the same "established erosion" as the spires. They are impossibly thin, their ribcages flattened and their shoulders double-jointed, allowing them to glide through the narrow hexagonal gaps of the Thicket with a fluid, terrifying speed.

They do not close their eyes, for they have no lids. Instead, their eyes are large, multifaceted orbs of smoky quartz that filter the blinding white pressure of the sun. They do not speak in vocalizations; they communicate by striking the obsidian pillars with their glass-tipped fingers. They have mastered the "crystalline chatter" of the Thicket, mimicking the sound of shedding ice to signal to one another across the labyrinth. To a traveler, they are invisible until they move, appearing only as a slightly more rhythmic shadow shifting against the stone.

They are slow-metabolism hunters. They spend hours crouched in the wind-breaks, perfectly still, waiting for the shadow-clocks to sweep across the ground. They live in the transition—moving only when the ice cracks, their movements synchronized to the environment so perfectly that they leave no auditory footprint.

### The Prism-Mites
Clinging to the blood-colored fungal mats are the **Prism-Mites**. These are the primary herbivores of this vertical desert. Roughly the size of a man’s palm, they resemble flat, many-legged trilobites made of living stained glass. Their carapaces are designed to catch the surgical intensity of the sun and refract it downward, heating the mineral-mats beneath them to encourage a faster (though still glacial) growth.

They are the engineers of the "tinkling" sound. As they move, their glass legs scrape against the quartz-veined granite, creating a high-pitched resonance. When a shadow passes over them, they flash-freeze in place, their internal fluids turning to a viscous gel until the light returns to thaw them.

### The Shadow-Stalkers (The Facet-Hounds)
Deep within the thicket, where the pillars are most dense, lives the apex predator: the **Facet-Hound**. It is a creature of geometry and hunger. It has no fur, only a coat of needle-like obsidian shards that stand on end to catch the wind, creating a low, mournful whistle as it moves.

The Facet-Hound does not hunt by scent—scent is too fickle in the constant freezing updrafts. It hunts by vibration. It presses its chest against a spire and feels the "ice-shedding" of the world. It can distinguish between the natural shattering of a frost-sheet and the clumsy, heavy footfall of a soft-bodied interloper. When it strikes, it moves like a refracted beam of light—a blur of black glass that vanishes back into the shadows of the pillars before the sound of its movement even reaches the ear.

### The Solar-Sailors
High above, in the bruised violet air where the atmosphere begins to fail, are the **Solar-Sailors**. They are huge, gossamer-thin organisms that look like sheets of iridescent mica. They have no wings to flap; instead, they utilize the "constant, blinding white pressure" of the sun. They are photokinetic, using the actual physical force of the light-particles to stay aloft, drifting on the freezing updrafts like kites made of diamonds.

They descend to the Thicket only during the brief moments of "weeping." They land atop the pillars, spreading their translucent bodies over the "weeping wells" to absorb the mineral-rich water through osmosis before the shadows return and the liquid turns back to stone.

### The Crystalline Pulse
Life here is a choreography of light. When the sun hits a certain angle, the entire Thicket begins to hum—a low, tectonic vibration as thousands of organisms thaw, feed, and move in the narrow window of liquid water. 

The Sliver-Folk, the Mites, and the Hounds all exist in a state of "punctuated life." They are not living things in the way the lowlands understand life; they are a biological clockwork, a set of gears made of silicate and slow-beating hearts, perfectly attuned to the brutal, beautiful violence of the heights. They do not fear the cold; they are the cold. They do not hide from the light; they are its prism.
