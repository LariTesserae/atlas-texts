The inhabitants of this basin are not merely occupants; they are an extension of the geology, a biological layer grafted onto the basalt.

### The Dwellers
The people who navigate this vertical labyrinth are thick-limbed and heavy-boned, built to withstand the constant, marrow-deep vibration of the air. Their skin is their most striking feature: it is translucent and pale, the color of a fish’s belly or wet curd, and entirely hairless. It is not smooth, but rather pebbled with fine, sensory nodules that allow them to "feel" the roar. To them, the tectonic sound is not noise; it is a map. They navigate by the variations in the hum, sensing the density of the rock and the proximity of others through the soles of their broad, calloused feet.

They move with a slow, deliberate suction. Their fingers and toes are long, tipped with flat, spatulate pads that secrete a mild adhesive, allowing them to cling to the weeping obsidian walls like pale geckos. Because the air is a permanent steam-bath, they do not sweat to cool down; instead, their skin is highly porous, absorbing the atmospheric moisture directly into their lymphatic systems. They do not drink in the traditional sense; they bask, standing motionless on the ledges for hours, their ribcages expanding and contracting in rhythmic sync with the valley’s roar.

They are a silent people. The atmosphere is too thick, the roar too absolute for the frailty of vocal cords. Communication is tactile and rhythmic. They speak by drumming on the stone, using small, polished basalt hammers to send vibrations through the hive’s foundation. A message sent from the valley floor can be felt in the soles of a dweller a thousand feet up the cliffside within seconds.

### The Six-Legged Ungulates (The *Basalt-Gnawers*)
The large, six-legged beasts that haunt the lower tiers are the engines of the city. They are massive, barrel-chested creatures with hides like cured leather, folded into deep, protective wrinkles. They lack eyes—the ochre twilight renders sight a secondary utility—and instead possess a crown of sensitive, swaying antennae that detect the heat signatures of the fungal growths.

Their hooves are split into three keratinous claws that can find purchase in the slickest tuff. They are the harvesters. They move through the city with a scripted, ancient grace, their massive heads lowered to scrape the "tumors" of white fungi from the rock. They don't just eat the fungus; they process it, their multi-chambered stomachs breaking down the mineral-rich fibers and excreting a thick, grey slurry. This slurry is the lifeblood of the city—the dwellers collect it in stone basins to use as the mortar for their homes.

### The Bile-Weavers
The armored, dog-sized insects—the *Bile-Weavers*—are the city’s maintainers. They move in swarms of twelve, their carapaces a matte, oily black that reflects no light. They possess a hive-intelligence that seems bonded to the architecture itself. They do not build new structures; they seal the old ones. 

As the water perpetually weeps from the rock, it threatens to erode the ash-mortar of the dwellings. The Weavers follow the moisture. They scuttle into the deepest crevices, their many limbs moving in a blur of rhythmic clicking, and vomit a fast-setting, acidic bile that fuses the volcanic ash into something harder than the basalt itself. They are the reason the city does not decay. They are the immune system of the stone.

### The Pale Scavengers
High above, the winged scavengers roost on the razor-edges of the cliffs. They are creatures of bone and parchment-skin, their wingspans wide and stiff, designed for soaring on the thick, rising thermals of the valley’s heat. They have no feathers—feathers would become waterlogged and heavy in minutes. Instead, their wings are translucent membranes, veined with hot, dark blood.

They watch the city with milky, lidless eyes. They are waiting for the "Stasis" to break in a single individual. When a dweller or a beast finally succumbs to the crushing pressure of the environment and ceases to move, the scavengers descend. They do not shriek for hunger; they shriek to cut through the roar, a high-frequency vibration that signals the others to the feast. They are the only things in the valley that move with speed, dropping like pale stones from the mist to claim the fallen before the Bile-Weavers can seal the body into the foundations.

### The Fungal Guts
The gargantuan fungi are the only "flora," but they behave more like organs of the valley. They are pale, rigid, and warm to the touch. They grow in the places where the rock "sweats" the most, acting as biological filters that draw minerals out of the volcanic runoff. 

The dwellers treat these growths with a quiet, ritualistic necessity. They do not cultivate them; they live *among* them. The interiors of the stone huts are often lined with the desiccated husks of smaller fungi, which provide a soft, spongy insulation against the bone-shaking vibration of the ground. 

### The Collective Pulse
In this place, there is no "self." The dwellers, the beasts, the insects, and the fungi exist in a state of high-pressure symbiosis. A dweller’s life is spent hammering the stone to the rhythm of the roar, gathering the slurry of the ungulates, and avoiding the gaze of the scavengers. They are a population of masons who have forgotten what it means to build, focused entirely on the act of *remaining*. They are as permanent as the obsidian, as damp as the steam, and as tireless as the grinding engine of the world they inhabit.
