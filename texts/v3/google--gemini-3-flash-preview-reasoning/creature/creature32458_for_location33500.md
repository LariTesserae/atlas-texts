To live in the Veridian Tier is to exist within a state of constant, rhythmic transition. The inhabitants do not dwell *upon* the machine; they are processed by it, moving in tandem with the shifting plates and the weeping copper. 

### The Slick-Skin Skimmers
The most common visible life forms are the **Pale Skink-Walkers**. These are the amphibians mentioned in passing, but they are more than mere lizards. They have evolved translucent, gelatinous skin that lacks pigment, allowing the internal copper-blue of their blood to show through. Their hands and feet are oversized, equipped with broad, vacuum-pressure pads that allow them to cling to a vertical steel pillar even as it vibrates with the force of a thousand-ton piston. 

They are deaf to the air, having lost external ears eons ago; instead, they press their bellies against the vibrating iron to "hear" the mechanical movements of the Tier. They know a terrace is about to slide long before it moves, sensing the buildup of hydraulic pressure as a low-frequency hum in their bones. They feed on the "bio-grease"—a thick, nutrient-rich sludge of algae and machine lubricant that accumulates in the joints of the massive gears.

### The Vapor-Moths
Above the marshy floors, in the space where the steam vents hiss into the cool mist, live the **Heavy-Winged Gliders**. These are the "heavy-bodied insects," creatures the size of dinner plates with wings that look like sodden velvet. They do not flap their wings so much as they navigate the thermal updrafts. Their wings are porous, designed to absorb the ambient moisture and ozone from the air, which they filter for microscopic spores. 

They are the pollinators of the Tier, carrying moss-spores from the cooling channels of one rotating sector to the gears of another. When the machine shifts, a cloud of these moths will rise in a silent, dark burst, looking like soot caught in a breeze, only to settle again as soon as the geometry of the world finds its new equilibrium.

### The Veridian Veins (The Flora)
The plants are the true masters here, and they possess a slow, predatory intelligence. The **Copper-Wringers** are a species of thick, waxy vine that does not seek soil. Instead, they seek heat and electricity. They wrap themselves around the copper wiring and warm pipes, their root-tendrils secreting a mild acid that etches into the metal, creating a permanent anchor. 

They act as the nervous system of the Tier. When a vine is crushed by a sliding gantry, it releases a sharp, minty pheromone that triggers neighboring mosses to swell with water, providing instant lubrication to the site of the friction. The plants and the machine have entered a state of perfect stasis; the machine moves the plants toward the light, and the plants protect the machine from the heat of its own friction.

### The Valve-Keepers
Deep within the "anthill" density, there are the **Keepers**. They are few, and they are lonely. It is unclear if they are the descendants of the machine’s creators or if they are a biological fluke of the Tier itself. Wrapped in heavy, oil-slicked rags and wearing brass-rimmed goggles to protect their eyes from the constant drip of condensation, they move with a slow, deliberate grace.

The Keepers do not build; they harmonize. They carry long tuning forks and jars of thick, black resin. They walk the vibrating gantries, listening. When they hear a screech that the moss hasn't muffled—a sign of a gear misalignment or a dry bearing—they apply the resin or adjust a manual valve with a heavy wrench. They do not speak, for the humid air is too thick for voices to carry well. Instead, they communicate through a language of rhythmic taps on the pipes, sending messages through the metal floor that can be felt miles away.

They live in the hollows of the great unmoving pillars, sleeping in hammocks woven from hanging lichen. Their skin smells of wet iron and cold ozone, and their heartbeats have slowed over generations to match the rhythmic, low-motion thrum of the Tier's central pistons.

### The Ghost-Cycles
Finally, there is the **Mechanical Memory**. Because the world is constantly reconfiguring, there are "ghosts" in the system—automated drones or maintenance bots that have long since been overgrown by ferns. These rusted shells still attempt to perform their ancient routines. A moss-covered crane might suddenly activate, swinging a ton of empty air across a chasm before settling back into a century-long slumber. These movements are not signs of life, but they are the "habits" of the place, as much a part of the ecology as the skittering amphibians.

In the Veridian Tier, life is a quiet, wet, and heavy affair. Everything that lives here has learned the most important lesson of the machine: **do not resist the motion.** To survive is to slide, to cling, and to breathe in time with the weeping steel.
