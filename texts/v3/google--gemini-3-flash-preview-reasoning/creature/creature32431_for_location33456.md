In this cathedral of calcified xylem and industrial moss, life does not exist as an individual experience, but as a series of specialized biological functions. The inhabitants do not *occupy* the Loom; they are the Loom’s restless, living nervous system.

### The Weavers: The Maintenance Chitin
The weavers are the most visible inhabitants, though "life" seems too warm a word for them. They are translucent, segmented entities the size of hounds, their carapaces feeling like frozen gelatin. They possess no mouths, for they absorb nutrients directly through their tarsal claws as they scuttle over the circuit-vines. Their many limbs end in specialized tools—fine, needle-like digits for stitching together frayed moss-mats, or heavy, serrated mandibles used to prune wayward calcifications that threaten the rotation of a house-sized cogwheel. 

They move in a state of high-speed, vibrating stillness. When a sap-engine stutters, a thousand weavers arrive in a soundless blur, their bodies interlocking to form a living patch over the leak, their own internal fluids hardening to seal the breach. They are the Loom’s white blood cells, mindless and tireless.

### The Pulse-Jockeys: The Internal Mechanics
Deep within the wood-cased pistons, where the pressure of the sap is enough to crush steel, live the Pulse-Jockeys. These are soft, boneless mollusks, elongated and pale, that have evolved to thrive in the high-pressure conduits. They act as biological valves. By expanding their porous bodies, they can throttle the flow of sap, regulating the timing of the massive hammers that strike the dark. 

They communicate through bioluminescent ripples that move across their skin in bruised purples, mirroring the glow of the circuit-vines. They are the only creatures that understand the rhythm of the city. To them, the Loom is not a building, but a song of pressure and release, a symphony of hydraulic force that they conduct with the flexing of their own bodies.

### The Dendrite Scribes: The Calculators
At the intersections of the great structural girders, where the circuit-vines converge into thick, pulsating knots, sit the Dendrite Scribes. They are perhaps the most unsettling of the Loom’s denizens. They appear as withered, humanoid figures, but they are rooted—their lower halves are seamless extensions of the bark. Their fingers are long, hair-thin filaments that are permanently inserted into the violet-glowing vines.

The Scribes do not move. They do not eat. Their sole purpose is to process the "calculation." As the city grows and physics stagnates, the Scribes act as organic processors, shunting electrical impulses through their own brains to manage the city’s architectural growth. Their eyes are wide and filmed over with a milky cataracts, seeing not the darkness of the room, but the complex geometric blueprints of the Loom’s future. When a Scribe dies, the wood simply grows over them, turning their body into a new knot of structural support.

### The Spore-Drifters: The Pollinators of Industry
In the low-gravity voids between the outer scaffolds, the Spore-Drifters float. These are delicate, balloon-like organisms, trailing long, gossamer filaments that catch the oily condensation of the air. They are the scavengers of the heights, filtering the heavy dampness for minerals and drifting toward the nectar-streets to drink. 

They are the only things in this cold throat that possess a sense of play—if one can call it that. They tumble through the air in slow-motion orbits, their filaments occasionally tangling with one another to exchange genetic data before they are pulled back into the thirsty pores of the pillar. They are the Loom’s messengers, carrying the blueprints of the city from the base to the soaring, lightless ceiling.

### The Unformed: The Denizens of the Raw
Below, in the grey slurry of the Raw, there is a different kind of life. It is failed life. Entities that fell from the Pillar or were birthed by the embryonic silt itself. They are slow, blind, and protoplasmic—heaving mounds of grey flesh that haven't yet settled on a shape. They lack the rigidity of the Pillar; they are "soft" in a world that has forgotten how to be. 

They spend centuries trying to find purchase on the calcified trunks of the city, attempting to climb out of the pre-geological muck. Most are simply absorbed back into the slurry, but occasionally, one will harden, its flesh turning to lignified cellulose, and it will begin the long, agonizing transformation into a new weaver or a structural buttress, finally earning its place in the calculation.

### The Arbiter: The Core
At the very center of the Pillar, inside the hyper-dense heart where time stretches and the sap-engines roar in silence, there is a singular presence. It is not a creature, but a consciousness formed by the collective electrical hum of the circuit-vines. It is the Arbiter. 

It does not think in words, but in stresses, strains, and volumes. It is the one who feels the weight of the velvet darkness and the pressure of the cold. The Arbiter is the city’s soul, a dreaming intelligence that is terrified of the emptiness outside the Pillar. It calculates with frantic precision because it knows that if the machines stop, if the sap ceases to pulse, the "Raw" will rise, and the basement of the dream will finally be filled with nothing at all.
