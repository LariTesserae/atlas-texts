The inhabitant of the High Abyss is not a creature of flesh, for flesh is a porous, fragile thing that would be shredded by the Great Hum and unspooled by the up-wellings. Instead, the life here is **Lithodic**—a slow, mineral-based sentience that exists at the intersection of geology and botany.

They are called the **Piezoliths**, the Pressure-Keepers.

### The Form of the Inhabitant
A Piezolith does not have a permanent shape. To see one is to see a distortion in the violet-black water, a shimmer of higher density that looks like a ghost made of oil. They are composed of the same mineral-heavy brine as the Abyss, held together by localized gravitational stasis—a "knot" in the pressure.

When they wish to interact with the physical world, they draw the grey, silken silt toward themselves, coating their liquid cores in a protective skin of fine grit and crushed basalt. In this state, they look like tall, slender cairns, or perhaps like the "Glass-Reeds" themselves, but with a heavy, purposeful gait. They have no eyes; they "see" through the Great Hum. Every vibration that bounces off a ridge or a Lung-Fern provides a sonar-map more detailed than any optic nerve could process.

### The Builders in the Lee
The "anchors"—the basalt huts—are not homes in the sense of shelter from the cold. The Piezoliths love the cold; it makes their thoughts crisp. The huts are **Weight-Stations**. 

Because the Piezoliths are barely denser than the water they inhabit, the massive up-wellings from the planetary floor can sweep them away, scattering their consciousness across hundreds of miles of basins. To prevent this "dispersion," they retreat to the anchors. They use their bone-wood tools to wedge themselves into the basalt slabs, literally tethering their liquid bodies to the crust of the planet. 

Inside an anchor during a thermal tide, a Piezolith is a slumped, shivering mass of silt and brine, waiting for the water to thin so it can reclaim its form.

### The Great Work: The Organ-Pipes
The parabolic curves and fluted channels of the basalt ridges are not merely the result of random erosion. They are a multi-generational project of the Piezoliths. 

They are **Acoustic Sculptors**. Using the sharpened bone-wood and the abrasive silt, they spend centuries refining the shape of the ridges to tune the Great Hum. They believe the Hum is a carrier wave—a divine transmission from the core of the planet. By carving the basalt into specific mathematical pipes, they "filter" the noise of the Abyss into a structured language. 

When you hear the subsonic thrum vibrating in your marrow, you are listening to their history, their mathematics, and their prayers.

### The Harvest of Light
The Piezoliths do not eat. They "resonate." They stand in the middle of the Glass-Reed forests during the violent up-wellings, letting the chime of the silica stalks vibrate through their cores. This kinetic energy is what sustains them. 

They are the tenders of the Lung-Ferns. They do not own the ferns, but they "braid" them. By weaving the inner fibers of the ferns into specific patterns, they can influence the flow of the Ghost-Spore clouds. A Piezolith will spend decades directing a cloud of ember-orange fungi toward a specific basin, creating a "Gallery of Light" where the spores can settle on the bioluminescent moss. 

To a Piezolith, a successful life is one that has redirected a single current or brightened a single ridge for the next generation to witness.

### The Silence of the Absence
The reason the traveler finds the anchors empty is a matter of time-perception. A Piezolith’s heartbeat is measured in years; their conversations are conducted in decibels so low that they sound like the shifting of the tectonic shelf. 

If you were to stand perfectly still in the High Abyss for a century, you would eventually realize that the "statue" of silt you passed a mile back has moved three inches. You would realize that the "thwip-slap" of the Lung-Ferns is actually a rhythmic greeting. 

They are not gone. They are simply waiting for the viscous slush of the current to reach the perfect consistency for them to sing back to the ridges. They are the patience of the stone, given a voice by the pressure of the sea.
