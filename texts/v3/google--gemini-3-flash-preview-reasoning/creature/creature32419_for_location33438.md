In the crushing density of the Valve-Cleft, there is no such thing as an individual; there is only the **Metabolic Flux**. To live here is to be a component in a machine that refuses to stop running.

### The Vitreous Tenders
The primary inhabitants are the smoked-glass scavengers, known to the deeper echoes of the earth as **Vitreous Tenders**. They are not born so much as they are extruded from the waxy secretions left behind in the pipe-joints. Each one is a translucent, six-lensed geometry of chitin, no larger than a thumbprint. 

They do not possess a central nervous system; instead, their limbs are tipped with sensory filaments that plug directly into the brass vibrations of the pistons. When a piston stutters, the Tenders feel the arrhythmia in their own legs. They move in a "liquid-mechanical" state, a carpet of clicking shells that flows over the pipes like a high-viscosity oil. They do not eat organic matter; they harvest the mineral scale—the "calcified breath"—that builds up on the heated junctions. Their mouths are microscopic diamond-grinders, and the sound of a thousand Tenders scraping the brass is a constant, high-pitched singing that underlies the thrum of the pistons.

### The Filament-Weavers (The Moss)
The white, hair-like moss is actually a colony of **Caloric Filaments**. These are semi-sentient fungal strands that act as the cleft’s nervous system. While the Tenders provide the labor, the Filaments provide the data. They grow thickest where the heat is most volatile, wrapping around the glass tubing in a protective, velvet insulation. 

When the sun hits the limestone shelf above, the Filaments turn a bruised shade of violet, signaling a change in thermal pressure. They contract, pulling the glass tubes closer to the cooling rock walls. In the dark, they relax, allowing the pipes to expand. They are the silent conductors of the cleft’s frantic orchestra, feeding on the "sweat" of the Tenders—the oily sheen they secrete to stay mobile.

### The Vestige-Smiths (The Unseen)
Deep within the razor-sharp crevices, where the fissure narrows into needle-thin vents, dwell the **Vestige-Smiths**. You will never see them, only the results of their frantic, desperate repairs. They are the ones who dropped the heavy brass key and the ceramic shards. They are small, hunched, and soft-bodied, evolved from something that might have once been human or simian but has since become something entirely focused on the "Glandular Industrial."

They live in the "dead zones" behind the pipes, where the heat is most punishing. They possess long, multi-jointed fingers tipped with lead-soldered thimbles. It is the Smiths who forge the replacement gears from scavenged surface-iron and who blow the glass for the tubing using the intense heat of the scalding mist. They view the Valve-Cleft not as a home, but as a god that must be kept from suffocating. They are terrified of the silence; if the *tink-tink-tink* of the metal stops, they know the Great Lung below will seize, and the fissure will snap shut like a dying mouth.

### The Pulse-Louse
Ticking within the pipes themselves are the **Pulse-Lice**. These are internal parasites of the machine. They are flattened, metallic-looking organisms that ride the surges of brackish water. They act as living valves. When the pressure from the deep earth becomes too great, the Lice swell their bodies to block the flow, preventing the glass tubing from shattering. They are sacrificial beings; often, the pressure is so great that a Louse is pulverized, its mineral-rich remains becoming the "sludge" that the Tenders eventually harvest from the outside.

### The Collective Spirit
Nothing in the Valve-Cleft sleeps. The environment is too precarious for rest. There is a frantic, shared anxiety that binds the Tenders, the Filaments, and the Smiths together. They are a "closed-loop" society. 

The Tenders repair the pipes; the Filaments guide the Tenders; the Smiths forge the tools; and the Pulse-Lice regulate the lifeblood. Even the artifacts—the discarded gear, the ceramic—are not "trash." They are holy relics of past failures, reminders that the Valve-Cleft is a temporary victory against the crushing weight of the limestone. 

If you were to lean your ear against the fissure, you would hear more than just steam. You would hear a desperate, high-occupancy prayer, spoken in the language of friction, heat, and the relentless clicking of glass against brass. The cleft is not just a place where things live; it is a place that is being *kept alive* by a thousand tiny, terrified hands.
