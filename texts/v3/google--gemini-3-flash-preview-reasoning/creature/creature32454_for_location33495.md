In the Great Valve, life does not exist *on* the landscape; it is a specialized extension of it. The inhabitants are the white blood cells, the ball bearings, and the pressure-release valves of a machine that happens to be made of lignin and sap. They do not possess the frantic, uncertain energy of creatures in the wild; they move with the somnambulistic certainty of parts in a clock.

### The Scourers (The Floor-Warden Beetles)
The most visible denizens are the **Scourers**, the iridescent, shield-shaped organisms that patrol the ironwood floor. They are roughly the size of a low-slung carriage, and their carapaces are not made of chitin, but of a high-density, bio-ceramic that mimics the dark sheen of the wood itself. 

They do not have eyes, for there is no darkness to hide in and no prey to seek. Instead, they possess long, whip-like antennae that trail behind them, constantly tasting the grain of the floor for microscopic irregularities. Their front limbs are tipped with diamond-hard scrapers. When a Scourer finds a stray splinter or a bead of crystallized sap, it does not eat it for nutrition—it grinds the protrusion flat, consuming the dust to reinforce its own shell. They are the reason the floor is a mirror; they are the living friction that prevents the Great Valve from accumulating the debris of ages. When a Scourer finally wears down after centuries of service, it simply stops, and the others of its kind methodically disassemble and reabsorb it until the floor is once again perfectly level.

### The Vane-Wings (Acoustic Regulators)
In the vast, emerald-lit void between the pillars, the **Vane-Wings** hover. These are the bird-like entities mentioned, but they are more like biological sensors than avian life. Their wings are stiff, translucent membranes stretched over a frame of hollow bone, vibrating at a frequency that matches the deep hydraulic thrum of the walls.

The Vane-Wings are the "ears" of the Great Valve. They are hypersensitive to changes in air pressure. When the sap-pumps deep in the trunk begin to over-pressurize, the Vane-Wings feel the shift in the air’s density. In a coordinated, wordless ballet, thousands of them will converge on a specific pressure-valve in the ceiling, their combined weight and the rhythmic beating of their wings triggering the release of the cooling mist. They do not sleep; they merely tilt their wings to catch the updrafts of the biological heat, drifting like kites in a factory rafters.

### The Intracortical Plugs
If one were to press an ear against the vibrating pillars, they would hear the **Plugs**. These are soft-bodied, cylindrical organisms that live entirely within the vertical conduits of the wood. They have no limbs and no external features save for a massive, circular mouth that creates an airtight seal within the sap-veins.

The Plugs act as living gaskets. As the hydraulic pressure of the tree shifts, these creatures move up and down the internal piping, expanding their bodies to block backflow or contracting to allow a surge of nutrient-rich fluid to pass. They are the "logic gates" of the organism. Their lives are spent in total darkness, governed by the crushing weight of the sap, responding to the chemical signals of the Great Valve like neurons in a brain.

### The Mist-Siphoners (Static Epiphytes)
Clinging to the upper reaches of the columns, where the green light is brightest, are the **Mist-Siphoners**. They look like clusters of pale, weeping willow branches, but they are rooted deep into the ironwood. They are the only "stationary" fauna, acting as the condenser coils of the space.

When the scheduled mist descends, the Siphoners unfurl their hair-like filaments to catch the moisture. They don’t just drink; they filter the ozone and heavy minerals from the air, purifying the condensation before it drips down to the floor-pools. They are the smell of the room—the sharp, medicinal tang of hemlock comes from the chemical processing occurring within their translucent fronds. They are the silent alchemists of the Valve, turning the "exhaust" of the tree’s respiration back into usable water.

### The Valve-Warden (The Singular Presence)
There is a sense, in the center of the hall where the pillars are widest, of a singular, presiding intelligence—though it is not a "ruler" so much as a central processor. The **Valve-Warden** is a massive, sessile entity encrusted in the floor, looking like a gargantuan, polished burl. 

It does not move. It has no mouth to speak. But its root system is the nervous hub for every Scourer and Vane-Wing in the chamber. It coordinates the industrial rhythm. When the Warden’s internal rhythm slows, the mist arrives later; when it quickens, the beetles move with a frantic, metallic clatter. It is the conductor of this biological orchestra, a living clock-spring that has been winding and unwinding for ten thousand years.

In this place, there is no "individual." To live in the Great Valve is to be a function. Every heartbeat, every scraping claw, and every vibrating wing is a necessary calculation in the survival of the titan. It is a world where the distinction between "inhabitant" and "infrastructure" has long since dissolved.
