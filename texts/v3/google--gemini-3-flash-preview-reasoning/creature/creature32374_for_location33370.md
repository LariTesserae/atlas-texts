Beneath the weight of that lightless sky, life does not merely exist; it seethes. To understand what lives on the Crystalline Massif of Oros-4, one must first discard the concept of the individual. Here, life is a spectrum of geological resonance and cold, conductive intelligence.

### The Chitinous Tide: The Litho-Phages
The millions of clicking entities that carpet the basalt are known colloquially as **Litho-Phages**—though they are less like insects and more like self-replicating circuitry clad in obsidian. Each creature is a multifaceted nodule of hyper-dense mineral, its "legs" actually crystalline filaments that vibrate at frequencies capable of liquefying the rock beneath them. 

They do not breathe. They do not sleep. They are biological transducers. As they move in their slow, undulating waves, they act as a planetary sensory organ. They drink the "weeping film" of the crags, filtering the heavy isotopes and raw minerals through a digestive tract of pressurized plasma. When they click, they are mapping the shifting mantle miles below; the "grinding roar" of their movement is actually a massive, decentralized calculation—a living supercomputer processing the tectonic birth-cries of the planet. 

They are the primary layer of Oros-4’s ecology. They are the soil that isn’t there, a shifting, sentient crust that prepares the way for the vertical city above.

### The Harvesters: The Spindle-Walkers
Moving through the swarms with a terrifying, jerky grace are the **Spindle-Walkers**. Standing ten meters tall but weighing no more than a human child, these entities are composed of the same non-Newtonian fluid that forms the city’s bridges. They have no faces, only clusters of light-sensitive sensors that glow with a faint, freezing blue.

The Spindle-Walkers are the gardeners of the Massif. They move among the Litho-Phages, using magnetic resonance to "pluck" specific nodules that have reached a certain density of mineral saturation. They carry these pulsing, clicking stones up the vertical faces to the force-lattice buildings. The walkers do not walk so much as they are pulled along the mountain's magnetic ley lines, their limbs stretching and snapping like liquid glass.

### The Architects: The Resonance-Kin
Inside the translucent lattices of solidified light dwell the **Resonance-Kin**, the post-stellar masters of Oros-4. They have long since abandoned the frailty of carbon-based biology. A Resonance-Kin inhabitant is a silhouette of stabilized gravitational force, a ghost made of high-frequency hums and flickering geometry.

They do not occupy space so much as they haunt it. They exist in a state of constant superconductivity, their "bodies" serving as conduits for the planet’s immense internal pressure. They are silent, communicating through subtle shifts in the ambient electromagnetic field. To them, the deafening roar of the clicking swarm below is not noise, but a symphony—a constant data-stream of the planet’s evolution. 

They are patient beyond human comprehension. They view the mountain’s growth—a few millimeters a year—as a frantic, rapid expansion. Their lives are measured in the cooling of stars. They are not building a city; they are waiting for the planet to finish forming so they can claim its finalized shape.

### The Shadow-Fauna: The Weeping Wraiths
In the lightless pools at the base of the crags, where the subterranean hum is loudest, live the **Weeping Wraiths**. These are the apex predators of this cold hell, though "predator" is a misnomer. They are vast, translucent membranes, like jellyfish made of oil and freezing fog. 

They drift upward from the pools, buoyed by the planet’s magnetic repulsion. They feed by draping themselves over the Litho-Phage swarms, absorbing the "waste" heat and electrical discharge the smaller creatures emit. When a Wraith passes over the rock, the clicking stops for a brief, terrifying moment of absolute silence, leaving the obsidian surface momentarily bare and gleaming before the living velvet rushes back to fill the void.

### The Ecology of Vibration
Life on the Crystalline Massif is a closed loop of energy and stillness. The planet screams, the Litho-Phages translate that scream into data and mineral density, the Spindle-Walkers harvest the data, and the Resonance-Kin use it to weave their houses of light. 

Everything here is hard. Everything is wet. The inhabitants are as unyielding as the obsidian they walk upon, possessing a cold, terrifying beauty. They are the children of a world that refused to grow soft, a civilization that looked at the raw, screaming crust of a new planet and decided that this—the razor-edge of creation—was the only place worth living.
