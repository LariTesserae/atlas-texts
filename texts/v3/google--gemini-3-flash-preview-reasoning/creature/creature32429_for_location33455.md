In the Great Cleave, life does not struggle against the environment; it is an extension of it. The inhabitants are not merely survivors of the heat and the dark, but are the slow-moving clockwork of a world that refuses to change.

### The Mill-Walkers (The Great Lithovores)
The house-sized trilobites, known to the sapient inhabitants as **Grinders** or **Mill-Walkers**, are the primary architects of the Cleave’s soundscape. Their carapaces are not smooth; they are terraced landscapes of calcified mineral deposits, often hosting smaller parasitic colonies of sulfur-polyps. They do not have legs in the traditional sense, but hundreds of crystalline pylons that move in a peristaltic wave. 

As they traverse the razor-flats, the sound is not a footfall but a continuous, bowel-shaking groan of stone against glass. They are the cleaners of this world. Their mandibles are essentially industrial-grade crushers that pulverize obsidian shards into the fine, frictionless dust that feeds the "seas." A Mill-Walker’s life is measured in centuries; a young one may spend fifty years crossing a single obsidian sheet, leaving behind a polished, mirror-like wake that marks their passage for eternity.

### The Weaver-Wraiths (The Sapient Shapers)
Those who build the Way-Markers are the **Weaver-Wraiths**. They are tall, spindly humanoids whose skin has the texture of scorched parchment—dry, tough, and entirely devoid of sweat glands. They do not possess eyes. In their place are three deep, vertical slits on their faces—thermal pits that allow them to "see" the heat signatures of the world in high-definition infrared. To a Wraith, a rising thermal plume looks like a towering pillar of brilliant gold, and a Mill-Walker appears as a slow-moving, smoldering coal.

They communicate through **Litho-Speech**. By striking the obsidian floor with their ore-hammers in specific rhythms, they send vibrations that travel for miles through the dense rock. A Wraith can "hear" a neighbor five kilometers away by standing barefoot on the glass and sensing the patterns through their skeletal structure. 

Their culture is one of "The Great Record." Because the Cleave never changes, the Wraiths believe that every action must be intentional. To move a stone is a sacred act. The Way-Markers are not just maps; they are tactile libraries. By touching the specific arrangement of slabs, a Wraith can read the history of a clan, etched into the stone or communicated through the specific resonance of the pile.

### The Sky-Mantas and the Spore-Drifts
High above, in the pressurized gloom, the **Manta-Drifters** function as a living ceiling. Their bodies are almost two-dimensional—vast, semi-translucent sails of organic polymer that catch the rising heat. They are social only in their positioning; they stack themselves in "galleries," thousands deep, to create a biological filter that catches the microscopic sulfur-fungi rising from the depths.

They have no hearts. Their "blood" is moved by the atmospheric pressure itself, squeezed through their veins by the undulating weight of the air. When a Manta dies, it does not fall. It is so light and the air so thick that it remains suspended, a shriveled husk drifting forever in the thermals, eventually disintegrating into the very dust that fills the lowland basins.

### The Dust-Swimmers
Within the frictionless "seas" of mineral dust, a different kind of life persists. These are the **Needle-Gars**—long, segmented creatures with bodies made of overlapping, diamond-hard scales. They "swim" through the bone-dry dust as if it were water, vibration-sensitive whiskers protruding above the surface like reeds. 

They are the only predators in the Cleave. They wait for a Mill-Walker to pass too close to a dust-basin, then dart out to harvest the soft tissues beneath the giant’s armored skirt. They are creatures of pure kinetic energy, moving with a terrifying speed that contrasts sharply with the agonizing slowness of the world above the dust.

### The Quiet Ecology
There is a profound intimacy to life in the Cleave. Because there is no wind to carry scent and no light to show form, every creature is intensely aware of the *touch* of the world. 
*   **The Symbiosis:** The Wraiths follow the Mill-Walkers, living in the polished wakes they leave behind, using the pulverized dust the giants produce to insulate their shelters. 
*   **The Nutrient Cycle:** The sulfur-fungi grow in the cracks of the deep magma-veins; the thermals carry them to the Mantas; the Mantas’ waste falls as a nutrient-rich grit to the floor, where it is consumed by the Lithovores.

Nothing is wasted, and nothing is rushed. A Weaver-Wraith might spend a decade shaping a single hammer, using the constant heat of a floor-fissure to slowly soften the ore. In the Great Cleave, to be alive is to be a part of the stone—slow, enduring, and etched with the memory of the deep heat.
