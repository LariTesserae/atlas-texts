In this valley of the First Hour, the inhabitants are not survivors, for there is no death to survive. They are, instead, **perpetual tenants of the cusp**. Because the geological clock has seized at the moment of birth, the creatures here exist in a state of terminal infancy—huge, bloated, and terrifyingly functional, yet possessing the raw, unpolished textures of things still inside the egg.

### The Aerial Heavy-Weights: The Lung-Bellows
The hawk-sized insects are not the brittle, skittering creatures of a weathered world. They are **Lung-Bellows**, organisms that are more respiratory system than predator. Their carapaces are not chitinous, but have the consistency of damp, pressurized fingernails—pliant, translucent, and throbbing. Through their amber-colored shells, you can see the frantic, rhythmic churn of green-black fluids. 

They do not have mouths for biting; they have wide, weeping membranes that filter the thick opal vapor for nutrients. They stay aloft not through graceful flight, but through sheer, desperate exertion, their leathery wings beating a heavy, wet rhythm that sounds like a thousand damp sheets being snapped in the wind. They are perpetually "nectar-drunk," their abdomens swollen with the fermenting sugars of the succulent ferns, making them clumsy. They often collide in mid-air, bouncing off one another with soft, meaty thuds, never falling, merely rebounding in the thick, supportive haze.

### The Floor-Dwellers: The Unfinished
Beneath the canopy of ferns, upon the matted bridge of air-roots, live **The Unfinished**. These are the multi-limbed creatures of the pools and the moss. They lack the economy of evolution. Because nature has not yet learned to prune, they possess "too much" biology: six eyes that have not yet decided where to focus, clusters of twitching, finger-like appendages that end in soft, suction-cup pads, and skin the texture of a peeled plum.

They are bioluminescent, but their light is internal—a rhythmic, sickly yellow glow that pulses deep within their translucent torsos, illuminating their own digestive tracts. They move with an undulating, boneless grace, sliding over the slick obsidian like spilled oil. They do not hunt; they absorb. They press their bellies against the "vein-stalks" of the ferns and drink the condensation through their skin. In this world, hunger is not a sharp pain, but a dull, heavy pressure that is constantly being satisfied.

### The Sedentary Mounds: The Thrum-Hearts
Deep within the thickest tangles of the purple moss, where the obsidian ribs of the earth form shallow bowls, lie the **Thrum-Hearts**. These are the largest organisms in the basin, though they appear to be nothing more than mounds of liver-colored velvet. They are the source of the "factory sound." 

A Thrum-Heart is a singular, massive organ—a heart without a body, a lung without a throat. They are the anchors of the stasis. They do not move, they do not see, and they do not eat. They simply vibrate. Their collective resonance is what holds the atmosphere in its 3.0-scale tension. If you were to touch one, your hand would not meet resistance, but a frantic, high-frequency shudder that would rattle the marrow in your bones. They are the metronomes of the First Hour, keeping the time that never passes.

### The Parasitic Clingers: The Glass-Blooms
Growing directly onto the carapaces of the Lung-Bellows and the skin of the Unfinished are the **Glass-Blooms**. These are animal-plant hybrids that look like crystalline anemones. They have no roots in the ground; they root in the living flesh of others. Because there is no aging and no death, the Glass-Blooms simply grow until their host is entirely encrusted in a shimmering, violet-veined armor. 

This is not a harmful relationship; the host provides mobility, and the Glass-Bloom provides a secondary layer of gas-exchange. Eventually, the insects and the crawlers become walking, flying reef-systems—baroque, heavy, and beautiful—carrying entire miniature ecosystems on their backs through the bruised twilight.

### The Absence of the Individual
In this valley, there is no "self." Because the biological density is so high and the stasis so absolute, the life forms have begun to physically fuse. A fern’s air-root might grow through the soft tissue of a resting Unfinished; a Lung-Bellow might be tethered to a vein-stalk by a web of symbiotic moss. 

It is a singular, breathing mat of existence. There is no competition, only **volume**. The creatures do not cry out in fear or hunger; their "screaming" is the sound of sheer, unadulterated presence. They are the inhabitants of a beginning that refused to end—a crowded, wet, and shimmering monument to the terrifying power of a world that has only ever known how to grow.
