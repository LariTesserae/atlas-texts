In the pressurized silence of the Drowned Burl, life does not "scurry" or "bloom." It manifests with the agonizing patience of a mineral forming in a vein. The inhabitants here are not merely residents; they are extensions of the Deep Weight, biological expressions of the sharp stone and the suffocating green.

### The Lithic Acolytes (The Builders)
Those who raised the sharp stone altars are still present, though a visitor might mistake them for the unweathered chert of the floor. They are the **Lithic Acolytes**, a race of stunted, hexapedal humanoids whose skin has the texture and durability of cured sharkskin. They possess no eyes, for eyes are a liability in a world of razors and absolute dark. Instead, their entire bodies are covered in microscopic, vibration-sensitive hairs that "map" the room through the low-frequency creaks of the cedar roots.

They do not use tools because their own fingernails are made of the same volcanic glass as the floor. They spend centuries—measured in the slow, rhythmic expansion of the burl—wedging stones into the crevices, not to worship, but to "tune" the room. They are acoustic engineers of the silence, arranging the obsidian to ensure that the deep, gut-thumping vibrations of the earth above are harmonized into a frequency that keeps their slow-beating hearts in rhythm. To them, a misplaced stone is a cacophony. They move with such deliberation that a moss-colony may grow across their shoulders and reach the floor before they have finished a single step.

### The Vitreous Skulkers
The pale, spindly scavengers mentioned in the old lore are the **Vitreous Skulkers**. They are not truly crayfish, but something more elemental—crustaceans whose carapaces are transparent, revealing internal organs that glow with a faint, bioluminescent bile. Their limbs are impossibly long and tipped with needle-points, allowing them to walk atop the razor-edged chert without ever touching the toxic sludge beneath.

They are the janitors of the impossible geography. They feed on the "fallout" of the upper world—microscopic flakes of prehistoric bark and the calcified remains of organisms that died before the cedar was a sapling. A Skulker does not hunt; it simply stands perfectly still for decades, its limbs interlocking with the whalebone ferns, until a drifting sphere of heavy water carries a nutrient-rich sediment into its path. When they do move, their joints produce a sound like glass grinding against glass, a sharp *skrit* that is the only high-pitched noise in the chamber.

### The Marrow-Moss (The Sentient Veil)
The gelatinous moss is not merely vegetation; it is a singular, distributed intelligence. It acts as the nervous system of the Burl. Because the water is so dense and the air non-existent, the moss uses the "Deep Weight" as a medium for a slow, chemical telepathy. 

If you touch a frond of the whalebone fern, the moss at the opposite end of the chapel-space knows the temperature of your skin within an hour. It is the moss that protects the stones, wrapping its translucent body around the sharpest edges. It does this not out of kindness, but to preserve the "stasis." The moss hates change. It grows with "crushing force" to fill the gaps, literally anchoring the geography so that the fraying physics of the place don't unravel further. It is the jailer of the room, ensuring that the water spheres stay suspended and the time stays thick.

### The Hovering Larvae (The Fruit of the Spheres)
Inside the spheres of water that pool upward like transparent fruit, something else waits. These are the **Aeon-Larvae**. They appear as tiny, coiled silver threads suspended in the center of the heavy liquid. They are the offspring of the Drowned Burl itself, or perhaps of the prehistoric cedar. 

They do not grow. They are held in a state of perpetual, pressurized potential—the literal embodiment of the "Held Breath." They are sensitive to the "textures" of the darkness. It is whispered that if the Deep Weight were ever to lift, if the water were to drain and the pressure to drop, these threads would uncoil and grow into something that could devour a forest. But here, in the cold purple-green glow, they are merely ornaments of a frozen biology, dreaming of a pressure that never ends.

### The Ghost-Pulse Fungi
Finally, there is the source of the light: the **Ghost-Pulse Fungi**. These are microscopic, yet they form vast, carpet-like colonies on the wooden walls. They are the only things in the burl that seem to possess a sense of urgency, though "urgency" here is relative. They digest the ancient cedar with a slow, acidic fervor, and their bruised purple light is the heat of that digestion. They are the "clocks" of the Burl; the Lithic Acolytes track the passage of eras by the shifting patterns of the fungal glow, watching as the light migrates millimetre by millimetre across the grain of the prehistoric wood.

***

In this space, there is no prey and no predator. There is only the **Endurance**. Everything that lives here has surrendered to the weight, trading the frantic energy of the sun-lit world for a jagged, crystalline immortality. They are the secret thoughts of the earth, huddled together in a room of glass and bone.
