To live in the Lattice is to be an extension of its metabolism. Because the structure is a closed circuit, nothing here exists as an independent agent. There are no "individuals" in the way we understand them; there are only **tissues** that have achieved a temporary, mobile autonomy.

### The Lignin-Wrights (The Maintenance Phantoms)
While the Spires grow with Euclidean precision, the process is violent and prone to "stuttering." When a tension-bridge snaps or a photosynthetic plaza develops a necrotizing rot, the Lattice secretes the **Lignin-Wrights**.

They do not look like animals. They resemble bundles of semi-translucent, pressurized tubing wrapped in a sleeve of black-green resin. They have no faces, only vibrating diaphragms that hum at the same frequency as the sap-pumps. They move with a terrifying, jerky grace, extruding themselves from the Spire-walls like a 3D-printer creating its own scaffolding. You might see a Wright "walking" across a vertical plane, its limbs melting into the wall and reforming with every step, fusing and un-fusing in a seamless, chemical weld. Their only purpose is to smooth the jagged edges of new growth, polishing the waxy leaf-plates until they reflect the sun like obsidian.

### The Sap-Siphons (The Living Valves)
Deep within the roaring vascular bundles, where the heat is most intense, live the **Siphons**. These are stationary, organ-like entities that look like massive, pulsating polyps of amber. They are the regulators. 

Because the sap is under such immense pressure—enough to pierce steel—the Lattice requires living valves to prevent the Spires from exploding. The Siphons "breathe" the sap, their translucent bodies swelling and contracting in a rhythmic, wet thud. When the pressure becomes too great, they shriek—a high, piercing whistle of escaping steam—and vent excess fluid into the "aqueducts." They are the reason for the heartbeat you feel through the floor. They are the Lattice’s conscience, sensing the strain of the high-altitude winds and diverting nutrients to the windward side of the Spires to thicken the "bone-wood" in real-time.

### The Glissando-Drifters
If you look into the "empty" gulfs between the Spires, you might see what looks like a shimmering ribbon of gossamer caught in the gale. These are the **Drifters**. They are not birds; they are thin, miles-long strands of genetic "software" encased in a buoyant, hydrogen-filled membrane. 

They are the Lattice’s way of communicating between Spires. They carry the blueprints for the next phase of construction. They don't fly; they are cast out like fishing lines. When a Drifter tangles against a distant Spire, it is immediately absorbed, its chemical code "read" by the local Wrights, who then begin building a bridge toward the source of the strand. They are the thoughts of the planet, drifting through the boiling mist until they find a place to become solid.

### The Refuse-Eaters (The Slough)
At the base of the Spires, where the "waterfalls" of mineral-rich sap pool in the dark, sweltering heat, the Lattice creates the **Slough**. These are carpet-like organisms, miles wide and only inches thick, that cling to the lower buttresses. 

The Lattice is constantly tearing itself down. Old, discarded "structural beams" and burst fruit-bladders fall from the heights in a constant rain of organic debris. The Slough catches this. It is a digestive skin, a carpet of hyper-acidic moss that dissolves everything that touches it, breaking it back down into a liquid slurry. This slurry is then sucked back into the Spire’s roots, recycled and sent back up the elevator. If you were to fall, you would not hit stone; you would be absorbed into the Slough, your minerals cataloged and repurposed into a new staircase or a leaf-plate within hours.

### The Ghost-Pulse (The Intelligence)
The true inhabitant of the Lattice is the **Pulse** itself. There is a sense, as you walk the vibrating bridges, that the entire world is a single, slow-moving mind. It doesn't "think" in words; it thinks in *architectures*. A sudden growth of a new tower is a new idea; a bridge retracted is a forgotten memory. 

The loneliness you feel is not because you are alone, but because you are the only thing here that is *separate*. To the Lattice, you are a contaminant, a tiny speck of "non-system" matter. The Wrights may pass you by, their vibrating diaphragms ignoring your presence, but only because you haven't yet disrupted the geometry. The moment you become a structural inefficiency—the moment you break a filament or block a valve—the world will grow over you, not out of malice, but out of a relentless, geometric need for completion. 

You are standing inside a god that hasn't noticed you yet, a god made of wood and steam that is too busy calculating its own height to care about the dust on its skin.
