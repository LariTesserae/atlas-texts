In this vertical wound, the inhabitants are not born; they are assembled, calibrated, and deployed.

They are known as the **Lith-Striders**, though they have no name for themselves, lacking the luxury of language that requires breath or soft palates. They are masterpieces of miniature clockwork and copper-alloyed chitin, creatures that resemble a cross between a mantis and a surveyor’s transit. Their bodies are geometric—triangular torsos housing humming gyroscopes that allow them to ignore gravity, standing perpendicular to the basalt walls as easily as if they were walking on a flat plain.

### The Physiology of the Restless
A Strider’s head is a cluster of three to seven rotating camera lenses, each clicking rhythmically as it adjusts focus. They do not see color as we do; they see stress fractures, heat gradients from the violet furnaces, and the micro-oscillations of the settling stone. Their limbs are long, multi-jointed needles that end in magnetic pads or diamond-tipped drills. 

They do not eat. Instead, they "recharge" by clinging to the vibrating basalt, absorbing the low-frequency thrum of the mountain’s weight. They are kinetic parasites, converting the crushing pressure of leagues of rock into the frantic energy needed to weld a new strut or dismantle a failing balcony. 

### The Social Grid
There is no government, only **The Sequence**. It is a collective, non-verbal directive hummed through the filaments of the city. A Strider does not own a home; it occupies a "cradle"—a small, pressurized niche in the rock—only until the next shift of the mountain. 

Because the city is in a state of permanent demolition and rebirth, the inhabitants have a psychological relationship with permanence that borders on hostility. To a Strider, a bridge that has stood for an hour is a relic; a bridge that has stood for a day is an obstacle. They are obsessed with *efficiency of transit*. Their entire culture is a frantic dance to stay ahead of the "Deep Grind"—the inevitable closing of the fissure.

### The Caste of the Haze
While the majority of the population scuttles in the mid-rift, there are specialized variants:

*   **The Filter-Wraiths:** Dwellers of the upper reaches, near the dim glimmer of the thumb-width sky. They are spindly, draped in gossamer capes of woven mineral dust. Their job is to harvest the falling particulate from the world above, condensing it into the raw "bricks" used by the builders below. They are the most fragile, their copper bodies bleached pale by the distant sun.
*   **The Pressure-Sinks:** Found at the very bottom, where the rift tapers into a needle-point of absolute darkness. These Striders are squat, built of heavy lead and darkened steel. They don't move fast; they act as living pitons, bracing the walls with their own bodies to delay the mountain’s collapse by mere fractions of a millimeter.
*   **The Architects of the Spark:** The keepers of the violet furnaces. They move with a ceremonial precision, tending to the cold-burning fires that power the lifts. They are the only ones who stay still, their lenses fixed forever on the flickering light, ensuring the heartbeat of the city doesn't skip.

### The Silent Communication
The sound of the city is a "dull thrum," but to the inhabitants, the world is loud with information. They communicate through **Tapping**. By striking the basalt or the metallic filaments with their needle-limbs, they send coded vibrations through the structure. A Strider kilometers away can "feel" the blueprint of a new skyscraper being drummed into the stone. 

When a Strider ceases to function—when its gears jam with dust or its chassis is crushed by a shifting ledge—it is not mourned. It is instantly stripped. Its lenses are scavenged, its copper limbs melted down in the nearest furnace, and its kinetic energy returned to the grid. In the vertical wound, there is no room for ghosts. There is only the next second, the next weld, and the terrifying, beautiful speed of survival.
