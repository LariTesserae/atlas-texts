To understand what lives within the Loom of the Xylocore, one must first abandon the concept of the individual. In a world where the distinction between a gear and a ganglion has vanished, life is not something that *inhabits* the forest; life is the process of the forest itself. 

Yet, there are distinct manifestations of agency here—entities that serve as the white blood cells, the maintenance crews, and the slow, grinding architects of this thermal hell.

### The Arbor-Architects
The primary "citizens" of the Xylocore are the trunks themselves. They are not passive timber; they are slow-motion titans of industry. Each pillar possesses a distributed, subsonic consciousness—a mind composed of rhythmic pulses traveling through the copper-laced bark. They do not "think" in words or images, but in stress-loads and thermal gradients. 

An Arbor-Architect may spend three centuries calculating the perfect geometric arc for a new canopy-strut. When the decision is made, it does not grow so much as it *extrudes*. You can hear them thinking: a deep, tectonic groan that vibrates in your marrow, the sound of a billion tons of calcified wood being pressurized until it behaves like a slow-moving liquid. They are the masters of the Loom, each one a localized god of geometry, forever negotiating space with its neighbors through the grinding of their obsidian peaks.

### The Friction-Walkers
Because the Loom is a closed circuit, nothing is ever truly discarded, but the constant movement of the ridges creates a vast amount of "chaff"—shards of polished obsidian, copper filings, and flakes of carbonized bark. 

The **Friction-Walkers** are the scavengers of this debris. They look like multi-legged, segmented urns made of scorched teak and brass. They lack eyes, for there is no light to see; instead, their hulls are covered in thousands of microscopic, heat-sensitive pits. They scuttle along the vibrating trunks on needle-like hydraulic legs, seeking out the heat-sores where two branches have rubbed together. They "heal" these friction points by secreting a slurry of the amber resin, buffing the joints back to a mirror finish with rotating, abrasive pads. They are the oil-cans of the planet, frantic and tireless, driven by a hard-coded instinct to eliminate all inefficiency.

### The Amber-Siphons
Within the pressurized heart of the massive trunks, life takes a fluid form. The **Amber-Siphons** are semi-liquid organisms that exist entirely within the hydraulic systems of the Xylocore. They are like sentient, viscous eels composed of the same resin that powers the forest. 

They move through the "veins" of the trees at terrifying speeds, propelled by the immense pressure of the Loom’s heartbeat. Their purpose is regulation. If a radiator-fin fails to deploy or a piston-stalk jams, the Siphons congregate at the site of the blockage. They act as a living solvent, dissolving clogs or hardening their own bodies into temporary gaskets and valves. They are the only things in this world that are truly "soft," yet they live under pressures that would turn a human into a thin red paste in a microsecond.

### The Valve-Singers
At the junctions where the great "wood-turbines" meet, you will find the **Valve-Singers**. They are perhaps the closest thing the Xylocore has to a voice. These are stationary, sessile organisms that resemble massive, fluted barnacles made of ironwood. 

They sit atop the pressure-vents of the lower strata. As the planet’s core heat builds, they regulate the release of searing gases. They do this by vibrating their internal chambers, creating a series of harmonic whistles and *hisses* that can be heard for miles. To a visitor, it sounds like a pipe organ being played by a hurricane. To the Loom, it is data. The pitch and frequency of the Valve-Singers' "song" inform the surrounding Arbor-Architects of the thermal health of the crust, triggering the massive reconfigurations of the landscape.

### The Weaver-Mind (The Loom Itself)
Ultimately, there is no "them"—there is only the **Loom**. 

Every Friction-Walker, every Siphon, and every groaning pillar is a specialized cell of a single, planetary-scale organism. The Xylocore is a lonely intelligence. It has no interest in the stars it cannot see. It is occupied entirely with the sublime mathematics of its own internal expansion. It is a sculptor that is also the clay, a clockmaker that is also the clock. 

It lives in a state of perpetual, agonizing perfection, forever folding and unfolding itself in the hot, dry dark, listening to the magnificent, rhythmic thrumming of its own copper heart. It does not know you are there. You are merely a microscopic impurity in the resin, a momentary friction in a world that has spent billions of years learning how to be smooth.
