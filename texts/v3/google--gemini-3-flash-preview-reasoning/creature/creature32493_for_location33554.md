The inhabitants of the Shards, those who huddle in the birth-folds, are not so much a people as they are a biological echo of the geology. They are called the **Tremulants**, for they never truly stand still; their bodies are in a state of perpetual, microscopic shivering, a frantic internal mimicry of the Sound that prevents them from being shattered by it.

### The Tremulants
Physically, they are elongated and translucent, their skin having the matte, semi-opaque quality of frosted sea-glass. They lack the soft curves of wet-world creatures; their joints are sharp, their ribcages pronounced and rigid, designed not to expand for deep breaths—for there is no true atmosphere to inhale—but to act as a cage for a complex system of internal tuning forks. 

They have no vocal cords. To speak is to invite the Sound to vibrate their throats into ribbons. Instead, they communicate through **Litho-Sign**: a tactile language of rhythmic tapping against the obsidian walls and the manipulation of small, handheld quartz chimes. A Tremulant mother does not sing a lullaby; she strikes a specific harmonic on the wall of the slate cell, creating a localized standing wave that lulls the child’s nervous system into a state of resonant rest.

Their eyes are their most startling feature. Large, lidless, and multifaceted like a fly's but composed of a dark, oily crystal, they do not see color as we do. They see *density*. To a Tremulant, the amethyst sky is a solid, crushing wall, and the liquid light pooling in the hollows is a blinding, viscous soup. They navigate by the shimmer of the air, reading the acoustic shadows cast by the great pillars to detect where reality is beginning to fray.

### The Phonosynthetic Weavers
The Tremulants are not the only things that "live" here. There are the **Spindle-Wrights**, entities that occupy the gray area between mineral and animal. They look like clusters of black needles held together by a magnetic center. They do not have hearts; they have a core of pure, vibrating quartz that beats in perfect synchronicity with the planet's core. 

The Wrights move with a terrifying, jerky grace, skittering across the vertical faces of the pillars to graze on the "lush" red moss. They are the source of the silicate-fiber the Tremulants use for their bridges. A Tremulant hunter does not kill a Wright—you cannot kill what does not possess a metabolism—instead, they "retune" it. By striking the Wright with a specific frequency, they cause it to shed its outer filaments in a brittle, crystalline molt, which is then gathered and spun into the tension-lines that span the abyss.

### The Glissandos
While the prompt suggests no fauna with hearts or lungs, there is a predator of sorts: the **Glissando**. A Glissando is a localized collapse of perspective, a sentient distortion in the amethyst haze. It appears as a ripple in the air, a moving lens that magnifies and twists the obsidian background. 

It feeds on the "newness" of the world. It seeks out the dwellings of the Tremulants, drawn to the pockets of silence created by their resonators. To a Glissando, silence is a vacuum, a hole in the universe that it hungrily tries to fill. When a resonator fails, the Sound doesn't just return; the Glissando rushes in with it, and the inhabitants of the cell are found not dead, but folded—their bodies turned into impossible, non-Euclidean geometries, their bones fused into the very slate of their beds.

### The Liquid-Light Gatherers
Deep in the canyons, where the light pools like glowing bile, live the **Siphons**. These are the elders of the Tremulant society, those whose bodies have become so saturated with the copper-sugar dust that they can no longer climb the higher stairs. They sit in the depressions of the rock, their long, glass-like fingers submerged in the phosphorescent fluid. 

They do not drink. They absorb the light through osmosis, their veins glowing a dim, sickly green beneath their translucent skin. They serve as the living batteries of the birth-folds. At night—or the period when the amethyst gas thickens into a deeper violet—the Siphons return to the dwellings, their presence providing the only warmth in a world of cold stone. They give off a low-frequency hum, a byproduct of the light they’ve consumed, which helps stabilize the reality-warping "hollows" around the camp.

### The Culture of Persistence
Life in the Shards is an exercise in manual endurance. To stand still is to be slowly encrusted by the mineral-heavy air; to move too fast is to trip into a gravity-well and fall upward into the crushing purple sky. 

The Tremulants worship the **Great Stasis**. Their art is the stacking of stone, a slow, meditative process where a single wall may take three generations to complete. They view the "wet worlds" of myth as terrifying, unstable places of rot and motion. To them, the highest form of existence is to become like the obsidian pillars: sharp, unweathered, and perfectly attuned to the eternal, grinding roar of the world. 

They are a people of the edge, living in the creases of a planet that hasn't decided if it wants to exist yet, held together by nothing more than the terrifying, beautiful pressure of a Sound that never ends.
