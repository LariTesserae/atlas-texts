In the High Sump, life does not possess a biography; it possesses a frequency. To inhabit the Sky-Shelf is to be a component in a hydraulic engine that never sleeps and never produces a single finished product. The inhabitants are not "born" so much as they are extruded or pressurized into existence by the rhythmic shifting of the peat.

### The Valvulars (The Gasket-Flesh)
Clinging to the sliding shafts of the Piston-Oaks are the most common denizens: the **Valvulars**. These are the "Soft-Machines" mentioned in the ancient scrolls of the lowlands. They resemble giant, translucent lung-sacs or inside-out jellyfish, anchored to the rough bark by suites of prehensile, vein-rippled cilia. 

They are the Sky-Shelf’s maintenance crew. As the Piston-Oaks hammer downward, the Valvulars expand their bodies to catch the overflow of mineral-syrup, filtering out the heavy salts and "breathing" the friction-heat generated by the wood-on-wood sliding. They have no eyes, for there is nothing to see but the charcoal mist; instead, they have arrays of copper-colored pits along their flanks that sense the *timing* of the world. A Valvular that falls out of sync with its tree’s stroke is instantly crushed or shaken loose to die in the freezing moss. They communicate through subsonic groans that harmonize with the thud of the oaks, creating a low, melodic drone that underlies the violence of the landscape.

### The Sleet-Wings (The Shuddering Hulls)
The door-sized insects, the **Sleet-Wings**, are not truly insects in the biological sense. They are sheets of sentient, chitinous parchment, evolved to navigate a sky where gravity is a suggestion. Their wings are not designed for lift but for resonance. 

They do not flap; they tremble. By vibrating their frost-heavy membranes at specific frequencies, they "surf" the standing waves of sound created by the Piston-Oaks. They are the scavengers of the air, their long, needle-like proboscises designed to pierce the floating spheres of upward-falling water before they shatter. In the dim light, they look like ghost-ships or discarded kites, drifting aimlessly until a sudden gust of "rhythm" catches them and hurls them miles across the plateau in a blurred second.

### The Slurry-Wyrms (The Deep Paws)
Deep within the miles-thick peat, in the black, pressurized slurry where the mountains went to die, live the **Slurry-Wyrms**. These are the architects of the basins’ shifting topography. They are immense, eyeless cylinders of muscle and lignin, miles long and barely thicker than a Piston-Oak. 

They move through the peat not by burrowing, but by peristalsis, their bodies mimicking the pumping action of the plants above. They are the reason a hill might pulse like a heart; they are the "paws" of the world, rearranging the sub-stratum. They never surface. The only evidence of their existence is the rhythmic buckling of the purple moss-flats and the occasional, terrifying geyser of black mud that erupts when a Wyrm’s internal pressure exceeds the weight of the plateau.

### The Echo-Mimics (The Refracted Ones)
Perhaps the most unsettling inhabitants are the **Echo-Mimics**. Due to the spatial looping and the hall-of-mirrors effect of the light, the ecosystem has produced a predator that lives within the glitch. 

The Echo-Mimics are tall, spindly, and draped in shaggy, water-logged moss that perfectly matches the texture of a dormant Piston-Oak. They do not move when you look at them; they only move when the light refracts, stepping through the "loops" in space. They don't hunt by scent or sight, but by stillness. They wait for a traveler or a wayward Valvular to become disoriented by a gravity-anomaly. When the victim sees their own back in the distance—a common occurrence in the Sump—the Echo-Mimic steps into the gap between the original and the reflection. They are the "stutter" in the rhythm, the silence between the hammer-blows.

### The Bell-Ringers (The Sky-Ascetics)
Finally, there are the **Bell-Ringers**. They are not native to the Sump, but are the descendants of those who climbed too high and could no longer remember the way down. They live high up, lashed to the **Wind-Vines** that hang from the clouds. 

Their bodies have become elongated, their skin the color of bruised plums, their bones replaced by flexible, cartilage-like tubing that can withstand the whipping turbulence of the sky-currents. They wear suits made of woven Wind-Vine fibers that whistle as the air rushes through them. The Bell-Ringers consider the Piston-Oaks to be holy metronomes, and they spend their lives tending to the vines, untangling them after storms, and singing in perfect counterpoint to the deafening roar of the ground. They are the only beings here that possess a will, yet their will is entirely subverted to the maintenance of the noise. 

In the High Sump, to be alive is to be a note in a song that has no end, played by a factory that has no owner, in a cold that never thaws.
