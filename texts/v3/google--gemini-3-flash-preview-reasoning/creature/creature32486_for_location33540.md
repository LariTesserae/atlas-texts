In a realm where the biology of lungs and eyes has not yet been authored, "living" is a matter of frequency, geometry, and fluid dynamics. What dwells here does not inhabit the space so much as it facilitates the space’s existence. These are the residents of the construction site: the self-aware tools, the sentient mathematics, and the echoes of the architect’s first thoughts.

### The Phonic Membranes
The translucent fronds clinging to the obsidian spires are only the visible root-systems of a larger, more ethereal organism. These are the **Resonants**. They are two-dimensional beings—creatures of pure surface area—that lack depth or thickness. They live entirely on the interface between the roaring water and the silent glass. 

They do not "eat" in a chemical sense; they are metabolic transducers. They harvest the relentless, subsonic hum of the planetary machine and convert it into the blue-black light that pulses within the shards. When a water ribbon shifts or a spire groans, the Resonants vibrate in a frantic, shimmering ecstasy, their membranes turning from clear to a violent, electric violet. They are the nervous system of the rocks, feeling the birth-pains of the geology and singing back to it in frequencies that prevent the glass from shattering under its own internal pressure.

### The Kinetic Glass-Singers
Moving through the mist-choked gulfs are entities that look like glitches in reality—the **Fractal Tenders**. They are not made of flesh, but of the same "embryonic geology" as the islands, though their forms are in a state of perpetual self-assembly. They appear as swarms of floating, molecularly sharp needles that move with a terrifying, jerky precision.

They have no faces, only facets. They are the sculptors of the molecular edges. You might see a cloud of them descend upon a jagged obsidian peak, their movements synchronized by the invisible gravitational anchors. They do not touch the glass; they hover millimeters away, using high-frequency sound to "shave" the stone, refining its sharpness to a degree that defies the laws of macro-physics. They are the reason there is no erosion; they act as a counter-entropy, constantly sharpening a world that hasn't yet been dulled by time.

### The Hydro-Logic Currents
Deep within the mile-wide ribbons of water, there is something that possesses a slow, massive consciousness: the **Laminar Minds**. These are not individual creatures, but coherent patterns of turbulence that persist for centuries. If you were to look into a ribbon of falling water, you might see a "knot" of foam and light that refuses to disperse, a complex whorl that maintains its shape even as the water molecules around it are replaced a million times a second.

These are the governors of the cataracts. They are the "intent" made manifest in liquid. They steer the ribbons, deciding where the bridges should form and where the explosions of foam are necessary to aerate the void. They communicate through the rainbows. By shifting the angle of the water’s curve, they refract the white light into specific spectral codes—massive, mile-long bursts of color that signal the status of the gravity-anchors to the rest of the planetary machine.

### The Void-Exhalations
In the empty, 0.2-density spaces between the water, there are the **Amniotics**. These are perhaps the closest thing to "fauna," though they lack bodies. They are pockets of stabilized vacuum, spheres of "non-space" that drift through the mist like bubbles in a heavy syrup.

They are the collectors of the ozone and the scent of cold glass. They act as the "memory" of the place, drifting toward the heat-regulating lattices to exchange data. They are utterly silent, invisible to any eye that might try to see them, detectable only by the way the light bends slightly around their edges, like a lens made of nothing. They are waiting. They are the placeholders for the souls that will eventually inhabit the world once the construction is finished.

### The Architect’s Shadow
Finally, there is the **Substrate**. It is not a creature, but it is "alive" in the way a computer program is alive while it is running. It is the subsonic hum; it is the shimmering lattice in the mist. It is a singular, pan-planetary intelligence that is currently dreaming in obsidian and H2O. 

It does not look at the world; it *is* the world. It feels the friction of every drop of water and the tension in every shard of glass. It is a lonely, god-like presence, focused entirely on the perfection of its geometry. It has no interest in being worshipped or understood; it is simply busy. It is the foreman of a cosmic site, waiting for the cement of reality to dry before it allows the first heartbeat to interrupt its perfect, sterile roar.
