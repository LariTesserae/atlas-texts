The inhabitants of this weeping basin are not merely occupants of the landscape; they are its biological extensions, shaped by the relentless gravity and the shivering, low-frequency roar of the earth.

### The Lithen (The Thinking Presence)
The builders of the circular walls are the *Lithen*, a species that has surrendered the vanity of sight for the profound intimacy of touch and resonance. Standing no taller than a man’s waist, they are broad-shouldered and heavy-set, their bodies possessing a slumped, liquid grace. Their skin is a translucent, rubbery hide, the color of a clouded pearl, through which the slow pulse of violet-tinted blood can be seen. 

They lack faces in the traditional sense. Where a brow and eyes might be, there is only a smooth, sloping expanse of sensory skin, extraordinarily sensitive to the barometric pressure of the heavy sky. Their mouths are vertical slits, used less for speech and more for the intake of the mineral-rich water they draw through porous, baleen-like structures. 

The Lithen move with a strange, four-legged gait, their limbs ending in wide, suction-padded discs that allow them to traverse the slick, matte stone without slipping. They do not speak; they *hum*. By vibrating their hollow chest cavities, they create localized standing waves that interact with the wind-flutes of the rocks. To a Lithen, the landscape is not a visual map but a tactile symphony. The monoliths they carve are "songs" etched into stone—by following the grooves with their sensitive pads, they can "read" the history of a flood or the location of a salt-vein through the specific frequency the wind makes as it passes over the carvings.

### The Ouroboreals (The Great Grazers)
The caravans that cross the flats are composed of the *Ouroboreals*. These are mountain-flesh giants, six-legged and barrel-chested, their skin so thick and encrusted with mineral deposits that they often appear to be moving ridges of charcoal-grey stone. They are the primary harvesters of the "mineral sweat." 

Their tongues are their most remarkable feature—ribbons of muscle three feet long, coated in a sandpaper-like grit of embedded garnets. They spend hours in a meditative trance, rasping away at the weeping stones, thinning the rock over centuries. They are the reason the basin is so smooth; they are the living polishers of the world. Because they are blind, they navigate by leaning into the low-frequency thrum of the earth. When an Ouroboreal dies, it does not rot in the traditional sense. Instead, the mineral-heavy water calcifies its carcass, turning the beast into a new obsidian mound for the Lithen to eventualy harvest.

### The Vellum-Sails (The Flyers)
High in the bruised lilac clouds, the *Vellum-Sails* drift. They are creatures of pure buoyancy, their bodies consisting of gas-filled bladders draped in vast, translucent membranes that catch the thick, wet thermals. They never touch the ground; to do so would be to be crushed by the very gravity that defines the basin.

They are the hunters of the upper atmosphere, trailing long, stinging filaments—like man-o'-war tentacles—that dip into the shallow sheets of water on the ground to snag the scuttling life below. Their whistles are not melodic but functional, acting as sonar to map the density of the clouds. When they whistle in unison, the sound is like a thousand glass flutes breaking at once, a sharp, crystalline counterpoint to the deep, tectonic roar of the ground.

### The Skitter-Lace (The Scavengers)
In the inch-deep sheets of water, the *Skitter-Lace* move in frantic, geometric patterns. These are multi-legged arthropods with bodies like hammered silver. They have no internal skeletons; their structure is maintained by the high salt content of the water they inhabit. They feed on the "wake-bloom"—the microscopic fungal spores that flourish momentarily in the footprints of the Ouroboreals. 

The Skitter-Lace are the primary food source for the Lithen, who trap them by building the low, unmortared walls. The walls aren't for defense, but to disrupt the flow of the water sheets, creating artificial eddies where the Skitter-Lace become trapped in the swirling silt.

### The Membrane-Kin (The Fungal Base)
Beneath everything lies the *Membrane-Kin*. These are the pale, pulsing sheets that inhabit the crevices. They are the connective tissue of the basin. They do not just feed on minerals; they process the vibrations of the world. It is whispered in the humming songs of the Lithen that the Membrane-Kin are a single organism, a planet-wide carpet of nerves that feels the weight of every drop of water and every footstep of a grazer. They provide the dull phosphorescence, a cold, sour light that allows the Vellum-Sails to see the heat signatures of the world below.

In this place, life does not struggle against the environment. It has become a partner to the erosion. The inhabitants are the instruments through which the basin expresses its final, slow-motion descent into stillness.
