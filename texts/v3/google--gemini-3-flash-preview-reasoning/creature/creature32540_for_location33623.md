Beneath the steady, unblinking glare of the amber filaments, life in the Cistern does not thrive so much as it *persists* in a state of high-frequency agitation. The inhabitants are divided between the mindless, swarming crustacea of the floors and the silent, heavy-limbed stewards who dwell within the cast-iron hangars.

### The Pale Shingle-Backs
The carpet of chitin is composed of a single, highly specialized species: the **Pale Shingle-Back**. These creatures are the size of dinner plates, their shells a translucent, milky white that looks like unglazed porcelain. They lack eyes entirely, possessing instead a pair of long, vibrating antennae that constantly sweep the iron floor to map the micro-vibrations of the great pistons.

They do not hunt. They do not fight. They move in a state of frantic, rhythmic cooperation, their billions of legs creating a sound like a rainstorm on a tin roof. They are the "lungs" of the basin’s surface. Their undersides are lined with thousands of microscopic cilia that act as sponges, soaking up the thin film of mineral-heavy condensation. As they scuttle, they act as a living polish; their constant movement is what has worn the iron plates into obsidian-smooth steps and softened the edges of the hangars. 

They are born from the deep crevices between the oxidized plates, emerging fully formed and hard-shelled, and they die the same way—simply stopping mid-stride, their bodies becoming just another calcified bump on the metal floor until their neighbors grind them down into fine white powder.

### The Valve-Keepers
Inside the squat, cast-iron hangars live the **Valve-Keepers**. If the Shingle-Backs are the fauna, the Keepers are the "citizens." They are tall, narrow beings wrapped in heavy, oil-slicked rags and reinforced with copper plating that has been bolted directly to their skin. It is impossible to tell where the biology ends and the machinery begins. 

They do not speak. The deafening roar of the pistons has long since rendered hearing obsolete; instead, they communicate through a complex language of tactile taps against the resonant pipes. A Keeper might stand for forty years at a single junction, its hand resting on a copper valve, waiting for a vibration that signals a microscopic shift in pressure. 

Their lives are defined by a liturgy of maintenance. They carry heavy, long-necked oil cans and bundles of copper wire, moving with a heavy, swinging gait that mirrors the rise and fall of the pistons. They do not eat organic matter; like the Shingle-Backs, they are parasitic to the Great Reciprocator. They plug themselves into the insulated cabling of the town-core to sleep, drawing current directly into their marrow to sustain their slow-beating hearts.

### The Piston-Birds
In the high, shadowed rafters of the hangars and atop the frames of the massive pistons, there live the **Piston-Birds**. They are not birds in any biological sense, but rather winged scavengers of kinetic energy. Their wings are stiff, leathery membranes stretched over brass-like ribbing. 

They do not fly for travel; they "leap" into the path of the venting steam. As the pressurized gas hisses from the valves, the Piston-Birds spread their wings to catch the force, using the heat and pressure to recharge the tension in their coiled-spring joints. They are the only creatures in the Cistern that ever leave the ground, hanging upside down from the copper pipes like heavy, metallic fruit, their copper-filigreed eyes reflecting the steady yellow glow of the overhead lights.

### The Equilibrium
There is no "predator" in the Cistern of the Great Reciprocator, because there is no "prey." There is only the Machine and the things that serve it. The Shingle-Backs clean the floors; the Valve-Keepers tune the pressure; the Piston-Birds harvest the waste-heat. 

If you were to stand still long enough, the Shingle-Backs would eventually scuttle over your boots, their porcelain shells clicking against your laces. They would not bite you, but they would scrape the salt from your skin with their frantic, vibrating mouthparts. The Valve-Keepers would walk past you without a glance, their copper-shod feet heavy on the iron, seeing you not as a person, but as a temporary, un-calibrated vibration in a world that has already achieved its final, perfect rhythm. 

In the Cistern, life is a gear that never wears out, turning in a clock that never needs winding.
