The inhabitants of the Endo-Arbor, known to themselves as the **Lignin-Scribes**, are as dry and deliberate as the world they curate. They are tall, spindly beings with limbs that possess the segmented grace of bamboo and skin the color of aged vellum. They do not sweat; they do not weep. Their respiratory systems are elaborate filtration arrays, their chests broad and rigid to house the delicate bellows that extract nutrients from the suspended silver silt. When they move, they do not produce the soft friction of flesh, but a faint, rhythmic *clack-slide*—the sound of polished wood meeting polished wood.

At the top of the social strata are the **Graft-Architects**. They are the visionaries who live in the upper honeycombs, wearing robes of spun spider-silk stiffened with amber resins. They are rarely seen without their "cautery-lances"—long, copper-tipped tools used to direct the flow of sap in the emerging zones. To a Graft-Architect, a building is not constructed; it is *persuaded*. They spend decades whispering to a single skyscraper-sized shoot, applying chemical poultices and mechanical tension to ensure it hardens into the precise geometry of a refinery or a residence. Their eyes are multi-faceted, covered by protective chitinous lenses that allow them to see the subtle heat-signatures of the living vines pulsing within the walls.

Beneath them are the **Gear-Tenders**, the industrial heart of the city. These inhabitants are shorter, sturdier, their hands replaced at maturity with modular brass and iron sockets. They plug themselves directly into the factory walls, becoming part of the machine. They possess a symbiotic relationship with the "living pistons." They can feel the tension in a vine-sleeve as if it were their own muscle, adjusting the flow of gas and ore with a twitch of a finger. They communicate not through speech—which is swallowed by the vastness of the hollow—but through a language of percussive taps delivered against the bark, a code that travels for miles through the resonant wood.

Living on the grey moss-plains are the **Silt-Nomads**, the most reclusive of the residents. They follow the migration patterns of the six-legged **Mahogany Grazers**. These nomads wear stilts made of petrified heartwood to avoid bruising the delicate velvet moss, which they treat as a holy, living map. They are the gatherers of the "fell-harvest," scavenging the city-sized leaves that drop from the ceiling-canopy. A single leaf-fall is a generational event for them; they strip the serrated edges for saws and process the massive central veins into structural beams for the city above.

The fauna of the Endo-Arbor fills the gaps where the Scribe’s machinery falters:

*   **The Mitre-Beetles:** Large as hounds, these insects possess mandibles capable of shearing through iron-wood. They are the "janitors" of the refineries, consuming the mineral slag that accumulates in the kilns and excreting a high-density charcoal that the Scribes use as a precision fuel.
*   **The Aeolian Skimmers:** These are the "clockwork birds" mentioned in the legends. They have no feathers, only thin, vibrating membranes of chitin. They don't fly by flapping; they catch the thermal updrafts created by the amber refineries, their wings emitting a low, flute-like whistle that serves as the only music in this silent world. 
*   **The Sap-Leeches:** Translucent, slug-like organisms that cling to the "emerging" shoots. They are both a pest and a resource. While they can drain a new building of its structural sap, their bodies, once bloated and crystallized, become the raw material for the city’s "glass" windows.

In the deepest, oldest districts, where the wood has turned to something harder than diamond, dwell the **Chroniclers**. They are the oldest Lignin-Scribes, individuals who have ceased to move entirely. They have grafted their own nervous systems into the main trunks of the world-trees. They are no longer people, but living archives, their consciousnesses spread thin across the rings of the world. They feel every tectonic shift of the gears, every new sprout, and every rhythmic thrum of the bellows. 

The society is one of absolute conservation. In a world without water, there is no rot, only desiccation. Death is simply a transition from active labor to structural support. When a Scribe dies, they are not buried; they are polished, lacquered, and built into the architecture. A staircase might be held up by the petrified arms of a dozen ancestors; a refinery’s main drive-shaft might be encased in the hollowed-out remains of its first foreman. 

Everything in the Endo-Arbor—the people, the beasts, and the machines—functions as a single, dry, ticking organism, endlessly growing into the dark, fueled by the silver dust and the slow, inexorable ambition of the wood.
