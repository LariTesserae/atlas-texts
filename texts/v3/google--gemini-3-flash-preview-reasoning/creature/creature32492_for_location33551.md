To live in the Lattice is to exist as a component in a machine that never turns off. The inhabitants are not residents in the traditional sense; they are the extensions of the architecture, the moving parts of a static, gargantuan metabolism.

### The Spindle-Kin
The primary inhabitants are the **Spindle-Kin**, creatures that resemble the intersection of a crane fly and a glass-blown marionette. They are elongated, reaching three or four meters in height, yet they weigh no more than a handful of dry husks. Their limbs are not jointed with bone, but with hydraulic bladders of the same amber sap that powers the Builds. 

They do not walk; they hook. Their digits are long, recurved needles of hardened lignin, designed to slot perfectly into the cross-hatched weave of the floor-mats. To move, they don't fight gravity; they lean into the tension of the world. They spend their lives suspended from the undersides of the vine-bridges or clinging to the weeping walls of the cathedrals, their bodies vibrating in sympathetic resonance with the deep, bass thrum of the nutrient pumps.

The Spindle-Kin have no mouths. Instead, their "feet" are porous. As they stand upon the weeping walls, they capillary-feed, drawing the pressurized, sugar-rich sweat of the Lattice directly into their translucent circulatory systems. Because there is no rot and no waste, they have no digestive tracts. They are biological closed loops, as pristine and embryonic as the stalks they inhabit.

### The Vellum-Shades
Floating through the hollow valleys of the Builds are the **Vellum-Shades**—the gelatinous organisms mentioned in passing. Close up, they are revealed to be living filters. They are wide, flat ribbons of pulsing, transparent jelly, trailing long, gossamer filaments that catch the ambient moisture of the air. 

They are the Lattice’s immune system and its cleaners. While there is no decay, there is "drift"—microscopic flakes of cellulose and stray droplets of sap that escape the structural flow. The Vellum-Shades drift through the gold-lit voids, their bodies undulating like slow-motion lightning bolts, vacuuming the air. They are hyper-sensitive to the "booming cracks" of new structural growth; when a new support snaps into place, the Shades swarm toward the site of the tension, secreting a fast-acting, translucent resin to seal the microscopic fractures in the lignin before they can become flaws.

### The Pulse-Singers
Deep within the walls of the massive stalks—living inside the very thickness of the amber lignin—are the **Pulse-Singers**. These are the most enigmatic of the Lattice’s residents. They are sessile, bulbous masses of silver-grey neural tissue, encased in protective nodules of woven root. 

They do not move, and they have no external senses save for vibration. They are the conductors of the Lattice’s deafening orchestra. By rhythmically constricting the local vascular channels of the plant-towers, they direct the flow of nutrients. The "hydraulic percussion" that shakes the marrow of the bones is their language. A long, low thrum might be a command to thicken a floor-mat; a rapid, staccato beat might signal the need for a new bridge to be woven across the void. They don't think in words or images; they think in pressures, tensions, and flow rates.

### The Absence of the Individual
In this world, there is no "self." Life is a collective property of the architecture. The Spindle-Kin do not sleep; they merely enter a state of "low-tension" where they become indistinguishable from the struts they cling to. There is no competition for resources, as the resources are the very air and walls. 

There are no young and no old. Occasionally, a Spindle-Kin will simply become too rigid, its sap-joints calcifying into solid lignin. When this happens, it doesn't fall or rot. It is simply woven over. A new vine will coil around its frozen form, incorporating the creature into the wall, turning the inhabitant back into the architecture. Every "bolt" and "seam" in the Lattice was once, perhaps, a living thing that reached the end of its cycle and chose to become a permanent support.

### The Sentience of Design
The most terrifying aspect of those who live here is their lack of agency. They do not "decide" to build a bridge; the bridge is felt as a necessity in the collective nervous system of the Pulse-Singers, and the Spindle-Kin move to weave it as naturally as a human body grows a scab. 

Everything—from the razor-sharp leaf edges to the rhythmic roaring of the air—is part of a singular, driving intent: **to remain.** To stay suspended above the abyss, to stay wet, to stay golden, and to never, ever let the silence of the void below enter the roaring, humid perfection of the Lattice.
