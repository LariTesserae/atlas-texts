To live upon the Aethel-Spires is to exist as a component within a planetary engine. The inhabitants are not merely residents; they are valves, filters, lubricants, and sensors, evolved to thrive in a world that functions like a sweltering, open-air clockwork.

### The Pulse-Tenders (The High Overseers)
If the Aethel-Spires have a consciousness, it resides in the **Pulse-Tenders**. These are spindly, three-meter-tall bipeds with limbs like peeled willow branches—pallid, translucent, and surprisingly strong. They possess no eyes, for the blinding white mist renders sight a liability; instead, their heads are crowned with a fan of sensitive, vibrating filaments that "read" the rhythmic thrum of the Vascular Pistons.

They move with a slow, agonizing grace, their skin perpetually slicked by the weeping stone. A Pulse-Tender does not eat in the traditional sense. Instead, they possess specialized, needle-like digits that they insert directly into the translucent conduits. They "drink" the high-pressure mineral slurry, filtering out the heavy metals that their own biology requires to maintain their rigid, metallic skeletal structures. They are the tuners of the spires; when a piston’s rhythm falters, a Tender will spend days pressed against the chitinous hull, humming at a specific frequency to stimulate the biological engine back into its proper cycle.

### The Siphon-Hulks
Clinging to the vertical faces of the spires are the **Siphon-Hulks**. If the Pulse-Tenders are the nervous system, the Hulks are the heavy maintenance. They resemble a cross between a massive tardigrade and a smooth boulder. Their shells are indistinguishable from the red-stone plateaus, often encrusted with the same thick, purple moss.

They possess terrifyingly powerful, vacuum-like mouths. Their primary role is to clear the "sludge"—the buildup of calcified minerals that threatens to clog the weeping walls. They move centimeters per hour, their multi-jointed legs ending in diamond-hard claws that can pierce the stone to anchor them against the weight of the vertical rain. In the rare moments when a steam vent erupts nearby, the Hulks extend their ribbed underbellies to absorb the thermal energy, glowing a dull, furnace-red as they store the heat.

### The Conduit-Eels (Flow-Wraiths)
Inside the massive, vine-like conduits that vein the plateaus live the **Conduit-Eels**. These are long, ribbon-like organisms that never see the sun. They spend their entire lives within the rushing, mineral-rich fluids, swimming against the high-pressure current. 

Their bodies are bioluminescent, pulsing with the same amber light as the exterior vines. They serve as the "cleaning crew" of the internal plumbing, their abrasive scales scrubbing the inner walls of the conduits to prevent mineral buildup. When a conduit is breached, these eels swarm to the wound, their quick-coagulating blood acting as a biological solder that seals the leak within seconds.

### The Steam-Dancers
High above the pistons, navigating the lethal geysers, are the **Steam-Dancers**. These are not the hawk-sized insects, but a smaller, more delicate caste of avian-reptiles. They have hollow, gossamer-thin bones and wings made of a heat-resistant, leathery membrane. 

They live for the "venting." When a Vascular Piston screams its plume of pressurized steam, the Dancers dive into the scalding cloud. They don't burn; their scales are coated in a layer of specialized, cooling mucus that flash-evaporates, protecting them while they hunt the six-winged dragonflies that are momentarily stunned by the heat. They are the only creatures that seem to exhibit joy, tumbling through the white geysers in a chaotic, screeching ballet.

### The Moss-Mind (The Carpet)
The three-foot-thick mats of purple moss are more than just vegetation; they are a collective, semi-sentient sponge. The moss acts as a thermal regulator for the entire plateau. It feels like warm, wet velvet to the touch, but beneath the surface, it is a dense web of neurons. 

The moss senses the approach of the heavy rains before they fall. As the pressure changes, the moss mats contract, squeezing their stored water into the basins to prime the pumps. The entire ecology is tethered to this carpet; the Tenders walk upon it, the Hulks sleep within it, and the Amber Vines draw their light from the chemical signals the moss sends through the soil.

### The Silence of the Inhabitants
None of these creatures make much sound. In a world defined by the low-frequency drone of the earth’s own machinery, vocalization is redundant. Communication is tactile and vibrational. They speak in the language of the Spires—a rhythmic tapping on the stone, a subtle change in the heat of a limb, a flickering of amber light. 

To live here is to be part of the rhythm. There is no hunger that the stone does not feed, no cold that the steam does not warm, and no purpose other than the eternal, wet, pulsing survival of the machine-mountain.
