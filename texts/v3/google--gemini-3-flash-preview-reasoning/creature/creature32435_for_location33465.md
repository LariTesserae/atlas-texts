In the **Caul of Zephyr-And-Ash**, life is not a slow unfolding; it is a high-speed collision between biology and geology. To live here is to be a part of the friction.

### The Vell (The Silt-Dwellers)
The primary residents of the Caul, the **Vell**, are a people shaped by the absence of shadow and the abundance of sound. Because the golden glare of the ionized ceiling never dims, their skin has adapted into a semi-translucent, opalescent membrane that reflects the harsh light rather than absorbing it. They appear as shimmering, wet ghosts moving through the haze.

They are hairless and lean, their bodies composed of ropey, hyper-oxygenated muscle designed for sudden bursts of movement. Their fingers are long and tipped with hardened, spatulate nails—perfect for prying salt-crusts from the mineral spires or clinging to glass cliffs during a sweat-tide. Most striking are their eyes: large, lidless, and shielded by a secondary transparent film to protect against the constant drift of mineral dust. 

Socially, the Vell are a collective of frantic synchronicity. In a world with no privacy, they have developed a "haptic language." They rarely speak—the air is too loud for nuance—and instead communicate through rhythmic drumming on the resonant rock or by the rapid, expressive twitching of their iridescent skin-pigments. Their lives are short and blazing, governed by the **Gestation Shifts**. When the earth groans and the floor buckles, the Vell do not flee; they dance atop the shifting plates, harvesting the raw minerals extruded by the new pressure.

### The Aether-Wrights (The Swarm)
The hawk-sized insects described as a "weather system" are the **Aether-Wrights**. They do not possess internal skeletons; their structure is maintained by the high-pressure, humid atmosphere. Their wings are not feathers or membrane, but sheets of solidified gas that vibrate at a frequency so high it creates a secondary golden glow around them.

They are the primary pollinators of this mineral world, though they carry dust instead of pollen. They feed on the ionized gas of the ceiling, diving down to the mineral spires to scrape their bellies against the sulfur, creating sparks that ignite tiny, controlled explosions of energy they use to propel themselves back into the heights. A single Aether-Wright is beautiful; a billion of them is a screaming, golden blizzard that can strip the moisture from a traveler’s throat in seconds.

### The Crystalline Lithovores (The Grinders)
Beneath the fluttering clouds of the Wrights lie the **Grinders**. These segmented, quartz-encrusted behemoths are the size of boulders and possess the intellect of a landslide. They move on thousands of glass-like cilia that vibrate in unison, creating the "hailstorm on a tin roof" sound. 

The Grinders are the great recyclers. They consume the fragile manganese and sulfur spires, processing the minerals in gut-furnaces that burn with internal chemical heat. Their waste is a hot, liquid slurry that the Vell harvest to build their aerodynamic huts. The Grinders are indifferent to other life; they will roll over a Vell dwelling as readily as a rock formation, forcing the inhabitants to live in a state of constant, architectural nomadism.

### The Apex: The Shudder-Whelps
Hidden within the deeper rifts, where the golden light turns to a bruised purple, are the **Shudder-Whelps**. These are the Caul’s apex predators—creatures of pure, predatory vibration. They have no eyes, for the haze is too thick for long-distance sight. Instead, they possess massive, sail-like ears that detect the heartbeat of a Vell or the clicking of a Grinder from miles away.

A Shudder-Whelp is a mass of pale, wet muscle and obsidian teeth. They do not hunt by stealth, but by resonance. They emit a low-frequency growl that matches the resonant frequency of the rock beneath their prey, momentarily liquefying the "soil" or shattering the glass-ledges to bring their meal tumbling down.

### The Micro-Ecology: The Glair
Everything in the Caul is coated in **The Glair**. It is not a fungus or a moss, but a colonial bacterial film that thrives on the "sweat-tides." It is the Glair that makes the obsidian slopes slick and gives the air its silken, clinging quality. 

The Glair is the memory of the Caul. It consumes the fallen—the snapped wings of a Wright, the shell of a dead Grinder, the bone-scrapers of a Vell—and breaks them down into elemental dust within hours. Because of the Glair, there are no fossils in the Caul, and no ruins. There is only the *now*, a frantic, glittering moment of survival held between the crushing weight of the ceiling and the rising heat of the core.
