In the Chlorophyll Basin, the inhabitant and the habitat are a distinction without a difference. There are no residents in the way a city has residents; there are only **Specialized Expressions**. To live here is to be a temporary organ of the sprawl, a localized spasm of the green will. 

Here are the things that move within the tectonic roar:

### The Lignin-Logicians
These are the closest the Basin comes to "citizens," though they are stationary and massive. They are the nodes of the bureaucracy-vines—swollen, polyhedral junctions where the translucent jade stalks meet. Inside these nodes, the sap doesn't just flow; it computes. The pressure is so high that the fluid becomes a liquid crystal, processing the geometric needs of the surrounding sectors. The Logicians "think" in angles and stress-points. They are the ones who decide when a bridge must snap or a plaza must be razed. They communicate through subsonic vibrations—the "grinding symphony" you hear is actually the city debating its own layout.

### The Tumble-Nodes (The Kinetic Packets)
While there is no traditional fauna, the Basin requires a way to move concentrated enzymes across its non-Euclidean streets. The Tumble-Nodes are spherical, tightly wound balls of high-tensile wicker. They are not alive in their own right, but are "excreted" by one sector and pulled toward another by shifting gravitational gradients or vascular suction. They careen through the jade corridors at terrifying speeds, bouncing off the razor-edged walls, carrying the chemical blueprints for the next hour’s growth. To an outsider, they look like frantic, rolling tumbleweeds, but they are actually the city’s high-speed data packets.

### The Photic Wraiths
In the "optical shadows," where the light has been consumed by the canopy above, lives a parasitic class of plant-life that has abandoned photosynthesis entirely. These are the Photic Wraiths—wafer-thin, pitch-black sheets of velvet-like tissue that cling to the sides of the skyscraper-vines. They do not eat light; they eat *heat*. They wrap themselves around the friction-points of the city, absorbing the thermal energy generated by the violent growth of the stalks. They are the only things in the Basin that are silent. They move by slowly dissolving their own trailing edges and regrowing their leading ones, sliding like ink stains across the jade glass.

### The Anthromorph-Weeds (The Mimic-Statues)
The most unsettling inhabitants are found in the empty plazas. The Basin, in its mindless attempt to optimize "urban-ness," occasionally produces Anthromorph-Weeds. These are stationary, life-sized growths that accidentally—or perhaps through some deep, biological mimicry of an ancient, forgotten blueprint—resemble figures. They are gnarled, bipedal shapes of compressed cellulose, standing in rows or sitting on the tiered moss benches. They have no faces, only the rough texture of calcified bark, but they stand perfectly still as the city screams around them, serving as "stress-testers" for the wind and the humidity.

### The Vascular Storm-Leeches
When a high-pressure trunk ruptures, the "leech" forms instantly. It is a localized, hyper-accelerated growth spurt—a frantic tangle of hair-like white roots that erupts from the ground to catch the spraying sap before it hits the pavement. They exist for mere minutes, a frantic, thrashing frenzy of white fluff, drinking their fill of the sugary mist. Once the rupture is sealed by the parent plant, the leeches dehydrate and turn to dust in seconds, leaving behind nothing but a dry, woody husk that the next Tumble-Node will crush to powder.

### The Soul of the Basin (The Singular)
Ultimately, you are looking at a singular entity with a trillion limbs. The "inhabitant" is the **Pressure**. It is the drive of the cell to occupy all available space. If you were to stay here for more than an hour, the Basin would recognize you not as a guest, but as a vacancy. Tiny, microscopic spores would begin to lace through your clothes, not to eat you, but to *incorporate* you. You would feel your skin hardening into bark, your blood thickening into sap, and your thoughts slowing down into the long, geometric considerations of a skyscraper-vine. 

In the Chlorophyll Basin, you don't live in the city. You become the city, screaming your way into the sky, one snapping cell at a time.
