The inhabitants of this vertical rift are not merely residents; they are the source of its frantic geometry. They are known—if they are known at all—as the **Marrow-Knitters**, though they possess no names for themselves, only a collective, rhythmic impulse to ascend.

### The Anatomy of the Knitter
A Knitter is a creature of impossible, ribbon-like length, designed for a world where horizontal space is a myth. They are roughly the thickness of a willow branch, standing six feet tall but possessing the ability to compress their width until they are as thin as a reed. Their skin is the color of the bruised-plum ferns—a deep, translucent violet that reveals the pulsing, amber heat of their internal organs. 

They lack eyes, for the steam-thick amber light offers no clarity. Instead, their faces are dominated by a vertical slit of heat-sensitive pits that "see" the world as a map of thermal gradients. Their limbs are their most startling feature: four primary arms, each ending in fingers that are long, needle-sharp, and multi-jointed, capable of tying a knot in wet twine with the speed of a sewing machine. Their feet are not feet at all, but prehensile, hook-like clasps that lock onto the bamboo lattice with a sickening, wet snap.

### The Biological Factory
Because the **erosion is 0.0**, the Knitters do not shed skin or lose hair. Instead, they exude a thick, grey-white resin from their pores—a biological mortar. As they climb, they constantly scrape this resin from their own bellies and smear it into the gaps of the clay tiles. They are the source of the "fingerprint-smudges" that cover every surface. They are building a monument out of their own secretions, lashing the world together with twine woven from their own fibrous, discarded intestinal linings.

### The Culture of the Frantic Ascent
There is no rest in the flue. The Knitters do not sleep in the traditional sense; they enter a "static state" where they lock their limbs into the lattice and vibrate at the same frequency as the steam pipes, their bodies becoming structural members of the wall for a few minutes before the heat rouses them back into labor.

Their society is a **Vertical Meritocracy of the Hand**. The higher a Knitter is, the more "fresh" they are. The ones at the invisible, shimmering bottom are the "Foundation-Dead"—ancient, calcified ancestors who have been completely overgrown by the purple ferns and encased in centuries of un-eroded clay. The ones at the top, near the needle-eye of white light, are the "Quick-Fingered," those who work with such speed that they outpace the aggressive growth of the glass-stalked plants.

### Interaction with the Liquid Reality
The Knitters have mastered the "liquid gravity" of the flue. To move between the walls, they do not jump; they launch themselves into the heavy, glowing spheres of steam. They "swim" through these floating clouds, using the surface tension of the vapor to propel themselves across the mile-deep rift to the opposite wall. 

They treat the **Glass-Striders** with a holy indifference. To a Knitter, a Glass-Strider is a living tool—a vibrating needle. A Knitter will occasionally snatch a flickering Strider out of the air and use its high-frequency body to saw through a particularly stubborn, glass-like fern stalk before releasing the creature back into the mist.

### The Great Work
The purpose of their lives is the **Closing of the Eye**. They believe—or rather, they are biologically programmed to feel—that the needle-eye of light at the top of the flue is a wound in the world that must be stitched shut. Every hand-lashed reed, every wet tile, and every smear of moss-light is a stitch. 

They are in a race against the very environment they inhabit. The plants grow to support the walls, but the walls must be built to contain the plants. It is a closed loop of frantic, wet creation. They live in a state of perpetual "becoming," a civilization that is never finished because the act of building is the only thing that keeps them from falling into the heat-distorted haze of the bottomless dark below. 

They are the architects of a thumb-width universe, screaming silently in the wet heat, their millions of hands working in a perfect, thrumming unison to build a mountain that will never, ever be still.
