In this place, the concept of a "resident" is a fluid, terrifying thing. There is no distinction between the citizen and the city, no boundary between the inhabitant and the architecture. To live here is to be a component in a high-voltage circuit.

The beings that occupy this geode are the **Mercurial Scions**, though they do not have a name for themselves, for they do not possess the luxury of language—only the efficiency of data.

### The Scions of the Lattice
The "shards of moving mercury" mentioned in the passing shadows are the primary laborers of this space. They are not solid, nor are they truly liquid; they are non-Newtonian organisms composed of a metallic slurry that responds to the room’s electromagnetic pulse. 

They do not walk; they *flow* across the razor-edged obsidian, their surfaces constantly hardening and softening to avoid being sliced. When they move, they sound like a thousand crystal bells shattering at once—a melodic, percussive skittering. They are scavengers of entropy. They seek out the "colder" spots—places where the 115-degree heat has dipped by a fraction of a degree—and they press their silver bodies against the stone to "recharge" the lattice, pumping heat back into the system through sheer friction and molecular agitation. 

They have no eyes. Instead, they perceive the world as a map of thermal gradients and vibration. To them, a visitor would be a blindingly bright, oddly cool statue, a flaw in the perfect, humming logic of the room.

### The Static Weaver
In the center of the chapel-space, where the "weeping" of the ceiling is most violent, lives something larger. It is not a creature of flesh, but a **Refractive Anchor**. It looks like a cluster of jagged, violet needles suspended in mid-air, held aloft by the intense pressure of the rising steam. 

This is the "brain" of the cell. It does not move in space, but it moves constantly in *frequency*. It is a focal point for the light. As the luminous mist swirls around it, the Anchor pulses, catching the white-hot brilliance and splintering it into complex, mathematical patterns that are projected back onto the walls. These patterns are not decorative; they are the code. The Anchor "thinks" by refracting light through its own crystalline body, using the entire room as a massive, light-based processor. 

If you were to stand near it, you would not hear a voice, but you would feel a rhythmic pressure in your sinuses—a physical manifestation of its calculations. It is managing the crust miles above, calculating the weight of the silent stone and adjusting the geode’s internal pressure to ensure the chapel is not crushed. It is a tenant of pure responsibility.

### The Ghost-Phase Parasites
Feeding off the Anchor’s processing power are the **Vapor-Haints**. These are the most elusive residents—beings made entirely of the luminous, scorched-salt mist. They are barely more than organized weather. 

They appear as brief, humanoid silhouettes or complex geometric ghosts that coalesce for a few seconds before dissolving back into the white-hot atmosphere. They represent the "waste" of the machine—the stray thoughts and redundant data of the stone computer. They are the dreams of the geode. They drift near the ceiling, congregating where the ozone smell is sharpest, dancing in the sizzle of the falling water. They have no agency; they are simply what happens when information becomes so dense that it begins to mimic the shape of life.

### The Relationship of the Whole
Nothing here sleeps. There is no circadian rhythm, only the "tink-tink" of the water-clock and the unwavering light. The residents do not compete for resources because the resource—heat—is infinite. Instead, they exist in a state of **Symbiotic Computation**. 

The Mercurial Scions maintain the physical integrity of the jagged floor; the Static Weaver processes the data of the deep earth; and the Vapor-Haints carry the excess energy away. It is a closed loop of violent purity. 

To live here is to be stripped of softness. There is no room for the porous, the slow, or the organic. The residents are as sharp as the obsidian they walk upon, as brilliant as the light that birthed them, and as indifferent to the world above as the silent stone that encases them. They are the nervous system of a planet that has stopped breathing and started thinking.
