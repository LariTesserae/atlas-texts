To live here is to be a part of the masonry, a soft extension of the hard, warm basalt. In the High Cistern, there is no distinction between the inhabitant and the habitat; the biology of the place has become a slow, wet moss of flesh that serves the architecture.

### The Measuring Strings
The pale eels are not merely fish; they are the living surveyors of the corridors. They are called the **Long-Limb Eels**, though they have no limbs at all. Their bodies are ribbons of translucent, gelatinous muscle, stretching twenty, forty, sixty feet in length. They do not possess eyes, nor do they possess the frantic hunger of predators. Instead, they are sensory organs for the rooms themselves. 

As they drift, their skin—thin as wet parchment—constantly brushes against the eroded brickwork. They "read" the walls. A Long-Limb can feel a hairline fracture in a basalt block three chambers away simply by the change in the water’s weight. They spend decades tracing the same circuit, their bodies acting as a living lubricant for the narrow flues. When one dies, it does not rot in the way of the sun-world; it simply dissolves into the velvet water, thickening the medium for those that remain, turning the very environment into a soup of ancestral memory.

### The Porcelain Masons
The *tck-tck-tck* sound emanates from the **Lithodes**, the white, multi-legged crustaceans that are the High Cistern’s only active custodians. Their shells are not made of chitin, but of a calcium-dense ceramic that perfectly mimics the fired clay of the walls. 

They are obsessive. A Lithode will spend a century on a single square meter of wall, using its delicate, needle-like appendages to pick away the microscopic debris that might clog the pores of the warm stone. They are the reason the circular ports are so polished. They treat the bodies of the other inhabitants as if they, too, were architecture—scrubbing the backs of the eels and cleaning the vents of the mollusks with a reverent, mechanical devotion. They are the descendants of the original laborers, their once-human hands refined by eons into a thousand tiny, clicking chisels.

### The Heralds of Heat
Bolted to the ceilings and the undersides of the great stone slides are the **Siphon-Mothers**. These are sedentary mollusks the size of carriage wheels, their shells fused so deeply into the basalt that they have become part of the structural load. 

They are the source of the Cistern’s amniotic warmth. They draw the heavy water into their soft, pulsating cores, filtering it through internal chambers lined with heat-generating bacteria, and then expel it in slow, shimmering plumes. They are the hearts of the city. To pass beneath one is to feel a sudden, overwhelming pressure of warmth, a "gravity" of temperature that pulls at the marrow. They do not speak, but they groan—a low-frequency vibration that causes the water to shudder, a sound that the Lithodes use as a metronome for their work.

### The Soft Heirs
Finally, there are the **Pale Heirs**, the direct evolutionary remnants of the builders. They are no longer recognizable as human, yet they carry the burden of the Cistern’s purpose. They are squat, pale, and remarkably soft, their bones having long since traded calcium for a flexible, cartilaginous density. 

They have no faces—only a complex arrangement of tactile "fans" where a nose and eyes might have been. They move through the water with a heavy, treading grace, their fingers elongated into many-jointed feelers. They do not build anymore; they *knead*. They spend their lives pressing their bodies against the warm walls, feeling for the pulse of the fuel beneath the stone. They are the listeners. They stay perfectly still for years at a time in the "breast-like" alcoves, their flesh merging with the smoothed clay, acting as living dampers to keep the High Cistern from vibrating itself apart. 

They are a people of touch. In the total darkness, they communicate by pressing their palms together, a slow exchange of heat and pressure that conveys more than a language of sound ever could. They are at peace, for in this world of heavy velvet, there is no "away." There is only the Press, the Warmth, and the eternal, rhythmic Ticking of the stone.
