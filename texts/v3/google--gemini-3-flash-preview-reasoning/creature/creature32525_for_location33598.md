The inhabitants of the Vexed Nursery are not "born" so much as they are **extricated**. 

They are the **Pulp-Wrights**, a caste of post-biological tenders who have long since forgotten the texture of dry wind or the concept of a shadow. To look at a Pulp-Wright is to look at a graft that has taken too well. Their skin has the matte, grayish-purple hue of a bruised plum, slick with the same protective oils that coat the copper wires above. They do not wear clothing; instead, their bodies are draped in "living cowls"—parasitic sheets of velvet-moss that provide them with a slow, capillary feed of nutrients and dampen the deafening thrum of the beetle-wings.

### The Physiology of the Tenders
The Pulp-Wrights have undergone a specialized evolution to survive the Nursery’s "thick soup" air. Their ribcages have flared outward, becoming wide and bellows-like to process the heavy cellulose vapor. They lack traditional ears; instead, they have rows of sensitive, feathery cilia along their jawlines and temples that pick up the subsonic vibrations of the machinery. They can "hear" a piston failing long before the sound reaches the air.

Their hands are their most specialized tools. The fingers are elongated, tipped with hooked, obsidian-like nails for climbing the tension cables. The palms are calloused into thick, leathery pads that allow them to handle the scalding brass of the boilers without flinch. In many of the elders, the distinction between tool and limb has vanished entirely—a heavy iron wrench might be fused into the ulna, wrapped in layers of healing cambium and scar tissue until the man and the tool are a single, functional lever.

### The Huddlers (The Living Heat-Sinks)
The "thick-furred mammals" mentioned in the dark corners are the **Huddlers**. They are the Nursery’s heavy labor and its primary source of warmth during the rare moments when a kiln is extinguished for cleaning. 

The Huddlers are massive, slow-moving heaps of muscle and fat, resembling a cross between a sloth and a subterranean bear, but with six or eight limbs designed for bracing against the vibrating floor. They are nearly blind, navigating by the "click-clack" of the lizards. The Pulp-Wrights treat them with a strange, mechanical reverence. They do not hunt the Huddlers; they "dock" with them. At night, a Wright will crawl into the steaming fur of a Huddler, plugging a copper-tipped umbilical—grown from the Wright’s own nervous system—into a specialized pore on the Huddler’s spine. This allows a bidirectional exchange: the Wright receives warmth and metabolic stability, while the Huddler receives the Wright’s excess adrenaline and the "data" of the day’s work, which keeps the beast’s primitive mind stimulated.

### The Scuttlers and the Resonators
The fauna is not merely wild; it is a collaborative workforce. 
*   **The Translucent Lizards:** These are the "Electricians." Their glowing gullets aren't for display; they contain highly acidic bioluminescent bacteria that they vomit into clogged pipes to dissolve obstructions. The Pulp-Wrights guide them using low-frequency whistles, directing the lizards to scurry into the guts of the machines to clear the "vascular" blockages of the city.
*   **The Six-Winged Beetles (The Resonators):** These insects serve as the Nursery’s cooling system. When the kilns grow too hot and the copper wires begin to glow a dangerous cherry-red, the beetles gather in massive, coordinated clusters. Their collective wing-flick isn't just noise; it’s a directed sonic blast that moves the heavy, stagnant air, creating a localized wind that strips the heat from the brass.

### The Lifecycle of the Vexed
There is no death here, only **Re-incorporation**. 

When a Pulp-Wright grows too slow, or when the "Vexation"—a neurological disorder caused by the constant metallic vibration—finally shatters their mind, they do not go to a grave. They are taken to the "High Roots" at the ceiling. They are strapped into the interlocking iron-wood with galvanized bolts. 

Over the course of months, the oily rain and the hungrier vines begin to claim them. The moss grows into their open mouths; the copper wires thread through their veins. They become part of the architecture. The "silos" where the inhabitants live are actually the hollowed-out, fossilized remains of their ancestors’ collective efforts, expanded and reinforced by the very plants that consumed them. 

### The Collective Mind
The inhabitants do not speak in sentences. The noise of the Nursery is too great for syntax. Instead, they communicate in a language of **Taps and Tensions**. By striking a tension cable or rhythmic thumping on a brass floor-plate, a Wright can send a message across the entire square. 

They are a "Vexed" people because they live in a state of permanent sensory overload. They are twitchy, hyper-alert, and possessed by a frantic, industrial work ethic. They move with a jerky, mechanical grace, their eyes constantly darting toward the steam valves and the growing vines. They are the nervous system of a machine that is trying to eat itself, and they have realized—perhaps centuries ago—that the only way to keep from being digested is to stay faster, louder, and more vital than the moss. 

In the Vexed Nursery, to be still is to become a garden. To be silent is to become a gear. The inhabitants choose the gear, every single time.
