The inhabitants of the Aethel-Shelf do not so much live *upon* the stone as they exist as a further crystallization of it. They are the **Lithics**, a people whose biology has slowed to match the tectonic heartbeat of the peaks.

To look at one is to see a vertical extension of the architecture. They are small of stature, rarely taller than a child, but they possess a density that defies their size. Their skin is the exact, unvarying shade of the pale grey slate—matt, hairless, and cool—mapped with the same dark, bruised veins that appear on the rock when the fog rolls in. They do not have the soft, expressive faces of those who live in the valleys; their features are planed and geometric, as if they were finished with the same steady, ancient chisels that carved the lintels of their homes.

The scent of wet wool that permeates the air comes from their "mantles." These are not garments, but a thick, fibrous growth that erupts from their shoulders and spines—a dense, lanolin-rich fleece that functions as a biological harvester. They spend the hours of the creeping fog standing perfectly still in the narrow plazas or on the edges of the cisterns, their bodies acting as living lightning rods for the moisture. The fog clings to their mantles, condensing into heavy silver beads that the Lithics then methodically wring out into the stone gutters with slow, deliberate movements of their thick-jointed fingers.

Their eyes are their most striking feature: large, lidless orbs the color of polished flint. They do not blink. In the white, honest glare of the high-altitude sun, their pupils contract to mere pinpricks, allowing them to stare directly into the light without flinching. They do not communicate through speech—the "low, resonant hum" of the chimneys is often their own breath, modulated through the stone flues to signal the arrival of the fog or the filling of a cistern.

They move with a terrifying economy of motion. A Lithic might spend three days sitting in a "hollowed bowl" of rock, watching the play of light on a single tuft of wire-grass. When they do move, it is with a heavy, grinding grace. Their feet are broad and flat, the soles toughened into a substance indistinguishable from the mountain itself, allowing them to navigate the "vein-like" stairways without ever losing their center of gravity.

They are the architects of the "frozen moment." They do not build new things because they have achieved a state of somatic perfection; they are the curators of the equilibrium. They eat rarely, scraping the silver-green lichen from the rocks with flat, spatulate teeth, a process that takes hours and produces a sound like a distant rockslide.

There is no youth among them, nor is there obvious decay. A Lithic does not die so much as they eventually cease to move entirely. When one of their number reaches the end of their animation, they do not fall; they simply find a niche in the cliffside or a corner of a plaza and settle. Over the decades, the wind polishes them, the moisture-runs map their features, and they become indistinguishable from the hand-carved statues that decorate the hearths.

They are the silent, sweating guardians of the Aethel-Shelf. They are a race that has traded the frantic heat of ambition for the cool, eternal honesty of the stone. To encounter one is to realize that you are the fleeting shadow, the "momentary blemish," while they are the mountain’s own slow, waking dream.
