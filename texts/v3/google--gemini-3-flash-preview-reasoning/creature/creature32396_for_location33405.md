In a world where the earth is a single, interlocking work of pottery, life does not occur by chance. It occurs by design, or as a consequence of the heat. The inhabitants of the Plateau of Poured Earth are not born; they are **tempered**.

### The Luster-Walkers
The most visible inhabitants are the Luster-Walkers, tall, spindly entities that look less like biological organisms and more like high-fire porcelain scaffolding. They are impossibly thin, their limbs no thicker than a potter’s needle, yet they stand twelve feet high. Their bodies are coated in a pearlescent, iridescent glaze that reflects 99% of the sun’s 2.7-level heat, allowing them to stride across the white-hot ceramic floor without cracking.

They do not have faces. Instead, their heads are wide, shallow bowls tilted toward the zenith. They are "thermal-vores," surviving entirely on the intense radiation of the sun. During the heat of the day, they stand perfectly still, their long shadows marking the passage of hours like the gnomon of a sundial. They only move during the "pavement winds," using the hot gusts to push their lightweight frames across the glassy surface, their feet making a rhythmic *clink-clink-clink* sound, like fine china tapping against a stone floor.

### The Joint-Siphoners
Deep within the expansion joints, where the grey-green succulents grow, live the Joint-Siphoners. These are the "hidden" fauna, long and segmented like centipedes, but made of unglazed, porous terracotta. They are the color of dried blood. 

They are hydrologists by instinct. Because the water only exists for an hour at pre-dawn, the Siphoners have evolved to be incredibly absorbent. Their bodies are essentially living sponges. When the "joint-rivers" begin to flow, the Siphoners stretch themselves out in the cracks, soaking up moisture through their skin until they swell to three times their dry size. For the rest of the day, they retreat into the cool, dark recesses beneath the slabs, slowly sweating that moisture back into their systems to stay cool while the world above bakes. If you put your ear to a seam during the noon-day heat, you can hear them—a faint, wet hissing as they breathe out the morning’s stolen water.

### The Resonance-Mites
The "fauna" that the silence hides are the Resonance-Mites. These are not insects, but small, geometric shards of vitrified glass, no larger than a fingernail. They are the scavengers of the plateau. 

They do not eat organic matter; they "eat" vibration. They congregate around the stress-points of the great slabs. When the plates expand and produce those deep, tectonic pops, the Mites swarm to the site of the sound. They cling to the vibrating edges of the ceramic, their bodies humming in sympathy with the earth. They convert the kinetic energy of the "settling" world into a slow, internal glow. At night, after the sun has set and the plates begin to contract and shriek, the seams of the world are lit from within by the soft, phosphorescent blue of millions of Mites gorging themselves on the planet’s groans.

### The Kiln-Gentry
Rarely seen, and perhaps the closest thing to the "potters" who built this place, are the Kiln-Gentry. They are massive, slow-moving mounds of molten-core ceramic that live near the far horizons where the pouring is still fresh. They resemble walking kilns, their interiors glowing with a dull orange heat. 

They are the maintenance crew of a world that refuses to age. As they move, they trail a slight, viscous liquid—a "self-leveling" slip—that fills in any micro-fractures or chips in the terracotta floor caused by the expansion pops. They are the reason the world remains "new." They move at the speed of a glacier, a few inches a day, their heavy, rounded feet smoothing out the sharp edges of the expansion joints. They do not acknowledge the Luster-Walkers or the Siphoners. They are obsessed with the geometry. To them, a crack is a sin, and a weathered corner is a failure of the design.

### The Echo-Crows
There are no birds in the sky, but there are "Echo-Crows" on the ground. These are hollow, wind-up curiosities of the environment. Formed from thin sheets of fired clay, they are shaped like kites or sails. They have no legs. They catch the "pavement winds" and tumble across the infinite flat, their hollow bodies acting as flutes. 

As the wind rushes through their ceramic ribcages, they produce long, mournful whistles that can be heard for miles. They are the only "voice" of the Plateau. They possess no intelligence; they are merely kinetic sculptures driven by the weather, but to a lonely traveler, their whistling sounds like a conversation between the ground and the sky. They eventually shatter when they hit a seam-dweller succulent or a Kiln-Gentry, only for their shards to be eventually collected and "re-fired" by the natural heat and the drifting silt of the plateau into new, strange shapes.

In this place, life is a hard, mineral thing. It does not bloom or rot; it only shines, rings, and persists.
