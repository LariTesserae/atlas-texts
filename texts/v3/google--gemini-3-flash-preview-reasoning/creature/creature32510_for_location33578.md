In the crushing, brilliant density of the Lumen-Cyst, the distinction between inhabitant and environment has long since collapsed. To live here is to be a component of a high-pressure, biological machine that has no output other than its own continued presence.

**The Tessellated**
The primary occupants—those thousands of translucent, dice-shaped organisms—are known, if they are known at all, as the **Tessellated**. They are not merely "stacked"; they are locked. Each organism is a biological prism, its chitinous shell polished to a degree that defies organic chemistry. They lack eyes, limbs, or orifices. They do not need to seek sustenance because they are powered by the structural light that saturates the chamber.

Inside their translucent carapaces, if one could look past the blinding refraction, there are no organs of soft tissue. Instead, there is a core of liquid geometry—a swirling, slow-motion mercury that reacts to the constant, deafening harmonic roar of the room. They are the source of the sound. The "static scream" is the collective vibration of their internal fluids, humming at a frequency that keeps the very atoms of the room from drifting. They are the anchors of this space. They do not think in sequences; they exist in a singular, eternal thought of *holding*.

**The Bone-Wick Flora**
The pale, calcified stalks—the **Bone-Wicks**—are the only things that seem to possess a direction. They are parasites of the light. Their mineral leaves are angled with impossible precision to catch the reflections bouncing off the Tessellated. They don’t grow toward a sun; they grow toward the points of highest resonance. 

The Bone-Wicks act as the nervous system of the Cyst. Though they appear brittle, they are harder than the basalt they spring from. They conduct the "roar," vibrating in sympathy with the dice-creatures, acting as tuning forks that keep the pressure of the room equalized. If a single leaf were to shatter, the sudden silence in that micro-sector might cause the ceiling to buckle.

**The Micro-Architects (The Absent)**
The most unsettling inhabitants are the ones who are not visible. The microscopic stairs and the alloy cubes suggest a presence that operates on a scale of size and time that renders them invisible to the macro-world. 

These are the **Lattice-Strider**s—entities or perhaps autonomous forces that exist in the gaps between the silver film of condensation and the jagged rock-spurs. They are the maintenance crew of the stasis. They do not "move" so much as they transition between points of high intensity. The tiny stairs are not for climbing in a physical sense; they are fractal antennas, shaped to bleed off excess static from the air. 

The alloy cube, so smooth it seems to repel the light, is a "Heat-Sink Soul." It is a concentrated point of absolute zero, a biological or technological anchor that prevents the intense, structural light from turning the chamber into a star. It is the silent heart of the Cyst, and something—perhaps the Tessellated themselves, or the force that placed them there—regards it with a molecular kind of reverence.

**The Collective State**
There is no "who" in the Lumen-Cyst, only a "we" that has forgotten the concept of the individual. The occupants are a single, crystalline organism distributed across thousands of bodies. They do not experience the passage of time as a line, but as a weight. 

If you were to touch one of the Tessellated, you would not feel warmth or a heartbeat. You would feel a vibration so intense it would liquefy the bones in your hand. You would realize that they are not resting. They are exerting every ounce of their biological intensity to ensure that *nothing ever happens.* They are the guardians of the Great Stillness, living batteries of white-hot stasis, screaming a song of perfect, unmoving order into the unyielding basalt.
