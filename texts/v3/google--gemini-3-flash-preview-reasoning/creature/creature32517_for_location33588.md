The inhabitants of the Elder Stumps are not a people of fire and fury, but a people of **viscosity and resonance**. They call themselves the **Pulseless**, a name derived not from a lack of life, but from the way their own heartbeats have synchronized over generations with the deep, thudding vibration of the mountain-pistons. 

### The Condensate 
The people here are physically shaped by the weight of the damp. They are broad-shouldered and barrel-chested, a physiological necessity for drawing oxygen from the thin, iron-scented air. Their skin has a translucent, waxy quality, pale as the underbelly of a fern, designed to shed the constant weeping of the clouds. 

They do not wear cloth, which would rot in days, but rather garments of **cured lichen-leather** and heavy, oil-slicked aprons of hammered copper. They move with a deliberate, tectonic slowness. In a world where every surface is slick and every chasm is a mile deep, haste is a death sentence. To watch a group of workers cross a moss-choked suspension bridge is to watch a slow-motion ballet; they lean into the updrafts, their heavy boots finding purchase in the "green masonry" as if they were part of the architecture themselves.

### The Silent Tongue
Speech is rare. The humidity and the moss swallow sound so effectively that a shout carries no further than a whisper. Instead, the inhabitants communicate through **Vibro-Cant**. By placing a hand or a specialized copper rod against the iron railings and catwalks that lace the city, they send rhythmic pulses through the metal. A master smith can feel a request for coal from three plateaus away simply by the hum in his fingertips. 

Their social life is dictated by the "Great Breath." When the mists thicken into a heavy drizzle, the Pulseless retreat to the external furnaces, huddling near the orange glow. They do not talk; they simply exist in a shared, meditative state, their eyes—dilated and dark to catch the permanent twilight—fixed on the rhythmic rise and fall of the steam valves.

### The Moss-Shepherds
While the factories produce the heavy machinery, a specialized caste known as the **Bryo-Tectonics** manages the vegetation. They do not see the moss as a nuisance, but as a structural adhesive. They carry long, curved hooks to "braid" the hair-like lichens into sturdy cables and use nutrient-rich pastes to encourage moss cushions to grow over the seams of leaking pipes. They are gardeners of the industrial, ensuring that the rust remains a protective patina rather than a corrosive rot.

### The Fauna of the Folds
Beyond the human inhabitants, the ecology is sparse but specialized:

*   **The Copper-Moths:** Large, heavy-bodied insects with wings that look like tarnished foil. They don't fly so much as crawl over the copper piping, vibrating their wings to shake off condensation. They feed on the mineral-rich soot that coats the factory walls.
*   **The Valve-Crabs:** Small, multi-legged crustaceans that live inside the steam-vents. They have evolved to withstand extreme heat and spend their lives scouring the interior of the pipes for mineral build-up, keeping the city’s "veins" clear in exchange for warmth.
*   **The Cloud-Rays:** These are the ghosts of the Stumps. More fungal than avian, these translucent, saucer-shaped entities float on the slow updrafts of the abysses. They have no eyes, sensing the heat signatures of the external furnaces. Occasionally, a Ray will drift too close to a catwalk, and the Pulseless will reach out to touch its rubbery, damp skin—a gesture of quiet communion with the only other thing that shares their thin, high air.

### The Auscultators
At the highest level of their society are the **Auscultators**, the priest-engineers who live in the roots of the Elder Stumps. They spend their lives suspended in harnesses over the dark abysses, their ears pressed against the basalt pedestals. They listen to the "Heart-Thud" of the planet. They believe the machines were not built by them, but discovered—that the steam and the iron are the mountain's way of breathing, and they are merely the parasites that keep the lungs from seizing.

In this world, life is not a struggle against nature, but a slow, rhythmic merging with it. The people do not fear the end of their civilization; they look at the moss-covered gears and the rust-proofed beams and see a state of perfect, damp eternity. They are the stewards of a world that has finished changing, content to pulse in the twilight until the mountains themselves finally crumble.
