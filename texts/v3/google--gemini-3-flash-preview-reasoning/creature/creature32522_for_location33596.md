The inhabitants of this obsidian throat are not individuals in any sense that a surface-dweller would recognize. They are the **Siphonophores of the Spire**, a collective biological engine that has traded the luxury of individuality for the efficiency of a closed-loop existence.

### The Litho-Wrights (The Ribbon-Creatures)
The ribbon-like beings tending the slurry are the architects and the nervous system of the fissure. They are composed of a dense, hyper-oxygenated muscle that feels like wet suede. They possess no eyes; instead, their entire dorsal surface is a photoreceptive membrane that drinks the white light filtering from above. 

They are the ones who understand the fraying reality of the crack. As they move, they do not just crawl; they *weave*. Their rhythmic stirring of the nutrient slurry is a form of calculation, a tactile computation that stabilizes the spatial folds. When the gravity shifts, it is often because a cluster of these Wrights has synchronized their pulses, creating a localized anchor point. They are the ones who used the acid—a concentrated, caustic bile secreted from their ventral pores—to etch the stone. They do not have hands, but they possess a prehensile dexterity in their terminus, allowing them to lash bone to glass with the precision of a master watchmaker.

### The Facet-Gazers (The Jelly-Trilobites)
The translucent, trilobite-like creatures are the fissure’s primary consumers. They are the "Gazers," though they do not look out; they look *in*. Their bodies are living prisms. When they crawl over the weeping obsidian, they are not just drinking the mineral-rich fluid; they are processing the reflections of the world below. 

They act as biological batteries. They store the 2.9 intensity heat within their gelid cores, glowing with a soft, internal infrared light. When one dies and is absorbed by the carpet, it is an act of data and energy transfer. The "clouding over" of their skin is the final hardening of their life’s experiences into a crystalline residue that the Wrights then harvest to sharpen their scrapers.

### The Absent Giants (The Source of the Ribs)
The ribs used for the tools belong to the **Ossuaries**, a caste of the species that no longer exists in a mobile form. Long ago, in the fissure's relative youth, certain members grew to the size of a man's torso. They were the bridge-builders, their bodies spanning the gaps of the spatial folds to provide a permanent walkway for the smaller kin. 

When they died, they didn't rot. The sterile, salt-heavy atmosphere calcified them instantly. Their skeletons are now part of the fissure’s geology, buried beneath the heap of glass shards. The current inhabitants mine their own ancestors, reclaiming the calcium and phosphate to create the only hard technology they possess.

### The Shadow-Echoes
Deep within the weeping walls, where the obsidian reflects the valley miles below, there are shapes that do not belong to the fissure. These are the **Refractions**. They are not physical beings, but sentient optical illusions birthed by the mountain’s unique geometry. They look like elongated, soot-colored versions of the Wrights, moving in the "outside" world of the reflection. 

The inhabitants of the crack interact with these shadows by pressing their soft bodies against the glass. There is a silent exchange—a transfer of heat against the cold image of the valley. The dwellers of the needle believe the valley below is a myth, a hallucination of the "cold-death," and they use the tools to scrape the glass, trying to "clean" the mountain of these terrifying, distant visions.

### The Collective Mind
The sound you hear—the *tock* of droplets and the *squelch* of movement—is their language. It is a slow, haptic conversation. They are a civilization of the "Now." Because space is unreliable, they have no concept of "Distance." Because they absorb their dead, they have no concept of "Was." 

They are a single, heaving lung of salt, copper, and glass, endlessly refining the nutrient slurry in the dark bowl, waiting for the day the mountain finally closes its eye or shatters into the void. To them, you are not a living thing; you are a spatial anomaly, a massive, dry intrusion of heat-thieving air that they will patiently wait to absorb once you succumb to the breathless, vertical fever of their home.
