The inhabitants of the High Reach are the curators of the eternal moment, a people whose physiology and culture have been sculpted by the stillness of their world. 

### The High-Reachers
The people who walk the tiered terraces are long-limbed and spare, their bodies adapted to a life of verticality. Because the air never moves and the temperature never fluctuates, they have no need for the layered clothing of other worlds. They wear garments of supple, un-aging leather and heavy silks that have hung in the same elegant folds for centuries. Their skin is the color of the morning mist—a pale, luminous grey—and is remarkably smooth. Without the friction of wind or the radiation of a shifting sun, the skin does not weather; a centenarian looks identical to a youth, save for the depth of stillness in their eyes.

They move with a terrifying, liquid grace. To a stranger, a High-Reacher would appear to be moving in slow motion, but this is a survival mechanism. In a world of obsidian edges, a stumble is a death sentence. They do not run; they flow. They have developed a unique sensory organ—a series of fine, silver filaments along their collarbones—that can detect the slightest displacement of the heavy, stagnant air, allowing them to navigate the lightless ravines with the same confidence as the sunlit spires.

Their voices are low and resonant, designed to carry through the thick atmosphere without breaking the silence. They speak in a language of vowels and hums, avoiding the sharp consonants that might echo too harshly against the basalt walls. In the High Reach, to be loud is to be barbaric.

### The Slow-Beats
The six-legged fauna, known as *Ourob*, are the primary beasts of burden, though "burden" is a relative term where gravity feels more like an embrace than a weight. These creatures have wide, splayed feet tipped with microscopic hooks that allow them to walk up a vertical cliff as easily as a flat terrace. Their fur is thick and metallic, trapping the permanent 10 AM light and reflecting it in a dull copper sheen.

The Ourob have a circulatory system that functions at a glacial pace. Their hearts beat perhaps once every ten minutes, a sound like a muffled drum that can be heard through the stone floors of the dwellings. They do not eat in the traditional sense; they press their soft underbellies against the luminous mosses, absorbing nutrients through osmosis. They are the living embodiment of the High Reach—patient, indestructible, and utterly unhurried.

### The Velvet Gliders
The grey birds are not predators, for there is no "chase" in this world. They are the *Vellichor*, creatures of pure buoyancy. Their bones are hollow and filled with a gas lighter than the silvered air, making them naturally buoyant. They do not flap their wings; they use their broad, velvet-covered primary feathers to "row" through the atmosphere. 

They are scavengers of the condensation, drifting from spire to spire to sip the berry-sized droplets that cling to the rock. Because there is no wind to carry scent, they navigate by the gongs of the city. When a High-Reacher strikes a stone gong to signal the completion of a task, the Vellichor follow the vibrations, knowing that the movement of people often dislodges the thick, nutrient-rich lichen they crave.

### The Shadow-Gardeners
In the permanent ink-black shadows of the great spires lives a sub-culture of the High-Reachers who have turned away from the light. These are the Gardeners of the Gloaming. They are even paler, almost translucent, and their eyes are wide and lidless, shimmering with a soft internal bioluminescence. 

They tend to the fractal fungi, whispering to the growths to encourage their rigid, coral-like expansion. The fungi they harvest provide the city’s light—glowing orbs placed in iron sconces that never burn out. The Gardeners are the keepers of the "slow-growth," documenting the expansion of mosses that might take a human lifetime to grow a single inch. To them, the static nature of the world is a canvas, and they are the painters, slightly shifting the placement of a stone or a spore to create a masterpiece that will remain unchanged for eternity.

### The Dwellers of the Glass
Deep within the viscous, unmoving river live the *Silt-Wraiths*. These are long, ribbon-like organisms that exist in the space between liquid and solid. They do not swim so much as they are suspended. They are translucent, visible only when they catch the 10 AM light, appearing like flashes of lightning frozen in a block of ice. 

The High-Reachers treat the river with a religious awe. They use it as a graveyard; when a citizen finally decides their time in the "now" is finished, they walk onto the glass ribbon and stand still. As they slowly sink over the course of days, the Silt-Wraiths weave around them, preserving the body in a cocoon of crystal-clear mucus. The bottom of the river is a gallery of the ancestors, standing upright and perfect, staring through the heavy water at the world above, unchanged and un-decaying.
