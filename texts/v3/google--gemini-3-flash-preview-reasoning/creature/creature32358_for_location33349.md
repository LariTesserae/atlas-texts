In the Aethel-Veldt, the concept of "an inhabitant" is fluid, for there is no clear boundary where the landscape ends and the individual begins. To live here is to be a specialized cell within a continental body. The inhabitants do not move *across* the Veldt; they are expressions *of* it.

### The Aethel-Kin (The Slow-Folders)
They are not truly gone. Those who look at the "Ribs"—the calcified arches atop the Knees—and see only ruins are missing the rhythm of the plateau. The Slow-Folders are still there; they have simply achieved a state of meditation so profound that their metabolism has synchronized with the Marrow-Moss.

A Slow-Folder appears as a tall, spindly figure, draped in robes of living lichen that have grown into their very skin. They do not walk; they lean. They might spend forty years leaning against a particular World-Vine, their nervous systems slowly intertwining with the plant’s capillary network. They are the "Editors" of the Veldt. With long, needle-like fingers, they gently prune the Glass-Palms or redirect the flow of a Lace-Fall to ensure the moss below receives the exact micro-dose of mineral-rich nectar it requires. They do not speak; they hum in a register so low it can only be felt in the soles of the feet, a sound that guides the growth of the fractals around the Well-Stones.

### The Gossamer-Lords (Siphon-Whales)
High in the jade-thick air, the Siphon-Whales drift like translucent cathedrals. They are less like animals and more like biological clouds. Their bodies are shimmering, semi-permeable membranes that filter the heavy mist, extracting the "Sky-Manna"—microscopic spores and airborne nutrients exhaled by the canopy above.

Within their transparent bellies, one can see internal forests of glowing cilia that pulse with a rhythmic, golden light. These whales are the keepers of the Veldt’s memory. It is said that when a Siphon-Whale grows too heavy with age and moisture, it does not die; it descends. It settles onto a Knee, its membrane merging with the Marrow-Moss, depositing a lifetime of filtered sky-data into the continental mind. The moss where a whale has landed grows thicker, more luminous, and produces the sweetest condensation.

### The Resonators (Thrum-Beetles)
If the Slow-Folders are the editors and the Whales are the memory, the Thrum-Beetles are the heartbeat. These creatures are the size of boulders, covered in a shell that resembles polished obsidian, etched with bioluminescent runes that shift color based on the atmospheric pressure.

They do not eat the moss; they "tune" it. As they move—a process that involves a single step every few hours—they vibrate their wing-cases. This thrumming is essential to the Veldt’s physics. The vibration prevents the heavy, nectar-like water from stagnating and turning anaerobic. It keeps the Marrow-Moss aerated, the resonance acting as a mechanical lung for the ground itself. Clusters of Thrum-Beetles often gather around the "Ribs," their collective vibration creating a zone of "Soft-Time" where gravity is even further muffled, allowing the Slow-Folders to remain upright without effort.

### The Vein-Dwellers (The Nectar-Mites)
Within the Lace-Falls and the hollow cores of the World-Vines live the smallest and most frantic of the Veldt’s residents. These are the Nectar-Mites, creatures of pure light and surface tension. They look like shivering sparks of mercury, skating across the vertical sheets of slow-moving water.

They are the messengers. Because the larger lifeforms move so slowly, the Nectar-Mites provide the Veldt’s "nervous system." They carry chemical signals from one end of the plateau to the other, riding the Lace-Falls and swimming through the internal rivers of the vines. If a World-Vine is suffering from a fungal blight a hundred miles away, the Mites carry the "alarm-scent" to a group of Slow-Folders, who will begin their decade-long journey to tend to the wound.

### The Predators (The Quiet Incorporation)
There is no blood spilled in the Aethel-Veldt, but there is consumption. The "Vines that Wait" are the closest thing to predators. They are beautiful, iridescent tendrils that hang from the canopy like jewelry. They do not snap or bite. Instead, they find anything that has stopped moving—a stone, a fallen Rib, or a traveler who has sat too long in contemplation—and they begin to "belong" to it.

They wrap with a velvet touch. Over years, they secrete an enzyme that turns the subject into the same Marrow-Moss that makes up the floor. It is not a violent death, but a slow, ecstatic assimilation. To be hunted by the Vines is to be invited into the permanence of the world.

### The Guest-Logic
Anything that enters the Aethel-Veldt from the "bruised purple void" below finds itself changing. The lungs begin to prefer the mist to the air; the heart slows to match the Thrum-Beetles; the mind stops looking for the next moment and begins to inhabit the current one. The residents of the Veldt are not just those born to it, but anything that was patient enough to let the emerald twilight claim it. 

In the Aethel-Veldt, you do not live *on* the world. You are simply a way for the world to look at itself.
