In this world of petrified ivory and indigo shadow, life has not merely occupied the space—it has become a fluid extension of the geometry itself. The inhabitants are creatures of tension and transparency, defined by their ability to withstand the crushing weight of the water and the violent caprice of the currents.

### The Pulse-Mites (The Living Light)
Clinging to the leeward side of the great ivory pillars are the **Pulse-Mites**. These are the bioluminescent crustaceans mentioned in the scrolls, but up close, they resemble shards of flickering glass. They do not have shells of calcium; instead, they have evolved carapaces of a semi-translucent, flexible resin that mimics the ceramic of the world. They are the nervous system of the lattice. 

They do not hunt; they "wait." When the subsurface storms kick up clouds of bone-silt, the Mites extend feathery, crystalline antennae to catch the microscopic organic matter clinging to the eroding dust. When a current becomes too violent, they synchronize their bioluminescence, pulsing in rhythmic waves of cyan. This isn't communication—it is a byproduct of their grip. The harder they cling to the glass-slick walls, the more their internal chemistry glows, turning a storm-lashed plaza into a strobing, rhythmic cathedral of frantic light.

### The Vellum-Weavers (The Ribbon-Dwellers)
Navigating the hollows of the megastructure are the **Vellum-Weavers**. These ribbon-like organisms are long, flat, and almost two-dimensional, some reaching the length of a ship’s mast. They are ghosts of pure muscle. Lacking eyes, they navigate by "tasting" the vibration of the water through their skin. 

They spend their lives in a state of perpetual flight. Because the water never rests, the Weavers have developed a way to "sail" the currents. They thread themselves through the geometric apertures of the lattice, using the architecture as a spool. During the most violent gales, you might see a thousand Weavers knotted around a single white archway, their bodies vibrating like the strings of a harp, producing a high, whistling harmonic that blends with the low moan of the world-flute. They eat the Pulse-Mites, peeling them off the walls with specialized, rasping tongues that can detect the heat of a living body against the freezing ceramic.

### The Aeolian Siphons (The Great Anchors)
The most massive inhabitants are the **Aeolian Siphons**. These are the gelatinous, stalked filters that resemble translucent, bruised-purple lungs. They are rooted deep into the ancient sockets where perhaps machinery once sat. Their "heads" are gargantuan bells, several stories tall, which they cast into the flow like nets.

The Siphons are the only things in this world that seem to possess a sense of time. They move with an agonizing slowness, their fleshy stalks thick as tree trunks but flexible as rubber. When the current shifts, the Siphon groans, its internal pressure equalizing with a sound like a distant cello. They are the scavengers of the deep; they catch the falling remains of Weavers that have been snapped by the turbulence, or the fine "snow" of organic waste that drifts down from the unreachable heights above. 

### The Litho-Polyp (The Silent Sculptors)
Though there is no vegetation, the white surfaces are not entirely barren. They are covered in a film of **Litho-Polyps**—microscopic, colonial organisms that are indistinguishable from the ivory ceramic they inhabit. They are the reason the world remains bone-white. They consume the minute traces of minerals in the water and excrete a thin, self-polishing layer of the same ceramic substrate that forms the world. 

They are the janitors of the graveyard. If a creature dies and its remains settle on a plaza, the Litho-Polyps swarm the site in a slow, invisible tide, breaking down the organic matter and encasing it in new, white "stone." Over millions of years, they have rounded the edges of the great cubes, turning sharp angles into the smooth, aerodynamic curves that allow the currents to flow without tearing the world apart.

### The Apex: The Glass-Wraiths
Rarely seen, and existing only as a shimmering distortion in the indigo light, are the **Glass-Wraiths**. They are predators of the open spaces, vast and jellyfish-like, but with a structural complexity that defies biological norms. They are almost entirely water. They do not fight the turbulence; they become it. 

A Glass-Wraith can flatten itself to the thickness of a leaf to pass through a narrow lattice-gap, or expand into a billowing cloud of stinging filaments to engulf a school of Weavers. They are the only creatures that seem to "understand" the geometry. They move through the corridors of the drowned cathedral not as trespassers, but as the rightful heirs to the silence, their translucent bodies refracting the dim indigo light until they vanish against the backdrop of the infinite, white ribs of the world.

There is no social structure here, no hunger for power, no memory of the architects. There is only the grip, the flow, and the song of the water through the bone. The life here does not live *in* the structure; it has become the living velvet draped over a skeleton that refuses to decay.
