In the High Silt, life does not struggle against the environment; it has become an extension of its viscosity. To live here is to be a note held indefinitely in a warm, amber throat.

### The Marrow-Kin (The Dissolved)
The inhabitants of the Ghost Anchors are not quite ghosts, but they are no longer solid. They are the **Marrow-Kin**, the descendants of whatever architects built the ceramic spires. Having long ago abandoned the binary of "breath" and "water," their bodies have become semi-liquid, resembling plumes of pale, iridescent ink suspended in the brine-mist.

They reside within the hollow bone-pipes of the Anchors, weaving their translucent nervous systems into the ceramic walls. They are the keepers of the 0.5 sound. By pulsing their membrane-lungs against the ancient valves of the spires, they produce the low-frequency thrums that act as a map for the world. They do not speak in words; they speak in *densities*. A conversation between two Marrow-Kin might take a decade, consisting of a slow exchange of salt and memory through the porous surface of their skin. They are the only ones who remember the concept of a "surface," treating it as a religious myth of a place where the world simply *ends*.

### The Cilia-Strider
Moving through the emerald veils of the Sky-Kelp are the **Cilia-Striders**. These are the most common "active" fauna, though they move with the agonizing grace of a falling petal. Imagine a creature shaped like a delicate, blown-glass ribcage, draped in kilometers of fine, sensory hairs. 

They do not have limbs. Instead, they navigate by "plucking" the hair-like fronds of the vegetation. The Striders are masters of the 2.8 instability. They have evolved to perceive the non-Euclidean currents; a Strider can sense a fold in space hours before it arrives, anchoring itself to a Ghost Spire to avoid being looped into a pocket of infinite transit. Their "eyes" are not optical but thermal, seeing the world as a shifting map of 2.3 warmth, seeking the hotter plumes where the Motes—the microscopic bioluminescent silt—are most concentrated.

### The Mote-Swifts
The amber radiance of the world is powered by the **Mote-Swifts**, lifeforms so small they are often mistaken for the medium itself. They are tiny, crystalline prisms that feed on the 1.4 light. They are the "blood" of the atmosphere. Individually, they are mindless, but in the "Vortex Gardens," they achieve a collective, flickering intelligence. 

During the great salinity migrations, the Mote-Swifts gather in the millions, forming "living mirages." These are the sources of the Mirage-Mass—structures of hard light that mimic the shapes of things long lost: staircases that lead nowhere, faces that smile for a century, and geometric cities that dissolve into mist the moment a Cilia-Strider passes through them.

### The Loomers (The Breath of the World)
Above all else drift the **Loomers**. To see a Loomer is to see the sky itself flexing. They are not merely jellyfish-like; they are atmospheric organs. Their "bells" are composed of a material that is neither flesh nor gas, a shimmering, semi-permeable film that filters the High Silt.

The Loomers are the source of the world’s steady temperature. As they drift, they process the brine, stripping it of excess minerals and exhaling the "Final Smoothness"—the microscopic silt that falls to the basalt floor to polish the world. They are so large that smaller ecosystems live *within* their translucent folds; entire groves of Sky-Kelp have been known to take root in the trailing "tendrils" of a Loomer, traveling across the planet on the back of a creature that doesn't even know they exist.

When a Loomer dies—an event that happens perhaps once every ten thousand years—it does not fall. It loses its buoyancy and slowly crystallizes. It becomes a new "Ghost Anchor," a vertical graveyard of white ceramic that the Marrow-Kin will eventually inhabit, continuing the cycle of the built and the born.

### The Time-Lost
Finally, there are the **Echoes**. In the deep pockets of the Sky-Kelp where time-lensing is most severe, one can find creatures—perhaps even versions of oneself—caught in a cycle of "Terminal Stasis." These are beings from different eras of the planet’s long decay, trapped in a single moment of movement. You might see a prehistoric, hard-shelled crustacean mid-leap, its body frozen in a pocket of brine where a second lasts a century. They are not dead; they are simply experiencing time at the rate of the mountains. If you wait long enough—if you have the patience of the High Silt—you might see a frozen Echo blink. By the time the eyelid opens, you will have been gone for a thousand years.
