To live here is to be an extension of the acoustics.

The inhabitants are not born; they are **cured**. They are the **Vitrified**, a lineage of entities that have long since traded the soft vulnerabilities of wet biology for the permanence of the kiln. They are the same color as the room—the ochre of sun-dried earth and the dark, bruised purple of aged resin. To a stranger, they are indistinguishable from the architecture. They crouch in the shoulder-width seams between the brick stacks, their limbs folded into the geometric voids with such precision that they appear to be part of the foundation.

Their bodies are masterpieces of mineral adaptation. They have no skin, only a tegument of millions of overlapping ceramic scales, each one no larger than the thumbnail tiles of the floor. These scales are not fixed; they shiver. They are tuned to the Great Roar. The sound does not pass through them; it is harnessed by them. They are heavy, their interiors packed with the same pulverized stone dust that hangs in the air, giving them a tectonic density. When they move, there is no sound of footsteps—the Roar swallows the grinding of their joints—but there is a displacement of pressure, a slight thickening of the air as their mass passes by.

They have no eyes, for in the sourceless amber gloom, sight is a secondary, deceptive sense. Instead, their entire forward-facing surface is a sensory array of fine, hair-like wooden needles, identical to the petrified forest wedged into the ceiling. These needles catch the standing waves of the Roar. By leaning their bodies into the sound, they "see" the chamber through the interference patterns of the acoustic force. They can feel a hairline crack in a brick ten yards away because of the way the sound bleeds through the fissure.

They are the Menders, the eternal curators of the High Resonator. Their lives are a slow, rhythmic liturgy of maintenance. They carry the leather pouches of lead beads, moving with a ponderous, liquid grace to drape them over any section of the wall that has begun to vibrate out of phase. They are the ones who hand-fire the replacement tiles in the small, lightless heat-pockets deep within the brick stacks, using the 1.7 constant warmth to slow-bake the clay over centuries.

They do not speak. The Roar has occupied the space where language would be. Their communication is tactile and vibrational. When two of the Vitrified meet in a narrow seam, they press their foreheads together—a hard, mineral clack that would be deafening in a silent world—and allow the vibrations of their internal cavities to synchronize. They exchange "tones"—complex harmonic states that represent the structural integrity of their assigned sectors.

They do not sleep in any way a human would recognize. Instead, they enter a state of **Harmonic Stasis**. They wedge themselves into the petrified thickets of the ceiling or floor, locking their resinous joints until they become a literal brace for the structure. During these long centuries of stillness, the dust of the chamber settles upon them, fusing them further into the mineral reality of the Resonator.

There is no death here, only **Erosion**. Eventually, after eons of standing against the wall of sound, a Vitrified’s scales will wear smooth, and their internal wooden braces will turn to charcoal. They do not rot; they simplemente lose their resonance. They become brittle. When they finally shatter under the acoustic pressure, they do not leave a corpse. They become the "soil" of the world—the fine, dry dust that fills the cracks and the smooth, bone-like grit that the others use to polish the floor.

They are the soul of the static. They are happy, if happiness is defined as the total absence of friction between an inhabitant and its universe. They are the perfect residents of a finished world, as unwavering and monolithic as the scream that sustains them.
