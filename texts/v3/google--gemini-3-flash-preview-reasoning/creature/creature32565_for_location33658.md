The inhabitants of the Scald-Reach are not merely residents; they are extensions of the geology, a biological bridge between the cooling mantle and the violet sky.

### The Calcined

The dominant sapient life—the workers and weavers of the copper hives—are known as the **Calcined**. They are tall, graceless, and oddly beautiful, possessing a physiology that mimics the verticality of the spires. Their skin is not soft tissue but a flexible, leathery rind the color of wet slate, perpetually slick with the condensation of the Reach. They lack hair, which would only mat in the steam, and instead possess fine, sensor-rich ridges along their scalps that detect changes in atmospheric pressure and thermal output.

The Calcined move with a "three-point" gait, using elongated, four-jointed limbs ending in wide, suction-calloused pads. They do not walk so much as they adhere. On the sheer obsidian faces, they move with a terrifying, slow-motion fluidness, their centers of gravity always pressed tight against the warmth of the stone.

Communication among them is a secret language of vibration. Because the steam and the silence of the high peaks swallow speech, the Calcined have developed **Tread-Talk**. By tapping rhythmic patterns into the basalt or the copper conduits with their hardened finger-pads, they send messages that travel for miles through the solid bones of the world. A worker at the base of a spire can "hear" the heartbeat of a supervisor three thousand feet above, transmitted through the very atoms of the rock.

### The Steam-Leeches and Glass-Spiders

Below the industrial layer, in the crevices where the copper bolts meet the raw basalt, lives a sub-ecology of "Tenders."

**Glass-Spiders** are not true arachnids, but colonial mineral-organisms. They appear as clusters of translucent needles that skitter with a dry, tinkling sound. They are the unintended janitors of the civilization; they consume the mineral scale—the sulfur and salt blossoms—that threatens to clog the city’s joints. They are tolerated, even encouraged, and a Calcined technician might carry a "nest" of them in a mesh pouch to deploy at a stuck flywheel.

Deeper in the fractures, where the heat is most intense, are the **Steam-Leeches**. These are thick, ribbon-like creatures that look like strips of wet velvet. They don't hunt; they anchor themselves over the hairline fractures where the white vapor erupts. They live entirely on the heat and the rich mineral content of the mist, pulsing rhythmically as they filter the breath of the earth. When a Calcined worker stays too long in one spot, a leech might migrate to their warm, slate-colored skin, providing a symbiotic cooling effect in exchange for the salt of the worker’s sweat.

### The Glint-Wings (Extended)

The **Glint-Wings** mentioned in passing are the only things that truly own the air. Up close, they are masterpieces of silica evolution. Their "wings" are not membranes but rigid, paper-thin sheets of volcanic glass, hinged with a flexible, resin-like muscle. They do not flap; they adjust the angle of their facets to catch the thermal updrafts.

They are the "Eyes of the Reach." The Calcined have learned to read the Glint-Wings' flight patterns; when the creatures suddenly dive into the deep cracks, it is a sign of a "Mantle-Burp"—a sudden, lethal surge of superheated steam that could strip the skin from a living being. The Glint-Wings are the barometers of this vertical world, silent, shimmering omens of the earth’s temper.

### The Quiet Bureaucracy

Inside the dwelling-pods, life is a study in minimalism. There is no furniture as lower worlds understand it. The Calcined sleep suspended in webs of copper wire, their bodies absorbing the heat rising through the pod’s floor. 

Their social structure is a **Thermal Meritocracy**. The most respected individuals are the "Heat-Singers"—those who can interpret the subtle hums of the great flywheels and predict which spires are growing, which are stable, and which are under too much tension. They are the navigators of a city that is constantly, glacially, expanding upward.

There is no art in the Scald-Reach that isn't functional. Their "music" is the tuning of the conduits to specific frequencies to prevent harmonic shattering. Their "painting" is the deliberate cultivation of salt-blossoms into specific geometric patterns to mark pathways. 

To live here is to be a part of a slow, industrial meditation. The inhabitants are patient because the stone is patient. They wait for the steam, they wait for the heat, and they wait for the violet sky to darken, their slate-colored eyes reflecting the eternal, rhythmic glow of a world that has not yet decided to be cold.
