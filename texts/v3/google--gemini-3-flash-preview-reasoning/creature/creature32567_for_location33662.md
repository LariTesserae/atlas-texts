The inhabitants of this basin do not inhabit the world so much as they extend from it. They are the **Palm-Pressed**, a race of sapient, slow-moving artisans whose bodies possess the matte texture of unfired clay and the structural suppleness of wet reeds. 

They are tall, elongated figures, their limbs lacking the sharp articulation of bone. Instead, their joints move with the heavy, hydraulic grace of pressurized silt. Their skin is the exact hue of the terracotta floor—a deep, sun-drenched orange—but it is perpetually damp, coated in a fine, glistening film of the world’s "perspiration." They have no hair, no scales, and no clothing; they are seamless, as if they were turned on a massive, invisible potter’s wheel and set down to dry in the eternal noon.

### The Great Stillness of the Body
The Palm-Pressed do not sleep, for there is no night to beckon it. Instead, they enter states of "Tessellation." They lay themselves flat against the tiles, their bodies aligning perfectly with the geometric seams, becoming indistinguishable from the floor itself. In this state, they absorb the warmth of the clay and the moisture of the grooves. A traveler might walk for miles across a seemingly empty courtyard, unaware that the ground beneath them is a carpet of breathing, conscious beings waiting for a thought to stir them.

When they move, it is with a deliberate, agonizing slowness. To them, a single step is a composition. They do not run; the very concept of "hurry" is a structural flaw they have evolved past. Their eyes are wide, lidless, and the color of the white sky, reflecting the glare so perfectly that they appear to have holes through their heads.

### The Architects of Maintenance
Though the world is "finished," the Palm-Pressed are obsessed with the integrity of the seams. They carry no tools; their fingers *are* the tools. The tips of their digits are hardened and flat, smelling faintly of mineral dust. They spend centuries smoothing the edges of the tiles with the friction of their palms, ensuring that not a single grain of grit disrupts the perfection of the basin. 

They are the ones who lashed the bamboo scaffolds, not to guard against invaders—for there is nothing outside the basin but the glare—but to serve as "Static Observers." The sentries on these towers are the oldest of their kind. They have stood so long in the humid heat that their skin has begun to vitrify, turning into a hard, ceramic shell. They do not move for decades; they simply watch the horizon to ensure the white glare does not falter, and that the water in the depressions remains at its precise, blood-warm level.

### The Symphony of the Pulse
Communication among the Palm-Pressed is non-verbal. In the ringing silence of the basin, sound is a sacred resource. They communicate through "Percussive Resonance." By striking a palm against a terracotta tile, a Palm-Pressed can send a vibration through the interconnected floor that carries for leagues. 

These are the *thwacks* heard across the flats—not random movements, but a language of deep, echoing thrums. A single strike might mean *“The moisture rises,”* while a rhythmic series of taps acts as a structural census, ensuring every tile and every inhabitant is still in its proper place. The pale, porcelain-skinned amphibians are their companions in this; the pulsing of the frogs' throats is a metronome that the Palm-Pressed use to coordinate their slow-motion labor.

### The Glass-Winged Attendants
The insects are not parasites, but living ornaments cultivated by the inhabitants. The Palm-Pressed "weave" these insects from the fibers of the fleshy green stalks, coaxing them into life with rhythmic humming. The insects serve as the world's only kinetic art. They are designed to hover over specific pools, their stained-glass wings refracting the white light into brief, stationary rainbows that provide the only color variation in the orange-and-green world. If an insect's flight wavers, a Palm-Pressed will spend a day slowly reaching out a finger to adjust the creature’s position, resetting it like a pendulum.

### The Philosophy of the Finish
The inhabitants believe that change is a form of decay. They view the absence of wind and rain not as a lack of weather, but as the achievement of a perfect, static climax. They do not birth new generations in the traditional sense; instead, when the population thins, the elders gather at the circular water depressions. They scoop up the heavy, wet clay from beneath the tiles, mix it with the blood-warm water, and spend years hand-sculpting a new member of their race. 

This new being is not considered "alive" until it is perfectly smooth, without a single thumbprint left on its skin. Only when it is a masterpiece of the basic—a flawless reflection of the world around it—does it open its white sky-eyes and take its place in the grand, silent stillness of the basin. 

They live in a state of terminal satisfaction, a race of statues living in a gallery they built for themselves, waiting for a sunset that will never come.
