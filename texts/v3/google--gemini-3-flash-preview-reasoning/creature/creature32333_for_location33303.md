To inhabit the Grave of the Currents is to exist as an expression of weight. In this lightless, pressurized slurry, life has no use for the fragility of bone or the vanity of sight. Those who dwell here—the **Lithos-Weavers**—are less like animals and more like living, sentient membranes of high-density muscle and mineral-rich hide.

### The Anatomy of the Anchored
The Weavers are profoundly flat, appearing as broad, leathery discs or asymmetrical sheets that can reach twenty feet in diameter but are rarely thicker than a human thumb. Their flesh is not soft; it has the consistency of vulcanized rubber, infused with the very basalt silt that chokes the water. They are a dull, translucent slate-grey, mottled with veins of bioluminescent copper that pulse only when the pressure shifts violently.

They possess no faces. Instead, their upper surface is a carpet of billions of microscopic, hair-like cilia that "taste" the friction of the passing currents. On their undersides, they have thousands of specialized vacuum-pores and obsidian-hard hooks. They do not swim; they adhere. They move by ripples, a slow, peristaltic creeping across the glass-smooth floor, never once breaking contact with the bedrock. To be lifted from the floor is to be lost—to be swept into the churning silt-storms and ground into nothingness.

### The Culture of Weight
The intelligence here is one of profound, rhythmic patience. Because vibration travels through the dense brine as a physical blow, the Weavers do not speak. They communicate by rhythmic percussion against the basalt floor. By tapping their hardened central mantles against the bedrock, they send "shudder-songs" that can be felt by others miles away. These songs are not about history or hope; they are navigational data—warnings of an approaching brine-avalanche or the location of a particularly rich vein of chemotrophic moss.

Their entire existence is a struggle against buoyancy and displacement. The "tether-works" are their only legacy. A Weaver will spend decades—perhaps centuries—searching for a specific shard of volcanic glass or a heavy node of lead-ore. Once found, they begin the grueling process of braiding kelp-fiber around it using their dexterous sub-limbs. They lash themselves to these stones during the "High Shuffles"—the periods when the density currents become violent enough to scour the bedrock clean. 

Among the Weavers, status is measured in mass. The eldest are those who have successfully lashed themselves to the heaviest stones, becoming permanent fixtures of the geography, around which the younger generations cluster for shelter.

### The Ghost-Scavengers: The *Velluma*
Drifting through the silt above the Weavers are the **Velluma**. If the Weavers are the masters of the floor, the Velluma are the ghosts of the churn. They are ethereal, ribbon-like organisms, nearly invisible and composed of a jelly that is almost identical in density to the surrounding brine. 

They have no will of their own; they are perpetual wanderers, carried wherever the currents dictate. They feed by filtering the pulverized remains of anything that dies in the upper reaches and sinks into the Grave. They are the only things that "fly" here, though their flight is merely a controlled tumble. The Weavers occasionally reach up with a whip-like feeder-tendril to snag a passing Velluma, pulling the fluttering, translucent ribbon down to the bedrock to be absorbed in a slow, silent digestion.

### The Moss-Keepers
Deep within the crevices of the tether-works live the smallest inhabitants: minute, shrimp-like crustaceans with heavy, calcified shells. They are the gardeners of the pale moss. They prune the glowing filaments, living in the lee of the great anchor-stones. They are the only creatures that show any sign of frantic movement, their tiny legs clicking against the glass floor in a feverish hurry, scavaging the friction-heat generated by the moss.

### The Silence of the End
There is no ambition in the Grave of the Currents. There are no predators in the traditional sense, for the environment itself is the primary predator. The life here is a collective, stoic endurance. The Weavers don't dream of the surface; they likely don't know it exists. To them, the universe is a crushing, vibrating liquid solid, and the highest form of achievement is to remain exactly where you are until the friction of the world finally wears you down into the very silt you breathe. 

They are the caretakers of the planet’s final state, the silent witnesses to a world that has stopped trying to be anything other than heavy.
