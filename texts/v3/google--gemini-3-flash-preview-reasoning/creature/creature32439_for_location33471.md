In the Crystalline High-Reach, life does not behave like a guest; it behaves like a mineral. The inhabitants of this jagged, screaming world are not visitors to the industry, nor are they victims of it. They are its secretions, its parasites, and its slow-motion ghosts.

### The Lithic Chorus (The Shard-Walkers)
Hidden in plain sight against the obsidian peaks are the **Glass-Gazers**. They are beings of pure, slow-time geometry, composed of the same dark volcanic glass as the mountains themselves. They do not have limbs so much as they have tectonic fractures. To a human eye, they would appear to be mere outcroppings, but over the course of a century, a Shard-Walker might migrate three inches down a cliffside to better catch the 45-degree sun.

They live on the vibration. The bone-shaking thrum of the cathedral-sized pistons is their heartbeat; they lean their flat, faceless heads against the vibrating steel of the Hubs, drinking in the kinetic energy. They are the only things that "hear" the subsonic pulse not as noise, but as a nutrient. They do not speak, but they resonate. When the pistons strike, the Glass-Gazers ring like tuning forks, a silent communication that ripples through the rock for a thousand miles.

### The Condensate-Wraiths
In the three-hundred-mile voids between the peaks, where the static mist hangs heavy, live the **Vapor-Whales**—though they are more like sentient atmospheric pressures than animals. They are vast, translucent membranes, miles wide and thinner than a soap bubble. 

They do not fly; they are buoyant on the sheer density of the supercooled air. They position themselves beneath the screaming steam valves of the Hubs, basking in the high-pressure exhaust. They are the "cleaners" of the void, straining the mineral-rich mist through their gossamer bodies. When a Wraith passes between two peaks, the sun is momentarily refracted, casting a brief, prismatic rainbow across the purple mosses—the only change in color this world ever sees.

### The Velvet Collective
The mosses and liverworts are not individual plants, but a single, planetary-scale nervous system known as **The Bruise**. Because there is no decay, the bottom layers of the mattress-thick vegetation are millions of years old, compressed into a peat that is nearly as hard as the obsidian. 

The Bruise is sentient in the way a glacier is sentient: it possesses a cold, crushing intentionality. It has learned to grow into the cooling towers and around the copper pipes, not to break them, but to act as insulation. In exchange for keeping the machines' heat from dissipating too quickly into the freezing air, the moss drinks the mineral-rich "sweat" of the pipes. If you were to step on the moss, the "bruise" would ripple; a signal would be sent through the copper-laced fibers, and miles away, a pressure valve might hiss in a specific, rhythmic response. The plants and the machines are in a marriage of shivering necessity.

### The Copper-Crawlers (The Maintenance Autonomies)
While there are no citizens, the Hubs are not truly empty. Within the miles of copper piping and the bellies of the bronze turbines live the **Pressure-Ticks**. These are not biological, but "emergent mechanicals"—tiny, crab-like fragments of brass and clockwork that have, over eons, ground themselves off the main engines. 

Driven by the magnetic fields of the electrified grids, these autonomous scraps of metal scuttle over the machinery. They don't think, but they have a function: they seek out cracks. When they find a leak in a pipe or a shudder in a piston, they wedge themselves into the gap and allow the pressure to weld them into place. They are the Hubs' own self-repairing immune system, a colony of metallic parasites that ensure the screaming never stops.

### The Dweller in the Well
At the very center of the planetary engines, deep within the "gut" of the young stone where the pistons disappear, there is a theoretical inhabitant known only by its effect: **The Governor**. 

The inhabitants of the High-Reach—the glass beings, the moss, the mechanical ticks—all seem to act in service of a central equilibrium. There is a sense that something sits in the lightless pressure of the world’s core, a mind made of heat and gravity, directing the flow of the weeping water. It is the architect of the "Flat Line." It is the reason the sun never sets and the steam never ceases. It is a god of stasis, obsessed with the beauty of a machine that does nothing but run perfectly, forever, in a vacuum. 

You are not looking at a wilderness or a factory. You are looking at the inside of a mind that has achieved total, frozen, and deafening peace.
