In the Grotto of the Weeping Ribs, the inhabitants do not live *upon* the land so much as they are suspended within its slow, wet digestion. 

The most pervasive residents are the **Amber-Pulmonates**, soft-shelled mollusks the size of a human fist that cling to the slick limestone walls. They are the primary source of illumination, their translucent mantles glowing with a bioluminescence that is less like a spark and more like the low, dying ember of a cigar. They move with a grueling, meditative slowness, leaving behind trails of iridescent mucus that serve as the grotto’s nervous system. This slime is not merely waste; it is a nutrient-rich pheromone map. Other creatures touch these trails to learn of the humidity levels above or the death of a tree deeper in the sinkhole. The Pulmonates are the keepers of the grotto’s clock, their rhythmic contractions pumping the heavy air through their vestigial lungs with a wet, bellows-like sound.

Moving through the mid-canopy of the bruised-plum vines are the **Gossamer-Hops**. These flightless birds have long since traded feathers for a coat of fine, silver-grey down that feels like wet velvet. They lack the frantic energy of surface birds; their metabolism has slowed to match the 98-degree stasis. They move in liquid leaps, their four-toed feet gripping the pulsing braids of the vines with prehensile precision. Their eyes are enormous, lidless black orbs that occupy half their skull, tuned to catch the faint violet-grey light filtering from the "eye" above. They do not sing. Instead, they communicate through a series of rhythmic, percussive throat-clicks that mimic the *tock* of the falling water, a camouflage of sound that allows them to vanish into the ambient noise of the cave.

On the floor, nestled in the hollows of the "sleeping beast" boulders, dwell the **Gulp-Hounds**. These are not canines, but massive, heavy-lidded amphibians with skin the color of wet wood and texture like corrugated rubber. They are the grotto’s philosophers of stillness. They do not hunt; they simply *are*. They sit for decades in the same limestone niche, their ribs—vast and prominent, giving the grotto its name—expanding and contracting in a visible, slow-motion heave. Their mouths are cavernous, lined with fine, baleen-like filaments. They live by simply inhaling the swarms of **Ghost-Gnats**—silent, translucent insects that dance in the humid drafts. A Gulp-Hound might blink only once every hour, its golden nictitating membrane sliding across its eye like a shutter.

High above, in the crevices where the orange rock meets the violet sky, live the **Shroud-Weavers**. These are not spiders of silk and geometry, but communal arachnids that secrete a thick, felt-like webbing. They do not spin webs to catch prey; they spin "lungs"—vast, hanging curtains of white fiber that drink the humidity from the air. They huddle in the center of these sodden shrouds, vibrating at a frequency that shakes the water droplets loose, creating a localized rain that feeds the ferns below. They are the architects of the grotto’s weather.

The most unsettling inhabitants, however, are the **Pale-Mimics**. They are barely distinguishable from the "trunkless trees" that sprout from the humidity. They are slow-growing crinoids, tall and stalk-like, with limbs that terminate in soft, hand-like fronds. They have grown around the ancient ceramic bowls and stone steps left by the departed, their "bark" gradually taking on the geometric textures of the ruins. If one stands still long enough, a Pale-Mimic might slowly unfurl a frond to brush a shoulder, not out of malice, but as a blind, tactile inquiry. They are the bridge between the stone and the flesh.

Nothing here possesses a sharp edge. The heat has rounded every corner, softened every shell, and dampened every cry. To live in the Weeping Ribs is to be part of a singular, communal organism that is constantly tasting itself. There is no hunger here, only a perpetual, thirsty absorption. The inhabitants are the grotto’s way of breathing, a slow, wet pulse in the dark, waiting for a sun that they have forgotten to believe in.
