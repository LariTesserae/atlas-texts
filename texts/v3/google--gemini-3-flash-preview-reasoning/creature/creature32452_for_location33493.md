To live here is to be an extension of the acoustics, a creature not of flesh, but of frequency and refraction. The inhabitants do not breathe air; they filter the vibrating mist through porous, crystalline membranes. They are the **Lithosonics**, the "Tuning-Folk" who are less a species and more a standing wave of sentient mineral.

### The Physiology of Resonance
The Lithosonics are tall, spindly silhouettes, their forms mirroring the needle-thin spires they inhabit. They possess no internal organs in the biological sense. Instead, their bodies are composed of interlocking shale-plates and translucent silica lattices, held together by the same harmonic forces that suspend boulders in the air. 

They are essentially living "sound-boxes." Their chests are hollow, ribbed chambers that catch the wind’s low-frequency thrum, amplifying it to power their movement. When they move, they do not walk with the heavy thud of weight; they glide, their feet clicking against the shale like glass shards, their bodies vibrating at a pitch that allows them to slide through the air as if the atmosphere were a liquid.

### The Sight of Sound
Because the light is so intense and the air is a prism, "vision" for a Lithosonic is a kaleidoscopic experience. They do not have eyes; they have sensory facets spread across their heads and shoulders. These facets perceive the world as a map of vibrations. To them, a spire is not a visual object, but a deep, resonant cello note. A "solid shadow" is a cold, silent void that they must navigate around, for to enter a shadow is to lose the solar energy that keeps their mineral blood—the weeping water—in its liquid, agitated state.

### The Shadow-Dwellers: The Static
While the Lithosonics occupy the light and the peaks, something else stirs in the "solid shadows." These are the **Umbral Lattices**. When a shadow becomes physical, it occasionally traps a pocket of the vibrating mist, which crystallizes into a dark, brittle facsimile of life. 

These Shadow-Dwellers are slow, dense, and dangerously silent. They are the "anti-notes" of the world. They do not build; they absorb. They wander the canyons between spires, looking for sources of heat and sound to consume. A Lithosonic caught in a solid shadow risks "freezing"—not from cold, but from the sudden cessation of the vibrations that hold their crystalline lattice together.

### The Architects of the Ear
The "Resonance Stations" you see are the work of the Lithosonics, but they are not homes. A Lithosonic does not sleep; it "tunes." They gather in these circular stone stations to undergo a process of structural maintenance. 

Using the heavy flint "sound-gouges," they carve grooves into their own limbs to adjust their personal pitch, ensuring they remain in harmony with the current seasonal resonance of the spires. The "hanging chimes" are their speech—a complex language of percussive strikes and sustained hums that can carry for miles across the thin, conductive air. 

The stations are also where they harvest the "weeping water." They don’t drink it for hydration, but for mass. By standing in the carved basins, they allow the mineral-rich film to coat them, adding new layers of silica to their frames, growing larger and more complex as they age, following the "biological pace" of the landscape itself.

### The Life Cycle of Shattering
Life here is a cycle of **Accretion and Cadence**. A young Lithosonic is small, translucent, and high-pitched, darting through the spires like a spark of light. As they age and absorb more mineral weeping, they become deeper, slower, and more resonant.

Eventually, every inhabitant reaches a "Critical Resonance." Their bodies become so dense and their internal frequency so powerful that they can no longer maintain their form against the external song of the wind. In a final, glorious moment of equilibrium, they do not die; they shatter. 

This shattering is a communal event. The elder stands atop a peak during a zenith-fog, vibrates until they glow with a blinding prismatic light, and then explodes into a million crystalline fractals. These shards become the seeds for new mineral growth on the spires, and the released sound—a single, perfect, world-shaking note—is said to be the "breath" that gives the next generation of Lithosonics their first spark of motion.

### The Transient Ecologies
There are smaller, stranger things too:
*   **The Glissando-Drifts:** Swarms of microscopic, winged mica-flakes that follow the horizontal water droplets. They act like a nervous system for the basins, reacting to changes in the wind’s pitch.
*   **The Null-Hives:** Near the sonic null-zones, small clusters of vibrating stones hover in permanent orbits. These are "petrified storms," localized pockets of physics where the mineral growth has spiraled into self-sustaining geometric shapes that hum a warning to anything that approaches.

In this world, to be "alive" is to stay in tune. To fall silent is to become nothing more than a rock, waiting for the next great wind to carve you into something that can finally scream again.
