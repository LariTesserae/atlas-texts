To inhabit the Knot is to exist as an extension of its geometry. The dwellers here are not so much residents as they are a specialized caste of biological and semi-biological maintenance, a population of **Tenders** whose very physiology has been dictated by three miles of overhead ocean.

### The Tenders
They are the descendants of whoever first bolted these domes to the seafloor, but the "human" has been pressed out of them by the weight of the water. They are short, barrel-chested, and terrifyingly dense. Their bones have undergone a process of mineralization, absorbing the surrounding salts and silts until their skeletons are as heavy and brittle as cast iron. They do not walk so much as they lean into the resistance of the heavy, oil-like water, moving with a tectonic slowness.

Their skin is the color of wet slate, thickened into a leathery, insensitive rind to combat the predatory cold. They have no use for eyes in the way surface-dwellers do; the amber glow of the sodium lamps is merely a navigational marker. Instead, their faces are dominated by sensory pits along the jawline and brow—organs that "hear" the vibrations of the Knot. They can feel a hairline fracture in a pipe two tiers up or sense the subtle shift in a flywheel’s rotation through the soles of their feet.

Communication is not vocal. The pressure makes air-based speech impossible and unnecessary. They speak in a language of percussion, striking the brass pipes with weighted rings worn on their fingers, sending rhythmic pings through the structure that others feel in their teeth.

### The Calcified Remnant
Living alongside the Tenders are the **Anchors**, individuals who have ceased to move entirely. These are the elders or the injured who have allowed the mineral crust to claim them. They sit in the recessed alcoves near the heat-vents, their limbs fused to the machinery by layers of compacted silt and iron-oxide. They have become living gargoyles, their consciousnesses perhaps merging with the rhythmic *thud-hiss* of the Knot. The Tenders treat them as sacred structural elements, occasionally scraping the rust-gardens from their faces so they can continue to sense the vibrations.

### The Parasitic Fauna
The Knot has birthed its own accidental ecosystem, drawn to the heat and the motion:

*   **Pressure-Moths:** These are not insects, but flat, translucent ribbons of cartilage that cling to the glass of the sodium lamps. They feed on the faint electromagnetic fields generated by the lights. When a lamp flickers, hundreds of them ripple in unison, creating a shimmying, oily distortion in the orange light.
*   **Piston-Leeches:** Hard-shelled, barnacle-like organisms that attach themselves to the moving flywheels. They don't eat the metal; they eat the *friction*. They possess specialized organs that convert the kinetic heat of the grinding machines into metabolic energy. A healthy machine is often covered in a vibrating, clicking layer of these creatures.
*   **The Silt-Lurkers:** In the dark spaces between the tiered domes, things move that even the Tenders avoid. These are long, eel-like scavengers with skin like sandpaper and mouths designed to rasp the "rust-lace" from the pipes. They are blind, guided entirely by the heat-plumes. They are the only things in the Knot that move quickly—a sudden, violent thrashing in the dark that vanishes as soon as the vibration is felt.

### The Collective Mind
There is no government in the Knot, only **The Calibration**. The inhabitants live in a state of perpetual, meditative labor. They do not wonder why the gears turn or what the reactors are powering. To them, the Knot is the entire universe, and the maintenance of its rhythm is the only morality. 

They sleep in the "dead zones" where the water is stilled by baffles, huddled together for warmth in heaps of heavy limbs, their heartbeats eventually synchronizing with the low-frequency thrum of the geothermal taps. When one dies, they are not buried; they are welded. Their body is placed in a non-essential junction or a structural gap, and the silt is encouraged to settle over them until they are just another rounded edge in the cathedral of rust. 

They are a people who have forgotten the sun, existing only as the living lubricant for a machine that must never, ever stop.
