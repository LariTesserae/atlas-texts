In the Xero-Marrow, life does not flow; it vibrates, shears, and accretes. To live here is to be a part of a perpetual, grinding combustion. The inhabitants are not merely residents of the Great Thicket; they are its friction-points, the sparks generated by the rubbing of silica-rib against amber-leaf.

### The Vellum-Kin (The Human Remnant)
Those who dwell in the Loom-Villages are no longer soft-fleshed. Evolution has cured them like hides in a smokehouse. Their skin is the texture of fine, high-micron sandpaper—a matte, ochre-colored integument that does not sweat, for there is no moisture to spare. Instead of pores, their skin features microscopic, overlapping scales that capture the mineral dust of the air, absorbing trace elements directly into a circulatory system of pressurized, hot gases and powdered electrolytes.

They are a people of silence and sudden, jerky grace. Because the world is deafening, they do not speak with vocal cords, which would wither in the desiccated air. They communicate through **Strigilism**: rubbing serrated, chitinous plates grafted onto their forearms against their ribs to produce a language of chirps, rasps, and harmonic hums that cut through the roar of the Thicket. Their eyes are dark, polished hematite spheres, shielded by three sets of translucent lids to keep out the scouring grit of the wind.

### The Harvesters of the Static
The most common fauna are the **Amber-Glints**—hexapedal scavengers the size of hounds, whose bodies are composed of translucent resins. They do not eat; they "charge." They are found clinging to the high-friction joints of the Great Thicket, where two massive boles rub together. As the pillars groan and grind, the Glints absorb the static electricity generated by the friction, their translucent bodies glowing a fierce, interior violet. 

When a Glint dies, it does not rot. It simply stops moving and hardens. Over centuries, these husks are absorbed into the pillars, becoming the "knots" in the wood of the world, providing structural reinforcement for the next layer of growth.

### The Apex: The Shatter-Saints
High in the light-drenched upper reaches, where the Pulse-Moths congregate in clouds that look like burning nebulae, live the **Shatter-Saints**. These are the Striders mentioned in the ancient scrolls, but seen up close, they are terrifyingly complex. Their legs are not solid, but are composed of thousands of independent, interlocking "vertebrae" of white silica, held together by sheer magnetic tension. 

The Shatter-Saints are the musicians of the Xero-Marrow. They move with a rhythmic, percussive gait, their footsteps calculated to trigger controlled collapses of unstable Thicket sections. They are the gardeners of catastrophe; by shattering a dying pillar, they clear space for the frantic "Scavenger-Vines" to weave new, stronger architectures. They are worshipped by the Vellum-Kin as gods of the necessary break.

### The Micro-Ecology: The Grinding-Swarm
In the "air" of the Xero-Marrow—which is less a gas and more a suspension of pulverized world-matter—lives the **Grinding-Swarm**. These are the Needle-Sworms, but at a microscopic level, they act as the world’s digestive system. They are tiny, winged crystals with serrated edges. As they fly, they collide with everything—the plants, the animals, the humans—acting as a constant, gentle abrasive. 

They wear down the old to make way for the new. A Vellum-Kin hunter who stays still for too long will find their leather garments slowly sanded away by the air itself. This constant erosion is why everything in the Xero-Marrow is polished to a high sheen; the world is being perpetually buffed by a trillion tiny jewelers.

### The Litho-Parasites
Attached to the undersides of the great horizontal boles are the **Resin-Bells**. These are stationary animals that look like clusters of translucent organ pipes. They have no senses other than a profound sensitivity to heat. They wait for a Pulse-Moth or a wayward Vellum-Kin to pass beneath them, then they drop a single, needle-thin strand of dry, hyper-tensile silk. 

This silk is not sticky—it is *hot*. Through kinetic friction, the strand vibrates at such a high frequency that it slices through chitin and bone like a wire through wax. The Resin-Bell then reels in the severed mineral-rich parts, incorporating the calcium and silica directly into its own tubular body, which rings out a hollow, mournful note whenever the wind catches it.

### The Absence of the Soul
There is no concept of "rest" among the denizens of the Xero-Marrow. To stop moving is to become a part of the substrate—to be woven over, sanded down, or calcified. The inhabitants live in a state of **Holy Agitation**. Their hearts (or the dry, thrumming pumps that serve as hearts) beat at four hundred cycles per minute. They are creatures of the kiln, forged in a world that is an eternal, dry explosion, living lives that are short, brilliant, and as sharp as a broken shard of glass.
