In this cathedral of static production, the inhabitants are not born; they are precipitated. They do not possess flesh, for flesh is a compromise of moisture and frailty that the Furnace-Garden does not recognize. Instead, the life here is an exercise in metallurgy and optics—beings of tempered grace who move with the agonizing deliberation of cooling lava.

**The Refractories**
The dominant denizens are the Refractories, towering tripod-like entities that stand twenty feet tall, their bodies constructed from interlocking plates of iridescent bismuth and matte-black tungsten. They do not have faces, but rather a single, massive lens of polished sapphire at their crown that drinks in the pooling light. 

They are the shepherds of the "plants." A Refractory will stand over a single copper-wire fern for three hundred years, its long, spindly limbs—tipped with precision-ground diamond needles—gently adjusting the tension of a metallic coil or polishing the surface of a ceramic gear to a mirror finish. They move so slowly that a layer of mineral dust often settles in the joints of their armor, only to puff away in a sudden cloud of ozone when they finally shift a limb. They communicate through sub-audible vibrations transmitted through the basalt floor, a language of pure mathematics and thermal resonance.

**The Scintilla**
Skittering across the crystalline plates are the Scintilla. These are the "insects" of the garden, though they resemble clockwork hummingbirds made of blown glass and quicksilver. They do not fly—the air is too thin and parched for wings—but they glide upon the magnetic currents emitted by the earth-engine. 

The Scintilla serve as the pollinators of heat. They harbor a small, white-hot core of plasma within their translucent abdomens. They dart from one gold-leaf fan to another, discharging a tiny spark of static electricity into the metallic foliage. This spark triggers the "assembly" of the plant, providing the momentary surge of energy required to fuse a new glass filament or click a ceramic gear into its next position. Their movement is the only thing in the Garden that is fast; they are streaks of violet light against the obsidian, gone before the *tink* of their landing can reach an observer's ears.

**The Time-Eaters (The Chronovores)**
Near the obsidian pillars that vent ribbons of distorted time, one finds the most elusive residents. They are silhouettes of shimmering air, barely visible except for the way they bend the light around them, like a heat haze given a vague, serpentine form. 

These beings do not consume calories; they consume the "friction" of passing time. They wrap themselves around the venting pillars, drinking the distorted temporal ribbons until they become heavy and sluggish. When a Chronovore is full, it crystallizes, turning into a motionless statue of translucent amber-glass. It will remain in this state for a millennium, slowly digesting the seconds it has swallowed, before shattering back into a gaseous, flickering ghost to begin the cycle again.

**The Anodized Hives**
Deep in the hollows where the light pools like oil, there are communal structures that look like vast, inverted chandeliers made of brass. These are the Hives of the Anodized—small, geometric drones that resemble faceted garnets. They are the Garden’s janitors and scribes. 

They do not think individually; they are a distributed intelligence, a cloud of clicking stones. Their sole purpose is to maintain the "industry." If a basalt ridge cracks prematurely or a glass leaf shatters, the Anodized descend. With tiny, high-frequency lasers emitted from their facets, they weld the world back together, smoothing the sharp edges of the reality-fold and ensuring the "unintentional industry" never grinds to a halt.

**The Presence**
Finally, there is the sense that the Garden itself is a singular, sleeping consciousness. The low-frequency drone is not just a machine; it is a breath. The inhabitants are not separate from the terrain, but are extensions of it—the Refractories are the Garden’s fingers, the Scintilla its nervous system, and the Chronovores its dreams. They exist in a state of perfect, thermal equilibrium, never hungry, never tired, functioning as the vital components of a machine that produces nothing but its own continued, shimmering existence.
