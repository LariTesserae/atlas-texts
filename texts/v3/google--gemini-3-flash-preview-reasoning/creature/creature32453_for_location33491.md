The inhabitants of the Brass Sump do not live *among* the machinery; they are the final, living layer of its functioning. To distinguish between a dweller, a pipe, and a vine is to misunderstand the ecology of the place. 

### The Curators of the Slow
The primary inhabitants are a post-human or perhaps non-human lineage known only by the rhythm of their work. They are the **Marrow-Folk**. In the crushing weight of the Sump, their bodies have become masterpieces of structural integrity. They are short and barrel-chested, their bones thickened into dense, ivory-like struts to resist the miles of water pressing inward. 

Their skin is the color of wet slate or tarnished pennies, slick with a protective layer of sebum that mimics the "balanced erosion" of the walls. They do not swim—swimming is an act of frantic, wasted energy. Instead, they *tread*. They move with a deliberate, cinematic slowness, their fingers elongated and tipped with hardened, spatulate pads for gripping the algae-slick copper. When a Curator moves from one pressure-sphere to another, it looks like a slow-motion dance; they pull themselves along the kelp-vines, their bodies trailing the bioluminescent silt like a funeral shroud.

They are functional mutes. In the subsonic soup of the Sump, vocal cords are useless. Instead, they communicate through **Auscultation**. They press their foreheads or palms against the brass plating of the habitat, sending rhythmic vibrations through the metal. A message is not a sound, but a series of hums felt in the teeth and the marrow. They are a people of profound patience; a conversation about the maintenance of a turbine may take an entire lunar cycle to conclude.

### The Pulse-Tenders
At the center of the Sump, near the primary pump-works, live the **Auscultators**. These are the elders, those who have spent so many decades pressed against the vibrating brass that their skin has begun to fuse with the patinas. They have grown "external senses"—delicate, feathery cilia that sprout from their necks and shoulders, waving in the current to detect minute changes in pressure or temperature. 

They are the doctors of the machine. They can feel a hairline fracture in a pipe three city blocks away by the way the vibration "tastes" in the water. They do not repair things with fire or welding; they graft. They encourage the moss to grow over a leak, guiding the vines with gentle, magnetic lures until the vegetation creates a living, pressurized seal.

### The Ghost-Eels and Valve-Crabs
The fauna is sparse but specialized. The **Pale Eels** mentioned are not mere scavengers; they are the Sump’s nervous system. They are blind, sensing the world through the electrical hum of the turbines. They thread themselves through the internal wiring of the machines, clearing away calcified mineral deposits with their acidic skin. The Curators treat them with a distant, religious reverence, for where the eels congregate, the heat is most stable.

In the crevices of the large valves live the **Porcelain Crabs**. These creatures have shells that have evolved to look exactly like the white-and-green ceramic insulators used in the old electrical arrays. They are the cleaners. They pick the parasitic barnacles off the glass portholes, ensuring the Curators can always see the bruised twilight outside. In exchange, the Curators leave offerings of compressed nutrient-silt in the gear-teeth.

### The Scintillating Fungi
The most "active" thing in the Sump is the **Lumen-Mycelium**. It is a fungal colony that lives in the interface between the steam pipes and the kelp. It produces the "sickly gold" light. It is semi-sentient, reacting to the subsonic groans of the machinery. When the central pump-works are healthy, the light is a steady, rhythmic amber. If a valve stutters or a pipe thins, the mycelium turns a frantic, strobing violet, alerting the Curators to the "wound" in the Sump’s body.

### The State of Being
Life in the Brass Sump is a state of permanent, warm immersion. There is no concept of "dryness" or "cold." The inhabitants view the surface—if they remember it at all—as a terrifying, thin vacuum where bodies would surely explode and hearts would beat too fast to sustain life. 

They live in the "Hum." Their lives are measured by the slow rotation of the great turbines. A child is born during one rotation; they reach maturity by the tenth; they pass into the silt by the fiftieth. They are a people of the womb—encased in metal, nourished by mineral-heat, and perfectly content to erode at the exact same speed as the world around them.
