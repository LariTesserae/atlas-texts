At the base of this hierarchy—if a crushing, horizontal density can be said to have a base—are the **Lichen-Mouths**. These are the finger-sized carpets of the basin, the primary layer that coats the polished basalt. They are little more than flat, muscular discs of pale, shivering flesh, equipped with undersides that act like industrial suction cups. They do not hunt; they "drink" the rock. Their ventral surfaces secrete a mild, localized acid that breaks down the water-smoothed mineral shelf, allowing them to absorb the ancient sediments and the weepings of the walls. They are the living skin of the basin, and when they move, it is with the sound of a thousand wet tongues peeling off a mirror.

Above them, scuttling through the gaps and over the backs of the Lichen-Mouths, are the **Lithoclasts**. These are the armored clackers, the size of hounds but shaped like flattened trilobites. They possess scores of jointed, needle-thin legs that find purchase in the microscopic pores of the stone and the soft tissue of the creatures beneath them. They are the cleaners and the grinders. Their carapaces are not bone or chitin, but a biological ceramic, a composite of the minerals they consume. They move in frantic, geometric patterns, their eyeless heads swinging rhythmically to catch the vibrations of the larger beasts. They communicate by drumming their carapaces against the floor, a sound that, when multiplied by millions, creates the "stones in a tumbler" roar. They are the primary recyclers; if a creature slows down, the Lithoclasts do not wait for it to die. They simply begin to vibrates their saw-toothed mandibles against its edges, turning flesh back into the collective slurry.

Rising through the press like slow-motion ghosts are the **Vellum-Whips**. These are the soft, translucent entities, sometimes stretching ten feet in length but remaining thin as a veil. They are the source of the basin's cold, internal glow. Their bodies are filled with a pressurized, bioluminescent fluid that pulses in time with the planet’s "inhalation." They do not have legs; they move by undulating their entire bodies, sliding through the slick condensation and over the armored plates of the Lithoclasts. They are sensory organs for the basin itself. Their skin is so thin it can detect the displacement of air from a mile away. They feed by trailing long, sticky filaments—like gossamer threads—into the heavy air, catching the microscopic mineral dust and the sloughed-off skin cells of the screaming swarm.

The "trees"—the calcified spires—are home to the **Siphon-Clingers**. These creatures are vertically oriented, wrapped tightly around the mineral towers like living bandages. They have become part of the architecture. Their bodies are rigid, calcified on the outside to match the spire, but their interiors are a churning mass of digestive enzymes. They wait for the condensation to trail down the spires, filtering the water through their bodies. They are the only creatures that are relatively still, contributing to the "shushing" sound of the basin as they constantly vent excess moisture through specialized pores, creating a localized mist around every pillar.

Finally, there are the **Goliaths**, the siege-engine leviathans. These are the size of houses, slab-sided masses of muscle and thick, rubbery hide that move with a terrifying, tectonic slowness. They do not have limbs so much as undulating "treads" of flesh. They are the source of the sub-bass thrumming that vibrates the ribs of any visitor. The Goliaths are the apex of the basin, but they are not predators in the traditional sense. They are mobile filtration systems. As they plow through the carpet of smaller creatures, they leave a wake of crushed biology behind them, which they then re-absorb through pores in their trailing edges. They are so massive that smaller creatures simply live *on* them, entire generations of Lichen-Mouths and Lithoclasts being born, living, and dying on the moving continent of a Goliath’s back without ever touching the actual basalt floor.

In this place, there is no sleep. The meat-engine does not stop. The "identity" of an individual creature is secondary to the "flow" of the biomass. They are a single, screaming organism distributed across a billion bodies, a frantic, wet, and mineral-hungry tide that has forgotten the sun and replaced the sky with the heavy, rhythmic weight of their own collective breath. It is a world where to be still is to be consumed, and to be silent is to be non-existent.
