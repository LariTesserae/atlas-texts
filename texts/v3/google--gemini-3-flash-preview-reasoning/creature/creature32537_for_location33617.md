To live in the Vertical Strands is to exist in a state of perpetual attachment. Here, life is not something that walks upon a surface, but something that clings, weaves, or floats within the updrafts of the Great Reeds.

### The Pulse-Dwellers
At the heart of the cacophony are the **Resonance-Kites**. These are not birds, but translucent, rib-caged organisms with skin like stretched parchment. They do not fly so much as they anchor themselves over the ancient erosion flutes of the Reeds. As the wind whistles through these vertical grooves, the Kites expand their throat-sacs to catch the vibration, amplifying the world’s flute-like wail into a booming, low-frequency thrum that they use to stun the smaller insects buzzing in the mists.

Deep within the hollows of the Reeds live the **Piston-Wasps**. These creatures have evolved to exploit the metabolic heat of the cellulose. They are the size of a man’s forearm, with exoskeletons that mimic the polished, bone-white texture of the Reed itself. They don't build nests; they use their vibrating abdomens to drill rhythmic bore-holes into the dead cellulose, creating honeycombed galleries that hum with the Reed's internal heat. When they emerge, they move in terrifying, synchronized bursts, their wings clicking with a metallic sharpness that adds a percussion layer to the roar of the Strands.

### The Velvet Predators
Amidst the "total vegetation" of the mats, the distinction between flora and fauna is a shifting border. The **Strangler-Orchids** are the dominant predators of the green carpet. They possess no roots, only a network of prehensile, capillary-thin filaments that "walk" across the moss at a rate of inches per day. They do not bite; they simply grow *over* their prey, a slow-motion consumption that replaces the victim’s tissue with their own vibrant, violet blooms. 

Beneath the moss layers, the **Grip-Saurians** lurk. These are six-limbed, flat-bodied reptiles with pads of microscopic hooks on their bellies, allowing them to sprint vertically up the smooth cellulose as if they were running on flat ground. They have no eyes—the sunlit glare of the open gulfs is too intense—but they possess "vibration-pits" along their flanks, allowing them to "see" the approach of a Drummer-Beetle or a human climber through the shivering of the Reed’s skin.

### The Drifters of the Void
In the miles of empty air between the spires, life is nomadic and buoyant. The **Float-Drifters** mentioned in the geography are more than just moss-colonies; they are aging, sentient bio-balloons. As a moss-clump grows too heavy for the Reed, it secretes a lighter-than-air methane byproduct into a central bladder and detaches. 

These drifting islands are inhabited by the **Sky-Skippers**—small, lemur-like mammals with patagia (skin membranes) stretching from wrist to ankle. They spend their entire lives on a single drifting colony, grazing on its ferns and defending it from "Air-Barnacles" that try to weigh the colony down. If two Float-Drifters collide in the wind, a frantic, high-altitude war breaks out between the resident Skippers for control of the new, larger territory.

### The Tethered (The Human Presence)
The humans of the Strands, known to themselves as the **Weavers**, are a people defined by the "Horizontal Taboo." To them, the idea of a flat world is a terrifying myth. They are lean, long-limbed, and possess a prehensile strength in their toes that rivals their fingers. Their skin is often stained a pale emerald from the moss-dyes they use to waterproof their woven clothing.

They live in the **Hanging Baskets**, architecture that breathes with the wind. A Weaver’s home is a masterpiece of tension; it is suspended by hundreds of cables made of "Spider-Steel"—silk harvested from the **Gorge-Spiders** that weave webs miles long across the chasms. 

The Weavers are not hunters so much as they are harvesters of the "Vertical Rivers." They have developed a complex culture around the condensation grooves. Their "Water-Shamans" can read the vibrations of a Reed to predict when a mist-storm is coming, allowing the tribe to deploy their gourds and silk-funnels. They communicate across the vast gulfs using **Light-Lances**—polished shards of cellulose that reflect the turquoise sun in complex, flashing codes. 

To a Weaver, the greatest terror is "The Weight." It is a rare vestibular sickness where a person loses their sense of verticality and feels the crushing pull of the emerald gloom far below. Those who succumb are said to be "calling the mulch," and they are often found staring down into the steam, mesmerized by the abyss, until they simply let go.

### The Silence-Eaters
Finally, at the highest tips of the Reeds, where the air is too thin for the Drummer-Beetles and the roar of the carpet fades, lives the **Spire-Wraith**. It is a creature of pure white filament, looking like a tattered shroud caught in the wind. It has no mouth and makes no sound. It feeds on the high-altitude static electricity that builds up as the Reeds sway against the thin atmosphere. It is the only thing in the Vertical Strands that knows silence, drifting like a ghost around the tapering points of the world, watching the frantic, loud, and clinging life below with an indifference as cold as the void.
