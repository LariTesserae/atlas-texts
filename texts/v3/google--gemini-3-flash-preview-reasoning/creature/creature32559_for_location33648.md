The denizens of this industrial wilderness are not so much "inhabitants" as they are mobile components of the greater mechanism. To live here is to be a part of the friction, a lubricant in the gears of a world that is constantly grinding itself into new shapes.

### The Friction-Slicks (The Grease-Ghosts)
The most common residents are the ones the prompt calls "grease." These are the **Litho-mites**, creatures whose carapaces are not made of chitin, but of a biological alloy sequestered from the metallic-barks they graze upon. They are flat, multi-segmented discs, resembling a cross between a trilobite and a high-performance bearing. 

They do not walk; they vibrate. By oscillating their under-plates at high frequencies, they create a cushion of pressurized air, allowing them to glide over the oily surfaces of the turbine-leaves. They are the ultimate maintenance crew. Where two massive, ship-hull limbs grind together, the Litho-mites congregate, feeding on the "slough"—the fine, metallic dust created by the attrition. In return, their excrement is a high-viscosity, translucent oil that prevents the plant-state from seizing up or igniting from the heat of its own movement. They are the reason the "slow-motion riot" remains fluid rather than shattering into a trillion copper shards.

### The Valve-Weevils (The Gasket-Eaters)
Deep within the bellows, where the ribbed lungs draw in the thin, freezing air, live the **Valve-Weevils**. These organisms are soft-bodied but possess mouths shaped like precision torque wrenches. They attach themselves to the intake valves of the piston-flowers, filtering the pressurized sap for nutrients. 

They serve as living seals. When a plant-vein develops a leak—a hiss of translucent sap that could depressurize an entire limb—the Weevils sense the drop in pressure through their sensitive, wire-like antennae. They swarm to the breach, their bodies expanding and hardening upon contact with the external air, effectively "corking" the wound. They are the scar tissue of a machine that cannot afford to bleed.

### The Spark-Kites (Static-Feeders)
Above the bucking "floor," in the stroboscopic shadows of the canopy, drift the **Spark-Kites**. These are paper-thin, translucent creatures with wingspans of several yards, looking like sheets of iridescent plastic caught in an updraft. 

They are the only things that utilize the static electricity generated by the rotating seed-pods. They possess long, trailing filaments—biological copper wire—that they dangle near the grinding flint-husks. When a spark jumps, they catch it, storing the charge in specialized cells that glow with a faint, neon blue. They don't eat matter; they eat potential energy. They navigate the thin air by manipulating their own magnetic polarity against the massive static field of the plateau, rising and falling like iron filings in a magnetic dance.

### The Tremor-Ticks (The Acoustic Sponges)
Because the air is too thin to carry sound, the inhabitants are profoundly sensitive to vibration. The **Tremor-Ticks** are small, crystalline spiders that build webs not of silk, but of rigid, vibrating fibers stretched across the gaps between piston-stalks. 

They act as the plateau’s nervous system. They can sense a limb shearing off miles away, or the upward surge of a new spire before it even breaks the surface. They do not hunt; they "tune." By adjusting the tension of their webs, they can dampen specific frequencies, contributing to the absolute 0.0 silence of the plateau. They are the reason a falling freighter-sized limb makes no noise—they catch the vibration in their webs and convert it into a low-grade thermal energy, which they absorb.

### The Deep-Anchorites
Miles below, in the lightless pressure of the lower strata where the "old wood" is crushed into a semi-liquid state, live the **Anchorites**. These are massive, slow-moving gastropods with shells of dense, black lignin. 

They are the weight that keeps the sea from drifting. They possess "roots" instead of feet, which they drive deep into the petrified layers below. They feed on the heat generated by the immense pressure of the stratified layers above them. Every few centuries, an Anchorite will shift its position, causing a mile-wide section of the plateau surface to buckle and fold—a slow-motion earthquake that the plants above simply treat as a change in weather.

### The Nature of their Existence
There is no "predation" here in the traditional sense. Nothing hunts for meat, for there is little meat to be had. Instead, the fauna competes for **position**. To be in the right place to catch a spark, to be near a high-pressure leak, or to be positioned where the slough is thickest—this is the struggle. 

These creatures have no voices; they have no eyes for color, only for the flicker of light and shadow. They are as cold and efficient as the plants they inhabit. They are the ghosts in a machine that was never built, the accidental survivors of a world that is not a home, but a relentless, self-manufacturing process.
