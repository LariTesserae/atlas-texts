In the Throat of the Basin, life does not occur so much as it *persistently happens*. The inhabitants are not visitors to this cold, deafening gloom; they are its slow-moving, vibrating extensions.

**The Marrow-Stilled**
The primary inhabitants are the descendants of those who built the slate walls, though they have long since shed the qualities of the men who arrived here. They are the **Marrow-Stilled**. They are hairless, their skin a translucent, waxy grey that feels like the wet basalt they cling to. Having no need for speech in a 2.9 roar that would swallow a scream, they have lost their vocal cords to evolution’s pruning shears. Their ears have folded inward, sealed by thick, cartilaginous flaps to protect the brain from the physical trauma of the river’s vibration. 

They do not hear the sound; they *occupy* it. They move with a rhythmic, swaying gait, their bodies perpetually tuned to the frequency of the subterranean water. Their eyes are enormous, lidless black orbs that have migrated slightly toward the sides of their heads, capturing the 0.3 light with a specialized layer of reflective cells that glow with a faint, ghostly violet. They do not walk so much as they adhere, their long, spatulate fingers tipped with suction-like pads that allow them to scale the damp mossy inclines without disturbing a single spore. 

They communicate through "thrumming"—pressing their chests against the stone or against one another to pass vibrations directly through the bone. In this way, they share a singular, collective consciousness of the cave’s state: where the moss is thickest, where the water-chill has dropped a fraction of a degree, and where the river’s roar has shifted its pitch.

**The Glass-Winged Vesper**
The pale, heavy-bodied moths are the **Glass-Winged Vespers**. They are the size of a man’s palm, their wings not covered in dust but in a fine, hydrophobic oil that repels the silver condensation. They do not fly for joy; their flight is a desperate, heavy struggle against the thick air. They feed on the microscopic spores of the black moss, their long proboscises vibrating at the same frequency as the river. They are the only creatures that ever reach the "lost country" of the ceiling, where they cling to the jagged basalt in great, shivering clusters, looking like patches of un-melted frost.

**The Milk-Walkers**
In the hairline fractures of the stone live the **Milk-Walkers**. These blind crustaceans are the cleaners of the Throat. They are long and segmented, with dozens of hair-thin legs that move in a constant, undulating wave. They feed on the dead—on a fallen Vesper or a Marrow-Stilled who has finally become too slow to cling to the wall. They are the heralds of the transition from biology to geology. When a Milk-Walker consumes, it excretes a calcium-rich paste that hardens into the very stone it walks upon, effectively rebuilding the cave from the inside out.

**The Velvet Veil (The Sentient Moss)**
The most pervasive inhabitant is the moss itself, the **Velvet Veil**. It is not merely a plant but a slow, dreaming predator. It grows in fractal patterns that mimic the sound waves of the hidden river. It is the primary source of the "bruised purple" light, emitting a low-level bioluminescence as it digests the iron and flint minerals from the damp air. The moss is the memory of the Throat. If a hand is pressed into it, the impression will remain for decades, a slow-closing wound in the indigo carpet. It is said that the primitive tools and slate walls are not being buried by the moss, but are being *tasted* by it, their history slowly absorbed into the rootless fibers.

**The Atmosphere of Being**
There is no competition here. The Marrow-Stilled do not hunt the Vespers; the Vespers do not fear the Milk-Walkers. Everything is subservient to the **Roar**. The sound is the apex predator and the provider. It dictates the pace of every heartbeat and the growth of every moss-frond. To live here is to be a part of a long, cold exhale. The inhabitants are patient because time has no meaning when the light never changes and the noise never ends. They are simply the shivering, damp witnesses to the endurance of the stone.
