To live here is to exist in a state of constant, rhythmic compression. The inhabitants do not feel the weight of the water as a burden, but as a confirmation of their own presence; to them, the vacuum of the world above would feel like a violent dissolution. The residents of this bone-city are defined not by their sight, but by their sensitivity to the *thrum*—the low-frequency heartbeat of the earth and the subtle pressure-shifts of their neighbors.

### The Architects: The Calcinates
The primary citizens and the unconscious builders of the spires are the **Calcinates**. They are slow, polyp-like entities, communal and ancient, whose soft, translucent bodies are hidden deep within the porous marrow of the towers. They are not individuals so much as a singular, city-wide nervous system. They do not think in words, but in secretions. When a thermal plume shifts, the Calcinates feel the change in mineral content and begin to weep liquid calcium, hardening the spires toward the heat. They are the reason the city grows; they are the living marrow of the metropolis. 

### The Silk-Spinsters: The Bridge-Makers
In the narrow canyons between the bone-towers live the **Arachne-Nautilids**. These are spindly, multi-limbed crustaceans with shells the color of bruised ivory. They possess no eyes, only long, whip-like antennae that can detect a single scale falling a mile away. They are the city’s engineers, scurrying across the vertical faces of the sponge-cathedrals to weave the bridges of mucus and sea-silk mentioned in the histories. They move with a clicking, frantic grace, tethering spire to spire, ensuring that the city remains a single, interconnected web. They feed on the marine snow, harvesting the grey silt with delicate, feathery fans before it can settle and choke the pores of the Calcinates.

### The Lume-Merchants: The Candelabras
Suspended from the vaulted ceilings of the hollowed-out sponges are the **Candelabras**. These are stationary, colonial siphonophores—long, stinging ribbons of jelly that glow with a steady, haunting amber light. They are the streetlamps of the abyss. They do not hunt actively; they simply hang, beautiful and lethal, luring the smaller, ember-like crustaceans into their stinging nets. Their light is not for vision, but for cartography; the other inhabitants use the specific frequency of a Candelabra’s glow to know exactly which district of the city they are traversing.

### The Nobility: The Vellum-Wraiths
The massive, translucent rays that glide through the corridors are known as the **Vellum-Wraiths**. They are the undisputed masters of the currents. Their skin is so thin it is almost invisible, revealing the pulsing, pale-blue bioluminescence of their internal organs. They move like ink dropped in water—fluid, silent, and hauntingly beautiful. The Wraiths are the record-keepers of the deep; they communicate by rippling their fins in specific patterns, creating pressure waves that carry complex "histories" to others of their kind. They do not touch the bone-city; they hover above it, their shadows (if there were light to cast them) would be the only things larger than the cathedrals themselves.

### The Lurkers: The Onyx-Gulpers
In the dark, hollowed "windows" of the spires, the **Onyx-Gulpers** wait. These eels are the color of an oil slick, their bodies long and muscled, ending in mouths that can unhinge to swallow prey twice their size. They are the city’s necessary predators, the enforcers of the biological balance. They are motionless for days, appearing as nothing more than a knot of shadow, until a school of silver fish pulses by. Then, a single flash of their bioluminescent lure—a tongue like a burning coal—draws the prey into the dark.

### The Dirigibles: The Siphon-Whales
High above the spires, drifting in the slower, colder currents, are the **Siphon-Whales**. They are not mammals, but gargantuan, gas-filled sacks of living tissue, trailing miles of digestive filaments like the roots of a floating island. They are the city’s weather-makers. As they drift, their massive intake of water creates the "hiss" and the "thrum" that the city breathes by. They are the gargoyles and the clouds of this world, ancient and indifferent, their low-frequency moans providing the soundtrack to a civilization that has never seen the sun.

In this city, there is no "away." Everything is consumed, recycled, and secreted back into the architecture. To inhabit this place is to be a part of a single, breathing organism, a cell in a body of bone and warm, heavy water. It is a life of tactile intimacy, where the touch of a current is a conversation and the heat of the floor is the only God.
