In the Basin of the Scalloped Kilns, life does not merely inhabit the architecture; it has become a secondary layer of grout, a living mortar that fills the cracks of the world. 

### The Knot-Weavers
The most prominent inhabitants are the architects of the void: the **Knot-Weavers**. Spindly, long-limbed hominids with skin the texture of sun-dried chamois, they possess fingers with an extra joint, allowing them to tie complex hitches with a single hand. They are the tenders of the rope bridges and the keepers of the verticality. 

The Weavers do not walk so much as they "swing-slide," moving along the hempen arteries of the Basin with a rhythmic, pendulum-like grace. They are functionally blind to distance but hyper-sensitive to vibration; they communicate by plucking the tensioned ropes of the bridges, sending coded shivers through the wood and fiber that can be felt blocks away. They dress in "shingle-cloaks" made of small, flat pottery shards sewn onto heavy lichen-fiber, which clack softly as they move, adding a metallic percussion to the Basin’s drone. To a Weaver, the Basin is not a room, but a giant, multi-stringed instrument which they must constantly tune.

### The Cotta-kin (The Scuttlers)
Below the bridges, clinging to the vertical faces, are the **Cotta-kin**. These are the "half-rodent, half-lizards" mentioned in the scrolls, but closer inspection reveals they are perfectly evolved for a world of fired earth. Their bellies are flat and slightly porous, acting like suction cups against the smooth terracotta. Their blood is unusually warm, even for mammals, allowing them to store the heat of the sun-shafts like living bricks. 

They are obsessed with the "Leaking Rains." A Cotta-kin will claim a single water-runnel as its territory, spending hours using its broad, sandpaper-like tongue to lick the grout lines clean of obstructing lichen, ensuring the precious moisture flows unhindered into the collection bowls. They are the janitors of the geometry, maintaining the flow that keeps the Basin alive. When the sun hits the floor and the thermal updrafts rise, the Cotta-kin flatten themselves against the walls and puff out their throat-sacs, catching the warm air to cool their internal organs.

### The Shard-Crabs
At the base of the chamber, where the "hills" of broken pottery accumulate, live the **Shard-Crabs**. These are heavy, slow-moving crustaceans whose shells are not grown, but assembled. As they grow, they secrete a natural, epoxy-like resin and glue discarded ceramic fragments to their backs. An elder Shard-Crab looks like a walking mosaic, a jagged mound of blue, ochre, and burnt-umber shards that camouflages it perfectly against the dunes of debris. They are the decomposers, crushing fallen lichen, dropped rope-fibers, and the occasional unlucky Scuttler between powerful, ceramic-tipped pincers.

### The Arch-Owls
The apex of the Basin’s food chain is the **Arch-Owl**. These birds do not have soft, silent feathers; instead, their plumage is stiff and waxy, patterned in a perfect imitation of running-bond brickwork. They do not flap their wings—the air in the Basin is too crowded with bridges for traditional flight. Instead, they are masters of the "controlled fall." 

An Arch-Owl will perch on a high, dark lintel for days, indistinguishable from a decorative carving, until it spots a Scuttler crossing a sun-drenched patch of wall. The owl then tucks its wings and drops, a heavy, terracotta-colored stone falling through the void. At the last micro-second, it flares its wings to create a thunderous "crack" of air—a sonic stall—that stuns its prey before its talons find purchase in the brick.

### The Grout-Mites
Finally, there are the invisible billions: the **Grout-Mites**. These microscopic insects live within the very pores of the terracotta. They feed on the minerals in the clay and the salt left behind by the evaporating "Leaking Rains." Their collective respiration is what gives the Basin its peculiar smell—a scent like a hot sidewalk after a summer thunderstorm. They are the reason the bricks are so smooth; over millennia, their constant grazing has polished the surfaces of the Kilns into the soft, organic curves that define the landscape.

In the Basin, nothing is wasted. The Weaver dies and falls to the Shard-Crabs; the Shard-Crab’s resin eventually fails, and its shell joins the dunes; the dust of the dunes is gathered by the Weavers to patch the grout of their nesting-holes. It is a closed-loop pulse, a frantic, terracotta-colored heartbeat inside a stone chest that forgot how to open.
