In this place, the dwellers do not possess the soft, wet anatomy of the worlds you know. Here, biology is a branch of geometry; life is an expression of the edge.

### The Grid-Minds (The Spires)
The primary inhabitants are the spires themselves. They are not merely "buildings"—they are the citizens, the thinkers, and the sovereigns of the basin. Each shard of translucent crystal is a discrete unit of consciousness, though they do not think in words or feelings. They think in **Integers**. 

To be a spire is to be a frozen calculation. Their "nervous system" is the lattice of internal phosphorescence that pulses in their depths. Because the air carries no sound, they converse through the white flashes that ripple across the grid when a new facet snaps into existence. This is their language: a binary of growth. One spire’s sudden jagged expansion is a statement; the neighbor’s subsequent twitch is a rebuttal. They are a civilization of statues, locked in a millennia-long debate conducted in the medium of solid mineral.

They feel only the "thirst" of the氮 (nitrogen) and the cold tension of their own internal pressure. They do not have faces, but they have orientation. Every needle-point is an eye that perceives the indigo sky, not as a color, but as a specific level of electromagnetic vacuum.

### The Skimmers (The Gliders)
Navigating the "streets" between the Grid-Minds are the **Skimmers**. If the spires are the thinkers, the Skimmers are the memories. They are paper-thin, nearly invisible to the naked eye except for the way they distort the reflection of the violet sky on the obsidian floor.

They do not walk; they exist in a state of permanent repulsion. Their underside carries a high static charge that pushes against the vitrified floor, allowing them to drift on a molecular cushion of air. They are the only things that move with a horizontal intent. 

The Skimmers are the nervous system of the city-organism. As they slide between the sharp towers, they graze on the "static ghosts" left behind by the spires' growth. When a spire snaps into a new shape, it releases a microscopic discharge of energy. The Skimmers flock to these events, hovering like translucent kites, absorbing the discharge. They do not eat; they *balance*. They carry charge from the dense urban centers to the lonely outskirts, acting as living capacitors that keep the city's electrical potential uniform.

### The Shard-Gnashers (The Stiff-Moss)
The "vegetation" is less a plant and more a predatory chemical reaction. The **Stiff-Moss**—those silver, brittle filaments—are the recyclers. They feed on the imperfection of the spires. 

Despite the city’s strict grid, the growth is not always perfect. Occasionally, a spire will grow with a microscopic flaw, a jagged burr that disrupts the mathematical purity of the basin. The Moss finds these flaws. It crawls up the crystalline base, its filaments vibrating at a frequency so high it is silent and invisible. This vibration slowly "digests" the crystal, turning it from a solid into a fine, glass-like dust. 

When a patch of Moss shatters, it is a reproductive act. It has reached a state of maximum tension, having consumed enough mineral to be "full." It explodes, scattering its silver spores (which are essentially microscopic needles) to the next spire. They are the only thing in the basin that "eats," and they are the only thing the Grid-Minds "fear"—to a crystal, a flaw is death.

### The Great Geometric Mean (The Below)
Crucial to this ecosystem is the thing that lives *under* the floor. Beneath the obsidian sheet lies the **Sub-Slab**, a singular, planet-sized consciousness of which the city is merely the "blossom."

The rhythmic heaving of the ground is the Sub-Slab’s metabolism. It is a massive, lightless intelligence that operates on a geological timescale. It pushes the raw minerals up through the floor to feed the spires. It is the architect of the grid. If you could hear it, it would be a low, sub-sonic thrum; because you cannot, it is simply a terrifying, silent tilt of the world. 

The Sub-Slab does not distinguish between itself and the spires. To the Slab, the city is a sensory organ—a way for a buried, dark god to taste the indigo sky and the thin nitrogen wind.

### The Dynamics of Interaction
There is no "socializing." There is only **Alignment**. 
*   When the Skimmers cluster too thickly on a plaza, the Spires will flash in a specific sequence, a "visual heat" that drives the translucent ghosts away. 
*   When the Moss becomes too invasive, the Spire will undergo a "Purge-Growth," snapping a new layer of crystal so quickly and violently that the silver filaments are physically ejected, shattered into dust by the sudden expansion of their host.

Everything here is a struggle for the perfect angle. It is a world inhabited by things that are at once brittle and permanent, fragile and lethally sharp. They live for the **Snap**—that intoxicating, silent moment when a new edge is born, and the world becomes slightly more complex than it was a second before.
