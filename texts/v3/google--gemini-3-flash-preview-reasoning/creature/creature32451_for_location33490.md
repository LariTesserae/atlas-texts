The inhabitants of the Valve-Garden do not live *in* the Sump so much as they are *distilled* from it. They are the **Lung-Tenders**, a lineage of slow-blooded survivors who have forgotten the concept of a horizon. 

### The Lung-Tenders
Physically, the Tenders have become mirrors of the environment. Their skin is not truly skin; it is a pale, waxy tegument, translucent enough to show the sluggish crawl of dark, oxygen-starved blood beneath. They are perpetually slick, coated in a fine, naturally occurring film of the same oily resin the machines produce. This serves as a secondary barrier against the heat, a biological cooling jacket that allows them to press their bellies against the scalding brass of the Great Pistons without blistering.

They move with a terrifying, liquid economy. A Lung-Tender may take ten minutes to reach for a wrench, their hand gliding through the thick, humid air as if moving through deep water. This is not due to weakness, but to a profound synchronization with the rhythm of the Sump. To move quickly is to generate friction, and friction is death in a place that is already fever-hot. 

Their eyes are large, amber, and lidless, protected by a thick, gelatinous membrane that filters the constant spray of condensation. They do not speak. The muffling moss and the sub-bass groan of the pistons have rendered vocal cords obsolete. Instead, they communicate through "Tapping"—a complex language of rhythmic strikes against the iron pipes. A sharp *clack-clack* on a steam line can carry a warning across the entire Stack, vibrating through the soles of every inhabitant’s feet.

### The Moss-Wrights
Deep within the crevices of the Stack live the Moss-Wrights, the elders who have ceased to move entirely. These are individuals who have chosen a final resting place against the warm, vibrating walls of the basalt throat. Over decades, the aggressive purple ferns and translucent vines have treated them as mere architecture. 

The Wrights are half-submerged in flora; their limbs are entwined with copper wiring, and their ribcages serve as trellises for the leather-leafed ferns. They are the living memory of the Sump. They do not eat; they absorb nutrients through the porous membranes of their skin, fed by the humid waste of the machinery. To the younger Tenders, a Moss-Wright is a sacred site—a living gauge. By observing the pulse in a Wright’s neck, a Tender can tell if the pressure in the Great Pistons is reaching a catastrophic limit.

### The Scavenger Hierarchy
Beneath the Tenders, the **Ghost-Crabs** are more than just pests; they are the janitors of the mechanical gut. These crabs have developed elongated, needle-like pincers designed specifically to reach into the teeth of moving gears to pluck out bits of calcified mineral or stray vegetation. 

There is a strange, wordless bond between the Tenders and the Crabs. The Tenders will often remain motionless for hours to allow a Ghost-Crab to pick the salt-deposits from their ears or the algae from their fingernails. In return, the Tenders protect the crabs from the **Valve-Leeches**—bloated, segmented mollusks that cling to the underside of the walkways, waiting to drop onto anything that breaks the rhythm of the stagnant air.

### The Geometry of Survival
Life in the Stack is entirely vertical. The inhabitants do not have furniture in the traditional sense. They live in "Slings"—heavy, oil-treated canvas hammocks suspended by rusted chains between the basalt walls and the iron scaffolds. 

Domestic life is a series of slow, deliberate maintenances. A family (though the term is loose, defined more by who shares a particular steam-vent than by blood) spends their waking hours polishing the brass gauges with handfuls of crushed moss and ensuring the vines do not choke the intake valves. They treat the machinery with a fearful, religious awe. To them, the Great Pistons are not tools; they are the lungs of the world. If the pistons stop, the heat will stagnate, the condensation will cease, and the "breath" of the Sump will turn into a suffocating vacuum.

### The "Oilers"
The most active (relatively speaking) members of this society are the Oilers. These are the youths who climb the outer skin of the Stack, carrying ceramic jugs of the thick, distilled resin. They move through the highest, hottest reaches where the steam is sharpest, pouring the lubricant into the ancient, groaning joints of the world. They are often stained a deep, permanent indigo from the plant dyes they use to mark which valves have been serviced. 

When an Oiler dies, they are not buried. There is no soil for a grave. Instead, they are placed in the path of the Great Pistons. The agonizingly slow descent of the massive iron foot takes an hour to make contact, pressing the body into the basalt floor until it is nothing more than a thin, dark layer of carbon—a new strata in the history of the Sump, reinforcing the very foundation of the Garden.

In the Valve-Garden, there is no ambition, no progress, and no light of the sun. There is only the wet, heavy pulse of the earth, and the people who have become the grease in its throat.
