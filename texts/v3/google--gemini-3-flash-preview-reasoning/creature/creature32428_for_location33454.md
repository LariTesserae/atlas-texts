In the Ocher Huddles, the line between the biological, the geological, and the mechanical has been rubbed thin by the constant friction of heat and time. Those who remain are not merely inhabitants; they are extensions of the pillars, slaves to the thermals, and curators of the damp.

### The Sumpters
The primary residents of the high fissures are the Sumpters, the descendants of the original industrial crews. They have evolved—or perhaps eroded—into something perfectly suited for the verticality of the Huddles. A Sumpter is a spindly, elongated creature, their limbs possessing extra joints that allow them to fold their bodies flat against the sandstone. Their skin has the texture of wet chamois, a mottled ocher and grey that makes them invisible when they press into the shadows.

They do not speak. The thin air makes vocal cords a luxury of the past. Instead, they communicate through a tactile sign language performed on the brass housings of the old machines. They tap out rhythmic codes that travel through the oxidized pipes, using the entire industrial network as a massive, vibrating telegraph. They are the "keepers of the sweat," spending their lives harvesting the suspended water globules into bladders made of manta-hide. They move with a agonizing, slow-motion grace; to move fast is to generate friction, and to generate friction in this heat is to die.

### The Scriptors (The Iridescent Beetles)
The millions of beetles that crawl in geometric patterns are more than just scavengers. To the Sumpters, they are the "Scriptors." Their movements are not random. They are attracted to the subtle electromagnetic pulses of the deep-heat turbines. As they follow the invisible ley lines of energy, they polish the rock with their carapaces, leaving behind shining, mirrored paths. 

These paths serve as a map for everything else. The Sumpters follow the beetles' trails to find the most stable heat-vents. The beetles themselves live in a collective trance, their individual lives unimportant, their only purpose to act as a living, shifting skin for the pillars. When one dies, it does not fall; its neighbors simply incorporate its shell into the geometry, pinning it to the rock with a fast-drying, resinous spit.

### The Lubricants
Living within the rusted guts of the great turbines and piston-elevators is a species of soft-bodied, pale mammals known as Lubricants. They are roughly the size of a cat but possess no skeleton, only a complex system of cartilaginous struts. They secrete a thick, oily tallow from their pores that smells faintly of toasted cinnamon and old iron. 

They are the reason the machines still work. They slide through the gears and cylinders, their bodies acting as living grease. In exchange for the safety of the mechanical hollows and the warmth of the friction, they keep the ancient metal from seizing. The Sumpters treat them with a quiet, religious reverence, feeding them scraps of lichen and fermented manta-meat to keep their oil flowing.

### The Ocher Vanes (The Mantas)
The great flat-bodied mantas, the Vanes, are the only creatures that truly own the open air. Their skin is not just the color of rust; it *is* rust, impregnated with the metallic dust of the eroding pipes. They are living solar sails. During the day, they rise to the highest peaks, spreading their wings to catch the scouring sun, absorbing the raw energy. 

They have no mouths in the traditional sense. They breathe through porous membranes on their undersides, filtering the mineral-rich steam that vents from the pillars. They are the shepherds of the thermals. Sometimes, a Sumpter will hitch a ride on a Vane, hooking a chain into the creature’s leathery trailing edges to be towed across a chasm. The Vanes do not seem to notice; they are heavy with the weight of the sky, preoccupied with the rhythmic oscillation that keeps them aloft.

### The Still-Hunters
Among the hanging cables and the groaning pipes sits the apex predator of the Huddles: the Still-Hunter. These are hawk-like in silhouette but reptilian in nature. They are masters of the "active stillness." Their metabolism is so slow that they can go months without a heartbeat, their bodies cooling to the temperature of the stone. 

They do not hunt by sight, for the shimmer of the heat makes eyes unreliable. Instead, they have sensitive pits along their snouts that detect the minute changes in the air’s density. When a Sumpter or a Scriptor breaks the rhythm of the thermal, the Still-Hunter uncoils. It does not fly; it drops. It uses gravity as its primary weapon, falling like a stone to strike its prey before using its hooked talons to haul itself back up the rusted chains, one agonizingly slow pull at a time.

### The Ghost-Workers
Deep within the hollowed-out summits of the largest pillars, there are whispers of the Ghost-Workers. These are not biological beings, nor are they quite machines. They are the "Residuals"—autonomous, brass-encased automatons that have been running for centuries. They are the ones who actually manage the gauges and the hissing vents. 

They are rarely seen, moving only in the deep violet twilight of the lower reaches. They are tall, clanking things with many-fingered hands, obsessed with the maintenance of the pressure levels. They do not acknowledge the Sumpters or the wildlife. They are the silent priests of the Ocher Huddles, ensuring that the planet’s deep heat never builds to an explosion, but never fades to a chill. They are the heartbeat of the cathedral, and as long as their pistons hiss, the Huddles remain alive.
