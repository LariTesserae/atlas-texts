The inhabitants of the Viridian Lacuna do not merely live within the green engine; they are the oil between its screeching gears, the nervous system of a body that is too large and too fast to coordinate itself.

### The Lignin-Folk (The Tenders)
The primary inhabitants are a race of tall, shockingly thin humanoids whose biology seems to have surrendered to the flora around them. Their skin is not flesh, but a supple, leathery rind, the color of a bruised lime, translucent enough that the golden light of the Lacuna shines through their limbs. When they stand before a glowing leaf-vein, you can see their internal organs—not wet and pulsing, but a dense, packed system of fibrous tubers and capillary bundles.

They lack hair, eyes, and ears in the traditional sense. Instead, their heads are topped with a crown of sensitive, fern-like fronds that shiver in response to the "Active" air. They do not see the world; they *feel* the vibrations of the grinding cellulose. To them, the Lacuna is a symphony of structural stress. They move with a jerky, stop-motion grace, using their elongated, four-jointed fingers to find purchase on the razor-sharp serrations of the city-sized leaves. 

They are the ones who create the **Weavings**. These are not homes, but stabilize-points. Because the Lacuna grows at a frantic, visible pace, a Tender cannot sleep in the same place twice. They spend their lives in a state of perpetual "Growth-Smithing." Armed with their petrified-gourd hammers, they strike the base of emerging shoots, rhythmic and heavy, guiding the direction of the expanding plant matter. They are the reason the titanic leaves overlap in a way that creates a walkable floor rather than a chaotic jumble of spikes. They are biological architects, herding the forest like a slow-motion riot.

### The Glider-Mites (The Amber Scavengers)
While the Tenders are the architects, the **Glider-Mites** are the waste-management system. These creatures are the size of hounds, but they possess no weight. Their bodies are composed of a rigid, amber-like resin, faceted like cut gemstones. They have no legs, only a series of gossamer-thin vanes that catch the great, slow-moving oxygen currents.

They are attracted to "The Leakage." When a redwood-sized vine snaps under the tension of the Lacuna’s growth, the resulting spray of pressurized, hyper-nutritious sap is a catastrophe of scale. The Mites descend in shimmering clouds to drink the airborne liquid before it can coat the environment in a sticky, suffocating film. They are the only things in the Lacuna that are silent. They drift like shards of broken stained glass, their internal geometries refracting the golden light into dancing rainbows that are the only "art" the Tenders ever experience.

### The Pulse-Lichen (The Memory)
Living in the deep crevices where the leaves meet the "wall" of the cyst is a colonial organism known as the **Pulse-Lichen**. It appears as a thick, throbbing carpet of violet moss. The Tenders treat these patches with a quiet, ritualistic reverence. 

The Lichen acts as a biological record. Because the Lacuna is in a state of constant, violent renewal, nothing "old" survives for long—except the Lichen. It anchors itself to the granite shell of the cyst, surviving even when the leaves above it snap and are replaced. The Tenders press their frond-crowned heads against the Lichen to receive "The Rote"—a sensory download of the Lacuna’s history, teaching them where the next great vine is likely to snap and where the water-globes will drift next.

### The Relationship with the Void
The inhabitants do not possess a concept of "down." To a Tender, the void is a predator. They live in constant fear of losing their grip and falling into the central abyss, where gravity becomes a confusing, swirling vortex. 

Their tools—the scrapers and seed-pod hammers—are always tethered to their waists by miles of hand-spun fiber. If a tool falls, it is gone forever, lost to the golden vacuum. This has created a culture of extreme precision and attachment. They do not speak, for the grinding of the plants would drown out any voice, but they communicate through a complex language of tactile percussion, drumming on the hollow vines to send signals that travel for miles through the rigid structure.

### The Parasitic Paradox
There is a sense that the inhabitants are not native, but rather a "mistake" that the Lacuna has learned to tolerate. They are parasites that provide a service. By hammering the growth and consuming the sap, they prevent the Lacuna from choking on its own vitality. They are the gardeners of a god that doesn't know they exist, living in the narrow, humid space between a world that is being born and a world that is growing too fast to survive.
