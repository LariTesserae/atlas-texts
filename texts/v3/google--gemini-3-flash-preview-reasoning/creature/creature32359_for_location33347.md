The inhabitants of the High Maw are not born of the sun; they are born of the pressure and the grind. They are creatures of transition, thriving in the margins between the planetary crust and the industrial void.

### The Vane-Wraiths (The Bone-White Kites)
High above the grinding maws, the Vane-wraiths own the updrafts. They are massive, translucent-skinned gliders with wingspans like the sails of forgotten ships. Their "feathers" are actually stiffened filaments of silica, clicking together in the wind with the sound of dry glass. 

They have evolved to be thermal parasites. They do not hunt in the traditional sense; they circle the exhaust vents of the cathedral-refineries, bathing in the plumes of superheated steam. Their skin is a pale, leathery vellum that absorbs the mineral-rich particulates from the steam, hardening into a natural armor. When a Vane-wraith dies, it does not rot; it simply becomes too heavy with accumulated stone to fly, plummeting into the mists like a falling statue to shatter on the basalt below. Their eyes are small, milky orbs that see only in the infrared, tracking the heat-signatures of the great machines as if they were moving gods.

### The Slurry-Ticks (Lithophagic Insects)
Where the machines’ grinding teeth meet the virgin rock, a grey, frothing paste is produced—a slurry of pulverized minerals and condensed mist. This is the lifeblood of the Slurry-ticks. These are heavy, trilobite-like organisms the size of dinner plates, armored in overlapping plates of dark, chitinous obsidian. 

They possess a horrifying, singular focus. Using hydraulic, suction-cupped undersides, they cling to the rock faces directly beneath the refineries’ maws. They are the "cleaners" of the Maw, their mouthparts designed to filter-feed on the mineral dust before it can be washed away by the weeping shale. They are so attuned to the vibrations of the machines that they can predict the path of a hydraulic limb minutes before it moves, scurrying with a rhythmic, metallic clatter to avoid being crushed. To touch one is to touch a stone that is uncomfortably warm and vibrating with a stolen heartbeat.

### The Grease-Striders
Inside the "cathedrals" themselves—in the dark, oily spaces between the massive gears and the hydraulic pistons—lives a hidden, symbiotic fauna. The Grease-striders are spindly, multi-legged scavengers that resemble a cross between a crab and a wire-frame sculpture. 

They are the uncommissioned maintenance crew. They feed on the thick, synthetic lubricants that leak from the machines’ joints and the grit that threatens to seize the gears. They are blind, navigating by the subsonic hum of the engine. In a strange biological trade-off, the machines provide a warm, sheltered womb, and the Striders keep the industrial joints polished and free of debris. They are the only things in the High Maw that know the interior of the titans, living their entire lives in a world of rhythmic thunder and black oil.

### The Piezo-Moss and the Marrow-Worms
In the "hanging bogs"—the black, silty mires on the high shelves—the life is slower, more elemental. 
*   **The Piezo-Moss:** This translucent, rubbery growth thrives in the seams where the mountain is still growing. It is pressure-sensitive; when the tectonic plates shift or a refinery passes overhead, the moss glows with a dim, flickering bioluminescence, a phantom blue light that bleeds through the mist. It tastes of salt and electricity.
*   **The Marrow-Worms:** Deep within the silt of the bogs live the Marrow-worms—long, segmented tubes of pale muscle that process the "crushed bone" of the planet. They are the ultimate recyclers, breaking down the minerals into a state that the moss can absorb. They move with an agonizing slowness, their bodies tensed against the crushing gravity of the High Maw.

### The Ghost-Signal
There are no humans here, but there is a "Ghost." The autonomous harvesters are governed by a decentralized intelligence—a mesh network of low-frequency radio waves that pulse between the machines. This signal is so pervasive that it has become a secondary environment. 

The fauna has adapted to it. The Vane-wraiths tilt their wings to catch the strongest signal-lines; the Slurry-ticks pulse their shells in time with the data-transfer. There is a sense that the biology of the High Maw is no longer separate from the machinery. The animals are not just living *alongside* the harvesters; they are becoming components of a single, sprawling, bio-industrial organism. 

In the High Maw, life does not struggle against the machine. It follows the machine into the dark, waiting for the rock to be crushed so it might feast on the dust of the world’s creation.
