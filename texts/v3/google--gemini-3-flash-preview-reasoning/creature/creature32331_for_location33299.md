In the Oul-Rha, life does not stand; it clings. It is a world of white-knuckled endurance where the concept of "rest" is merely a change in how one holds on. 

### The Hushed-Kin (The Inhabitants)
The people who inhabit the Thread-Nests—the **Sway-Born**—are a study in biological economy. They are long-limbed and deceptively thin, their muscles not bulky but wound tight like the lianas they navigate. Their skin has the texture of wet chamois, a specialized, high-friction dermis that allows them to grip the polished basalt even when it is slick with the Vomiting Orchid’s spray. 

They are a people of profound silence. Having evolved or adapted to the Roar, their external ears have receded into narrow, slit-like orifices protected by thick cartilaginous flaps. They do not hear sound as a melody; they feel it as a topographical map. They can sense a shift in the wind’s pressure through the soles of their feet—which are prehensile and wide—seconds before a "Hammer-Gust" arrives to snap a tether. 

Their eyes are their most striking feature: oversized and shielded by a double set of transparent eyelids. The inner lid filters the blinding, bioluminescent friction of the sound-ice, while the outer lid protects against the scouring mist. When two Sway-Born meet on a bridge of braided bone and fiber, they do not speak. They lean their foreheads together, allowing the vibrations of their skulls to sync, communicating through a language of sub-dermal hums and the rhythmic tapping of fingers against the other’s collarbone.

### The Weaver-Crabs (The Maintenance)
Living in the deep, fluted grooves of the pillars are the **Litho-Arachnids**. These are flat, dinner-plate-sized crustaceans with sixteen legs, each ending in a microscopic hook that can find purchase in the very atomic grain of the basalt. They are the true architects of the Oul-Rha. 

They secrete a biological resin that is liquid for only a second before the Roar flash-hardens it into a substance stronger than carbon fiber. The Sway-Born have a symbiotic relationship with these creatures, guiding their growth to reinforce the anchors of their Thread-Nests. The Weaver-Crabs eat the "Wind-Dross"—the pulverized organic dust of everything the Roar has destroyed elsewhere—which they filter through baleen-like fans on their underbellies.

### The Harmonic Predators
Not everything in the Oul-Rha is content to simply hold on. The **Vane-Stalker** is a nightmare of aerodynamics. It is a creature of hollow bone and translucent membranes, resembling a skeletal umbrella. It spends its life in the "Dead-Zones"—the tiny pockets of low pressure directly behind the pillars. 

The Vane-Stalker waits for a Sky-Ray or a Sway-Born to lose a tether. When a creature is caught in the Roar and begins to tumble, the Stalker collapses its ribs, diving into the 200-mph stream like a needle. It doesn't use teeth to kill; it uses the wind. It latches onto its prey and extends its broad, stiff wings at an angle that creates a localized, violent vortex, literally vibrating the prey’s internal organs to a pulp before the body even hits the "nothing" below.

### The Glint-Moths (The Sound-Ice Feeders)
When the sound-ice forms—those gravity-defying, glowing geometric crystals—the air between the pillars becomes a minefield of light. This triggers the emergence of the **Glint-Moths**. These are not insects in any traditional sense, but clusters of colonial organisms that form a shimmering, kite-like shape.

They feed on the bioluminescent friction of the ice. They dance in the most turbulent currents, their bodies vibrating at the exact frequency of the Roar to remain stationary. To a Sway-Born observer, a swarm of Glint-Moths looks like a stationary constellation of neon blue, a fixed point of light in a world where everything else is a blur of motion. They are the only things in the Oul-Rha that seem to find the Roar peaceful.

### The Soul of the Pillar: The Marrow-Singers
Deep within the fluted vents of the pillars themselves, there are rumors of the **Marrow-Singers**. These are believed to be the original architects or perhaps a final evolution of the Sway-Born. They live inside the stone, in the natural organ-pipes carved by a billion years of wind. 

They are never seen, only felt. They are the source of the "subterranean thrum." It is whispered that they have learned to manipulate the Roar by opening and closing sphincters of living tissue within the basalt flutes. They play the Oul-Rha like a colossal woodwind instrument. The Sway-Born believe that if the Marrow-Singers ever stopped their "song," the harmonic balance that keeps the pillars from shattering under the wind’s pressure would fail, and the last bones of the world would finally crumble into the abyss.

### The Cycle of the Fallen
In the Oul-Rha, there is no burial. When a Sway-Born grows old and their grip weakens, or when a Sky-Ray’s wing-membrane finally tatters, they undergo the **Release**. They unclip their tethers and step into the Roar. 

They are not seen to fall. The wind is so thick, so violent, that it carries the body upward or sideways, scouring the flesh from the bone in minutes until only a fine, organic mist remains. This mist is what feeds the velvet-moss and the Vomiting Orchids. In the Oul-Rha, you do not die; you simply become the humidity that allows those who remain to keep their grip.
