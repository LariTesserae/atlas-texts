Inside the **Lace-Towers**, the inhabitants are no longer flesh, but a memory of architecture. These are the **Vibrants**, though the name implies a movement they have long since traded for resonance. They are thin, ribbon-like organisms, translucent and stiff as dried parchment, clinging to the interior honeycombs of the towers. They do not eat; they are "fed" by the specific frequency of the wind as it whistles through the bone-colored apertures. They are the ghost-minstrels of the plateau, their entire existence dedicated to trembling in sympathy with the earth’s low thrum. When the Slow-Movers pulse, the Vibrants shiver against the resin walls, creating a harmonic friction that keeps them warm. They are the only things here that remember the sky, catching the high-altitude light in their bodies and refracting it into the dark hollows of the towers like dim, amber lanterns.

Living within the velvet folds of the **Slow-Movers** themselves are the **Dew-Gilders**. These are tiny, gastropod-like creatures with shells made of the same dark-ochre stone as the domes. They are the custodians of the suede. They move in the wake of the midday condensation, trailing paths of iridescent slime that protect the Slow-Movers’ skin from the mercilessly clear sun. The relationship is ancient and absolute: the Gilder drinks the salt from the Slow-Mover’s hide, and in exchange, its trail acts as a cooling lens, preventing the haystack-sized giants from cracking under the heat. To look closely at a Slow-Mover is to see a map of silver veins, a delicate calligraphy of survival written by a thousand tiny, patient scribes.

Deep within the "weeping cracks" of the stone domes, where the water is tea-colored and still, live the **Glass-Larvae**. They are the youth of a species that may never reach maturity. In this world of finished work, the Larvae remain in a state of perpetual, liquid potential. They are perfectly transparent, visible only when they disturb the white-filament grass. They act as the plateau’s nervous system, sensing the microscopic shifts in the weight of the Slow-Movers. When a Slow-Mover dies—which happens perhaps once a century—the Glass-Larvae congregate in the pool beneath it. Over decades, they calcify the fallen giant, turning the soft suede into the very ochre stone that forms the domes. They are the masons of the vertebrae; they ensure the sleeping god never loses its shape.

There is a final inhabitant, solitary and rarely seen: the **Hush-Owl**. It is not a bird, but a heavy, four-winged drifter with feathers that look like frayed grey silk. It does not hunt in the traditional sense, for there is no scurrying prey to catch. Instead, it feeds on the *stasis*. It lands on the brow of a Slow-Mover and stays there for a year, its beak pressed against the lidless eye of its host. It drinks the dreams of the slow, the long-form images of shifting stars and eroding mountains. The Hush-Owl is the keeper of the plateau’s history, a living vessel for a million heartbeats it will never return. When it finally takes flight, it does so without a sound, a shadow detaching itself from a shadow, drifting over the mammillary hills like a scrap of a cloud that forgot to stay beneath the ridge.

These inhabitants do not compete. There is no hunger here that cannot be sated by a drop of dew or a gust of warm wind. They are the components of a clock that has slowed almost to a stop, each one a gear polished by eons of coexistence. To live on the Plateau of the Still-Housed is to be a part of the stone, a part of the pulse, and a part of the long, golden wait for a darkness that may never come.
