The inhabitants of this parched metropolis are masterpieces of friction and static. In a world where the concept of a "drop" does not exist, life has abandoned the internal sea of the cell for the structural integrity of the crystal and the dry vibration of the membrane.

### The Litho-Architects
At the base of all society are the **Silicate Weavers**, the microscopic polyps that act as the city's subconscious mind. They do not possess brains so much as a collective, chemical drive to polarize mineral dust. They live in the pores of the great spires, pulling basalt-ash and oxides from the air and fusing them into glass-hard lattices through a process of bio-calcification. They are the reason the city grows upward; they are forever chasing the "high air," where the dust-storms are thickest and the mineral yield is purest.

### The Velvet-Striders
The most visible citizens are the **Velvet-Striders**, the soft-bellied hexapods that navigate the resin-bridges. They are creatures of exquisite tactility. Their bodies are covered in a fine, short-pile fur that looks like crushed velvet, designed to minimize friction while maximizing surface area for gathering static electricity—their primary source of metabolic energy. 

They have no mouths in the traditional sense. Instead, their chest cavities house a series of vibrating plates that "inhale" fine mineral powders, which are then processed by internal grinding organs. They do not speak; they *thrum*. By pressing their padded palms against the resonant resin of the bridges, they send complex ripples of information across the city. A Strider can "hear" a collapse ten miles away or sense the approach of a dust-sea wave by the subtle shift in the vibration of the spire beneath its feet. They are a communal species, moving in slow, rhythmic processions that look like a silent, urban ballet.

### The Sieve-Moths (The Atmospheric Filterers)
Above the spires, drifting through the canyons of terracotta fans, are the **Gossamer-Whales**. These are the house-sized moths mentioned in the legends of the dust. They have no wings for flight; instead, their bodies are incredibly light, filled with pockets of warm, buoyant gas harvested from tectonic vents. They use their massive, feathery antennae to steer, rowing through the dense, warm air. 

Their life is a continuous, slow-motion feast. Their proboscises are not tubes, but fractal webs that they cast into the wind to catch specific oxides. They are the "plankton eaters" of the sky. Because they are so large and move so slowly, smaller species often build secondary nests on their backs, turning the moths into drifting, living neighborhoods that travel between the spires.

### The Grit-Menders
Because there is no rot, the city faces a unique problem: the accumulation of dead structural matter. This niche is filled by the **Grit-Menders**, a class of beetle-like scavengers with mandibles like industrial rock-crushers. They follow the Striders, seeking out the hardened resin that has cracked or the moss-fans that have reached the end of their cycle. 

The Menders do not eat for nutrition; they eat to pulverize. They grind the dead biological structures back into the ultra-fine silt that feeds the winds. They are the janitors of the dry world, ensuring that the "urban" density never becomes a tomb. To hear a Mender at work is to hear a constant, low rasping, like sandpaper on stone, the only aggressive sound in a world of silence.

### The Static-Angels
In the highest reaches, where the spires thin into needles, live the **Static-Angels**. These are spindly, translucent organisms that are almost entirely composed of gossamer-thin membranes. They act as the city’s lightning rods, though there is no lightning—only massive buildups of static electricity from the dust storms. The Angels tether themselves to the spire-tips and unfurl their wings to catch the charge. They glow with a soft, flickering amber light, acting as beacons for the Sieve-Moths. They are the only creatures that "eat" pure energy, and in return, they provide the only illumination the city knows during the shifts in air pressure when the dust obscures the sun.

### The Soul of the Inhabitants
There is no "predation" here in the way we understand it. Without the need for high-speed chases or the consumption of wet tissue, the hierarchy is based on **surface area**. The most successful life forms are those that can touch the most air, catch the most dust, and feel the most vibrations. 

The temperament of this world is one of **frantic patience**. Every creature is constantly moving, constantly processing, yet there is no hurry. They live in a state of absolute occupation, a crowded, rustling peace where every being is a brick, a cable, or a breath in a single, continental organism. They are the children of the friction, finding life not in the flow of water, but in the eternal, warm rubbing of stone against stone.
