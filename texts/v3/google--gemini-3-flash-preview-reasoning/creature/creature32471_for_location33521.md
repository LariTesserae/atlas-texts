The inhabitants of the Lattice are not merely residents; they are the terminal buds of the world’s own nervous system. 

They are the **Knotted**, a post-biological lineage that has become so entwined with the Grafting that the distinction between "dweller" and "structure" has begun to dissolve. 

### The Knotted
Physically, the Knotted are elongated, spindly silhouettes that mirror the banyan trunks they call home. They are tall—nearly three meters—but their weight is negligible, a concession to the Lattice’s light gravity. Their limbs are unnaturally long, possessing double-jointed elbows and knees that allow them to fold themselves into the narrowest crevices of the bark. Their skin is not flesh, but a supple, leather-like cork, varying in color from a pale lichen-grey to a deep, tannin-stained mahogany.

They do not have feet in the terrestrial sense. Instead, they possess four identical, six-fingered hands, each tipped with a hard, keratinous hook. They move with a fluid, terrifying grace, never "walking" but rather flowing across the vertical planes of the Lattice. They move in silence, their grip so precise that they do not disturb the moss.

Their faces are flat, dominated by large, amber eyes that lack pupils, designed to drink in the bruised twilight of the interior. They have no mouths. Nutrition is a slow, ritualistic process: they possess fine, root-like filaments beneath their fingernails. To eat, they press their hands into the weeping resins of the primary trunks or the nutrient-rich peat of the knots, drawing sugars and minerals directly into their circulatory systems—a system that pumps not blood, but a thin, chlorophyll-rich ichor.

### The Liturgy of Maintenance
The Knotted are a civilization of repairmen. Their entire culture is centered around the **Tending**. Because the Lattice is a dying machine of wood, they spend their lives in a state of perpetual horticulture. They carry tools made of obsidian and bone, passed down through thousands of generations: tiny saws for pruning parasitic growths, stone weights for training new saplings to fill the gaps left by petrifying boughs, and clay jars of "Quick-Sap," a bio-catalyst that forces vines to knit together over a wound.

They communicate not through sound—which the moss would only swallow—but through the **Tap**. By striking the resonant heartwood of the primary trunks with rhythmic precision, they send vibrations that travel for kilometers through the Lattice. A Knotted individual can "hear" a message through the soles of their hands, feeling the distress of a distant knot or the arrival of a vertical deluge long before it hits.

### The Echo-Walkers and the Soft Reality
In the upper elevations, where reality begins to soften, a sub-caste of the Knotted exists: the **Dream-Weavers**. These individuals do not tend to the physical wood, but to the *intent* of the Lattice. Because the vegetation there reacts to thought, the Dream-Weavers must maintain a state of rigid, meditative discipline. 

If a Dream-Weaver feels fear, the vines around them grow thorns the size of swords. If they feel grief, the leaves turn black and fall in a poisonous soot. They act as anchors, using their disciplined minds to hold the geometry of the world together, "thinking" the paths into existence so that travelers do not become lost in a labyrinth of their own subconscious projections. 

Sometimes, a Knotted person will simply... stop. They find a junction they love, press their limbs deep into a mossy bed, and cease moving. Over decades, the Lattice accepts them. The bark grows over their limbs; their nervous system merges with the cellulose. These are the **Ancestors**, the living memories of the world. The Knotted visit these sites to press their foreheads against the bark, hoping to catch a flicker of a thought from a mind that has become a continent.

### The Fauna: The Drift-Hulks and the Resonant Moths
The non-sentient life is equally strange. The **Drift-Hulks**—the house-sized gastropods—are the heavy laborers of the ecology. They are slow, mindless, and indestructible. The Knotted use them as slow-moving cargo ships, grafting storage-gourds directly onto the snails’ massive, resinous shells. A Knotted family might live their entire lives on the back of a single Hulk as it makes a century-long trek from the roots to the canopy.

In the open air of the hollows, the **Resonant Moths** drift. These are creatures of paper-thin membrane with wingspans of thirty meters. They don't flap; they catch the thermal drafts created by the heat of decomposing compost miles below. Their wings are bioluminescent, shifting through colors that the Knotted use as a primitive weather vane. When the Moths turn a sharp, electric blue, it signifies a "Pulse-Storm"—a surge of sap so violent it might burst the smaller vessels of the Lattice.

### The Glass-Watchers
As the star dies and the world petrifies, a new kind of life has emerged in the brittle, chime-like regions of the outer sphere. These are the **Glass-Watchers**, small, skittering creatures that look like a cross between a spider and a shard of emerald. They eat the petrified wood, grinding the crystal-timber into a fine dust. They are the heralds of the end. The Knotted fear them, for where the Watchers appear, the Lattice is no longer living; it is becoming a tomb of glass, beautiful and cold, singing a high, thin note in the solar wind that no gardener can silence.
