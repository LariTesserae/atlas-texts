The inhabitants of the Sharp Peaks do not simply live *in* the city; they are the slow, deliberate pulse within its mineral veins. To understand who resides here, one must first discard the notion of a "life span" as a sequence of breaths or heartbeats, and instead view it as a long, unwavering gaze.

### The Custodians of the Lattice
The **Glass-Striders** are the primary residents, yet they are less like citizens and more like the meditative thoughts of the mountain itself. Standing on six or eight spindly, multi-jointed limbs that terminate in soft, suction-dry pads, they cling to the vertical faces of the litho-coral with an effortless, static grace. Their bodies are masterpieces of transparency—vessels of toughened silica and vitreous humors that allow the harsh sunlight to pass through them rather than burn them.

Internally, they lack the wet complexity of mammals. There is no blood, only a slow-circulating, pressurized gas that expands and contracts with the temperature of the obsidian. They do not eat in a way that involves tearing or chewing; instead, they possess "Solar Fritters"—fine, fan-like appendages tucked beneath their carapaces that they unfurl during the peak of the day. These fans capture the high-energy photons and the fine, mineral-rich volcanic ash, converting them directly into a slow-burning chemical energy.

### The Chromatic Weave
The Striders are a silent people, but their inner lives are a riot of color. Because their exoskeletons are translucent, their internal organs—which shift in hue based on mood, thought, or metabolic state—are visible to all. A Strider in deep contemplation may be a pool of deep, stagnant violet; one engaged in the "work" of tending the coral might ripple with rhythmic waves of amber and gold. 

A conversation between two Striders can last for a week. They stand perfectly still, facing one another, their internal colors swirling in a slow-motion dance. To a human, they would look like statues; to each other, they are reciting epic poems of light, debating the structural integrity of a new spire, or sharing the "memory" of a tectonic shift that happened three generations ago.

### The Lithe-Gardener Caste
Among the Striders are those specialized for the growth of the city. These individuals possess elongated, needle-like forelimbs used to "tune" the litho-coral. They do not build with mortar; they stimulate growth by vibrating the mineral membranes at specific frequencies. They are the conductors of a geological orchestra, coaxing the recursive geometries of the towers to branch left or right, ensuring the sun-shadows fall perfectly to heat the lower galleries. They treat the city as a bonsai tree of ivory stone, pruning away calcified excesses over decades to maintain the balance of the caldera.

### The Ghost-Moths (The Air-Sifters)
Higher up, near the tips of the thinnest spires, live the **Ghost-Moths**. These are not insects, but paper-thin, kite-like organisms with wingspans the size of a man’s reach. They are almost entirely weightless, consisting of little more than a framework of hollow bone and a sail of iridescent, mica-like skin. They do not fly so much as they anchor themselves to the peaks and flap with agonizing slowness, acting as biological bellows. Their movement helps circulate the warm volcanic gases through the hollow vines of the city, ensuring that the stagnant heat never becomes toxic. 

### The Crystalline Symbiotes
Deep within the fluted tubes of the city dwell the **Marrow-Gleams**. These are tiny, colonial organisms that look like clusters of glowing sand. They are the janitors of the Litho-Coral, breaking down the rare organic waste—shed exoskeletons or spent crystalline leaves—into the base minerals the city needs to continue its upward growth. They provide the only light in the city’s interior depths, a soft, bioluminescent hum that guides the Striders through the labyrinthine bridge-roots during the rare eclipses or the long, cold nights of the high peaks.

### The Philosophy of the Still
There is no concept of "hurry" in this place. The inhabitants view speed as a symptom of fragility. To move quickly is to risk shattering; to speak loudly is to disturb the precise vibrations of the living stone. 

When a resident reaches the end of its long, slow cycle, it does not decay. It climbs to the highest, sharpest point of the obsidian rim and simply stops moving. The sun and the dry air slowly leach the last of the gases from its frame until it becomes indistinguishable from the glass beneath its feet. The Sharp Peaks are not just a home for the living; they are a monument to every resident who has ever stood there, their bodies eventually becoming the very foundations upon which the next generation of litho-coral will be coaxed to grow. 

They are a people of patience, survivors of a vertical desert who have realized that in a world made of glass and sun, the most radical act is to remain perfectly, beautifully still.
