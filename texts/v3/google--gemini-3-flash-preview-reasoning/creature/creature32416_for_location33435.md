In this basin of condensation and iron, life does not struggle against the environment; it is an extension of the plumbing. The inhabitants are the living organs of a machine that forgot its purpose but kept its rhythm.

### The Languid Lungs (The Bell-Drifters)
Those pale, bell-shaped drifters are the **Vapor-Medusae**. They are nearly weightless, their bodies composed of a high-density mucous that is only marginally more solid than the air they inhabit. They do not hunt; they aspirate. As they drift, their translucent bells pulse with a slow, hypnotic dilation, pulling the mineral-rich broth of the air through fine, silken filaments that hang like gossamer threads beneath them. 

To look closely at a Medusa is to see a map of the room itself: their translucent guts are flecked with the same indigo bioluminescence as the siphons, and their vein-networks are stained the burnt-orange of the rust-polyps. They are the room’s atmosphere given a temporary, floating shape. When they die, they do not fall; they simply lose their internal tension and dissolve into a heavy mist, raining back down into the pools to be sucked up once more by the siphons.

### The Lubricant-Eaters (The Brass Scuttlers)
Near the floor, the **Clutch-Crabs** move with a terrifying, silent fluidity. Their shells are not true brass, but a biomineralized plating grown from the copper-wire verdigris and the iron-oxide dust they consume. They are the custodians of the leaks. 

They possess long, needle-like proboscises designed to slide into the microscopic fissures of the ancient pistons. They don't just drink the pressurized lubricants; they *sense* the friction. A Clutch-Crab will sit for days atop a seizing joint, its many legs vibrating in sympathy with the machine’s groan, slowly secreting a biological grease that eases the metal’s torment so it can drink the resulting overflow. They are the reason the gears still turn; they are the unintentional mechanics of the abyss, driven by a hunger for the taste of hot, pressurized oil.

### The Tenders of the Pulse
Deep within the forest of siphons, there move things that are larger, slower, and infinitely more patient. These are the **Silt-Walkers**, the apex inhabitants of the basin. Standing twice the height of a man, they are spindly, tripod-like organisms with limbs that resemble the very copper cables they walk among. Their skin is the texture of wet, oxidized velvet—a deep, charcoal grey that swallows the indigo light.

The Silt-Walkers have no eyes, for there is nothing to see that cannot be felt. Instead, their "heads" are wide, flat discs covered in sensory pits that detect the sub-audible shudders of the floor. They move with a deliberate, agonizing slowness, placing their pointed feet precisely into the shallow pools to avoid breaking the high-tension surface. 

They are the gardeners of the rust-polyps. They use long, multi-jointed fingers to prune the calcified growths, ensuring the siphons have room to pulse. They have a symbiotic relationship with the "built" world; a Silt-Walker will often wrap its long, cable-like arms around a cooling piston, absorbing the escaping heat through its chest. In these moments, the machine and the creature become a single silhouette, breathing together in the charcoal haze.

### The Ghost-Currents
Finally, there is the **Microbial Weave**. The water in the pools is not just H2O; it is a conductive soup of dissolved information. If you were to dip your hand into one of those bulging, lens-like puddles, you would feel a faint, electric tingle. This is the "broth" communicating. The siphons don't just move water; they move data—chemical signals from a leaking pipe at one end of the hall to a thirsty rust-polyp at the other. 

Nothing here possesses a central brain. The intelligence is distributed across the entire hall. The machine provides the heat and the minerals; the biology provides the maintenance and the circulation. It is a closed-loop consciousness, a dream being dreamt by a damp, rusting engine. The inhabitants are merely the cells of that dream, each performing a task they never learned, in a place they will never leave, under a charcoal sky that never breaks.
