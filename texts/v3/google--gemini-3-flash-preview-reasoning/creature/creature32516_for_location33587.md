In the High Weave, life does not simply inhabit the structure; it is the structure’s metabolic extension. Every creature is a specialized cell in a continental organism, existing in a state of perpetual, humid digestion.

### The Lithic Swarm (The Architects)
At the base of the food web are the **Calcifiers**. They are not individuals so much as a white, viscous tide of microscopic polyps that flow over the "established" bone-spans. They are the source of the ivory crust. They move with the agonizing patience of a glacier, consuming the detritus of the upper layers—fallen leaves, dead chitin, and the mineral-rich "Cold Drip"—and secreting it back out as hardened, porous calcium. They are the reason the ground is never still; if you stand in one place for a month, your boots will be fused to the floor by a fine, pearlescent webbing of new bone.

### The Strident Chitin (The Sound-Makers)
The "Fauna: 2.9" wall of noise is generated largely by the **Vibro-Moths**. These are not the delicate insects of a sunlit world. They are heavy, armored things the size of dinner plates, with wings of stiffened, translucent silk. They do not fly so much as vibrate through the heavy air, their wings blurring at frequencies that generate heat, helping to maintain the "stagnant soup" of the atmosphere. They cling to the undersides of the great ivory vaults in clusters of thousands, their collective humming creating a physical pressure that can rupture a human eardrum. They feed on the waxy black nectar of the succulent vines, their mouthparts elongated into needle-thin bone-straws.

### The Drip-Catchers
Where the "Cold Drip" falls, life congregates in wet, shivering heaps. The most prominent are the **Siphon-Slugs**. These are soft-bodied behemoths, translucent and pulsing with a faint, internal violet light. They wedge their massive, bloated forms into the cupped leaves of the giant bromeliads. They act as living filters; their skin is incredibly porous, absorbing moisture and toxins alike, purifying the water before it trickles down to the next level. They are the kidneys of the Weave. When they bellow—a sound like a foghorn heard through deep water—it is to signal that a reservoir is full, calling the smaller scavengers to drink from their overflowing mantles.

### The Velvet-Walkers
To move through the three-foot-deep moss is to invite the attention of the **Muffle-Cats**. These are not felines, but hexapedal predators with skin like bruised plum-colored suede. They have no eyes; the darkness of the Weave is absolute, rendering sight a wasted caloric expense. Instead, their entire bodies are covered in fine, sensory hairs that map the vibrations of the air. They move with a horrifying, liquid grace, their padded feet making absolutely no sound as they sink into the moss. They hunt the Vibro-Moths, plucking them from the vaulted ceilings with long, sticky tongues that lash out like whip-cracks.

### The Weaver-Worms
The gargantuan vines that coil around the pillars are tended by **Weaver-Worms**. These are segmented, pale annelids as thick as a man's thigh and dozens of feet long. They lack teeth, possessing instead "spinneret-maws" that secrete a fast-hardening biological glue. They are the repair crews. When a storm in the upper atmosphere causes a spire to crack or a walkway to erode, the Weaver-Worms migrate to the wound in a slow, pulsing caravan. They stitch the world back together with cords of hardened silk, reinforcing the ivory struts with new, flexible biological cables.

### The Apex: The Lung-Bellowers
Deep in the lightless valleys, where the ivory spans are thickest and the darkness is most profound, live the **Great Lungs**. These are the "soft-bodied behemoths" whose territorial bellows anchor the soundscape. They are sessile, mountain-sized mounds of heaving, purple-black flesh rooted into the very foundations of the Weave. They do not hunt; they breathe. They draw in the heavy, stagnant air, filter out the trillions of microscopic spores and airborne nutrients, and exhale the warmth that keeps the High Weave from freezing. They are living furnaces. To be near one is to feel a rhythmic heat that syncs with your own heartbeat, a reminder that the world is not a place, but a breathing, hungry entity.

In the High Weave, there is no death that is not immediately recycled. A creature that falls is covered by the Calcifiers before its body is cold. A leaf that withers is absorbed by the Siphon-Slugs. You are either a builder, a filter, or a pulse in the throat of a world that has finally, perfectly, closed in on itself.
