To live in the Weave is to exist in a state of permanent, high-velocity tension. There is no concept of "softness" or "cushioning" here; life has evolved as a series of gaskets, hooks, and heat-resistant membranes. The inhabitants do not inhabit the world so much as they are integrated into its grinding, industrial rhythm.

### The Anchor-Walkers (The Scour-Claw Swarm)
The primary inhabitants are the ones glimpsed in the nodes: the **Scour-Claw Swarms**. These are the beetle-glider hybrids, but close up, their biological nature is almost entirely obscured by evolutionary "industrialization." Their carapaces are not made of simple chitin, but a mineral-rich bio-ceramic, polished to a mirror sheen by the abrasive wind. This reflective surface protects them from the 2.6-scale brilliance of the sky, turning them into darting sparks of silver as they move.

They have no feet. Instead, their limbs terminate in pneumatically-powered, carbon-fiber talons that function like industrial clamps. To move, they don't walk; they "ratchet." You can hear them over the roar—a frantic *clack-clack-clack* as they move along the titan-vines, their hooks piercing the bone-white bark to keep them from being atomized by the Great Gale. When they need to cross the void, they don't fly so much as they *deploy*. They wait for a specific harmonic surge in the vine's vibration, unhook, and are slingshot into the emptiness, trailing thin, high-tensile silken guide-wires that allow them to reel themselves into the next beam fifty miles away.

### The Chute-Worms (The Internal Maintenance)
Inside the titan-vines, within the clattering darkness of the gravity-fed resin chutes, lives a different kind of inhabitant: the **Litho-Augers**. These are segmented, cylindrical organisms, thirty feet long and as hard as ironwood. They lack eyes, ears, and limbs. They are essentially living drill-bits. 

The Litho-Augers live their entire lives inside the "plumbing" of the world. They feed on the clattering resin pellets, grinding them down further into a fine, pressurized dust that the titan-vines use for growth. They are the reason the "factory" doesn't clog. They move by counter-rotating their segmented body rings against the interior walls of the trunks, creating a high-pitched, metallic shriek that adds a piercing soprano to the world's 2.8-scale bass roar.

### The Piston-Kites (The Apex Predators)
High above the primary lattice, where the wind reaches its most violent velocities, dwell the **Piston-Kites**. These are the world’s apex predators, though they look more like runaway pieces of machinery than birds. They are composed of vast, translucent membranes stretched over a frame of hollow, pressurized bone.

They do not flap. They are "tether-hunters." A Piston-Kite will anchor itself to a high-altitude junction using a mile-long umbilical cord of braided tendon. It then casts itself into the gale, using the wind to stay aloft like a stationary kite. When it spots a Scour-Claw crossing a gap, it releases a burst of pressurized gas from its internal chambers, diving at terminal velocity. The "kill" is not a bite, but a collision; they strike with a heavy, blunt prow of solidified resin, shattering the prey’s ceramic shell instantly. The sound of a Piston-Kite strike is like a gunshot echoing through a cathedral.

### The Harmonicists (The Sentient Nodes)
The most mysterious inhabitants are found at the glowing, friction-heated nodes. These are the **Harmonicists**—sessile, colonial organisms that resemble clusters of giant, upright organ pipes made of translucent amber. They are the only creatures that seem to possess a form of culture, though it is entirely based on the physics of the Weave.

The Harmonicists do not speak; they *resonate*. By opening and closing various valves in their pipe-like bodies, they catch the Great Gale and modulate the deafening roar of the world into specific frequencies. They are the conductors of the lattice. They "sing" to the titan-vines, and some believe their vibrations actually steer the growth of the megastructures, ensuring the lattice remains balanced against the terrifying leverage of the wind. 

At night—if the term can be used for a world of constant light—the nodes where the Harmonicists dwell glow with a deep, frictional infrared. Around them, the smaller creatures gather not for warmth, but for the "quiet." The Harmonicists can create pockets of destructive interference, canceling out the deafening roar of the wooden gears and providing the only places in the sky-sea where a creature might experience the terrifying novelty of silence.

### The Static-Motes
Finally, there is the micro-fauna: the **Static-Motes**. These are tiny, crystalline insects that don't eat. They are powered by the triboelectric effect—the static electricity generated by the miles of titan-vines rubbing against one another. They appear as clouds of blue sparks drifting near the junctions. They are the "lubricant" of the ecology; as they die, their bodies break down into a fine, graphite-like powder that coats the wooden gears of the leaves, preventing the world-machine from grinding itself into white ash.

In this world, life is not a soft bloom; it is a hard-won mechanical advantage. To live here is to be a part, a gear, or a counterweight in a factory that produces nothing but its own continued existence.
