To inhabit the Hollow Ribs is to exist as a creature of verticality and viscosity. In this lightless, humid cathedral, biology has traded speed for endurance and sight for a profound, tactile sensitivity to vibration.

### The Marrow-Kin
The beings who leave the obsidian scrapers in the alcoves are not "people" in any sense that a surface-dweller would recognize. They are the **Marrow-Kin**, a lineage of hominids who have been winnowed by the crushing proximity of the basalt until they have become as thin and supple as the ghost-grass.

Their bodies are elongated, their joints possessing a double-hinged fluidity that allows them to press their torsos entirely flat against the stone. Their skin is the translucent grey of a fish’s belly, slick with a natural, hydrophobic oil that protects them from the constant "cloud-drip." They lack hair, which would only heavy them with moisture; instead, their limbs are tipped with long, spatulate fingers ending in hooked, coal-black nails—biological pitons designed to find purchase in the microscopic pores of the basalt.

They do not speak. In a world defined by the deafening, rhythmic thrum of millions of moths, vocalizations are useless. Instead, they communicate through "Skin-Knocking"—sharp, rhythmic taps against the stone that travel through the mountain’s bones. They are masters of the silence that exists *between* the vibrations. 

For twenty-three hours a day, they are shadow-treaders. They move with an agonizing, fluid slowness, harvesting the Wax-Drift with their scrapers, peeling back the nutrient-rich secretions of the moths like skin from fruit. They eat the wax raw; it is a dense, sulfurous paste that fuels their high-metabolic heat, allowing them to remain active in the thin, frayed oxygen of the peak.

### The Solar Paralysis
The most sacred and terrifying moment of their existence is the **Eleven Minutes of Gold**. When the sun aligns with the vertical cleft, the Marrow-Kin do not celebrate. They freeze. The sudden influx of ultraviolet light and heat is a sensory overload that triggers a catatonic state. They press themselves into the deepest cracks, their pupils—which usually encompass the entirety of their eyes—contracting into needle-points. They become indistinguishable from the stone, enduring the light as if it were a physical weight, waiting for the bruised twilight to return so they may breathe again.

### The Needle-Skulled Vultures
The "large, extinct birds" whose bones form the handles of the Kin's tools were once the **Suture-Birds**. A few still remain in the highest, most precarious reaches of the Ribs, though they are no longer capable of true flight in the traditional sense. Their wings have evolved into leathery, clawed sails used for controlled gliding down the two-kilometer drop. 

They are scavengers of the vertical waste. When a moth dies or a Marrow-Kin loses their grip, the Suture-Birds drop from the heights like falling knives. They have no beaks; instead, they possess a serrated, needle-like proboscis designed to pierce the calcified wing-cases of the Chitin-Moths and suck the liquified minerals from within. They are the only things the Marrow-Kin fear—not because they are hunters of men, but because they are opportunistic, and in the Ribs, a single shove is a death sentence.

### The Great Pulse
Beneath the carpet of moths, in the deepest, hottest marrow of the fissure where the sulfur vents are most violent, lives the **Matriarchal Substrate**. It is not a single creature, but a fungal-insectoid colony that acts as the brain of the Ribs. 

This substrate produces the indigo bioluminescence that lights the fissure. The Marrow-Kin treat these glowing patches with a reverence bordering on the parasitic; they sleep pressed against the glowing fungal mats, absorbing the warmth and the low-frequency vibrations. It is said—though there are no voices to say it—that the Kin do not die; they simply stop scraping. When a Kin grows old, they descend to the very bottom, lie down upon the Matriarchal Substrate, and allow the Chitin-Moths to settle upon them. 

Within a week, the moth-wax begins to encase them. Within a year, they have become a bulbous, organic formation on the wall—a new rib for the mountain, contributing their calcium back to the vertical world that sustained them. 

In the Hollow Ribs, there is no waste. There is only the slow, humid recycling of life into stone, and stone into life, played out to the heartbeat of a million vibrating wings.
