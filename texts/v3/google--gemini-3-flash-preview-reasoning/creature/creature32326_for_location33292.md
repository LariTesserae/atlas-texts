The masters of this vertical empire are the **Ciliata**, a race of hexapedal architects whose bodies are masterpieces of specialized fragility. They are no longer than a grain of rice, yet they possess a terrifying, lithe grace. Their carapaces are not hard like a beetle’s, but possess the milky, semi-translucent flexibility of a deep-sea shrimp. Through their bellies, one can see the rhythmic thrum of blue-tinted ichor being pumped by a tubular heart, a visual ticking clock of their high-velocity lives.

They are entirely blind. Where eyes might have been, there are instead twin arrays of feathery, multi-branched antennae that never stop moving. These antennae "read" the air, tasting the chemical signature of a neighbor’s fear, the approaching weight of a water droplet, or the distant, oily scent of a predator. To a Ciliata, the city is not a visual space but a symphony of vibrations and scents; they navigate the silk-walks by feeling the specific "note" each tension-line hums in the updraft.

### The Social Stratigraphy
The Ciliata are divided not by rank, but by their "Molts." 

The **First-Molts** are the laborers, their chitin still soft and tacky. They are the living glue of the city. They spend their days secreting the resin that binds the leaf-ribs together, pressing their own bodies into the joints of the architecture until the resin hardens. 

The **Elders (the Fifth-Molts)** are heavy and calcified, their limbs ending in serrated hooks. They are the "Tension-Keepers." They sit motionless at the intersections of the great silk-cables, their many legs spread wide to monitor the structural integrity of the entire district. When a tectonic shift in the granite occurs—a micro-tremor that would be unfelt by a human—these Elders pluck the cables, sending coded vibrations through the city to signal a mass evacuation or a structural reinforcement.

### The Beasts of the Fissure
Living alongside the Ciliata are the **Great Mites**, the low-slung behemoths of the Low-Forest. To the Ciliata, these are oxen. Covered in a shaggy, velvet-like fur that traps air bubbles, the mites graze on the silver-grey lichen, keeping the urban sprawl from being swallowed by the vegetation. They are stubborn, slow, and possess a singular, crushing strength. The Ciliata ride them, steering the beasts by tugging on the sensitive sensory hairs behind their mouthparts, using them to haul the massive spore-pods into place.

In the dark voids between the suspended plazas, the **Silver-Dragons** (the eyeless silverfish) hunt. They are the scavengers and the terrors of the moss-canopy. They do not walk; they swim through the saturated air, their scales creating a localized slipstream. They are beautiful and mindless, driven by an insatiable hunger for the translucent larvae that pulse on the ceiling. The Ciliata treat them as a weather pattern—unpredictable and dangerous—building "break-waters" of jagged pine-needles to divert the silverfish away from the nurseries.

### The Shadow-Gods
Then there are the **Stilt-Walkers**, the Opiliones. They are the nightmare of the fissure. With legs that span the entire two-inch width of the canyon, they do not belong to the city. They are the ghosts of the periphery. Their bodies are tiny, dark beads suspended on impossibly thin, multi-jointed stilts. They move with a horrifying, liquid silence, plucking Ciliata from the catwalks as if they were picking berries. 

The inhabitants do not fight the Stilt-Walkers; they out-produce them. The Ciliata way of life is one of "calculated loss." They build three pods for every one they need, knowing the predators will take their share. The city’s high-velocity pace is a direct response to this predation; they breed, build, and innovate faster than the shadows can consume them.

### The Culture of Damp and Dark
Because they lack fire, their "hearth" is the **Larval Ceiling**. The millions of pulsing larvae generate a low-grade metabolic heat. The elite of the Ciliata dwell in the "High Districts" closest to this ceiling, basking in the humid warmth and the rhythmic, drumming sound of a million tiny hearts. 

Communication is a tactile language of "Strids." A Ciliata will approach a companion and rub a specialized scraper on their hind leg against the other’s thorax. The resulting vibration conveys complex information: the quality of a resin-secretion, the location of a fresh water-bead, or a mourning-song for a collapsed pod-cluster. 

Their greatest art is the **Water-Catch**. Since a single drop of water can crush a dwelling, the Ciliata have turned disaster into a resource. They weave intricate funnels of hydrophobic silk that catch the manifesting mist, channeling the water into "Gravity-Wells"—hollowed-out galls where the water is stored. These wells serve as the city’s batteries; the weight of the water is used to power the pulley systems that lift their stone-shard tools.

In this jagged crack, life is a frantic, beautiful, and terrifying performance. The Ciliata do not hope for a sun they have never seen, nor do they fear a world beyond the granite. To them, the freezing mist is the breath of the universe, and the dark is a warm blanket that hides their miraculous, fragile industry from the cold indifference of the heights above.
