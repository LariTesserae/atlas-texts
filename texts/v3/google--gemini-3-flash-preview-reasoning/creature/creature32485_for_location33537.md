In the Kiln-Basin, "inhabitant" is a functional term rather than a biological one. Nothing here possesses a heartbeat, a nervous system, or a genome. Instead, the basin is populated by **The Labor-Forms**: semi-sentient manifestations of the 2.6-scale physics, entities of fired clay and mineral slurry that exist only to further the construction of a world that has no blueprint.

### The Thumb-Wrought
The most common denizens are the Thumb-Wrought. They are the size of houses, yet they move with a terrifying, liquid grace. They resemble unfinished, headless torsos or massive, multi-jointed limbs made of wet, terracotta-colored clay. They do not walk so much as they smear themselves across the tiles, leaving thick, ribbed trails behind them. 

The Thumb-Wrought are the primary masons. You will see them huddled around the base of a shifting mountain, their blunt, finger-like appendages pressing into the soft seams between blocks. They do not communicate with sound, but with pressure; they lean their massive weight against one another to signal where the next tile must be laid. They are perpetually damp, their "skin" constantly weeping the same mineral-heavy water as the ground, giving them a slick, glistening appearance under the bronze sky.

### The Calcified Scribes
Within the rectangular sluices, where the hot water flows with heavy, copper-scented intent, dwell the Scribes. These are not creatures, but rhythmic, self-organizing mineral deposits. They look like jagged, geometric crystalline growths—perfectly hexagonal or dodecahedral—that skitter along the bottom of the sluices against the current.

They act as the world’s sensory organs. As they scrape along the ceramic floor, they "read" the vibrations of the shifting tiles. When a section of the basin becomes too unstable or the geometry begins to loop illogically, the Scribes gather in massive, clashing clusters, their sharp edges grinding against the sluice walls to produce a high-pitched, metallic shriek. This sound draws the Thumb-Wrought to the site to begin the dismantling and rebuilding process.

### The Trowel-Lords (The Architraves)
Atop the distant, tapering ziggurats, there are entities that rarely move. These are the Architraves. They are massive, hollow shells of high-fired, obsidian-black ceramic, shaped like inverted pyramids or vast, unblinking eyes. 

The Architraves are the source of the *thud-thud-thud* that punctuates the silence. They possess a primitive, gravitational consciousness. They do not build; they *will*. By shifting their internal weight, they exert a localized pressure on the reality-damp basin, forcing the ground to flatten or the steam to thicken. They are the "hand-crankers" of the local physics. If a traveler were to stand beneath a ziggurat, they would feel the Architrave’s attention as a sudden, sickening increase in gravity, a command for the intruder to become part of the masonry.

### The Slurry-Strays
In the vast, flat stretches between monuments, one finds the Strays. These are the "waste" of the Basin—failed attempts at form. They are puddles of sentient, boiling mud that have gained enough cohesion to lift themselves off the ground. They take the shape of hollow spheres or thin, vibrating ribbons of clay. 

The Strays have no purpose. They are the Basin’s only true nomads, drifting aimlessly through the orange steam. They are attracted to heat and will often swarm around the rare points where the 2.6-scale reality breaks down, throwing themselves into the "soft" spots of the world to be absorbed back into the raw material of the floor.

### The Steam-Walkers
Finally, there are the things that live not on the ceramic, but in the atmosphere above it. The Steam-Walkers are spindly, translucent structures of salt and sulfur, held together by the surface tension of the perpetual perspiration. They are nearly invisible, appearing only as distortions in the orange light, like heat-haze given a skeletal form.

They are the harvesters of the "exhaled" water. They trail long, gossamer filaments of mineral-silk behind them, catching the rising vapor and condensing it into heavy droplets which they then let fall back into the sluices. They are the keepers of the Basin's cycle, ensuring the moisture never leaves the womb, keeping the clay forever wet, forever workable, and forever unfinished.

### The Collective Drive
None of these entities sleep. There is no day-night cycle in the bronze twilight, only the tireless, mindless progression of the work. They do not know what they are building. They do not know who the "Giants" were, whose thumbprints they now inhabit. They are simply the moving parts of a continental machine that is trying to solve a geometric equation that has no answer. To live in the Kiln-Basin is to be a tool that is also the craftsman, working in a fever-dream of wet brick and hot steam until the world finally, impossibly, feels "correct."
