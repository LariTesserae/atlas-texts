In this basin of iron and steam, the distinction between "animal" and "apparatus" has begun to dissolve. The inhabitants do not merely live among the machines; they have become the biological extension of the landscape’s mechanical will.

### The Lithic Carpet (The Silt-Sifters)
The billions of trilobite-like crustaceans that coat the mud are known as **Scutellers**. Their carapaces are not made of simple calcium, but are reinforced with the hematite and manganese they ingest. They are the color of dried blood and bruised iron. They move in waves, a shifting mosaic of overlapping plates that sounds like a thousand typewriters clicking in a distant room. They do not possess eyes; instead, they have arrays of sensitive, hair-like copper filaments that detect the subsonic hum of the pumping stations. They gravitate toward the vibrations, clustering around the foundations of the towers where the rhythmic thrum is strongest, vibrating their own shells in a sympathetic frequency that helps break down the dense mineral crust into edible silt.

### The Atmospheric Veils (The Condensate-Moths)
The translucent insects that drift through the steam are the **Mist-Gills**. Their bodies are long, segmented cylinders of pale, gelatinous tissue, looking like floating sections of semi-clear pipe. They do not fly so much as they navigate the density of the air. Their wings are broad, shimmering membranes that act as cooling fins. As they pass through the superheated plumes of the pressure valves, they trap the mineral-rich moisture in their porous skin. They are the world’s distillers. When they become heavy with liquid, they drift lower, settling onto the backs of larger creatures to share their moisture in exchange for the heat radiating from the larger beasts’ hides.

### The Great Thermal Scavengers (The Hulls)
The large, multi-legged creatures moving between the hubs are the **Hulls**. They are immense—some the size of the silos themselves—but they are incredibly low to the ground, their six or eight squat legs spread wide to distribute their weight across the treacherous mud. Their skin is a thick, vulcanized leather, perpetually slick with a coating of anaerobic bacteria that feeds on the sulfurous steam. 

The Hulls are the ultimate thermal opportunists. They do not hunt; they "berth." A Hull will find a massive, vibrating outflow pipe and press its broad, flat underbelly against the hot metal, remaining motionless for weeks. They have no mouths; they absorb energy through a specialized patch of vascular tissue on their ventral side, literally drinking the heat and the electrical leakage of the automated stations. Their slow, booming heartbeats often synchronize with the *clack-hiss* of the station’s valves, making it difficult to tell where the creature ends and the architecture begins.

### The Gasket-Leeches
Inside the pipes and clinging to the weeping joints of the silos are the **Lubricants**. These are long, ribbon-like annelids that thrive on the "heated grease" mentioned in the logs. They are a brilliant, synthetic-looking orange, providing the only splash of color in the grey world. They serve a strange, symbiotic function: they seek out micro-fractures and leaks in the industrial piping to feed on the escaping chemicals. In doing so, their own thick, viscous secretions seal the leaks, inadvertently maintaining the machinery that sustains the environment. They are living caulking, moving with a slow, peristaltic grace through the rusted interior of the gantries.

### The Sentinels of the Sump
Deep within the perfectly circular subsidence craters, where the water is still and black, live the **Resonators**. These are sessile, mound-like organisms that resemble piles of discarded slag. They are almost entirely composed of a dense, acoustic-sensitive jelly. They act as the "ears" of the basin. When a distant pump fails or a pipe bursts, the change in the subsonic frequency causes the Resonators to pulse with a dim, bioluminescent throb. This light, visible through the thick fog, serves as a beacon for the Hulls and the Scutellers, drawing them toward the new source of heat or vibration created by the mechanical distress.

There is no struggle in this place, no frantic chase of predator and prey. Life here is a patient, heavy process of absorption. The creatures are the curators of the rust, the harvesters of the heat, and the quiet, clicking witnesses to a world that functions with the cold, rhythmic certainty of a clock submerged in a swamp.
