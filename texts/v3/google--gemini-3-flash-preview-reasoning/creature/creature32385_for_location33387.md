In the Ocher-Conduct, the residents do not breathe so much as they aspirate the output of the world. They are the living viscera of a planetary engine, and their bodies have evolved to be as durable, patient, and heavy as the brass cathedrals they inhabit.

### The Chime-Wrights
Those creatures described as being the size of cathedral bells are the **Chime-Wrights**. They are not merely clinging to the pipes; they are fused to them by a biological weld of hardened, calcium-rich secretions. Their carapaces are magnificent, domed structures of iridescent, copper-toned chitin, layered like the scales of a pinecone. 

They serve as the planet’s acoustic filters. The Chime-Wrights possess hollow, vaulted interiors filled with a complex arrangement of resonant membranes. As the steam-bellows vent their scalding exhaust, the Chime-Wrights gulp the vapor, filtering out mineral particulates and heavy metals to nourish their sedentary bodies. In doing so, they vibrate. When a cluster of a thousand Chime-Wrights resonates in unison, they transform the harsh shriek of escaping pressure into the deep, rhythmic thrumming that defines the atmosphere. To look at a pipe covered in them is to see a living, pulsing lung, humming a song of high-pressure steam.

### The Sediment-Eaters (The Slow-Steppers)
The leather-skinned bipeds are the **Slow-Steppers**, the true giants of the Corrugated Vales. Their skin is not merely hide, but a living bio-polymer, thick and cracked like sun-baked mud, perpetually slicked with a protective coating of lubricating oil they exude from pores along their spines. They have no eyes; in a world of heat-shimmer and thick mist, sight is a liability. Instead, they possess massive, dish-like facial plates that "feel" the subsonic vibrations of the subterranean furnaces.

They move with a grace that is almost agonizing to watch, each step a calculated placement of a three-toed foot designed like a heavy-duty tripod. They do not hunt. They graze on the "Industrial Scurf"—the thick mats of iron-oxidizing bacteria and mineral-rich soot that coat the valley floors. Their digestive systems are essentially mobile smelting furnaces; they swallow rusted filings and crushed quartz, extracting trace nutrients and passing the rest as dense, smoothed pebbles of slag that form the very ground the next generation will walk upon.

### The Mica-Drifts
The gossamer-winged insects, the **Mica-Drifts**, are the most delicate things in the Conduct, yet they are made of the hardest materials. Their wings are transparent membranes reinforced with lattices of spun silica, allowing them to withstand the abrasive winds of the high towers. They do not possess mouths; they live for a single decade, fueled entirely by the static electricity they harvest from the air. 

They follow the heat-plumes. You can track the operation of a distant factory-district by watching the Mica-Drifts; they gather in swirling, miles-long ribbons where the warm exhaust meets the cooler high-altitude air. They are the Conduct’s nervous system, their constant flickering and humming carrying low-frequency signals across the highlands, alerting the slower life forms to shifts in the world’s tectonic rhythm.

### The Wire-Weavers
Hidden in the chaotic web of catwalks and junction boxes are the **Wire-Weavers**, small, multi-limbed scavengers with needle-thin digits. They are the Conduct’s mimics. They build their nests not in trees, but within the hollows of non-functional machinery, weaving together discarded copper wiring, shed scales from the Slow-Steppers, and solidified droplets of lead. 

The Wire-Weavers are the only creatures with an active interest in the machines themselves. They have developed a symbiotic relationship with the slow-pistons and gears; they pick away the corrosive salt-crusts that threaten to seize the joints, eating the mineral buildup and, in exchange, gaining a warm, vibrating home that no predator can breach. Their dexterity is such that they can strip a dead machine of its internal wiring in a matter of days, repurposing the "nerves" of the world into intricate, conductive webs used to trap the falling condensation.

### The Oil-Lurkers
Deep within the warm, shallow pools atop the factory roofs, where the water is agitated into a constant froth, live the **Oil-Lurkers**. These are flat, expansive organisms, resembling sheets of living, hammered silver. They float on the surface of the mineral runoff, tension-bound, soaking up the chemical energy of the industrial pollutants. They are the only creatures of the Ocher-Conduct that move with any speed—rippling across the surface of a pool like a spilled reflection to catch a falling Mica-Drift or a piece of organic debris.

***

In the Ocher-Conduct, there is no death in the traditional sense, only a cessation of movement. When a creature dies, it does not rot. The humid, mineral-rich air begins to coat it immediately. Within years, a fallen Slow-Stepper is indistinguishable from a rusted hummock of iron; a dead Chime-Wright becomes a permanent brass fixture on the pipe it once inhabited. 

The living and the built are in a state of constant, slow-motion exchange. The machines provide the warmth and the water; the animals provide the maintenance and the song. It is a closed loop, a world where the soul is made of wet leather and brass, and the heartbeat is the rhythmic *thud-hiss* of a planet that has forgotten how to be anything but a hive.
