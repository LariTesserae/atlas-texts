To live within the Great Lens is to exist in a state of perpetual, high-speed collision. The inhabitants do not merely reside here; they are the survivors of a world that is being born and destroyed every hour.

### The Suture-Kin
The primary residents—those responsible for the lashed bones and the manual knots—are the **Suture-Kin**. They are creatures of translucent, glass-like muscle and elongated, multi-jointed fingers that end in hooked, obsidian-hard nails. They possess no eyes in the traditional sense; the raw, bleaching sun of the apex would sear any retina. Instead, their entire torso is a photo-sensitive membrane, a shimmering "skin-eye" that perceives the world as a shifting map of heat and caustic refraction.

They are few—perhaps only three or four at any given time. They do not have a language of words, for no sound could carry over the deafening, bone-shaking thrum of the water. Instead, they communicate through the **Tethers**. By plucking the braided kelp lines they have anchored to the basalt spires, they send rhythmic vibrations through the sphere. A sharp, high-frequency snap might mean a salt-spire is about to shatter; a low, steady pulse is a territorial claim.

Their life is a desperate choreography of grip. Because the geography of the Lens is embryonic and unstable, the Suture-Kin spend their lives tying the world together. They hunt the emerging shards of basalt, lashing them to older, more stable rocks to create a temporary anchor. They live in the "nests"—not for sleep, as the turbulence makes sleep a lethal luxury, but as waystations where they can wedge their bodies into the crevices of the bone-frames to keep from being pulverized by the sphere’s internal gyre.

### The Gyre-Mites
The "spinning top" organisms are the **Gyre-Mites**. They are the primary food source and the greatest hazard for the Suture-Kin. These thumb-sized creatures have shells of biological glass, perfectly smooth and harder than diamond. They don't swim so much as they *ricochet*. They feed by filtering the micro-bubbles created by the pressure, turning the chaos of the water into kinetic energy. 

When a Gyre-Mite reaches a certain velocity, it begins to glow with a pale, bioluminescent violet, acting as a living tracer-round in the dark, churning water. The Suture-Kin hunt them with their bare hands, timing the grab to the micro-second, risking broken fingers to catch a single, calorie-dense burst of gelatinous meat.

### The Litho-Whale (The "Unknown Creature")
The large ribs lashed to the obsidian spikes belong to the **Litho-Whale**, a creature that is not truly an animal, but a biological crystal. Once every few decades, a mass of minerals and ghost-kelp fibers coalesces into a singular, slow-moving entity the size of a carriage. It has no mouth; it absorbs minerals directly from the water, growing massive, curved ribs of porous, bone-like salt to protect its soft, pressurized core.

The Litho-Whale is the only thing in the Lens that moves slowly, and for the Suture-Kin, its appearance is a holy event. They swarm it, not to kill it, but to inhabit it. They lash their anchors to its slow-growing frame, building a "city" that may last for a month before the Whale inevitably grows too dense, loses its buoyancy, and is shattered by the sphere’s centrifugal force, scattering its "bones" to be used as building materials by the survivors.

### The Ghost-Kelp
The **Ghost-Kelp** acts as the nervous system of the sphere. It is a pale, shimmering weed that grows not from soil, but from the friction of the water itself. It anchors its roots into the sound—literally wrapping its tendrils around the points of highest vibration. 

The Suture-Kin harvest it with reverence. It is their only tool, their only rope, and their only bedding. It is incredibly strong, capable of holding a basalt shard against the pull of a vacuum, yet it feels like cold silk. When the light hits the kelp at the right angle, it reflects the prismatic colors of the sun, making the lashed nests look like tangled, underwater rainbows.

### The Experience of Being
To be a resident of the Great Lens is to live in a "now" that is violent and absolute. There is no history here, only the current knot. There is no future, only the next spire of salt. The inhabitants are the architects of a house that is constantly falling down, tied together with the bones of the past and the weeds of the present, all while the sun screams through the water in a blinding, silent roar. They are the guardians of the apex, the lonely weavers of a world that refuses to stay built.
