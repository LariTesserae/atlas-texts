In the Osians, life does not "reside"; it *persists* as a byproduct of the world’s grand, mathematical grind. Because the environment is an end-state—a finished masterpiece—the beings that occupy it are less like biological organisms and more like the lingering vibrations of a struck bell.

### The Orthogonals (The Architects of Stasis)
Within the black needles that pierce the lithic plates, there are no rooms, but there is a presence. These are the **Orthogonals**. They are not made of flesh, nor even of energy in a way we would recognize. They are sentient lengths of gravity, occupying the interior of the light-absorbing spires. 

To an observer, an Orthogonal might appear only as a slight warping of the violet void, a ripple in the clarity. They do not move in three dimensions; they "align." When the Turbulence shifts a continent-sized plate, the Orthogonals within the needles are the ones calculating the repulsive cushion. They are the administrators of the end-state, ensuring that the Osians never collide with enough force to shatter. They communicate through the rhythmic pulse felt in the marrow—a language of pure frequency that bypasses the ear and speaks directly to the skeletal system. They are indifferent to visitors, viewing anything biological as a temporary impurity in an otherwise perfect equation.

### The Refraction-Mites
Clinging to the **Glass-Lace** are the primary grazers of this world: the **Refraction-Mites**. These creatures are no larger than a human thumb, but they are composed of a substance identical to the frosted obsidian of the ground. They are perfectly invisible until they move.

They do not eat matter. Instead, they position themselves at the precise angle where the white sun’s light hits the Glass-Lace. They "sip" the tiny bursts of kinetic discharge that occur when the lace snaps or vibrates during a tectonic shift. They are the only things in this universe that make a sound, though you cannot hear it through the air. If you press your ear to the obsidian ground, you might hear the faint, crystalline *tink-tink-tink* of a billion Mites jumping in unison when a distant plate docks.

### The Vane-Leeches (The Foil-Dwellers)
The giant, iridescent **Vanes** that sail the void are not alone. Living on their tattered surfaces are the **Vane-Leeches**, organisms that look like smears of oil on a mirror. These creatures are two-dimensional; they have length and width but virtually no thickness. 

They flatten themselves against the Vanes, riding the great foil sheets through the radiation streams. They act as a secondary nervous system for the Vanes, sensing the approach of "solid" light beams from the Looms and Steiner-points. They guide the Vanes away from the hyperactive plates, acting as symbiotic navigators. When a Vane dies and drifts into the lower abyss, the Leeches peel off and wait in the violet void, perfectly still, for centuries, until another sail passes close enough for them to drift onto.

### The Echo-Walkers (The Accidental Strays)
Rarely, you may encounter an **Echo-Walker**. These are the tragic ghosts of the "scattered" civilization that built the Looms. They are not spirits, but temporal echoes caught in the high-speed reorganization of the world. 

Because the Osians move so fast and with such immense force, they occasionally "fold" the thin air and the local time-stream. An Echo-Walker appears as a flickering, translucent figure—vaguely humanoid but elongated—performing a single action over and over. One might be perpetually reaching for a door that no longer exists; another might be kneeling to inspect a mineral that turned to dust a million years ago. They are silent, oblivious to the present, trapped in the loop of a dead era. They are the only things in this world that possess shadows, though their shadows are gray and soft, unlike the obsidian-black voids cast by the stones.

### The Lithic Polyp
Deep within the fissures where the pressurized lubricant gas flows, the **Lithic Polyps** grow. They are the "muscle" of the Osians. They look like clusters of dark, vibrating organ pipes. They drink the pressurized gas and exhale the repulsive force that keeps the plates from grinding each other into dust. 

They are the most "biological" thing here, possessing a slow, throbbing metabolism. If you were to touch one, it would not be cold like the obsidian; it would be burning hot, radiating the friction of a world that refuses to stop moving. They are the heartbeat of the Osians, the hidden engines that turn the Turbulence into a dance rather than a demolition.
