In this cathedral of amber and wind, life does not merely exist; it is an extension of the Great Pulse. To live here is to be a note in a song too loud to hear, a song that vibrates the marrow of the bone until the inhabitants are less "individuals" and more resonant frequencies of the organism itself.

### The Vestibulars (The Silent Dwellers)
The primary inhabitants of the xylem-walkways are the **Vestibulars**, a species of lanky, translucent bipeds who have long since traded their hearing for an exquisite, total-body sense of balance. Their skin is the color of the honeyed light, a pale, semi-clear membrane that reveals the slow, rhythmic flow of their own internal fluids, synchronized perfectly to the plant’s 3.0-decibel thrum.

They have no ears—in a world of deafening sound, such organs would be liabilities, bleeding and useless. Instead, their bodies are covered in microscopic, feather-like cilia that act as seismic sensors. They "hear" with their skin, reading the vibrations of the sap-flow to predict when a walkway might be reabsorbed or when a sheet of rain is about to break from the ceiling-vaults.

They move with a slow, liquid grace, walking up the walls and across the ceilings without a change in posture. Because gravity pulls toward the living tissue, their "streets" are the entire surface area of the chambers. They do not build houses; they sleep in shallow, resin-filled depressions in the walls, floating in a stasis that allows their skin to fuse temporarily with the organism, sharing nutrients and heat. Their language is one of touch and rhythmic drumming against the cellulose membranes—a percussive telepathy that cuts through the roar of the god’s throat.

### The Nectar-Glints
Filling the vast, empty air are the **Nectar-Glints**, the six-winged insects mentioned in the travelers' tales. While they are the size of hawks, they are fragile, their bodies made of hollow, chitinous tubes and iridescent film. They are the plant’s gardeners and its white blood cells. 

They do not fly by muscle alone; they possess internal bladders of buoyant gas harvested from the fermenting nectar. They drift on the thermal currents like kites, using their six wings to steer toward the "pools" of light in the center of the chambers. They are attracted to the concentrated phosphorescence, diving through the glowing gas to incinerate microscopic spores and parasites that might clog the plant’s stomata. When they move, they leave trails of shimmering dust—pollen that tastes like bruised mint and acts as a mild hallucinogen to any other creature that inhales it.

### The Pith-Clamberers
On the undersides of the bridges and in the deepest shadows of the pitcher-pools live the **Pith-Clamberers**. These are the hairless primates, but they are more like amphibious lemurs than monkeys. Their suction-cup digits are so powerful they can peel the thickened cellulose away from the walls, and they spend their lives scavenging the "scabs" of the organism—areas where the plant has healed over a wound or reabsorbed a redundant structure.

They are the only creatures here that seem to feel the weight of the sound. They huddle in groups of thirty or forty, pressing their chests together to cancel out the vibration of the sap-flow. They are the scavengers of the tea-pools, diving into the dark, enzyme-rich liquid to retrieve the drowned bodies of Glint-Wasps or pieces of fruit that have fallen from the high, budding balconies. Their vocalizations are silent to the world, but if you press your ear to their chests, you can hear a frantic, high-pitched chattering—a secret, quiet life hidden beneath the roar.

### The Membrane-Weavers
Perhaps the most mysterious are the **Membrane-Weavers**, tiny, spider-like crustaceans that live within the walls themselves. They are the architects of the "urban" grid. They do not spin webs of silk, but secrete a chemical that catalyzes the growth of the plant’s own xylem. 

When a Vestibular drums a specific rhythm against a wall, the Weavers respond to the vibration, congregating in that spot to stimulate the growth of a new balcony or a spiraling staircase. They are the bridge between the will of the inhabitants and the biology of the host. They are rarely seen, existing as moving lumps beneath the translucent skin of the walls, like marbles rolling under a carpet.

### The Ghost-Flow
Finally, there is the **Ghost-Flow**. Not a creature in the traditional sense, but a localized consciousness formed by the pooling light and the airborne pollen. In the centers of the largest gourds, where gravity is weakest and the light is thickest, shapes begin to form. These "ghosts" are brief, flickering apparitions of the plant’s own memory—silhouettes of walkways that vanished a century ago, or echoes of long-dead inhabitants. They drift in the golden haze, mindless and beautiful, occasionally being inhaled by a Glint-Wasp or dispersed by a sudden sheet of interior rain, only to reform when the air grows heavy again.

In this place, there is no distinction between the city and the citizen. The inhabitants are the flora's thoughts, the insects its immune system, and the primates its lingering, nervous dreams. Everyone here is a part of the god’s song, whether they wish to be or not.
