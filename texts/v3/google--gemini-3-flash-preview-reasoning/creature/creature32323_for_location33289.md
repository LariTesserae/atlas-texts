To live in the Shatter-Sink is to exist in a state of perpetual, high-velocity friction. Evolution here has not favored the elegant or the complex, but the durable and the frantic. The inhabitants are masterpieces of specialized trauma.

### The Lithic-Wraiths (The "Silents")
The beings who use the tools are not a single race, but a desperate, converging lineage of hominids who have long since traded speech for survival. They are tall and unnervingly thin, their skeletons composed of a high-density mineral graft that makes their bones as heavy and hard as the basalt they walk upon. Their skin is their most remarkable feature: a grey, multi-layered hide that feels like cured sharkskin or industrial sandpaper. It is thick enough to slide across an obsidian razor without parting, allowing them to move in a "liquid-slide"—a method of travel where they throw themselves onto the jagged slopes and coast on their own calloused bellies and palms.

They have no external ears; the metallic roar of the valley would liquify delicate drums. Instead, they possess a series of pits along their jawlines and collarbones that track vibrations through the air and the rock. They communicate in the "Quiet," a language of tactile taps and rapid, rhythmic drumming on the stone floor during the ten-minute steam-pulses when the sound of the environment reaches a predictable, white-noise peak.

They are obsessed with *weight*. Because the swarm can carry off anything light, the Silents carry heavy obsidian "anchors" strapped to their waists with silk-vine. When the wind or a swarm becomes too violent, they wedge these anchors into the floor-cracks and hunker down, becoming part of the geology until the danger passes.

### The Chitin-Grinders
The dominant fauna are the **Pneumatic Swarmers**. These creatures, ranging from the size of a finger to the size of a wagon, are not powered by traditional musculature. Instead, they have internal bladders that harvest the hourly steam-pulse. When the valley "sweats," these beasts inflate, their segmented plates interlocking with a series of wet, mechanical clicks.

For the fifty minutes of dryness, they are hyper-active, fueled by the stored pressure of the steam. The largest are the **Sunder-Striders**, tripod-like behemoths with legs like obsidian needles. They don't walk; they "stitch" themselves across the landscape, their legs punching deep into the rock with every step, vibrating at such a high frequency that they shatter the basalt beneath them to gain purchase. They eat the rubbery fungal stalks, grinding the fibrous indigo pulp between rotating plates of dental-stone that sound like a rock-crusher in a quarry.

### The Pulse-Feeders and Glint-Moths
In the vertical jungle of the canopy, life is smaller but no less violent. The **Glint-Moths** are not insects in the traditional sense, but winged shards of living glass. Their wings are stiff, translucent membranes of biological silica. They do not fly by flapping; they are gliders that catch the thermal updrafts of the steam-pulses. They are predatory, diving from the velvet vines to impale smaller creatures on their wing-tips, then retreating to the indigo stalks to lap up the phosphoric fluids that leak from the wound.

Beneath them, the **Velvet-Stranglers** (the vines) are the slowest predators. They are sensitive to the "bruised" light. When a creature’s bioluminescence passes near a vine, the sensory hairs trigger a localized contraction. The vine doesn't just squeeze; it secretes a fast-acting caustic enzyme that dissolves chitin. A hound-sized grazer caught in the vines will be a hollowed-out shell by the next steam-pulse, its minerals absorbed to fuel the vine’s next three inches of growth.

### The Scavengers of the "Now"
At the very bottom, in the narrowest cracks where the obsidian meets the true crust, live the **Marrow-Lickers**. These are soft, slug-like organisms—the only soft things in the Shatter-Sink—but they survive by being unreachable. They inhabit the "negative space" of the valley. When the steam-pulse hits, they emerge to feast on the salt-residue and the microscopic debris left behind by the blender-like food chain above. 

They are the record-keepers of the Sink; their translucent bodies often contain the undigested jewelry, teeth, or tool-fragments of those who died hours before. They are the only things that stay still, tucked into the microscopic pores of the basalt, waiting for the world to grind itself into a powder they can finally digest.

### The Ecology of the Sound
Everything here is a musician of necessity. The **Shriek-Beetles** use the metallic roar as a cloak; they synchronize their own high-frequency hunting cries to the exact pitch of the valley’s grinding plates, rendering themselves sonically invisible to their prey. 

The "ghosts," the Silents, have learned this. They hunt by looking for the *absence* of sound—the small, circular pockets of "dead air" created when a predator’s cry cancels out the background roar. In the Shatter-Sink, silence is the sound of something about to strike.
