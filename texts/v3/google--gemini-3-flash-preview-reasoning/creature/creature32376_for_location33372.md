In this cradle of slow-motion genesis, the inhabitants are not born; they are compiled. They exist as specific sub-routines of the room’s greater architecture, each a localized expression of the frost-bound logic that governs the substrate.

### The Lignin-Stitchers (The Weavers)
The small, multi-limbed organisms mentioned in the logs are the **Lignin-Stitchers**. They are the most mobile of the residents, though "mobile" is a relative term—a Stitcher may take a lunar cycle to cross the village-square floor. Their bodies are composed of pressurized carbon, faceted like black diamonds to withstand the crushing atmospheric pressure of the deep earth.

They do not possess mouths or digestive tracts. Instead, their undersides are a forest of microscopic, silver filaments that they "plug" into the floor’s mesh. They are the room's mobile processors, carrying complex genetic instructions from the churning proto-cells below to the fractal leaves above. When a Stitcher moves, it sounds like a violin string snapping under extreme tension. They are the editors of the space, painstakingly rearranging the violet-lit veins of the plants to ensure the "circuitry" of the room remains closed.

### The Tecton-Pillars (The Sentinels)
Rising from the embryonic substrate are the **Tecton-Pillars**. To a casual observer, they are merely thick, pillar-like stems, but they are the most sophisticated thinkers in the chamber. Their "bark" is a series of overlapping, slate-gray plates that shiver with a rhythmic, tectonic vibration. 

These are not plants in the traditional sense, but massive, vertical hard drives. Each plate on their surface stores a billion years of evolutionary history, held in a state of cryogenic suspension. They do not perceive the world through light, but through the "blood" that weeps from the ceiling. As the supercooled droplets hit their plates, the Tectons read the solutes within—measuring the exact mineral composition of the loam above and the faint, seismic echoes of the world outside. They are waiting for a specific chemical signature in the water, a "password" from the surface that will tell them it is safe to begin the final unfurling.

### The Glass-Lunged Spores (The Sensorium)
The "air" is inhabited by the **Glass-Lunged Spores**. These are the room’s nervous system, suspended in the viscous atmosphere. They are tiny, crystalline geometries that drift with the weight of gold coins. Each spore is a sensory node, capable of detecting the thermal footprint of a single atom. 

They do not fly; they swim through the supercooled vapor, their movements dictated by the low-frequency thrum of the walls. They act as a communal brain, a cloud of floating neurons that process the "calculations" performed by the vegetation. If an intruder were to enter, the spores would not attack; they would simply incorporate the intruder into the calculation, slowing the visitor’s molecular vibration until they, too, became a static, frosted part of the architecture.

### The Protagonist of the Seed (The Sleeper)
At the very center of the cradle, where the violet light is dimmest and the "blood" falls in the heaviest globules, resides the **Nucleus**. It is not a creature, but a silhouette—a shape that looks like a human figure caught in the act of waking, or perhaps a star being compressed into a marble. 

The Nucleus is the "seed" itself. It is the only thing in the room that is not yet fully formed. It is a dense, throbbing knot of unspooled genetic code, held in a state of absolute potential. All the work of the Stitchers, the calculations of the Tectons, and the sensory drifting of the Spores are for the sake of this one entity. It is the template for whatever universe is intended to replace the current one. It does not breathe, but it *pulses*—a slow, deep systolic thump that occurs once every decade, driving the supercooled slick through the room’s vascular system.

### The Silicate Parasites (The Glitches)
Occasionally, the process of creation produces errors. These are the **Silicate Parasites**—jagged, translucent growths that resemble shards of broken mirrors. They emerge from the floor like jagged teeth, attempting to siphon the information-carrying liquid for no purpose other than to grow into mindless, sharp geometries. 

The Stitchers treat these as "dead code." In a process that takes years, the Stitchers will surround a parasite, vibrating at a frequency that shatters the silicate structure back into dust, which is then re-absorbed into the embryonic substrate to be re-written.

Everything in this space is a version of the same thing: the room itself. There is no ego here, only the patient, frozen labor of a machine that has forgotten it is made of meat, and a forest that has realized it is a computer.
