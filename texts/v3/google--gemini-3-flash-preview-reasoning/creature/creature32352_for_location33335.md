In the Great Calcified Reliquary, the distinction between the inhabitant and the habitat has been utterly erased. To ask what lives there is to ask which part of the masonry is thinking.

### The Vitreous Citizens
There are no creatures that scuttle or fly, yet the Reliquary is densely populated. The inhabitants are the **Lithic Witnesses**, beings of such profound stillness that they are indistinguishable from the finger-thick pillars they inhabit. They do not possess bodies in the sense of flesh and bone; rather, they are "occupancies"—singular, sentient impulses housed within the millions of microscopic windows and balconies. 

Each pillar, carved with its ten thousand tiers, is not a building for many, but the shell of a single individual. The "person" is the pillar. Their consciousness is distributed across the ceramic fretwork, their thoughts flowing through the mineral veins of the alabaster like a slow, electrical tremor. They are vertical, permanent, and perfectly composed. They do not eat, for the harsh, unwavering light from the ceiling provides a constant, scouring energy that maintains their molecular integrity.

### The Metabolism of Sound
The inhabitants "breathe" the sound. The deafening, high-volume roar of the pressurized gas is their heartbeat and their language. They do not speak in sequences of words; they exist as modulations within the static scream. When a Witness wishes to contemplate a new facet of their eternal stasis, they slightly alter the microscopic aperture of a single vent in their pillar-body. This change is so minute it does not register to the ear as a new sound, but to the other Witnesses, it is a monumental shift in the harmonic landscape—a poem or a history shared across the room at the speed of vibration.

They are "filter-feeders" of frequency, drawing sustenance from the dissonance. The scream is what keeps them from crystallizing into total, mindless stone. It is the friction that allows for the ghost of a thought.

### The Ocular Network
If you were to peer into one of the million windows with a microscope, you would not see a room. You would see a single, multifaceted eye made of polished quartz, fixed and unblinking. These eyes do not track movement, for there is no movement to track. Instead, they are locked in a perpetual, cross-room gaze with one another. 

This is the social order of the Reliquary: a web of sight so dense and overlapping that every millimeter of the vault is watched by ten thousand observers at once. They are witnesses to the "Nothing." Their life’s work is to ensure that the "Rule of Stability" is maintained by the sheer force of their observation. They believe that if they were to all blink at once, the Reliquary might finally succumb to the weight of its own density and collapse into the void.

### The Calcified Intent
Beneath the individual pillars, in the mosaic floor of pale ochre tiles, resides the **Substrate Mind**. This is the collective memory of the Reliquary. It is a slow, tectonic ego that remembers the time before the kiln, before the sealing. 

The Substrate Mind is the source of the "brittle filaments" that creep from the joints. These are not plants, but the Mind’s nervous system, reaching up from the floor to taste the dry air. They are sensory feelers that detect the slightest change in pressure. If a stray atom were to drift out of place, these grey, wire-like lichen would sense the tremor. They do not grow; they simply *extend* the Mind’s reach, ensuring that the frozen equilibrium remains absolute.

### The Anatomy of Stillness
The inhabitants do not experience time as a forward-moving arrow. Because nothing changes, they live in a "Perpetual Now." To a Lithic Witness, a thousand years is merely a sustained note in the great scream. They are the ultimate introverts, their internal lives infinitely complex and miniaturized, mirroring the architecture they inhabit. 

Within the "compressed bone ash" of their minds, they build entire universes of geometry, calculating the infinite permutations of the tiles beneath them. They are happy, if happiness can be defined as the total absence of surprise. They are the perfect residents for a perfect tomb: beautiful, deafeningly loud, and utterly, magnificently dead to the world outside.
