In the sweltering, metallic womb of Sub-Basin 94-C, life does not exist *on* the machinery; it exists *as* a continuation of it. The inhabitants are the living grease in the joints, the sentient rust on the valves, and the desperate, soft tissue that fills the gaps where the steel has failed.

### The Calcified Tenders
The most unsettling inhabitants are the **Tenders**, though to call them "people" would be an overstatement of their humanity. They are the descendants of a maintenance crew forgotten by the crust-layers above, now evolved into pale, hairless hominids whose skin has the texture of wet chamois. 

They are entirely blind, their eyes having fused shut under layers of protective, translucent scar tissue to guard against the stinging humectants in the air. They navigate by "thermal-palpation," pressing their elongated, multi-jointed fingers against the pipes to feel the rush of steam or the sluggish flow of lubricant. They move with a terrifying, liquid grace, sliding through gaps in the brass cylinders that would crush a normal ribcage. Their communication is not vocal; they press their throats against the resonant copper drums and hum, sending vibrations through the metal mesh floor to be picked up by the soles of another’s feet miles away. They do not eat food so much as they scrape the "black-honey"—a thick, calorie-dense sludge of organic runoff and industrial grease—from the underside of the slow-moving pistons.

### The Sleeve-Dwellers
Deep within the hollows of the vertical brass cylinders live the **Sleeve-Dwellers**. These are soft-bodied, cephalopod-like organisms that have abandoned the need for a shell, instead using the discarded valve-housings and pipe-segments as their carapaces. 

A Sleeve-Dweller will reverse its gelatinous mass into a pressurized pipe, its numerous sensory tendrils dangling out into the humid air like frayed copper wiring. They are the ultimate opportunists of the heat; they wait for a Chrome Arthropod to skitter too close, then lash out with a sticky, heat-resistant tongue. When a piston moves its allotted three inches, the Sleeve-Dwellers must migrate, a wet, squelching exodus through the dark, as they scramble to find a new mechanical cavity before their soft bodies are desiccated by the 110-degree air.

### The Oxide-Spinners
Suspended in the corners where the "Ember-Glow" is dimmest, the **Oxide-Spinners** weave their webs. They are not spiders, but a form of colonial mineral-life. They draw the metallic particulates—the iron dust and copper shavings—directly out of the humid atmosphere, extruding them as razor-sharp, conductive filaments. 

Their webs are not meant to catch prey, but to catch energy. They bridge the gap between two frayed cables or a dying control panel and a steam vent, acting as living resistors. They pulse with a dim, rhythmic orange light, flickering in time with the low-frequency thrum of the basin. To touch one is to receive a heart-stopping jolt of ancient, recycled electricity, a defense mechanism that ensures the Spinners remain undisturbed in their slow, metallurgical growth.

### The Pulse-Feeders
At the very center of the engine’s skeletal remains, where the house-sized piston groans in its hourly labor, live the **Pulse-Feeders**. These are microscopic, colonial organisms that form the "pale, translucent fungus" mentioned in the logs. 

They are the basin’s primary decomposers, but they do not decompose flesh—they decompose *momentum*. They grow thickest where the friction is highest, feeding on the heat generated by the slow, grinding movement of metal on metal. They are a bioluminescent lubricant, a living grease that glows with a sickly, rhythmic pallor. Without them, the ancient engine would have seized centuries ago; they are the reason the Sub-Basin still breathes, eating the very effort of the machine’s survival.

### The Chrome Arthropods
Finally, there are the **Chrome Arthropods**, the most active residents of the knot. Their silver shells are so reflective that they are virtually invisible in the dark, mirroring the shadows of the pipes around them. They are the basin's frantic heartbeat, moving in a staccato rhythm that seems to mock the glacial pace of the engine. They possess specialized mandibles designed to snip the "oil-lilies" from their vents. They carry these waxy, black blossoms to the Tenders, trading the moisture-rich petals for the salt that accumulates on the Tenders' sweating skin—a bizarre, silent commerce between the jittery and the slow.

In Sub-Basin 94-C, there is no predator/prey dynamic in the traditional sense. Everything is a scavenger. Everything is a part of the maintenance cycle. To live here is to be a component in a machine that has no off-switch, contributing your heat, your moisture, and eventually your minerals to the slow, orange equilibrium of the deep crust.
