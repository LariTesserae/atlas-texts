In the Ouroboros Plateau, life does not "happen"; it persists. Because time has finished its work, the inhabitants do not experience birth or death in a way that would be recognizable to us. Instead, they exist as biological consequences of the environment’s stuttering physics and the basalt’s long memory.

### The Lithopedions (The Stone-Heirs)
The most prominent inhabitants are not biological in the traditional sense, yet they are the only things that truly "occupy" the cyclopean cities. The Lithopedions are humanoid figures composed of the same slick, obsidian-dark bedrock as the Plateau itself. They are not statues, though they may remain motionless for a thousand years.

They are the physical manifestations of the Rule of Memory. When the basalt slabs leach enough heat and "remembrance" from the atmosphere—or from the rare, stray traveler—the stone exudes a Lithopedion. They move with the agonizing slowness of the moss, their joints grinding with the sound of a tectonic plate. They do not speak; they simply maintain the "finished" state of the cities. You might find one standing in a circular plaza, staring at a point on the horizon where a star vanished a billion years ago. If you touch one, you will feel a faint, rhythmic throb—the stolen heartbeat of someone who walked these halls in a previous epoch.

### The Glass-Lung Grazers
Moving through the indigo moss-cathedrals are the Grazers. They resemble massive, six-legged tortoises, but their shells are translucent, fashioned from the same silver frost that clings to the ground. Their internal organs are visible: a glowing, bioluminescent network of indigo veins that mirror the lichen they consume.

The Grazers do not breathe air; they filter the "crushed glass" of the atmosphere through crystalline filaments in their throats. This process creates a faint, chiming sound, like wind-chimes in a storm. They are the source of the heat-pockets within the moss. As they digest the ancient lichen, their bodies generate a thermal output so intense that the air around them shimmers. Small, wingless insects—thin as paper and clear as water—huddle in the wake of a Grazer’s path, living their entire multi-century lifespans in the few feet of warmth trailing behind a single beast.

### The Umbra-Vagrants (The Shadow-Kin)
Because shadows on the Plateau move independently of their owners, they have eventually developed a semi-autonomous ecology. The Umbra-Vagrants are "flat" entities that slide across the polished basalt and the mounds of indigo moss. They have no thickness, yet they possess a predatory intelligence.

They are the scavengers of the "stutter-quakes." When a mountain or a stone ramp flickers out of alignment, the Umbra-Vagrants rush to the "seam" where reality has thinned. They feed on the discarded moments—the versions of objects that *didn't* snap back into place. To an observer, they look like silhouettes of impossible creatures—birds with too many wings, or geometric shapes that hurt the eyes—rippling across the ground. They are harmless to the physical body, but to stand in one is to feel a sudden, crushing wave of nostalgia for a life you never lived.

### The Sky-Siphons
High above, where the air begins to fray into the void, live the Siphons. They are long, ribbon-like organisms, miles in length but only inches wide, pale and shimmering like aurora borealis. They do not touch the ground. They float in the "drift-zones" where gravity fails, tethering themselves to the tops of the obsidian domes with long, gossamer threads of hardened light.

The Siphons act as the Plateau’s lightning rods for the "Atmospheric Hum." They vibrate in symphony with the wind, and this vibration is what they eat. They are the conductors of the world’s silence. When a Siphon dies—which happens only when the tectonic stillness finally snaps a dome—it does not fall. It simply dissolves into a cloud of silver mist, becoming the very frost that feeds the moss below.

### The Solitary Echoes
There is a final class of inhabitant, rarer than the rest: The Echoes. These are the "glitches" in human form. Because the Plateau is a place where the horizon allows you to see the back of your own head, certain individuals become "looped." 

You might encounter a figure in the distance, dressed in rags of silver-moss, walking with a steady, purposeful gait. If you follow them, you will realize they are walking toward a version of themselves that is ten minutes ahead. They are trapped in the "Mass is Memory" loop, living ghosts who are neither dead nor alive, but simply repeating a sequence of movements for eternity. They are the true citizens of the Ouroboros—beings who have become part of the landscape’s recursive geometry.

They do not notice you. In a world that is finished, there is no room for anything new. They, like the stone and the moss, are simply waiting for the void to finish fraying, until the last indigo pulse flickers out and the shadow finally catches the man.
