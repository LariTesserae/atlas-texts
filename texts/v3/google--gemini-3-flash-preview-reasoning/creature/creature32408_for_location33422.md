The inhabitants of the lithic lung are not born so much as they are precipitated. In a realm where moisture is a myth, life has forsaken the fluidity of flesh for the permanence of the mineral.

### The Lithos-Kin
The builders of the triangular hovels are the **Lithos-kin**, fox-sized entities that resemble animated sculptures of smoked quartz and fused ash. Their bodies are masterpieces of geometric economy. They possess four primary limbs—long, multi-jointed stilts ending in hardened obsidian points—which allow them to navigate the razor-sharp terrain like tightrope walkers. Their torsos are encased in a "cuirass" of overlapping mica plates, which shiver in a constant, micro-vibratory blur.

They have no mouths, for there is no ingestion here. Instead, their chest-plates act as permeable bellows, drawing the heavy sulfurous gases and mica dust directly into a central, glowing core—a "hearth" of concentrated phosphorus that serves as their metabolic engine. 

Their heads are faceless, save for a single, wide band of polished, dark opal that wraps around the skull. This is not an eye in the traditional sense, but a thermal and seismic sensor. They do not "see" the vault; they *feel* the heat-signatures of the plumes and "hear" the architecture of the space through the constant tectonic roar.

The Lithos-kin are the architects of the silence. They move with a deliberate, choreographic grace, their obsidian tools used not for killing, but for *tuning*. They chip at the walls to alter the resonance of the tectonic chord, treating their home as a gargantuan musical instrument. The ochre circles they draw are maps of vibration—zones where the roar is purest, where they huddle in their triangular huts to enter a state of "deep-ringing," a mineral trance that serves as their equivalent of sleep.

### The Hematite Grazers
Clinging to the vertical facets of the quartz outcroppings are the **Scour-Crabs**. These hexapedal creatures have shells of polished, mirror-finish hematite, making them look like droplets of mercury frozen in mid-drip. They are the vault’s cleaners. Their limbs end in specialized abrasive pads that they use to grind away the sulfur crusts that accumulate on the silicate plumes. 

They move with a geological patience, perhaps an inch every hour. Because there is no decay, their life cycles are immense; a Scour-Crab may spend a century grazing a single crystal pillar. When they die, they do not rot; they simply stop moving, eventually becoming a permanent, metallic extrusion of the rock they once tended.

### The Vitrified Weavers
The wingless, iridescent insects hanging in the air are the **Glint-Flies**. They are the only creatures that bridge the gap between the stone floor and the bioluminescent ceiling. Their carapaces are spun from fine, transparent glass, making them nearly invisible save for the way they refract the amber light into tiny rainbows.

They are predators of a sort, though their hunt is passive. They hang from threads of spun mineral glass—invisible, razor-thin filaments that catch the fine mica dust drifting in the air. The Glint-Flies live on the static electricity generated by the tectonic roar; their long, hair-like antennae act as lightning rods, drawing micro-sparks from the vibrating atmosphere. They are the "neurons" of the vault, their collective flickering providing a dim, pulsing map of the air currents.

### The Silent Equilibrium
There is no competition here, no hunger, and no fear. The inhabitants are as much a part of the geology as the basalt ridges. The Lithos-kin do not hunt the Scour-Crabs; the Glint-Flies do not bother the Lithos-kin. They are a closed circuit, a biological clockwork mechanism that has reached a state of stasis.

If you were to watch them for a thousand years, you might see a single Lithos-kin complete a single ochre circle. You might see a Scour-Crab move from one side of a quartz facet to the other. To them, the monolithic roar is not noise—it is the very breath of God, the steady, unchanging pulse that reminds them they are cradled in the ribs of the world. They are the curators of the eternal, living statues in a cathedral that will never know a sunset.
