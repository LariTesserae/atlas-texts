The inhabitants of the Hollow are the **Sutured**, though they do not call themselves by any name that requires breath or a tongue. They are the authors of the "surplus of intention" that defines this place—beings whose very existence is an act of defiance against the unraveling of their world.

### The Weavers (The Sutured)
They are tall, agonizingly thin figures, resembling columns of smoked leather and sun-bleached bone. They do not have skin so much as a series of tightly wrapped, overlapping membranes that look like the cured linen the air smells of. They have no eyes; in their place are deep, horizontal slits packed with fine, golden sand that spills out in microscopic grains when they tilt their heads. They do not see the world; they feel the vibration of the mica-ferns and the thermal weight of every tile.

Their hands are their most remarkable feature. Each Weaver possesses fingers that have been elongated into needle-like shards of obsidian or polished slate. They do not walk so much as they *re-place* themselves, moving with a tectonic slowness that mirrors the shadows on the tiles. You might see one standing at a waist-high altar, its needle-fingers moving in a blurring, hypnotic flicker, threading miles of vegetable fiber through the microscopic pores of a slate slab. 

They do not eat or drink. Instead, they stand for hours in the artificial indigo shade of the roofless pillars, their chest-membranes expanding and contracting with a dry, raspy sound, absorbing the "cool" energy of the localized gravity shifts. They are the ones who lash the world together. When a wall begins to drift upward or a floor tile threatens to dissolve into non-existence, a Weaver is there, stoic and focused, sewing the physical plane back into the substrate with twine that never rots.

### The Stutter-Hounds (The Chitinous Scuttlers)
The many-jointed creatures that navigate by heat-shadows are the Weaver’s hounds and janitors. Their bodies are made of a substance harder than the terracotta—a sort of living ceramic. They have no internal organs, only a series of hollow chambers that resonate with the hum of the mica-ferns.

Their "stop-motion" movement is a biological adaptation to the fraying physics. They do not travel through space because space in the Hollow is unreliable; instead, they "blink" from one heat-gradient to another. They are the cleaners. They scuttle over the tiles, their hooked limbs picking up the stray flakes of mica or bits of snapped twine. They carry these fragments to the Weavers, who recycled them into the endless architecture. If you stand still for too long, they will gather around your feet, waiting for you to become a permanent fixture, ready to stitch your boots to the terracotta.

### The Grass-Moths (The Loom-Drifters)
The large, moth-like entities with wings of woven grass are the only things in the Hollow that seem to ignore the labor. They are the pollinators of the non-biological. Their flight is a "jagged scribble" because they are following the invisible lines of the twine that stretch into the upper atmosphere. 

They feed on the vibrations of the mica-leaves. You will see a moth descend upon a fern, its wings shivering in a frantic rhythm that matches the plant's own frequency. In this exchange, the moth sheds a fine, copper-colored dust—a mineral seed—that falls into the cracks between the tiles to begin the slow, fractal growth of a new leather-plant. Their bodies are light, almost weightless, and they are the only inhabitants that seem to feel the "fickle gravity" as a joy rather than a problem to be solved.

### The Presence in the Walls
There is a final inhabitant, one that is never seen but always felt: the **Architect of the Deep Foundations**. Below the layers of tiles and the sun-dried bricks, something vast and rhythmic pulses. The Weavers often press their "ears"—flat, calcified patches on their temples—against the floor, listening. 

The Weavers are not the masters of this place; they are its caretakers. They live in a state of perpetual, quiet emergency. They are repairing a leak in reality that never stops. Every stitch of twine and every hand-pressed tile is a bandage. They are a people of absolute labor, living in a beautiful, parched cathedral, working tirelessly to ensure that the "warm, silent dark" beneath them stays beneath them. 

When you look at a Weaver, you realize they are not just making a garden—they are holding a door shut.
