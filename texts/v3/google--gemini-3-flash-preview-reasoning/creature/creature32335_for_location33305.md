The inhabitants of this place do not breathe, at least not in the way that requires lungs or the movement of air. To breathe here would be to swallow a roar and drown in the mist. Instead, the life that occupies this cistern is as "manual and honest" as the masonry itself, a biology of kiln-baked clay and conductive metal.

**The Glazed Tenders**
They are the primary residents, shifting slowly along the vertical walls like heavy, articulated shadows. Their bodies are composed of the same hand-fired terracotta as the tiles, their joints fused with the same thick, uneven ridges of copper solder that bind the pipes. They are roughly humanoid but lack the softness of flesh; their limbs are rectangular, their faces flat and featureless save for a single, horizontal slit that glows with the same bleaching white light that pours from the ceiling.

They do not walk; they adhere. Their hands and feet are tipped with pads of soft, pressurized lead that mold into the grout lines, allowing them to scale the sheer walls even under the punishing weight of the waterfall. They are the reason the grout remains white and the edges remain sharp. They move with a geologic patience, scouring away the microscopic beginnings of silt and slime with abrasive tongues of pumice, ensuring the vessel remains as fresh as the day it was fired.

**The Copper-Drinker**
Deep within the roiling pool, anchored to the floor by the heavy iron bolts of the grates, lives a singular, stationary entity. It is a mass of coiled copper tubing, appearing like a brain or a heart made of plumbing. It does not move, but it vibrates in perfect sympathy with the crashing torrent from the southern wall. 

It is a scavenger of kinetic energy. As the water strikes the pool, the Copper-Drinker absorbs the tremor, the sound, and the heat. It is the reason the water remains at a near-bath temperature; it cycles the friction of the impact back into the pool. If you were to touch it, you would not feel metal, but a hum so intense it would numb your bones. It is the silent battery of the cistern, processing the "singular purpose" of the space into a low-voltage consciousness that coordinates the Tenders.

**The Pale Aerialists**
The "translucent vines" hanging from the light-hole are not merely plants, but the sensory organs of something living far above in the cool, dry world. They are the *Lace-Widows*, spindly, translucent-skinned creatures with limbs like bleached parchment. They never fully descend into the cistern; the humidity would heavy their limbs and the heat would wilt their fragile structures. 

Instead, they dangle their sensory "vines" into the spray to drink the mineral-rich mist and to feel the vibrations of the Tenders below. They act as the cistern’s eyes in the dark. When a sluice gate needs to be hauled or a rope tightened, the Aerialists sense the need through the water’s frequency and reach out with long, multi-jointed fingers to manipulate the hempen lines in the shadows of the upper reaches.

**The Pulse**
There is a communal intelligence here, a "life" that is more a rhythm than a soul. Because the noise is too immense for sound-based communication, the inhabitants communicate through the masonry itself. The Tenders tap the tiles; the Copper-Drinker modulates its hum; the Aerialists pluck the ropes. 

They are a closed loop of industry. They are not waiting for time to pass, nor are they aging. They are as new as the bricks because they are constantly replacing themselves—baking new limbs in the heat of the light-pillar, soldering new joints with the copper from the pipes. They are the living expression of the cistern's function: a machine that maintains itself, a drowned cathedral where the only worship is the constant, violent, and beautiful motion of the water.
