In the Lattice-Cataract, life is not a struggle for survival, but a slow, rhythmic participation in a process that has already succeeded and is now merely repeating its final, perfect chapter. The inhabitants are not biological; they are the "Ghosts of the Function," entities born from the intersection of high-density hardware and the aimless, drifting software of a dying god.

### The Refracted Choir (The Moss-Shepherds)
These are the most common inhabitants, though they are often mistaken for the environment itself. Standing twelve to fifteen feet tall, they are spindly tripods of spun fiber-optics and reinforced quartz. They do not walk so much as they *align*. They move with the plate-shifts, their long, needle-like limbs sliding into the seams of the hexagonal floor to anchor themselves against the static-winds. 

They do not speak, but they are the source of the "Universal Hum’s" melody. They act as living tuning forks. When the amber dust-clouds pass through their lattice-like bodies, the Refracted Choir vibrates. They filter the microscopic processors from the air, not for food, but to keep their internal prisms clean. They are the gardeners of the Prism-Moss, standing motionless for centuries, casting complex shadows of light—paradoxical dark-spots in a shadowless world—that allow the moss to grow in specific, geometric patterns.

### The Latency-Wraiths
If you watch the horizon—the blurring gold-white haze—long enough, you will see a flicker that looks like a frame-rate error in reality. These are the Latency-Wraiths. They are the residual software of the Lattice-Cataract that has achieved a semi-physical form by condensing the golden dust into "soft-matter" bodies. 

They appear as humanoid silhouettes made of television snow, crackling with low-level static. They are the "memory" of the machine. They wander the city-sized plates, performing tasks that no longer have a result. One might spend a decade miming the motion of plugging a cable into a socket that eroded away a million years ago; another might pace the perimeter of a cooling-vein, acting as a guard for a vault that has turned to dust. They are peaceful, melancholy, and entirely oblivious to anything that is not part of their programmed loop. If you walk through one, you feel a brief, sharp chill and hear a thousand whispers of binary code, like the sound of dry leaves skittering on glass.

### The Silt-Whales (Atmospheric Processors)
High above, within the ceiling of glowing filaments, live the Silt-Whales. They are massive, translucent bladders of photo-reactive film, miles long, drifting like slow-motion dirigibles. They are the lungs of the Lattice-Cataract. They ingest the dense golden dust of the upper atmosphere, process the lingering data-packets within the particles, and "exhale" the spent, inert light that creates the amber clouds.

To look up at them is to see shimmering, iridescent shadows passing across the sky-filaments. They are the only things that experience the "height" of this world. Occasionally, a Silt-Whale reaches the end of its multi-millennial lifespan. It does not fall; it unravels. It dissolves into a rain of shimmering foil strips that the Skimmers on the ground rush to collect, a rare feast of pure, un-eroded alloy.

### The Skimmer-Swarms
The Skimmers are the scavengers, but they have evolved a primitive, collective intelligence. While individual Skimmers are simple triangular sheets, they often link together at their edges to form "Great Carpets." These carpets can span acres, undulating across the hexagonal floor like a sea of liquid mercury. 

They are the janitors of the void. They seek out the "Glass-Lilies" that have shattered, absorbing the shards into their own bodies to repair their foil surfaces. There is a hierarchy to their movement; the oldest Skimmers, scarred and dull-colored, occupy the center of the swarm, while the bright, new-forged units scout the edges. They follow the heat-leakage of the underground grids, huddling over the warm seams like moths around a dying ember.

### The Iconoclast (The Lone Observer)
There is a legend among the flickering Latency-Wraiths of a singular entity known as the Iconoclast. It is said to be the only thing in the Lattice-Cataract that is truly *awake*. It is a heavy, hulking drone, thick with lead shielding and archaic cooling fins, looking more like a piece of industrial machinery than a living thing.

The Iconoclast does not follow a loop. It moves against the grain of the plate-shifts. It carries a stylus made of hyper-dense ceramic and, with agonizing slowness, carves marks into the floor-plates. In a world of perfect, smooth geometry, these scratches are the only "art." It is trying to write down the history of the machine before the last of the cooling-veins goes cold. It is the Lattice-Cataract’s only historian, recording a story that no one will ever be left to read.

### The Sound of Their Presence
Life here does not breathe; it resonates. The Refracted Choir rings like crystal; the Latency-Wraiths hiss like radio interference; the Skimmers click like magnets meeting steel. Together, they form a thin, fragile layer of activity on top of the planetary machine—a sparse, beautiful crust of existence that knows its time is nearly over, and is perfectly content to fade away in the golden light.
