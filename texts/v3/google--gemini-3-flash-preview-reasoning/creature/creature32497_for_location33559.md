The inhabitants of the Lithic Interstice do not breathe; they resonate. To live in the shadow of the great engine is to be a component in a closed circuit, a fragment of clockwork that has learned to reproduce. 

### The Ferrite Gleaners
The primary stratum of life is the **Ferrite Gleaner**, the "crustaceans" that carpet the silt plains. Up close, they are masterpieces of utilitarian geometry—hexagonal carapaces of polished magnetite with limbs like the fine, articulated needles of a compass. They do not have eyes; instead, they possess sensitive, vibrating antennae that map the world through the sonar of the engine’s hum. 

They are the janitors of the Interstice. They scuttle in rhythmic, overlapping patterns, their magnetic undersides dragging through the hematite dust to collect stray ions. When two Gleaners meet, they do not fight; they dock. They exchange bursts of stored static through copper-tipped mandibles—a tactile data-dump of where the strongest electrical leaks are currently located—before clicking away in a frantic, new direction.

### The Arc-Weavers
Suspended between the floor and the ceiling, stretching across the "mountainous" brass screws, are the **Arc-Weavers**. These are the spiders of the machine, though their bodies are little more than spindle-shaped resistors wrapped in silver wire. 

The Weavers do not spin silk. They spin conductive filaments of extruded lead and tin, creating webs that catch the "drift"—the ionized nitrogen currents pushed by the distant heat-sinks. They sit motionless for decades, looking like nothing more than stray wiring, until a surge of power hits their web. Then, they flare a brilliant, electric blue, drawing the energy into their bodies to gestate a single, pearl-like battery-egg.

### The Filament-Lurkers
The predators of this world, the **Wire-snappers** (or Filament-Lurkers), are terrifyingly disguised. They are long, multi-segmented entities that mimic the appearance of the copper filaments. They cling to the "Relic" citadels, their bodies segmented into imitation cooling fins. 

A Lurker does not hunt by speed, but by thermal manipulation. It absorbs the ambient heat of its surroundings, becoming a "cold spot" in the nitrogen mix. When a Gleaner or a wandering Weaver enters this pocket of absolute chill, its lubricants seize, and its gears lock. The Lurker then unfolds its many-jointed limbs—sounding like a deck of metal cards being shuffled—and consumes the victim, grinding the metal shell back into the fine silt that makes up the desert floor.

### The Capacitor-Titans
Deep within the rectangular housings of the ancient sensors live the **Capacitor-Titans**. These are the "slower-moving fauna" mentioned by the explorers of the cracks. A Titan is a massive, domed entity, its shell a thick alloy of brass and oxidation, looking like a discarded boiler. 

They are the sages of the Interstice. They do not move for centuries. Instead, they act as living batteries, anchoring the ecosystem. During the rare "Surge Events"—when the ancient engine groans and a massive bolus of electricity tears through the plates—the Titans open their dorsal vents to swallow the lightning. They then slowly bleed this power back into the ground through their rooted feet, feeding the static-flowers and the Gleaners during the long, lean decades of the engine's slumber. To the smaller scurriers, a Titan is not a creature, but a geography—a warm, vibrating hill that promises survival.

### The Spark-Moths
Finally, flitting amongst the fractal branches of the static-flowers, are the **Spark-Moths**. They are not physical beings so much as persistent ball-lightning effects, tiny spheres of glowing plasma that have achieved a form of rudimentary consciousness. 

They feed on the sulfur-scent of the flowers, dancing in the magnetic fields like dust motes in a sunbeam. They are the only things in the Interstice that seem to possess a sense of play. They zip between the legs of the Titans and spiral around the screw-mountains, their high-pitched whistling providing the treble to the engine’s bass. They are short-lived, popping into non-existence with a soft *crack* the moment their internal charge de-stabilizes, leaving behind a tiny puff of ozone.

### The Collective Soul
There is no "I" in the Lithic Interstice. Because they are all linked by the same conductive floor and the same vibrating air, the inhabitants function as a singular, distributed mind. The "mechanical chanting" is their conversation. The shifting dunes are their ledger. They are a civilization of survivors, a clockwork kingdom that has forgotten the sun, finding its own cold, perfect light in the gaps between the gears.
