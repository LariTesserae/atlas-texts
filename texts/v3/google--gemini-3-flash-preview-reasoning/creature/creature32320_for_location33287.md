In the Great Cistern, life does not evolve to survive the elements; it evolves to serve the architecture. Biology here is a form of auxiliary plumbing, a living grease that ensures the gears of the world never grind to a halt.

### The Scourers (The Micro-Sanitizers)
The reason the hexagonal floor plates remain matte-grey and free of silt is due to the **Litho-Crabs**. These are not crustaceans in the traditional sense, but flat, wide discs of calcified bone and sensory bristles, no thicker than a coin. They move in interlocking carpets across the floor, their underside equipped with abrasive pads that vibrate at ultrasonic frequencies. They do not eat organic matter so much as they consume friction. They hunt for the microscopic imperfections—the first hint of a salt crystal forming, the smallest speck of mineral deposit—and buff it away until the metal shines. When the primary currents reverse, the Litho-Crabs lock their serrated legs into the seams of the plates, becoming a seamless part of the floor until the turbulence passes.

### The Flow-Synaptics (The Schooling Silver)
The silver-scaled fish, known as **Current-Callers**, are the Cistern’s primary sensors. Their scales are not merely defensive; they are piezoelectric plates that generate a faint electrical charge as water moves over them. A school of a million Current-Callers is, in effect, a massive, swimming supercomputer. By shifting their density and formation, they can detect a failing turbine or a clogged valve miles away, long before the mechanical sensors register a drop in pressure. They move toward these malfunctions, their collective bio-electricity acting as a beacon for larger maintenance organisms. They do not have eyes; they have lateral lines so sensitive they can "see" the shape of a rivet in total darkness by the way the water curls around it.

### The Hydraulic Vipers (The Cable-Eels)
Living within the structural bracing and the dark recesses of the ceiling conduits are the **Sheath-Eels**. These creatures are indistinguishable from high-voltage cables—long, segmented, and wrapped in a dull, rubbery hide. They possess a unique form of mimicry: they don't just look like the machinery, they sound like it. They can vibrate their internal air-bladders to match the 60-hertz hum of the nearby power strips, rendering them acoustically invisible to their prey. They wait for a school of Current-Callers to pass, then lash out with a strike so fast it creates a cavitation bubble, a tiny explosion of vacuum that collapses with enough force to shatter the silver scales of their target.

### The Piston-Goliaths (The Filter-Feeders)
In the vast "negative space" between the pillars, the giants roam. These are the **Bellows-Whales**, translucent, gelatinous masses the size of freight ships. They are almost entirely hollow. They do not swim by moving fins; they function like living ramjets. They take in thousands of gallons of water through a gaping forward maw and expel it through a muscular rear siphon, mimicking the action of the very pumps that drive the world. Their bodies are reinforced with a flexible, carbon-fiber-like lattice that prevents them from being crushed by the immense pressure. They filter the "nutrient slurry" through a series of internal grates that resemble industrial sieves. A Bellows-Whale following a current line looks like a ghost ship—a pale, flickering shape illuminated by the rhythmic pulse of the conduit lights.

### The Valve-Keepers (The Custodians)
The most unsettling inhabitants are the **Manifold-Spiders**. These are multi-limbed, pale organisms that live exclusively on the intake grates and pressure-regulating valves. Their limbs are long, spindly, and tipped with specialized tools—some have hooked claws for clearing debris, others have flat, paddle-like appendages for scraping algae. They are the only creatures in the Cistern that seem to possess a rudimentary intelligence. They have been observed "tuning" the vibrations of the grates, plucking at the metal bars like harp strings to alter the flow of water. They do not eat the fish; they eat the waste that the machine rejects. They are the ultimate janitors, obsessed with the purity of the flow.

### The Ghost-Algae (The Kinetic Flora)
Even the "vegetation" is mechanical in its behavior. The **Vibro-Moss** that clings to the grates does not photosynthesize. Instead, it converts the constant percussive roar of the turbines into chemical energy. Its translucent filaments are tuned to specific frequencies; when the turbines reach peak RPM, the moss glows with a faint, bioluminescent blue, pulsing in time with the heartbeat of the world. It grows in geometric patterns—triangles and grids—following the electromagnetic leakages of the pipes it inhabits.

In the Great Cistern, there is no "wild." There is only the functional and the non-functional. To be alive is to be a part, a gear, or a lubricant. The inhabitants are not residents of a world; they are the crew of a ship that has forgotten it is traveling, dedicated to the eternal, roaring maintenance of a machine that never ends.
