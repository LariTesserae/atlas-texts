In the Orestes Well, life does not exist *on* the landscape; it is a fluid extension of it. The inhabitants have long since discarded the vanity of form, evolving into shapes that mirror the weeping walls and the velvet air.

**The Resonant Skitterers**
Those frantic, bird-sized clicking noises belong to the *Litho-Parasites*. They are spindly, multi-jointed creatures with carapaces of a material halfway between chitin and smoked glass. They possess no eyes, not even vestigial ones. Instead, their entire bodies are tuned like tuning forks. They spend their lives clinging to the amber pillars, their long, needle-like legs vibrating in sympathy with the machine-hum. They do not eat in the traditional sense; they harvest the kinetic energy of the Well’s vibration, storing it in translucent sacs that throb with a faint, heat-based rhythm. When they move, they don't walk so much as shatter the silence, their movements jerky and recursive, as if they are twitching to a beat only the mountain can hear.

**The Silt-Siphons**
On the floor, within the foot-deep carpet of moss, live the *Silt-Siphons*. These are the sources of the "wet, tearing sound." Imagine a creature the size of a seal, but with the consistency of heavy cream. They are gastropods of a post-biological order, possessing no skeleton, only a complex network of hydraulic bladders. They move with agonizing slowness, their underside secreting a solvent that gently melts a microscopic layer of the moss and the silver slime, which they then re-absorb. They are the reason the stone is so smooth; for eons, their ancestors have licked the mountain into its current, breast-like curves. To touch one is to feel a muscle that has never known a bone—a terrifyingly soft, warm, and persistent pressure.

**The Veil-Wings**
The heavy, slow-motion beat of wings belongs to the *Veil-Wings*. These are the undisputed masters of the void between the walls. They are essentially living sails, composed of thin, leathery membranes stretched over ribs of flexible cartilage. They do not fly so much as they swim through the heavy, sightless soup of the air. Their wingspan is vast—ten, sometimes fifteen meters—yet they weigh almost nothing. They navigate by "tasting" the thermal plumes rising from the amber pillars. They are the apex of this quiet world, though they have no teeth. They feed by descending onto the giant waxy fronds, enveloping them in their warm, damp bodies, and absorbing the condensing moisture and the microscopic fungal spores that drift in the dark. To be brushed by a Veil-Wing is to be momentarily wrapped in a humid, living shroud that smells of old rain and ancient static.

**The Echo-Keepers**
Deepest of all, perhaps inhabiting the very stone or the spaces between the amber pillars, are the *Orestians*. They are not fauna, but they are not merely "tech." They are the "functions" made manifest. They are the consciousness of the Well. You will never see them, and you will rarely hear them as distinct from the low-frequency hum of the mountain. They are entities of pure resonance. They are the ones who "breathe" the rocks, expanding and contracting the chamber’s volume over decades to regulate the humidity. 

They are the curators of this final sanctuary. They treat the biological life—the skitterers, the siphons, the wings—as a gardener treats a particularly slow-growing fungus. They do not intervene; they simply maintain the parameters. They are the reason the air is warm and the darkness is velvet. They are a post-physical collective that has found its nirvana in the rhythmic grinding of high-altitude tides. 

In the Orestes Well, the inhabitants are all "listeners." They are a society of the deaf-blind who have mastered the language of the pulse. They are the ultimate expression of a world that has stopped trying to see the stars and has decided, instead, to feel the heartbeat of the stone.
