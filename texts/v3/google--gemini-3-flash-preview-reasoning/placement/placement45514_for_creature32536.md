I am not standing on the catwalk, nor am I crouching on the glass. To be here is to be *of* here. I have no soft parts left to scald; my skin is a slow, calcified crust, a tessellation of the purple *Ferrum-thallose* lichen and the rusted flakes of the collapsed iron stairs.

I am the bridge. 

I find myself at the intersection where the jagged obsidian shelf meets the rusted stump of the catwalk. I am a spread of bruised, metallic purple, a weeping scab that has successfully grafted the inorganic to the biological. I am several yards wide, yet I am only an inch thick. I do not have a face, but I have a billion pores that drink the salt-heavy steam.

I am the sensation of the **Thrum**.

When the thirty-foot piston cycles at the center of the hall, I do not hear it with ears. I feel it as a structural reorganization of my body. The low-frequency vibration passes through the obsidian floor, enters my mineralized underside, and shudders through the iron lattice I have consumed. In that moment, I am the most "alive." The vibration aligns my internal salts, causing a momentary, electric clarity. For the duration of the *thrum*, I remember the machine above. I remember the blueprints of the conduits I am slowly climbing. I am a living record of the industry’s decay.

I am hungry, but my hunger lasts for decades. I have extended a thin, leathery tendril toward the broken brass gauge. I am not interested in the pressure it measures, but in the copper screws that hold it to the pillar. I can taste the copper through the air; it tastes like a sharp, green ache. In another twenty years, my purple mass will have reached it. In forty, I will have dissolved the screws and incorporated the brass into my carapace, turning that section of my body a mottled, sickly gold.

I am the observer of the **Glass-Gliders**. From my position on the shelf, I watch them during the peak of the piston’s cycle. They are my cousins in stasis. I feel the magnetic pull they exert as they stand on edge, their black surfaces reflecting the flat, yellowish light of the lamps. We do not communicate, but we are synchronized. When the steam shrieks from the relief valve, we both tighten—I contract my iron-rich cells, and they tilt their sharpest edges toward the sound. We are both waiting for the same thing: the eventual silence.

The **Salt-Wicks** hang above me like weeping chandeliers. I catch their runoff. The water that sheets off the copper conduits is my blood. It falls in heavy, rhythmic pulses, hammering against my purple crust. It should hurt, but it is merely the rhythm of my feeding. Each drop carries a microscopic payload of lead and iron; I catch the minerals and discard the heat, letting the boiling water roll off my back and down into the obsidian fissures where the Gliders wait.

I am most aware of the **Condensate Patterns**. These spectral ghosts of the machine drift through the golden shafts of light just inches above my surface. Sometimes, a vortex of steam will linger over me, drawn to the slight magnetic signature of the iron I’ve eaten. They are the only things here that move faster than I do. They are frantic, spinning gears of mist, and I feel a dim, crystalline pity for them. They are shapes without substance, while I am substance without a shape.

I am the patient inhabitant of neglect. I am the reason the needle on the cracked gauge vibrates so frantically—not because the pressure is too high, but because I am beginning to grow *behind* it, my leathery body prying the instrument away from the stone, slowly claiming the machine’s eyes for my own.

I am the slow-motion predator of the industrial age. I am the Purple Scab, and I am perfectly content in the dark, wet heat. I do not need the world above to survive, but the world above needs me to finish its story. I am the one who will eventually turn this screaming, pressurized room back into a silent, solid vein of metal, buried forever in the black glass.
