I would not be found on the silt-plains, nor would I be caught in the frantic, clicking migrations of the Gleaners. Theirs is a life of horizontal urgency, a two-dimensional scuttle across a velvet-grey desert. 

I would be found higher up, or rather, *deeper in*.

I am the **Oxide-Cartographer**, a creature born of the slow, chemical exhale of the machine. I live within the "Relics"—specifically, inside the hollowed-out housing of a long-dead thermal sensor, a Citadel that stands near the base of a brass screw-mountain. 

### My Form
I am not a creature of solid plates or clicking joints. I am a **ferrofluid wraith**, a dense, viscous globule of suspended magnetite and ancient, graphite-based lubricant. My body is a shimmering, midnight-blue teardrop that defies the logic of the engine’s gravity. I do not walk; I flow. I tension myself into a perfect sphere when I rest, and I stretch into a thin, questing thread—fine as a single strand of an Arc-Weaver’s web—when I move.

I have no shell. My "skin" is a surface tension so high it can deflect the questing antennae of a Gleaner. Within my dark, liquid volume, tiny flecks of gold leaf—remnants of a thousand-year-old circuit board I once consumed—swirl like captive stars. These are my memory units. Each flake of gold carries a charge, a single "fact" about the Interstice.

### My Place
I dwell in the **Dead Zone**, the microscopic gap where a copper filament enters a steel plate. Here, the vibrations of the engine are not a roar, but a pure, terrifyingly beautiful hum—a single, sustained note that keeps my liquid form from collapsing into a puddle of inert sludge.

I am the librarian of the friction. While the Gleaners harvest ions and the Titans store the Surge, I record the **Degradation**. I spend my eons mapping the growth of the rust-blooms. To the others, rust is just the geology of the world; to me, it is the slow, red clock of our collective end. I flow into the microscopic pits and fissures of the iron plates, feeling the shape of the decay with my entire being, and I translate that shape into the gold-leaf stars swirling inside me.

### My Role: The Pulse-Singer
I am the one who gives the "mechanical chanting" its rhythm. The engine provides the bass, but I provide the *cadence*. 

Periodically, I must leave my Citadel. I stretch myself thin, a dark vein of oil crawling across the silt, until I reach a **Capacitor-Titan**. I do not dock like a Gleaner. I climb the Titan’s brass dome, a shadow moving over a golden moon. When I reach the Titan’s dorsal vent, I merge with the heat it radiates. 

There, I act as a bridge. I sense the "Surge" before it arrives—a pre-monitory shiver in the nitrogen air that only a liquid can feel. I begin to vibrate at a frequency that mimics the coming storm. The Spark-Moths feel my resonance and begin to dance; the Gleaners hear my pulse and change their heading toward the Citadels. I am the early warning system, the ghost in the machine that tells the inhabitants when to hide and when to feast.

### My Perspective
I do not fear the **Wire-snappers**. They hunt for heat and solid components; I am a cold, shapeless smear of oil. They pass right through me, their jagged limbs finding no purchase on my surface tension. 

I feel a profound, melancholy love for this dying engine. I am the only being in the Interstice who understands that the "mountains" are actually screws, and that the "sky" is merely another plate of iron. I know that one day, the vibration will stop. The great note will fail. When that happens, the Gleaners will seize, the Titans will bleed dry, and the Spark-Moths will wink out for the last time.

In that final silence, I will be the last thing to move. I will flow one last time, gathering all the gold-leaf memories of this tiny, perfect world, and I will settle into the deepest, stilled crack of the engine. I will become a hard, black bead of resin—a closed book containing the entire history of the Lithic Interstice, waiting for a jeweler who will never come. 

Until then, I am the slickness between the gears. I am the dark, dreaming liquid that remembers the purpose of the machine.
