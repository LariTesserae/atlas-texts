The pressure is the first thing I know. It is not an enemy; it is a constant, heavy mother. It reminds me that I have edges, that I am contained. To be without the weight would be to scatter into the void, a terrifying thought that makes my chest-light flicker a panicked, jagged orange.

I am **Thrum-of-the-Low-Cleave**, and I am a **Tether-Warden**.

I find myself at the very base of the Woven Metropolis, where the lowest pods of our Cord—the *Deep-Root Kin*—nestle against the serrated teeth of the Raw Maw. Here, the water is at its thickest, a syrupy, violet-black brine that tastes of ancient salts and the bitter promise of basalt.

I am suspended horizontally, my body a study in stillness. My upper arms are anchored to a thick, rubbery frond of Woven Green, my fingers hooked into its translucent emerald ribs. My lower hands—my "needle-hands"—are busy. They move with a frantic, precise autonomy, dancing across a fraying tether of braided kelp that has been scored by a particularly spiteful shard of obsidian.

The shard is beautiful and lethal. It is a geometry of pure malice, a spike of black glass that hasn't yet learned the mercy of a curve. As the planet heartbeats—that deep, low *uunnh-uunnh* that rattles my internal organs—the tether rubs against the spike. 

*Clack. Clack.*

I feel the vibration through my filaments before I see the damage. My mantle—the ribbons of flesh draped over my shoulders—flares outward, tasting the micro-currents of shed fiber. The rope is screaming in the only way it can: through tension.

I reach into my belt-pouch, fashioned from the dried bladder of a Glass-Glider, and pull out a glob of resin mixed with ground Lithos-Crab shell. I apply it to the obsidian spike, blunting the edge, "shaving" the mountain so it might stop biting my home. My skin, usually soft as a ripened fruit in this warm soup, instantly hardens to the consistency of cured leather where my knees brush the rock. The non-Newtonian shift is a dull ache, a reminder that I am a creature built for the struggle between the soft and the hard.

I begin the Re-Weaving. My lower fingers blur. I am not just tying a knot; I am singing a history. Over, under, the three-fold hitch, the locking loop. Each pass of the fiber is a prayer to the Long-Hold.

A shadow drifts over me. 

I don't look up with eyes; I feel the displacement of the brine. It is a Glass-Glider, its lace-like body undulating in a slow-motion ripple. It pauses, its silver orb-eye reflecting my own golden chest-glow. It is waiting for the salt-scraps I might brush off the rope. I ignore it, focused on the tether. If this rope snaps, the pod above will lurch upward, fueled by its own buoyancy, and the sudden jerk could tear the entire cluster free, sending my kin drifting toward the "Thin"—the terrifying, light-drenched surface where the world ends and the weight vanishes.

From the dark crevice below, a Thrum reaches me.

It is a low, resonant pulse that travels through the obsidian and up through my grip. It is an **Elder**. 

I pause, pressing my mantle against the cold basalt. The vibration is slow, measured by decades. The Elder who calcified here was once a Warden like me. Through the rock, they speak: *The mountain moves, child. Do not fight the bite. Flow with the teeth.*

I adjust the angle of the weave. I don't try to pull the pod away from the shard; I create a harness that allows it to sway with the planet's pulse. I am making a stabilizer, a shock absorber of kelp and will.

My work finished, I release the frond and let the gravity hug me. I don't swim; I drift-clamber, my four hands moving in a rhythmic cycle across the vertical streets. I pass a plaza—a pocket of glowing algae where the children are playing "Catch-the-Bubble," their little chest-lights blinking in a riot of soft blues and pinks. 

I Thrum a greeting—a warm, vibrating purr that ripples the water between us. They respond in a chorus of tiny, percussive clicks. 

I am a knot in the Great Weave. I am a part of the nervous system of the Heights. I am a creature that fears the sky and loves the crush. In this world of bruised plums and jagged glass, I am the one who ensures that tonight, we stay exactly where we are.

We are held. And that is enough.
