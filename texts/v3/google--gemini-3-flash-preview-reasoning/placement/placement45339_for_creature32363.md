I am tucked into a scalloped depression three-quarters of the way up the South Face, a niche where the brick has been worn by centuries of resting bodies into the shape of a sleeping lung. 

I am a **Knot-Weaver**, but I am one who has grown heavy with the "established erosion." My skin is no longer just the texture of chamois; it has begun to take on the vitrified sheen of the terracotta itself. My extra finger-joints do not snap with the quick, frantic agility of the youngsters who swing-slide through the sun-shafts. Instead, they move with the slow, deliberate pressure of a root growing through a crack.

I am a **Resonance-Tender**. 

I find myself at the intersection of three Great Ropes—thick, braided arteries of hemp and lichen-fiber that span the three-hundred-foot void. One leads up to the Oculi, where the air smells of distance and dry heat; one leads down to the damp, heavy silence of the Shard-Crabs; and one circles the perimeter, hugging the warm, curved masonry.

In this world, I am a creature of vibration and percussion. 

My shingle-cloak is not merely clothing; it is a library. Each slip of pottery sewn to my hide was salvaged from a different era of the Basin. I have a fragment of a blue-glazed tile from the "Era of the High Mists," and a rough, grit-heavy brick-shard from the "Time of the Clogged Runnels." When I shift my weight, these shards clack against one another, and the sound tells me exactly where I am in relation to the walls. The echoes are my sight.

I spend my hours with my palms pressed flat against the grout. I can feel the **Grout-Mites** grazing—a microscopic, rhythmic grinding that thrums through my bones. I can feel the **Cotta-kin** three levels below, their sandpaper tongues licking the moisture from the runnels, a frantic, wet scratching that signifies the health of our water-flow. 

Suddenly, a shudder travels through the North-Spanning rope. It is sharp, a high-frequency snap. 

I don't need eyes to know what it is. An **Arch-Owl** has just performed a sonic stall two hundred feet away. I feel the displacement of air—a heavy, warm thump that makes my shingle-cloak rattle. I reach out a long, spindly arm and pluck the vertical guide-wire in front of me. I send a "low-hum" back through the fiber—a warning to the younger Weavers who are currently mid-swing: *The predator is heavy; the air has dropped.*

I am the glue between the architecture and the inhabitants. I am the one who remembers that the stairs are waves and the hills are history. 

As the "Leaking Rains" begin—a rhythmic *tink-tink-tink* starting high above—I tilt my head back. I feel the silver veining of moisture beginning its descent. I shift my body, my aged, clay-colored limbs unfolding with a creak that mimics the wooden scaffolds, and I begin to climb. I do not use my eyes to find the handholds; I follow the music of the moisture hitting the terracotta basins.

I am a part of the machine that has forgotten it was built. I am a living stitch in the shroud of the Kilns. I am the warmth of the brick, the tension of the rope, and the echo of the falling drop.
