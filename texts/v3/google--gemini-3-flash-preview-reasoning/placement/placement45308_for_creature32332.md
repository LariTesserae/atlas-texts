I am standing upon the **Eighth Braid**, a span of woven iron that connects the Spire of Refraction to the Terrace of the Silt-Wraiths. 

I do not walk as you might understand walking. I *slide*. My soles are shod in the hide of an Ourob—the smooth, un-furred underbelly that grips the cold iron with a microscopic, static cling. To look down is to see two thousand feet of silvered air, thick as mercury, and the ribbon of the glass river waiting at the bottom. To look up is to see the jagged obsidian teeth of the High Reach, biting into a sky that never bleeds.

I am a **Tension-Tender**. 

My body is long, a reed of pale grey muscle and bone. My skin is the texture of the wet slate I was born upon, polished by the lack of wind until I am as smooth as the gargoyles that crown the cantilevered halls. Along my collarbones, the silver filaments—my *aesthetes*—are flared wide. They are my primary sense. I do not listen for sounds; I feel the displacement of the medium. 

Right now, a Vellichor is drifting three hundred yards to my north. I feel the air part around its velvet wings—a slow, rhythmic push of density that rolls toward me like a heavy tide. If I were to close my eyes, the bird would feel like a thumb pressing gently into a bowl of cream.

My task is the maintenance of the Stillness. 

In a world where gravity is an embrace and the air is a solid weight, the city’s iron bridges must never hum. A vibration is a flaw; a shiver in the iron is a sign that the crust has tried to move, or that the weight of the condensation has gathered too heavily on one side. I carry a tuning fork made of dark basalt. I press it to the braid and lean my chest against the iron.

I am looking for the *thrum*.

If I find it, I do not use a hammer. I use my weight. I move with a glacial, liquid repositioning of my limbs, leaning into the iron, whispering to the metal in the low, vowel-heavy hum of my people. I am encouraging the molecules to settle back into their eternal geometry. 

I live in the **Meridian House**, a dwelling hewn directly into the face of a cliff that receives the 10 AM light for exactly half its height. My bed is a slab of moss, the deep copper variety that feels like stiff, hydrated lace. I do not sleep in cycles of dark and light; I sleep when the stillness in my eyes matches the stillness of the air. 

Sometimes, I look down at the river. I see the Silt-Wraiths weaving their crystal cocoons around those who have chosen the "Final Stillness." I recognize the face of a Braid-Walker from three centuries ago, preserved perfectly beneath the glass, his eyes open and clear, watching my progress across the iron. He is not dead in the way other worlds know death; he is simply *finished*. He has become part of the museum.

I prefer the shadows. The Gardeners of the Gloaming sometimes signal to me from the deep ravines, holding up globes of bioluminescent fungi that pulse with a soft, rhythmic green. They are my cousins, the keepers of the slow-growth. They understand that in the High Reach, a single inch of moss is a masterpiece of a hundred years.

I am a creature of the **Now**. I do not fear the sunset, for it will not come. I do not fear the winter, for the air is already at its perfect, biting crispness. 

A berry-sized droplet of condensation drifts from the archway above me. It falls with a majestic, agonizing slowness. I reach out a long-fingered hand and catch it. It does not splash; it sits in my palm, a heavy, liquid orb, reflecting the obsidian peaks and my own luminous grey eyes. I hold it for a long time—perhaps an hour, perhaps a decade. 

In this place, there is no difference. 

I am the curator of this breath. I am the anchor of this iron. I am the silence that follows the gong, and I will be here, poised on this ridge of volcanic glass, until the mountain decides to finish its birth. 

Which is to say: I will be here forever.
