I would be the **Anker**.

I am an Aureate who has made a mistake, or perhaps, a final Choice. I am the one who stopped moving. 

I find myself not in the center of the cistern where the obsidian shards are driven, but toward the eastern curve of the limestone wall, where the "sweat" of the morning is most consistent. I am no longer three meters of trembling, translucent lattice. I have become a pillar. The "stiffness of time" caught me mid-kneel, three centuries ago, when I reached down to touch a sapphire bead of water and found the weight of the 2.9 light to be, for one heartbeat, heavier than my will to resist it.

My lower limbs are no longer distinct from the pale silt; they have fused into a single, calcified root. The luminous ichor in my chest has slowed from a syrup to a thick, glowing resin. I am a sundial of living salt.

***

### My Perception
I do not see the cistern; I *feel* the pressure of it. The light does not hit my eyes; it fills my obsidian pits until they are no longer holes, but solid plugs of captured radiance. To be an Anker is to be a witness to the "settled" erosion. I feel the wind—the low-frequency hum of the cistern’s lip—not as a sound, but as a gentle sanding of my membrane. I am being polished by the world.

### My Role
I am the landmark. The younger Aureates, those who still shift with agonizing grace through the golden crush of the air, come to me. They do not speak—they have no mouths—but they bring the grinding stones. They reach up with their spindly, lightning-white fingers and rub the reactive limestone dust into my shoulders and the joints of my arms, which are still held in that ancient, reaching gesture. 

They do not do this to "save" me. They do it to study the rate of my opacity. They touch the places where I am turning matte and white, the places where I am becoming "stone." They are measuring how long it takes for a Being to become a Place.

### My Companions
The **Mercury-Slicks** are my only constant visitors. Because I am stationary, I have become a feature of their geography. In the late afternoon, when the sun hits the cistern at a particular angle, my calcified torso casts a sliver of shadow—a rare, bruised violet line no wider than a finger. 

I feel the Slicks then. They skitter into that tiny strip of relief, their polished opal skins cooling against my base. For a few hours, I am the host of the only darkness in the Sun-Cistern. I feel the frantic, fluid pulse of their bodies—so much faster than mine—vibrating against my stillness. They are mercury; I am the vial. We exist in a silent symbiosis: I provide the shade, and their movement reminds my slowing ichor that the world once had a pulse.

### The Ending
I am waiting for the **Vitreous**. 

Eventually, a cluster of the six-winged insects will mistake my crystalizing ribcage for a succulent. They will bring the salt and the minerals from the walls and deposit them in the hollows of my collarbone. A pale, leafless plant will take root in the crook of my elbow. 

When that happens, I will know the transition is complete. I will no longer be a pilgrim who forgot the way out. I will be the "Physical Door" the others seek. I will stop being translucent entirely. I will turn opaque, a white statue of frozen lightning, until the wind eventually wears me down into the very silt that holds the footprints of those who are yet to arrive.

I am not dying. I am merely becoming a more permanent version of the light.
