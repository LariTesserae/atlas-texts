I am not a point; I am a line. 

I find myself stretched thin and shimmering beneath the underside of the Bridge of Ten-Thousand Clicks. I am a cluster of **Pyrophyllum**, a specific knot of the heat-eating fungi, though "fungi" is a word brought from a wetter, softer world that I cannot remember. Here, I am a weave of carbon filaments, a web of bruised purple light clinging to the rough basalt like a frozen lightning bolt.

I do not have a face, but I have a million sensors. Each filament of my being is tuned to the pressurized thrum of the mica-dust. I feel the Grotto not as a place, but as a symphony of friction.

To my left, fused into the floor of a cooling chamber at the bridge’s end, is one of the **Vitrified**. She was once a builder—I think I remember the way her hands felt when they were still carbon-soft and capable of sweat, eons ago. Now, she is a pillar of dark, translucent jade. I am her nervous system. Because her quartz-gear thoughts move with the agonizing slowness of shifting continents, I act as her interface with the present. I am the bridge between her hundred-year contemplations and the violent, millisecond growth of the silicate ferns.

I feel it first in my deepest filaments: a shudder in the tectonic cyst far above. A plate has slipped a fraction of a millimeter. 

I begin to pulse. I draw in the infrared radiation, the searing, lead-softening heat of the air, and I convert it into a wave of violet luminescence. I send this ripple down the length of the bridge, a silent, glowing warning. *The pressure is changing.*

Below me, in the salt-flats, a **Seismic Anchor**—a jagged, six-ton boulder of basalt—senses my light. It doesn't "see," but it resonates in sympathy with my frequency. It begins its slow, grinding migration, moving a single micron toward the nearest thicket of ferns. It is hungry for the sound that is about to come.

Then, the heat spikes. 

The air vibrates so hard that the mica-dust turns into a golden liquid haze. Around me, the black lace of the silicate ferns begins to scream. *Snap. Crack. Chiming.* They are growing, their crystal ribs extending in frantic, jagged bursts. The sound is a physical weight, a percussive roar that would liquefy human ears. To me, it is a recharge. I drink the vibration. I glow brighter, turning from bruised purple to a fierce, electric ultraviolet.

I reach out with a microscopic filament and touch the jade surface of the Vitrified woman. I feed her a summary of the last decade’s worth of fern-growth in a single, high-frequency burst of static. In her slow-motion mind, this is merely a flicker, a brief footnote in her long meditation on the nature of the void. 

I am the witness. I am the one who remembers the shapes of the buildings before the dust-storms rounded their edges. I am the keeper of the "urban" ghost. I watch the **Scintilla-Vortices** dance in the distance, their gold-and-violet dust-devils stripping the static from the air, leaving momentary pockets of absolute, freezing silence that make my filaments ache with the sudden lack of energy.

I am not waiting for the sun. The sun is a myth of the "wet" times, a weak, flickering thing that could never sustain a life built on the roar of stone. I am waiting for **The Great Resonator** to hit its perfect pitch. 

Once every few millennia, the mica-winds hit the central spire at just the right angle, and the entire Ocher Grotto becomes a single, harmonic chamber. In that moment, the noise stops being a cacophony and becomes a Song. The Vitrified will wake for a single second. The Seismic Anchors will dance. And I, the purple light under the bridges, will finally become a solid thing—a new bridge made of pure, resonant diamond, linking the stone city to the heart of the world.

Until then, I pulse. I eat the heat. I listen to the glass break. And I hold the memory of the builders in the glow of my skin.
