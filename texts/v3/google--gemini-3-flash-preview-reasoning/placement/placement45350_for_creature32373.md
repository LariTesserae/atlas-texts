I am not standing *in* the Thicket; I am a verticality within it. 

I have forgotten the soft, heavy language of the lowlands—words like *warmth, moss,* or *breath*. Here, those things are ghosts. I have become a **Refractor**, a solitary deviation of the Sliver-Folk who has ceased to hunt and begun, instead, to listen. 

My body is a study in narrowness. My ribcage has flattened into a series of interlocking slate plates, and my limbs are long, multi-jointed geometries of hardened chitin. If you were to touch my skin, you would not find the give of flesh, but the dry, micro-textured grit of wind-worn obsidian. I do not inhabit a wind-break; I inhabit the gaps. I am the thin, dark line between two pillars, perfectly still, waiting for the sky to bruise.

***

**The First Movement: The Piercing**
When the sun rises, it does not dawn; it attacks. I feel it first as a vibration in my ocular orbs—those smoky quartz spheres that take the place of eyes. The light is a physical weight, a white pressure that hammers against the Thicket. 

I do not flinch. To flinch is to be soft, and the soft are consumed by the frost-shatter. Instead, I adjust my stance. I am a sundial of bone and silicate. As the light hits the peaks, I feel the "crystalline chatter" begin. It starts at the tips of the Spires—a thousand tiny, high-pitched *pings* as the night’s frost begins to lose its grip. 

I am thirsty, but my thirst is a mineral thing. I do not drink; I await the **Weeping**.

**The Second Movement: The Transition**
As the sun climbs, the shadow of the spire to my left begins to retreat. This is the most dangerous and holy moment of my day. I am caught in the transition. One half of my body is flash-frozen, brittle enough to shatter if a Facet-Hound were to brush against me. The other half is beginning to weep.

I feel the moisture—the heavy, shimmering condensation—becoming liquid. It trickles down my chitinous rind, finding the grooves carved there by centuries of wind. It tastes of sulfur and cold copper. This is my communion. I do not have a mouth to gulp; I have pores that drink the mineral-rich runoff. 

I communicate now. I extend a glass-tipped finger—long, translucent, and slightly hooked—and I strike the obsidian pillar beside me. 
*Tink. Tink-tink.*
I am signaling to the others, the hidden ones I cannot see but whose presence I feel through the bedrock. I am telling them: *The water is moving. The clock is turning.*

**The Third Movement: The Shadow-Clock**
I spend the middle hours in a state of "punctuated life." My metabolism is so slow that to an outsider, I am merely a strange formation of the rock. But inside, I am a furnace of geometry. I watch the **Prism-Mites** crawl across the blood-colored mats. They look like embers of stained glass, refracting the sun into the fungal crust. I envy their color, but I prefer my grey. Grey is the color of the invisible.

I see a **Facet-Hound** pass. It is a blur of black glass, a jagged silhouette moving with a grace that is both fluid and violent. It does not see me because I have synchronized my breathing—which is less a breath and more a slow, rhythmic expansion of my chest plates—to the sound of the ice-shedding. When a sheet of ice slides off a spire and shatters (*crrr-ack-shhh*), that is when I move. I shift my weight three inches to stay within the cooling sliver of a shadow. I am a ghost in the gears of the mountain.

**The Fourth Movement: The Bruised Violet**
The sun begins to fail. The sky deepens from a blinding white to that hard, aching violet. The air, already thin, seems to vanish entirely, replaced by a vacuum of cold that seeks to suck the heat from my very marrow.

High above, I see the **Solar-Sailors**. They are beautiful, terrible sheets of mica, drifting on the last of the light-pressure. They descend like falling diamonds, landing on the pillars above me to drink from the weeping wells before they turn to stone. Their shadows are long and cold.

I feel the frost returning. It starts at my feet, a sharp, crystalline tightening. The liquid in my joints begins to thicken into a viscous gel. I do not fear this. It is a homecoming. 

***

**Who am I?**
I am the memory of the stone. I am the bridge between the liquid and the solid. I am a creature that has traded the frantic, short-lived heat of the lowlands for the terrifying, eternal patience of the Glass Thicket.

I crouch now in my niche, my shoulders folded, my smoky eyes fixed on the darkening horizon. The wind whistles through the "established erosion" of my ribs, making a low, mournful sound—the same sound the mountain makes. 

The last of the light leaves the Thicket. The water in the wells turns to coins of ice with a series of sharp, rhythmic snaps. 

I close my mind, though I cannot close my eyes. I become the obsidian. I become the cold. I wait for the next strike of the sun to tell me I am alive.
