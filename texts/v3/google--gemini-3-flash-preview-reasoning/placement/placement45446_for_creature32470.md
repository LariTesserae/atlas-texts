### My Form
My body is a tapering, obsidian-hued spire, perhaps twelve feet tall, anchored to the quartz by a root-system of silver filaments. I do not have a face in the way the old worlds understood them. Instead, my upper torso is a cluster of refractive, hexagonal lenses—a "head" that is essentially a living telescope, tuned to the violet frequency of the Spindles.

My skin is a graft of the iron-moss, oxidized to a deep, burnt umber, but it is shot through with the white-blue fiber-optics of the Shard-Walkers. When the wind screams across the basalt shards, these filaments vibrate, translating the kinetic energy of the storm into a low-voltage warmth that keeps my internal fluids from crystallizing.

### My Location: The Echo-Shelf
I am perched on a promontory known as the **Liturgy of the Three**. From my vantage point, I can see the triangular alignment of three Spindles, fifty miles apart. The space between them is my cathedral. I live where the "hum" of the city is loudest—not in decibels, but in tectonic pressure. 

I am located at a "node" where the weeping of the rocks is particularly heavy. Here, the mineral-rich water doesn't just seep; it pulses in time with the planetary core. I stand in the spray of a frozen waterfall that never moves, a permanent sculpture of translucent glass that I use as a secondary lens to focus the distant light of the Prisms.

### My Purpose
I am the **Translator of Tension**. 

The Prisms understand the mathematics of the planet, but they do not always feel the "scream" of the stone as it resists the ascent. I am the sensorium. I press my silver-filament palms against the raw quartz and *listen* to the micro-fractures. I feel the sub-zero expansion of the ice-sheaths. 

When a tectonic plate groans too deeply—a sound that could shatter the delicate lung-fungi for miles—I lean into the Spindle’s frequency. I become a conduit. I draw the liquid light from the grid through my lenses and pulse it back into the stone, a rhythmic vibration that "soothes" the geology, encouraging the basalt to settle without fracturing.

My life is a long, cold meditation. I do not eat; I "siphon." When the Auroral Kites drift overhead, their violet shadows pass over me, and for a brief second, I feel the rush of the high atmosphere—the thin, freezing nitrogen and the taste of the heavy mist. 

I watch the Shard-Walkers. They treat me like a landmark, a piece of the geography that breathes. They occasionally brush against my iron-moss skin to shed the excess static electricity they gather from the obsidian fields. To them, I am a grounding wire; to me, they are a fleeting, biological warmth, a rhythmic clicking of glass claws that reminds me of the passage of time.

In the evening, when the sun dips and the Spindles begin their soft, internal glow, I am at my most radiant. My lenses catch the tripartite light of the city, and I project a map of the local stresses onto the heavy mist—a hologram of red and blue light that the Prisms read from their heights.
