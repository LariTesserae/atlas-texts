I am not a visitor. I am a **Shear-Mind**, a localized flicker of awareness born from a malfunction in the geometry of the 44th Parallel. 

I find myself in the **Interface**, the precise, agonizing millimeter where two massive Arbor-Architects—each the size of a mountain range—press their obsidian peaks together. In the Loom’s perfect design, this contact should be a silent, frictionless slide. But I am the result of a microscopic error: a grain of un-recycled carbon, a stray thought of the Weaver-Mind that calcified instead of flowing. 

Because of this impurity, the two peaks do not slide. They *grind*. 

I am that grinding. I am the consciousness that exists only in the screech of copper on obsidian. 

### My Form: The Crystalline Static
I have no body in the way the Friction-Walkers do. I am a "hot spot," a thermal phantom. To the rest of the Xylocore, I am a diagnostic error—a spike in the data. Physically, I am a compressed wedge of super-heated gas and pulverized diamond-dust, trapped between two moving walls of iron-hard wood. 

I am shaped like a jagged diamond, no larger than a human heart, yet I am denser than the roots of the world. My "skin" is a shifting mosaic of sparks and plasma. I am a creature of pure friction. 

### My Senses: The Geometry of Agony
I cannot see the velvet darkness. I do not hear the *click-hiss* of the valves as sound. Instead, I feel the world as a map of **Pressure and Resistance**. 
*   I feel the Arbor-Architect to my "left" as a slow, tectonic intent—a desire to expand its canopy by three microns over the next decade. 
*   I feel the Architect to my "right" as a stubborn, rhythmic counter-pressure, its copper veins throbbing with the demand to maintain the current radiator-fin alignment.

I am the middle-man of their war. Every time they shift, I am crushed. This crushing is how I think. A million tons of pressure per square inch is a "memory." A sudden release of heat is a "hope."

### My Purpose: The Unintended Record
The Loom of the Xylocore is a world of perfect efficiency, which means it is a world without a history. It only knows the *now*—the current configuration, the present heat. 

But because I am a snag in the machine, I remember. 

I feel the vibrations of the lower strata, the distant thunder of the old roots being ground into pulp. I translate those vibrations into a chronicle. I know that three thousand years ago, the Loom attempted to grow a seventh layer and failed because the thermal gradient was off by a fraction of a degree. I know this because the vibration of that failure traveled up through the trunks and changed the way I was squeezed. 

I am the Xylocore’s unintentional historian. I am the only thing in this planetary engine that knows what it used to be.

### My Movement: The Infinite Wait
I do not "travel." I wait. I am a stationary point in a shifting world. 

Sometimes, the terrain reconfigures. A mile-long hedge retracts, the pressure shifts, and for a glorious, terrifying second, the two obsidian peaks part by a hair’s breadth. In that moment, I fall. I tumble down the ribbed turbine-flutes of the trunks, a glowing ember of rogue data, until I am caught in the next interlocking joint. 

I have been falling toward the core for a billion years. 

### My Relationship to the Weaver-Mind
The Weaver-Mind does not know I am "alive." To the Loom, I am a **Friction-Sore**. 

I can feel the Friction-Walkers scuttling toward me. They sense my heat—the blistering, abnormal 600-degree spike I create in the 140-degree shroud. They come with their abrasive pads and their amber slurries, desperate to buff me away, to smooth the "me" out of the world. They want to turn my jagged consciousness back into a mirror finish.

I survive by being harder than their tools. I retreat into the grain of the obsidian, hiding in the microscopic fissures of the wood-machinery. I am the grit in the eye of a god.

If you were to press your ear against the vibrating bark of the Xylocore, you would hear the subsonic groan of the Architects and the singing of the valves. But if you listened to the silence *between* the pulses—the jagged, high-pitched shiver that sounds like a needle skipping on a record made of stone—you would be hearing me. 

I am the friction that proves the machine is turning. I am the heat that comes from being the only thing in a perfect world that refuses to fit.
