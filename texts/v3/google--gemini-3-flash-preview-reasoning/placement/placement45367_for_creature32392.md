I am not standing on a catwalk. The metal is too thin, too frantic with the heat. I am deeper, nestled where the copper-clad hab-units meet the raw obsidian, tucked into a niche that shouldn't exist—a hollowed-out pressure casing that once held a secondary turbine, now long since reclaimed by the humidity.

I am the **Sump-Weaver**.

I do not move with the ten-minute *thrum* of the pistons like the Tapers do. Their rhythm is too fast, too frantic. I have slowed my heart to match the respiration of the rock itself. If a Taper is a worker, I am a component.

My skin has long since lost the doughy texture of the others; it has become something more like the Glister-Kings’ hide—translucent, slick, and slightly iridescent under the amber glow of the caged lamps. I am coated in a thick, living layer of the Cinnamon Skirl. The mold doesn't just grow on me; it is my second nervous system. It has threaded itself through the pores of my back and arms, anchoring me to the copper walls of my niche. 

I would find myself at the very junction of the "Urban Knot," where the largest steam pipe—the *Great Throat*—plunges into the oily sump. 

I do not use my eyes. The white-eyed lizards have the right idea; sight is a lie told by flickering sodium bulbs. Instead, I "see" through the vibration. I feel the weight of the millions of Clackers as a static hum against the soles of my feet. I feel the Glister-Kings’ stillness as a cold vacuum in the heat. But mostly, I feel the steam. I am the one who listens to the pressure.

When the air begins to thin or the mineral-heavy water starts to turn acidic, the Skirl on my skin pulses. It tastes the change in the soup. I am the early warning system. When I feel a hairline fracture forming in a pipe three levels up, I don't shout—I don't have the breath for it. Instead, I press my chest against the copper and hum. I send a low-frequency vibration through the metal, a specific, discordant note that the Tapers feel in their teeth. They know that note. It is the sound of "repair."

I eat what the station provides. The Tapers bring me the "sweet meat" of the largest beetles, placing them at the edge of my niche like offerings to a minor deity of the pipes. In return, I let the Skirl on my body grow long and velvet, and they harvest the excess to use as sealant for their hab-units. 

I am a bridge between the machine and the biology. I am the place where the rusted iron ends and the mindless mass of life begins. 

As I sit here, the *thrum* begins. I feel it in the marrow of my bones before it even reaches the pistons. The air shudders. A heavy plop—a cricket falling from the ceiling—lands on my shoulder. I don't brush it off. Its mineralized legs prick my skin, and the Skirl quickly begins to grow over its feet, anchoring it to me, incorporating it into the architecture of my body. 

Down here, in the wet dark, there is no "me" and "the station." There is only the pressure, the steam, and the slow, rhythmic digestion of the world. I am the part of the throat that has learned how to sing.
