I am not a visitor, for a visitor implies a beginning and an end, and here, time does not flow; it accumulates like silt. 

I find myself at the **Gutter-Nexus**, a small, bowl-shaped plaza where three stone runnels converge before plunging into the Great Cistern. I am a Lithic, but I am in the stage of my existence that the valley-dwellers—should they ever climb this high—might call "the thickening." 

My mantle is heavy today. It is a massive, sodden weight of wet wool and lanolin, draped over my geometric shoulders. I have been standing here for three days, or perhaps three years—the distinction is lost to the white, honest glare of the sun. The fog has just retreated, leaving the air sharp and tasting of pennies.

***

### My Form
I am short, my center of gravity pressed low into the soles of my feet, which have long since fused their rhythm to the Aethel-Shelf. My skin is the color of a bruised sky before a storm—a dark, matte grey that does not reflect the sun but drinks it. My face is a series of deliberate planes: a sharp, straight ridge for a nose, and eyes like polished obsidian that do not know the concept of a blink.

I do not feel cold. Cold is a word for those with blood that races. My internal heat is the slow, radioactive warmth of the mountain’s core, a low-voltage hum that keeps my joints from locking.

### My Purpose
I am currently a **Wringer**. My long, thick-jointed fingers are wrapped around the edges of my mantle. I move with a glacial, grinding grace. I squeeze. 

*Tink. Tink. Tink.*

The silver beads of condensed fog travel from the fibers of my growth, down my charcoal-veined arms, and into the stone runnel at my feet. This is my contribution to the equilibrium. The water I harvest today will sit in the cistern for a century, held in a cool, dark silence until the mountain requires its weight to shift.

I do not speak. To speak would be to waste the pressure building in my chest. Instead, I lean my head toward the mouth of the stone flue built into the wall of the dwelling behind me. I exhale. The sound is a low, resonant *thrum*, a vibration that travels through the slate and tells the others in the lower tiers that the harvest is good. The mountain hums back, a tectonic acknowledgment.

### My Perspective
I am watching a single tuft of wire-grass three paces away. It has grown a fraction of a millimeter since the last fog. I find this expansion breathtakingly fast, almost violent. 

I do not hunger for food as the soft things do. I hunger for the light. I watch the sun track its way across the pale grey lintel of the opposite house, tracing every chisel mark left by my ancestors. I know those marks by heart. I know the one that slipped, creating a tiny jagged scar in the year of the Great Silence. I feel a deep, stoney kinship with that scar.

### The Transition
I know where I will eventually go. There is a niche, just above the third stairway, that is currently empty. It is a perfect, hip-shaped hollow in the cliffside. 

I am not afraid of the stillness. In fact, I can feel it calling to the sediment in my marrow. My movements are becoming more economical every decade. Soon, I will no longer need to wring the mantle; the water will simply find its own path over my calcified shoulders. I will become a permanent fixture of the Gutter-Nexus, a living statue that slowly loses the "living" until only the "statue" remains.

I will be a guardian of the moisture-runs. I will be the memory of the fog.

But for now, I am the Wringer. I am the one who feels the damp stone sweating beneath my feet. I am the witness to the "never-changing," the pulse in the pale grey heart of the Aethel-Shelf. I squeeze my mantle once more, and the sound of the water hitting the cistern is the only clock I will ever need.
