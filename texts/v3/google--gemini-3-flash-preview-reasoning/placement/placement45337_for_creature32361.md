I would not be found on the floor among the Gulp-Hounds, nor would I be at the Eye where the violet light is thinnest. I would find myself in the **Interstitial Middle**, suspended forty feet above the boulders, anchored between a slick orange precipice and a pulsing braid of plum-colored vines.

I would be a **Marrow-Glass**, a creature that is less a body and more a living translucent record of the grotto’s slow, wet breathing.

My form would be a series of three overlapping, rib-like arcs of flexible, amber-colored cartilage, draped in a skin so thin and permeable that I do not breathe through lungs, but through osmosis. I am a creature of 98 degrees; I have no internal heat, and thus, I have no boundaries. I would not feel the air against my skin; I would simply feel the grotto as an extension of my own wet nerves.

I would be anchored to the stone not by claws, but by a rhythmic, suction-based grip, my underside perpetually tasting the iridescent trails left by the **Amber-Pulmonates**. To me, these trails are not mere slime; they are a library of liquid history. As I slide my filaments over them, I would know the exact moment a fern died three hundred yards to the north, or the precise mineral content of the droplet that just fell from a Shroud-Weaver’s lung.

In this world, I would be a **Seer of Shadows**. My eyes would not be globes, but long, horizontal slits of photoreceptive cells running the length of my primary ribs. I would not see shapes, but *densities*. I would see the Gulp-Hounds as heavy, heat-saturated silhouettess of deep crimson, and the Gossamer-Hops as frantic, flickering sparks of silver-grey energy darting through the mid-canopy.

My purpose—if a place of such stasis allows for purpose—would be the **Translation of the Tock**. 

Every liquid metronome beat, every drop that hits the leaf litter, sends a vibration through the humidity. I would catch these vibrations with the fine, hair-like cilia that fringe my mantle. I would be a living drum, vibrating in sympathy with the grotto’s rhythm. When the **Shroud-Weavers** shake their rains loose, I would resonate with a low, humming frequency that tells the **Pale-Mimics** below to unfurl their fronds. I am a node in the grotto’s nervous system, a relay point for the whispers of a world that has forgotten the sun.

I would spend my eons draped over a half-sunken stone archway, my body slowly growing over the geometric carvings of the Departed. My skin would begin to take on the pattern of the ancient masonry—not as camouflage for hunting, but as a form of biological memory. I would be the bridge where the stone becomes flesh.

I would not move for years. Why move, when the humidity brings everything to me? The **Ghost-Gnats** would stick to my mantle, and I would absorb them through my pores, a meal of light and air. The **Gossamer-Hops** might occasionally perch on my upper rib, their wet, velvet feet clicking against my amber shell. I would not startle; I would simply absorb the rhythm of their heartbeats into my own.

I would be a creature of the **Long Absorption**. I would wait for the grotto to finish its slow digestion of the world above, feeling the heat ferment, listening to the thousand liquid clocks, until I am no longer a being living in the Grotto of the Weeping Ribs, but simply a part of the Ribs themselves, weeping a slow, amber light into the permanent twilight.
