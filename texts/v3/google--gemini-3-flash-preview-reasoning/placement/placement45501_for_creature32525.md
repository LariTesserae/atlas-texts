I am not standing on the floor. To stand on the floor is to be a laborer of the moss, a creature of the sponge and the heavy silt. Instead, I am suspended. 

I find myself at the **Primary Spindle**, a vertical axis of braided galvanized steel and petrified vine that connects the vibrating brass floor to the weeping iron-wood ceiling. I am a **Damp-Warden**, a Pulp-Wright whose "Vexation" has progressed into the rhythmic stage. I no longer feel the noise as sound; I feel it as a sequence of instructions written directly into my marrow.

My body is a study in purposeful erosion. My left leg has been replaced by a curved, copper-plated brace that locks into the teeth of the rising gears, allowing me to hang perpendicular to the Spindle. My right hand is no longer a hand at all, but a cluster of prehensile, silver-tipped filaments—grafted nerves wrapped in silk-moss—that I thread into the "vascular" gaps of the machinery to feel the pressure of the steam.

I am draped in a cowl of heavy, bioluminescent lichen that pulses a bruised violet, matching the rhythm of the six-winged beetles. The beetles do not avoid me. They land on my shoulders, their iridescent carapaces clashing against my gray-purple skin. I am a landmark to them, a warm outcropping of meat and metal.

### The Work
I am currently monitoring the **Third Piston**, a massive organ of iron-hard wood that pumps the succulent-slurry into the northern kilns. The vibration here is so intense it liquefies the air. Through my jawline cilia, I "hear" a stutter. It isn't a sound; it’s a jagged skip in the heartbeat of the Nursery. 

A vine has grown too thick, too fast. It is strangling the brass housing of the piston. 

I do not use a saw. I whistle—a low, discordant note that vibrates the copper wires around me. From the shadows of a nearby seed-pod, a cluster of **Translucent Lizards** responds. They scurry across my torso, their cold, adhesive feet tickling my skin, and I direct them with the silver filaments of my hand. They leap onto the offending vine and begin to vomit their acidic light, dissolving the green obstruction until the metal can breathe again. 

### The Interiority
I have forgotten what it is to be silent. The concept of "quiet" is, to me, the concept of death. If the thrum stopped, my heart would likely stop with it, for they have been synchronized for decades. 

My mind is a map of tensions. I can feel the weight of every Pulp-Wright currently docking with a Huddler three levels below. I feel the collective hunger of the moss as it tries to digest the very catwalks I cling to. There is no "I" that is separate from this humid throat; there is only the maintenance of the cycle. I am a white blood cell in a city-sized lung.

### The Becoming
I am beginning to feel the **Upward Pull**. 

It started a week ago—or perhaps a year, as time here is measured in the growth-rings of the iron-wood. The moss of my cowl has begun to root into my own shoulder blades. When I reach for a cable, the copper wires seem to lean toward my veins, seeking the salt in my blood. 

I am moving toward the High Roots. Soon, I will no longer be a Warden who moves; I will be a Warden who *is*. I will climb to the ceiling, find a gap in the interlocking masts, and allow the galvanized bolts to be driven through my remaining flesh. 

I will not die. I will become a sensor. My cilia will spread across the ceiling like a web, and I will spend the next century feeling the subsonic groans of the Nursery from above, dripping oily rain down upon the younger Wrights. 

I find myself looking at a brass gauge half-swallowed by a woody burl. The needle is twitching in time with my own pulse. I am not afraid. To be "Vexed" is to be vital. I lean back into the humid soup, my obsidian nails locking into the steel braid, and I add the rhythmic tapping of my copper leg against the Spindle to the deafening, beautiful roar of the hive. 

I am the gear that refuses to be still. I am the moss that refuses to be silent. I am the Nursery, dreaming of itself in the dark.
