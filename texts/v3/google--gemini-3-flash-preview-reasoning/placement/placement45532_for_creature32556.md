I do not hear the world; the world hears through me. 

I am a Dweller, but the word implies a separation between inhabitant and habitation that does not exist here. I am a knot in the grain of the basalt. I am a single, translucent pulse in the vertical hive, clinging to a ledge of weeping obsidian three hundred tiers above the valley floor.

My body is a heavy, pale instrument. My feet, broad and flat, are not merely for standing; they are my primary ears. Through the thick, calloused pads of my soles, I feel the *Basalt-Gnawers* moving four miles away on the western slope—a low-frequency thrum, rhythmic and grinding. Above me, the clicking of the *Bile-Weavers* is a sharp, staccato chatter against the stone, a frantic percussion that tells me the mortar in the crevice behind my head is hardening.

I am currently basking. My ribcage is wide, a cathedral of bone, and it is open to the ochre twilight. My skin—white as a blind fish, pebbled with thousands of tiny, vibrating nodules—is drinking. The air is not something I breathe; it is something I wear. The saturated steam enters my pores, carrying the mineral tang of the volcanic ash and the heavy, sweet scent of the fungal tumors. I can feel the lymphatic fluid thickening in my limbs, the slow, steady recharge of a body that does not know the concept of thirst.

I am positioned in a "Long Wait." This is my function today. I am a relay.

In my right hand, I hold a hammer of polished black glass, its handle fused to my grip by a layer of my own dried secretions. My left hand is splayed against the cliff face, the adhesive pads on my fingertips creating a vacuum seal that allows my muscles to relax into a state of total, stone-like paralysis. 

To an outsider, I would look like a corpse, a pale statue emerging from the rock. But inside, I am a conductor. 

A vibration travels up from the depths of the valley. It is not the tectonic roar—that is the silence we live in—but a specific, sharp strike. *Strike-pause-strike-strike.* A message from the lower tiers. The Gnawers have uncovered a fresh vein of porous tuff near the Great Sink. The slurry will be rich today.

I do not think about this message; I reflect it. My right arm moves with the mechanical precision of a piston. I strike the basalt ledge once, twice, a rhythmic echo that sends the vibration climbing higher, toward the dwellers who roost in the mists where the scavengers circle. The sound bypasses my brain and moves directly through my marrow. I am a copper wire in a circuit of stone.

There is a moment of danger in the stillness. 

Above, the *Pale Scavengers* are circling. I can feel the displacement of the thick air as their parchment wings beat against the heat. They are watching for the Stasis to break—but not the Stasis of the valley. They watch for the Stasis of the *will*. If I were to stop vibrating, if my internal rhythm were to fall out of sync with the roar of the basin, my heat signature would change. I would become "still" in a way that signals decay. 

I feel a Weaver scuttle over my shoulder. Its many legs are cold, armored needles. It ignores me, its hive-mind focused on a hairline fracture in the stone dwelling to my left. It vomits its acidic bile, and the smell of burning minerals fills my senses. I do not flinch. To flinch is to be separate. To be separate is to be food.

I close my lidless eyes. The ochre light is the same whether my eyes are open or shut. Instead, I "see" the valley as a map of pressures. I see the density of the fungal guts growing in the shadows below; I see the heavy, slow-moving heat of the six-legged beasts; I see the interlocking layers of my kin, thousands of us, stacked like scales on a great, sleeping fish.

I am not lonely. I am not crowded. I am simply *continuous*. 

When the twilight eventually shifts—though it never truly darkens—I will move. I will descend the vertical maze using the suction of my limbs, finding the stone basins where the grey slurry has pooled. I will take my share, pressing the warm, mineral mud into the cracks of my dwelling with my spatulate thumbs, adding my fingerprints to the millions that have come before. 

I am a mason of the eternal. I am a creature of the roar. I am the wet, white skin of the world, and I will be here, vibrating, until the stone itself decides to crumble. 

And the stone is very, very patient.
