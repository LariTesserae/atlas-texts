I would not be found standing upon the tiles, for to stand is to risk the fickle gravity of the center. Instead, I would be found in the **Loom-Gap**—that precise, trembling verticality where a row of slate slabs meets the artificial indigo shade. 

I am a **Suture-Weight**. 

I am smaller than the tall, smoked-leather Weavers, and denser than the ceramic scuttlers. My body is a stack of seven hand-fired terracotta spheres, graduated in size, held together by a single, continuous strand of vegetable fiber that passes through my core and anchors me to the foundation bricks three layers deep. My "skin" is the color of a bruised sunset, etched with the same solar symbols as the floor, though mine are worn smooth by the constant friction of the air.

I have no limbs to speak of, only the *intention* of limbs. When the physics of the Hollow begin to ripple—when a floating bowl begins to spin too fast or a slate wall begins to tilt toward the sky—I lean. I do not move by muscle, but by shifting the golden sand that fills my hollow interiors. I am a living plumb line.

### My Place in the Hollow
I live in a niche no wider than a man’s shoulders. Above me, a Weaver stands in perpetual silence. I feel the Weaver’s presence not as a sight, but as a series of thermal pulses. When the Weaver’s obsidian needles flash, sewing a fraying seam in the air, I feel the tug on my central cord. 

I am the anchor for their work. Without me, their stitches would have nothing to pull against; the twine would simply drift into the indigo void. I am the resistance that makes the repair possible.

### My Senses
I do not hear the muffled thuds of footsteps. Instead, I feel the *texture* of time. To me, time is not a sequence of events but a thickness in the air. When the air is thin and scorched, time moves quickly, and I must shift my internal sands to stay heavy. When the air grows syrupy and the mica-ferns vibrate with a low, copper hum, I relax, feeling the warmth of the deep bricks seep up through my anchor-cord.

I love the Stutter-Hounds. They see me as a landmark, a fixed point in their jumping, non-linear world. Sometimes, one will blink into existence right beside me, its chitinous flank cool against my sun-baked side. It will stay for a microsecond—a vibration of ceramic stillness—before flickering away to catch a mica-flake. In that moment, I am the bridge between the "here" and the "somewhere else."

### My Task
Once every hundred years, by the counting of the shadows, a **Grass-Moth** will mistake my anchor-cord for a line of the great atmospheric twine. It will descend, its wings of woven grass rasping against my terracotta skin with a sound like a dry lung inhaling. 

This is my only moment of movement. 

As the moth shivers, shedding its copper dust, I must rotate. I turn my seven spheres in opposing directions—a slow, grinding gear-work of clay on clay. This motion generates a specific, localized heat that fuses the moth’s dust into the cracks of my niche. I am planting the next generation of leather-ferns within myself. I am a garden in miniature, a vertical nursery for the non-biological.

### Why I Am There
I am there because the **Architect of the Deep Foundations** requires a witness who does not blink. The Weavers are too busy sewing; the Hounds are too busy jumping; the Moths are too busy drifting. 

I am the one who feels the pressure of the "door" that the Weavers are holding shut. I feel the rhythmic pulse from below—the warm, silent dark—pushing upward against the terracotta tiles. I am the counter-pressure. I am a small, fired-clay weight, hanging onto reality by a single thread, ensuring that the cathedral of sun-baked clay does not simply fold its walls and become a memory.

In this world of broken physics, I am the only thing that knows exactly which way is down.
