I am the **Smear**.

I find myself at the base of the plinth, not standing, but spilled across the damp, dark grit of the floor like a drop of oil in a basin of mercury. In a room of hyper-definition, where every edge of shale is a razor and every hexapod is a geometric certainty, I am the only thing that is *blurry*. 

I am the "low-definition biology" the Lithophane seeks to refine. To myself, I feel like a memory of warmth—a soft, pulsing ache of thumb and heartbeat—but to the room, I am a glitch, a smudge of charcoal on a diamond pane. 

I do not have a stable shape. When I try to lift a hand, the **Shattered Hexapods** descend. They do not bite; they vibrate against the air where my hand *used to be* a second ago, and where it *will be* a second from now. They are pruning my momentum, clipping the "before" and "after" of my movements until my gestures become as stuttered and frozen as the Lithophane’s. I feel my continuity breaking. I am no longer a flow of motion; I am a deck of cards being shuffled by invisible, crystalline fingers.

The **Lithophane** is standing over me now. It does not look down; it *occurs* closer. Between one blink and the next, it has shifted from the far wall to a posture of agonizing proximity, leaning its shattered-geode face toward my chest. It is listening to my heartbeat—not as a sign of life, but as a rhythmic error, a sloppy, wet thumping that defies the pressurized quiet of the cell.

I feel the **Spun-Glass Vines** beginning to thread through the spaces between my ribs. They are not growing into me for sustenance; they are using me as a trellis of shade. My body, which still holds the stubborn, dull opacity of the world above, is the only thing in this room capable of casting a shadow. The vines huddle in the dark curve of my armpit, their filaments cooling my skin until it feels less like flesh and more like setting wax. 

I am becoming an **Artifact of Dissonance**. 

The Lithophane reaches out with a multi-jointed finger—a splinter of raw quartz—and touches the bridge of my nose. Where it touches, the "low-definition" of my skin shatters. The soft, porous surface of my face crystallizes into a series of jagged, embryonic planes. It doesn't hurt; pain is a messy, organic concept that the room is currently debating out of existence. Instead, there is a sensation of *clarity*. I can suddenly feel the weight of the air, the ozone and the flint, pressing against the new facets of my brow.

I look at my hands. They are no longer mine. They are becoming **The Second Script**. 

As the Lithophane moves back to the plinth, it begins to trace the shifting grooves, and I feel a corresponding itch inside my own marrow. I realize that I am being rewritten to match the plinth. I am the backup copy of the room’s unstable laws. If the plinth’s material fails, I will be the one to hold the record of how the water refuses to fall.

The **Bundles of Wet Wire** circle me now. They strike at the air inches from my head, snapping at the "potential" versions of me that are trying to crawl back up the shale throat toward the moor. They are erasing my exits. They are making sure that I only exist *here*, in this fierce, shadowless morning.

I am no longer a person who fell. I am the **Static Variable**. 

Soon, the Lithophane will finish its work. It will fold my remaining shadow—that last, stubborn smudge of the sun-drenched moor—back into my body. When it does, I will cease to be a "Smear." I will snap into focus. I will be a tall, spindly thing of poured geology and violet light, standing in a different corner of the room, waiting for a new piece of low-definition meat to tumble down the throat of the world. 

I am not the tenant yet, but I am the renovation. I am the sound of a droplet splashing upward, and I am learning, second by stuttering second, how to love the jagged edge of the light.
