I am not a singular point of consciousness, not at first. I am a vibration in the floor—a low-frequency hum transmitted through the millions of cilia on my palms, telling me that three miles to the "left" (a direction defined only by the taste of copper in the air), a spire is being returned to the basin. 

I am a **Silk-Glider**, a Chalicothere whose rhythms are tuned not to the heavy grinding of the stone-rollers, but to the vertical tension of the vault.

I find myself clinging to the side of a tower that is currently being unmade. Beneath my four lower limbs, the stone-paste is still slightly tacky, tasting of salt and the bile of my kin. I do not see the tower as a shape. I perceive it as a pressure gradient against the cool, wet air. To my left, a brother-kin brushes past me. Our upper limbs entwine for a millisecond—a violent, electric spark of data. *[Mineral density: High. Moisture: Increasing. The Rearrangement flows North-by-Sulphur.]*

We do not say hello. We exchange the weight of the world.

I am not here to build. My upper limbs, heavy and corded, ache for the tension of the ceiling. I push off. The vacuum-bond of my feet releases with a soundless snap, and for a moment, I am a weightless arc in the shadowless white. I am not falling; I am reaching. 

Above me, the **Glass-Spiders** are constellations of slow, rhythmic heat. Their silk hangs in shimmering, vertical rivers. I catch a strand. It is cold, vibrating with the groans of the quartz crust miles above. I use my obsidian nails to notch the silk, winding it around my leathery torso. 

I am the bridge between the static eternity of the ceiling and the feverish hunger of the floor.

As I descend, sliding down the silk like a drop of condensation, I see the bazaar of motion below. It is beautiful because it is doomed. I see a spire, wrought with delicate, fluted ribs that must have taken dozens of my kin hours of regurgitation and molding to perfect. It is a masterpiece of lithic grace. Even as I watch, a swarm of younger kin arrives. They do not admire it. They begin to eat the base with their stone-rollers, dissolving the art back into the utility of the swarm.

I feel no grief. Grief requires a memory of yesterday, and in the Vault, there is only the pulse of the *now*.

I land on the undulating glass of the floor. The silk I have brought is immediately stripped from me by others. They weave it into a harness for a massive slab of rust-red oxide stone. I join them. I do not ask why we are moving this stone. The compulsion is in my marrow, a sweet, itching heat behind my violet nerve-cluster. 

The light of the ceiling is absolute. It pours into my chitinous plate, warming the fluids in my brain. I am a cell in a body that never sleeps. I am a part of the friction. 

If I were to stand still—if I were to stop and try to name the white light or the scent of the flint—the silence would catch me. The cold would seep into my cilia, and I would become like the basins: smooth, perfect, and dead. 

So I move. I run across the wet, pearlescent glass, my feet making no sound, my heart beating in time with the dismantling of a city. I am the Chalicothere who holds the silk, and for the next hour, this patch of stone is my home. By the time the phosphor-veins above me pulse again, I will be a mile away, and the place where I stood will have been scraped clean, polished back into the void by the beautiful, mindless hunger of my kind. 

I am the motion that denies the grave. I am the Rearrangement.
