I do not have a face to turn toward the dark, for the dark is not in front of me; it is *in* me. It is the marrow of my leather and the grit in my pores. 

I am a Weaver, but I have forgotten the time when I crept across the glass floor in search of a center. I have found it. I am **The First-Anchored of the Obsidian Lee.**

I find myself at the convergence of three great rivers of brine—three "slow-avalanches" that meet in a perpetual, soundless collision. To your eyes, if you could see, I would look like a spill of dark, vulcanized oil upon a mirror of black flint. I am twenty-two feet of shivering, mineral-dense muscle, pinned to the bedrock by a weight that has become my soul.

Below me, clutched in the thousands of vacuum-pores of my underside, is the Heart of the Lee: a three-hundred-pound sphere of pure volcanic glass, perfectly round, smoothed by a million years of grinding silt. I did not find this stone. I *earned* it. I spent four of your centuries dragging it from the far reaches of the High Shuffles, moving a fraction of an inch every time the current ebbed, lashing my body to the basalt floor with my own hooked hide, refusing to be unmade.

Now, I am the anchor of a tether-work. I am the mountain that the mountains forgot to be.

**The Sensation of Being**
I feel the world as a sequence of pressures. To me, there is no "water." There is only the *Heavy Press* and the *Violent Press*. 

My upper surface is a carpet of a billion cilia, each one a tiny nerve ending tuned to the friction of the silt. When the density currents careen over me, it does not feel like a storm; it feels like a coarse, frozen tongue licking my skin. I taste the history of the surface in the silt—the pulverized remains of things that once had bones, the dust of continents that haven't seen the sun in an epoch. It is delicious. It is the taste of everything finally becoming the same.

**The Shudder-Song**
I am speaking now. Not with a throat, but with my central mantle—the thickest, hardest part of my body, where the copper veins are most concentrated. I strike the basalt floor. 

*Thrum. Thrum-low. Pause.*

The vibration travels through the glass floor, faster and clearer than it ever could through the thick brine. I am telling the others—the smaller discs huddling in the lee of my body—that the Current of the Pale Silt is shifting. I feel the change in the friction on my northern edge. The brine is thickening. A "High Shuffle" is coming.

Below me, tucked into the folds of my hide and the gaps of my tether-braids, the Moss-Keepers are frantic. I feel their tiny, calcified legs clicking against the rock. They are cleaning the filaments of my moss, harvesting the friction-heat I generate by simply resisting the current. We are a machine of stillness. 

**The Moment of the Storm**
The current hits. This is the moment I exist for. 

The brine becomes so dense it is like being buried under a falling wall of lead. The pressure spikes, squeezing the bioluminescent copper in my veins until I glow with a fierce, dying orange light. I am a ghost-sun in the abyss. The kelp-fibers of my tethers groan, pulled taut against the obsidian sphere. 

I do not fear being swept away. I am too heavy for fear. I am the expression of the planet's gravity. I press my vacuum-pores harder against the floor, creating a seal that not even the death of a world could break. 

A *Velluma*—a long, mindless ribbon of translucent jelly—is caught in the churn above me. It is whipped downward, its fragile body shredded by the turbulence. I reach up with a single, whip-like tendril, snagging a fragment of it as it passes. It is cold, tasteless, and perfect. I pull it under my mantle, into the slow, rhythmic crush of my digestive pores.

**The Philosophy of the Weight**
You might wonder if I dream of the surface, of the thin, screaming air and the light that does not crush. I do not. To me, the surface is a myth of ghosts—a place where things are so light they have no meaning. To be light is to be nothing. To be light is to be at the mercy of the wind.

Here, in the Grave of the Currents, I have achieved the ultimate dignity: I am immovable. 

I am the end-state of evolution. I have no eyes to see what is lost, no ears to hear the silence, and no legs to run away. I am the Lithos-Weaver, the First-Anchored. I will stay here, tapping my shudder-song into the bedrock, until the friction of the moving world wears my leathery hide down to the bone-meal, and I too become the silt that I once tasted.

Until then, I hold the stone. I feel the press. I am.
