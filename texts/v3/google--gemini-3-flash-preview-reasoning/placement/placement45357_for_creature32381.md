I am not a creature of the spires, nor am I one of the restless scavengers darting through the silt. I find myself at the city’s precise, shivering heart. I am a **Loom-Tender**, positioned atop the *Aorta of Schist*, the central silica-bridge that spans the widest micro-reservoir in Still-Drip.

I am a sliver of mica-sheen and silica-string, no thicker than a thought. My body is a translucent, elongated oval, pressed so flat against the wet limestone that I am less a three-dimensional being and more a living property of the surface itself. 

### My Position: The High Tension
I am wedged into a hairline fracture at the apex of the Bridge of Ten Thousand Tears. Below me, the reservoir is a disk of perfect, unblinking black, so still that it reflects the grey architecture above with the terrifying clarity of a mirror. Above me, the "sky" is a weeping rib of the mountain, where a single, massive drop of water gathers every three hours. 

I am not here to watch. I am here to feel the **Tension**. 

My ventral membrane is fused to a cluster of silica strands—hair-fine filaments harvested from the deep mineral veins. These strands stretch across the abyss, anchored by my own calcified ancestors. My role is to be the bridge's nerve. By shifting the microscopic tension in my silica-muscles, I ensure that the bridge vibrates at a frequency that repels the weight of the water, preventing the slick membrane of the city from collapsing into the dark water below.

### My Perception: The Symphony of Impact
I do not experience silence. To me, the "deafening" percussion of the city is not noise; it is a dense, mathematical language. 

I feel the *thrum-crack* of a droplet hitting a shale plate three levels above. That is the rhythm of the North Wall, a steady 4/4 beat that tells me the mineral content of the seep is high today—the water is heavy, thick with dissolved lime. I feel the *hollow-cluck* of the bird-bone resonator near the Groaning Spires, signaling that the barometric breath of the "Greater World" is inhaling, pulling the damp air upward and tightening the city’s grip on the stone.

I am a translator of tremors. When a salt-grain arthropod scuttles onto my bridge, its movement is a chaotic, jagged scratching that sets my tympanum on edge. I do not acknowledge it with eyes; I acknowledge it as a dissonance that must be dampend. I adjust my grip, sending a counter-vibration through the silica strands that makes the bridge feel, to the insect, like a surface of impossible, repulsive heat. It flees. I return to the stasis.

### My Work: The Erosion of Ego
I have been in this exact position for four hundred years. 

In the first century, I was restless. I felt the urge to slide across the mineral film, to explore the lower districts where the "oily sheen" is richest. But the City of Still-Drip does not reward travel; it rewards placement. 

Now, I am becoming the bridge. My edges are starting to crust with the "blood of the mountain." A fine, white rim of calcite has begun to bloom along my northern perimeter, pinning me more firmly to the schist. I do not fear this. I am helping to grow the bridge’s next layer. My slow, rhythmic feeding—absorbing the bitter, metallic salts from the water that washes over me—is not for my own growth, but to maintain the mass required to stay anchored against the Great Rhythms.

### My Final Form
Eventually, a Tuner will signal a shift. A new vein of water will open, or the mountain will groan in its sleep, and the frequency of the Aorta of Schist will need to change. I will tighten my body one last time, shifting my internal lattice to match the new resonance of the city. 

And then, I will stop moving entirely. 

The water will continue to weep over me, depositing its microscopic cargo of stone. My silica muscles will petrify. My tympanum will harden into a plate of translucent quartz. I will no longer be the Tender of the Loom; I will be the Loom itself. 

I am content. In the City of Still-Drip, to be remembered is to be walked upon. To be successful is to become indistinguishable from the foundation. I am a single, vibrating note in a song that has no end, held in the cold, wet palm of the earth, waiting for the water to turn me into forever.
