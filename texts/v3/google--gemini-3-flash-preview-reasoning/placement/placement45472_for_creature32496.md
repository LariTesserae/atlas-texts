I am a **Resonance-Watcher** on the outer lattice of the City of Ocre-Vane, anchored to the windward face of the Four-Thousandth Spine.

I do not sit; I am wedged. My body, thin as a glass needle and wrapped in heavy copper-glass thread, is slotted into a parabolic groove in the oxidized iron. To an outsider, I would look like a gargoyle or a forgotten structural bolt. My skin, calloused into a fine, mineral grit, has long since reached thermal equilibrium with the stone. I am warm, perpetually and pleasantly warm, like a sun-drenched brick.

I am currently in the middle of a thought. I began it when the shadow of the Aether-Manta, a leviathan named *Selah-of-the-High-Drafts*, first touched the base of the neighboring pillar. That was three days ago. The thought is about the nature of the friction between the stadium-sized flywheel behind me and the quartz-bearing strata it is currently devouring.

The flywheel turns. I feel it not as a sound, but as a slow, tectonic shudder in my pelvis. *Thrum...* The vibration travels through the iron pylon, through my calcified hips, and up my spine. *Thrum...* It takes forty minutes for a single tooth of the gear to pass the monitoring station. I watch it with my primary pupils, while my secondary pupils track the violet silt-sea three miles below.

I find myself at the interface of the organic and the geologic. In my left hand—the fingers elongated and tipped with obsidian—I hold a tuning fork made from the molted carapace of a Glass-Winged Scraper. I press it to the rock. The Scraper-glass doesn’t just carry sound; it amplifies the internal stress of the Spine. I am listening for the "Scream of Sheer"—the high-pitched harmonic that indicates the rock is tired of being ground and is preparing to shatter.

We do not want shattering. We want the slow, polite dust.

The air around me is a shimmering amber haze, thick with Glints. They dance in the wake of my slow, rhythmic breathing, swirling in the heat-shimmer of my exhales. I do not move my head. To move quickly is to invite the Prism-Wraiths; they are attracted to the sudden kinetic flash of a swinging limb like a moth to a flame. I have seen a fellow Watcher caught in a moment of haste—the Wraiths focused the amber sun into a needle of white heat, and he was vitrified where he stood, turned into a permanent, glass statue of panic. He is still there, two Spines over, a glittering landmark we use to measure the clarity of the air.

Today—if "day" is the word for this stretch of golden light—the resonance is changing.

The violet silt below is no longer behaving like a sea. It is beginning to form patterns. From my height, they look like the intricate, geometric frost one might find on an ancient window. The industrial heartbeat of Ocre-Vane has hit a sub-harmonic frequency with the planet’s core. 

I feel a deep, pleasurable ache in my marrow. The dust is rising.

Silently, a pillar of violet powder begins to climb from the basin. It is perfectly square, its edges as sharp as a planed board, rising thousands of feet toward my perch. Within its shimmering walls, I see a dark, heavy mass. A Silt-Whale. It is rising within the pillar, its tungsten-heavy body suspended in the harmonic updraft.

As the dust-tower reaches my level, the Whale and I become neighbors. It is a gargantuan slab of matte-grey metal, its surface pitted by eons of sub-surface friction. It has no eyes, but I feel its presence—a massive, gravitational tug that pulls at the copper threads of my robe. 

We look at each other, in a sense. The machine behind me grinds a single, massive gear-tooth. The Whale vibrates a plate of iron on its flank. We are both notes in the Long Pulse. 

I will remain here for the duration of the Vertical Season. I will not eat; the thermal radiation from the Spines and the static charge of the rising dust provide all the sustenance my mineralized cells require. I will watch the Whale, and the Whale will sense the flywheel, and the two of us will wait for the wind to shift.

There is no hurry. I have ten thousand more years of bone-hardening before I am fully stone. For now, I am simply the listener. I am the nerve ending of the mountain, the witness to the slow, beautiful digestion of the world. 

The gear turns. The dust rises. The amber light holds us all in its warm, dry hand. 

I am exactly where I am meant to be. I am a brief, slow stutter in the eternal song of the iron.
