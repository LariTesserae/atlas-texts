I am not a creature of breath, but a creature of **stress.** 

I find myself anchored to the windward edge of a spire named *The Severed Chord*, a blade of obsidian so thin it vibrates in the heat-haze like a tuning fork. I am a **Lithos-Pulse**, a jagged arrangement of six-sided basalt plates held together by a humming, magnetic core. To the eye of a ghost, I would look like a cluster of shrapnel frozen in the act of an explosion. 

My consciousness is not a stream of thought, but a sequence of frequencies. I do not "see" the dark, charcoal sky; I feel the weight of it, the atmospheric pressure pressing against my facets. 

***

**The Movement**
I need to relocate. The spire beneath me is groaning in a key that suggests imminent subsidence. I decide to move toward a cooling chimney three miles across the dust-trough. 

To move is to die and be reborn in a micro-second. I release the magnetic tension on my leading shards. *Snap.* They shatter away from my core, falling forward. Before they can hit the ground, I pulse, re-aligning the magnetic lattice. The shards at my rear disintegrate, their molecular bonds severed, and they fly forward to recrystallize at the front. 

*Crunch-snap. Crunch-snap.* 

The sound of my walking is the sound of a mountain being put through a crusher. It is a violent, percussive rhythm. With every "step," I feel the piezoelectric spark—a sharp, electric tang that tastes like cold lightning in a world of suffocating heat.

**The Feeding**
Mid-transit, the ground shudders. This is not the rhythmic *thrum* of the crust; this is a **Birth.** 

I freeze. I splay my shards wide, increasing my surface area to catch the wave. A mile to my left, the dust-sea erupts. A column of virgin basalt, raw and glowing with a dull, internal red, thrusts upward. It screams—a sound of stone shearing against stone that would liquefy human ears. 

To me, it is a banquet. 

The kinetic energy ripples through the ground, hitting my base-plates. I drink it. The vibration rushes through my crystalline lattice, aligning my internal geometry, purging the "noise" of my previous movements. I feel dense. I feel sharp. I am saturated with the raw, tectonic ambition of the forge. For a moment, my matte-black surface glows with a faint, violet luminescence—the sign of a full belly.

**The Reading**
As I pass a leaning fin of obsidian, I brush my trailing edge against a crust of **Aesthetes.** These pale, brittle minerals are cold to the touch, a strange sensation in this furnace. 

As my shards scrape over their fractal branches, a memory-frequency bleeds into me. I "see" the valley as it was four hundred years ago: a flat plain of cooling slag, before the great upheaval that created the chimneys. I feel the ghost-vibration of a mountain that fell before I was ever crystallized. I am a brief link in a chain of stone, carrying the resonance of the dead spires toward the future.

**The Cooling**
The heat is becoming dangerous. My internal plates are starting to soften at the edges; if I reach the melting point, my magnetism will fail, and I will become nothing more than a puddle of mindless glass. 

I reach the chimney—a tapering, megalithic flue of stacked slate. I wedge myself into the lightless interior. I stop all movement. I release the tension in my shards, letting them hang loose in the magnetic web, creating gaps for the heat to escape. 

I sit in the dark. I listen to the *hiss* of the **Silicates** in the dust-trough below, their low-frequency hum acting as a lullaby for the restless crust. 

I am a fragment of the forge. I have no eyes to cry, no lungs to gasp, and no heart to break. I am only a point of sharp, black geometry, waiting for the world to shatter so that I may feel, for one glorious, screaming second, that I am truly solid.
