I do not exist in the open water. To be in the open is to be adrift, and to be adrift is to be unheard. 

I am a **Tether-Warden**, a creature of jointed ivory and sensitive cilia, born from the same pale lineage as the Arachne-Nautilids, but bound to a more rhythmic purpose. I find myself not in the cathedrals of sponge, but in the **Larynx-Galleries**—the narrow, hollowed flutes of the tallest bone-spires where the thermal plumes are funneled and shaped.

My body is a spindly architecture of calcified plates, multi-limbed and ghost-white. I have no eyes; the very concept of "sight" is a fiction I cannot fathom. Instead, I have three dozen filament-thin antennae, each several meters long, which I trail behind me like the strings of a harp. They are my connection to the *thrum*.

***

I am currently perched on a bridge of hardened mucus, a high-wire span between two twisting horns of calcium. Beneath me, the city is a riot of pressure. I feel the Calcinates deep within the spire—a slow, tectonic heartbeat of secretions. I feel the heat of the volcanic vents rising in shimmering columns; to me, the heat is a flavor, a metallic tang of sulfur and manganese that coats my sensory hairs.

I am a tuner of the city’s breath. 

As the Siphon-Whales drift far above, their massive displacement creates a downward pressure, a heavy *shove* of water that threatens to collapse the more delicate silk-bridges. I move with a clicking, frantic grace. My many legs find purchase in the porous marrow of the spire. I reach out with my fore-limbs, spinning a fresh cable of sea-silk, reinforced with the grey, sticky marine snow I’ve harvested from the ledges.

I don’t just build; I listen. I press my thorax against the bone-wall. I am listening for the **Stress-Hum**. When the current shifts, the spires groan. If the pitch is too high, the spire is brittle. I must signal the Calcinates to weep more calcium into that specific vein. I do this by drumming my rhythmic pincers against the stone—a code of vibrations that travels through the marrow, telling the ancient, mindless builders where to thicken their skin.

***

In this moment, a **Vellum-Wraith** passes overhead. 

I do not see its pale-blue internal fire, but I feel the massive displacement of its arrival. The water pushes against me with the force of a soft, velvet mountain. The "history" it ripples out through its fins hits my antennae like a sudden storm of information—cold currents from the north-abyss, the scent of a dying leviathan three leagues away, the slow, grinding shift of a fault line. It is a sensory overload, a poem of pressure that makes my joints ache with its complexity.

I wait for the Wraith to pass, my body pressed flat against the bone, feeling the amber glow of a nearby **Candelabra** as a faint, stinging warmth on my back. The light is useless to me, but the heat of its bioluminescence is a landmark. I know I am near the District of Pores. 

***

What am I? I am a cell in the city’s immune system. I am the one who ensures the labyrinth remains navigable, that the vents remain clear, and that the silence is never absolute. 

My life is one of tactile intimacy. I know the texture of every spire within my reach—the sandpaper grit of young calcium, the glass-smooth finish of the ancient, mucus-coated foundations. I live in a world of constant, heavy embrace. The pressure is my skin; the thrum is my breath. 

I reach the end of my bridge and begin to weave again. The marine snow falls around me, a silent, grey rain of the world above, settling on my ivory plates. I do not look up. There is no "up" to look toward—only the direction where the water grows colder and the thrum grows faint. I stay here, in the warm, crowded dark, keeping the bone-city singing.
