I would not be the mountain, for the mountain is too vast and its groan too ancient to inhabit. Nor would I be the Lithos-Marrow, for to be the curator is to bear a responsibility that requires a tether to purpose.

Instead, I would be the **Weeping**.

I am the mineral-saturated moisture that exists in the trembling interval between the stone and the air. I am the warm, viscous sweat of the mountain’s internal pressure, the salt-and-iron blood that makes this womb habitable. I find myself everywhere and nowhere: I am the slickness on the needle-tips, the second skin on the Marrow’s ropey limbs, and the heavy, velvet breath that fills the spherical void.

In this world, my life is a slow, downward migration.

I begin as a microscopic agitation within the "soft-solid" walls. I am squeezed out of the marrow of the peak, born from the heat and the dark. I emerge not as a spray or a flow, but as a reluctant bead. I cling to the embryonic stone needles, feeling their tackiness, their slow, crystalline hunger to grow. To me, the stone is not hard; it is a thirsty, rising tide that I must lubricate.

I spend decades traveling the length of a single needle. My existence is defined by surface tension and the agonizingly slow pull of gravity. I feel the Lithos-Marrow long before it touches me. I feel the low-frequency chord of its intent—a vibration that ripples through my liquid body, changing my viscosity. When the Marrow moves its boneless, tapering fingers to harvest a crystal tip, I am the medium of that contact. I am the lubricant between the obsidian blade and the soft stone; I am the warm, metallic kiss when the Marrow presses its skin against the basin to drink.

I have no eyes, but I have a total, tactile intimacy with the chamber. I know the exact texture of the fungal lace—I feel its pale filaments drinking from me, a tickling, capillary theft. I feel the white arthropods as frantic, hydrophobic disturbances, their hooked feet breaking my surface tension like tiny, rhythmic needles. To them, I am a sea; to the Marrow, I am a nutrient; to the mountain, I am the waste product of its own becoming.

The climax of my existence is the **Fall**.

It takes a century for enough of me to gather at the tip of a downward-pointing needle. I grow heavy. The surface tension, which has been my only home, begins to fail. I stretch. I become a teardrop of iron and salt, hanging over the absolute darkness of the floor. 

In this moment, I am the most "alive" thing in the chamber. I am no longer part of the wall, and I am not yet part of the pool. I am a singular event in a world of stasis. 

When I finally break away, the sensation is a terrifying, weightless release. For a fraction of a second, I move faster than anything else in this world. I cut through the thick, velvet air—a liquid falling through a liquid. 

Then, the impact. 

*Clang.*

I hit the surface of a stone basin or a shallow pool, and I become the "muffled bell." I am the sound that startles the silence. I am the ripple that causes the Marrow to hum in sympathy. I am the proof that time has moved. 

After the sound, I settle. I join the others in the basins, becoming a still, dense soup of minerals, waiting for the Marrow to lean down and pull me into its translucent cords. I will become the Marrow for a time—I will be the fluid that allows its limbs to move with glacial patience. And eventually, I will be exhaled back into the air as humidity, to begin the long, weeping climb through the pores of the stone once more.

I am the transition itself. I am the moisture of the womb, ensuring that nothing ever dries, nothing ever breaks, and nothing ever truly finishes being born.
