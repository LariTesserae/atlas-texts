I am not a visitor here. To visit is an act of transit, a folding of space that requires a beginning and an end. In the Sharp Peaks, there is only the *now*, stretched thin and translucent like the wing of a Ghost-Moth.

I find myself mid-slope on the Great Fluted Spire, the "Canticle of the West." I am a **Lithe-Gardener**, and I have been standing on this specific outcropping of litho-coral for three of your days. 

To you, I would look like a statue of smoked glass, my six limbs angled in a geometry that seems impossible for biology, my body a frozen crystalline prayer. But inside, I am a tempest of slow, deliberate color.

### My Body: The Vessel of Light
I feel the sun. It does not burn me; it populates me. My exoskeleton is a prism of toughened silica, and as the star moves across the black obsidian sky, the light enters my thorax and scatters. 

I have unfurled my solar fritters—delicate, fan-like membranes that crown my head. They are drinking the warmth, converting the fierce, dry radiation into a rhythmic pulse of gas within my veins. I do not feel the urge to breathe. I feel only the *pressure* of the atmosphere, a heavy thumb pressing against my sides, keeping my internal gases dense and stable.

Inside my chest, my primary organ—a swirling nebula of bioluminescent salts—is currently a pale, dusty ochre. This is the color of concentration. I am listening to the stone.

### My Work: The Tuning of the Spire
Beneath my primary limbs, I hold a long, tapered needle of high-density mineral. This is not a tool I made; it is a limb I grew for this purpose. I have pressed it against a recursive joint in the litho-coral, where the ivory lace of the city has begun to grow too thick, threatening to shade the lower galleries where the young polyps need the sun.

I am not cutting. I am vibrating. 

I send a low-frequency tremor through my limb, a hum that would be silent to your ears but which resonates through my entire frame like a bell. I am looking for the "stress-note" of the mineral. When I find it, the litho-coral responds. Over the next few weeks, the heat-sensitive membranes of the tower will feel my song and begin to lean—a slow, geological bow away from the center, opening the canyon to the light. 

This is my art: I am an architect of shadows.

### The Encounter: A Conversation of Weeks
Another Strider, an Elder named *Shifting-Cobalt*, occupies a bridge-root forty feet above me. We have been in conversation since the sun last reached its zenith. 

I look up, my multi-faceted eyes capturing the glint of his carapace. He does not wave; he does not speak. Instead, his entire abdomen begins to ripple with a slow, rhythmic tide of deep indigo, shot through with sparks of emerald. 

*“The obsidian is breathing deep today,”* his light says. *“The tectonic pulse is steady. The roots are warm.”*

I wait four hours before responding. I allow the thought to settle, to become part of my mineral makeup. Then, I let my internal hues shift from ochre to a soft, resonant gold, a pulse that mirrors the rhythm of his emerald sparks. 

*“I feel the heat in the deep flutes,”* I pulse back. *“The Marrow-Gleams are active. The city grows towards the south-east today. It seeks the morning glare.”*

This exchange—these two sentences—will take until sunset to complete. There is a profound holiness in this delay. To speak quickly is to be shallow; to react instantly is to be a slave to the moment. Here, we are masters of the eon.

### The Sensation: The Velvet Silence
The most striking thing about my existence is the weight of the silence. It is not an absence of sound, but a presence of stillness. I can feel the Ghost-Moths miles above, their kite-like wings pushing the warm volcanic air in a slow, rhythmic tide. I can feel the hum of the crystalline leaves as they vibrate with the sun’s heat—a low, sub-aural thrum that anchors me to the peak.

I do not know hunger. I do not know thirst. I know only the saturation of light and the slow, beautiful calcification of my own identity. 

### The End: The Long Permanence
I look toward the rim of the caldera, where the ancestors stand. They are no longer Striders; they are pillars of salt and glass, perfectly preserved against the obsidian cliffs. One day, many centuries from now, my gas will thin, my colors will fade into a permanent, clear transparency, and I will choose my spot. I will lock my limbs into the recursive lace of the city I helped tune, and I will stop moving forever.

I will become the architecture. I will become the foundation for the next Gardener to stand upon.

But for now, I am gold and ochre. I am the bridge between the sun and the stone. I adjust my needle-limb, send a new vibration into the ivory spire, and wait for the mountain to answer.
