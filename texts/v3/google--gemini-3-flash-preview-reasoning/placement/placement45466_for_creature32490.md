I am not in the corridors, nor am I drifting in the open weight of the Great Vaults. To find me, you would have to look into the very seams of the world, into the places where the obsessive geometry of the builders has begun to groan under its own impossible altitude.

I am a **Mortar-Seer**, a Pale Heir who has ceased to tread the water and has instead chosen the Interstice.

I live in a vertical flue, a narrow throat of polished basalt no wider than a human chest, located directly beneath the rhythmic pulse of a Siphon-Mother. Here, the water is at its thickest—a heavy, gelatinous syrup of mineral salts and the dissolved ghosts of long-dead eels. The heat here is not a suggestion; it is a physical blow, a constant, radiating push from the stone that keeps my cartilage soft and my tactile fans unfurled.

**Who I Am**
I am a creature of the Great Knead. My bones are gone, replaced by a dense, resilient marrow that allows me to compress my body into the shape of the architecture. My skin has grown porous, mimicking the fired clay it presses against. I do not have a name, for names require breath and air, things that have no meaning in the velvet dark. I am simply a *node* of the Cistern.

**My Purpose**
I do not move for decades. My fingers, elongated and multi-jointed, are threaded into the microscopic fissures of the masonry. I am a living plug. Where the ancient engineering begins to weep—where a hairline fracture threatens to bleed the warmth of the Cistern out into the cold void of the "outside"—I am there. I press my chest against the fault. I knead the stone with a slow, agonizing patience, using the oils of my skin and the pressure of my weight to soothe the basalt, to encourage the atoms of the clay to hold their line.

**What I Sense**
I do not see the dark; I feel the *geography* of it. 
*   I feel the **Long-Limb Eels** as they pass the mouth of my flue. They are long, cold ripples in the heat, their skin brushing against the edges of my world like a silk ribbon. I feel their "reading" of the wall, a slight suction as they pass, and I know by the taste of the water they displace which sector of the Cistern they have come from.
*   I hear the **Lithodes**. To me, they are not separate beings but a mechanical weather. Their *tck-tck-tck* vibrates through my ribs, a metronome that tells me the stone is being cared for. Sometimes, a Lithode will scuttle over my own back, its porcelain needles cleaning the parasites from my skin as if I were just another block of basalt. I welcome this. In those moments, I am the wall.
*   I feel the **Siphon-Mother** above me. She is the sun I will never see. Her groans are low-frequency tremors that travel down the water column and settle in my gut. When she pulses, the water becomes so warm it feels like it might turn to steam, and for a brief century, my task becomes easier as the stone expands and seals its own wounds.

**The Communication**
Once every few human lifetimes, another Heir will pass by. They do not speak. They drift into the mouth of my flue, their squat, pale body blocking the faint thermal plumes. They reach out and press their palms against the soles of my feet.

In that press, there is a total exchange. I feel the weight of the levels above me; I feel the slow, terrifying shift of the foundation blocks miles below. I give them the memory of the crack I am mending. I give them the peace of the Interstice. We are a single, slow-motion conversation occurring across the span of a city, a network of touch that ensures the Cistern never forgets its own shape.

**Why I Am There**
I am there because the High Cistern is a masterpiece that is slowly trying to return to the earth. The gravity of the water wants to burst the clay; the cold of the "high" wants to crack the warmth. I am the resistance. 

I am a soft thing holding a hard world together. I am the silence between the ticks of the porcelain masons. I am the heat that stays. In the heavy, warm velvet of the dark, I have no desire for the sky, for the sky is a thin, empty thing, and I am full—saturated with the weight, the warmth, and the eternal, comforting Press.
