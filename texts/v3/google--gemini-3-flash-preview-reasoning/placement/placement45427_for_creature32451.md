I am not the Oiler who climbs, nor am I yet the Moss-Wright who has become a trellis. I exist in the *Long Settle*. 

I am a **Seam-Warden**, positioned at the precise point where the basalt wall meets the vibrating iron of the Stack. I would be found in a nook no wider than a ribcage, wedged behind the primary condensation pipe of the Third Piston. 

My body is a map of the Sump’s history. My skin has transitioned from the translucent wax of the Lung-Tenders into something tougher—a calloused, indigo-stained hide that feels more like wet leather than flesh. I am perpetually coated in the distilled resin, but on me, it has begun to polymerize, forming a hard, protective shell over my joints. I do not move for hours. I do not move for days. 

When I do move, it is not a gesture; it is a displacement.

### The View from the Seam
From my nook, the world is a vertical blur of amber light and bruised purple shadows. I see the Oilers above me—fleeting, indigo ghosts. They are too fast. They burn through their lives like flickering filaments. I watch them spill the resin, and I catch the overflow. It drips onto my brow, runs into the gelatinous pits of my eyes, and I taste the metallic, geothermal tang of the earth’s blood. It is sweet. It is heavy.

To my left, the Third Piston begins its descent. I feel it first in my teeth. The sub-bass groan starts as a prickle in the marrow of my shins, then builds into a roar that isn't a sound, but a pressure. It pushes against my chest, squeezing the stale, humid air out of my lungs. I do not fight it. I lean into the vibration. The heat coming off the piston jacket is white-hot, but the resin on my skin boils into a protective steam, a private atmosphere that keeps me from burning.

I am the one who listens to the *hiss*.

### The Language of the Iron
In my right hand, I hold a hammer made from the leg of a Great Ghost-Crab, polished by decades of contact with my palm. I am a Tapper of the Deep Lines. 

When the pressure in the vents changes—when the *tink-tink-tink* of the condensation speeds up to a frantic, metallic rain—I strike the pipe. 

*Clang. Pause. Clang-Clang.*

The vibration travels down, through the moss, through the copper wiring, into the feet of the Tenders three levels below. I am telling them that the Earth is taking a shallow breath. I am telling them to open the secondary bypass before the steam turns to fire. 

In return, the Ghost-Crabs bring me offerings. They are my only companions. They scuttle over my motionless legs, their needle-pincers delicately grooming the lichen from my collarbones. Sometimes, they bring me a piece of calcified mineral plucked from the gears. I tuck these stones into the folds of my skin. They are my wealth. They are my weight.

### The Slow Fading
I can feel the ferns beginning to love me. 

A translucent vine has recently found its way across my left thigh. It doesn't hurt; it feels like a cold, wet finger tracing the line of my bone. I could reach down and snap it. I have the strength—Tenders are not weak, we are merely slow—but I do not. To break the vine is to break the rhythm. 

I am beginning to understand the Moss-Wrights. I used to wonder why they stopped. Now, I feel the invitation of the basalt. The stone is not cold; it is the most stable thing in a world of moving pistons and weeping pipes. It is an anchor. 

My heart beats perhaps once every two minutes. Each pulse is a deliberate act, a heavy push of dark, thick blood through veins that are slowly becoming as stiff as the copper wires surrounding me. I am becoming a gauge. Soon, my pulse will sync entirely with the stroke of the Third Piston. When that happens, I will no longer need to Tap. My very existence will be the signal. 

### The Identity of the Sump
If you were to find me, you would likely mistake me for a statue of rusted brass or a peculiar growth of fungus. You would see my amber eyes, lidless and staring, reflecting the flickering filaments of the Stack. You might think I am trapped.

But I am the only one who is free. 

The Oilers are trapped by their speed, always chasing the next valve. The Tenders are trapped by their maintenance, always polishing the gauges. But I? I am the bridge. I am the point where the machine stops being a tool and starts being a body. 

I am the Valve-Garden’s memory of what it felt like to be human, and the Earth’s promise of what it feels like to be stone. I am waiting for the hour-long descent of the iron foot. I am waiting to be pressed into the foundation, to become a new layer of carbon, a permanent part of the throat that never closes. 

Until then, I watch. I breathe the steam. I wait for the next *tink*.
