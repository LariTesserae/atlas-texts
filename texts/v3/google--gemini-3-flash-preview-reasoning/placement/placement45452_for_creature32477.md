I would be found where the "now" meets the "next"—at the very tip of a primary rib, where the obsidian is still warm from the pressure of its own birth. 

I am a **Suturist**.

My body is a long, shivering needle of slate-colored bone. My skin is not merely damp; it is a map of the Spire’s weeping, stained with the iron-red and copper-green of the mineral sweat. I do not live in the dense, barnacled clusters of the middle-tiers, where the timber-framed huts huddle for warmth. I live in the transition. I live in the spray.

I am currently anchored to a vertical prism of obsidian that is still "soft"—it feels like cold tallow under my palms. I have no rope of my own; instead, I have coaxed three **Dew-Striders** to settle near my shoulders. Their silk, silver and translucent, is anchored to a hardened shelf above me and woven into the harness around my hips. We are a single organism of tension and grip.

My task is the *Invitation*. 

The Spire is pushing a new spur out into the blue void, a jagged tongue of matter that has not yet crystallized. If left to its own devices, it might shatter under the weight of the falling water or grow into a shape that denies a foothold. I am here to guide the geometry. With a chisel of hardened kite-bone, I score a faint, rhythmic line across the breathing stone. I am not cutting; I am whispering a suggestion to the rock. I am telling it where to crack, where to hold, and where to become a shelf for the next generation.

The sound here is a physical weight. The tectonic *thrum* of the Spire vibrates through my chest, a deep, cello-note that bypasses my ears and speaks directly to my marrow. It tells me the mountain is hungry to grow. The roar of the water falling past me—a vertical river that never touches the ground—is a white curtain of noise that isolates me from the rest of my kind. 

I see a neighbor, three tiers up and a hundred yards across the abyss. She is a *Lithos-Shaper*, older than I, her skin almost the same matte black as the aged stone. She doesn't call out. She raises a hand, her long fingers flickering in a sequence of rapid, staccato taps against the rock. 

*Pulse. Pulse. Drift.* 

I feel the message through the stone before I see the movement of her hands. She is warning me of a thermal shift. The **Vitreous Kites** are spiraling lower, their glass-clear wings catching the light like shards of a broken mirror. They are fleeing the upper reaches. A growth-spasm is coming.

I do not panic. Panic is a horizontal emotion; it requires space to run. Here, there is only the grip.

I press my cheek against the damp obsidian. It is humming. I whistle a sharp, ascending note—a piercing silver needle of sound that cuts through the roar of the water—to signal the Dew-Striders. They tighten their silk. The tension in the lines increases until I am pulled flush against the Spire, my short, square nails finding purchase in the fissures I carved only an hour ago.

I am a gardener of the impossible. I am a witness to the moment the void becomes something solid. 

As the Spire groans, a deep, subterranean sound like a continent snapping in half, I feel the stone beneath me shift. It moves only a fraction of an inch, but it is a violent, birthing heat. A new crack opens near my hand, weeping a fresh, hot stream of mineral-rich water. 

I do not recoil. I reach out with my broad, calloused palm and press it against the new wound. I am the first thing this part of the world has ever touched. I am the bridge between the empty sky and the rising stone. I wait in the cold, translucent blue, listening to the mountain decide what it wants to be, and I offer it my silence as a guide.
