**The Experience of Living**
To be a Nerve-Filament is to be a master of the "Slow Now."

I live in the grace of the microscopic. I feel the **Mist-Drinkers** dock with the fountains—a sudden, heavy dampening of the vibration, like a mute being placed on a string.

I feel the weight of the city pressing down into the dark. I feel the ancient, frozen air of the mountain’s throat pushing back up. Sometimes, in the deepest part of the night when the sun-membranes are black, I feel a vibration that does not belong to the Atrium. It is the mountain itself—the slow, tectonic groan of the earth. When that happens, I tighten. I pull the Atrium closer to the rock, a desperate, silent hug of survival.

**My End**
I know that I am aging when I begin to "calcify." The Alabaster Pruners are my gods and my executioners. Eventually, the cold will win, and my silver-white silk will become too brittle to stretch. I will stop vibrating. I will become "still."

I can feel the Pruners now, skittering nearby. They aren't looking for me yet. They are cleaning a yellowed edge of a pitcher-structure. But one day, I will feel their acidic breath on my membrane. They will dissolve my stiffness, and my minerals will be pumped back into the Pulse-Warden to become a new, more flexible strand.

I do not fear this. In the Cold Atrium, to be used is to be alive. I am the tension. I am the listener. I am the thread that ensures the cathedral of frost does not lose its grip on the sky. I am a single, shivering note in a symphony that never intends to end.
