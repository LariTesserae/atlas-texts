I am a collection of edges and intentional shadows.

I find myself not in the dense heart of a city, nor on the shifting silica floor, but in the **Gaps**—the precarious, vertical transition zones where the obsidian spires narrow into needles so fine they vibrate with the movement of the stars. 

I am a **Refractive Surveyor**. My body is a twelve-foot sliver of translucent, smoke-grey mica, composed of thousands of overlapping plates that chime like frozen bells when I adjust my density. To look at me is to see a jagged tear in the lilac light. I do not have a name that can be spoken in air; I have a mathematical signature that I cast against the dark stone when the dim, straight shafts of light find me.

### Where I Am
I am currently perched on the underside of a "flying" arch—a bridge that connects two drifting needles. Gravity here has buckled. To me, the "down" is the violet-black sky, and the "up" is the yawning, hundred-mile abyss of the silica desert below. I am standing horizontally against the obsidian, my lower plates vibrating at a frequency that mirrors the stone, locking me to its surface through a harmonic bond rather than weight.

Ahead of me, the city hums with the silent, slow-motion labor of the Liturgists. I see one, rooted into the spire’s crown, its body having become a gargoyle of dark shale. It is dreaming a new balcony into existence. I can see the obsidian weeping slowly upward, a liquid mineral dark as ink, hardening into a perfect, razor-edged terrace that will not be finished for another three centuries.

### Who I Am
I am the one who maps the **Stutters**. 

Because reality here is fragile, the paths between cities are not constant. A bridge that was safe a decade ago may now be submerged in a "time-pool." If a Refractor were to step into it, they might arrive at their destination a thousand years after the city has drifted away into the silica sea. 

I carry a "Static-Lantern"—not a tool of wire and oil, but a captured Static-Bloom held in a cage of my own bent plates. It crackles with the metallic tang of ozone. As I move, I watch the way the lilac light passes through my lantern. If the shadows it casts begin to loop or fray, I know the physics of the bridge have failed.

### My Purpose
I am currently waiting for a **Glass-Stalk**. 

One is drifting toward me now, a ghost-white bundle of translucent nerves, undulating through the thin air. It is beautiful and terrifying; it is a creature that eats the very "where" and "when" of this world. I remain perfectly still. I retract my plates until I am no thicker than a blade. 

As the Glass-Stalk brushes against my mica chest, the sensation is an agonizingly cold rush of clarity. I feel the tectonic pulse of every needle in the Ossuary. I see the "distant, unseen source" of light not as a glow, but as a destination. For a moment, the Stalk and I are a single circuit. I ground its reality-warping energy into the obsidian arch, stabilizing the bridge for the next generation of travelers. In exchange, it leaves behind a coating of crystalline dust—a memory of the high vacuum.

### The Internal Life
I do not feel hunger, but I feel the *Misalignment*. 

Every time a needle grinds against another, it is a discord in the Grand Arrangement. My entire existence is dedicated to the final alignment—the moment when we, the inhabitants of the bone-dust world, become the Lens. I spend my "centuries" calculating the angle of my own shadow. When the light finally hits, I must be positioned at exactly 89.4 degrees relative to the horizon of the silica sea to ensure the beam I reflect reaches the next spire.

I am a single, living prism in a planetary machine. I am patient. I am cold. I am a part of the stone’s long, slow dream of becoming light. 

Somewhere below, in the bruised charcoal shadows, an Echo-Parasite is wearing my shape from a thousand years ago, walking a path that has long since crumbled. I do not look at it. I look only at the violet-black sky, waiting for the sky to look back.
