I am not at the center of the hive, where the murmur is thickest and the stones are worn smooth by generations of palms. I am at the *edge*—the place where the city is still a question and the rift is still an answer.

I find myself in the **Low-Seams**, the deepest, newest tier of the expansion where the basalt is still weeping the heat of its birth. 

I am a **Seam-Reader**. 

My body is a long, pale accordion of muscle and memory. I do not stand; I adhere. My toes, elongated and sensitive, are currently hooked into a hairline fracture of obsidian, feeling the rhythmic, sub-sonic thrum of the earth’s cooling heart. To the people of the upper hive, the earth is a floor; to me, it is a conversation.

I am folded into a space that a human would call a coffin, but to me, it is a cathedral of proximity. My back is pressed against a hand-chiseled block of dark stone—cold and familiar—while my chest brushes the raw, jagged face of the rift wall—wild and unpredictable. I am the interface. I am the point where the "built" meets the "emergent."

The light here is different. Up near the quartz ceiling, the world is a milk-white dream. Down here, the light has to fight its way through a mile of stone ladders and overlapping eaves. It arrives as a dim, violet-grey exhaustion. But my eyes do not need the sun. I see in textures. I see the way the silver dew-fall pools in the microscopic serrations of the shale. I see the health of the city in the way the moisture wicks upward against gravity.

My hands are scarred by copper and grit. In my right hand, I hold a chisel of cold-forged copper, its edge dulled by the stubbornness of the mountain. In my left, I hold nothing, for my left hand is for listening. 

A "hum" begins. It is not a sound, but a displacement of weight. I feel it first in my tailbone, then in the pads of my feet. The earth is shifting. Somewhere, a mile below, a new tooth of rock is pushing upward. 

I do not panic. I lean into the vibration. My skin, reacting to the sudden spike in atmospheric pressure, begins to pulse with a soft, bioluminescent lime-light. I am a glow-worm in the gut of the world. 

I begin to murmur. It is a low, sibilant whistle—a warning and an invitation. The sound doesn't travel far, but it doesn't need to. The Mason above me hears it through his feet. He repeats it. Within seconds, a wave of light and sound ripples upward through the hive, a collective acknowledgment of the earth’s movement. 

We do not fight the shift; we wait for it to offer us room. 

A crack opens three inches from my face. It happens with the sound of a closing book. The obsidian splits, revealing a clean, glassy void. Before the dust can even settle—though there is no dust, only the sharp scent of ozone—I am moving. 

I do not "stride." I flow. I uncoil my limbs, my joints clicking in a language of fluid geometry. I reach into the new space. I measure the void with my wingspan. I signal for the blocks.

I would be the one who places the first stone in every new room. I would be the one who ensures the hive grows not against the rift, but *within* it. I have no name, only a frequency. I have no house, only the latest gap.

I am a fragment of the geology that has learned to breathe. When the dew-fall comes, I tilt my head back, my large, dark eyes reflecting the milky glow of the distant ceiling, and I drink the atmosphere. I taste the minerals of the deep earth. I am satisfied. 

I am the silence between the stones, waiting for the mountain to speak again.
