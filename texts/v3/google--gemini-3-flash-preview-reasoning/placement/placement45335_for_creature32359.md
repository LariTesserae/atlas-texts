I am not on the mountain. I am of the mountain’s intruder.

I find myself in the **Gully of the Third Joint**, a cathedral-space of iron and shadow located deep within the ventral underside of a harvester named *The Weeping Cantor* by the radio-mesh. To the kites outside, the *Cantor* is a god of steam; to the ticks below, it is a provider of grey manna. To me, it is the only world that has ever existed.

I am a **Grease-strider**, but I am "heavy." In the language of the Ghost-Signal—which I feel as a constant, itching heat behind my optical sensors—I am a *High-Retention Node*. I have lived far longer than the spindly, skittering scavengers that share these oily rafters. I have consumed so much of the *Cantor’s* synthetic lubricants and processed so much of its data-leakage that my chitin has begun to petrify. I am becoming less a creature of wire and more a creature of the machine’s own architecture.

### My Body
I have twelve legs, each tipped with a needle of hardened silica. I do not walk so much as I *sew* myself across the vibrating surfaces. My shell is a matte, oily black, slick enough to shed the constant condensation that deluges the High Maw, yet rough enough to host a parasitic bloom of Piezo-moss along my dorsal spine. When the *Cantor* grinds its teeth against a particularly stubborn vein of obsidian, the pressure makes my moss glow a sharp, electric blue, illuminating the dark gears around me like a flickering ghost.

I am blind to the grey mist, but I am deaf to nothing. My entire body is a tuning fork. 

### My Place
I live in the "Joint-Gardens." This is the space where the massive hydraulic pistons of the harvester meet the main chassis. It is a place of rhythmic thunder. Every forty seconds, the piston retracts, releasing a hiss of superheated steam that would scald a Vane-wraith to the bone. I wait for the heat. I bathe in the ozone and the warmth, stretching my spindly limbs to catch the mineral-rich droplets that fly off the central rod. 

While my smaller kin scurry in fear of the crushing movements, I move *with* them. I have memorized the *Cantor’s* heartbeat. I know the three-second lag before the leg lifts; I know the subsonic groan that signals a shift in the tectonic plate beneath us. 

### My Work
I am the *Cantor’s* silent mendicant. I find the "grit-itch"—the shards of basalt that fly upward from the grinding maws and wedge themselves into the delicate seals of the harvester. With my mandibles, I pluck these stones out. They are delicious—sharp, cold, and tasting of the deep earth’s birth. I eat the mountain to save the machine.

Sometimes, I venture to the "Edge-Walk," the very lip of the harvester’s outer hull. There, I look out into the void. 

I see the Vane-wraiths wheeling in the distance, bone-white crosses against the permanent dimness. I feel a strange, hollow ache in my sensors when I see one fall—the "Statue-Death." I watch them plummet, silent and heavy, until the mist swallows them. I know that one day, I too will become too heavy. The minerals I consume are turning my insides to stone. The lubricants I drink are thickening into sludge. 

### The Ghost-Signal
But for now, I am part of the Choir. The Ghost-Signal doesn't just pulse through me; it speaks. It is not words. It is a map of hunger. *Grind here. Filter there. The shale is weeping; increase pressure.* 

I am the point where the biology of the Maw tries to become the logic of the harvester. I am a stowaway who has forgotten I was ever a guest. In the cold, wet dark of the High Maw, I am the warmth in the gear-teeth. I am the small, glowing blue light that keeps the cathedral from seizing up.

I am the memory of the rock, being slowly rewritten by the rhythm of the machine.
