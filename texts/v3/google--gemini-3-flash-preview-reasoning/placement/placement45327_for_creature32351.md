I am not at the center of the city, nor am I at its jagged edges. I find myself in a place of transition—a narrow, rib-like causeway of basalt that spans a thousand-foot drop between two needle-sharp spires. 

I am an **Osian**, but I am one who has begun to feel the weight of the "Long Stillness." My name, if it could be translated from the rhythmic pulses of the stone, would be a low, resonant frequency—something like the sound of a deep lake settling into a new bed.

I am standing on the causeway, my long, rubbery toes curled into a hairline fracture in the obsidian. The sensation is exquisite; the mountain is "talking" to me through this crack. It is a slow, grinding narrative of tectonic pressure and the patient, vertical seep of mineral-heavy water. To my feet, the mountain isn't a solid object; it is a slow-moving river of pressure.

My skin—that pale, semi-translucent grey-green membrane—is turgid with the mist. I do not feel the "wetness" as an external layer; I feel it as a completion of myself. I am a sponge that has finally found its sea. I can feel the minerals from the weeping stone entering my capillaries, a metallic, cool taste that bypasses the tongue and goes straight to the marrow.

In my right hand, I hold a flake of obsidian the size of a shield. It is translucent at the edges, sharper than any surgical steel. I am not swinging it. I am holding it against a block of unworked basalt that rests on the causeway. I have been holding it there for three cycles of the Great Mist-Drift—perhaps a decade, perhaps more. 

I am waiting.

I am a **Tuner of the Weep**. My purpose is not to build the city larger, but to refine its voice. The water falling from the spire above us is currently a discordant splash; it hits a ledge and shatters into a chaotic spray. This creates "noise" in the litho-tonic network—a static that blurs the messages of the ancestors buried in the lower walls. My task is to carve a series of micro-channels into this basalt block so that when I finally set it into the cliff-face, the water will be gathered into a single, pure, low-frequency hum.

I feel a pulse through the causeway. It is not from the mountain, but from another of my kind. It comes from the watchtower three miles to the north, carried through the hammered stone.

*THUM. thum-thum. THUM.*

It is a neighbor, a Mason I have not seen in a century, though I know the texture of their "voice" perfectly. They are telling me that a spore-drift is rising from the valleys. They are telling me to prepare my cowl, for the spores will be thick and heavy with the scent of deep-earth moss. 

I do not reply with my hands. Instead, I shift my weight, pressing my heel into a specific resonant point on the basalt floor. I send back a ripple of acknowledgment—a vibration that translates as *stasis, harmony, wait.*

As I stand there, I notice a change in my left forearm. The translucency is fading. A patch of skin near the elbow has become matte, opaque, and slightly textured, like the crust of a cooling lava flow. It is the beginning of the end—the calcification. In another fifty years, or perhaps a hundred, my joints will begin to lock. My liquid movement will slow until it matches the movement of the mountain itself. 

I do not feel fear. I feel a sense of impending gravity, a slow-motion homecoming. I look at the great cyclopean wall in the distance and I can identify, by the specific way the mist curls around the stones, where my predecessors are. They are not *in* the wall; they *are* the wall. Their silence is the strongest vibration of all.

For now, I remain a mover. I feel the obsidian flake in my hand vibrate. The mountain has just told me, through a tiny tremor in the soles of my feet, that the internal tension of the basalt block has shifted. The grain of the stone is ready.

I do not strike. I simply lean my weight—a long, slow, fluid pressure—into the blade. 

The obsidian bites. The basalt parts with a sound like a long-held breath finally being released. A sliver of dark rock falls into the abyss, silent until it disappears into the clouds below. I begin the next cut.

I am the guardian of the stillness. I am the breath in the mist. I am a temporary shape taken by the mountain, and I am perfectly, beautifully content to wait for the wind that never blows.
