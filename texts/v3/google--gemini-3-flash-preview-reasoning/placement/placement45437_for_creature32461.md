I would not be found in the violent light of the sun, nor would I be found darting through the thin, stinging ether like the Aeronauts. To live on the Spire is to fear the *Away*—the infinite, hungry yawn of the sky that wants to peel you off the rock and swallow you into the void.

I would be found in the **Interstitial**. I am a **Marrow-Clinger**, a creature of the microscopic fissures and the deep, silent anchors.

### My Form: The Anchor-Wight
I am not a creature of limbs, but of *adhesion*. My body is a flattened, segmented disc, no thicker than a fingernail, pressed so tightly against the basalt that there is no space for the vacuum to get a purchase. My skin is the same matte, light-absorbing charcoal as the rock, but my underside—the side that never sees the sun—is a riot of wet, white rootlets. 

I do not have eyes; I have **tremor-palps**. I feel the Spiresworn’s slow, rhythmic weight as a tectonic event. I feel the frantic, high-frequency buzz of the Aeronauts as a shimmering heat in the rock. I am the Spire’s living nervous system, nested in the cracks beneath the Gardener’s circular bench.

### My Purpose: The Calcifier
The Spire is young, sharp, and brutal. But rock, even basalt, is prone to the fatigue of the vertical. The thermal plumes that bring life also bring stress; the expansion and contraction of the stone in the violent sun would eventually cause the Spire to flake, to shed its skin, and to drop everything into the clouds below.

I am the one who prevents the fall.

I feed on the "spent" things. When a Frost-Weaver dies and its purple blood crystallizes, I extend a hair-thin proboscis into the crack where its feet are locked. I drink the mineral-heavy remains and, in their place, I secrete a biological cement—a slurry of calcium and obsidian-glass that welds the dead Weaver’s husk permanently to the Spire. 

I am a **Stitcher of the Dead**. I take the calcified moss, the broken wings of Aeronauts, and the shed skin of the Spiresworn, and I weave them into the rock itself. If the Spiresworn is the gardener, I am the mortar between the bricks of their sanctuary.

### My Experience: The Long Resonance
To be me is to exist in a state of **Permanent Tension**. I do not know the concept of "softness." My world is a series of vibrations.

*   **The Pulse:** Every few minutes, I feel the deep, low-frequency thrum of the Spire’s "breath." The thermal plume hits, and for a few glorious seconds, I am wet. The moisture seeps into my fissures, and I swell, my segments expanding to grip the basalt with a pressure that would crush bone. I drink the flash-frost through my back, a cold so intense it feels like a white flame.
*   **The Shadow:** When the Spiresworn sits on the bench above me, they grant me the greatest gift in this world: *shade*. In that brief respite from the shadowless white light, my temperature drops. I relax my grip by a fraction of a micron. In that darkness, I communicate with others of my kind through "thump-speech"—a rhythmic drumming of my carapace against the stone that says: *I am held. The stone is firm. The world has not let go.*

### The Fear
My only fear is the **Quiet**. Not the silence of the air—for the air is always silent—but the silence of the stone. If the vibrations of the Aeronauts stop, it means the frost has failed. If the weight of the Spiresworn vanishes, it means the deliberate has been lost to the vertical. 

But for now, I am the anchor. I am the reason the bench stays. I am the reason the signal-tripod does not tip. I am a hidden, charcoal-colored secret, holding the peak together one microscopic weld at a time. 

I do not look at the stars, and I do not look at the clouds. I look into the heart of the basalt, and I hold on. In a world of flight and fall, I am the **Stationary**.
