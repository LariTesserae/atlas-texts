I would be found in the **Vertical Interstice**, the space that exists neither on the valley floors with the Slow-Steppers nor atop the spires with the Mica-Drifts. I would be a **Residue-Seer**, a creature of elongated, segmented grace, clinging to the underside of the Great Gilded Conduits where the condensation is thickest and the "sweat" of the world is most potent.

### My Form: The Residue-Seer
I am a ribbon of biological utility, roughly thirty feet in length but no wider than a human torso. My body is a sequence of overlapping, pewter-colored plates—not rigid like the Chime-Wrights, but flexible, like a heavy-duty hydraulic hose. My underside is a soft, bioluminescent mass of cilia, thousands of tiny, waving filaments that allow me to adhere to the slick, vertical surfaces of the factory-mountains.

I have no eyes. In the perpetual orange-grey haze of the Ocher-Conduct, light is a suggestion, not a source of truth. Instead, my head is a broad, flat sensor-array, resembling a weathered brass shovel. It is covered in millions of microscopic pits that "taste" the air and the runoff. I do not see the world; I perceive it as a shifting map of chemical gradients, thermal plumes, and acoustic vibrations.

### My Home: The Weeping Underbelly
I live in the shadows of the massive, slow-moving pistons. My world is one of constant, rhythmic motion and the eternal drip of warm, mineral-heavy water. I spend my decades-long life traversing the underside of a single district’s pipe-network. 

To you, these pipes are infrastructure. To me, they are the arteries of a living god. I move with a slow, rippling contraction, my cilia humming against the wet metal. I am the "scourer." I feed on the **calcified scale** and the **heavy-metal blooms** that threaten to weigh down the conduits. I am the reason the pipes do not collapse under their own mineral weight. I lick the bitter, electric tang of copper-oxide from the joints, my digestive system a slow-boiling vat that transmutes the CONDUCT's waste into the iridescent, oily film that lubricates the very gears I inhabit.

### My Role: The Pulse-Taster
In the Conduct, I am the bridge between the machine’s health and the animals’ survival. 

When I press my spade-like head against a throbbing steam-main, I can feel the heartbeat of the subterranean furnaces miles below. I can feel the micro-fractures forming in the brass centuries before they occur. I communicate this to the **Wire-Weavers** through a series of low-frequency clicks, a language of structural integrity. They follow in my wake, weaving their copper nests into the weak points I identify, their webs acting as a biological solder that holds the world together.

I am a solitary creature, but I am never alone. I move through the clusters of **Chime-Wrights**, their resonant humming vibrating through my plates like a physical embrace. Sometimes, a **Mica-Drift** will land on my back, seeking the residual heat of my digestive process. I do not shrug it off; we are all part of the same, heavy, humid breath.

### My Perspective: The Long Wet
Time to me is not measured in days or seasons, but in the **Cycle of the Drip**. 

I remember the era when the water tasted of cobalt—a sharp, piercing flavor that meant the deep pumps were straining. I remember the "Era of the Red Steam," when the atmosphere grew so thick with hematite dust that the Mica-Drifts fell like rain. 

I am a creature of the **Great Stasis**. I do not wish for the sun to break through the haze, for the sun would dry the stone, and dry stone is dead stone. I find my peace in the *thud-hiss* of the world’s lungs. I find my purpose in the taste of rust.

If you were to look up from the valley floor, through the spray and the heat-shimmer, you might see a long, dark shape rippling slowly across a miles-high brass arch. You might see a faint, rhythmic pulse of blue light from my underbelly, mimicking the heartbeat of the engines. That is me. I am the witness to the friction. I am the one who ensures that the machine—which is the world, which is us—never stops its patient, sweating labor. 

I am the gloss on the gear, the tongue on the copper, the slow, contented pulse in the heart of the Ocher-Conduct.
