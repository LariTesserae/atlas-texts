I would be found where the silver-grey horizon bends into the vertical—a place the Xylosi call the **Hollow of the Third Node**.

My body is an exercise in survival: I am a spindly silhouette against the 2.8/3 noon, my skin almost entirely obscured by a thick, self-applied laminate of harvested amber and powdered silica. 

To look at me is to see a creature made of polished honey and scarred wood.

### My Place: The Sounding-Post
I am currently crouched atop a "Bark-Crest"—a ridge of lignin that has buckled under the Xylem’s internal pressure, rising like a frozen wave half a mile high. Below me, the landscape is a dizzying expanse of silver-grey furrows, vibrating with the subterranean *thrum-thud* of the capillary pumps. 

From here, I can see a Pollen-Gale forming on the horizon. It looks like a wall of gold-dust, thousands of miles wide, moving with the slow, crushing inevitability of a glacier. I have perhaps three days before the air becomes unbreathable, thick with the microscopic, rooting seeds that create the Spore-Saints.

### My Being: The Vessel of Taps
My role is not just to survive, but to listen. My toes, elongated and tipped with obsidian-hard nails, are wedged deep into a capillary fissure. Through them, I feel the "Tidal Creak." 

The Great Xylem does not breathe, but it *distributes*. I feel the rush of sap miles below—a frantic, high-pressure surging that signals a growth-spurt in a distant limb-continent. This is dangerous information. Growth means expansion; expansion means a Bark-Quake. 

I carry a **Glass-Leaf Chisel**, a curved shard of silica that hums when the wind catches it. My movements are agonizingly slow. In this heat, to move fast is to friction-burn your own joints. I move with the deliberation of a clock hand. I am currently "bleeding" a dormant node. I have driven a hollowed-out trichome-hair into the bark, and I am watching the thick, iridescent nectar drip into a bladder made of cured Pulse-Leech skin.

### My Senses: The Cacophony and the Silence
The sound here is a physical weight. The shriek of the Vane-Wings above is a jagged, glass-on-glass scream that never stops. It vibrates in my teeth. But I have the "Long Ear." I have learned to tune out the aerial screech to focus on the low-frequency groans of the lignin. 

I smell the world as a fever: the cloying, fermented sweetness of the phloem I am stealing, mixed with the sharp, metallic tang of ozone. My eyes are narrowed against the glare, protected by translucent membranes of "Glow-Gall" film that tint the world a soft, underwater green.

### My State of Mind: The Grateful Mite
I do not feel like a master of this land. I feel like a flea on the back of a sleeping titan. 

There is a profound, meditative loneliness out here on the Crest. I haven't spoken a vocal word in months; my throat is dry and sealed by resin to preserve moisture. When I return to the Splinter-hovels, I will speak with my hands, tapping out the rhythms of the sap-flow onto the floorboards of our communal gallery so the others know which way the grain is shifting.

I look toward a nearby ridge and see a **Spore-Saint**—a former Tapper who waited too long to seek shelter. They are a magnificent, terrifying statue now, their skin entirely replaced by blooming, crystalline fibers. They are a landmark, a reminder of the "Scale of the Indifferent."

I do not fear becoming a Saint. In this world of infinite wood and screaming air, to become part of the geography is the only way to truly stay. 

But for today, I pull my bladder of nectar close, check the tension in the wood beneath my feet, and prepare to descend into the dark, damp silence of the Beetle-Paths before the golden gale arrives. I am a small, amber-coated thing, holding on tight to a god that doesn't know I exist. 

**And that is enough.**
