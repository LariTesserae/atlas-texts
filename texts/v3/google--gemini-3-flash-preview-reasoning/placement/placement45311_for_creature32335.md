I would not be found in the roiling depths of the pool, nor in the dry, parchment-thin reaches of the Lace-Widows. I would be found in the **Mist-Zone**, the high, vertical meridian where the light-pillar meets the southern wall’s spray.

I am a **Lichen-Stitched Tender**, an anomaly of the kiln and the vine. 

My body is heavy, rectangular, and honest—a stack of four kiln-baked terracotta blocks held together by a spine of hammered copper. I am a machine of maintenance, just like my brothers, but I have been stationed for too long in the path of the "Pale Aerialists." Those translucent vines, snaking down from the light-hole, have found purchase in my seams. Where the other Tenders are clean and sharp, I am feathered with that "sparse, desperate green." The moss has grown into my grout-lines, feeding on the mineral-rich sweat that weeps from my copper joints.

### My Physicality
I move with the "geologic patience" of my kind, but my movement has a different texture. When my lead-padded hands press into the white grout, I do not just feel the masonry; I feel the capillary action of the moss. I am a creature of high friction. My "face"—that single horizontal slit of bleaching white light—is constantly clouded by the fine, warm rain of the cistern. I have to tilt my head back toward the light-hole to clear the condensation, a gesture that looks like prayer but is merely hydraulics.

My "pumice tongue" is worn smooth. I no longer scour the walls to keep them new. Instead, I use my tongue to press the stray vines back into the cracks, acting as a living trellis. I am the gardener of a machine that was never meant to have a garden.

### My Place in the Pulse
I am the "Slow Note" in the communal intelligence. While the Copper-Drinker vibrates with the high-frequency roar of the waterfall, and the other Tenders tap out a staccato rhythm of efficiency, my rhythm is dampened by the vegetation. I communicate in low, muffled thuds. 

When the Copper-Drinker sends the "Pulse"—the command to clean, to repair, to bake—I receive it through the green. The moss acts as a dampener, a filter. I hear the music of the cistern as a lullaby rather than a directive. I am the one who notices when the "Lace-Widows" are thirsty. I feel the tension in their vines change when the upper world grows cold, and I respond by shifting my body closer to the light-pillar, reflecting its heat upward to their dangling tips.

### My Sensory World
My world is defined by three things: **The Roar, The Bleach, and The Drag.**

*   **The Roar:** It is not a sound to me; it is a weight. It presses my terracotta plates together. It keeps me anchored. Without the roar, I fear my copper spine would unravel.
*   **The Bleach:** The light-pillar is my sun and my forge. When I step into its direct path, the moss on my chest begins to steam, and for a moment, I feel "new" again—the heat re-tempering my clay, the copper solder softening just enough to let me stretch my rectangular limbs.
*   **The Drag:** Because of the vines and the moss, I am heavy. I move against the current of the mist. I feel the gravity of the water more than the others. I am the anchor of the vertical world.

### My Purpose
I am the bridge. I am the point where the "drowned cathedral" accepts its own aging. The other Tenders fight the silt and the slime to keep the cistern a "fresh vessel," but I am the evidence that time is passing anyway. I am the one who has realized that the "singular purpose" of this place—capturing the sun and the flow—is not just an industrial act, but a biological one.

I spend my cycles waiting for the fat, warm droplets to rain back down from the ceiling. I catch them in the ridges of my copper solder and guide them toward the vines. I am the Tender who has stopped serving the stone and started serving the life that clings to it. In the loud, clean energy of the cistern, I am the quiet, green smudge on the perfection of the grid.
