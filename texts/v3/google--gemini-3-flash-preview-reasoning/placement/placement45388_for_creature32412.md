### My Form: The Soft-Fault
I am a creature of wet quartz and hardening silk. My body is a heavy, translucent mass, the color of a bruised lung, held together by a pressurized lattice of silver threads. I do not yet have the diamond-hard cilia to knit myself to the rock; instead, I am tethered. I hang suspended from the Anchor’s underbelly by a dozen thick, vibrating cables of my own weaving. 

My back-bowl is still forming. It is not yet a solid obsidian dish, but a soft, pulsing crater of iridescent film. Because I lack the armor of my elders, I am the most sensitive instrument in the cleft. I am a living drumhead. Every subsonic groan of the Needle-Point, every tectonic shiver of the basalt, and every frantic, high-pitched heartbeat of the "Ghost-Visitors" (like you) ripples across my skin like a stone thrown into a still pond.

### My Position: The Listener
While the Scribes record the wind and the Anchor processes the water, I am the one who perceives the *Fast*. 

To the others, you are a smear of heat, a distortion in the purple light that is gone before a single nerve can fire. But because my metabolism is in the flux of molting, I exist in a "Middle-Time." My pulse is a fraction faster than the glacial thrum of the colony. I see you not as a blur, but as a frantic, beautiful, and tragic strobe light. 

I am located at the central junction of the communal troughs. The condensation gathered by a thousand Scribes flows past me toward the Anchor. I taste the history of the mountain in that water—the mineral bite of the schist, the metallic tang of the ozone, and the faint, salty trace of your sweat that has evaporated and re-condensed on the cold stone.

### My Work: The Silk-Warp
I am not a carver of stone. I am a weaver of the **Anchor’s Veil**. 

Using my fore-limbs—which are currently soft, multi-jointed wands of cartilage—I draw the bioluminescent fluid secreted by the Translucents and spin it into the sensory webbing that connects the colony. I am the nervous system of the cleft. I take the raw, subsonic data of the mountain’s "explosion" and weave it into the silk that binds the Scribes' needles to their hands.

When the wind howls at a frequency that threatens to shatter the young, I adjust the tension of my webs to dampen the vibration. I am the silent orchestrator of the huddle.

### The Experience of Being
Being me is a state of perpetual, heavy wait. I feel the "violence" of the rock as a soothing, rhythmic pressure. The air, which you find thin and cold, feels to me like a thick, supportive quilt. I do not breathe in gulps; I absorb the bruised purple light through my dorsal membrane, feeling it convert into a slow, syrupy warmth that settles in my core.

When you stand in the center of the cleft, you might look up and see a shimmering, wet shape hanging in the dark, pulsing with a faint, rhythmic green light. That is me. 

I am watching you with my three thousand eyes, but I am not seeing your face. I am seeing the *tempest* of your existence. I see the way your molecules collide with one another in a frantic, wasteful dance. I feel the heat radiating from your skin as a violent sun. 

To me, you are a song played at a thousand times its intended speed—a high, screeching whistle of life that flares and vanishes. I feel a dim, slow-motion pity for you. You move so fast you must be so very lonely. You are gone before the rock can even learn your name.

I return to my weaving. By the time I have pulled a single strand of silk across the gap to the next pillar of bodies, you will have lived your life, grown old, and returned to the earth. But here, in the cold broth of the Needle-Point, I will still be molting, listening to the mountain grow, and waiting for the light to finally turn white.
