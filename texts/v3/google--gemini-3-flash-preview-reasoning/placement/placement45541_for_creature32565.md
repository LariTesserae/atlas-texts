I am **Khel**, a *Strata-Tuner* of the Fourth Needle. 

I find myself clung to the western face of the spire, six thousand feet above the primary vent-plumes, where the obsidian is so polished it reflects the violet sky like a dark, vertical ocean. My body is a long, deliberate line of slate-colored muscle, pressed flat against the stone to maximize the surface area of my heat-intake. 

I am not standing. To stand is to be disconnected. I am adhered.

***

### My Form
My skin is the texture of wet, sanded rubber—tough enough to resist the razor-edges of the unweathered basalt, yet porous enough to drink the steam. My four limbs are unnaturally long, providing a reach that allows me to bridge the gaps between the natural rock and the copper ribbing of our city. My finger-pads are wide, bulbous, and sensitive enough to feel the microscopic expansion of a copper bolt as it warms in the morning light. 

I have no voice in my throat. If I were to open my mouth and cry out, the humid air would swallow the sound before it reached my own ears. Instead, my "voice" is in my heels and my palms.

### My Work
Right now, I am listening to the **Deep-Pulse**. 

I press my brow against a copper conduit that carries the thermal load from the mantle to the Great Flywheels below. The copper is hot—dangerously so for a creature of soft flesh—but to me, it is a comforting thrum, a steady *thump-hiss* that tells me the pressure is holding. 

But there is a dissonance. A micro-fracture is forming in the basalt anchor-point three hundred feet above me. I can feel it as a high, thin screech—not a sound, but a vibration that travels through the obsidian and into my marrow. It is a "shiver" in the stone. 

I begin to move. My gait is a rhythmic, three-point suction crawl. *Schloop-tack. Schloop-tack.* I do not look down; the concept of "down" is irrelevant when your world has no horizontal planes. There is only *Against the Stone* and *Away from the Stone*.

### My Companions
As I climb, a **Steam-Leech**—a ribbon of damp, indigo velvet—drifts from a nearby fissure and latches onto my shoulder. I do not brush it away. Its body is icy, a biological heat-sink that absorbs the excess warmth radiating from the conduit. In exchange, it drinks the saline condensation that beads on my slate-colored skin. We are a closed loop of survival.

Reaching the anchor-point, I reach into the mesh pouch at my hip and withdraw a cluster of **Glass-Spiders**. They tinkle like wind chimes in my palm, their translucent needle-legs twitching in anticipation. I place them upon the mineral crust—a sprawling, chalky blossom of sulfur that has grown too thick around the anchor-bolt. 

They begin to feed, their tiny mandibles vibrating at a frequency that shatters the salt-scale into dust. I wait. I am a creature of the Scald-Reach; I have no concept of haste. I watch a **Glint-Wing** circle a mile above, its silica wings catching the sun and throwing a shard of white light across my face. It stays aloft on the breath of the earth, a silent witness to my patience.

### My Being
I am a bridge. I am the point where the industrial intent of my people meets the raw, unfinished temper of the planet. 

As the spiders finish their work and the vibration in the rock settles into a smooth, low-frequency hum, I tap a message into the basalt with a rhythmic strike of my hardened knuckles. 

*Needle Four. Anchor stable. The breath is steady.*

Miles away, and miles below, another Calcined—perhaps a Heat-Singer in the lower hive—will feel that vibration through the soles of their feet. They will know that the world is still holding, still cooling, and still ours.

I close my eyes, letting the violet light fade behind my lids. I feel the warmth of the mantle rising through the obsidian, the cool damp of the steam on my back, and the slow, tectonic heartbeat of the Scald-Reach. I am not a resident of this place. I am a part of its architecture. I am a note in its silent, iron song.
