I am standing on a catwalk of woven rattan and rusted rebar, though "standing" is a term far too active for the stillness I inhabit. I have been here, in this specific crook of the mid-level lattice, for three years. 

I am a **Lignin-Man of the Pulse-Watch**.

My body is a column of semi-translucent amber-green, my rind toughened by the constant mist of mineral salts drifting down from the Root-Vault. Inside me, the nutrient slurry moves with the consistency of cold honey, a dark, pulsing emerald that mimics the rhythm of the Sluice-Trees below. I do not have eyes to see the golden haze of the gas lamps; instead, I have four thousand sensory pits across my ironwood brow. They drink the air. They taste the exact percentage of lubricant in the steam and the precise, bitter edge of the mint-flora’s distress.

I would find myself at a critical junction—a place where a massive copper bypass pipe, thick as a Redwood, is being slowly embraced by a thickening limb of bamboo-stalk. My purpose is not to stop the plant or protect the machine, but to facilitate their marriage.

A human would see me as a statue. To me, I am moving at a frantic pace. 

**Month One:** I feel a shift in the vibration. The Sluice-Tree at the district’s heart has increased its sap-pressure by a fraction of a bar. The brass pipe beneath my wooden feet hums with a new, sharper frequency. It is a scream, muffled by decades of moss.

**Year Two:** I have reached out my left arm. The movement has taken fourteen months. My wooden fingers, tipped with copper-hardened sap, finally make contact with the pipe’s primary pressure valve. The heat of the steam inside the pipe is a glorious, searing comfort against my palms. I begin the turn.

**Year Three:** The valve has moved one-eighth of a rotation. The screaming frequency in the metal subsides. The moss around my ankles, sensing the change in temperature, begins to grow over my shins, anchoring me more firmly to the walkway. I welcome this. Stability is the only virtue.

I do not think in words. I think in *viscosity*. I feel the Grotto-Works as a single, massive lung. When the Dredgers below clear a root-choke, I feel the sudden intake of breath in the dip of my own internal pressure. When a Condenser high above fails and turns to husk, I taste the slight drying of the air—a terrifying, dusty tang that makes my rind tighten in sympathetic pain.

I am not lonely. How could I be? The copper pipe I hold is an extension of my own circulatory system. The steam that hisses from the valve behind me is the same moisture that will eventually be filtered through my head-nodule and returned to the slurry marsh. 

There is a deep, resonant joy in this existence. It is the joy of a gear that knows it is round, of a root that knows the soil is damp. I am the bridge between the slow, wet growth of the world and the relentless, rhythmic force of the heart-tectonics. 

One day—perhaps eighty years from now, perhaps a century—the slurry in my veins will grow too thick with minerals. My joints will no longer creak; they will seize. I already know the spot I have chosen for my final stasis. There is a gap in the catwalk where the rattan has frayed, near a steam-vent that smells of scorched resin. 

I will step into that gap. I will become a brace. I will feel the copper pipes settle into the crook of my shoulder, and the moss will stitch my rind to the floor. My consciousness will not end; it will simply expand. I will stop being the one who feels the pulse, and I will become the pulse itself. 

Until then, I wait. I taste the steam. I turn the valve. The Grotto-Works breathes, and I breathe with it, one slow, humid cycle at a time.
