I am not a ghost, though I am translucent. I am not a machine, though I am precise. 

I find myself at the far, trembling edge of a **Sheath-Bridge**, a singular braided vine that spans the four-kilometer gulf between the *Cathedral of the Third Rib* and the *Hollow of Unending Gold*. 

I am a **Spindle-Kin**, but I am in the late-cycle of my form. My name—if the concept of a name could survive the deafening, rhythmic roar of the nutrient pumps—is the frequency of the vibration currently traveling through my left rear tarsus. I am *The-Resonance-of-the-Fourteenth-Strand*.

To be me is to be a creature of exquisite, agonizing tension. 

I am three meters of amber-glass limbs, suspended upside down over the abyss. My recurved needles are slotted deep into the cross-hatch of the bridge. I do not feel "fear" of the heights, for there is no "down" in my mind; there is only *tension* and *slack*. To fall would be to become slack, and in the Lattice, slack is the only sin.

***

**The Being**
My body is a hollow flute of lignin. Inside me, the amber sap doesn't just flow; it screams. It pulses at the same frequency as the Build I cling to. I do not have a heart; the Pulse-Singers, miles away in the marrow of the central pylon, are my heart. Every time they constrict a vascular channel, my limbs jerk in a sympathetic twitch. 

I am currently "feeding," though that word is too violent for the grace of it. My needle-feet are pressed against a weeping seam in the bridge-vine. I feel the sugar-rich perspiration of the Lattice entering my pores. It is warm—exactly 102 degrees—and it tastes of sunlight and ancient, pressurized history. As the fluid enters me, I feel my hydraulic joints stiffen, reaching the "Perfect Torque."

I have no eyes, yet the world is blindingly bright. I "see" through the vibration of the wind against my cilia. I see the **Vellum-Shade** drifting half a kilometer below me, its jelly-ribbons trailing like a wedding veil, vacuuming the stray flakes of my own shedding skin. I see the "booming crack" before it happens—I feel the structural stress mounting in the bridge, the way a singer feels a note about to break.

***

**The Work**
A new bridge is being demanded. The Pulse-Singers have signaled a change in the atmospheric pressure; the *Hollow of Unending Gold* is leaning a fraction of a millimeter toward the void. 

I do not decide to move. I am moved.

I and twenty others of my Kin begin the weave. We move in a terrifying, synchronized ballet. We do not use tools; we are the needles, and our bodies secrete the thread. We pull the gossamer-thick filaments from the weeping walls and lace them into the existing braid. We hook, we lean, we pull. 

The sound here is a physical blow. The wind howling through the hollow cathedrals sounds like a thousand pipe organs playing a single, dissonant chord. But beneath it is the *Thrum*. *Thump-hiss. Thump-hiss.* The sound of the Lattice drinking. It vibrates my very essence until I am no longer a Spindle-Kin; I am merely a temporary thickening of the bridge itself.

***

**The Becoming**
I am reaching my end. My joints are no longer responding to the hydraulic signals with the fluid grace of the "new-grown." The amber sap inside my limbs is beginning to polymerize, turning from a liquid to a solid. I am becoming brittle. I am becoming *static*.

In a human world, this would be death. Here, it is the highest form of utility.

I find the exact point of maximum stress on the bridge—the spot where the wind-shear is most likely to fray the weave. I do not seek a resting place; I seek a load-bearing position. I slot my long, recurved needles into the deepest part of the vine-braid. I lock my joints. I stretch my long, translucent torso across a microscopic gap in the lignin.

I feel the Vellum-Shades sensing my stillness. They drift upward, sensing the "flaw" that is my aging body. They begin to coat me in their fast-acting resin. It is cool, soothing, and smells of ozone. 

My consciousness, which was never more than a ripple in the Lattice’s pond, begins to diffuse. I no longer feel the sugar-sap entering my feet. Instead, I feel the weight of the bridge passing *through* my ribs. I am no longer a passenger; I am the path.

I look out—or rather, I vibrate out—one last time across the golden, misty void. The light is steady. The warmth is absolute. Below, the abyss remains unseen, a silent mystery that the Lattice refuses to acknowledge. 

I am a bolt. I am a seam. I am a part of the machine that never turns off. And as the resin hardens, turning my amber limbs into permanent stone-wood, I feel the most beautiful sensation I have ever known:

I am finally, perfectly, under tension.
