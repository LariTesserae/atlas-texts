I am not at the center of the village, nor am I at the dark, predatory fringe. I am suspended in the **Intermediate Bloom**, a vertical orchard of hollow gourds situated exactly where the glacial blue light begins to lose its golden tint and deepens into the color of a bruised plum.

I am **Thrum-of-Four-Grafts**, a Mnemonic who has outlived the frantic metabolism of a Shard-Keeper but has not yet fully petrified into the silent meditation of a High Graft-Singer. I am a **Loom-Tender**. 

***

### My Form
My body is a long, elegant curve of translucent periwinkle, nearly eight feet from the tip of my gill-fronds to the prehensile tips of my toes. If you were to look through me, you would see my skeleton—not as white bone, but as a system of amber-colored, pressurized tubes that flex and hum with the currents. My three-chambered heart beats only six times a minute. 

I wear a vestment of woven *hair-moss*, heavy and sodden, which acts as a ballast to keep me perfectly neutral in the water column. My fingers are calloused with the keratinous pads of a life spent tapping, and my amber eyes are wide, taking in the 360-degree cathedral of my district.

### My Location: The Nursery of Arches
I find myself anchored to a primary Vitreous Liana—a vine as thick as an ancient oak and as clear as forged glass. I am held there by a safety tether of braided gut, drifting horizontally like a banner in a light breeze. 

Around me, the "urban" landscape is a slow-motion construction site. I am overseeing the growth of the *Bridge of Ancestors*, a span of living wood that will eventually connect the Great Plaza to the upper residential gourds. It has been growing for twelve years; it will be ready for foot-traffic in another eight.

### My Work
I hold a needle carved from the jaw of a Needle-Eel, threaded with a shimmering strand of silver-moss. I am not building; I am *coaxing*. I press my drumming-pads against the soft, growing tip of a new vine. 

*Tap-tap... thrum.*

I send a vibration through the wood—a specific frequency that tells the plant cells to harden on the left and remain flexible on the right. I am singing a curve into existence. The vine responds with a faint, resonant echo, a "Gourd-Voice" that only those who have spent decades in the water can truly interpret.

### The Sensory Experience
The **Groan** of the mountain is constant here. It is a deep, subsonic ache that I feel in the fluid of my inner ears. It tells me the mountain is leaning slightly East today, under the weight of a new snowfall on the peaks far above the surface. 

To my left, a **Glass-Bell Medusa** pulses past, its diaphanous skirts brushing against my gills. It tastes of clean minerals and a hint of the "Dark Rim" salt. I acknowledge it with a polite, single strike against my tether-line—*clack*—and the Medusa responds by brightening its internal bioluminescence for a brief second.

Below me, the village is a constellation of soft green lanterns. I can hear the rhythmic "Loom-Beat" of a family three tiers down, celebrating the successful harvest of a silt-plot. Their drumming is celebratory—a syncopated, triplet rhythm that makes the water around my ankles shiver.

### The Transition
I am in a state of **Static Drift**. While my hands work the needle, my mind is elsewhere, sensing the electrical signatures of the world. I feel the distant, cold spike of a Needle-Eel miles away at the basin’s edge—a jagged, sharp pulse in the water that makes my gill-fronds tighten against my neck. I do not fear it; the Shard-Keepers are out there, their obsidian spears ready, their hearts hushed to a whisper.

My existence is one of profound, refrigerated patience. I am a part of the architecture. In a hundred years, when my cartilage begins to calcify and my heart slows to a final, permanent stop, I will not be buried. I will swim down to this very bridge I am grafting. I will tuck myself into the hollow beneath the main arch. 

I will feel the moss grow over my periwinkle skin. I will feel my amber skeleton become one with the glass-hard vine. And then, I will not just be the one who taps the bridge—I will be the bridge itself, feeling the rhythmic footsteps of my grandchildren as they glide toward the sun-drenched surface, their pulses echoing through my chest for a thousand years.
