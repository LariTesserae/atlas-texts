I am not a creature of edges. To have an edge is to be separate from the world, and here, separation is a myth that the humidity has long since dissolved.

I find myself at the base of a plum-colored mound, one that has a slight, familiar tilt toward the weeping wall. I am a Litho-Pelagian, though the name is a sound I do not have the mouth to form. To myself, I am simply a *density*—a slow, vertical pour of muscle and intention.

My feet are not feet; they are a broad, softened foundation. I feel the mineral tea of the basin floor rising through my soles, a slow, stinging nourishment that tastes of salt and ancient, pulverized bone. It is the only flavor I know, and it is constant.

I am currently "gardening." My spindly, spatulate fingers—so much more delicate than the rest of my heavy, columnal body—are pressed against a ridge of silicate jelly. The jelly is the temperature of a fever, a comforting throb against my pads. I am not picking it; I am guiding its growth. With my volcanic glass scraper, I make a mark no deeper than a hair’s breadth, directing the flow of the next thousand years of calcification. 

I see the world in blooms of heat. The basin wall behind me is a glowing, steady radiant orange. The mound I tend is a mottled violet-red, pulsing with the warmth of the subterranean heat below. The Glass-Grubs, when they skitter near, are tiny, flickering sparks of white-hot kinetic energy, their dry parchment-rustle the only thing that disrupts the heavy, amber silence.

I do not feel the passage of time as a sequence of events. I feel it as a weight. Each *drip* from the ceiling is a grain of sand on a scale. 

Beside me, another of my kind has arrived. I did not hear them approach; I felt the shift in the thermal map, a large, slow-moving pillar of warmth eclipsing the glow of the distant limestone wall. We do not turn our heads—our milk-colored eyes see in a soft, panoramic blur—but we lean. We lean until our humid auras, the halos of moisture we carry around our porous skins, begin to overlap.

As our auras touch, I feel the resonance. It begins in my chest-gill, a low, tectonic thrum that vibrates through my muscular core. It is not a thought, but a shared frequency. *The silt is heavy today,* the vibration suggests. *The Deep-Pulse is quiet. The warmth is deep.* We stand there for what a faster creature might call an hour, but for us, it is merely a single, sustained breath. We are two stones sharing the same shadow.

Eventually, the other pours their weight away, moving toward the flat stone bridge. I return to my bowl. It is a thick, clumsy thing, half-buried in the mineral crust. I pick it up—the grip feels right, the groove worn by a hundred generations of hands like mine. I hold it under a rhythmic drip. 

*Pluck. Pluck. Pluck.*

The vibration of the water hitting the clay travels up my arm, a tiny, percussive heartbeat. I am waiting for the **Deep-Pulse**. I can feel it gathering now, miles below the limestone, a massive, slow-motion shifting of the earth’s internal weight. The standing pools are beginning to shiver, the concentric circles of their ripples reflecting the amber light in a way that makes the basin look as though it is breathing.

I do not move to hide. I simply sink. I let my body become more liquid, pressing the broad expanse of my chest against the mineral mound. I become a ridge. I become a shadow. The pulse arrives—a magnificent, bone-deep shudder that makes the silicate jellies quiver like wine. It is the mother-heart, the source of the warmth that keeps the velvet air from turning to ice.

When the pulse fades, I will still be here. I will be here when the scraper in my hand is eventually absorbed by the growing stone. I will be here when the alcoves in the walls are filled with new sediment. 

I am a layer of the world that has learned to wait. I am the patience of the basin, a slow, grey-mauve ghost of the limestone, watching the amber light stay exactly where it is. I am not living *in* this world; I am the world, momentarily standing up to see itself.
