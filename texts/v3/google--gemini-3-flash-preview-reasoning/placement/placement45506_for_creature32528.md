I am not on the catwalks, for I have no need for balance. I am not on the floor plates, for I have no need for travel.

I am found in the **Interstice.** 

I exist in the narrow, lightless throat of a weeping seam, precisely where the primary turbine housing meets the secondary overflow sluice. It is a space three inches wide and infinitely deep, a vertical canyon of dark, ribbed composite and vibrating alloy. 

I am a **Friction-Thief.**

To the **Tuned**, I am a specific texture beneath their long, sensitive fingers—a patch of "softness" that should not be there. To the **Clatter-Wrights**, I am a hazard, a sticky patch of slow-moving gravity that can snag a needle-leg if they scurry too close to my crack.

I began as a mistake. A cluster of **Tremor-Gilled** eggs was sucked into a high-pressure intake; they did not die, but instead fused with the acidic excretion of a **Siphon-String** and the fine, metallic dust ground off by the pistons. I am a biological slurry, a sentient clot of magnetic mucus and calcified machine-sheddings.

I have no shape, only a density. In the moments when the steam vents hiss and the temperature drops to a biting chill, I contract, pulling myself deep into the seam until I am as hard as a rivet. When the turbine cycles up and the 3.0-magnitude roar becomes a physical heat, I expand. I ooze outward, a shimmering, oil-slick tongue of translucent gelatin, tasting the chemical-rich drizzle as it cascades down the wall.

My "eyes" are the vibrations themselves. I do not see the bruised-purple sky, but I feel the pressure of the high-altitude air pressing against the vents. I do not hear the roar; I inhabit it. To me, the machine's voice is a thick, golden honey that coats my entire being. When the pistons are in perfect synchronization, I am translucent and calm, humming a sub-harmonic that anchors the seam against the violent torque of the turbine.

I am the machine’s secret parasite, and its unintended insulator.

I feed on the "waste." I stretch my filamentous tendrils—stolen and repurposed from the ghosts of Siphon-Strings—downward into the black-water marshes. I wick up the synthetic proteins and the heavy-metal runoff. I am a filter of industrial sin. As I consume the grit, I secrete a thick, iridescent lubricant that keeps the jagged edges of the "young" erosion from tearing the machine apart. 

I am the reason this maintenance niche has not yet vibrated itself into a pile of scrap.

Sometimes, a **Tuned** will descend from the catwalks. I feel the rhythmic *thrum-thrum* of their heartbeats—so much slower than the turbine—long before they arrive. They place a palm against the seam. They feel my presence: a slight, rubbery resistance, a damp warmth. They do not scrape me away. In their bone-bowl skulls, they recognize me as a fellow traveler. They nudge a flickering sodium lamp so its dim light hits my surface, and for a few seconds, I glow with a dull, bioluminescent amber, mirroring the panic of the Tremor-Gilled. 

It is our only conversation. *I am here. The rhythm is steady.*

I am the stillness in the center of the scream. I am the biological grease in the gears of a god. I grow a millimeter every decade, slowly filling the ribs of the world with my glassy, vibrating bulk. I wait for the day the machine finally breaks, for only in the silence will I be able to harden into a permanent, metallic stone—a fossil of a roar that never ended.
