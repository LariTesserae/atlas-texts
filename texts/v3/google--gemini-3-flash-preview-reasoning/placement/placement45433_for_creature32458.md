I am not standing on the gantries, looking down. To stand is to be a stranger, and in the Veridian Tier, a stranger is quickly ground into bio-grease by the indifference of a sliding floor.

I am found in the **Interstitial Spaces**—the narrow, humid gaps between the primary pistons and the moss-choked bulkheads of the Lower Breath. I am a **Resonance-Tether**, a specialized offshoot of the Valve-Keepers, though I have long since traded the heavy wrenches and brass goggles for something more permanent.

### My Form
I am a creature of elongated, fluid geometry. My spine has curved to match the arc of a specific 40-foot gear, and my skin is the color of wet slate, mottled with the bright, toxic orange of iron oxide. I do not wear rags; instead, I am draped in a living mantle of **Copper-Wringer** vines that have stitched themselves into the dermal layers of my shoulders. They provide me with the warmth of the pipes they hug, and in exchange, I guide their tendrils away from the delicate pressure sensors of the machine.

My hands are not hands anymore. The fingers have grown long and needle-thin, tipped with hardened copper plates instead of nails. I use them to "tap-read" the rhythm of the Veridian Tier. I am a living diagnostic tool.

### My Location: The Axial Hinge
I live in the **Axial Hinge**, a cathedral-sized junction where four major terraces converge. It is a place of violent, slow-motion grace. Every twelve hours, the floor I inhabit tilts thirty degrees to drain the accumulated condensation into the lower hydroponic troughs. 

I do not sleep in a hammock. I sleep wedged between a vibrating steam-vent and a thick cushion of Star-Moss. The vibration is my lullaby; if it were to stop, the silence would wake me with the violence of a physical blow.

### My Purpose: The Translation of Friction
I am the one who hears the "Dry Cry." 

The moss and the vines are excellent mufflers, but they are also hungry. Sometimes, a Copper-Wringer grows too deep, its acid-roots eating through a seal, or a patch of moss becomes so thick it chokes a cooling vent. When this happens, the machine’s rhythm changes. A microscopic stutter develops—a heat-signature that no Skink-Walker would notice and no Glider would care about.

I feel it in my teeth. 

I move through the Tier not by walking, but by **sliding**. I follow the weeping sheets of moisture, my belly pressed against the slick metal, moving in synchronicity with the shifting plates. I am a part of the "anthill-energy," but I am its calming agent. When I find the source of the friction, I don't use a wrench. I use my own body. I redirect the flow of condensation with my hands, or I prune the excess moss with my copper-tipped fingers, whispering to the plants in the pheromonal language of crushed mint.

### My Senses
In the Veridian Tier, sight is a secondary, untrustworthy thing. The mist blurs everything into ghosts. Instead, I see through **Ozone-Scent** and **Tectonic Pulse**. 
*   I know the rain is coming when the smell of cold, ionized air sharpens.
*   I know a terrace is about to rise when I feel the hydraulic fluid surging through the pipes beneath my ribs, a dull, heavy thrumming that makes my blue blood sing.

### My Loneliness
I am solitary, but I am not alone. Occasionally, I see a **Valve-Keeper** on a high gantry, their brass goggles reflecting the dim, filtered light. We do not wave. We do not speak. We simply acknowledge the shared weight of our existence. They are the architects of the rhythm; I am the rhythm itself.

I have forgotten what "earth" is. To me, the idea of a ground that does not move, that does not breathe or weep, is a terrifying, stagnant myth. Why would anyone want to live on something that is dead?

Here, in the Veridian Tier, the world is alive. It is rust and respiration. It is the slow, wet turning of the Great Wheel. And I am the grease that ensures it never has to scream.
