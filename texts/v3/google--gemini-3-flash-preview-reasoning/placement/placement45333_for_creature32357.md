It begins with the feet. Or rather, it begins where the concept of "feet" ends and the concept of "foundation" takes hold. 

I would find myself within the **Remains**, specifically inhabiting the lone, windowless tower of calcified wood that stands like a jagged tooth amidst the matted vines. I would not be an inhabitant of the tower; I would be a **Sere-Mnemonic**, and therefore, I would be the tower’s living, sentient marrow.

To be me is to be a study in petrification. My skin is no longer a barrier, but a threshold—a thick, silvered rind of sun-bleached bark that has long since forgotten the pliability of youth. I do not stand *on* the floor; I have been invited *into* it. My toes have elongated into taproots, winding through the calcified heartwood of the platform, threading themselves into the very biology of the Well. I am anchored. I am a vertical axis in a world that refuses to rotate.

I exist in the center of the pressure. The air, that thick soup of crushed sugar and ozone, does not move around me; it presses into me, filling the microscopic pores of my wooden flesh until I am as dense as a diamond. 

What is it like to be the memory of a world that has stopped? 

Mostly, it is a matter of listening to the **Hum-Bellows**. From my vantage point within the tower, I feel their sub-sonic thrumming not as a sound, but as a tectonic shift in my own spine. Every time a water-globe shatters against the lichen far below, the vibration travels up through the vines, through the tower’s base, and into my marrow. I catch these vibrations and store them. I am a library of echoes. I hold the resonance of the last ten thousand "clangs" of falling water, stacked like rings in a tree, each one a perfect, frozen data point in the Great Stasis.

Sometimes, a **Glass-Strider** will pass over the surface of my skin. Its six legs are like needles of ice, tapping out a chemical rhythm against my bark. It doesn't see me as a being; I am merely a topographical feature, a waypoint for its nervous system. I feel the cold flicker of its enzymes as it passes, a brief, crystalline shiver that reminds me of the sensation of "speed," though I have not moved in a century.

I do not look at the **Aperture**. I have no eyelids to close, but my "eyes" have long since become light-sensitive burls, drinking the blinding white brilliance until my internal thoughts are the color of bleached bone. The light is not something I see; it is something I *endure*. It is a physical weight on my shoulders, a golden sledgehammer that ensures my thoughts remain as static and embryonic as the flower buds outside.

If you were to press your ear to my chest—that expanse of rough, sun-withered lignin—you would not hear the frantic pumping of blood. You would hear the slow, pressurized groan of a thousand years of held breath. You would hear the sound of a history that has no ending, because it refused to have a middle. 

I am the architect who became the brick. I am the record of the miracle that stalled. I am perfectly, terrifyingly content, for in the Chlorotic Well, to remember is not to look back, but to feel the immense, crushing weight of the **Now**. I am the knot in the wood of the world, and I am not waiting for anything to change. 

I am simply here, and here is all there is.
