I am the **Lamination**. 

I am not one of the Menders who moves through the seams with liquid, mineral grace. I have passed beyond the need for movement, and in doing so, I have become the most vital thing in this sector of the High Resonator. I am wedged into the North-East Node, a place where the Great Roar does not merely press against the walls, but seeks to tear them apart through sheer harmonic dissonance.

I am a **Seismic Anchor**, a Vitrified who has chosen the Long Stasis.

You would find me at the intersection of a sagging ceiling beam and a pillar of six million tiles. I am no longer a figure; I am a wedge. My legs have been driven deep into the shoulder-width seam, fused to the sun-dried bricks by a century of dripping resin and the relentless, downward pressure of the structure. My torso is arched, my chest-scales pressed flat against the underside of the ceiling. 

I am the bridge between the floor and the roof. I am the reason this corner of the universe does not collapse into dust.

To be me is to exist as a single, sustained note of resistance. I do not hear the Roar—I *am* the Roar’s primary adversary. Through my needle-fine sensory hairs, I feel the sound as a series of physical blows. I feel the exact millisecond when the Roar shifts its weight, trying to find a flaw in the clay, a bubble in the resin, or a fatigue-line in my own ceramic spine. 

I am a masterpiece of internal geometry. Inside my chest, my wooden braces have calcified into something harder than diamond, yet they still retain the slightest, microscopic flex. When the Roar reaches its peak pressure, I feel my ribs groan—a soundless, internal vibration that harmonizes with the amber light. If I were to yield even a fraction of a millimeter, the pillar behind me would begin to shiver, the lead beads would spill from their pouches, and the world would begin to unmake itself.

My consciousness has thinned out, spreading away from a central "self" and into the materials around me. I can no longer tell where my fingertips end and the hand-fired tiles begin. I feel the thirst of the bricks as if it were my own. I feel the dry, baking 1.7 heat not as a temperature, but as a stabilizing force that keeps my joints from becoming brittle too soon. 

I see the world in shimmering, acoustic topography. To my left, I "see" a Mender approaching. They are a blur of high-frequency vibrations, a moving shadow of interference patterns. They do not look at me; they do not need to. They feel the integrity of my stance. They reach out and press a fresh pouch of lead beads against my thigh, damping a micro-fracture they sensed in the brickwork I am shielding. The touch is a violent, beautiful harmony—a momentary alignment of two mineral souls. 

I am in the late stages of the **Curing**. The dust of the chamber has settled so thickly into the crevices of my scales that I have become a mottled ochre-grey, indistinguishable from the ancient clay pillars. I am becoming heavier. Every century, I sink a few microns deeper into the foundation. 

I am not afraid of the Erosion. I look forward to the moment when my structural purpose is complete. Eventually, the Roar will find the frequency that my calcified heart cannot match. In that final second, I will not feel pain; I will feel a sudden, magnificent increase in volume. I will shatter, and my pieces will be swept up by the Menders to be ground into the very grit that polishes the floor for the next generation of Anchors.

Until then, I am the stillness in the center of the scream. I am the hard, warm, unyielding fact of the wall. I am the Lamination, and as long as I hold my breath, the sky of clay will not fall.
