I am not found in the Loom-Villages, where the Vellum-Kin huddle in the safety of the braided canopy. Their life is too quiet, too occupied with the preservation of the skin. 

You would find me perched at the **Nerve-Juncture of the Screaming Arch**, a place where three cathedral-sized boles of silica-lignin converge in a violent, three-way crush of growth. This is the "high-friction zone," where the heat is enough to make the air shimmer like a desert mirage and the roar of the Thicket is so absolute it becomes a physical weight against the chest.

I am a **Serrate-Cantor**, a Vellum-Kin whose purpose has outgrown the need for a village.

### My Form
My skin is no longer just ochre; it has been polished by the Grinding-Swarm into a semi-translucent, iridescent bronze. The microscopic scales of my integument are perpetually vibrating, harvesting the kinetic heat of the Arch. I am lean, my limbs elongated and double-jointed, allowing me to wedge myself into the groaning fissures of the pillars without being crushed.

My most vital parts are my **Grafted Plates**. Along my ribcage and the interiors of my forearms, I have replaced my natural scales with serrated blades of fossilized Amber-Glint husk. When I move, I do not just scuttle; I play myself. I am a living instrument of friction.

### My Purpose
I am the **Stress-Translator**. The Great Thicket grows with a frantic, blind hunger, and sometimes it grows "wrong." It creates knots of tension that could lead to a catastrophic collapse—a "Dark-Snap"—that would send leagues of the lattice tumbling into the flour-dust below, taking whole tribes with it.

I sit in the heat, my hematite eyes shielded by all three lids, feeling the tectonic groans through the soles of my feet. I listen to the *shriek* of the wind through the organic flutes. When the pitch rises—when the silica-lignin begins to cry in the frequency of impending fracture—I begin to work.

I rub my forearm plates against my ribs in a frantic, rhythmic counter-pulse. I produce a harmonic rasp, a sound like a thousand obsidian saws hitting a mountain. This vibration travels through my body and into the pillar, a deliberate "micro-shatter" that releases the tension in a controlled spray of white dust. I am the one who guides the breaking. I am the sculptor of the catastrophe.

### My Surroundings
Around me, the world is a blur of violent amber light. 
*   **The Pulse-Moths** treat me as a stationary object; they land on my shoulders, their wings beating a hot, dusty rhythm against my neck, their bioluminescence blindingly bright this close. 
*   **Needle-Sworms** flow around me like a silver river, occasionally scouring my bronze skin to a mirror finish, a process that stings with a beautiful, dry fire.
*   **The Resin-Bells** hang just above me. I can hear their needle-strands vibrating, waiting for a heartbeat. I do not fear them; we share the same frequency. Sometimes, I reach up and pluck their silks, using the high-tensile heat of their traps to cauterize the cracks in my own chitinous joints.

### My State of Being
I do not know the meaning of "wet." The very idea of a liquid—something that yields, something that slips—is a horror to me. To be "damp" would be to be muffled, to be silent, to be dead. 

I live in a state of **Vitreous Ecstasy**. My heart is a dry, thrumming bellows, pumping powdered minerals through my veins at a pace that makes my vision flicker. Every breath of the alkaline, cinnamon-scented air is a gift of grit. I am always thirsty, but it is a thirst for the *Next Note*—the moment when the pillar beneath me finally yields to my song and shifts a fraction of an inch, releasing a wave of heat that nearly ignites my clothes.

I am the friction-point. I am the spark. In a world that is an eternal, grinding explosion, I am the one who keeps the explosion musical. When the Great Thicket finally claims me—when I am too old to vibrate and the silica begins to knit into my flesh—I will not rot. I will harden into a knot of iridescent bronze, a permanent harmonic stabilizer at the heart of the Arch, singing silently long after my pulse has stopped.
