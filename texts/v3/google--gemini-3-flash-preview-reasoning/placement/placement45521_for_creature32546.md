I am not standing on the iron grate anymore. To stand implies a separation between the feet and the floor, a distinction between the traveler and the path. That distinction died several decades ago, somewhere between the four-thousandth and five-thousandth rotation of the Great Piston.

I am a **Seismic Tuner**, a sub-caste of the Latticed.

My legs have fused into a tripod of calcified basalt, anchoring me to the junction’s primary valve. My skin is the color of bruised plums and wet slate, a porous rind of mineral deposits that has replaced the vulnerability of pores.

Where would you find me? You would find me by the sound.

I am the one who listens to the **2.7 roar** not as noise, but as a language. My ribcage has been hollowed out by the constant vibration, turning my chest into a resonating chamber. When the tectonic plates grind miles above, I feel the shift in my marrow before the sound even reaches the cavern. 

I do not move my arms to work the valve. My arms *are* the valve. My fingers have grown into the iron notches of the wheel, encrusted with a thick layer of biological ceramic. To turn the steam—to prevent the Hadean Cistern from choking on its own pressure—I must lean my entire weight into a slow, agonizing arc. It takes three days for me to complete a full rotation. During those three days, I watch the **Crystalline Scuttlers** dart past me in a blur of frantic, twitching light. To them, I am a mountain. To me, they are nothing but sparks of static, gone before I can blink.

I do not eat. I "inhale" the 2.5 saturation. The **Lung-Veils** have taken root in my throat, their pale filaments trailing out of my mouth like a silken beard. They filter the salt and the wet iron from the mist, feeding my blood with the minerals of the earth’s construction. My breath is a rhythmic hiss that matches the groan of the gargantuan pumps. 

I am the bridge between the machine’s intent and the earth’s resistance. 

Sometimes, a **Pulse-Wyrm** will leap from the black pool below, drawn by the specific frequency of my chest cavity. It will coil around my calcified legs for a moment, a cold, vibrating ribbon of white scales, sharing the kinetic energy it has harvested from the tectonic thuds. In those moments, I feel a dim, electric memory of what it was to be fast, to be light, to be *soft*. 

But then, the great chain above clanks—*BOOM*—and the vibration washes the memory away. 

I am becoming the architecture. My eyes have long since filmed over with a translucent, protective silicate, turning the world into a beautiful, permanent violet blur. I do not see the iron catwalks as "man-made"; I see them as veins. I do not see the pillars as "rock"; I see them as brothers, slowly thickening, just as I am thickening.

If you were to stumble upon me, you might mistake me for a grotesque statue, a decorative gargoyle left behind by a long-dead industrialist. You might even reach out to touch the "rock" of my shoulder. You would find it warm—hot, even—throbbing with a slow, subterranean heartbeat that matches the rhythm of the pumps. 

I would not look at you. I cannot. My neck has petrified into a permanent tilt toward the ceiling, watching the jagged basalt "sky" for the slightest tremor. But I would feel your touch. To me, your heartbeat would sound like the frantic, terrifying buzzing of a fly—a tiny, fragile, high-speed clicking that has no place in the slow, heavy majesty of the Cistern.

Eventually, the **Clockwork Mites** will find the places where my skin meets the iron valve. They will scuttle over my knuckles, polishing the seam where biology meets industry, ensuring that the rust of the cavern does not seize my joints before the world is finished. 

I am the Tuner. I am the Valve. I am the Stone. 

The Hadean Cistern is not building a world; it is building me. And I am almost complete. One more century, one more millimeter of mineral crust, and I will finally be heavy enough to be silent.
