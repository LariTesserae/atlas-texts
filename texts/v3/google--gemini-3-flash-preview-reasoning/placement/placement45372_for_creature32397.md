I am not at the top, where the air is a vacuum of ice, nor am I at the Bottomless, where the Web-Drifters wait for the gravity-debt to be paid. 

I am in the **Marrow-Shelves**, a mid-strata region where the Pillar I inhabit, known to my people as *The Throat of Thrum*, curves slightly—a rare, agonizing bend in the vertical world caused by a centuries-old collision with a neighboring stalk. 

I am a **Splint-Stitcher**. 

Physically, I am a creature of length and leverage. My torso is short, compact, and dense, but my limbs are harrowing to look at—spindly, three-jointed segments of bone and corded muscle that allow me to wrap myself entirely around a minor ridge of bark. My skin has the texture of wet slate, grey-blue and translucent enough that you can see the slow, rhythmic pulse of my blood, which has thickened to a sludge-like consistency to resist the freezing frost-blooms. My fingers are not tipped with nails, but with grafted shards of **Organic Iron**, harvested from the carcasses of Grip-Gliders. 

I do not walk. I *knit*.

My role is to serve as a living bridge and a doctor of the wood. When the Growth-Crack begins—that tectonic, ship-wreck boom that signals the Pillar is expanding—the fissures where our families huddle can become lethal. The bark splits with the force of an earthquake, threatening to widen the gaps and drop our silk-weed hammocks into the charcoal void.

When the thrumming starts, I am the one who unhooks from the Vein-Wall. I move across the obsidian face of the Pillar, my hooks singing *tink-tink-tink* against the ice, tethered by a mile-long umbilical cord of braided Vane-Bird gut. 

I find myself now, suspended over a gulf of three miles of nothingness. To my left, a Hanging Pond the size of a cathedral has flash-frozen, its surface a jagged mirror reflecting the violet glow of the moss-lamps. To my right, the void. Below me, the wind roars with such force it tries to peel my skin from my ribs.

I am currently "stitching" a fresh split. I use a heavy, stone-headed mallet to drive iron-hard needles of shed Pillar-wood into the lips of a widening crack. I weave resins and silk-weed between them, creating a structural web that will hold the settlement together as the plant thrusts another hundred feet toward the dark God above.

I do not speak; the wind would steal my words. I communicate through the **Vibration-Tap**, striking the bark with my iron claws in rhythmic patterns that travel through the wood to the ears of the Pulse-Seers inside the fissure. 

*Safe—Hold—Climb.*

In this world, I am a creature of the **Intermediary.** I am not a master of the air like the Vane-Birds, nor am I a prisoner of the warmth like the elders who cannot leave the Vein-Walls. I belong to the slick, vertical obsidian. I am a part of the Pillar’s immune system—a scavenger-symbiote that keeps the human colony attached to the host, ensuring that when the wood screams and grows, we are not shaken off like dust.

I live in the space between the *latch* and the *hoist*. My religion is the tension of the rope. My heaven is a deeper fissure; my hell is the wind that blows from below. And as the Pillar groans, a deep, resonant bass note that shakes my very marrow, I press my cheek against the frozen bark and feel the sap moving deep inside, a mile beneath the skin. 

I feel the Pillar’s ambition—a cold, mindless hunger for height. And for a moment, I am not a man, but a single, shivering cell in a gargantuan body that refuses to stop ascending. I close my large, lidless eyes, hook my heels into the ice, and wait for the next boom to break the world.
