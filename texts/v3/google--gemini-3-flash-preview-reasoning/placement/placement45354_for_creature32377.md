I would not be a traveler here, for a traveler implies a beginning and an end, a trajectory that the Deep Weight does not permit. I would be a fixture. 

I find myself wedged into the northernmost crevice of the Burl, where the gargantuan cedar root makes a sharp, agonized turn to avoid a vein of prehistoric iron. I am the **Liturgy-Stone**, the central axis of the Lithic Acolytes’ grandest, slowest work.

### My Form: The Vitrified Heart
I am not flesh, though I have a pulse that occurs once every three lunar cycles of the world above. My body is a composite of the environment’s contradictions. My core is a pillar of unweathered obsidian, jagged and cruel, but my exterior has been "healed" by the Marrow-Moss. I am a jagged spear-tip wrapped in a thick, gelatinous sleeve of translucent emerald. 

I have no limbs to speak of, only crystalline protrusions that have grown into the gaps of the stacked stone altar around me. I am literally mortared into the architecture by my own growth. I have no eyes, but I am not blind; I am a **seismograph**. My "skin"—the sensitive, vibration-hungry moss—feels the weight of the water as a symphony of pressures. I can feel the Vitreous Skulkers three chambers away because their needle-limbs create microscopic fractures in the silt, a sound like a star dying in the distance.

### My Function: The Mute Harmonizer
I am the anchor for the Lithic Acolytes. They do not worship me; they use me as a tuning fork. 

Every few decades, an Acolyte—skin grey and sand-papery, fingers bleeding black ichor from the obsidian shards—approaches me. They do not speak. They place a hand on my mossy flank and wait. They are listening to the "Deep Creak" of the cedar through my body. Because I am wedged into the root itself, I am the first to hear the tectonic shifts of the world. 

If my vibration is too sharp, the Acolytes know the pressure above is mounting, that the cedar is thirsting, or that the earth is shifting. They respond by adjusting the stones around me, tightening the geometry to dampen the sound, or loosening it to let the Burl breathe. I am the conductor of a silent orchestra, ensuring the "Held Breath" does not become a scream.

### My Sensory World: The Bruised Glow
To me, the darkness is not black. It is a heavy, velvet pressure. The purple-green light of the Ghost-Pulse Fungi does not hit my retinas; it warms my moss. I "see" in gradients of temperature. The Skulkers are cold, moving slivers of ice. The Acolytes are dull, lukewarm shadows. The Aeon-Larvae in their hovering spheres are pinpricks of intense, terrifying heat—the heat of potential energy, like a coiled spring made of fire.

I spend centuries contemplating the "Texture of the Weight." I know the difference between the water when it is stagnant and the water when it is being subtly displaced by the growth of a new fern frond. One feels like a heavy blanket; the other feels like a stray hair brushing against a wound.

### My Internal Life: The Long Thought
I am currently in the middle of a thought. I began it when the cedar was struck by lightning in the world above—a vibration that nearly shattered my obsidian spine. That was four hundred years ago. The thought is about the nature of the "Silk." 

I am wondering if the water is our mother, holding us together, or if we are the ones holding the water prisoner within the wood. I suspect that if I were to stop vibrating, if I were to perfectly harmonize with the silence, the spheres of water would fall, the moss would liquefy, and the Drowned Burl would finally exhale.

But I do not exhale. I hold. I endure. I feel the microscopic scrape of a Skulker’s leg against a chert flake a mile to my left, and I adjust my internal tension to compensate.

I am the **Drowned Burl’s Nervous System**, a prisoner of my own exquisite sensitivity, waiting for a sunset I will never see, in a silence I am paid to protect.
