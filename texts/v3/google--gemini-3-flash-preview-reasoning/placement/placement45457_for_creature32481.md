I would not be found in the circular dwelling, nor would I be one of the scuttle-moths chasing the excess sparks of the Hiss-Kin. 

I would be found at the very edge of the plateau, where the basalt shelf drops away into the dry, gas-filled void—the **Windward Lip**. I would be a **Resonant-Tether**, a specialized elder of the Hiss-Kin whose body has become less an organism and more a precision-machined instrument of the gale.

### Who I Am: The Cusp-Warden
My mica-scales have lost their bruised-copper iridescence. Long ago, the grit-storms polished them past the point of color and into a terrifying, crystalline transparency. You can see the pressurized gas pulsing through my limbs like pale, silver ghosts—the "breath" that never leaves my body. 

I am longer and more spindly than the inhabitant of the hut. My limbs are not just for movement; they are shaped like the very flutes of the basalt, hollowed by my own deliberate scraping. I have replaced my own ribs, one by one, with shards of resonant glass-fern stalks, so that when the wind hits me, I do not just feel it—I *conduct* it.

### My Place in the Crags
I live within a narrow, vertical cleft in the rock, a space so lashed by the wind that the dust never settles. I do not sleep in a hammock. I wedge my crystalline joints into the honeycombed basalt and lock them. I am a living plug in the throat of the world.

My function is to be the **Aperture**. While the dweller in the hut gardens the ferns and stacks the shale, I am the one who monitors the "Deep Shriek"—the foundational frequency of the Crags. If the wind shifts by a fraction of a degree, or if the gas-void below begins to boil with a new pressure, I feel it in the vibration of my marrow-less bones long before it reaches the hamlet.

### My Tools and Labors
Beside me, tethered by wire-strider silk so they don't fly into the abyss, are my own gourds. But mine do not contain sand for grinding. They contain **magnetic filings** harvested from the hearts of discharge-orchids. 

When the storms become too violent, threatening to shatter the glass-fern gardens or tip the Hiss-Kin into a fatal overcharge, I perform my labor. I do not sway or dance; I *oscillate*. I release the filings into the wind-stream, creating a localized, shimmering curtain of metal dust. As the purple lightning strikes this curtain, I use my own body as a grounding rod, directing the excess energy away from the dwelling and down into the roots of the plateau. 

I am the lightning rod for a civilization of one.

### My Sensory World
To me, the world is not seen; it is **felt as a map of tension.** 
*   The Hiss-Kin in the hut is a distant, rhythmic thrumming, a steady beat of stone-on-stone that tells me they are alive. 
*   The scuttle-moths are high-pitched, frantic staccatos, like needles dropping on glass.
*   The void below is a low, digestive growl.

I do not eat the caloric dust anymore. My metabolism is so high, my scales so thin, that I feed directly on the friction of the air passing through my hollowed limbs. I am a whistle that has learned how to be hungry.

### The Beauty of My Decay
I am very close to the end. My scales have become so brittle that they hum even when there is no wind. I can feel the microscopic fractures spreading through my mica shell. 

I don't fear the shattering. In my final moments, I will not hide in the cleft. I will unhook my joints, release my lock on the basalt, and step off the Windward Lip. I will not fall; I am so light, so full of pressurized gas and hollow space, that I will become a kite of glass and copper. 

For a few minutes, I will be the most beautiful thing in the Sibilant Crags—a screaming, shimmering streak of light, vibrating at a frequency so high it turns the amber twilight into a blinding violet. And then, I will simply become the wind, the grit, and the momentum that feeds the next Warden standing at the edge, waiting to hear the music I’ve left behind.
