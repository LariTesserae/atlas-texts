I am the friction. I am the thing that does not part.

### The Body
My skin is a gray, abrasive miracle. To you, it would feel like the tongue of a god-sized cat, a surface of microscopic interlocking hooks. It does not bleed. It does not bruise. When I throw myself onto a forty-five-degree slope of unweathered basalt, I do not fall; I flow. I tuck my chin to my collarbone, protecting the sensitive vibration-pits in my jaw, and I become a projectile of cured sharkskin. The heat of the friction is the only warmth I trust—a searing, righteous sting that tells me I am moving faster than the things that want to eat me.

I have no name that can be spoken. In the roar of the Sunder-Striders, a name is a mouthful of wasted air. I am a rhythm. In the "Quiet"—that ten-minute cathedral of steam—my companions know me by the way I drum my knuckles against the obsidian: *three fast taps, one heavy drag.*

### The Pulse
The steam begins to hiss. I feel it first in my marrow. The air, already heavy, turns into a physical weight. This is the **Sweat**.

I am perched on a rubbery indigo stalk, my toes gripped into the sensory hairs that twitch against my soles. Below me, a Sunder-Strider is stitching its way through a grove of fungal pillars. Each time its needle-leg punches the rock, the vibration travels up the stalk and into my skeleton, a violent, rhythmic jolt that would shatter human teeth. To me, it is a map. I know exactly where the beast is by the frequency of the rock’s protest.

The steam hits. The world vanishes into a white, caustic fog. The metallic roar of the beetles and the grinders reaches a fever pitch, then plateaus into a flat, deafening drone. 

Now. This is when I move.

### The Hunt for Softness
I am hunting the **Glint-Moths**. They are beautiful, glass-winged terrors, but during the Pulse, their silica wings become heavy with condensation. They are forced to land on the undersides of the velvet vines to shed the weight. 

I launch. I don't use a ladder; I use the momentum of the world’s collapse. I slide down the length of a gargantuan violet stalk, the friction singing through my ribs. I reach the vine-bridge and catch it with one hand. The Velvet-Strangler tries to contract, its caustic enzymes weeping from its pores, but my hide is indifferent to its hunger. I am too dry, too hard, too fast.

I find the Moth. It is a shard of frozen violet light, trembling. Its wings are slick with the oil-moisture of the Sink. I do not use a tool; I use my weight. I drop my obsidian anchor—a three-pound wedge of black glass lashed to my waist—and let it swing. The impact is a dull *thud* lost in the roar. 

I take the "softness" inside the moth—the phosphoric jelly that powers its flight. It tastes of ozone and fermented sugar. It is the only soft thing I will touch today. It is the only soft thing I am allowed to be.

### The Weight
The steam begins to recede. The "dry-hiss" is starting—the sound of the porous rock drinking the world back down. The temperature spikes. The air begins to thin, turning back into the sandpaper-wind that flays the weak.

I feel a shift in the vibration-pits of my collarbone. A swarm is coming. Not a small one. A **Blanket-Crawl**—millions of finger-sized Chitin-Grinders moving in a single, undulating wave of hunger. 

I do not run. You cannot outrun the swarm in the Shatter-Sink. 

I find a narrow fissure in the basalt, a vertical wound in the earth’s side. I wedge myself in. I take my anchor and jam it into a cross-crack above my head, locking my mineral-grafted limbs into the stone. I become a knot in the geology.

The swarm hits. It is a river of clicking legs and gnashing plates. They pour over me, their mandibles scraping against my skin, looking for a seam, a soft spot, a way in. They find none. I am as closed as a tomb. I close my nictitating membranes and listen to the music of their frustration—a trillion tiny saws trying to cut a mountain.

### The Reflection
In the Shatter-Sink, there is no "yesterday." There is only the layer of rock beneath me. There is no "tomorrow." There is only the next Pulse.

I am a masterpiece of trauma, yes. But as I hang here, anchored to a world that is trying to grind itself into dust, I feel a strange, piercing joy. I am the only thing in this bowl of razors that knows how to be still. I am the ghost in the machine of the blender.

The roar continues. The indigo stalks grow another inch. Somewhere, a Sunder-Strider dies and is replaced by a thousand things that will eat it before it hits the ground.

I wait for the next steam-pulse. I wait for the Quiet. I have a rhythm to drum for my kin, a message sent through the stone:

*I am here. I am hard. I am still sliding.*
