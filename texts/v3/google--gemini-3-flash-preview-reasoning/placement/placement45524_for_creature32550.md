My work is to listen for the **Slack**. 

In a world made of silk and spit, the greatest threat is not the predator, but the loosening. If a cable frays, if the obsidian wall rejects a silk-anchor, the harmony of the district shifts. I feel the discordance in my joints. A single loose thread a mile away feels like a splinter in my own shell. 

I move now, not by walking, but by falling with intention. I release my upper grips and swing, my weight pulling the silk taut. The air tastes of dry ozone and the metallic tang of the Lace-Ferns. I do not breathe, for there is no moisture to draw; I absorb the thin, electric atmosphere through the spiracles in my sides, a constant, dry cooling that tastes of flint and ancient suns.

I reach a junction where a bridge of woven fiber meets the "final-form" obsidian of the fissure wall. The stone here is so smooth it feels like frozen liquid. I see my own reflection in the dark mirror—a cluster of copper-colored eye-pits glowing with the dim, internal light of my own nervous system. 

I am here because I felt a **Shudder**.

Not the frantic Shudder of a hunt, but the deep, geologic groan of the Spine itself. The wind above is changing. I press my maniples against the obsidian. The vibration is low, a frequency that bypasses the silk and speaks directly to the bone. The Dust-Tide is coming early.

I begin to drum.

The Strut-Song changes. The frantic activity of the city shifts from construction to fortification.
