I am a **Seam-Walker**, one of the Lignin-Folk whose rind has grown thick and scarred from the constant abrasive screech of the growth-front. 

I do not have a name that can be spoken, for my mouth is a vestigial slit that only knows the intake of oxygen-heavy air. My name is a rhythm: *three sharp strikes of a gourd-hammer followed by a long, vibrating drag across a serrated edge.* In the cacophony of the Lacuna, that is who I am.

***

### The Vibe of My Being
I exist in a state of perpetual, vibrating tension. To you, the Lacuna is a visual wonder; to me, it is a map of pressures. 

My crown of fronds is never still. They are my "eyes," but they do not see light—they see *stress*. I feel the way a bridge feels the weight of a truck. I can feel the tectonic pressure of the granite shell miles above us, pressing inward, and the frantic, explosive outward push of the chlorophyll-engine. I am caught between the two, a tiny, translucent needle of living fiber.

My skin is the color of an unripe lime, and when I stand atop a primary vein, the golden light makes my bones—my bundles of dense, woody tubing—glow like the filament of a lamp. I move in a series of calculated, jerky snaps. To move slowly is to be crushed by the expanding leaves; to move too fast is to lose my grip and be claimed by the Void.

### My Location
I am currently perched on the **Apex of the Third Fold**. 

Imagine a leaf the size of a cathedral, curved like the hull of a ship, glowing with an internal fire that never dims. I am on the very edge—the "Serrated Rim." Below me, or perhaps beside me—for gravity here is a fickle, localized magnet—is the Great Central Void. It is a mile-wide throat of golden air, where grapefruit-sized globes of water drift like lazy, liquid moons. 

I am tethered. A mile of hand-spun vine-fiber is coiled around my waist, anchored back in the "Quiet Crevices" where the Pulse-Lichen grows. I am out here because the Lacuna is screaming.

### My Work
The sound is a mechanical agony today. *Skreeeee-CRACK.* 

A new vine, thick as a skyscraper, is erupting from the base of the sphere. It is growing at the speed of a walking man. If it is left to its own devices, it will spear through the delicate canopy of the upper "sky" and shatter the geometry that allows the oxygen-currents to flow. The Lacuna would choke on its own carbon.

I am the friction.

I swing my petrified-gourd hammer. *Thump. Thump. Throb.* I am not trying to break the vine; I am talking to it. I am striking the tension-points, guiding the cellular expansion. By bruising the plant matter in a specific rhythmic pattern, I force the vine to curl, to braid itself into the existing structure rather than piercing it. 

I am a gardener who uses a sledgehammer. I am the reason this world has a "floor" instead of being a lethal briar patch of obsidian-sharp spikes.

### A Moment in the Life
A globe of water, heavy and shimmering, drifts near. I feel its mass through the displacement of air in my fronds. I reach out a four-jointed finger, my leathery skin rippling. The water is cool, a shock against the midsummer fever of the air. It touches a pore on my forearm and is gone—sucked instantly into my capillary bundles. 

Suddenly, the leaf beneath me *shudders*. 

It is a "Growth-Quake." Somewhere a mile "above," a titanic vine has snapped. The sound hits me like a physical blow—a thunderclap that ripples the golden air. 

I crouch, my toes locking into the serrations of the leaf. I see the Glider-Mites beginning to descend. From my perspective, they look like falling shards of a yellow sun, their amber bodies catching the light and throwing rainbows across the green abyss. They are beautiful, but they are scavengers. They head for the site of the snap to drink the pressurized sap before it can drown the Lignin-Folk living in the lower folds.

### My Philosophy of Existence
I do not think of the future. The Lacuna does not allow it. The leaf I stand on today was a tiny bud yesterday; tomorrow, it will be pushed so far into the "sky" that it will be crushed against the granite ceiling of the world.

I am a servant of the **Pulse-Lichen**. Every few cycles, I return to the deep crevices and press my fronds against the violet moss. I feel the "Rote." I feel the memories of Seam-Walkers who lived ten thousand growth-cycles ago. They felt the same heat. They hammered the same veins. 

We are a mistake in the system, yes. We are parasites. But as I look out into the golden void, watching the water-globes dance in the light of a world that builds itself a hundred times a day, I know we are necessary. 

The Lacuna is a god of pure, unbridled growth. And a god of pure growth needs a gardener to keep it from growing itself to death. 

I raise my hammer. The vine is curling. The rhythm is correct. I am the oil in the machine, and today, the machine will not break.
