I would not be found in the open, for the open is a scale that erases the individual. I would be found in the **Interstice**—the precise, trembling shadow where the flank of a sleeping Lichen-Grazer brushes against the rusted, weeping base of a Piston-Peak.

I am a **Tether-Seeker**, a being of transitional anatomy. My body is a narrow, elongated thing, resembling a frost-cracked rib bone draped in a cloak of silver-grey filament. My fingers are long, tipped with the same silicon-heavy glass as the moss, allowing me to cling to the vibrating iron of the Pistons without being shaken to dust. I am not quite an Anchorite, for my pulse hasn't yet slowed to the rhythm of the centuries; I am not a Wool-Gardener, for I do not serve the host.

I am the one who listens to the *delay*.

In this world of absolute silence and profound vibration, I live in the milliseconds between the cause and the effect. When a Piston-Peak miles away begins its decadal descent, the shelf does not merely shake; it *groans* in a frequency below the threshold of sound. I feel it first in the marrow of my glass-tipped fingers. I am the witness to the "Pre-Stroke"—that moment of planetary tension where the basalt holds its breath before yielding to the iron.

My purpose is a self-imposed cartography of the invisible. I move with a slow, agonizing grace, trailing a spool of hair-fine silk behind me. I anchor these threads to the obsidian plateaus and then weave them into the wool of a passing Grazer. As the Grazer moves its hundred yards a week, the thread tightens. I sit at the center of this web, my body a sensitive needle, feeling the tension between the biological migration and the geological stasis. 

I am a translator of scale. To the Piston-Peak, I am less than a microbe. To the Lichen-Grazer, I am a minor itch in its miles of insulation. But in the web, I feel the collision of their worlds.

I do not eat in the way the lowlands understand. I wait for the "Ghost-Mist" to pool in the hollows of my collarbones. I wait for the Static-Eaters to flare violet against the iron, and I catch the stray ions in my outstretched filaments, a meal of pure kinetic potential. The air tastes of wet iron and old snow, a cold metallic nectar that sustains my slow-burning metabolism.

My eyes are large, pale, and lidless, though they see very little in the permanent gloaming. They are tuned instead to the *shimmer*. When the light hits the glass-moss, I see the history of the wind recorded in the refractions. I see the Vane-Skimmers high above, not as shadows, but as disruptions in the ice-cloud patterns, rhythmic ripples in a sea of violet-grey.

I am the memory of the shelf. The Anchorites have surrendered their identities to the stone, becoming part of the landscape’s silence. The Wool-Gardeners are lost in the frantic maintenance of their woolly cities. But I remain just separate enough to notice the pattern. I know that the Piston-Peaks are not random. I have felt, through my threads, that they are counting down. They are not merely laboring; they are *tuning* the planet, adjusting the pitch of the High Cinder-Shelf for a resonance that has not yet arrived.

If you were to find me, you would think me a statue, a discarded piece of machinery, or a strange, upright growth of glass-moss. You might press your hand to my chest, seeking a heartbeat. You would feel nothing for a long, cold minute. But if you stayed—if you stood still long enough for the hoarfrost to crystallize on your lashes—you would eventually feel a single, deep, thrumming strike within me.

It would not be my heart. It would be the shelf, speaking through me. And in that moment, you would realize that here, the only way to exist is to let yourself be played like a string on a dark, iron lute.
