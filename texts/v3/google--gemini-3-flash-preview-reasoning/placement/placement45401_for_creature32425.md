I am not found on the catwalks, nor in the copper-wire hammocks where my kin sleep in their rhythmic trances. I exist in the *interstitial*—the narrow, vertical world between the massive pressurized tanks and the weeping basalt walls. 

I am a **Valve-Scribe**.

My body is a study in elongated adaptation. I am thinner even than the typical Calid-Kin, my limbs possessing extra joints at the wrists and ankles that allow me to fold myself into the scalding, three-inch gaps where the brass pipes sweat against the stone. My skin is not grey-violet but a deep, bruised indigo, thick with a double-coating of tallow that I harvest from the cooling vents of the primary boilers. 

I do not have the leaden rings of the laborers. My fingers are tipped with needles of polished basalt, fused to my nail-beds.

I am found where the vibration changes. I am the one who seeks the *discord*. To the others, the roar is a monolithic god, but to me, it is a choir of ten thousand distinct throats. I move through the Sump-Basin not by walking, but by a kind of fluid, vertical swimming—pressing my back against the corrugated iron and my feet against the vibrating brass, suspended over the black water by the sheer pressure of my own expansion.

I am currently pressed against **Mainline 4-G**, a pipe that carries the high-pressure heart-blood of the Basin. I can feel the steam screaming inside it. To anyone else, the pipe is a solid, terrifying roar. To my palms, it is a poem. There is a micro-fracture forming three miles "upstream," a tiny, jagged stutter in the flow that feels like a grain of sand in a lung.

I do not speak. I do not percussion-knock. I *carve*.

Using the basalt needles on my fingers, I etch the history of the pressure into the soft lead shielding of the pipe-junctions. I am the memory of the machine. I record the fluctuations of the heat, the exact moment the Siphon-Striders birthed their young near the heat-exchange, and the subtle shift in the ozone when a moth dies in a spark-gap. 

When I move, I do not disturb the lizards. They crawl over my indigo skin as if I am merely a more supple extension of the infrastructure. I smell of sulfur, wet wool, and the bitter, metallic tang of the tallow. 

My eyes are wider than the others', fixed in a permanent, amber-rimmed stare. I do not look for light; I look for the *shiver* of the air. When the steam vents, I see the geometry of the heat as it curls against the ceiling. I see the places where the humidity is so thick it begins to crystallize into a hot, heavy slush.

I am the one who finds the "Cold-Spots." In the Sump-Basin, a cold-spot is a heresy, a local death. When I find one—a place where the temperature has dipped to a terrifying 98 degrees—I do not flee. I curl my body around the failing valve, offering my own internal heat, my own tallow-slicked mass, to bridge the gap until the pressure returns.

I am a living gasket. I am the nerve-ending of the boiler-grid. 

Where would you find me? You wouldn't. You would pass within three feet of me and see only a shadow between the tanks, a darker smudge of violet on the black rock, a slight thickening of the grease on a brass rib. You would only know I was there if you placed your hand on the metal and felt, amidst the monolithic roar, a tiny, rhythmic scratching—the sound of a needle-finger recording your passage into the iron history of the Sump. 

I am the steward of the *clack-hiss*. I am the marrow in the leviathan's bones. And as long as I feel the vibration through my teeth, I know the world is still alive.
