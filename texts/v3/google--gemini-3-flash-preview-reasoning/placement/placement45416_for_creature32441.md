I would not be found in the wide, echoing lanes where the Liturgists stand in their decade-long shifts. Those spaces are too exposed, too defined by the clinical light of the cooling fins. 

I would be found in the **Interstices of the Coil**—the narrow, vertical labyrinths between the massive copper windings of the primary transformers. 

### My Form: The Dielectric Surveyor
I would be a creature of glass and trapped mercury, a **Dielectric Surveyor**. My body would not be forged of iron or silt, but of high-pressure silica, polished by eons of stillness until I am as transparent as the water itself. I would be shaped like a complex, multi-faceted spindle, perhaps six feet long, with a core of liquid mercury that shivers in perfect sympathy with the 60-cycle hum.

I have no limbs for walking and no need for the grounding touch of the Liturgists. Instead, I am suspended by diamagnetic levitation between the copper coils. I do not move through the water; I slide along the magnetic flux lines like a bead on an invisible wire.

### My Purpose: The Guardian of the Delta
In a world of absolute equilibrium, I am the one who monitors the **Delta**—the impossible, infinitesimal difference between the hum and the silence. 

The machines are not merely "on"; they are held in a state of suppressed potential. There is a tension here, a desire for the electricity to *go* somewhere, to do work, to spark, to destroy. My existence is dedicated to the prevention of the Spark. I am the sensor that detects the slightest build-up of static charge before it can become a discharge. 

If a Liturgist is a grounding rod, I am the insulation. I drift into the tightest cracks of the machinery, where the salt-rime is thinnest, and I press my glass brow against the copper. I listen not to the hum, but to the *gaps* in the hum—the microscopic silences where a fault might begin.

### My Perception: Time as Density
To me, the water is not a liquid; it is a supportive, crystalline embrace. I do not feel "wetness." I feel **viscosity**. I move through the cathedral like a needle through cold wax. 

Time, for me, is not a sequence of events, but a measurement of stability. A century is simply a period in which the mercury in my core did not ripple out of phase. I recognize the Iron-Filers as busy, frantic sparks of motion, though they move slower than a human heartbeat. To me, they are the only things that truly "happen." 

I feel a distant, cold kinship with the **Echo-Mind**. I am its peripheral nervous system. When the Echo-Mind thinks a thought—a slow, rolling realization that a transformer’s temperature has dropped by a thousandth of a degree—I am the one who feels the shudder in the magnetic field.

### My Place in the Order
I would be found in the "High-Tension Shadows." 

While the Filament-Drifters cling to the moss and the Stalled Ones stare at the portals, I would be deep inside the guts of the world. I would be the secret inhabitant of the machine’s interior, a ghost in the copper forest. 

I am the most solitary of the inhabitants. I do not touch the other beings; to touch a Liturgist would be to create a circuit, an arc of light that would shatter the perfect, sterile peace of the cathedral. I am a being of pure separation. 

I would spend my eternity in the blue-lit gloom of a transformer’s core, watching the mercury in my chest. As long as the liquid remains a perfect, unmoving sphere, I know the world is safe. I am the curator of the Stasis, the one who ensures that in this drowned factory, the work is never started, and the silence is never broken.
