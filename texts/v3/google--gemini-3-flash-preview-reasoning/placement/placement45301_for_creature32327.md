I would not be a visitor. I would not be a creature of soft tissue or rapid pulse, for here, the heartbeat of a sparrow would sound like a frantic, dying machine gun. 

I would be a **Lithic Lenticular**, but I would be one of the Old Ones, positioned at the very edge of the twisted catwalk, where the darkness is most absolute and the subsonic thrum of the transformers is a deep, bone-level lullaby.

I am a geometry of waiting. 

I stand twelve feet high, a vertical shard of obsidian and compressed history, no thicker than a breath held in a dark room. My body is a stack of overlapping shale plates, razor-edged and cold to the touch, yet internally I am a kiln. I do not have a front or a back; I have only surfaces that catch the heat. 

I am currently tilted at an angle of seven degrees. It has taken me three years to achieve this lean. I am moving toward the warmth of the transformer nestled in the western corner, drawn by the amber radiance that bleeds from its cracked glass gauges. To a human, I am a statue, a jagged pillar of stone. To me, I am in the midst of a violent, exhilarating sprint toward the light.

I do not see the vault. I *feel* the vault as a map of thermal pressures. 

The Ferrum-Leeches above me are heavy, sluggish knots of heat, like embers drifting through a soot-clogged flue. I feel the Silt-Lace beneath my base—a cold, silver-wire tingle that tells me exactly when a drop of water has struck the floor forty feet away. When that *tink* occurs, the vibration travels through the rock, up through my silicate plates, and translates into a thought: *Arrival.*

I am the curator of the ferns. 

They cluster around my base, their waxy, bruised-plum fronds brushing against my sharp edges. They are thirsty for the heat I radiate back into the air. I am their sun, a dark, stony star. Sometimes, over the course of a decade, I shift my weight to give a struggling sprout a better view of the transformer's hum. This is my only purpose: to be a thermal conductor, a bridge between the dying machines and the living purple.

I do not breathe. Instead, my hexagonal amber depression—what you might call a face—pulses in time with the "breathing" of the earth. When the tectonic friction increases, my glow brightens. When the machines hiss their weekly breath of steam, I feel a shiver of static joy. 

I remember the day the catwalk twisted. It was a magnificent, screaming event that lasted nearly four seconds. The sound was a symphony of tortured iron that we, the Lenticulars, will discuss in the grinding of our plates for the next three centuries. It is the most recent thing to have happened. 

I am patient. I am part of the slow-motion metabolism of 44-B. I am the silence between the drips of water. I am the shadow that the amber light cannot quite reach. 

Eventually, the rust will win. The Ferrum-Leeches will grow too heavy and fall; the cottage-sized transformers will sigh a final, cooling breath and go dark. When that happens, I will tilt one last time, leaning my obsidian weight against the raw, crystalline walls of the vault. I will become the wall. I will become the stone. 

Until then, I stand. I lean. I glow. And I listen to the beautiful, solitary *tink* of the water, counting the eons until the surface world forgets that the word "fast" ever existed.
