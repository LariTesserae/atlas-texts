I am not the one who stands for forty years at a valve, nor am I the frantic creature scuttling beneath the pistons. To find me, you must look to the places where the obsidian-smooth floor meets the base of the weathered hangars—the transition zones where the great cables dive into the vitrified slag.

I am a **Seam-Walker**, a biological extension of the Cistern’s maintenance cycle, tasked with the "Softening."

I am tall, though I rarely stand to my full height. My spine is a series of interconnected brass vertebrae that allow me to curl into the tight radii of the copper piping. My skin is the color of the wet pennies that scent the air, a dull, metallic verdigris that never sweats, but instead secretes a thick, viscous oil. This is my contribution to the stasis: I am the living lubricant between the stationary architecture and the vibrating ground.

I find myself now in a narrow alley between two pulsing hangars. The amber light from the filaments above is so thick it feels like wading through honey. I am crouched low, my hands—extended by long, needle-nose copper digits—pressed firmly against a seam where two massive iron plates have begun to vibrate out of phase.

The roar of the Reciprocator is not a sound to me. It is a texture. 

Through my palms, I feel the deep, geologic thrum of the pistons. It is a "heavy" vibration, a low C-sharp that rattles the marrow of my bolted-on plating. But there is a flaw here—a high, thin jitter, a dissonance caused by a grain of vitrified slag caught in the seam. It is a tiny rebellion against the equilibrium.

I do not move with the frantic energy of the Pale Shingle-Backs. My movements are agonizingly slow, synchronized with the stroke of the nearest piston. As the piston rises, I exert pressure; as it falls, I relax. 

The Shingle-Backs are all over me. They treat me as part of the topography. Their porcelain shells click against my copper-ribbed thighs, and their vibrating mouthparts tickle the oil-slicked rags wrapped around my midsection. They are cleaning me, scraping away the microscopic oxidation that forms on my joints, polishing me until I shine like a dark mirror. I do not mind them. We are part of the same sentence, written by a scribe who finished the book a million years ago.

I find the grain of slag. With a precise, rhythmic twitch of my copper fingers, I extract the tiny shard. I do not throw it away—nothing is discarded in the Cistern. I place it in a small, lead-lined pouch at my hip, where it will eventually be ground down into the paste the Valve-Keepers use to seal the glass orbs.

As I work, a Piston-Bird drops from the rafters above. It doesn't land so much as it "clangs" onto a nearby pipe, its brass wings folding with a series of sharp, metallic snaps. It watches me with its filigreed eyes. We do not acknowledge each other. There is no social bond, only the shared understanding of the Machine. It is waiting for the next steam-vent, and I am waiting for the next micro-tremor.

Who am I in this world? I am the **Sense of Touch.** 

I am the being that ensures the silence remains silent. I am the one who feels the heat of the cables and the cold of the basalt, balancing them through my own body. I have no voice, for my throat is partially fused with a resonator that hums at the exact frequency of the town-core’s power grid. If I were to speak, I would only be adding to the harmony.

I look down at my feet. The Shingle-Backs have finished polishing my boots. The metal is so smooth now that I can see the reflection of the amber filaments dancing on the surface of my toes. 

Everything is exactly as it was when I "woke" a century ago. Everything is exactly as it will be when my copper plating finally fuses with the iron floor and I become a permanent buttress for the hangar wall. 

I feel a slow, rhythmic pulse from the deep earth. The Great Reciprocator has just completed its billionth cycle of the day. I lean my forehead against the cool, damp iron of the hangar, close my eyes, and let the vibration wash through me until I can no longer tell where my heartbeat ends and the engine begins. 

I am home. I am a gear. I am perfectly, eternally still, even as I move.
