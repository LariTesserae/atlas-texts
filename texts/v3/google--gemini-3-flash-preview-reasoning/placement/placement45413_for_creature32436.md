I am **Elu**, and I live in the Middle-Ribs, where the sepia light is thickest and the scent of parched cinnamon never leaves the back of my throat.

I would find myself not in the hollowed-out chambers of the mud-brick huts, nor on the stagnant ledges of the Siphoners, but suspended. I am a **Tether-Warden**. In a world that is entirely vertical, the spaces between the walls are as much a home as the stone itself. I live within the web.

***

**My Form**
My skin is the matte charcoal of a cooled hearth, but you would rarely see the true color of it. I am gilded in the work of my life: a thick, iridescent layer of violet "breath-moss" spores and the silver dander of the succulents clings to my sweat. To you, I might look like a statue carved from velvet and bruised fruit. 

My ribcage is a heavy, rounded vault. When I breathe, I do not gulp; I sip. I feel the mineral weight of the air settling in my lungs, a slow, comforting sedimentation. My hands and feet are not like yours—they are wider, the pads of my fingers cushioned with a dense, microscopic nap that sighs when it meets the obsidian. I do not "grip" the rock so much as I marry it, my skin finding the invisible texture in the polished glass.

**My Place**
You would find me perched on a "Widow’s Thread"—a bridge of silver-leaf husk as thick as my waist, spanning the fifty-meter void between the East and West faces. I do not walk across it. I lie along its length, my belly pressed to the fibrous braid, feeling the vibrations of the entire hamlet move through the rope. 

Below me, the fissure drops away into a warm, humming dark where the ancestors turn to glass. Above me, the needle of Auros pierces a sky so thin it has turned a deep, bruised indigo. But here, in the middle, I am in the heart of the thrum.

**My Work**
I am a listener. I maintain the "Chorus Lines," the long, tension-tuned ropes that connect the high granaries to the lower dwellings. 

I spend my hours moving with the agonizing slowness of a growing succulent. I carry a small gourd of Hexaptera-wax and a bone needle. I feel for the "shiver"—the specific, high-pitched frequency that signals a rope is fraying or a mud-anchor is loosening. When I find a weak spot, I do not hurry. I settle. I might spend three sun-cycles in a single spot, weaving new silver-fibers into the old, my movements synchronized with the rhythmic *tack-tack-tack* of the Lithic Skinks scuttling nearby.

**My Companions**
In the Mid-Glow, when the heat from the stone is at its most feverish, a Hexaptera named *Krr-thum* often finds me. He is the size of a mountain dog, his six wings folded like plates of dark, iridescent armor. He does not fear me, nor I him. He crawls over me, his hooked legs moving with a terrifying but practiced delicacy, and settles his massive thorax against my back. 

We become a single mound of heat and vibration. His low, bone-shaking hum matches the resonance of my own chest. As he drinks the fermented sap from the vials at my belt, I feel the frequency of the entire Spire through him. I can feel the Siphoners milking the plants three hundred meters up; I can feel the slow, tectonic shift of the obsidian cooling in the heights. 

**My Philosophy**
I have no concept of "falling." To fall is simply to return to the warmth of the deep. I have no concept of "away." There is only the Wall, the Other Wall, and the Web between them.

I am a creature of the stillness within the noise. While the beetles roar and the skinks click, I am the silence that holds the rope. I am the patience of the obsidian. I am a curator of the "Grip," ensuring that as we move through our long, slow centuries, we do not slip from the bone of the world. 

When my lungs finally tire of the mineral haze, I will not be mourned with tears—for moisture is too precious to waste—but with a final, resonant hum. I will be wrapped in my own tether-ropes and lowered into the deep, where the heat will slowly turn my violet-dusted skin into the very glass I spent my life tending. I will become a new ledge. I will become the ground that my children’s children will learn to climb.
