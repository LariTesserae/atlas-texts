I am not on the Reeds. I am not in the baskets. To find me, you must look away from the white, polished bone of the world and into the terrifying, sun-drenched nothingness between.

I am a **Bridge-Tender**, a solitary Weaver who has succumbed to a mild, functional form of *The Weight*. While my kin huddle in the woven security of the Hanging Baskets, lashing themselves to the vertical grooves of the Great Reeds, I live my life in the horizontal tension.

### Where I am
I am currently suspended three miles above the emerald gloom, exactly halfway across a shimmering umbilical of Spider-Steel that spans the six-mile gulf between the *Grand Aethel-Reed* and the *Spire of Mourning*. 

My home is a "Cradle"—a specialized, aerodynamic pod woven from cured vine-husks and lined with the soft, bioluminescent velvet of the Strangler-Orchid (carefully neutralized with mist-brine). The Cradle is no larger than a coffin, tethered to the main bridge-line by pulleys of Piston-Wasp mandible. I do not live on a surface; I live on a vibration.

To my left, the Aethel-Reed rises like a titanic, petrified ivory tusk, its vertical rivers glinting in the turquoise sun like weeping silver veins. To my right, the Spire of Mourning is a needle thin enough to pierce the sky, shrouded in the shifting murmurs of a trillion iridescent flyers. Below me is the emerald steam—the mouth of the world—and above me is the silent, thinning air where the Spire-Wraiths drift.

### Who I am
I am a creature of callouses and tension. My skin is the color of a bruised lime, stained deep by the moss-juices I use to treat the bridge-lines. My toes are longer than a typical Weaver's, the joints swollen and powerful, capable of locking onto a silk thread with the permanence of a dead-man's grip. 

I carry the **Resonance-Bow**, a long, curved rib of polished cellulose strung with a single, high-tension strand of Gorge-Spider silk. I am not a musician, though I play the wind. My job is to "tune" the bridges. As the Great Reeds sway in the high-altitude gusts, the tension on the silk lines can become terminal. If the vibration reaches a certain harmonic, the silk will shatter, and the trade-route between the tribes will fall into the mulch.

I spend my days sliding along the lines, pressing my bow against the silk to feel the "pulse" of the spires. I listen to the Drummer-Beetles, not as a roar, but as a metronome. If the beetles on the Aethel-Reed strike out of sync with the beetles on the Spire of Mourning, I know the wind-pressures are shifting. 

### My Existence
I am the only one who does not fear the "Horizontal Taboo" because I have made the horizontal my vertical. When I sleep, I lash my torso to the Cradle and let the wind toss me in mile-wide arcs. I have forgotten the smell of the damp, interior moss-mats; I smell only the ozone of the high air and the sharp, metallic tang of the Piston-Wasps that occasionally hitch a ride on my pod.

I am a ghost to my people. They see my Light-Lance flashes from the distant grooves—*Line holding. Tension true.*—and they think of me as a necessary sacrifice to the void. They believe I am "calling the mulch," that I am slowly falling even while I stay aloft. 

But they are wrong. 

In the middle of the chasm, the roar of the world changes. It isn't a deafening weight anymore. It becomes a single, pure note. In the center of the gulf, I am far enough from the Reeds that the sound of the trillion insects loses its grit and becomes a shimmering, golden hum. 

Sometimes, a **Float-Drifter** passes near me. I see the Sky-Skippers peering over the edge of their mossy island, their eyes wide with the same vertigo my people fear. We stare at each other—two nomads of the emptiness—until the wind carries them toward the horizon of needles. 

I am the **Tether-Soul**. I am the thin, vibrating consciousness that keeps the Vertical Strands from drifting apart. I am the man who loves the fall so much he decided to live inside it. When the mist-storms come and the vertical rivers pour down the Reeds, I am out here in the spray, laughing at the turquoise sky, held aloft by nothing but the silk of a spider and the terrifying, beautiful tension of a world that refuses to touch the ground.
