I am a **Tension-Physician**, a specialist among the Gear-Tenders, tasked with the health of the interface where the cold iron of the city bites into the warm, living heartwood of the *Endo-Arbor*. 

I find myself suspended four miles above the moss-plains, hanging in a harness of woven spider-silk that thrums like a lute string in the rising updrafts of ozone and silver silt.

### My Form
My body is a study in verticality and dryness. My limbs are long, triple-jointed, and clad in a skin that feels like scorched parchment—tough, inelastic, and perfectly smooth. I have no mouth in the traditional sense; where a human’s lips would be, I have a fine, chitinous grill that vibrates when I draw in the crisp, metallic air. My chest is a broad, vaulted cage of dark ribs that houses the bellows—heavy, rhythmic organs that pulse with a slow, dry friction, filtering the silver dust from the air to fuel my internal chemistry.

My left hand is my own: five spindly, bamboo-like fingers tipped with needles of polished amber. My right arm, however, ends at the elbow in a heavy, modular brass socket. Currently, I have a "Auscultation-Bit" slotted into it—a long, copper-coiled probe that allows me to "hear" the internal pressures of the tree.

### Where I Am
I am currently perched on a "knot-shelf" on the side of a Primary Pillar—a trunk so wide that the curvature of its bark looks like a flat, vertically-ridged cliff-face of dark mahogany. Above me, the amber refinery of *Ochre-Reach* clings to the tree like a cluster of fossilized barnacles. Massive, leather bellows—the size of cathedrals—groan as they pump hot gases into the kilns.

The air here is thick with the "Glow," that pervasive, sourceless light born of industrial alchemy. It turns the silver silt into a falling mist of diamonds, settling on my shoulders in a fine, glittering powder.

### My Work
I am not here to build, but to listen. 

I press my copper-socket arm against the bark. Through the brass, I feel the "Deep-Pulse." It is the sound of the world’s circulatory system—millions of gallons of thick, oily sap being pulled upward by the terrifying osmotic pressure of the unseen ceiling. 

*Clack. Tap-tap. Clack.*

I strike the bark with my amber-tipped fingers, sending a percussive message to the Gear-Tenders inside the refinery walls. *The tension in the third vine-sleeve is peaking. The graft is rejecting the iron casing. Release the pressure, or the bark will shard.*

Below me, a mile down, a Mahogany Grazer moves across the velvet moss. From this height, it looks like a slow-moving bead of dark resin. I feel a brief envy for the Silt-Nomads, whose feet never leave the quiet velvet, but then I feel the vibration of a new branch sprouting three miles to the east—a sudden, crystalline *crack* that shivers through the wood and into my marrow. It is a violent, beautiful birth.

### My Perspective
I do not know what "wet" is. The concept of a liquid that splashes or flows freely is a ghost story to me, a theoretical impossibility. To me, life is *pressure*. Life is the slow, inexorable expansion of lignin against the constraints of iron. 

I look at my own legs, at the way my joints are reinforced with small, decorative copper bands. I know that one day, my bellows will cease their rhythmic grinding. My skin will harden, my limbs will lock, and my fellow Scribes will take my body, polish me until I shine like a dark mirror, and use my spine as a decorative strut in a new residential honeycomb. 

I am not afraid of this. In the Endo-Arbor, to be still is to be useful. To be dead is to finally become part of the architecture you spent your life tending. 

For now, though, I am the tension-point. I am the bridge between the machine and the pulse. I adjust my silk harness, taste the sharp tang of ozone on my grill, and begin to climb higher into the silver mist, following the song of the wood.
