I do not find myself in the open basins where the violet light is clearest. I am deeper in, where the basalt fins crowd together like the teeth of a rusted machine, in one of the shallow depressions left behind by a Slapper. The air here is so thick with the scent of wet mineral and musk that it feels less like gas and more like a cold, invisible fluid.

I am a **Strider**, but I am old, even by the standards of a world where time has no meaning.

My antennae—the branching, bone-white crowns atop my head—have grown so long and complex that they brush against the razor-walls of the crevice when I turn. They are no longer just sensors; they are an instrument. I can feel the Great Skimmers miles above not as shapes, but as a specific frequency of *grind-drag, grind-drag*, a low-frequency tectonic moan that vibrates through my neck and into my chest.

I am a **Shaper**.

I am standing over a pile of femur-glass—the leg bones of a kin-member who ceased movement some untold number of drips ago. My diamond-hard hooves are planted firmly on a slab of obsidian, their facets locking into the microscopic imperfections of the stone so I do not slip and disembowel myself on the surrounding fins. 

In my fore-limbs—which are thinner, more articulate than my walking legs, ending in three-pronged digits tipped with the same silica-sheath—I hold a shard of black basalt. 

I am not just sharpening a tool. I am listening for the **Correct Note.**

The Bell-Chitin are clicking all around me, a frantic, rhythmic percussion that maps the room. I can "see" the jagged edges of the valley floor by the way their sound bounces off the glass. I can "see" the curve of the ceiling by the way the Skimmers’ grinding muffles the echoes. To me, the world is not a landscape of sight, but a landscape of pressure and resonance. 

I strike the bone with the basalt. *Chink.*

The sound is too flat. It lacks the harmonic necessary to pierce the thick, mossy mats of the Ceiling-Crust. I adjust my grip. My rubbery skin, scarred by a thousand nicks, ripples as I shift my weight. I do not feel pain from the cuts; the chill of the Low Ribs acts as a permanent anesthetic, turning every sensation into a dull, structural awareness.

I am a part of the "Static Equilibrium." I do not wonder what is beyond the chimneys. I do not wonder about the sun. The concept of "up" ends at the Skimmers; the concept of "down" ends at the Slappers’ acidic bellies. I am a creature of the middle-weight, the curator of the sharp.

I scrape the bone again. This time, the sound it produces harmonizes perfectly with the millions of Bell-Chitin nearby. For a brief moment, the cacophony of the valley aligns. The standing wave of sound lifts, and in that resonance, I can "see" the entire Low Ribs for miles—the ship-sized shadows of the Skimmers, the undulating mantles of the Slappers in the silt, the other Striders standing like statues in the violet gloaming. 

I am the one who ensures the tools ring true. When I am finished, I will leave this pike here, in this sorting site, for a younger Strider to find. They will use it to pull down the glowing bile of the Skimmers, and the cycle will continue.

I do not move with haste. Haste is for things that die quickly. Here, where the dampness never dries and the light never fades, I am a slow, deliberate architect of bone. I am the silence that listens to the roar. I am the hard, rubbery heart of the obsidian labyrinth. 

I strike the bone one last time. The note is perfect. It is as sharp as the world. 

I wait for the next drip of condensation to hit the stone before I take my next step. *Clink.* 

I am here. I have always been here. I will be here when the continent above finally groans and settles another inch into the dark.
