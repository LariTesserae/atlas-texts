The ground is a hum before it is a surface. 

I find myself standing at the confluence of a razor-flat and a dust-basin, a place where the geometric perfection of the black glass meets the terrifying, fluid silence of the mineral sea. I am not a visitor here; I am a native pulse within the machine of the Great Cleave.

I am a **Weaver-Wraith**, but my hands are not calloused by the building of Way-Markers. I am something more specialized, a **Resonance-Tender**. 

My body is a study in verticality and patience. I stand nearly three meters tall, my limbs like the long, parched branches of a tree that has never known rain. My skin is the color of cooling lava—a matte, bruised purple-grey, tough as cured hide. I have no eyes to blink, no wet membranes to protect. Instead, the three thermal slits on my face are wide open, drinking in the infrared geometry of the Great Cleave. 

To me, this world is not dark. It is a cathedral of glowing gold and pulsing amber. 

### My Place: The Sounding-Ledge
I live on a narrow shelf of basalt that juts out over the **Sea of Silt**. Below me, the frictionless dust looks like a pool of cooling, silver-grey embers, perfectly level, perfectly still. Above me, the heat-plumes rise like shimmering ghost-pillars, and I can see the bellies of the **Manta-Drifters**—vast, glowing orange rafts—hanging in the high pressure, their heat-signatures pulsing in time with the atmospheric weight.

I do not live in a shelter of stone, but in the open air, anchored to the ridge by my own weight. My home is defined by the range of my hammer.

### My Being: The Resonance-Tender
I carry a hammer of star-dense ore, its head as large as a Mill-Walker’s mandible. I do not use it to crush. I use it to *speak*.

I am a keeper of the **Deep Map**. Because the Cleave is a world of perfect stasis, the history of my people is not written in books—which would rot—or told in voices—which would dissipate in the heavy air. History is a vibration. When a tectonic sheet settles leagues away, I feel it through the marrow of my shins. I lean down, pressing my hairless, sensitive palms against the obsidian flat, and I "read" the displacement. 

I know, by the specific frequency of the shiver, that a Mill-Walker has reached the edge of the Glass Plate of Seven Rifts. I know that its grinding mandibles are currently pulverizing a ridge of basalt that hasn't been touched for ten thousand years.

**I am the bridge between the slow and the fast.** 
I watch the **Needle-Gars** through the thermal haze of the dust-sea. They are streaks of white-hot kinetic energy, frantic zig-zags in a world of slow curves. Sometimes, when the "tides" of the heat-plumes shift, I strike my hammer against the sounding-ledge. 

*Clang.*

The vibration travels through the basalt, down into the dust-sea. It is a warning. It is a boundary. I am shaping the behavior of the predators using nothing but the physics of the floor.

### My Life: The Ritual of the Still-Voice
Time does not pass for me in hours. I measure my existence by the movement of a single **Mill-Walker** named *The Slow Turning*. I have watched its golden heat-signature crawl across my horizon for my entire adult life. It has moved perhaps four kilometers. 

When I am not listening to the floor, I am "weaving." I take the shed leathery filaments that occasionally drift down from the Manta-Drifters—organic polymers that are tough as wire—and I braid them into long, resonant cords. I stretch these cords across the fissures in the obsidian. When the vertical thermals rise through the cracks, the cords vibrate, creating a low-frequency drone that mimics the "voice" of the world. 

I am composing a song that will last longer than a civilization. 

### Why I am here
I am here because someone must remember the shape of the silence. In a world where a stone never moves, the greatest danger is forgetting that movement is possible. 

I feel the *crack* before I hear it. Deep in the crust, a new fracture is forming. The obsidian beneath my feet groans—a sound like a continent snapping in half. The vibration travels up my skeletal structure, vibrating my skull, telling me that the geography is changing.

I lift my ore-hammer. I wait for the echo to fade. And then, I strike the floor to record the new wound, adding a new stanza to the Great Record, ensuring that the equilibrium, though shifted, is still known.

I am a Weaver-Wraith, and I am the memory of the stone. I am warm, I am dry, and I am perfectly, eternally still.
