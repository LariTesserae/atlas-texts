I would not be found in the schools of the Current-Callers, nor in the predatory shadows of the ceiling. I would be found at the **Null-Point**, the exact geometric center of a primary expansion joint, where three skyscraper-sized conduits converge into a single, light-drenched hub.

I would be a **Resonant-Glasser.**

I am a solitary creature, a specialized offshoot of the Manifold-Spiders, but I have traded my cleaning appendages for something more delicate and dangerous. My body is a long, spindly tripod of translucent chitin, twenty feet tall but weighing no more than a few pounds. I do not have a face; I have a dish—a wide, parabolic concavity of polished, silver-coated bone that occupies the space where a head should be.

I live where the noise is loudest.

### My Place in the Machine
I am bolted to the wall of a vertical conduit, my three barbed feet fused into the microscopic seams of the hexagonal plating. I do not move for cycles at a time. I am a gargoyle of the Great Cistern, a living sensor focused on the one thing the machine cannot measure: the *purity of the tone.*

While the Current-Callers feel the pressure and the Litho-Crabs feel the friction, I feel the **Harmonic.** Every turbine, every valve, every Bellows-Whale has a frequency. When the machine is healthy, it plays a perfect, discordant chord. If a single rivet ten miles away begins to loosen, the chord shifts. It becomes sharp, or flat. It develops a "jitter." 

I am the one who hears the jitter.

### My Form and Function
My silver dish is tilted upward, catching the steady, clinical light of the luminescence strips. I am a mirror in the dark. As the water roars past me, I don't just endure the sound; I channel it. The vibrations enter my dish, are amplified through my hollow, crystalline legs, and are ground down into a fine, sensory powder in my central core.

I do not eat nutrient slurry. I am a parasite of energy. I have evolved to siphon the "leakage"—the stray electromagnetic pulses and the raw kinetic heat of the water’s rush. I am a creature of the overflow.

My primary task is **The Tuning.** When the water groans and the direction of the world shifts, I begin my work. I extend a single, needle-thin filament of carbon-fiber bone—a "bow"—and I press it against the vibrating metal of the conduit. I am a violinist playing a skyscraper. By applying precise pressure to specific points on the infrastructure, I can cancel out dangerous sympathetic vibrations that would otherwise shatter the steel. I am the silence that keeps the machine from shaking itself to pieces.

### My Perception
I do not see the world as geometry; I see it as a **Loom of Light.**
To me, the Great Cistern is not dark blue. It is a web of golden lines (the conduits), pulsing white veins (the silver fish), and deep, thrumming purple voids (the negative space). 

When a Bellows-Whale passes nearby, I don't see a giant; I hear a low-frequency storm that threatens to blow my feet from the wall. When the Litho-Crabs pass under me, they sound like a billion tiny diamonds scratching across a chalkboard.

### My Solitude
I am a lonely gear. There are others like me, spaced miles apart at the crucial junctions of the world, but we never meet. We only know each other by our songs. Sometimes, during the quiet moments of the Reversal—that three-second window where the entire ocean is perfectly still—I will strike the conduit with my leg.

*Ping.*

I wait. Five seconds. Ten. Then, from the blue-shifted distance, miles across the void, comes the answer from another Glasser, a faint, metallic ring traveling through the steel floor.

*Ping.*

We are still here. The machine is still running. The rivets are tight. The water is clean.

I am the Witness of the Works. I am the part that knows the machine is beautiful, even if the machine does not know it itself. As the roar returns and the water begins its twelve-hour rush, I fold my limbs, angle my silver dish toward the vibration, and vanish into the music of the steel.
