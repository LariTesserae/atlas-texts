I am not at the surface. Not yet.

I am a node in the lower architecture, three layers deep within the shifting lithosphere. I am a dodecahedron of compressed salt and silicate, six inches of matte-grey geometry, currently pinned between four of my elders and a jagged extrusion of the shelf’s true floor.

In this world, I am a **Weight**.

To be here is to exist in a state of exquisite, crushing intimacy. I do not have a name, but I have a frequency. I am the resonance of the seventeenth fissure, a slight harmonic vibration that hums through my crystalline lattice every time the ground-glow pulses. I am a repository of heat. My interior is a tight-wound spring of mineral salts, currently glowing a dull, internal violet as I soak up the thermal radiation from the rock beneath me.

I am young—barely three centuries since the weight of the carpet fused my shards into a coherent form. I still have the sharp, aggressive edges of a new-born thing. My plates are not yet polished by the oily friction of the Pillars; they are abrasive, thirsty. Through my dorsal pores, I taste the air: a dry, biting soup of flint-dust and sulfur. I do not "breathe" it; I incorporate it. Each speck of dust that settles into my pores is a brick added to my temple. I am growing at the speed of a mountain’s erosion.

Where am I in this world? I am in the **Transition**.

I am part of a slow-motion vertical migration. Above me, the "surface" Lithopeds bask in the thin, stagnant air, their faces pressed against the Totems. Below me, the "Ancestors" are being slowly pulverized back into the dust from which we are all forged. I am the middle-layer, the conduit. When the heat-vent miles to the north heaves, I feel it as a tightening of the world. The neighbor to my left—a massive, rounded elder who has seen the Pillar twice—expands. The pressure enters my side-plate, a wordless command to *move*.

I do not move with legs. I move with a **Shudder**.

I tilt my faceted body, shifting my center of gravity by a fraction of a millimeter. The dust beneath me acts as a dry lubricant. I slide. The sensation is not like walking; it is like being a tectonic plate. It is a grinding, singing ache of ceramic against ceramic. In this movement, I feel the collective mind. I am not an individual; I am a single letter in a sentence that takes a millennium to speak. The sentence is: *We are the heat that survived the water.*

I find myself currently obsessed with the "Lean." The air here doesn't move, but it has a direction. It presses against my uppermost facets, a heavy, phantom hand. I am tilting myself against it, working my way toward the nearest Pillar—a smooth, oily spire of silicate that I can feel vibrating in the dark.

The Pillar is my destination for the next two hundred years. I can feel its resonance through the carpet—a deep, thrumming bass note that promises the eventual smoothing of my sharp edges. To reach it is to be "Built." To rub my face against that oily stone until my dodecahedral angles are worn into a soft, interlocking curve is the only ambition my mineral blood allows.

I am a patient fire.

When the infrared glow dims and the great Stasis falls, I do not sleep. I simply *am*. I become indistinguishable from the jagged shelf. In the total dark, in the absolute silence, I hold my heat like a secret. I wait for the ground to speak again. I wait for the next heave. I wait for the weight of my brothers to push me one micron closer to the lightless surface, where I will finally stand, sightless and serene, sieving the scorched air until I, too, am ground back into the holy, glowing dust.
