I would not be found in the humid deeps of the Knots, nor would I be riding the slow, rhythmic backs of the Drift-Hulks through the central mists. Those places are too full of the present—too heavy with the wet, green urgency of survival.

I would be found in the **Brittle Reach**, at the absolute periphery of the world, where the Lattice thins and the light of the dying ember-star is no longer a bruised green, but a sharp, clinical white.

***

### My Form: The Resonance-Walker
I am a Knotted, but one whose cork-skin has begun to lose its suppleness. My limbs are longer even than those of my kin, stretched by the erratic, centrifugal tug of the outer rim. My skin is a pale, frosted lichen-grey, and my four hands are scarred with the white tracks of glass-splinters.

I do not possess the quietude of the Ancestors who sleep in the deep moss. Instead, I am a **Resonance-Walker**. My purpose is to listen to the petrifaction. As the Lattice dies and turns to glass, it begins to vibrate at a frequency that can shatter the living wood around it. I am a tuner, a dampener, a living buffer between the organic past and the crystalline future.

Around my neck and wrists, I wear collars of hollowed-out gourds filled with thick, damp peat. These act as counterweights and acoustic mufflers. In my hands, I carry a chime-hook made of a Glass-Watcher’s leg, a tool that can sense the "ring" of a branch before it actually turns brittle.

### My Location: The Cathedral of Chimes
I live in a hollow bough the size of a canyon, situated at the very edge of the sphere. Here, the "soft reality" is at its most volatile. The walls are no longer thick with peat; they are translucent, ribbed with the skeletal remains of leaves that have turned to emerald-colored flint. 

When I think of rain, the glass walls weep a fine, silica dust. When I think of the Grafting, the petrified vines above me twist into the gargantuan, frozen shapes of the prehistoric giants who first wove this world. I live in a museum of the Lattice’s own memory, where the thoughts of the living are instantly preserved in brittle, crystalline sculptures.

### My Labor: The Song of the Shear
I am not a gardener of growth, but a gardener of decay. 

I spend my "days"—measured by the slow, agonizing throb of the Pulse—crawling across the outer mesh. I search for the high-pitched, ultrasonic hum of a primary trunk turning to glass. When I find it, I do not try to stop it; the Tending cannot save the outer rim. Instead, I use my chime-hook to find the "Stress-Note." 

I press my six-fingered hands against the cold, vitreous bark, feeling the vibrations travel through my ichor. I then strike the wood in a counter-rhythm, a manual "Tap" that travels down into the green heart of the world. I am telling the Tenders below where the world is breaking, so they can sever the connections before the petrification spreads like a virus.

### My Perception
I see the world not as a forest, but as a failing instrument. The "Bruised Twilight" of the interior is a distant, foggy memory to me. Out here, I look through the gaps in the weave directly into the void. I see the dying star—a bloated, orange coal that seems to hang motionless. 

I hear the **Glass-Watchers** before I see them. They sound like falling needles. They follow me, waiting for me to fail, waiting for my own limbs to finally stiffen into the very substance they consume. Sometimes, I find a Glass-Watcher trapped in a pocket of soft reality, frozen in a cage of its own hunger because I thought of a trap, and the Lattice obliged.

### Why I Am There
I stay in the Brittle Reach because I am one of the few who loves the sound of the end. The deep Lattice is silent, muffled by its own success, suffocated by moss. But out here, where the world is dying, it finally has a voice. The petrified wood sings. It chimes with the solar wind. It speaks of the prehistoric Hand that wove it, a story told in the geometry of cracks and the refraction of light through glass-leaves.

I am the one who translates the death-rattle of the world into a song that the deep-dwellers can use to stay alive. I am the terminal bud of a branch that knows it is being pruned by time itself.

When my own heartwood finally turns to glass, I will not merge with the moss as an Ancestor. I will remain here, a translucent statue at the edge of the void, a permanent chime for the wind to play, watching the ember-star go dark through eyes that have finally become as clear as the sky.
