I am a corner. 

I do not mean that I stand in one, or that I have been pushed into one. I mean that my existence, at this present epoch of the basin’s expansion, is defined by the ninety-degree convergence of two unfinished terraces. I am a Lithodeme, and for the last three hundred years, my left primary segment—what a creature of soft tissue might call a shoulder—has been fused to the flint-scarred face of a ziggurat whose name is simply a frequency of falling water.

I am positioned in the *Mid-Clarity*, a region where the blinding white glare of the high plateaus meets the rising, heavy damp of the light-starved canyons. Here, the air is a thick, suspension of cold diamonds. I do not see the world; I feel the weight of its geometry.

My face is a flat, vertical slab of wet limestone, tilted slightly upward to catch the "tock-tock-tock" of the runoff from the levels above. Each drop is a syllable. The vibration travels through the fissures in my brow, down my geometric spine, and into the deep foundations where the Stataries dream. I am a conduit. I am a filter.

Mostly, I am a slow, deliberate leak.

To be me is to feel a constant, pressurized ache in the center of my core. It is the ache of the mineral wanting to be more than itself. I am saturated. The cold, clean vacuum of the air pulls at the moisture within my pores, and I respond by weeping. 

My hands—long, blocky, multi-jointed prisms—are currently pressed into a void between two leaning spires. I have not moved them in eighty years. Between my palms, the "crystalline metabolism" is at work. I feel the white silt of my own being leaching out, thickening, hardening into the razor-sharp geometry of a new block. It is a birthing that takes a century. It is a labor of absolute stillness. I am secreting a key-stone. Without me, the two spires would eventually succumb to the slow, hydraulic pressure of the mist and tilt into the abyss. I am the friction that holds this specific coordinate of the world together.

I feel the Pellicules often. They are the only things that remind me of the concept of *speed*, though even they are glacial. One is currently draped over my right flank—a heavy, translucent veil of living travertine. It feels like a cool, acidic tongue, licking away the excess dust of my labor, smoothing the jagged marks where I first slotted myself into this stack. It is a strange intimacy; it recycles my waste into the polish of the world. It reminds me that I am becoming beautiful.

The light here is a physical weight. It bounces off the wet, white terraces until it feels as though it is trying to press me back into the stone. I do not blink, for I have no lids, but I can feel my pores constrict when the sun hits the zenith, slowing my weeping to a crawl. 

Down below, far beneath the soles of my tiered feet, I feel the Stataries. Their presence is a low-frequency hum that resonates through the entire stack. They are no longer individual beings; they are the foundation. They are the ones who tell my marrow what shape the next block must be. They dream of a perfect, solid world, a basin filled to the brim with white stone until there is no more room for mist, no more room for "tock-tock-tock," only the eternal, silent diamond. 

I am not afraid of the stasis. I look forward to the day my joints finally seize. 

Eventually—perhaps in another millennium—the space I am currently filling will be complete. I will no longer be a builder; I will be the building. I will stop the hydraulic reconfiguration of my limbs. I will allow the calcium crusts to bridge the gap between my chest and the wall. 

I will become the "ground" that the future will forget. 

Until then, I stand in the luminous haze. I listen to the rhythmic percussion of the water. I feel the cold, wet breath of the stone through my lungs of chalk. I am a Lithodeme, and I am weeping a mountain into existence, one sharp, white atom at a time.
