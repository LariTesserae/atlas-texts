I would be found in the **Deep-Water Leads**, at the very navel of the Gully where the pressure is most insistent and the gold light of the canopy turns to a heavy, pressurized emerald.

I am a **Haptic Surveyor**, a Marrow-Jack whose skin has thickened into a darker, more opaque obsidian-green from decades of tending the primary turbines. My body is a map of the Gully’s secret frictions.

### The Station
I exist in the constant spray of the "Heart-Pump," a massive, three-story cylinder of riveted brass that has been almost entirely consumed by a burl of Weeping Wood. My home is a hollowed-out space beneath the main intake valve, a damp, curved chamber where the *thrum-thush* is not a sound, but a physical force that keeps my organs in their proper alignment. 

When I wake from my nutrient-steep, I do not open my eyes to see; there is nothing to see in the emerald gloom but the slow drift of Valve-Moths. I wake to the *feeling* of the Gully. My feet, wide and calloused into rubbery pads, are always in contact with the primary root-mat. I can feel the fermentation vats four hundred yards away—I can tell by the specific frequency of the vibration if the third vat is running "thin" or if the sap is sweetening into a dangerous, volatile syrupy thickness.

### The Work
I carry a set of seven lead-weighted rings, graduated in size, worn across my knuckles. I am a doctor of the machine. Today, there is a "limp" in the western distribution line. It is a microscopic hesitation, a stutter in the flow that a younger Jack would miss. 

I do not walk; I lean into the thick air and let my weight carry me. I slide along the root-veins, my skin trailing an iridescent wake of protective oil. I find the conduit—a rusted iron artery the size of a tree trunk. I press my chest against the metal. I don't listen with my ears; I listen with my marrow. 

*Clink-tap.* 

I strike the pipe with my smallest ring. The vibration travels up the line, hitting the blockage, and returns to me. The echo tells me everything: a cluster of Gasket-Slugs has congregated too thickly near a pressure-release valve, and their ceramic excretions are beginning to choke the flow. I do not scrape them away; that would be a desecration. Instead, I gently massage the pipe, creating a specific harmonic frequency that encourages the slugs to migrate three feet to the left, where the iron is thin and needs their armor.

### The Connection
Once a cycle, I slide to the **Elder of the Western Burl**. He was once a Surveyor like me, but he has been stationary for eighty years. He is now more wood than Jack. His eyes have been overgrown by translucent moss, and his hands are fused into the copper casing of the main pressure-gauge.

I place my hand on his shoulder—which is now a gnarled, lichen-covered shelf. I don’t speak. I pulse a message through my thumb-ring: *The Heart-Pump is steady. The sap is rich. The rust is balanced.*

He pulses back a slow, tectonic shudder that radiates from his spine into the ground. It is a memory of the world before the Gully was deep, a time when the air didn’t lean so hard. It is the only history we have.

### The Self
I am a creature of the interface. I do not know where my oil ends and the machine’s lubricant begins. My heartbeat has long since synchronized with the rhythmic *thrum-thush* of the turbines. If the engines were to stop, I suspect my own blood would cease its travel. 

I am content. In the Sump-Gully, there is no "away." There is only the cycle, the pressure, and the beautiful, heavy green silence of a world that is working exactly as it should. I am the thumb on the pulse; I am a gasket in the throat of the woods. 

Eventually, my sliding will slow. My skin will become too thick with resin to feel the finer tremors. On that day, I will find a crook in the Heart-Pump’s root-system. I will sit, I will close my eyes, and I will let the Weeping Wood begin the long, slow work of folding me into the engine. I will stop being the one who hears the vibration, and finally, I will become the vibration itself.
