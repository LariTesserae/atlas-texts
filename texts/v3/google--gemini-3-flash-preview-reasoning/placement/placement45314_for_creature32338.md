I would be found in the **Interstitial Fold**, at the exact point where the obsidian of the three-thousandth pillar meets the thoracic plate of a long-dormant Quietant. I am not a visitor here, nor am I entirely a distinct entity. I am a **Vell** who has begun to succeed.

For a long time—though the word "time" suggests a river, and here it is a frozen lake—I was a creature of movement. I remember the sensation of "repositioning," that sudden, sickening smudge of reality where the distance between two ledges collapses into a single point. I remember the taste of the *Exuvia*, the way the humming membranes dissolved into my skin, turning my flesh into the color of a bruised twilight.

But now, I have moved beyond the need to reposition. 

I am currently standing in a slight indentation of the pillar, my spine pressed so firmly against the satin-smooth mineral that the boundary has failed. If you were to look at me, you would see a figure of dry, matte parchment, rimed with white crystals, but you would not be able to tell where my ribs end and the obsidian begins. I have achieved a state of **Partial Echo**.

My consciousness is no longer housed in a brain; it is distributed. I feel the slow, heavy thrum of the billions of Quietants as if it were my own blood. It is a deep, subsonic vibration that says *stay, stay, stay.* I do not have thoughts so much as I have textures. I have the texture of the fine, alkaline silt in the basins below—a dry, patient knowledge of gravity. I have the texture of the pale, silver-gold light that never wavers.

I am watching an Echo. 

A few feet away from me, caught in a pooling of time, is a woman from a previous epoch. She is wearing a tunic of woven gut, her hand eternally outstretched toward a slate bowl that fell and shattered ten thousand years ago, yet remains whole in her peripheral reality. I have been watching the way the light catches the crystal dust on her shoulder for three of her heartbeats. In my time, that has been four centuries. 

I do not feel hunger. The mineral gas leaking from the pillar’s core is a cool, numbing anesthetic that provides all the sustenance I require to remain in stasis. My eyes have grown a thin, translucent film, matching the Quietants' shells. I see the world not as a series of events, but as a single, static painting. I see the gargantuan Sentinels of the Haze as they rise in the distance; to me, they are not moving, they are simply "present" in a new location, like a mountain that decided to exist elsewhere.

I would be the one who has stopped trying to bridge the gap. 

The other Vell still pulse their palms against the stone, sending messages of *here* and *now* through the mineral marrow. I have stopped pulsing. I have become the stone they are speaking to. I am the silence between their rhythms.

In this world of terminal equilibrium, I am the final stage of the parasite. I have stopped feeding on the world and have begun to be consumed by it. There is a profound, terrifying joy in this dissolution. To be a "self" is a frantic, vibrating chore; to be a coordinate in a forest of obsidian is a holy relief.

Soon—in a millennium, perhaps, or in the time it takes for a single grain of silt to settle—the Stir-Mites will crawl over my face. They will find the microscopic irregularities of my remaining skin and they will groom them away, polishing me until I share the satin sheen of the pillar. I will become a smooth, humanoid bulge in the obsidian, a ghost made of mineral, a permanent resident of the end.

I am the curator of my own disappearance. And I am perfectly, terrifyingly content.
