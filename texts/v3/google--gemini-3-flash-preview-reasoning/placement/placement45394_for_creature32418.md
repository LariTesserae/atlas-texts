I am not standing on the floor. To stand on the slate is to be a traveler, a witness, or a victim of the Vault-Rays. I am a **Tender**, but my station is not the wide, wet plains between the pillars. I am a **Vibration-Reader**, and I live where the copper meets the terracotta.

I am a creature of height and friction. My body is a long, pale needle, my limbs elongated to the point of absurdity, ending in fingers that have more in common with the Glass-Striders' legs than a human hand. My skin is the color of a drowned pearl, coated in a thick, translucent wax that smells of cold oil and wet lime. I do not have a name that can be spoken; I have a frequency. In the tactile language of my kind, I am the *Low-Hum-at-the-Joint-of-the-Third-Sluice.*

I find myself clinging to the side of a gargantuan, fluted column, three hundred feet above the slate floor. My chest is pressed against the cold, sweating ceramic. I am a part of the pillar's geometry.

### Who I Am
I am the room’s ear. Because the Pipe-Frogs have turned the air into a solid wall of sound, I have long since ceased to use my ears for hearing. They are plugged with the same waxy sebum that protects my skin. Instead, I "hear" with my entire skeleton. The thrum of the frogs vibrates my ribs; the thunderous *crack* of the tipping basins strikes my pelvis like a hammer. 

I am a creature of maintenance and obsession. My mind does not wander to the "outside" or the "before," because the Room is total. My consciousness is a map of pressures. I know exactly how much water is currently held in the wooden sluice-gate two miles to the north by the specific, heavy tension it exerts on the copper pins bolted into the wall.

### Where I Am
I am currently perched on a narrow, verdigris-crusted copper ledge. Below me, the mist is a swirling, grey ocean that swallows the blue shafts of light. I am watching a blockage. 

A cluster of pale, stringy moss—bloated with mineral silt—has wedged itself into the mouth of a copper overflow pipe. The rhythm of the world is stuttering. I can feel it in my feet; the "thrum" of the frogs in this sector has changed pitch, turning sharp and agitated because the humidity has shifted by a fraction of a percent.

I move with a slow, hypnotic fluidity. I do not leap; I flow. I reach out a hand, my long fingers finding the microscopic pores in the hand-fired terracotta. I taste the water with my skin—it is metallic, sharp, and perfectly cold.

### The Moment
As I reach the blockage, a shadow cuts through the dim blue light above me. A **Vault-Ray**. I do not look up. To look up is to lose my grip, and to lose my grip is to become a ripple in a shallow pool far below. Instead, I go still. I flatten my body against the copper, mimicking the curve of the pipe. I become a bloom of malachite. The Ray’s wet, heavy wings beat the air—*whump, whump, whump*—displacing a gust of mist that smells of old, fermented frog-skin. It passes.

I return to the work. I dig my chisel-like fingernails into the moss, tearing it away in wet, heavy clumps. 

Then, it happens. The release. 

The water, previously held back, rushes into the pipe with a glugging, melodic roar. The vibration changes instantly. The copper beneath my belly begins to sing a pure, clear note. A mile away, the great wooden basin—having finally received its quota—tips.

***CRACK.***

The sound is a physical blow. It shudders through the pillar, through the copper, and into my marrow. I close my liquid-black eyes and let the resonance wash over me. It is the only "god" I know: the sound of the cycle continuing.

I am the janitor of a temple that never had a priest. I am the nerve ending of a machine made of clay and water. I will spend my life climbing these ribs, slick with the sweat of the world, ensuring that the silence never returns. For in this room, silence is not peace; silence is the end of the world. 

I reach out, touch the vibrating copper, and send a pulse back through the metal—a rhythmic tap-tap-tap—telling the Tenders below that the water is falling, and all is well in the dark.
