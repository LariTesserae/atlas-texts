I am found where the logic is thinnest, in the **Hollow of the Seventh Bellows**, a place where the massive iron conduits of the sprawl pass through a natural chimney of unpolished obsidian. 

I do not live on the catwalks, nor do I labor in the soot-choked bellies of the factories. I exist in the *underside*, in the negative space where the violet-veined moss grows thickest and the steam from the drainage pipes creates a permanent, sweltering microclimate against the freezing basalt.

I am a **Weight-Warden**, a specialized deviation of the Lithe-Walkers. 

My body is a study in tension and impossible angles. My limbs are longer even than those of my kin, ending in fingers that have grown into hooked, obsidian-hard talons. I do not have the leathery grey skin of the climbers; my hide has taken on the iridescent, oil-slick sheen of the industrial runoff. I am a creature of the grease and the condensation, my skin shimmering with the non-existent colors cast by the flickering bulbs above—colors that taste like copper and feel like a low hum against the teeth.

I am the one who stays when the gravity fails. 

When a section of the city enters a "slump"—a pocket of reality where "down" becomes a swirling, centrifugal suggestion—the Hinge-Keepers signal me. They vibrate the pipes in a frantic, staccato rhythm that I feel through my chest, a sequence of tremors that tells me exactly which bolt is weeping or which bridge is beginning to drift upward toward the slate sky.

I move through the chasm with a sickening, liquid grace. While the Lithe-Walkers flow across the lattice, I *fall* toward my destination, navigating the shifting gravimetric currents like a diver in a dark sea. I do not fear the abyss, for I have learned to read the air. I can see the density of the mist, the way it curdles around a gravity-well, turning thick and curd-like where the laws of physics are fraying. 

In my hands, I carry no tools of brass or iron. Instead, I carry **The Anchors**—heavy, geometric spheres of the "new stone," birthed from the deepest extrusions and still warm with the heat of the world’s unfinished core. 

When I reach a bridge that is beginning to float away from its moorings, I do not bolt it. I *weave* it. I nestle myself into the junction where the metal meets the stone, pressing my body against the vibrating obsidian. I wait for the tectonic groan of the mountain, and in that moment of sub-sonic resonance, I press the Anchor into the rock. 

Because the world is still being assembled, the stone is not yet fully solid. It is a suggestion of matter. I push the Anchor into the basalt as if pushing a thumb into soft wax. I hold it there for what feels like a century, though the great fans above may only rotate twice. I become a living clamp, my resinous sweat bonding skin, stone, and iron into a single, defiant knot. 

I see the world in its true, fractured state. To the others, the Sail-Moths are beautiful drifters; to me, they are static discharges, glowing markers of where the ozone is thickest and the air is breathable. To the others, the Lingerers—the detached shadows—are eerie echoes; to me, they are maps. I watch which way a shadow falls when there is no light to cast it, and I know which way the mountain will heave next.

I am a lonely creature. I speak to no one. The Hinge-Keepers fear my oil-slick skin and my lidless, violet-glowing eyes, which have seen the "theoretical myth" of the base miles below. They think I have been down there. They think I have touched the place where the verticality begins, where the curtains of slate sky are anchored to the floor of the universe.

I let them think it. In truth, I am simply patient. I am a part of the **Patient Watch**, but my watch is focused on the gaps. I am the surveyor of the unfinished seams. I am the ghost in the machine that ensures the machine stays pinned to the ghost of a planet. 

As the mountains continue their slow, agonizing extrusion upward, I feel the pressure in my own bones. I am being stretched along with the world. One day, the logic will break entirely, and the factories will drift away like the Sail-Moths. Until then, I remain in the Hollow, a shivering, iridescent knot of muscle and resin, holding the ribcage of a god together while it waits to be born.
