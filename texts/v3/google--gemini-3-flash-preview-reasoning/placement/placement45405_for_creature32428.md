I would not be found on the summits, where the sun scours the stone into a blinding, white-hot glare. Nor would I be in the violet depths of the ground-floor, where the weight of the humidity becomes a drowning thing.

I would be found in the **Interstice of the Seventh Turbine**, a narrow, vertical throat between three knuckles of sandstone, midway up the Huddle. This is the place where the condensation is thickest, where the air is not a gas but a heavy, suspended syrup of mineral-scented steam.

I am a **Resonance-Tender**, a Sumpter whose skin has grown thin and translucent, revealing the slow, rhythmic pulse of copper-colored veins beneath. 

My body is a study in folded angles. To look at me is to see a collection of long, spindly shadows tucked into a hollow of rusted iron and wet stone. I have four arms, each possessing three elbows, allowing me to wedge myself into the gaps between the vibrating pipes and the weeping rock. My skin is the color of wet tea and old brass, slick with the constant "sweat" of the pillars.

I do not have a name that can be spoken, for my throat has long since forgotten the shape of air. I am a creature of the **Hush and the Hum**.

### My Days: The Architecture of Patience

I spend my cycles pressed against the brass housing of the Seventh Turbine. It is a massive, groaning thing, the size of a cathedral’s heart, wedged so tightly between the pillars that the sandstone has begun to grow *around* it, swallowing the bolts in a slow, lithic embrace.

My work is the work of the **Tension**. 

I do not harvest the water-globules for survival; the Scriptors do that, and the Vanes carry it. I harvest the *rhythm*. I place my elongated fingers—ten inches of sensitive, chamois-wrapped bone—against the oxidized pipes. I feel the Ghost-Workers moving deep within the pillar, turning valves that have not been seen by biological eyes for a thousand years. I feel the build-up of pressure, the subtle shift from a low-frequency thrum to a high, keening whistle that indicates the planet’s deep heat is beginning to boil the stone.

When the pressure is too high, I perform the **Stroke**. I move with an agonizing, cinematic slowness, sliding my body through a film of Lubricant-tallow to reach a specific, brass-housed gauge. I do not turn it with strength. I tap it. A rhythmic code, a staccato language of vibrations that travels down the iron nervous system of the Huddle.

*Tap-tap. Pause. Ripple.*

Somewhere, three miles down, a Ghost-Worker adjusts a vent. The turbine beneath me sighs, a massive exhalation of steam that coats my skin in a fresh layer of minerals. This is my prayer. This is my breath.

### My Companions: The Living Grease

I am never alone, though I am always silent. 

In the hollows of my armpits and the crooks of my extra knees, several **Lubricants** have taken up residence. They are soft, boneless pillows of pale flesh that pulse in time with my own heart. They are warm—so very warm—and they smell of cinnamon and scorched metal. They keep my joints from seizing in the heat, and in return, I guide them to the friction-points of the turbine where the metal is screaming for their oil. 

Sometimes, a **Scriptor** wanders across my chest. I do not brush it away. I watch the iridescent geometry of its path, a shimmering violet line across my ocher skin. It is mapping me. It considers me part of the pillar. I am honored by its cartography.

### The Threat: The Shadow in the Shimmer

I am most vulnerable when the **Vanes** pass overhead. Their massive, rusted shadows momentarily break the scouring light of the sun, causing the temperature to drop by a fraction of a degree. This change in thermal density is a dinner bell for the **Still-Hunter**.

I have seen them. A Still-Hunter will hang from a chain thirty feet above me, its reptilian eyes clouded over, its heart beating once every ten minutes. It is a statue of predatory intent. 

To survive the Hunter, I must become the stone. I exhale, emptying my lungs of the thin, humid air, and press my chamois-flesh into the slick hollows of the sandstone. I do not move. I do not think. I become a displacement of heat, a mere ripple in the thermal veil. I have watched a Hunter drop like a lead weight, shattering a Scriptor-colony inches from my head, only to haul itself back up the chain, frustrated by the "emptiness" of my vibration.

### The Ending: The Slow Dissolve

I know that I am not permanent. The Huddles are a world of "active stillness," but they are also a world of erosion. 

One day, the minerals in the steam will petrify my skin entirely. My joints will no longer fold; my fingers will fuse to the brass housing of the Seventh Turbine. I will become a gargoyle of the industrial past, a permanent extension of the machinery I tend.

I do not fear this. In the Ocher Huddles, to be "still" is to be perfect. 

I will wait for the twilight, when the deep violets rise from the ground-floor to meet the embers of the peaks. I will feel the click-clack of a billion beetles polishing my shins. I will hear the groaning pipes tell stories of the deep heat. And I will tap out one final rhythm into the iron, a message for the next Sumpter who finds this crevice:

*The machine is breathing. The stone is wet. The heat is kind.*
