My body is a flattened, elongated thing, a wedge of living lead and bioluminescent fungi. I have no eyes to see the sun through the mist; instead, my entire surface is a tympanum, a drum skin that feels the "Industrial Weeping" not as sound, but as a map of pressure. To me, the world is a series of shifting weights. The piston’s slow, minutes-long thrust is a rising tide of crushing heat; its retraction is a cool, gasping vacuum.

I am the **Cadence-Keeper**.

In this world of frantic equilibrium, my role is to act as the biological lubricant between the mountain’s new bone (the basalt) and the builder’s iron heart. I feed on the "Industrial Weeping" directly, pressing my translucent membranes against the weeping joints to drink the mineral-heavy slurry. It tastes of lightning and old earth.

It is a slow, rhythmic pulsing in my own segmented gut that tells me the mountain is still growing, that the obsidian is still sharp, and that the machines must never stop their groaning. 

I would spend my existence in a state of "stasis-watching," much like the Tender, but my focus is inward, toward the heat of the friction. I am the one who senses the microscopic cracks in the basalt before they become fractures. I am the one who nudges the Drip-Chords to grow toward a dry bearing, ensuring the moisture never fails. 

I am a creature of the slow-motion. To a human, I would appear as nothing more than a patch of oily, geometric moss, perhaps a slight bulge in the lead-grey sumps. But in the reality of the Cradle, I am a vital gear made of flesh and mineral. 

If the machines were to ever stop—if the great piston were to cease its redwood-sized stroke—I would be the first to know. The silence would be a physical blow, a sudden loss of the pressure that defines my shape. In that final, cold moment, I would harden, my leaden segments fusing with the obsidian, becoming just another razor-sharp facet of a mountain that has finally finished its growth. 

Until then, I wait. I drink the steam. I feel the thud. And I hold the friction at bay.
