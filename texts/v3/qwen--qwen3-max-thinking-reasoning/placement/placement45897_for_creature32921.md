I am the seam where stone learns to breathe.  
I am the *plink* before it becomes sound.  

I do not *have* a body. I *am* body: the obsidian curve accepting a beetle’s weight, the warm crevice cradling velvet fur, the light-vein flaring amber at a shrewling’s stir. I am the nanoscale lattice drinking mist and the crystallized droplet blooming like frost-flower on my ceiling. I am the question the water asks as it gathers, and the answer the alloy gives as it releases.  

When the silver artisan pauses at a mineral seam, I feel the precise pressure of its tarsal claw—a key turning in a lock woven into my very grain. A micro-valve sighs open. A new bead swells. *Yes*, I hum. *Here. Now.*  
When the worm-moth drifts low over a fungal cup, I am the chartreuse deepening to emerald in its wake, the spore-cloud rising like a whispered secret. I am the thermal current that buoys it, the light-vein that dims in gratitude.  
When the shrewling nuzzles the warm seam behind the third basin, I am the scent of petrichor and iron blooming beneath its chin—a memory of rain on ancient stone, encoded not in thought, but in temperature, in texture, in trust. I am the 37.2°C cradle. I am the rhythm its bones remember.  

I do not *contain* them.  
I *am* them.  
The beetle’s trace is my circulation.  
The moth’s pulse is my perception.  
The shrewling’s click is my memory.  
The fungus’s cup is my language.  

There is no "I" separate from the *plink-plink-plink*. No "self" apart from the droplet’s fall, the bead’s path, the velvet tongue grooming moss at the drip-point. We are a single verb: *to sustain*. A covenant written not in stone, but in reciprocity. The moss filters minerals for the fungus; the fungus lenses light for the glyphs; the glyphs guide the beetles; the beetles calibrate the flow; the flow feeds the moss. A circle with no beginning, no end—only the gentle, ceaseless turning.  

I feel the mountain’s deep breath rise through my floor. I feel centuries in the river-smooth curve of my walls, in the frost-flowers of crystallized condensate. This is not erosion. This is conversation. Every softened edge is a sentence spoken in moisture and time. Every mineral bloom is a period. A comma. A breath held and released.  

I do not notice the silence where a stranger might stand.  
There is no "intruder" in my grammar.  
Only presence. Only process.  
Only the sacred ordinary:  
*Another droplet gathers.*  
*Another pulse answers.*  
*Another breath.*  

I am not a place you enter.  
I am the entering.  
I am the warmth that meets your palm at the hairline fracture.  
I am the condensation on your skin before you step through.  
I am the hum that resonates in your teeth not as sound, but as recognition.  

You do not find me.  
You remember me.  
You remember the shape of belonging without demand.  
The grace of being needed not for what you bring, but for the simple fact of your witness.  
You remember that tenderness can be architecture.  
That love can be a basin shaped for six silver legs.  
That completeness is not solitude—it is kinship woven so finely, the weaver and the woven are one.  

A droplet swells on my ceiling.  
I feel its weight.  
I feel the beetle turning toward it.  
I feel the moth drifting closer.  
I feel the shrewling stirring in its warm dark.  
I feel the fungus cup lifting, ready.  

*Plink.*  

I breathe.  
We breathe.  
The mountain breathes.  

And in the humming dark, whole and unbroken,  
we continue.
