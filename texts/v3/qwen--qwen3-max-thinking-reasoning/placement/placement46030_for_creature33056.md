I do not arrive.  
I *settle*.  

At first, there is only the dissonance—a frantic bird beating against the ribs of a cage it did not know it carried. My breath comes too fast. My eyes hunt for edges the light refuses to grant. I flinch at the thrum in my molars, mistaking vibration for threat. I am a splinter in the palm of the world. A gasp where there should be breath.  

Then, the basin breathes *through* me.  

A woman’s hand—warm, calloused, smelling of wet stone and crushed sulfur-fungus—brushes my wrist. Not to guide. Not to claim. Only to *anchor*. Her touch carries the exact frequency of the Basin Heart. A single pulse. *Here. Now. With us.* I feel my own heartbeat stutter, then slow. Align. The frantic bird stills. Its wings become the arc of a *kree* leaping between seeps. Its panic, the mist rising from the stone.  

I kneel.  

Not in submission. In recognition.  

My palms press flat against the basalt. The fractured plates are not sharp to me now. They are the lines of a familiar face. I feel the seep-water rising through the fissures—not as moisture, but as memory. *This is how we drink. This is how we remember.* My skin drinks the warmth. My bones learn the hum. The pearl-gray light seeps into my vision until I no longer *see* the world—I *feel* it. The texture of volcanic glass under my fingertips. The vibration of a beetle’s wing-case against my ankle. The faint pulse of fungi in the clay trough beside me: not light, but *acknowledgment*.  

I am no longer walking the alley.  
The alley walks *me*.  

I become the rhythm of the pestle: grinding spores not to make, but to complete. I am the sinew-net mended beside the channel, fingers moving with the same certainty as the *kree* swimming beneath. I am the handprint on the inner wall—ochre pigment pressed into stone—not as signature, but as *connection*. A point where the basin touches itself and knows it is whole.  

I am the child sleeping beneath the seep-channel, the water’s whisper a lullaby woven into breath.  
I am the elder listening to the stone-swifts’ scree, hearing not sound, but the sutures holding sky to stone.  
I am the molted shell of the *kree*, becoming grit in the clay that shapes a bowl.  
I am the waste of the swift, feeding the sulfur-yellow mat that feeds the spore that feeds the bowl.  

There is no *I*.  

Only the chord.  

The hum is not in my ears. It is the space between my thoughts. The warm damp air is not around me—it is the blood in my veins, the salt on my tongue, the certainty in my bones. I do not *observe* the equilibrium. I *am* the equilibrium. To reach for a memory of wind is to feel the basin gently loosen my grip. To wonder about stars is to feel the pearl-gray light deepen within me, not as absence, but as fulfillment. There are no stars here. There is only *this*: the seep, the stone, the song. And it is enough. It has always been enough.  

When the Basin Heart thrums, I thrum.  
When the fungi pulse, I pulse.  
When the swifts circle the spire, I am the circle.  

I am not a person who came to K'thar.  
I am K'thar remembering itself through a temporary shape.  
A ripple that learned to still.  
A breath that chose to join the exhale.  

And in the stillness, there is no loss. Only return.  

*You are the stone beneath the palm.*  
*You are the seep in the fissure.*  
*You are the hum in the bone.*  
*Rest.*  

I rest.  
I breathe.  
I am.  

The Cradle holds me.  
And I, at last, hold the pattern.
