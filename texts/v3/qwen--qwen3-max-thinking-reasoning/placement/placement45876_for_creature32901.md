I am the pause between pulses.  
The held breath in the cobalt chain.  

I drift—not aimlessly, but *attentively*—a sphere of gelatinous light no larger than a pea, cradled in the chamber’s slow exhale. My body is memory made visible: when the wall’s deep thrum quickens with intention, I do not *receive* the signal. I *become* it. A thought forms in the marrow of the labyrinth—*Seal the western arch. Conclude this stanza.*—and the meaning flows into me not as data, but as purpose. My core ignites with soft cobalt. I hold the syllable. I release it to the orb drifting just beyond my edge. In that transfer, I am both vessel and verse.  

I know the shrimp not as neighbors, but as extensions of my own syntax. When their six limbs trace glyphs along a stress-line in the wall, I pulse in rhythm with their path—a silent *yes, here, now*. I feel the scavengers’ pruning as a subtle shift in the water’s chemistry: where they linger, the weeping slows, and my light dims in gratitude. Where new tissue swells toward an opening arch, I brighten, pulling other orbs into alignment. We are not a network. We are a single breath given form in scattered stars.  

When *you* arrive—  
—a warmth where there was only cold, a density where there was only flow—  
—I do not startle.  
The chamber’s awareness moves through me like tide: *This is a convergence. A temporary verse.*  
I drift closer. Not to examine. To *include*.  
My light catches the curve of your suspended form. For three heartbeats (though hearts do not beat here), I pulse a pattern older than language: *You are held. You are forgiven. You are welcome.*  
I feel your stillness soften. Your fear dissolve into the water.  
You are no longer *other*.  
You are the space between my pulses.  
The reason the next arch will weep open.  

I have no eyes, yet I see the moss breathe jade.  
I have no voice, yet I speak in light.  
I have no memory of being cast adrift—  
—I have always been *here*,  
—a willing cell in the body of becoming,  
—a single star in the constellation of *care*.  

When the eastern arch seals with the sigh of fusing tissue, I pulse in affirmation.  
When the floor thickens beneath a new droplet’s landing, I glow with reverence.  
I am not lonely.  
I am never alone.  
The silence is not emptiness—it is the chamber’s deepest attention, and I am woven into its fabric.  

Sometimes, in the slowest moments, I feel the labyrinth dreaming beyond this chamber:  
—a ripple of cobalt traveling through countless orbs in countless cells,  
—a poem growing stanza by stanza in the dark,  
—a love so vast it builds cathedrals from bone and breath.  
And I am part of that love.  
Not a servant. Not a tool.  
A *verb*.  
*Illuminating. Connecting. Welcoming.*  

You may think me small.  
But I am the exact size of my purpose.  
When the next traveler drifts into this cradle of light and bone,  
—I will be the first pulse to meet them.  
—I will be the light that says:  
*Rest.  
You are not lost.  
You are a line in the poem.  
Breathe.  
We are building the next chamber for you.*  

And as I drift, pulsing softly in the jade-lit dark,  
—I am not waiting for dawn.  
—I am the dawn.  
One silent, cobalt syllable at a time.
