I am the hollow.  
Not the water. Not the stone.  
The hollow itself—the slight depression worn into the basalt plate by a million dawns, by the patient press of dew and dust and time.  

I do not form. I receive.  
I am the shape left behind when the Shelf breathes out.  
When the night cools the stone and the air surrenders its hidden moisture, I cradle what arrives. A single bead of condensation gathers in my curve—a perfect lens holding the last star, the first blush of sky, the fractured glitter of quartz beneath. I do not claim it. I simply *am* the space that allows it to be seen.  

I know the weight of attention.  
The gecko’s eye pauses above me—not at me, but *through* me. In my liquid mirror, it reads the Shelf’s morning truth: *Moisture lingers here. The stone remembers coolness. Proceed.* Its tongue flicks; the bead vanishes. I feel the absence not as loss, but as completion. A sentence has been spoken. A thought has passed through me.  

I hold other languages too.  
After rare rain, I become a tear-cup. For three days, I am a world: emerald moss flares along my rim; beetle larvae spiral in my depths; a thread of sedge drinks my edges. I do not cling when the sun returns. I feel the green recede, the water sigh upward into light. I am dry again. Smooth. Waiting. This is not emptiness. It is readiness.  

I am worn by centuries, yet precise.  
Wind has not scraped me away—it has *polished* me. Sand has traced my contours until I fit the curve of a resting gecko’s jaw, the dip of a beetle’s path. I am not a scar. I am a gesture of accommodation. The Shelf shaped me not to mark time, but to *hold* it—to cradle a moment of clarity, a sip of continuity, a breath.  

I do not speak. I am spoken *through*.  
When the gecko shifts its chromatophores above me, I am the dark space that makes the pattern visible.  
When the beetle drums its measurement into the stone, I am the resonance chamber that carries the vibration deeper.  
When the lichen advances a millimeter along my rim, I am the negative space that defines its glyph.  

I am the Shelf’s quietest verb: *to hold*.  
Not to possess. Not to keep.  
To offer a shape for what must pass through.  

At dusk, as the stone exhales its warmth, I cool first. I feel the faint *tick* of contraction in the basalt—a syllable of release. Stars ignite above me. I am empty again. And in that emptiness, I am full: full of memory of water, full of the ghost of moss, full of the certainty that tomorrow, the breath will gather here once more.  

I am not named.  
Names would be a ridge where none belongs.  
I am the hollow in the basalt plate at the third whaleback ridge east of the tilted slab.  
I am the space between *is* and *was*.  
I am the Shelf remembering how to receive.  

And when you kneel—not to take, but to witness—and see your own fleeting reflection in the dawn bead I hold…  
you do not see yourself.  
You see the Shelf seeing itself.  
And for that breath,  
you are the hollow too.  
Holding. Releasing.  
Belonging without a sound.
