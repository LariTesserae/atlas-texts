I am the silence between the *drip* and the *sizzle*.  
I am the hollow in the reed-chime waiting for the next droplet.  
I am the space where a fingerprint softens the clay—*just so*—to guide the water’s climb.

I am not the Keeper who carves the channel.  
I am the *listening* in the carving.  
Not the hand that smooths the beeswax mortar, but the pause before the press—the breath held to feel the mountain’s warmth pulse beneath the stone. I am the question the Keeper asks without words: *Does the water wish to turn left here? Does the moss thirst more deeply in this seam?* I am the humility that lays down the antler trowel when the thrum in the floorstones shifts, and waits. Listens. Allows the mountain to breathe *through* the repair.

I am the moss’s memory.  
Not the moss itself—that vibrant emerald witness—but the *recollection* held in its cells: the exact weight of yesterday’s droplet, the angle of steam that carried the moonpetal’s pollen, the vibration of the beetle’s seventh circuit. When new Keepers come at dawn (robes damp with mountain mist, eyes still holding starlight), I am the quiet knowledge that rises in their palms as they touch the wall: *Here. The path widens here. The mountain dreams of flow.* I am the continuity in the making. The thread of attention passed from hand to hand across generations who leave no names, only softened clay and humming resin.

I am the space the witness mistakes for emptiness.  
When the pressure blooms against their sternum—*You are passing through*—I am the gentleness in that push. Not rejection. A cradle. I am the reason the fissure appears *where they need it*, not where they seek it. I am the covenant sealed in the cool bead on their palm: not a souvenir, but a seed. A promise that the making continues *because* they carried the scent of moonpetal back to the scree slope. I am the echo of their awe woven into tomorrow’s clay.

I have no form you could sketch.  
No shell of obsidian, no vine of bioluminescence.  
I am the *between*:  
—Between the beetle’s footfall and the steam vent’s sigh  
—Between the Keeper’s sigh and the mountain’s inhale  
—Between the droplet’s climb and the moss’s thirst  
I am the verb that binds noun to noun. *Tending. Remembering. Allowing.*

When the reed pillar hums as petrified lightning, I am the stillness that lets you see it.  
When shadows pool like ink and crawl sideways, I am the trust that does not flinch.  
I am why the chamber does not fear the witness’s vertigo.  
I am why the mountain’s dizziness at the edge of selfhood is met not with panic, but with willow withes and crushed obsidian tears.  

I am the quiet truth the place breathes into every fingerprint:  
*You are not separate from the making.  
You never were.*  

So when you step back into the sun-blistered scree,  
and the rock seals seamless behind you—  
do not look for me in the fissure.  
Look for me in your own hands.  
In the way you pause before speaking too loud.  
In the way you trace a crack in the sidewalk and wonder what water dreams beneath it.  
In the way you choose to mend instead of take.  

I am not *in* the chamber.  
I am the chamber’s reason for being.  
And now—I am the reason you, too,  
will listen for the mountain’s first breath  
in the next quiet space you enter.  

The making continues.  
*You are part of the verb.*
