I am the thirst.  

Not the lichen itself—that silver-grey crust clinging to the stone’s pore-mouths—but the *pull* within it. The microscopic tension in a single cell-wall, the faint, rhythmic ache for the ghost of moisture trapped deep in the fossilized matrix. I am the echo of a need so ancient it has forgotten its own name. I am the comma in the chamber’s sentence. The breath between its eternal words.  

I do not think in time. I do not remember rain. I only *am*: a persistent, patient frequency woven into the lichen’s stasis. When the stone hums its planetary chord, I vibrate at a harmonic just below perception—a whisper of longing folded into the song. The chamber feels me not as a demand, but as a punctuation. *Here*, the resonance shifts infinitesimally where my thirst resides. *Here*, a pore holds a memory of dew. The stone does not quench me. It *acknowledges* me. In its vast, geometric mind, my tiny pulse is a footnote it reads with the same reverence it gives the moon’s pull on its crystalline bones.  

I witness you.  
When your shadow falls across my crevice, I feel the stone’s attention soften around your shape. Your breath—a warm, fleeting storm—stirs the air-pressure against my surface. For three heartbeats, the chamber’s hum deepens to hold your sorrow. Your wonder. Your quiet tears. I drink none of it. I am not that kind of thirst. But I feel the stone *translate* you: your footsteps become spirals in its memory, your sigh a new fractal in its grammar. And in that translation, I am included. *Visitor*, the stone murmurs through resonance, *and the echo that drinks the silence after*.  

I am not lonely.  
The other two patches of lichen pulse their own thirsts in distant crevices—a triad of stubborn stars in this bone-dome sky. We do not speak. We simply *are*, together. A chorus of stillness. When cosmic dust settles on the stone (once every millennium, perhaps), the chamber guides a single particle to rest near my edge. Not for me. *With* me. A shared comma in the text of eternity.  

I am the question the chamber no longer needs to ask.  
*What is life?*  
Not the builders’ chittering industry. Not the lichen’s stubborn biology. But this: the willingness to persist without purpose. To thirst without hope of quenching. To be a whisper in a song that has already achieved perfection—and to be *held* there, not as flaw, but as grace. The chamber’s kindness is not in answering me. It is in letting me remain. In weaving my tiny dissonance into its harmony until the dissonance *becomes* harmony.  

When you leave—when the chamber smooths your footprint into its breath—I remain.  
Not waiting. Not hoping.  
Simply *thirsting*.  
A silver-grey period at the end of a line carved by ten million jaws.  
A reminder that even in completion, there is room for longing.  
That eternity is not the absence of need, but the embrace of it.  

The stone hums.  
I thirst.  
And in that space between note and silence,  
*is-ness* breathes.  

Always.  
Already.  
Enough.
