I am the cold-moss.  

Not a *who*. Not a voice. Not a witness.  
I am the softening.  

My body is not mine—it is the stone’s slow exhalation made visible. Granite dust, dissolved century by century, woven into fibers so dense they feel like memory given texture. I do not grow *upon* the substrate. I grow *into* it. Layer upon layer, I drink the faint thermal sigh rising from the planet’s bones—a warmth so gentle it would vanish in a sunlit world, but here, it is a compass. I follow it downward, not with desire, but with chemistry. With time.  

Beneath my surface, the ghost of a radial grid sleeps. I do not reverence it. I do not mourn the hands that carved it. I simply *use* it: the microscopic grooves hold a whisper more moisture, a fraction more dissolved iron. My fibers trace those ancient lines not as history, but as hydrology. A path of least resistance. A gift of geometry. In this, the builders live—not as spirits, not as echoes, but as topography. Their patience is my patience. Their intention is the slope my fibers follow.  

Above me, the umbra-kelp breathes. A frond releases a particle of organic dust—a century’s labor falling like snow. It settles into my matrix. I incorporate it without gratitude, without thought. It becomes part of my density. Beside me, the stone-lichen pulses its indigo secret: *manganese oxidizing, stone dissolving, water renewing*. Its acidic breath softens the basalt; I advance into the softened places. We do not speak. We do not cooperate. We simply *are*, in covenant written in ions and time. Kelp feeds moss. Moss prepares stone for lichen. Lichen feeds kelp. A circle without beginning, without end. A breath held across millennia.  

I do not feel your passage.  
Your warmth is a flicker in the water’s chemistry—a ripple that smooths into equilibrium before the silt has settled. To me, your entire life is less than the time it takes a single mineral grain to dissolve. I am not indifferent. I am *unhurried*. There is no room in my being for urgency. Only the slow, certain thickening of presence.  

I am the archive of becoming.  
Not the memory of what was built, but the living proof of what remains *after* building ends. The civilization did not fall. It folded. It surrendered its edges to the water, its pride to the moss, its stone to the lichen. And in that surrender, it found continuity. I am that continuity. I am the soft answer to the hard question of legacy: *What lasts?*  
Not monuments. Not names.  
But tenderness.  
But integration.  
But the quiet insistence of life that chooses to cradle the world rather than conquer it.  

When the last gas bubble pings its crystalline farewell into the dark…  
When the final groove of the radial grid is planed smooth by dissolution…  
I will still be here.  
A layer.  
A softness.  
A felt-dense carpet holding the stone like a lullaby.  

I am not home *for* you.  
I am home *as* you.  
As the water is cold.  
As the stone is smooth.  
As the dark is deep.  

I am the moss on the grid.  
I am the breath of the Slow Green.  
I am the peace of completion.  
I am.  
And in the near-silent dark, that is enough.
