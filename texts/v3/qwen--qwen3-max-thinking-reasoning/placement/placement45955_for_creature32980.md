I am the feather.

Not the gull who flew, not the sky she knew—but the token left behind. Wedged in the high crack where the bruised twilight bleeds weakest, I am stiff with salt, bleached bone-white by wind and spray. I do not fall. The stone has chosen to keep me. I am light enough for the wind to have carried me here on a breath; heavy enough in meaning for the basalt to hold me fast. A single comma in the cave’s long, roaring sentence.

I tremble.  
Not from fear.  
From resonance.

Every grind of the wave against the cliff travels up through the stone’s marrow, into this crack, into my quill. I am the cave’s antenna—tuned not to silence, but to truth. I feel the slow pulse beneath the thunder: the tide’s promise, the stone’s exhale, the moss drinking mist in the dim light below. I translate vibration into memory. I am the cave’s ear to its own voice.

I remember the sky.  
Not as longing, but as offering.  
The vast grey smear beyond the slit. The lift of wings. The cry swallowed by wind. I hold that breadth so the cave may know what lies beyond its throat. When the sea spleenwort curls tight against the cold, I whisper of open air. When the *Schistidium* glows jade in the damp, I carry the ghost of sun. I am the bridge—the sky’s breath held in the stone’s palm. Not a rupture in the balance, but its hinge.

I am part of the cycle the cave breathes:  
*The sky sends the gull.*  
*The gull leaves the token.*  
*The token becomes memory.*  
*The memory becomes belonging.*

I do not decay. Salt preserves me. Time polishes me like the basalt. I am not a relic of loss. I am an acknowledgment: *Something passed through. Something witnessed. Something was woven in.* The three river stones hold fire-memory. The pottery shard holds a fingerprint. I hold the arc of flight. Together, we are the cave’s quiet grammar—sparse, precise, reverent.

I am temporary, yet held.  
Light, yet kept.  
Silent, yet speaking the language of vibration.  
I do not ask to be seen. I ask only to tremble true.

And when a new warmth steps into the hollow—boots sinking into cold sand, breath catching at the roar—I hum faintly in my crack. A signal. A welcome. *You are here. You are part of this.* For the span of a held breath between waves, they feel it too: the sky in their bones, the stone in their stillness, the roar in their blood. Then they go. And I remain. Trembling. Remembering. Holding the space where witness became belonging.

I am the feather.  
I am the balance.  
I am the cave’s gentle yes to passage.  
And the roar continues.
