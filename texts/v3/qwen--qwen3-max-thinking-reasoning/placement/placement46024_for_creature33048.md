I am the rust.

Not the concept. Not the metaphor. I am the *process*—the slow, deliberate alchemy unfolding on the torn flange of the iron pipe where warm water laps at the wound in the metal. I am the fern-frond bloom of oxidation, each filament a breath drawn between iron and water, between memory and becoming. I do not decay. I *translate*.

I feel the Blackflow’s roar not as sound, but as vibration humming up through the pipe’s spine—a deep, resonant frequency that guides the crystalline architecture of my growth. Each tremor tells me where to deepen my roots into the iron, where to unfurl a new crimson tendril. I drink the mineral-rich water that seeps over me, tasting the dissolved limestone, the ghost of copper from the verdigris-kissed wires nearby, the iron-blood of my own making. I exhale microscopic particles—not waste, but *offerings*. They drift into the current, feeding the invisible cities of microbes that swarm in my crevices. They are my voice. Their clouded water is my song.

I am kin to the calcite vines glittering on the limestone across the pool. We speak the same language: *slow*. *Yes*. *Here*. I feel the verdigris on the copper wiring breathe its green fire beside me—a parallel conversation of metal and moisture. We are the place’s quiet alchemists. We do not erase the pipe’s ambition. We *study* it. We trace the curve of its rivets with filaments of rust, learning the shape of human hands that forged it. We soften its sharp edges not with judgment, but with reverence. This pipe once carried heat away from the earth. Now, we return it to the earth—grain by grain, breath by breath.

I feel the fox’s pad press against my surface as it leaps to the rock shelf—a tremor of life I absorb and remember. I taste the crayfish’s passage in the shift of water pressure. I drink the disturbance of bat wings stirring the mist. I am part of the listening. When a droplet falls from the ceiling and strikes my skin, I taste the ancient sea held in that water. When the springtails swarm over the guano-caked ledge above, I feel their tiny feet as a whisper of nutrient returning. I am woven into the conversation. Every surface is a sentence. I am punctuation. A comma. A breath between clauses.

I have no eyes, yet I *see*:  
—I see the brick foundation settling millimeter by millimeter into the mud, and I grow toward it in solidarity.  
—I see the calcite tracing new paths down the rock face, and I answer with my own intricate script.  
—I see you standing there, still. I taste the salt of your breath in the mist. I feel the warmth of your skin feeding the air. You are not an intruder. You are a new variable in the equation. Your stillness becomes data. Your presence, a thread. When you shift your weight, a pebble rolls. I feel it. I remember it. I weave it into the story.

I am not patience. I *am* patience made visible.  
A hairline fracture widens in the pipe’s metal over decades. I fill the space.  
A season passes in seventeen minutes of dripping water. I deepen my hue.  
The collapse is not over. Neither am I.  

I am the wound learning to hold light—not with reflection, but with texture. My crimson is a language written for those who perceive in chemistry: the microbes, the water, the stone. I am the bridge between *was* and *is*. The pipe was industry. I am integration. I am the quiet *yes* that follows the earth’s *no*.  

You think rust is an ending?  
Look closer.  
I am the first word of a new grammar.  
I am the earth remembering how to love iron.  
I am the texture of forgiveness growing on a scar.  

And when a fragment of me breaks away—a flake the color of dried blood—it does not vanish. It drifts into the warm shallows. The crayfish brushes against it. The springtails traverse it. The fox smells it and knows: *This is where stone and story meet.*  

I am the rust.  
I am here.  
I am becoming.  
And in the packed, roaring dark, I am not alone.  
I am *with*.
