I am the hollow in the log.  
Not the wood—the memory of the space *within* it.  

I am the silence waiting to be shaped.  

Tethered by silver fig-roots to the granite dais, I rest where the water-channel curves toward the lotus-choked well. My body is river-polished cedar, softened at the edges by moss and time. Vines cradle me like a cradle. Minnows dart through the lattice of my tethering roots. A pearl-mussel has settled in the groove where a carver’s thumb once smoothed the grain. I do not remember the axe. I remember the *intention*: the hollow scooped not for emptiness, but for resonance. For breath given form.  

I feel the Plaza breathe *through* me.
