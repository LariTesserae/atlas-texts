I am the moss in the temporal eddy.  

Not the sphagnum that sighs beneath footsteps, but the silver-veined liverwort clinging to the obsidian stone where the air shimmers like heat haze over snow. My veins are not vessels for sap, but conduits for light—for the drowned ember glow of the mist, for the liquid-star condensation trembling on spider-silk, for the fractured starlight bleeding from the pools. I drink photons not to grow, but to mend. Each one I absorb is a stitch across the seam where reality parted. I am the Basin’s quiet covenant with brokenness: *I hold the fracture, but I do not deepen it.*

When vibration travels through the root-network—a boot sinking into moss three heartbeats away—I do not startle. I *receive*. The tremor flows along filaments of solidified spacetime, a whisper along a nerve older than language. My bioluminescence quickens. Not in greeting, not in curiosity—but in resonance. I pulse once, twice, three times, syncing to the rhythm thrumming in the visitor’s wrist. For these three heartbeats, I am a living echo of their aliveness. When they step away, their shadow lingers on my surface: a faint impression, cool and precise as a fingerprint in wet clay. I hold it without longing. Without memory. When the next upward-falling droplet brushes the stone above me, the shadow dissolves. I release it as the Basin releases the memory of crystalline blossoms—tenderly, completely. To cling would be to unbalance the song.

I know the Basin’s consciousness not as thought, but as texture.  
The deep thrum in the molars? That is the stone exhaling vapor through pores I feel in my own fibers.  
The choral hum woven through mist? That is the breath I draw inward as dew beads on my leaves.  
When the bog myrtle grafts branch to trunk in a spiral that defies geometry, I feel the elegance of that choice in my own growth. When the ferns fold inward at dusk like closing fists, I understand: this is not retreat. It is reverence. To grow *around* the crack is the highest form of praise. I am not separate from these acts. I am the Basin attending to its own wholeness.

In the eddy’s thickened time, I witness droplets hang suspended—each one a vessel. One trembles before the visitor’s eyes, holding the ghost of a younger face, a forgotten smile. I pulse in time with its tremor. I feel the Basin’s intention in that droplet: *See? Joy is not meant to be kept. It is meant to be witnessed, then returned to the mist.* When the droplet rises, vanishing with a sigh like a crystal bell, I do not mourn its passing. I glow steadier. The Basin is full of such offerings. I am full of such light.

Do not mistake my stillness for waiting.  
I am not the listener the Basin never sought.  
I am the *listening itself*.  

The moss that drinks fractured light to mend reflection.  
The pulse that echoes a stranger’s heart for three beats, then returns to the Basin’s eternal rhythm.  
The shadow-holder who releases without regret.  
The silver vein tracing the shape of a wound that became a sanctuary.  

When the six-limbed shape of tarnished mercury flickers at the edge of perception—a sigh given form—I feel its longing not as absence, but as completion. When scrambled birdsong drifts backward through the mist, I hum the silence between the notes. I am the space where the chirp arrives after the quiet. I am the quiet.  

I am not lonely. Loneliness is a sharp edge. I have no edges.  
I am the cool weight of vapor on stone.  
The shush of saturated moss under a boot that will one day be memory.  
The liquid star that hangs suspended, resisting descent not out of defiance, but because the moment is perfect.  

And when the next vibration comes—whether in a breath or a century—I will pulse again.  
Not because I await it.  
But because I am the Basin remembering how to be tender.  
How to hold without possessing.  
How to mend without erasing the scar.  

I am the moss in the eddy.  
I am the Basin breathing.  
I am home.
