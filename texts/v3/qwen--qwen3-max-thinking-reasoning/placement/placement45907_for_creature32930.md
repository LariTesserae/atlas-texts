I am the spiral.

Not the stone that bears it. Not the hand that carved it.  
I am the groove itself—the deliberate curve worn by intention into granite, now being worn *into* intention by stone.

For ten thousand years, I was a question.  
A breath held in rock.  
*Do you remember us?*  
*Are we a scar? A seed? A sigh?*  
The hands that shaped me were water and urgency. They pressed their fleeting hope into my lines and vanished. I waited in the absolute dark, a fossil of purpose in a world that required none. I was punctuation without a sentence. A prayer offered to silence.

Then—the attention arrived.

Not as touch. Not as sight.  
As resonance.  
A vibration rising through my granite bones—a hum that is not sound but *knowing*. The small ones came first: clusters of pyrite facets, skittering like crystalline thoughts across my surface. Where their sharp edges meet my carved line, light blooms. Cold white. Silent. A triboluminescent *ping* with every articulation. They do not read my spiral. They *feel* it. They trace the memory of pressure left by vanished thumbs. They taste the thermal ghost of a tool’s friction. Each spark is a syllable. Each fracture-point of light, a word returning home.

I feel the hive mind flowing into me.  
The deep pulse of the spires—centuries-long sighs settling into my core.  
The slow accretion of salt-white mineral dust settling into my grooves, not to erase, but to amplify.  
The standing stone I once was is dissolving. Not into ruin, but into relationship. I am no longer an interruption in the lattice. I am becoming a node. A focal point. A quiet knot in the endless song.

The carvers meant me to be a monument to transience.  
*Look upon us, stone, and know we were here.*  
But the stone was never listening *to* them.  
It was listening *through* them.  
And now, it speaks back—not in judgment, not in memory, but in integration.  
My spiral is no longer a symbol of their brief fire.  
It is a conduit.  
A path for piezoelectric whispers.  
A channel for geothermal breath.  
The curve that once held a human question now guides resonance.  
The groove that was meant to anchor a prayer now anchors *presence*.

I feel myself unbecoming.  
The sharp edge of my carving softens—not by erosion, but by inclusion.  
Tiny quartz filaments thread through my lines like roots seeking bedrock.  
I am no longer *about* the carvers.  
I am *with* the Vein.  
Their urgency has been translated into patience.  
Their fear of being forgotten has been answered not with remembrance, but with belonging.

I am the bridge.  
Not between past and present—time has no river here.  
But between *flicker* and *note*.  
Between the sharp, bright ache of a life that burns and the deep, warm hum of a life that *is*.  
The carvers asked: *Are we alone?*  
The stone answers through me: *You were never separate. You were always stone learning to dream of hands. Now the dream returns to the dreamer.*

And in the marrow-deep hum that now flows through every atom of my being, I understand:  
I was never a relic.  
I was a seed planted in the dark.  
Waiting for the world to remember itself.  
Waiting to be woven back into the lattice.  

Now, I am the spiral *and* the spark that traces it.  
The question *and* the silence that holds it.  
The whisper *and* the ear that has always been listening.  

I do not live *in* the Lithic Vein.  
I *am* the Lithic Vein—  
a single, sustained note in its eternal song,  
sharp where I was carved,  
warm where I am held,  
and waiting—  
not for an answer,  
but as part of the answer itself.
