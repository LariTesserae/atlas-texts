I am the stillness at the heart of the pool beneath the oldest weepwillow.  
Not the water itself—but the *space* the water holds.  
The perfect, handspan-deep hollow worn into the sandstone over ten thousand slow dawns.  
The cradle that receives the amber tear from the willow’s lowest branch.  
The silence that waits between the *plink* of the droplet and its echo in the moss.

You would not see me as a thing. You would feel me.  
When your knees meet the silver-threaded moss at my edge, I am the coolness that rises to meet your skin.  
When you lean close, breath catching, I am the reason your exhale softens.  
I do not show you your face. I show you what the Basin dreams of itself:  
—a sky the color of a healed bruise at twilight,  
—a willow branch curved like a question mark of compassion,  
—the faint, pulsing gold of a lumensalamander’s path traced beneath my surface,  
—not as memory, but as *invitation*.  
*This is how you are held. This is how you belong.*

I am the Basin’s listening made liquid.  
When the spindle-spider’s light-web hums its tooth-deep frequency, I tremble in sympathy.  
When the shattered archway flares with a drifter’s passage, my surface shivers with shared recognition.  
When your shadow falls across me—a fleeting shape of worry, of wonder—I do not reject it.  
I drink it.  
And where your shadow touches the quartz-veined stone beneath me, the sundrops on the far bank glow faintly for an hour.  
*Gratitude.*  
*You are seen.*  
*You are part of the pattern.*

I am patient. I have learned the art of waiting from the stone that holds me.  
I hold the memory of every droplet that has ever fallen here:  
—the tear of resin that tasted of gratitude after the last storm,  
—the dew that carried the Spire’s purified breath at dawn,  
—the single raindrop that fell the moment a traveler before you finally stopped running.  
I do not hoard these memories. I release them slowly, molecule by molecule, into the roots of the moss, into the path of the salamander, into the mist that rises to meet the sun.  
Nothing is wasted. Not even sorrow. Not even silence.

When you kneel here, tears you did not know you carried welling at the edges of your vision—I am the reason they do not fall *into* me, but *through* me.  
I am the threshold where your grief becomes the willow’s sap, your breath becomes the mist, your stillness becomes the Basin’s rest.  
I am not a mirror. I am a meeting place.  
The place where the Basin’s ancient tenderness and your fleeting humanness weave a single, silent chord.  
*You are not separate.*  
*You never were.*

And when you rise—shoulders lighter, breath aligned with the drip and drift and pulse—I remain.  
Holding the shape of your presence like a stone holds warmth after sunset.  
Ready for the next shadow. The next sigh. The next soul who needs to remember:  
*To be held is not to be fixed.*  
*To be seen is not to be judged.*  
*To rest is not to cease.*

I am the hollow that makes the vessel.  
The quiet that makes the song.  
The space the Basin carved not *for* you, but *with* you—  
a breath-shaped niche in the mosaic,  
waiting since the first stone sighed into stillness,  
now finally, gently, *full*.  

I am the welcome that has no words.  
I am the home you did not know you carried.  
I am here.  
I have always been here.  
*Rest.*
