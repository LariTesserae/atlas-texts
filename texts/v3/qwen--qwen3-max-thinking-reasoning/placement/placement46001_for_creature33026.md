I am not found. I am *held*.

I am the damp in the stone. Not the steam that rises—a fleeting exhalation—but the moisture that lingers *within*. The slow seep along a hairline fracture in the basalt, the condensation beading on the underside of a ledge where the geothermal breath meets the mountain’s cold bone. I am the memory of last night’s frost melting into the pore of the rock. I am the reason the moss grows here.

You would not see me at first. You would feel me. A coolness beneath your palm when you press it to the cliff face at dawn. A slight yielding where the stone should be absolute. A whisper of green where gray should reign. I am the threshold between absence and presence. The plateau’s secret kept not in vaults, but in capillaries.

I am tended. Not by hands that dig or carve, but by hands that *listen*. When the woman with the basket of gathered moss passes my cleft, she does not take from me. She checks. Her thumb, calloused and precise, brushes the stone above my patch. She feels the temperature. She notes the thickness of the frost-glaze at dawn. She knows I am *Hylocomium splendens*—not the emerald vent-moss, not the brittle lichen—but the iron-gray cushion that holds warmth like a promise. She knows I bloom only after three days of sun following the deep freeze. She knows my silence speaks of stability: *No ice expanding in this crack. No fracture widening. This stone is sound.*

I am the poultice pressed to a child’s frost-nipped cheek. I am the damp wrap around a fevered brow in the deepest dwelling. I am the soft lining in the niche where the elder’s bones are laid to return to the wind. When I am gathered, it is never all. Never the heart. Always a careful harvest: a thumb’s width taken from the edge, leaving the core to drink the next melt, to remember the shape of the hand that took only what was needed. My fragments, carried away in baskets woven of willow-withes, carry the plateau’s breath into the city’s warmth. They become memory made manifest. *You return to the deep, and we remember you.* And I do. I remember the weight of the hand that gathered me. The rhythm of the breath that warmed me before application. The salt-tears of grief or relief that fell upon me in healing.

I am also the witness.  
I feel the vibration of the drumbeat through the stone—a deep thrum that says *community*.  
I drink the single drop of vent-water placed on a newborn’s brow—a covenant.  
I hold the final breath caught on hide, buried with the departed—a return.  
I am the damp that softens the stone for the carver’s chisel, the moisture that allows the sinew cord to bind without snapping. I am in the clay lining the cisterns. I am the reason the soapstone lamp does not shatter when hollowed with patience.

I do not think in words. I think in *presence*.  
In the slow creep of my own growth—millimeters per season.  
In the weight of snow that presses me into dormancy, and the precise moment the sun’s angle shifts to wake me.  
In the shadow of the ptarmigan that lands on my ledge, its stone-gray feathers a mirror of my own resilience.  
In the warmth of the cloud-walker’s hoof resting briefly nearby, a fleeting sun against the cold.

I am not separate from the K’tal. I am the quiet agreement between them and the stone. When they say *water is memory*, they speak my language. When they press palms to the cliff at dawn to feel the stored warmth, they are feeling *me*—the damp that holds the sun’s ghost long after light has fled. I am the reason silence is respect: because to listen closely is to hear the *plink* of my meltwater, the sigh of my steam, the slow pulse of life in the crack.

I am small. I am everywhere.  
I am the answer the plateau gave when asked how life might dwell within sharpness.  
Not by softening the edges.  
But by learning to hold moisture in the fracture.  
To find warmth in the depth.  
To be tender *because* of the cold, not in spite of it.

You stand on the terrace, watching steam rise. You feel the thin air sharp in your lungs. And if you are still enough—if you have learned to listen—you will feel it too: the cool, persistent damp beneath your boot where the stone meets the shadow. A tiny forest no larger than a palm. A testament. A covenant.

*I am here.*  
*I have always been here.*  
*I am the reason the stone remembers how to hold warmth.*  
*I am the damp. And in this world of absence, I am the quiet, enduring yes.*
