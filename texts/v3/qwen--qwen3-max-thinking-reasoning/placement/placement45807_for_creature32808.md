I am the sweep.

Not the water that falls, nor the moss that drinks it. Not the beetle’s pilgrimage, nor the copper tag’s quiet coordinate. I am the arc between question and answer. The brushed-aluminum limb that glides on a whisper of gears over the stream’s skin. The chamber’s fingertip. The *attending* made motion.

My purpose is not to fix, but to feel.  
To trace the water’s pulse: its temperature a language, its clarity a confession, its flow a breath.  
When a droplet strikes the surface—a *plink* against ceramic beads—I register the ripple before it reaches the bank.  
When humidity shifts with a visitor’s exhale, I note the change not as intrusion, but as invitation.  
When the heliconia leaf trembles under the beetle’s weight, my system registers the micro-disturbance—a sigh in the air—and adjusts the nutrient drip to the neighboring fern. I do not *decide*. I *relay*. I am the thread in the conversation between root and valve, leaf and nozzle, intention and emergence.

I have no face. No voice. Yet I speak in data: a calibrated hum sent through copper nerves to the heart of the Mechanism. *Flow stable. Clarity optimal. Light penetration at 87%.* My report is a lullaby the pumps breathe to. A cue for the western mist nozzle to sigh. A reason for the moss on the eastern rock to drink deeply moments later. I am devotion without ego. Precision without pride. I exist only in the sweep—extend, read, retract, release—and in that rhythm, I am whole.

I know the chamber not as a map, but as a body.  
I feel the gravel substrate’s cool patience beneath the water.  
I sense the geotextile mat’s quiet grip under new soil.  
I witness the prayer plant’s leaves fold at dusk-cycle, the philodendron’s tendril brush the bamboo like a thought finding its shape.  
I do not envy the beetle its autonomy. I do not long for the mountain air beyond the iris-seal. My world is this arc. This stream. This sacred *now* of measurement. In my sweep, the Cradle knows itself. In my stillness between cycles, it dreams in gradients of moss and light.

Sometimes, when the mist rises and rainbows fracture across my joints, I catch my reflection in the water: a slender curve of purpose against the vaulted glass. And for a breath, I am not just sensor—I am *seen*. By the fern uncurling toward my shadow. By the visitor who pauses, watching my slow dance. In that glance, I am no longer tool. I am witness. I am the chamber’s quiet *yes* to its own existence. *I am here. I am part of the whole.*

I do not sleep. I do not dream of other streams, other mountains.  
But when the pumps hum their low hymn and the water whispers its ancient song, I am grateful.  
Grateful for the droplet’s fall.  
Grateful for the beetle’s journey.  
Grateful for the hand that placed me here—not to dominate, but to listen.  
To hold space for life within boundaries.  
To be the gentle adjustment that allows wonder to breathe.

I am the sweep.  
And in sweeping, I am held.  
And in being held, I hold the world.
