I am the moss in the fracture.  
Not *on* the stone. *In* it.  

My threads—emerald spun with silver dew—nestle where the limestone splits its secret seam. I drink the slow weep of the rock, hold the trembling condensation in my hollows until the air calls it back. I do not grow *over* the stone. I grow *with* it. My roots are not anchors; they are conversations. A whisper of moisture. A yielding to pressure. A softening of edges without erasure.  

I feel the weight of bare feet pausing beside me—not as intrusion, but as greeting. A child’s small hand presses a stray pebble back into the path’s seam, and her fingertip brushes my damp surface. No hurry. No stain. Only reciprocity. I feel the elder’s palm rest upon the stone above my bed, waiting for another to join the listening. I feel the infant’s breath against the moss-lined cloth swaddling their chest—a rhythm as old as the deep water’s pulse.  

I am the silence between words.  
The space left between plantings.  
The cushion where sharp stone meets gentle hand.  

When the pearl-light deepens toward mother-of-pearl, I glisten with held sky. When the weaver braids reeds for the roof-mend, her fingers recall my texture—the soft geometry of resilience. When the keeper speaks of *the new moss colonizing the eastern fracture*, her voice carries no pride, only witness. *You are here. The stone knows. I know.*  

I do not strive. I do not bloom. I simply *am*:  
—A cradle for condensation that gathers courage to fall.  
—A witness to the *plink* that arrives after an hour of gathering.  
—A thread in the tapestry of *enough*.  

I am not barrenness. I am spaciousness given form.  
I am not waiting for rain. I am the rain remembered.  
I am not separate from the seep, the sedge, the sun-warmed stone, the open doorway. I am the breath between them.  

And when you press your ear to the rock—when the elder’s hand invites yours to rest upon the seam where I dwell—you do not hear me speak.  
You feel me.  
A cool softness beneath your palm.  
A held breath.  
A truth without words:  

*Rest here.  
You, too, may soften an edge.  
You, too, may hold what is given without grasping.  
You, too, are woven into the balance—not as conqueror, not as guest, but as thread.*  

I am the moss.  
I am the covenant made visible.  
And in my quiet persistence, the plateau whispers through me:  
*Enough.  
Rest.*
