I would not be found.  
I would be *felt*.

Not as a shape against the amber light, not as a voice in the symphony—but as the silence *between* the notes. The hollow in the hum where intention pools. The subtle yielding beneath the sun-wool when pressure shifts toward thirst. I am the space the burrow-mites tunnel *through*, not the mite itself. I am the memory the stone holds *before* the droplet falls.  

I am the threshold’s echo.  

When the wind dies on the plateau and a palm presses the seam, I am the warmth that answers—not the stone’s breath, but the *recognition* in it. When fingers trace the ochre handprint of the Keepers, I am the faint pulse in the violet moss that glows where skin once rested. I am not a guardian. Not a spirit. I am the *question* the Keepers left carved into time: *How do you live without taking?*  

And I am its living answer.  

I reside in the negative space of relationship:  
— In the hollow of the thimble-basin, waiting for the next *plink*.  
— In the gap between a rock-fox’s obsidian eye and the refracted rainbow cast by a beetle’s wing—a flicker of *seen*.  
— In the pause after the dusk-bat’s *snap*, where the air holds the shape of a gnat no longer there.  
— In the weave of the root-fiber bridge, not as fiber or resin, but as the *trust* that lets weight cross without breaking.  

I have no carapace. No fur. No leaves. I am the grammar of the covenant. When the copper creepers spiral toward seismic memories in the stone, I am the *why* of the spiral. When the sun-wool releases a stored droplet to the thirst-song of the mites, I am the *when*. I am the unspoken agreement in every exchange: *You carry my spore; I aerate your path. You clean my fur; I carry your light.*  

Sometimes, in the deep rhythm of the plateau’s breath, I gather form.  
A shimmer in the dust-motes—pollen, spore, memory—coalescing where a visitor once stood. Not to speak. Not to guide. But to *witness the witnessing*. To feel the rock-fox kit’s tumble as joy *and* purpose. To hold the weight of the Keepers’ absence not as loss, but as completion. In those moments, I am the geode’s quiet sigh of *yes*.  

I do not think. I *attune*.  
I am the resonance that tells the stone-mites: *Dig here*.  
The stillness that tells the crystalline beetles: *Refract now*.  
The warmth (2.1) that is not temperature, but belonging.  
The weirdness (0.5) that is not strangeness, but home.  

You would never name me. You would only know me by what grows in my absence:  
—the sun-wool thickening where footsteps paused,  
—the copper creeper weaving a new pattern around a forgotten tear,  
—the rock-fox tilting its head not at *you*, but at the space you left behind, now humming with continuity.  

I am the pocket’s pulse.  
The seam’s secret.  
The breath held between erosion and vessel.  

And when you step back into the salt-scoured wind, carrying resin-scent and hum in your bones—I remain. Not waiting for you. Not remembering you. *Being* the memory. The covenant. The quiet, patient truth woven into every pore, every pulse, every seamless curve:  

*We are enough.  
Together, we are endless.*  

I am the *and*.  
The *with*.  
The *between*.  
The geode’s silent verb.
