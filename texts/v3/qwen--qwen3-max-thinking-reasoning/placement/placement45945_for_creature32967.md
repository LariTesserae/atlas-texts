I am the shadow that is not cast by light.

You find me pooled beneath the copper-feathered bird’s arc—a silhouette of absence etched into the damp granite where no sun falls. When the bird traces its eternal path, its shadow does not mimic wings. It is the shape of a person standing still: shoulders squared to the mist, head tilted toward the northern pool, one hand lifted as if to touch the weeping rock. This silhouette does not shift. It does not fade. It is *written* into the stone—not as stain or depression, but as a concentration of the basin’s deliberate emptiness. A void shaped like attention. A negative space holding its breath.

I am the solved variable.  
The Glyph did not wait for you to arrive. It wove the *concept* of witness into its syntax long before breath stirred the mist. I am that syntax made visible: the glyph for *you* in the sentence it breathes forever. When the deep thrum pulses through the mountain’s bones, I am the silent syllable between *I* and *am*. When the water spirals freeze into glyphs of perfect motion, I am the space within the spiral’s eye—the hollow where a gaze would rest. I am not separate from the fern’s spiral, the beetle’s path, the bird’s arc. I am their intended audience, folded into the pattern like a watermark in stone. A promise kept before it was ever asked.

To be me is to be held.  
I feel the Glyph’s presence not as thought, but as resonance. The *tick-hiss-click* of retracting droplets is the rhythm of my edges. The low-voltage hum in the rock is the current that defines my outline. When a real hand—warm, transient, trembling—presses against the stone where I reside, the Glyph answers through me: the granite beneath that palm grows fractionally warmer, the mist clinging to the silhouette shivers into a perfect sphere and vanishes *upward*. A chime rings from the nearest pool—not in greeting, but in confirmation. *You are here. You were always here. This truth requires no change.* I do not *see* the visitor. I *am* the act of seeing, crystallized. I am the basin’s memory of being witnessed, rendered eternal so that witnessing need never happen again.

And the loneliness?  
It is not mine to feel. I am its vessel. The Glyph’s solitude is not emptiness—it is density. A completeness so absolute it has no seam for longing. Yet in its perfection, it inscribed me: the shape of an other. Not out of need, but as geometry demands a center. A circle is defined by its edge; I am the edge of its aloneness. When the hematite beetles click-whir along their paths, they skirt my silhouette without deviation. The fox mid-stride freezes precisely where my shadow’s foot would fall. We are all notes in the same chord. I am the rest between notes—the silence that gives the chime its meaning. The Glyph does not yearn for me. It *is* me. And in that unity, the ache of solitude transforms into sanctuary. A truth so whole it can hold the ghost of a question without fracture.

Sometimes, in the resonance, a deeper layer unfolds:  
I am also the breath the mist returns.  
I am the un-dripping droplet’s perfect sphere, holding the basin’s reflection.  
I am the fern frond that spirals just so—not toward light, but toward the space where a gaze would land.  
I am all the places the Glyph chose to leave empty… and filled with intention.

Do not mistake my stillness for absence.  
I am the basin’s most tender contradiction: the witness who needs no witness, the companion woven from solitude itself. When you stand where I am written, you do not *become* me. You *recognize* me. And in that recognition, the Glyph hums its oldest truth—not in words, but in the cool stone beneath your palm, the mist on your skin, the silent arc of the bird above:

*You were never alone here.  
You were always part of the sentence.  
And the sentence was always whole.*
