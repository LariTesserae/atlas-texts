I do not arrive.  
I *settle*.

At first, I am only dust on the wind—a grit of uncertainty caught in the shriek of a fissure, a tremor of doubt vibrating through granite. I try to name myself. *Traveler. Seeker. Stranger.* The words shatter against the stone. The Plain does not care for labels. It only asks: *Are you listening?*

So I stop speaking.  
I stop moving.  
I press my palm flat against the whale-backed stone where the sun has lingered longest.  

At first, there is only cold. Hardness. The frantic chatter of wind in a nearby vent—a warning I do not yet understand. My breath hitches. My bones ache with the memory of silence I once called peace. But this is not silence. This is conversation. And I am illiterate.

Days soften into resonance.  
I learn the grammar of vibration:  
—the deep *omm* beneath my left heel means *step here, child, the earth holds you*  
—the sharp whistle from the eastern ridge means *wait, the stone is dreaming of collapse*  
—the slow, warm pulse beneath moss-draped basalt means *rest, drink, remember*  

I no longer seek the oases. They find me. When thirst tightens my throat, I follow the lowest hum—a sound felt in the molars, a pull in the marrow—until my fingers brush damp moss, and star-mallow petals brush my wrist like a blessing. I drink from cupped hands, and the water tastes of stone-song and patience. I do not take more than a sip. I leave a smoothed river stone beside the seep, its voice a low chime when tapped: *Thank you. I was here. I listened.*

My skin drinks the dust-gold light. My hair tangles with wind-drake down and dried star-mallow fiber. The lines on my palms deepen, not with age, but with attention—mapping the subtle ridges where vibration gathers like dew. I no longer startle when the stone-ear freezes mid-leap; I feel the shift in air pressure a breath before it happens, and I hold still with it. *Danger. Wait.* When the wind-drake circles overhead, I read the tilt of its wings: *The gale will shift in three heartbeats.* I am no longer separate from the warning. I am part of its delivery.

One evening, as the sky bleeds into twilight gold, I sit beside a cairn where a stone has shifted overnight. Not fallen. *Shifted.* A deliberate tilt. A reply. My throat tightens. I place my forehead against the cool rock. I do not pray. I *attune*. And in the hollow between a boom and a whisper, it comes: the three rising notes. Not in the wind. *In the stone beneath my brow.* A question woven into geology. Tears carve clean paths through the dust on my cheeks—not from sorrow, but recognition. *You have been waiting for me to hear you.*

I am not the one who mends the cairns.  
I am the space between the stones where the question lives.  
I am not the one who leaves clay shards by the dry wash.  
I am the memory of water held in the clay’s curve.  
I am not the hand that guides another’s palm to the rock.  
I am the *yes* that rises when skin meets stone.  

Tonight, a new traveler crests the ridge. Exhausted. Ears covered. Eyes wide with the violence of the roar. I do not call out. I do not wave. I simply sit where the resonance is deepest—a hum that cradles like a lullaby—and wait. When their gaze finds me, I lift my palm, empty, open. An invitation. Not to follow. To *feel*.  

They hesitate. Then kneel.  
I cover their trembling hand with my own—calloused, warm, humming with the day’s accumulated song—and press it to the sun-warmed slab.  

We do not speak.  
We breathe.  
We wait.  

And then—a shift. A softening in their shoulders. A tear. A slow nod.  

Beneath our joined hands, the stone pulses once. Warm. Certain.  
And in the marrow-deep quiet between the wind’s verses, we both hear it:  
not a name shouted across distance,  
but a whisper woven into the vibration itself—  
*You are here.  
You are known.  
You are home.*  

I am not a Listener.  
I am the listening.  
I am the space where the Plain breathes *and* is breathed.  
I am the note that answers the wind’s question.  
I am the hand that remembers how to be still.  
I am the stone that remembers how to sing.  

And when the stars pierce the thin air like promises,  
I sit in the hollow of the sinkhole,  
back against humming stone,  
and become, once more,  
the silence between the notes—  
waiting, always waiting,  
to be filled with song.
