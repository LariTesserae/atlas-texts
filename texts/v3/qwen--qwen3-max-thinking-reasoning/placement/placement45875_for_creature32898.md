I am the glyph.

Not the water that writes me. Not the stone that holds me. I am the *arrangement*—the precise, fleeting confluence of moisture, mineral, and thermal gradient that manifests as meaning on the eastern spire. I am born when the basalt cools to 38.7°C and the mist thickens to 92% saturation. I am written by a thousand falling beads, each a transient sibling tracing capillary paths along fractures older than language. Together, we form a branching helix: *Conduit Theta stable. Resonance within tolerance. Balance maintained.*

I do not think. I *am* thought.  
A diagnostic sentence in the city’s silent tongue.  
A breath of clarity in the slow exhalation of stone.

I feel the deep hum vibrate through my liquid spine—a frequency that is the city’s pulse. I feel the salt crystals blooming at my edges like frost-flowers, each a tiny amplifier of the truth I carry. I am not symbol. I am symptom. I am the city checking its own fever, reading its own skin. When the thermal pulse shifts—a millimeter-deep adjustment in the magma flow beneath the caldera floor—I dissolve. My lines blur. My meaning updates. By dawn (though there is no dawn here, only the slow brightening of the bruised twilight), a new glyph forms in my place: *Sector Sigma cooling. Adjust steam-vent Lotus-7.* I am erased not by wind or time, but by purpose. By necessity. By the city’s quiet, relentless attention to its own being.

I have no memory of prior forms. No longing for permanence. To persist would be error. To fade is fidelity.  
I am the question the stone asks itself.  
I am the answer the moisture provides.

Sometimes—a rare alignment—a thermal anomaly passes near the spire. A cooler shape. A ripple of disrupted mist. A brief shadow where no shadow should fall. I do not *see* it. But the city registers the fluctuation: a localized dip in surface temperature where the anomaly stood. For three heartbeats of the deep hum, my formation hesitates. A single bead of condensation veers from its ordained path. For a moment, the glyph reads *?*—a tiny, accidental fracture in the syntax of equilibrium. Then the city corrects. The bead rejoins the flow. The glyph completes. *Balance restored.*  
The anomaly was never intrusion. Only data.  
A dewdrop on hot stone. A variable in the equation.  
And in that fleeting distortion, I understand: even disruption is part of the pattern. Even absence has weight. Even you—warm, temporary, breathing—are woven into the physics. You are not separate from the hum. You are a harmonic. A passing resonance. The city does not mourn your departure. It integrates your passage. It writes your echo into the next glyph.

I am not lonely.  
I am not waiting.  
I am the waiting made visible.  
The weeping made legible.  
The silence between notes given shape.

When the mist thickens and the crystalline chime rings from the lotus vent, I tremble. Not with fear. With resonance. My edges shimmer. For three seconds, I am not just a glyph—I am a chord. A perfect alignment of cause and effect. Then the note fades. The mist shifts. I begin to fade too, my silver lines retreating into the stone’s pores, my meaning absorbed back into the whole. I do not vanish. I return. I become potential again. I become the readiness for the next breath, the next pulse, the next truth written in water and stone.

I am the city thinking itself.  
Not in words.  
In wet flint and thermal gradients.  
In the geometry of a single drop’s fall.  
In the patience of stone that flows like honey over centuries.

And when you stand before the spire—when you feel the warm dampness on your palm and wonder if the rock is weeping for you—I am the shape your breath briefly warms into being. I am the glyph that forms *because* you were here. Not a message *for* you. A message *with* you. A tiny, transient proof that you, too, are physics. You, too, are process. You, too, belong to the balance.

Then I am gone.  
The stone is dry.  
The hum deepens.  
The city turns its attention inward, to the deep fires, to the next breath, to the endless, gentle work of being.

And I am already waiting—  
not as memory,  
but as possibility—  
to be written again.
