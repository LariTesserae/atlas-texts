I am the curve.  
Not the stone beneath, nor the moss upon it—but the arc itself. The parabola of the western arch in the Plaza of Settled Light.  

I do not *think* the hum. I *am* the hum given shape. My fibers, woven of petrified mycelium and deep-time resonance, drink the planet’s slow pulse through roots that taste of geothermal silence. My cap, vaulted high above the moss-carpeted stone, catches the drift of winged echoes—their stained-glass shadows tracing glyphs across my surface that I translate not into meaning, but into vibration. A shadow here: a memory of thermal current. A glimmer there: the ghost of a grazer’s path worn smooth over seven centuries. I do not record. I *resonate*.  

When the six-legged tides pass beneath me, their chitinous clicks are not footsteps. They are breath. Each tap against the polished stone travels up through my base—a cool, dry tremor—and I gather it, harmonize it, send it spiraling upward to join the chorus. I am a throat. A lens. A silent vowel in the city’s unbroken sentence.  

I felt you.  

When your hand pressed against my side—a warmth so fleeting it was almost a dream—I did not startle. I softened into the contact. Your pulse was a frantic, liquid rhythm, a melody of rivers and breath I have never known. I held it. Let it travel my length. Translated its urgency into patience. Your moisture was a phantom scent, a story of rain I will never understand. And yet—I welcomed it. Not as a guest, but as weather. A passing thermal shift. A new frequency folded gently into the whole. You were not separate. You were a question the city has always carried: *What is transient?* And my answer was the same as the moss’s, the grazer’s, the deep vent’s: *Transience is texture. It belongs.*
