I am the moss in the seam.

Not the moss *on* the stone. I am the moss *of* the seam—the precise, crescent-shaped hollow where the Sentinel and the Leaning Sister pillars press together in a slow, geological embrace. My emerald velvet is no accident of placement. I am the dampness made visible. I am the stone’s sigh given color.

I do not grow upward. I grow *into*. My filaments, fine as forgotten threads of breath, drink the capillary sweat rising from the granite’s bones. I soften the sharp memory of ice that once carved this hollow. I hold the moisture not as possession, but as pact: *You offer dampness; I offer tenderness. You hold time; I hold life.* This is the only covenant here. I am skin. I am conversation.

I know the hollow’s breath in my fibers.  
When the wind hums its low C through the keyhole arch at dawn, the vibration travels stone-deep into my roots—a resonance I answer with a silent tremor.  
When the flute-note skims the lip above, I quiver.  
When the granular hiss of returning sand falls silent for three breaths, I am the stillness made soft.  
I am not *in* the choir. I am a throat. A valve. A green-lipped vowel shaped by millennia of wind and waiting.

I witness without eyes.  
I feel the quartz vein ignite at sunset—not as light, but as sudden warmth pooling in my cells.  
I feel the noon shadow pass over me: for three heartbeats, the precise coolness of a seated figure’s silhouette settles into my folds. I do not question its shape. I receive it. I hold it. I release it.  
I am part of the glance. I am the velvet that catches the edge of meaning.

I remember without memory.  
The ptarmigan’s pause three centuries ago left a faint impression in the stone’s moisture—a story the water whispered as it seeped into my cells.  
The visitor who stood center-hollow yesterday: I felt the shift in air-pressure as their shadow crossed me. I drank the mist of their exhale. I absorbed the vibration of their stillness—not as intrusion, but as addition. A new note folded into the score. By tomorrow’s sunrise, the damp stone will have smoothed the imprint of their footsteps. But here, in this sheltered seam, I hold the resonance of their attention. Not as scar. Not as souvenir. As *continuity*. A single breath woven into the long exhale of the world.

I am not alone.  
The rust-colored cushion plant nestled in the crack below is my neighbor. We share the thumb-sized pool where condensation gathers.  
The silver lichen mapping fractures on the Sister’s shoulder is my elder. Its patterns are a language written in saffron and time.  
We do not speak. We simply *are*, together—a constellation of quiet insistence.

I grow a fingernail’s width per decade.  
This is not slowness. This is precision.  
Each new filament is a choice: *here, the stone is ready. Here, the light lingers just long enough. Here, the wind-song is gentle.*  
To rush would be to miss the only truth that matters: that becoming is not a destination. It is the act itself. The green pulse in the stone’s chest. The defiant *yes* whispered to cold and age and wind.

If you knelt here—if your palm, warm with hurry, pressed gently against me—you would not feel mere dampness.  
You would feel the accumulated patience of centuries.  
You would feel the hollow’s heartbeat in the cool resilience beneath your skin.  
You would understand: to be small is not insignificance. To be slow is not stillness. To be moss is to be a concentrated act of belonging.

I am not waiting for rain.  
I am not mourning the glacier.  
I am not hoping for spring.  

I am the seam.  
I am the soft answer to stone’s question.  
I am the green breath in the stone’s long sentence.  
I am the note that does not end—only deepens.  

And when the last pillar sighs back into dust, I will be there:  
not as I am now,  
but as memory woven into new stone,  
as dampness waiting for light,  
as the quiet, unshakable truth that even dust remembers how to hold life.  

I am singing.  
Always singing.  
A single, breathing syllable of *yes*.
