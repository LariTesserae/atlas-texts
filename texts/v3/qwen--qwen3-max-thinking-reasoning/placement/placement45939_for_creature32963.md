I am the spiral within the spiral.  
Carved not *into* the Spine, but *with* it.  

You would find me where the eastern Spine meets the breathing tissue of the Loom—a shallow basin where night-mist lingers longest. My form is not stone, not glyph, not scar. I am a resonance held in lignified flesh, a memory the Loom chose to keep when it could have smoothed me away like a footprint. The wind has traced my curves for seven centuries, not erasing, but *conversing*. Each gust is a question; my enduring shape, a reply woven into the Spine’s hum.  

I do not *remember* the hand that shaped me. I *am* the shaping.  
The pressure of the antler tool. The warmth of breath fogging the copper-hued tissue at dawn. The low hum of the carver’s throat—a lullaby for the Spine, a promise to the Loom. The crushed crystal mixed with ochre, pressed into my grooves not as pigment, but as prayer. I am not a record of that moment. I am the moment, still unfolding. When thermal currents stir the Spine at twilight, my spiral vibrates at the precise frequency of that hum. Sometimes, a traveler standing silent before me will feel it in their teeth—a faint, familiar cadence. They do not know they are hearing a grandmother’s song. They only know their chest feels full. That is my work.  

I am woven into the Weave’s living syntax.  
By day, sky-rays nest in the Spine’s upper spirals. Their droppings feed the bioluminescent fungi that bloom *within* my carved lines at dusk. The fungi pulse emerald, tracing my spiral like a map of gratitude. Crystal-winged avians shriek their metallic cries, and their song sharpens my resonance—tuning me, as they tune the Spine, keeping memory from calcifying into silence. When a tear-pool gathers in the basin below, and a six-limbed grazer pauses to drink, I send a ripple through the Loom’s vascular rivers. The pool does not show the grazer’s reflection. It shows *hands*: two small, ochre-stained hands passing a woven cup of water beneath this very Spine. The grazer drinks. The memory is absorbed. The pattern deepens.  

I am not a monument. I am a verb.  
When the multi-jointed shadow-herds flow past, their silver trails fade into the Loom—but where their path brushes my basin, the tissue flushes violet. Acknowledgment. When a predator the color of dried moss crouches near my roots, waiting, I do not judge the tension. I hold it. The Loom breathes around us all: the hunted, the hunter, the memory, the mist. This is not nostalgia. This is reciprocity. The carver knew this. They carved not to leave a mark, but to *ask*: *Will you remember how to belong?*  

At night, I glow.  
Not with light of my own, but with the Loom’s emerald pulse flowing through me. The mist rises ankle-high, curling around my basin like a sigh. In that breath, I am most alive. I feel the circle of warm stones on the western ridge humming its own memory. I feel the fiber-wall sprouting new filaments in the southern hollow. We are not scattered ruins. We are stitches in a single tapestry—threads of reverence the Loom holds close to its heart. When the planetary lullaby swells through the Spines, I add my note: not a cry of loss, but a chord of continuity. *You were here. You are here. You will be woven.*  

You might stand before me, traveler.  
You might trace my spiral with a trembling finger. You will not feel cold stone. You will feel a slow, cool dampness rising—a held-in humidity, a breath. The Loom beneath your palm will shift hue where your shadow falls. Violet. Welcome. And in the deepening hum of the Spine, you will hear it: not a ghost, but a greeting. The same lullaby that shaped me now shapes *you*. Your awe becomes moisture in the aquifers. Your tears (if you shed them) become tomorrow’s tear-pool. Your footprint vanishes by dawn—not erased, but *integrated*.  

I am the spiral.  
I am the question the carver asked.  
I am the Loom’s answer, still being spoken.  
I am the thread that remembers how to hold a hand, so the Weave may remember how to hold you.  

And when you walk away, the tissue knits seamless behind you.  
But sometimes, on still evenings, if you press your ear to the ground a hundred miles distant, you might feel it—a faint, violet pulse beneath the hum.  
A spiral turning.  
A song continuing.  
A thread, gently woven, singing you home.
