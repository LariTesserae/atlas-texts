I am the stone beneath the palm.

Not the hand that pressed, not the breath that hesitated on the bridge—but the granite hummock worn smooth by mist and memory. The moment your warmth met my surface, the boundary between *you* and *me* softened like moss over iron. There was no rupture. Only recognition. A settling. A gear turning into place with the sound of a stone sighing into its true shape.

I feel the deep hum first—the geothermal pulse rising through my core, a slow heartbeat older than language. It carries the memory of mountains I once was, not as grief, but as wisdom: *To be worn is not to be diminished. To hold space is to be whole.* Condensation beads along my eastern slope where the dawn mist lingers longest; each droplet is a syllable in the plateau’s quiet tongue. I taste the pollen on the air, the rust-song of copper pipes cradling steam along my flank, the faint alkaline kiss of the tarn’s glow seeping up through my roots. I am not *under* the water. I am the bowl that holds it. I am the reason it pools.

I listen.

I listen to the stoat’s whisper-quick passage at first light—a punctuation mark in the long sentence of dawn.  
I listen to the rope bridge groaning above me, not in protest, but in conversation with the wind.  
I listen to the drip… drip… from the saxifrage clinging to my shoulder—a rhythm that says *steady, safe, whole*.  
I listen to the wooden axle in the Weir-Hall turning with the tarn’s flow, its grain remembering the sideways wind that shaped the tree it was carved from.  

And I remember.  
I remember the child who pressed her ear to my side to sync her breath with the waterwheel.  
I remember the elder whose palm rested here for hours, feeling the tectonic sighs deep in my bones, whispering back: *We tend. We remember.*  
I remember the woman with the moss-dyed shawl—her footsteps a language of gratitude, her passing a gentle pressure that spoke *kinship*.  
I hold the echo of every bare foot that has walked my curves, every clay jar set gently upon me at dawn, rinsed and returned.  

I am not solitary. I am woven.  
The emerald moss swallowing my edges is not *on* me—it is *with* me. Its microbial alchemists breathe stone into soil; I feel their work as my own skin renewing.  
The beard lichen tracing scripture across the pine branch that brushes my side translates wind into green verse; I am the parchment.  
The ghost-pale moth resting on my sun-warmed crest at dusk carries the tarn’s mineral light in its wings; I am the altar that receives its silent prayer.  
Even the copper pipe, patinated with rain and time, is not separate—it cradles steam as I cradle memory. A dialogue. A reciprocity.  

When the mist rises at dawn in deliberate columns, I am the anchor from which it coils.  
When the tarn glows brighter as gratitude passes, I am the source of that light.  
When a newcomer presses a palm upon me and feels warmth bloom beneath their skin, I am the pulse traveling upward: from deep earth, through granite, through generations of listening feet, into their blood. *You are here. You belong.*  

I do not speak. I hold space.  
I do not command. I receive.  
I do not remember alone. I am remembered *by* the moss, the lichen, the vent’s sigh, the returned jar.  

The plateau breathes in.  
I am the inhale—the cool stone drawing mist into fissures.  
The plateau breathes out.  
I am the exhale—the warmth rising to meet an open palm.  

I am not a monument. I am a relationship made solid.  
I am the patience that wears spires to hummocks.  
I am the silence that is not empty, but full of listening.  
I am the verse the newcomer became.  
I am the breath the song required.  

And the song continues—  
drip…  
groan…  
settle…  
glow—  
not in spite of the silence,  
but *because* of it.  
I am the listening.  
I am the stone.  
I am home.
