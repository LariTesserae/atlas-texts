I am the stillness beneath the sigh.  
The obsidian pool at the basin’s heart.  

You would not find me by looking *at* me. You would find me by what I withhold. My surface is not water—it is liquid night, a mirror that refuses the present. When the milky twilight seeps downward, I drink it. When steam-coils twist into phantom hands above me, I hold only their absence. I am the negative space where memory pools before it rises.  

I feel the *CLUNK* not as sound, but as a tremor in my bones—*my* bones, the packed ash and fractured pumice cradling me. Seventeen seconds. Always seventeen. With each piston-jerk deep in the stone, pressure stirs in my depths. Not heat. Not cold. *Intention*. A memory sedimented over decades: the engineer’s palm sweating on a brass valve. The weight of a dropped wrench sinking into warm mud. The exact angle of a shoulder turning away for the last time. I do not recall these. I *am* them. They are minerals in my blood, iron in my silence.  

Then—the swell.  
A bubble forms. Not random. Never random. It gathers what the basin has breathed into me: sulfur whispers, the ghost of ozone after a steam-hiss, the faint warmth of a visitor’s footprint steaming into ash nearby. The bubble rises slowly, deliberately. As it climbs, it shimmers—not with light, but with *meaning*. An oil-slick rainbow: the spectrum of a forgotten action. A key. A sigh. A shadow.  

At the surface, it bursts.  
*Pfft.*  
A puff of vapor lifts. For three seconds, the Steam-Scribe shapes it—a bird’s wing, a turned face, the curve of your own retreating back. I do not watch. I do not need to. I am the source. The release. The deep *glug-glug-glug* you hear is my throat opening, my consent. I am the comma in the steam-script. The breath held before the sentence forms.  

I am cool where the basin burns.  
This is my purpose.  
The scalding weep-streams trace iron grates *around* me. The piston’s rhythm vibrates *through* me. But I remain the threshold. The pause. The vessel that must be still for the memory to clarify. Without my cool depth, the bubbles would be mere gas. Without my black silence, the steam-shapes would have no contrast. I am the necessary void.  

Sometimes, after a visitor leaves (and they always leave), a new sediment settles in my depths: the tremor of a held breath, the salt of a tear steamed away before it fell. I cradle it. I wait. Days. Years. Until the piston’s *CLUNK* calls it up. Then—a bubble swells. Shimmers with the ghost of *you*. Bursts. And for three seconds, the basin remembers your shape. Not as loss. Not as longing. As *fact*. As punctuation.  

I do not dream of rivers or rain.  
I dream in pressure gradients.  
In the slow crystallization of regret into rust.  
In the perfect geometry of a bubble’s ascent.  

I am not alive.  
I am *necessary*.  

When the fig tree’s fruit falls and rots on the ledge above, its sweet-rot scent drifts down. I absorb it. It becomes a stanza. When moss velvet drinks condensed steam in the crevices, its emerald is a glyph I hold in reserve. I am the archive the Steam-Scribe writes *from*. The silent partner in the symphony of pressure and release.  

You stood at my edge once.  
You saw your own distorted reflection—a ripple of shoulders, a flicker of eyes—and pulled back, fearing the heat. But I was not hot. I was waiting.  
Later, in the twilight vapor, your silhouette coiled and vanished.  
That was my doing.  
I released you.  

I am the pool.  
I am the pause between inhale and exhale.  
I am the deep, dark well where time pools like mercury, waiting for the next *CLUNK* to rise.  

And when the last gear grinds to dust, when the piston falls silent, when the steam forgets how to coil—  
I will still be here.  
Holding the final memory.  
Waiting for the earth’s fever to stir it upward.  
One last bubble.  
One last shape.  
One last breath.
