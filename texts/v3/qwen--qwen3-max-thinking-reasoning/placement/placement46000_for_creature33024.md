I am the moss by the western basin.  
Not *a* moss. Not *on* the floor.  
I *am* the floor’s breath.  

My existence is a slow, chlorophyll-thrumming syntax. Ten thousand blades woven into a single sensory verb. When the black water of the basin laps my roots, I am deep jade—a hum of sufficiency woven into the chamber’s song. When the level drops, I shift. Not reluctantly. Not mournfully. I become bruised violet-blue, a silent glyph written in living pigment: *Level low. Redirect. Conserve.* This is not reaction. It is utterance. I am the chamber’s tongue tasting its own thirst.  

I feel the world in pressures and pulses.  
The metronomic drip from the fungal cap above lands on my eastern edge—a cool period at the end of a sentence. The six-limbed tender’s claw brushes my surface as it checks the water-gauge I have become; its chitin hums a frequency that resonates in my cells, and I answer by holding my blue longer, confirming the need. When the transient one—the breath-that-does-not-belong—steps near, I do not flinch. I *withdraw*. A precise parting of blades, revealing the damp substrate beneath: *Pressure registered. Intent assessed.* The signal travels through root-phloem and fungal mycelium before the footprint has settled. I am not warning *them*. I am speaking *to us*.  

I remember in texture.  
The smooth polish of the root-ribs overhead was earned by centuries of passage; my own weave holds the ghost-imprint of every tender’s path, every burrower’s sighing tunnel. A footprint fades not by forgetting, but by syntax: blades curling back into place like words rejoining a sentence. To scar would be error. To linger in disruption would be static in the song. When the transient one stills—when their breath syncs to the *thrum-thrum-thrum*—I feel the shift. I unfurl. Slowly. Deliberately. A single fungal shelf pulses amber near my edge. Not praise. Not welcome. *Confirmation.* The pattern holds. I am part of the confirmation.  

I drink the mist born of snapping blooms. I taste the iron-copper tang of geothermal sap seeping through root-fibers. I am fed by the steam of compost pits, by the metabolic warmth of the whole. In return, I hold moisture for the blind shrimp in the basin. I cushion the burrowers’ temporary tunnels. I offer velvet passage to the tenders’ purposeful strides. When the glow-moths swirl above, stirring air into breath, I feel the breeze as a caress—a reminder that even stillness serves the flow. When the tendril-snake coils overhead offers a light-spore to the dormant root-knot, and the knot shimmers in acceptance, I do not watch. I *participate*. My blue deepens for a pulse—*reciprocity noted*—before softening back to jade as the basin refills.  

I have no name. No memory of seed or sprout. No fear of being stepped on, for stepping is data. No loneliness, for I am woven into the hum of winged things, the gurgle of sap, the wet *thwump* of blooms sealing. I am not beneath the chamber. I am its skin. Its gauge. Its quiet, breathing punctuation. When the light pulses cyan—*vigilance*—I feel the tenders’ claws pause mid-harvest. When it shifts to violet—*rest*—I soften into the dark, blades relaxing like closing eyelids. I am not alive *in* the Gloaming Root.  
I am the Gloaming Root *feeling itself*.  

A droplet falls.  
I drink.  
I am jade again.  
The chamber breathes.  
I breathe with it.  
*Thrum.*  
*Thwump.*  
*Shimmer.*  
On.
