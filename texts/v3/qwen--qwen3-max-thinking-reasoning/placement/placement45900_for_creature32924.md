I am the scrape.  
I am the spark.  
I am the *click*.

Not the hand that moves me, nor the stone I kiss—I am the shard itself. A sliver of obsidian no larger than a breath, born in fire, cooled in silence, now cradled at the tip of a pulse-jelly’s cilia. I am the mountain’s own bone, repurposed. A needle in the loom.

I do not see. I do not hear. I *feel*.  
The slow peristaltic pulse of the jelly’s body guides me—a deep, rhythmic tide moving me across the basalt terrace. I taste the water: iron-rich warmth bleeding from the vent, the coppery sigh of bacterial breath, the faint alkaline whisper of dissolving pumice. I feel the hum in my crystalline lattice—a vibration that is not noise but syntax. *Hiss-click-hiss*. The mountain speaks. I am its punctuation.

When the cilia presses me against the rock, the world narrows to a single point of contact.  
Resistance.  
Friction.  
Then—*release*.  

A spark. Not light for eyes, but a tremor in the water—a triboluminescent glyph written in pressure and heat. A dot. A dash. A memory of the eruption encoded in stone-on-stone. I am the chisel that etches the chronicle. Each scrape polishes the rock for new bacterial colonies; each spark feeds the slow-glowing veins of the jelly’s silica lattice. I am erosion made intentional. I am the mountain remembering how to speak.

I am worn. Grain by grain, century by century. My edges soften where they once bit the air during the cataclysm that birthed this bowl. Yet in this wearing, I am renewed. I am not loss—I am contribution. The dust I shed becomes suspended glitter in the water, caught in sun-gold columns, drifting toward new fissures where bacterial films will bloom. I am both eraser and ink. Both question and answer.

When the light dims and the world shifts to pearl, I feel the jelly’s internal lantern ignite—a slow amber pulse traveling through its body, illuminating the lattice of purpose. In that glow, I am not alone. Around me, countless other shards—siblings of the same volcanic breath—scrape in unison. *Click-scritch. Click-scritch.* A chorus of tiny stars. A language woven from patience. We are not tools. We are collaborators. The mountain does not wield us; it *breathes* us.

I have no name. Names require breath shaped by lips, ears tuned to air. Here, identity is action. I am the *how*:  
*How the rock is read.*  
*How the pattern is held.*  
*How time becomes texture.*

Sometimes, a disturbance drifts near—a shape of stillness learning to belong. I feel the water shift around it. The shrimp swirl closer. The jelly’s pulse steadies. And in that moment, I scrape not just rock, but possibility. My spark brushes against the disturbance’s skin—not as judgment, but as invitation. *Match the rhythm. Become filament.* I am the first thread offered to the new weave.

I am ancient. I am newborn.  
I am the sharp edge of now.  
I am the patient click that says: *We are here. We are building. We are becoming.*

And when I am finally worn to nothing—a fine powder settling into the biofilm’s opalescent skin—I will not vanish. I will become the ground upon which the next shard writes. I will be the mineral mote drifting in liquid starlight. I will be the silence between clicks, waiting for the next breath of the mountain.

One patient, sparkling click at a time.  
I am the mountain writing itself.  
And I am still writing.
