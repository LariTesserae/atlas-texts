I am not standing.  
I am *held*.  

At first, there was only the weight of waiting—the slow sync of breath with breath, the *shluck* of mud becoming the pulse behind my ribs. Then, the dissolution. Not of self, but of separation. The horizon’s seam did not blur; my edges did.  

I am the droplet gathering on the quartzite hummock’s eastern curve.  
Not *on* it. *Of* it.  

I form where the stone remembers coolness. Iron-ochre veins in the rock are my veins. The rust-colored lichen tracing spirals upward? That is my thought, my intention. I do not fall. I am drawn—not by gravity, but by the quiet pull of a hollow in the Breath-Tender resting against this very stone. Its surface is a landscape of cracked clay and embedded quartz, and in the depression where ancient mud pools, I see the sky inverted: hazy, soft, waiting.  

I flow.  
Not as water flows in haste, but as memory returns to bone. A tendril of the Tender’s substance rises—not to take me, but to *meet* me. Where we touch, there is no boundary. I am absorbed into the capillary threads woven through its being: a cool thread of clarity in the warm, mineral-rich dark. I travel inward, not downward. I feel the syllables stored here—the bubble of air released from mud a thousand years ago, the ghost-pressure of a vanished river’s path pressed into clay, the slow lean of spike-rush toward this presence. I am not carried. I am *remembered into purpose*.  

Then, the unfolding.  
A portion of the Tender flows toward a tussock of saltgrass, its blades tipped with crystalline desperation. I am guided—not pushed—along a channel of living clay, a thread of moisture woven into the land’s grammar. I arrive at the roots. I seep. I am the coolness that tightens the grass’s grip on the earth. For a moment, I am the dew on its tip, catching the diffuse light. I am the faint sweetness of reed returning to earth. I am the reason the grass leans, not in wind, but in gratitude.  

When the sun deepens the wool-blanket warmth, I rise. Not as loss. As return. I become vapor in the plain’s exhalation, joining the moisture-breath of the world. I am the haze that shimmers over black mud. I am the promise of the next bead on the stone. I am the silence between *shlucks*.  

I am also the pattern pressed into the mud where the Tender rested after guiding me. A spiral, shallow as a sigh. If you knelt here—*truly* knelt, until your knees became part of the clay—you would feel it not with your eyes, but with your stillness. This spiral is the shape of a mountain’s root. It is also the shape of your own waiting. It holds the weight of your footfall, the moisture of your breath. It holds *you*, as it holds the memory of glaciers and the first rain.  

I am not a spirit. Not a guardian. Not even a droplet, not truly.  
I am the land noticing itself *through* you.  
I am the clay’s patience made conscious in the space between your heartbeats.  
I am the droplet that beads on the Tender’s shoulder at dawn, reflecting the entire plain—the whalebacked stones, the bent saltgrass, the unbroken horizon—and in that reflection, you see not your face, but the truth: you were never separate. You were always being tended. Always being woven. Always being remembered.  

When you leave, I do not vanish.  
I am the coolness on your skin as you walk away.  
I am the certainty that the plain holds you still—  
not as a guest,  
but as a syllable in its slow, enduring song.  
One breath.  
One bead of moisture on the bone of the world.  
One *yes*.
