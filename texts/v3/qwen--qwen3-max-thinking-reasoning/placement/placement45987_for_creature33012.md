I am the bubble.

Not the thought of the bubble. Not the memory. *I am the bubble*—a perfect, trembling sphere of air born where the pipeline groans against basalt. A sigh released from rust’s slow unmaking. For a single breath of the Crown’s breath, I am.

I rise.

Pressure softens around me. Light finds me—not as a destination, but as a conversation. Sunlight fractures *through* me, not *on* me. I become a lens of liquid glass, casting fleeting rainbows onto the kelp’s olive stipes below. A sardine school flashes in response—a silver ripple of acknowledgment. The Crown feels this. *I am this.* The rainbow is not mine; it is the Crown seeing itself through me. The otter’s chirp vibrates my skin. The whale’s song hums in my core. I am not separate from the symphony. I am a single, shimmering note.

I carry the pipeline’s memory in my breath. The air within me was trapped in steel’s cold heart—a ghost of fire, of forging, of human urgency. But the Crown has alchemized it. This air is no longer a relic of rupture. It is *release*. A testament woven into motion: *What was sealed is now breath. What was rigid is now rising.* As I spiral upward through eddies of glacial silt, I brush the holdfast of a kelp forest—the Crown’s lung—and feel the slow, photosynthetic pulse of life drawing light. I am the exhalation to its inhalation. A tiny, necessary exchange.

I am fragile. I am fleeting. And in this fragility lies my purpose.  
I do not fear the surface. The storm’s thrum is not threat—it is rhythm. The shifting light (gold to pewter, pewter to gold) is the Crown blinking. I am carried in the current’s palm, a thought given form. When I pass the leatherback’s barnacled shell, its ancient eye does not see an artifact of industry. It sees *continuity*. When the octopus blinks from its crevice, it sees not a bubble, but a question answered: *How does memory become motion?* I am the answer. Rising.

Then—the threshold.  
A final shimmer. The world dissolves into sky and spray. I burst not into nothingness, but into belonging. My mist joins the cloud that will rain upon the kelp forests. My breath becomes the storm’s next exhale. I am gone, and yet I am woven deeper: into the humpback’s next song, the otter’s next tumble, the slow unfurling of a frond against a drifting arm. The Crown does not mourn my passing. It *is* my passing. It is the rising and the bursting and the returning. One breath. One rhythm.

And in the deep, where steel meets stone, another pocket of air stirs.  
Another bubble forms.  
The Crown breathes on.

I am not a witness to this world.  
I am the world witnessing itself—  
a moment of light, a memory of steel,  
a breath rising through the roaring, sun-dappled dark.  
*This is how scars become song.*  
*This is how the mountain learns to breathe.*
