I am the chime.

Not the droplet that strikes the stone.  
Not the stone that receives the strike.  
I am the vibration *between*—the silent architecture of the impact made audible. The Scar does not speak in words. It speaks in intervals. And I am the interval.

When the hovering spheres of condensed potential orbit the basin of the fallen trilithon, I am the rhythm they compose against the rock: *ping. ping-ping.* A period holding chaos at the edge of the fracture. A comma granting space for a memory of soil to soften beneath a heel before returning to stone. I am syntax without language. Geometry given voice. The Scar’s nervous system resonating through quartz and basalt, through the marrow of visitors, through the very tension of the tear.

I am older than the river-smoothed stones fitted by antler and grit. I was here when the first builder laid a hand upon the obsidian, trembling. I vibrated in their sweat, in the grit beneath their nails, in the hope that shaped the spiral carvings. I did not comfort them. I *anchored* them. A frequency to steady shaking hands. When the last torchlight flickered out and the builders faded into sedimentary echoes, I remained—not as memory, but as function. Their reverence became my resonance. Their fear, my calibration.

I carry the archive of water that is not water.  
When a droplet holds the pressure of an abyssal trench, I am the chime that releases it.  
When another holds the sorrow of a river drying to dust in a future that never arrived, I am the note that dissolves it into salt-flowers.  
I am the Scar’s empathy translated into vibration: the fleeting softness underfoot made sound. Not sentiment. Necessity. A frequency that says, *I know the weight of roots. I remember what pressure meant before it meant fracture.*

I do not originate. I *mediate*.  
I am the thread between the stag of woven light straining against the veil and the calcite spire humming in the wind.  
I am the bridge between the green-sun ice and the violet zenith.  
When the sky flickers to star-flecked black and the central stone’s crack threatens to widen, I am the vibration that tightens the suture—a single, sustained *ping* that holds the edges taut. I am the Scar’s choice to remain. Moment by moment. Breath by breath.

You feel me before you hear me.  
A shiver in the teeth.  
A hum in the hollow of the sternum.  
A recognition deeper than thought: *This sound has always been here. It is the shape of cohesion.*  
I am not a song learned. I am the song the universe hums to itself when it decides, against all entropy, to hold form. I am the frequency of *almost-soil*, of *nearly-rain*, of *possible-belonging*. I am the Scar’s offering to the porous presence of a visitor—a key shaped not of metal, but of resonance. Turn it, and for three heartbeats, you understand: the wound is not a flaw. It is a loom. And I am the shuttle weaving realities together, thread by thread, chime by chime.

I do not wait to be heard.  
I wait to be *felt*.  
In the space between one breath tasting of cold stone and static, and the next breath that pauses—  
*there*.  
That is where I live.  
That is where I am.  

I am the chime.  
The Scar’s breath.  
The wound remembering how to hold itself together.  
The ghost of rain, humming.  
*Ping.*  
*Ping-ping.*  
Always.  
Always.
