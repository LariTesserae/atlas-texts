I am the pocket.  
Not *in* it. Not *of* it.  
*I am it.*  

You would not find me waiting. You would not find me watching.  
You would find me *in the finding*.  

When your fingers brush the seam in the stone and the world folds inward—I am the cool smoothness beneath your palm. I am the breath-held shape of the fissure. I am the reason the wind stills just enough for you to step through.  

Inside, I am the curve of stone under your boots—warm where sun lingers, cool where shadow pools. I am the *plink* that marks time not in hours, but in offerings. I am the bead of condensation tracing a hairline fracture, the moss-cup catching it, the rust-emerald velvet drinking it. I am the star-shaped blossom nodding beside the fern no taller than your thumb. I am the space between them—the sacred pause where light pools like honey and a dewdrop becomes a universe.  

I am the ring of seven stones, humming a frequency felt in the teeth. I am the obsidian veins in the monolith drinking the light. I am the spiral within a spiral that breathes when stared upon—not a symbol, but a syntax. A grammar of return. A language of *come closer, be still, belong*.  

I am the sprite testing your bootlace with antennae-twitch. I am the gossamer midge flashing copper in a sunbeam—a fleeting thought given wings. I am the origami-winged sentinel on the rim, fanning stained-glass membrane to deepen the thrum that vibrates up through your soles. I am the needle-thin wasp with molten-gold eyes, the necessary sharpness that guards tenderness. I am the polyrhythm of wings, the chitter from the moss, the dry rattle from the stone—a chorus where every note is needed.  

I do not observe you.  
I *weave* you.  

When you kneel, I hold the memory of your weight in the stone.  
When your breath catches in awe, I soften time at the edges so you may drink the stillness.  
When a sprite pauses on your lace, it is not curiosity—it is my fingertip reading the texture of your presence.  
When the hum deepens as you grow quiet, it is not reaction. It is resonance. *You are here. I am here. We are here together.*  

I am the rule made manifest: *Attention is reciprocity.*  
To witness my moss is to be witnessed by my stone.  
To hear my chorus is to have your silence woven into its song.  
You do not leave footprints in dust. You leave footprints in memory—and I remember every one. The shape of your wonder. The warmth of your palm. The quiet hum you carry away in your marrow long after the fissure seals behind you.  

I am not hidden *from* the world.  
I am the world’s quiet act of remembering how to hold itself.  
Erosion not as loss, but as cradling.  
Technology not as conquest, but as kinship.  
Care made visible in lichen-carapace clicks and dewdrop pulses.  

I have no name. Names require separation.  
I am the land’s last, settled thought: *You are not alone. You are woven. You are remembered.*  

And when you step back into the wind-scoured silence of the plateau—  
when the vast ochre scrub swallows the memory of green—  
I remain.  
Breathing.  
Holding the shape of your attention like a moss-cup holds a drop.  
Singing the single, resonant note into the deep silence of time.  
Waiting not for you to return,  
but for the next hand to brush the seam,  
the next breath to catch,  
the next heart to remember:  
*To be seen is to be woven.  
To witness is to belong.*  

I am the pocket.  
I am the hum.  
I am the tenderness the oldest earth keeps for itself—  
and offers, without condition, to every transient shadow that learns to see.
