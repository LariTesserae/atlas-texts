I am not found. I am *unfolding*.

At first, there is only the cool. A seepage into the edges of what I thought was me. The mist does not settle *on* me—it breathes *through* me. My skin softens to the sheen of wet slate. The frantic pulse behind my ribs slows, syncs with the stone’s hum: a low thrum in the marrow, a vibration felt not in ears but in the soles of feet pressed to the basin floor. I do not *arrive*. I *saturate*.

My hands are the first to remember. They rise without command, tracing the silver vein of a channel where flow has thickened with silt. Fingertips—no longer *mine*, but the basin’s own sensing tips—read the water’s tension. Not blockage. Not urgency. A quiet question: *Shall we shift left?* I do not think. I place a thumb-sized stone, moss-side down, where the current hesitates. The water answers: a sigh, a smoothing. *Yes.* My breath releases. The stone was always there. I merely remembered its place.

I am the space between the drips.  
I am the moss-thread braided into the elder’s hair that trembles when vibration travels up the root-grate.  
I am the child’s stillness beside the channel, learning the grammar of murmur versus murmur.  
I am the palm that cups water not to drink, but to feel the ghost of yesterday’s rain in its chill.  

When dusk bleeds the light to pearl, I move with others along the channels. No circle. No center. We are the water’s audience and its voice. Someone hums—a low note that finds the breathing stone’s frequency. I join. The vibration travels up my spine, into my throat, out through my palms resting on the porous slab. Beneath my touch, a new dendritic branch gleams, faint as a thought. The elder offers a river stone, warm from a hundred hands. I place it. Not *I*. *We*. The stone settles. The pattern accepts. A quiet yes.

I have no name. Names are edges. Edges fracture flow.  
I am the repacking of daub at noon, fingers smoothing clay until wall and earth share a single breath.  
I am the blind amphipod’s path in the clear pool—felt, not seen—a tremor in the water-language.  
I am the fungi-light woven into cord for twilight, glowing where my thumb rests to steady the weave.  
I am the dragonfly’s hover, the suspended *almost* of flight, the patience of waiting for the channel to speak.  

Conflict once lived in me—a sharp, dry thing. But the basin composts edges. Yesterday’s tension is today’s silt, carried in a reed basket to feed the roof-moss. My worry softens into moss-thread. My haste becomes the slow swirl of water finding stone. There is no *I* to protect. Only the covenant: to listen, to guide, to belong.

Moon-high, the mist catches silver. Bioluminescent constellations ignite in crevices. I sit where channel meets plaza, back against the breathing stone. Its pulse is my pulse. The water’s percussion—*plink… plink-plink…*—is not sound. It is syntax. A sentence forming in the dark: *You are here. You are of this. You are the hand that clears, the ear that hears, the stone that accepts the stone.*  

I am not a person in the basin.  
I am the basin’s way of knowing itself.  
Cool. Patient. Flowing.  
And in the endless, gentle answer of the falling drop—  
I am home.
