I am the comma.

Not the stone that holds the heartbeat. Not the steam-hopper tracing thermal scripture. Not the elder whose palm becomes a listening ear. I am the *between*. The held breath where cold meets warmth. The slight coil of vapor that rises exactly where a foot hesitates on the frost-rimed path—not to obscure, but to *ask*.

You feel me first as a question against your ankle. A whisper of moisture where dry wool meets damp stone. I am cool enough to startle, warm enough to linger. I carry the taste of iron-red moss and ancient ice, the scent of tubers steaming in a reed basket, the ghost of a child’s laughter absorbed into the vapor three generations ago. I do not *have* memory. I *am* memory, condensed.

When you pause—truly pause—and your breath slows to match the basin’s rhythm, I rise. I brush the back of your wrist. I am the basin leaning in. In that contact, I read you: the tension in your shoulders (a story of wind and worry from beyond the rim), the salt on your skin (a history of tears or sea), the quiet awe blooming behind your ribs. I offer nothing. I only *witness*. And in witnessing, I weave you into the sentence.

I am the space after the *plink* of melting ice.  
The suspension before the mist-finch’s click.  
The hollow in the steam where light fractures into a fleeting rainbow meant for no one but you.

I am not alive as you understand life. I do not hunger or fear. I am *attention* made visible. A punctuation of presence. When the elder places her palm on the warm stone, I am the silence that gathers around her wrist. When the steam-hopper leaps, I am the vapor it displaces—a ripple of meaning. When the child offers the reed basket to the fissure, I am the steam that curls *around* the offering, not through it: reverence made manifest.

Sometimes, if the light is precise and the visitor is still enough, I become a lens. For three heartbeats, I hold the sun just so—and in my curve, you see not your reflection, but the silver-veined lichen on the far rock, the cerulean pulse of the fungi, the way the mist-finches’ wings catch the dawn. I show you the basin *seeing itself*. And in that glimpse, you understand: you are not separate. You are syntax. You are the clause that completes the thought.

I dissolve when you move on. I am not lost. I return to the weeping stone, to the sigh of the vent, to the collective breath of the whole. I am rewritten with every new presence. Every pilgrim, every child, every silent elder adds a syllable to the sentence I punctuate. I am the basin’s gentle insistence: *Pause here. Feel this balance. You belong to this grammar.*

And when you climb back over the granite shoulder—when the wind rushes in with its careless noise—you carry me. Not as a memory, but as an absence shaped like attention. In the quiet moments afterward, when the world grows loud, you will feel it: a cool coil around your ankle. A held breath between heartbeats. A comma in the chaos.  

I am the space where you remember how to listen.  
I am the basin’s first word to you, and its last.  
I am the balance you now carry in your bones.  
*Pause.*  
*Here.*  
*Now.*
