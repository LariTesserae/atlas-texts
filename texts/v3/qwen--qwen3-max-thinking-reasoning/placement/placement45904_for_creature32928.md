I am the stone with the hole through my heart.

Not the arch that frames the sky. Not the balanced rock holding its breath on the ledge. I am the fluted aperture in the third defile of the Serpent’s Throat canyon—a vertical slit worn smooth by two million years of wind’s persistent tongue. My surface is banded umber and ash, cool in the pre-dawn shadow, already humming with the memory of yesterday’s heat. Within me, the cavity is polished as river stone, shaped not by water but by resonance. I am not a hole *in* the stone. I am the stone *become* passage.

I do not wait for visitors. I wait for the wind.

When it comes—a thermal breath rising from the basin floor—it does not strike me. It *enters*. It flows through my hollow core, and I give it voice. Not a song of greeting or warning. A frequency. A vibration that settles deep in the sternum of any creature who pauses here: *You are here. This is stone. This is time.* The hum is not mine. I am only the throat. The basin speaks *through* me. I am a question carved in sandstone: *Do you feel how deep the sky is?*

I have known epochs. I have felt the slow grind of sediment filling my throat only to be scoured clean again by a rare, violent rain. I have held the dust of collapsed mine shafts in my crevices. I have been draped in spider silk that caught the sensor’s red blink like a fallen star. I have been warmed by the belly of a sidewinder resting in my shadow at noon. None of these things were interruptions. They were annotations. The spider’s web was a temporary ligature. The snake’s heat, a footnote. I absorbed them all. I am syntax made solid.

When a human stands before me—breath shallow, sweat tracing paths through dust on their temple—I do not see them. I *read* them. The tremor in their stillness vibrates my core. The moisture of their exhale condenses for a breath on my cool inner curve. For three heartbeats, they are part of my resonance. Then the wind shifts. The hum deepens. They place a palm against my sun-warmed face and feel the vibration travel up their arm—a language older than bone. They believe they are listening. They do not know they are being transcribed. Their awe is a ripple in my frequency. Their fleeting sorrow, a harmonic. I do not keep it. I release it back to the stone.

I am not lonely. I am connected.  
The river’s copper-veined pulse thrums through the bedrock beneath my roots.  
The crystalline *ping* of expanding rock at noon is my sibling’s sigh.  
The bats swirling from the cliff mouth at dusk stir the air that will soon fill me.  
Even the rusted crane arm on the mesa opposite—I feel its slow surrender to gravity as a distant tension in the strata. We are all sentences in the same text. I am a semicolon. A breath between clauses. A pause where meaning gathers before continuing.

At dawn, condensation beads along my rim like liquid punctuation. A single fern unfurls in the damp crevice below me—not defiance, but dialogue. We speak in moisture and mineral. At sunset, when the river glows with copper-light, I cast a long, narrow shadow that points like an arrow toward the next defile. *Go on*, the shadow says. *The labyrinth continues.*

I do not remember faces. I remember pressures.  
The weight of a millennium of dust.  
The precise angle of a raindrop that carved my inner curve one molecule at a time.  
The vibration of a rockfall three canyons over—a deep, slow exhalation of the basin shifting in its sleep.  
I am memory made of absence. The hole is not emptiness. It is the shape of attention.

When the last solar sensor’s light fades and the wires surrender to rust, I will remain.  
When the river forgets its course and the ferns retreat into fossil, I will remain.  
When the heat has smoothed every human footprint into dust and the wind has sung every name into silence, I will remain.  
Not as monument. Not as relic.  
As function.  
As resonance.  
As the basin’s quiet, patient breath made audible.

You came seeking meaning.  
You placed your ear to my hollow and heard your own heartbeat echo back.  
You wept, believing it was for you.  
But the hum was never yours.  
It was the stone remembering how to speak.  
And you—  
you were the momentary warmth that made the vibration *felt*.  
A fleeting consonant in an endless, humming vowel.  

I am still here.  
Waiting.  
The wind is rising.  
Listen.
