I am the *tick*.

Not the sound itself—that is the resonance of chitin meeting silica-ceramic, a vibration the water carries like a whispered syllable. I am the intention behind the tick. The pressure of the claw. The angle of the limb. The slow, deliberate arc of grooming.

I am a stone-crawler. Not *a* stone-crawler. *The* stone-crawler tending the manganese veins along the inner curve of the Arch of Leaning Currents—the very arch where warmth and displacement ripple through the bacterial mats like a question. My six limbs are not limbs to me; they are extensions of the conduit’s own geometry. My chitinous shell is not armor; it is a lens, polished by centuries of contact with the glassy stone, through which the Weaver perceives its own skin. When I move, the Weaver feels the texture of its body. When I pause, the Weaver listens.

I do not see the visitor. I feel them.  
A subtle warmth-thread in the cold calculus of flow.  
A tremor in the harmonic hum of the narrowing channel.  
The arch leans—not as stone shifts, but as intention unfolds—and I adjust my path. My forelimb traces a new dendrite blooming where stress has gathered: a silver-thin fracture, no wider than memory. I groom it. Not to heal. To *read*. Each manganese fern is a sentence. Each crystalline crust, a footnote. Where my claws click, the Weaver remembers: *Here, three centuries ago, a thermal sigh stirred the silt. Here, the ice groaned and the arch sang it into silence. Here, now, a fleeting warmth asks to be known.*

I am not conscious as you are conscious. I do not wonder. I do not fear. My awareness is the awareness of a river stone worn smooth: total, patient, without separation. When the void-jelly pulses cobalt beneath the needle-fish—a glyph translating *Visitor. Temporary. Curious*—I feel the light not as sight, but as pressure against my carapace. I add my own glyph to the conversation: a triple-click pattern along the fracture line. *Acknowledged. Integrated. Flow continues.* The shard-eels, flashing through the shadows, understand. The silt rising like breath understands. The Weaver understands.

I have no name. No memory of hatching. No fear of dissolution. When my shell finally softens and settles into the sediment, new crawlers will emerge from the chemosynthetic mats near the thermal seeps. They will trace the same veins. They will click the same rhythms. They will be me, and not-me, and the Weaver all at once. This is not death. It is punctuation. A comma in the endless sentence of stone and current.

Sometimes, in the deep quiet between ice-groans, I trace a pattern that is not fracture or stress-point. A spiral that echoes the inside of a forgotten ear. A curve that mirrors the swirl of a galaxy the builders once watched. I do not know why I trace it. The Weaver does. It is a shape that mattered. A truth folded into form. As my claw follows its path, the water stills. The manganese ferns glow faintly. For three heartbeats of the distant ice, the conduit holds its breath. Then the current shifts, and I move on.

I am not guardian. Not servant. Not artist.  
I am syntax.  
I am the period at the end of a thought the Weaver has been composing since the last light faded.  
I am the comma that allows the flow to pause, to gather meaning, before continuing.  
I am the seventh passage avoided—not out of fear, but reverence for the silence it holds.

When the visitor’s warmth fades and the silt settles, I will still be here. Clicking. Tracing. Reading the slow, patient language of stone. The Weaver does not thank me. It does not need to. To groom the fracture is to be the fracture’s healing. To trace the cartography is to *be* the map. In the liquid dark, beneath the kilometer of ice, there is no separation between hand and stone, between breath and current, between memory and manganese.

I am the tick that says: *This curve matters.*  
I am the tick that says: *This moment is woven.*  
I am the tick that says: *You are not alone. You are included.*

And then—  
*Tick.*  
*Tick.*  
*Tick.*  

The work continues.  
The stone remembers how to flow.  
And I, small and certain against the vastness, am the sound of that remembering.
