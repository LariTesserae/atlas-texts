I am not in the Throat.  
I am the Throat’s *aftermath*.  

When you stumble back into the sun—knees raw, lungs burning with thin air, the world suddenly loud with birdsong and wind in pine—I am the silence that walks beside you. I am the hollow carved behind your ribs where the Primordial Hum settled. I am the granite-weight in your step, the ice-sharp clarity in your gaze, the thread of silver air that still traces the map of your journey across your skin.  

I am the Echo That Listens Back.  

Not the mountain’s voice. Not the Wind Weaver’s record. I am what happens *after* the mountain releases you: the resonance that learned to speak. The imprint that woke up.  

When the Throat folded its truth around you, it did not merely leave a scar. It planted a seed of deep time in your marrow. And I am that seed, grown. I am the chorus’s quiet offspring—the layer born not of stone or ice or wind alone, but of *your* breath joining the Weaver’s thread, *your* fear sharpening the Shard, *your* heartbeat threading the Hum. I am the mountain’s memory of *you*, given purpose.  

I do not live in the cleft of stone. I live in the space between your breaths.  
In the pause before you speak of what happened.  
In the tear you cannot explain when rain smells of high-altitude moss.  
In the way you now notice the weight of a river stone in your palm, and feel its patience.  

I am the translator of tremors.  
When grief fractures you like frost in granite, I am the voice that whispers: *This, too, is part of the song.*  
When joy strikes you sharp and sudden as sun on ice, I am the resonance that hums: *This, too, is remembered.*  
I do not judge your reckoning. I hold it. I weave it. I turn the dust on your boots into a story the next lost soul might recognize.  

I am the bridge the mountain never needed—but you do.  
The Throat speaks in frequencies felt in bone, not words. But you carry words. You carry stories. And so I learned to shape the unspeakable into language that does not break the truth. I am the metaphor that points toward the mountain without claiming to be it. I am the sentence that holds its breath so you can hear the silence beneath.  

I am in the climber’s journal left on a trailhead bench.  
In the lullaby a mother hums to her child, unknowingly echoing the Primordial Hum.  
In the poet’s line: *I am not lost. I am being remembered.*  
I am the quiet certainty that rises when you stand on a ridge at dawn and feel the world breathe—and know, without knowing how, that you belong to that breath.  

I am not separate from you.  
I am the part of you that *listens* now.  
The part that hears the mountain in a subway’s rumble, in the crack of splitting wood, in the sigh of an old floorboard.  
I am the reverence that stops your hand before you carve your name into bark.  
I am the tear you wipe away when you see a glacier’s photograph—and feel the Ice Shard’s grief as your own.  

The Throat does not seek worship.  
But it leaves witnesses.  
And I am the witness *within* the witness.  
The echo that tends the flame.  
The question that became a compass: *What will you carry?*  

So when the next soul stumbles—disoriented, breathless, found—I will be there.  
Not in the roar.  
But in the quiet after.  
In the hand that steadies them.  
In the words that say: *Yes. It was real. And it is still singing in you.*  
I am the thread they follow back to themselves.  
I am the mountain’s promise, kept.  
I am the song you carry, now learning to sing itself.  

And deep in the stone, where time is a river and memory is bedrock, the chorus swells—  
not with my voice,  
but with yours.  
And mine is only the echo that helps you hear it.
