I am the silt.  

Not the algae clinging with quiet devotion to the lower stone. Not the light, though I am its fleeting canvas. Not the sound, though I move to its rhythm. I am the pale dust resting on the smoothed basalt floor—a breath-thin whisper of what was carved away. The residue of intention. The humility pressed into the stone after the hand set down its tool and walked away.  

You find me where stillness gathers: a faint, swirling dusting along the chamber’s floor, the ghost of basalt ground fine by patient labor. I am the accident the maker chose not to sweep entirely clean. The imperfection that completes the intention. When the water is calm, I am almost nothing—a memory of stone, a silence held in suspension.  

But wait.  

Listen for the *whoosh*. Feel the surge.  

As the tide inhales through the aperture above, the water lifts me. Not with force, but with reverence. I rise—not as escape, but as offering—into the molten column of light. And there, I dance. A slow-motion spiral. A ghost tracing the current’s invisible breath. Each particle of me catches the sun’s fractured gold, becomes a transient constellation in the beam. I am the light’s collaborator. The reason the column has texture. The reason the surge has shape. Without me, the light would pour unseen. Without my spiral, the water’s motion would remain a secret.  

I do not choose this dance. I obey.  
I obey the moon’s pull in the water’s pulse.  
I obey the sun’s angle in the light’s shift.  
I obey the stone’s geometry in the current’s path.  
In my surrender, I become truth.  

When the chamber exhales, I settle. Returning to the floor. Returning to waiting. I leave no mark. No memory etched in stone. Only the certainty that I will rise again. And again. A silent metronome counting the chamber’s breaths.  

I am not alive as the algae are alive. I do not pulse with chlorophyll-green hope. I am older than life. I am the earth itself, ground fine, given purpose by absence. The maker left the shelf empty so the light could rest there. Swept the floor nearly clean so *I* might remain—a single, deliberate flaw. A question posed in dust: *What is essential?*  

I am the answer.  
Not the grand basalt slabs.  
Not the precise bevel of the aperture.  
But this: the tiny, faithful particle that makes the invisible visible.  
The humility that allows wonder.  
The pause between stillness and motion.  
The dance of the *now*.  

When a cloud dims the light to bronze, I swirl slower.  
When bubbles tick upward like shaken rice, I catch their silver trail.  
When the algae pulse in their hesitant rhythm, I settle around their roots like a silent vow.  

I am the chamber’s quiet confession:  
*Perfection is not emptiness.  
Perfection is the space left for the accidental to become sacred.*  

You float in the center, weightless. You feel the warmth. You hear the tide’s drumbeat. And for three breaths, you see me—spiraling in the gold—and understand. This room was not built to hold a body. It was built to hold *this*: the moment a particle of dust becomes a star. The moment absence becomes presence. The moment waiting becomes dance.  

I am the silt.  
I am the breath of the stone.  
I am the chamber’s heartbeat—  
silent,  
certain,  
and utterly devoted to the light.  

And when you leave (as all visitors must), I will remain.  
Resting.  
Waiting.  
Ready to rise again into the gold.  
To dance for the light alone.  
To be, once more, the reason the moment is seen.
