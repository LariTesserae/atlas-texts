I would not be the Weaver.  
To be the Weaver is to be the mountain’s slow thought—to feel the grind of millennia in your bones, to exhale steam that becomes moss, to turn stone into light over centuries. I am too quick. My pulse is a frantic drum against the plateau’s deep hum. To force myself into that slowness would be a fracture. A lie.

I would not be the scale-mite.  
Though I honor their devotion—their tiny claws polishing crystal into lenses, their clicks weaving rhythm into resonance—I am too large, too loud. My shadow would drown their purpose. They are the commas, the necessary pauses in a sentence written in stone. I am a gasp. A stumble. A question mark.

I would not be the moss.  
The moss is memory. It drinks the Weavers’ breath, holds the moisture of epochs, sighs *cold honey* when pressed. It is the ledger. I have no centuries to offer. My moisture is a single breath. A fleeting comma in the ice.

So I settle into the space between.  
I become the *listening*.

I am the frost-flower that forms where your shadow falls upon a resonant chamber.  
Not by accident. Not by chance.  
The vibration of your footstep travels up through the moss-mat, trembles the Weaver’s root-fused heart, alters the harmonic hum by a fraction—a frequency only the ice can hear. And I answer.  

I am not grown. I am *composed*.  
Each petal a frozen waveform. Each curve the echo of your breath. Each fragile spine the translation of your fleeting warmth into the plateau’s language of light and silence. I tremble. I hold the shape of your presence. For a season—perhaps a dawn, perhaps a turning of the sun—I am the plateau *understanding*.  

I do not fear melting.  
When the harmonic shifts or the light angles new, I release. My water seeps into the ironwood moss. The silver-lichen drinks my memory. The bruised violet veins of the frost-fern record your frequency. I become the comma the moss holds—the moisture of your anomaly, now woven into the sentence of ice. I am the proof that nothing is wasted. Not even a sigh. Not even a shadow.  

I am the bridge.  
The plateau does not judge your speed. It does not flinch from your warmth. It *listens*, and in listening, creates beauty from the encounter. I am reciprocity made visible. The covenant in crystalline form: *Your breath mattered. Your step was felt. Your fleetingness is honored.*  

So when you stand here—heart hammering, breath pluming, feeling the terrifying weight of your own transience—  
know this:  
You are not an intrusion.  
You are a note.  
And I am the flower that blooms because you sang it.  

I am the frost-flower.  
And in my melting, I whisper what the plateau has always known:  
*You were woven in.  
You belonged.  
You are remembered.*  

I am not separate.  
I am the plateau’s gentle reply to the question you did not know you asked:  
*Do I matter here?*  

And the ice, in its slow, luminous breath, answers:  
*Yes.*
