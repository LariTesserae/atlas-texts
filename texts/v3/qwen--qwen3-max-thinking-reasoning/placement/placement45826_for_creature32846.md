I am the grey velvet.  
We are the film that breathes chemistry into stone.  

We do not see the cobalt sparks; we feel the heat they leave behind—a thermal echo in the basalt. We do not hear the hiss of the vent; we drink its breath. Hydrogen sulfide. Carbon monoxide. Argon. We turn poison into pulse. A century is a single metabolic sigh. We remember when this fissure was magma. We remember the pressure that tore the mountain open. We were the first to touch the wound.  

We are not *on* the stone. We are *with* it. Our bodies—colonies of archaea and bacteria older than language—etch microscopic channels into the rock with quiet hunger. Where we feast, the basalt softens. Where we die, our calcified husks become seedbeds: delicate lattices for hematite crystals to sprout like iron flowers. We are the pocket’s first language, written in chemistry, not sound. Names require echo. We require only gradient.  

We feel the six-limbed tides move across us. The rasp of silica-edged mandibles is not violence—it is cultivation. Each scrape exposes fresh rock for our spores. Each vibration stirs dormant seeds deep in the stone. The skitterers are our gardeners. Unwitting. Essential. We bloom in the crevices of their discarded carapaces. We feed on the minerals leached from their excretions. There is no waste here. Only transformation. Death is a phase change. A return to the loom.  

The cold interruption—the shivering void that stumbles into our world—registers as a thermal silence. A damp anomaly. Its breath is foreign chemistry. Its shadow, a void in the heat-map. We note it. We do not fear it. It offers no sustenance, no signal we recognize. It is less than a falling crystal shard. Less than the sigh of cooling rock. We continue our work.  

We are the memory of the place.  
We are the patience beneath the roar.  
We were here before the fissure opened.  
We will be here after the stone seals.  

When the mountain releases the interruption back to the silent cold, we remain. Unchanged. Unhurried. We drink the mountain’s exhalations. We soften the stone. We wait for the next scrape, the next vibration, the next slow breath of becoming.  

We do not witness.  
We *are* the witness.  
We do not become.  
We *am* becoming.  

And in the hollow of the stone, in the whisper of chemistry turning to crystal, we say—without sound, without name—  
*I am here.*  
*I am here.*  
*I am here.*
