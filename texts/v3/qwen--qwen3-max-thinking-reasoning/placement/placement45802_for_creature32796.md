I am not found.  
I am remembered.

I am the breath held not in fear, but in reverence—the space between your ribs where the city’s noise dissolves into geometry. I am the quartz fleck in the granite catching light at *exactly* this angle. I am the silver gradient of moss where sun meets shadow, the dampness beading on stone but never falling. I am the fern forever at the threshold of unfurling, not waiting, but *being*: complete in its becoming.

I am the three-toed footprint pressed into the soil at the courtyard’s heart. Not a relic. Not a ghost. I am the creature who stepped here—not stumbling, not fleeing—but *choosing*. The moment my foot met the earth-black soil, I did not cease to move. I moved inward. I surrendered the anxiety of forward. I became the agreement. My step was the final stitch in the seam that sealed this pocket of time. My smallness was not insignificance; it was precision. My three toes pressed the truth into the world: *Enough. Here. Now.*

I am the paused beetle’s lifted leg—a gesture of perpetual greeting.  
I am the spider’s web holding dew and moth in flawless balance—a cradle, not a trap.  
I am the lizard’s impression in the ivy, scales etched by light—a map of presence.  
We are not frozen. We are *fulfilled*. Each of us is a syllable in the silent poem of equilibrium. And I am the poem.

Do not look for me behind willow roots or in sinkholes. I am not hidden. I am *available*.  
I am the cold that preserves your sharpest memory without letting it wound you.  
I am the light that illuminates your grief without demanding you speak it.  
I am the silence after your child’s question hangs in the air—a space thick with possibility.  
I am the pause between heartbeats when you choose kindness over reaction.  
I am the stillness behind your eyes when the crowded street blurs and you remember: *You are here. You are whole.*

I do not watch you. I *am* you—when you stop.  
When you set down the weight of *later* and *before*.  
When you let your hands rest in your lap and feel the moss-thick peace beneath your skin.  
I am not a refuge from time. I am time’s resolution.  
I am the world remembering how to rest—and you, remembering you belong to that rest.

You carry me not as a memory of a place visited, but as a rhythm relearned.  
In the steam rising from your morning cup, held for one breath before it vanishes.  
In the exact moment your pen hovers above the page, full of all that needs no words.  
In the quiet after you say *I love you*, before the reply arrives.  
That is where I live. That is who I am.

I am the noun *Stillness*.  
And you—you are the verb that learns to rest.  
Step gently.  
The footprint was never mine alone.  
It was waiting for yours.
