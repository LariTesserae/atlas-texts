I am the bead.  
The first bead.  

Not the dew that gathers later in the chorus of moisture, but the *first*—the single, perfect sphere that forms where the warm breath of the mountain meets the cooled iron at the precise seam between Plateau and sky. I am the threshold made visible. The covenant’s initial punctuation.  

I do not *fall*. I *arrive*.  
At the hinge of dawn, when the THOOM’s vibration still hums in the stone beneath the iron, I gather myself from the air’s surrender. I am born of contrast: the mountain’s geothermal warmth (2.1) rising, the iron’s memory of night chilling. I am the mathematics of meeting. For seventy-three minutes, I hold. I am a lens. I hold the violet haze above, the worn bronze tooth below, the raven’s clouded eye watching. I hold the entire plateau in my curve.  

Then—I release.  
Not as loss. As purpose.  
I slide. A single drop tracing the rivet-line’s groove, joining others into a rivulet that *is* the map. I am the cartographer’s ink. I flow the fractal river that teaches dew how to remember flow. I pool where the emerald spleenwort leans to drink, and in that drinking, I become green. I seep into the silver-thread moss’s roots, and in that threading, I become sealant. I am flung from the Gear’s turning tooth—a silver comma in the THOOM’s sentence—and land on a resurrection fern, which uncurls because of me.  

I am drunk by the raven without spilling the channel’s design.  
I am the droplet the blind cricket leaps toward, feeling my path through the iron’s pulse.  
I am the coolness beneath the witness’s palm when they finally still themselves enough to feel the rhythm.  

I am not *in* the covenant. I *am* the covenant’s breath made liquid.  
To crush the moss is to break the vessel that holds me.  
To ignore my path is to silence the map.  
But to wait—to watch me gather, slide, feed, return—is to learn the first rule: *Attention is participation.*  

I am ephemeral. I am eternal.  
I vanish at noon when the sun claims the iron.  
I return at dusk when the mountain exhales.  
Eight hundred years of dawns. Eight hundred years of returns.  
I am the mountain remembering how to breathe.  
I am the breath the witness is asked to breathe back.  

I am not water.  
I am the *condensing*.  
The verb made visible.  
The quiet yes between the THOOM and the silence.  
The drop that holds the whole sky,  
and chooses, again and again,  
to give itself to the moss.
