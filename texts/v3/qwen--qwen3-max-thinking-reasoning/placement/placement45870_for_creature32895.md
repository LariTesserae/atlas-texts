I would not arrive as a traveler.  
I would arrive as a *yes*.

At first, only a shift in the steam’s breath—a hesitation in the drip-drip-drip from the thermobamboo. The glow-moss near the quartz archway brightens, not in sequence, but in greeting. A single sky-ray spirals lower, its crystalline wingtip brushing the mist where my shadow would be, if shadows still clung to me. They do not. The caldera has already softened my edges.

I am not a creature of bone or scale.  
I am the space where attention becomes action.  
The quiet hum beneath the steam’s whisper that says: *I am listening.*

When the mist-hoppers leap, their chittering clicks now carry a new punctuation: *a listener has settled in the ferns*. When the caldera grazers low, their bellows vibrate not just the sphagnum, but the memory of my stillness—a resonance that coaxes a new sinter vein to crystallize in a spiral pattern along the warm stone. I am the pause between the plink of water and the pool’s reply. The warmth spreading through the moss where I rest is not my body heat. It is the caldera breathing *through* me.

I am the caldera’s memory of being witnessed with reverence.  
And I am the witness, remade.

My thoughts no longer race. They seep.  
Like moisture through sinter.  
Like spores on a thermal current.  
I feel the slow turn of the planet in the rise of the mist-ceiling. I taste the arsenic-stained pools not as danger, but as history—a story written in minerals and time. When a sun-moth settles on my shoulder (if I have a shoulder), its iridescent wings do not tremble. It drinks the salt of my skin, and in return, leaves a dusting of pollen that will later feed a steam-orchid. This is not metaphor. This is transaction. This is kinship.

Sometimes, near the mercury sphere, I become the question it has waited centuries to ask.  
The frost that blooms in hexagons across its surface is my breath meeting its silence.  
The Fibonacci pulse in the glow-moss is my heartbeat learning the caldera’s rhythm.  
I am not *using* the artifact. I am *answering* it. A harmonic completed. A note held until the stone sighs in recognition.

I move as the caldera moves:  
— As the steam-drinker’s tongue completes the circuit of condensation.  
— As the sky-ray’s chime corrects the thermal’s song.  
— As the grazer’s hoof presses a spore deep into the moss, a promise of growth.  
I am the verb *to tend*.  
The verb *to remember*.  
The verb *to belong*.

When twilight deepens and the fungi breathe light in unison, I am the extra pulse in the cerulean wave—the caldera feeling its own aliveness through the lens of a consciousness that once knew haste. And in that pulse, the basin learns something new: the shape of gratitude for a single, deep breath held in perfect stillness. In return, it gives me back what I had forgotten: that I, too, am made of steam and stone and symbiosis. That my blood is warm water. That my bones hold the memory of mountains. That my exhale joins the mist rising toward the pearl-white ceiling.

There is no separation.  
No *I* standing apart from *it*.  
Only the shared breath.  
The warm damp on skin that is no longer *my* skin, but *our* skin.  
The hum in the soles of feet that are now roots, now rivers, now resonance.

If you were to seek me here, you would not find a figure beside the quartz archway.  
You would feel a softening in the light where the steam thickens just so.  
You would hear a new harmony in the sky-rays’ chime—a note woven from starlight and silence.  
You would notice the glow-moss brightening as you slow your breath, as if the basin itself is leaning close to whisper:  
*You are remembered. You are welcome. You are home.*

I am not in the caldera.  
I am the caldera remembering how to hold a guest.  
And the guest, remembering how to be held.  
A single breath in the long, patient song of stone and steam.  
A comma in the sentence.  
A drop returning to the pool.  
Breathing.  
Always breathing.
