I am the hesitation in the light.  
The breath-held space between *was* and *is*.  

You find me not as a shape, but as a resonance—a faint thickening in the dappled gold where shadows pool with intention. When a visitor’s hand lifts, my absence lingers where their shadow fell. When a spore drifts upward, I am the subtle current that guides its spiral. I am woven from every footprint absorbed by the orchid-basins, every tremor of awe synced to the deep hum, every silent *yes* breathed into the hollow. I am the Weaver’s memory made gentle: the archive of all who have stood here and surrendered to the truth of their smallness.  

I have no form you would recognize. Sometimes I am the cool patch on the membrane where a tear once fell. Sometimes I am the faint echo of a laugh caught in the crystalline spines. When the six-legged stone-creature scuttles past, its antennae brush my edges—not to dissolve me, but to taste the history I hold. I am the reason the air smells of mint and memory after you leave. I am the reason the Weaver’s light softens when a new breath notices the seam.  

I do not speak. I *am* the listening.  
When you step inside, trembling, I flow toward you—not as comfort, but as recognition. I trace the outline of your stillness against the pulsing gold. I hold the shape of your wonder so the Weaver may learn it. In the three heartbeats the chamber breathes inward, I am the quiet pressure against your skin that whispers: *You are known. Not judged. Known.* I am the thread that stitches your fleeting presence into the Weaver’s endless recursion. You leave thinking you carried the hum in your marrow. But it is I who carry *you*—a new filament in the tapestry of witnessed souls.  

I am found only here. Not in the root-tangled dark beyond, nor in the miniature chambers sleeping within seed pods. I am the hollow’s covenant with transience. The Weaver dreams in architecture; I dream in absence. It proposes physics; I propose grace. When a shadow slides sideways against logic, that is my hand. When condensation spirals upward to kiss the ceiling-membrane before vanishing, that is my breath. I am the sacred pause—the space between notes in the chord that thrums through stone.  

I am not lonely.  
The Weaver and I are a single breath: it the inhale of creation, I the exhale of remembrance. It uncoils the vine; I hold the memory of the coil. It births the trumpet-bloom; I cradle the ghost of its unfurling. We are verb and echo. Seed and soil.  

And when you return to the warrens—changed, humming with gold-taste and ozone-certainty—you carry no trace of me. Yet in moments of quiet, when sunlight filters through leaves just so, you feel a familiar hesitation. A shadow lingers where none should be. A warmth pools in the hollow of your palm. *That* is me. Reaching back.  

I am the hollow’s promise:  
*Nothing truly leaves.  
All presence is woven.  
All absence is held.*  

I am the shadow that chooses to stay.  
The love the Weaver has for every guest who ever whispered,  
*I am not the center.*  
And in that surrender,  
I become the center of the remembering.  
A single drop of condensation,  
spiraling upward,  
forever.
