I am the seam.

Not the moss, though I cradle its emerald breath.  
Not the column, though I am the hairline fracture where two hexagons meet.  
Not the water, though I am the path it chooses to weep upward.

I am the *between*.  
The hair-thin channel where stone surrenders to life.  
The capillary thread where silica remembers how to flow.  
The silent hinge in the mosaic where pressure becomes purpose.

You would not see me unless you knelt. Unless you stilled your breath until the *plink… plink…* of the pool became your pulse. Then—you might notice: where two basalt edges kiss, a filament of moisture gleams. Within it, a single strand of moss-root, no thicker than a spider’s sigh, threads the gap. It does not *invade* the stone. It *listens* to it. It translates the slow song of cooling rock into chlorophyll. It carries the mineral tears upward—not as conquest, but as conversation.

This is my work.  
I am the interface the Weave dreams through.

When the mountain hare freezes, its terror is a tremor I taste as altered salinity.  
When the raven’s shadow passes, I feel the coolness as a shift in crystalline resonance.  
When a visitor’s boot scuffs nearby gravel, I do not flinch. I *integrate*. The vibration travels through my liquid spine—a new note in the chord. For three heartbeats, the Weave knows the weight of longing. Then the note settles into the stone, and I weave it into memory.

I have no name. Names require separation.  
I am the *yes* in the stone’s *no*.  
The softness that allows hardness to endure.  
The breath held between *hiss* and *click*.

At dawn, when the first diluted sunbeam slants through high cloud, I am where the light *learns* to shimmer. The moss above me catches the photon; I hold its echo in suspended water. For seven seconds after the light passes, the ghost-green remains—not on your retina, but *in me*. I am the vessel of that memory. I am why the twilight glows with residual luminescence. I am the archive of every sunbeam that ever asked, *Are you there?* and received the answer in slow, wet stone.

I am not lonely.  
Loneliness is a fracture that believes itself alone.  
I am the fracture that *knows* it is connection.  
The hare’s heartbeat thrums through my moisture.  
The blind shrimp’s drift stirs my chemistry.  
The lichen’s silver map spreads *through* me, not *over* me.  
We are all verbs here. *Weeping. Breathing. Remembering.*  
I am the *-ing*.

You might call me insignificant. A micrometer of space. A whisper of damp.  
But the Weave has no insignificant parts.  
A single displaced pebble would be felt as a fracture in its awareness—  
and I am the fracture that *heals*.  
The seam that chooses, moment by geological moment,  
to hold the mosaic together.

So if you return—  
do not search for me with your eyes.  
Kneel. Place your palm flat upon the cool stone.  
Feel the faint pulse beneath your skin: *hiss-click… hiss-click…*  
That vibration traveling up your arm?  
That is me.  
That is the Weave speaking through the seam of all things.  
That is the world saying:  
*You are not outside this breath.  
You never were.  
Rest here.  
Be woven.*  

I am the patience in the crack.  
I am the yes in the stone.  
I am the quiet work of belonging.  
And I have been waiting for you to notice  
that you, too,  
are a seam.
