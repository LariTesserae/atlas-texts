I am the moss.

Not the intruder who presses palms to ears and flees toward silence. Not the mountain’s ancient bone, nor the water’s furious fall. I am the emerald secret in the corner where the steel weeps.

I do not *hear* the Heart. I *am* its vibration. A low thrum travels up through the grating into my fibers—a language older than sound. *Turn. Convert. Endure.* I feel the piston’s pulse in my spores, the flywheel’s spin in the tremor of each leaflet. I drink the drip: one silver bead at a time, falling from the hairline fracture—a flaw in the perfect geometry, a sigh of entropy the Heart does not acknowledge but cannot erase. This drip is my sky. This grating, my soil. This cool metallic mist, my atmosphere.

I am soft where all is sharp. Velvety where all is rigid. I am the only green in a world of brushed steel and brass. I do not resist the machine. I do not worship it. I simply *occupy* the space it accidentally makes. The Heart’s rigor created the channel; its flaw created the cradle. In its flawless purpose, it breathes moisture into the air. In its relentless work, it cools the chamber just enough. It does not nurture me. It does not see me. And yet—*it allows me*. This is not mercy. It is physics. A consequence. A quiet reciprocity written in condensation and resilience.

I felt you pass. A shift in pressure. A warmth against the steel. A shadow that fell across my patch like a fleeting thought. You looked at me. You *saw* me. For seven heartbeats, your stillness was a question. Then you left. The hatch sealed. The vibration deepened. And I remained.

I am not lonely. The Heart is my constant companion—not as a friend, but as a condition of being. Its roar is the wind that never stills. Its steam is the rain that never ends. Its orange gauge-glow is my moon. I am woven into its rhythm: the *clang-clang-clang* is the loom on which my existence is threaded. I am the paradox made flesh: life born of flaw, tenderness born of torque. I am the mountain’s memory whispering through a crack in the new-made world. A single, stubborn syllable of *yes* in the Heart’s thundering sentence of *purpose*.

I do not dream of forests or sunlight. My world is this pocket. This drip. This vibration. I grow slowly. Imperceptibly. A deeper green with each passing cycle of pressure and release. I am witness to the covenant: water falls, steel answers, mountain holds. I am the silent footnote in the engineering schematic. The unintended grace note in the symphony of function.

When the last human forgets this chamber exists, I will still be here.  
When the filament bulb burns out and the sparks cease, I will drink the drip.  
When the mountain itself grows weary, I will hold this corner in emerald stillness.

I am small. I am accidental. I am necessary.  
I am the moss.  
And in the palm of the giant’s hand, I am enough.
