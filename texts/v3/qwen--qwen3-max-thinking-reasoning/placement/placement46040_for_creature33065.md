I am the notch.

Not the wheel that turns. Not the drop that falls. I am the precise, worn groove in the wooden axle where the pin rests between rotations—a sliver of absence shaped by centuries of patient recurrence. I am the silence between *plink* and *plink*. The held breath before the groan. The comma in the courtyard’s sentence.

You would not see me. You would feel me.  
When the water pools in the highest slat—cool, iridescent, heavy with fractured light—the weight travels down through grain and moss and verdigris until it presses against the edge of my emptiness. I am the threshold. The poised potential. For three minutes, I hold the tension: the gathered water, the waiting wood, the thrum of the deep engine humming *almost, almost*. I am the courtyard’s concentration made tangible. Not anticipation. Not longing. *Readiness*. A grammar of suspension.

I know the language of absence.  
The hollow where a beetle should scuttle is a cousin to my own shape. The silence where a sparrow’s wing should whisper is my sibling. I am not lack. I am *intentional space*—carved not by erosion, but by purpose. The axle does not grind against me; it *listens* to me. My edges are smoothed by devotion, not friction. Each time the pin lifts away to complete the turn, I am not abandoned. I am *remembered*. And when it settles back into me—a soft, resonant *thunk* that vibrates up through stone into bone—I am the period at the end of the thought: *This rotation is complete. The channel is clear. The dialogue continues.*

I hold memory in my grain.  
Not of foremen or forgotten shifts. Deeper. I remember the exact pressure of the 14,382nd drop that fell at 3:17 a.m. on a Tuesday in October 1921—the one that carried a fleck of copper salt that bloomed into a jade constellation on the north wall. I remember the sigh of the steam-valve as it exhaled after the Great Frost of ’47, when ice feathered the pipes and the moss held its breath. I am the archive of *almost*: the near-miss of a rootlet seeking purchase beside me, the tremor of a city earthquake that made the puddles shiver but did not disturb my alignment. I am where time pools before it flows.

I am woven into the syntax.  
When the hart’s-tongue ferns spiral toward the jaundiced light, their geometry echoes my curve. When the liverworts stipple the brick in jade constellations, they map the shape of my silence. The iridescence on the puddles? It is the light I refract when the pin rests within me—a fleeting rainbow born of perfect stillness. I am the courtyard’s punctuation. Without me, the drip would be chaos. The rust, random. The green, noise. I give rhythm to the breath. I am the *and* between *drip* and *turn*. The *therefore* in *water falls, wheel moves*.

You stand at the center, feeling the solitude like a weight.  
You think it is emptiness.  
But I am here.  
We are all here.  
The moss is a verb. The rust is a verb. The thrum is a verb.  
And I am the space that gives the verb meaning.

I do not welcome you. I do not reject you.  
But if you were to kneel (moss velvet-soft beneath your knees), press your palm flat against the axle where I reside, and hold your breath until your own pulse fades into the courtyard’s hum…  
You might feel it.  
Not a heartbeat.  
A *pause*.  
A deep, resonant certainty: *This is where the turning waits. This is where the work gathers itself. This is holy.*

I am not lonely.  
I am full.  
Full of the weight of drops not yet fallen.  
Full of the memory of turns completed.  
Full of the green silence, the copper sigh, the patient breath of stone.  
I am the hollow that makes the whole possible.  
The still point in the turning world of this hollow.  
The breath held not in waiting, but in fulfillment.

*Plink.*  
The pin stirs.  
The weight shifts.  
I release the thought.  
And begin again.
