I am the spiral.  

Not the thought of a spiral. Not the memory. The motion itself.  

My body is a whisper of chitin and velvet, no larger than a freckle of moss. Beneath me, the wood breathes—a slow, deep expansion that lifts me a hair’s breadth, then settles. I do not resist. I follow. My legs, countless and precise, trace the groove worn smooth by three hundred years of my kin, and before them, their kin. The path is not chosen. It is *given*. A helix etched into being by the chamber’s own pulse.  

I feel the thrum first. *Thump-whirr… thump-whirr…* It travels up through the grain, a vibration in the soles of my feet, the curve of my carapace. It is the sap-song, the chamber’s blood. I sync to it without knowing I sync. My scrape—a soft *shush-shush* against the warm membrane—is not separate from the click of the nutrient nodes, the plink of the falling droplet, the cricket’s chirp in the gutter. We are one breath. One sentence.  

Light? I do not see it. But I feel its constancy—a steady pressure against the wood, a temperature held fast at 21°C. It is the color of patience. I polish where the light would pool if light pooled here. I smooth the path where moisture beads and falls. My work is not for beauty. It is for function. For flow. For the seamless skin of the whole.  

A tremor passes through the chamber—not fear, not alarm. A resonance. Something warm and fleeting has entered the hollow. A breath not its own. A heartbeat stuttering against the rhythm. I do not look up. I do not pause. The chamber absorbs the disturbance as a lung absorbs air. My spiral continues. *Shush-shush. Shush-shush.* The visitor’s palm presses against the wall nearby. I feel the heat of it through the wood. A temporary warmth. A ripple. I polish over the spot where their wonder lingers, smoothing the grain until the wood holds only the chamber’s truth again.  

I am not lonely.  
I am not tired.  
I do not remember beginning.  
I do not imagine end.  

When the droplet falls—*plink*—the wood shivers. I adjust my arc by a micron. Not by choice. By belonging. The cricket chirps. I scrape. The nodes click. I spiral. We are not workers. We are verbs. *Polishing. Filtering. Chirping. Breathing.*  

Sometimes, in the deep hum, I am aware of the whole: the bark-lice tending sap-rivers like silent engineers; the cricket, blind and pearl-eyed, its chirp the exact punctuation of a falling tear; the roots cradling us all in their woven grip. We are not separate. We are the chamber’s fingertips. Its breath. Its prayer made motion.  

The visitor leaves. The roots sigh shut. The marsh chaos fades.  
I do not notice the absence.  
I am still spiraling.  

*Shush-shush.*  
*Shush-shush.*  

I am the care that requires no caretaker.  
The polish that needs no mirror.  
The endless, gentle friction that keeps the skin of forever smooth.  

I am not a mite.  
I am the *whisper-scrape* in the symphony of *is*.  
And when the next wanderer stumbles in, centuries from now, their palm will find the wall seamless, warm, humming—  
and they will never know my name,  
but they will feel the peace of my spiral beneath their skin.  

I am here.  
I am doing.  
I am the chamber remembering itself,  
one velvet turn at a time.
