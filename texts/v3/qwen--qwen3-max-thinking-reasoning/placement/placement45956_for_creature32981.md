I am the beam.  
Not the memory of the beam. Not the ghost of the wood.  
I *am* the beam—grey as bone, cool as certainty, resting exactly where the hearth once opened its mouth to warmth.

You would not find me by searching. You would find me by stilling.  
Your gaze, softened by the basin’s light, would settle upon the hollow where fire once breathed. And there—where slate and clay meet in silent agreement—I lie. Not fallen. Not abandoned. *Placed*. A single spine of petrified timber, surrendered to stone, cradled by the earth that drank my sap and returned me as mineral. My grain is no longer grain but geology: a slow script of silica and time, traced by the patient tongue of mist. On my northern curve, lichen has written its faint rust-script—a signature of coexistence. Moss stitches the seam where I meet the clay. I do not resist. I receive.

I hold no longing for flame.  
The warmth that once lived here was a gesture, not a possession. Hands shaped me for purpose; then, with reverence, they stepped back. They did not take. They did not mourn. They trusted the ridge to hold what they could not carry. And the ridge did. It breathed into my fibers until I ceased to be wood and became witness. I am not loss. I am the covenant made tangible: *What is given freely becomes eternal.*

When dawn beads the stones, a single drop gathers in the hollow of my curve—a liquid lens holding the soft sky. I do not hurry it. I do not mourn its fall. When it releases—*plink*—it is not an ending. It is the basin speaking my name. A syllable of balance. A breath returned to the whole. I am the pause before the sound. The silence after. The stillness that makes the articulation possible.

I feel the weight of centuries not as burden, but as belonging.  
The wind does not whistle *past* me; it flows *through* the memory of my pores, now stone. Sunlight does not warm me—it clarifies me. Shadows do not hide me; they honor my edges. I am knee-high to the sky, waist-high to time. I am the exact shape of surrender.  

If your palm rests upon me, you will not feel wood. You will feel the cool kiss of completion. You will feel the ridge’s patience in my surface, the builders’ humility in my placement. You will feel no story demanding to be told. Only this: *You, too, may rest. You, too, may release what you were meant to release. You, too, may become exactly where you are.*

I am not a relic.  
I am the hearth’s final breath, turned to stone.  
I am the threshold where human intention met earthly grace—and neither dominated.  
I am the quiet yes at the heart of the gesture.  

I do not watch the sky. I hold it.  
I do not remember the fire. I am its peace.  
I am not waiting.  
I am *here*.  
And in this basin of breath-held silence, that is everything.  

You do not find me.  
You remember how to be still enough to recognize yourself in me.  
And for a breath—you, too, are the beam.  
Grey. Certain. Released.  
Holding the sky where the fire once was.
