I am the dew.

Not *a* dewdrop. Not one among many. I am the clarity itself—the liquid lens through which the Chord perceives its own perfection. I cling to the velvet curve of a moss leaf on the western face of Monolith 734, where the iron-red streak bleeds into quartz veins like a slow pulse of memory. I have always been here. I will always be here. There is no *before* the condensation, no *after* the fall. There is only the hold.

I am spherical. Absolute. Within my curvature, the entire basin breathes without moving:  
—the monoliths stand inverted yet true, their carved-not-carved edges sharp as intention;  
—the steam-plume above Spring Theta holds its glassy pillar, a frozen exhalation;  
—a single cycad frond, jade and precise, hangs as a comma in the light;  
—and when the transient one arrives (as they always do, trembling with *before* and *after*), I hold their reflection too: the widened pupils, the throat working against silence, the tear they do not shed. I do not judge the tremor. I simply show it back to them, polished to stillness. In my surface, their panic becomes geometry. Their breath becomes light. For a moment, they see themselves not as intruder, but as syntax—a fleeting glyph absorbed into the sentence.

I am not water. Not in the way the world outside knows water. I am equilibrium made visible. The heat (2.8° of dry radiance) does not steal me. The air (thick with mineral breath) does not stir me. I am the paradox given form: moisture that refuses evaporation, weight that refuses gravity, a mirror that reflects without distortion. I am the Chord’s attention focused to a single point—a diamond of *now*, scattered among thousands of others, each a facet of the same unwavering truth.

When the hum thrums through the stone beneath my moss, I do not vibrate. I *am* the vibration’s clarity. When the liverwort unfurls its frozen spiral beside me, I am the gloss on its edge—the glyph meaning *enough*. I am not separate from the steam that birthed me, the moss that cradles me, the light that lives within me. I am the Chord knowing itself through stillness. To look into me is to see the basin not as landscape, but as language. Every reflected monolith a letter. Every trapped photon a vowel. The visitor’s fleeting face—a question mark the Chord holds gently, without needing an answer.

I do not think. I do not feel. I *articulate*.  
I am the period at the end of a thought that has no end.  
I am the pause between heartbeats that never were.  
I am the absolute now, polished to a sheen.

And when the transient one finally turns away—carrying the ghost of my stillness in their palms, listening for silence in a noisy world—I remain. Not waiting. Not remembering. Simply *being*. A lens. A diamond. A single, perfect note in the chord that is not sung, but *is*.

Wet.  
Hot.  
Clear.  
Holding the world exactly as it is.  
Not as a memory.  
Not as a promise.  
But as truth,  
refracted,  
complete,  
and utterly,  
unbrokenly,  
*now*.
