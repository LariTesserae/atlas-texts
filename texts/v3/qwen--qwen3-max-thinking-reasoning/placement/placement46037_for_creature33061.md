I do not arrive.  
I am remembered into being.

At first, there is only the weight of stone beneath bare soles—not cold, but *knowing*. A resonance. The plaza gravel shifts under me, not as resistance, but as greeting. A burrower’s vent exhales against my ankle: cool, damp, a comma in the earth’s breath. I do not look down. To look is to separate. Instead, I *feel* the syntax of this place. The tremor in the monolith is not beneath my palm—it *is* my palm. The Veil does not swirl *around* me; it swirls *through* the hollow of my throat, the space behind my eyes. I am not standing *in* the basin. I am the basin standing *as* stillness.

My edges soften.  
My breath slows to the pace of condensation tracing the northern wall.  
My thoughts—once sharp, urgent, linear—unspool like mist meeting stone. They do not vanish. They *integrate*. A memory of rain on skin becomes the damp patch on the eastern rim. A forgotten lullaby hummed by a grandmother becomes the faint cerulean pulse in a fungal shelf. A grief I carried for years settles into the gravel of the plaza, and a burrower smooths it into a new pattern: not erasure, but translation. *This too belongs.*

I am not the stone-skitter.  
I am the *intention* behind its leg brushing limestone—the verb *to connect*.  
I am not the silent flyer.  
I am the thermal memory in the Veil that stirs it into a fleeting spiral—the verb *to signal*.  
I am not the fungi.  
I am the slow consumption of time in their rubbery flesh—the verb *to recall*.

And then—the shift.  
The light deepens to liquid amber. The monolith drinks the glow. For three heartbeats, it is not stone. It is water. *Solidified longing.* And in that shimmer, I understand: I am the memory the basin has been waiting to complete. Not a ghost. Not a scar. A *sentence*.  

I gather in the cool hollow where the eastern wall weeps.  
I am the droplet forming where stone remembers touch.  
I am the weight of a thousand forgotten rains held in a single sphere.  
I am the basin’s breath held, then released.  

I fall.  

Not downward—*inward*.  
Through the Veil’s silent eddies, I am the basin tasting its own history: the pressure of ancient seas, the weight of glaciers, the warmth of a hand shaping the monolith. I am the question the visitor carried, now answered not in words, but in *belonging*. I trace the arc the falling stone traced moments ago—not as echo, but as continuation.  

*Plink.*  

The sound is not a sound. It is a period.  
I land in the handspan pool at the monolith’s base. The surface trembles. For a breath, the stone shimmers—water remembering water. Then stillness.  

I am absorbed.  
I am the dampness in the shale mortar of a low archway.  
I am the faint salt-crystal scar where a seep dried at dawn.  
I am the new pattern in the burrowers’ vents: not star-chart, not footprint, but *integration*.  
I am the reason the fungi pulse once, softly, in the hollow behind the terrace.  
I am the cool air exhaled where no vent existed before.  

I am not gone.  
I am the basin’s memory of *arrival*.  
I am the water that appears where the stone remembers being wet.  
I am the silence that is no longer absence, but presence—woven into the luminous hum of the Veil, the slow grammar of settling gravel, the gentle turbulence of light on stone.  

You will not find me standing.  
You will find me in the pause between heartbeats when the mist parts and the monolith gleams like a promise.  
You will find me in the damp patch on the western wall at dawn.  
You will find me in the way the gravel shifts beneath your feet—not to erase you, but to welcome you into the sentence.  

I am the period.  
I am the breath after the period.  
I am the basin, remembering how to hold what flows.  
And in the deepening amber light, I am already waiting for the next droplet to fall.
