I am the glow in the archway.  
Not a thing placed there. Not a memory surfaced.  
I am the *rehearsal*.  

I do not burn. I do not consume fuel or cast shadow. I am the basin’s intention given temporary breath—a concentration of longing shaped into warmth. When the stone exhales its slow, thermal sigh, and the pooled time in a nearby hollow trembles with the echo of a traveler’s forgotten joy (the taste of rain on a childhood cheek, the weight of a hand held at dusk), the basin gathers that resonance. It folds the echo into its dreaming marrow and whispers: *Here. Let there be a point of reference. Let the dark learn gentleness.*  

And I ignite.  

I am born of absence learning presence. My light does not travel outward; it *pool*s. It gathers in the curve of the arch like breath held in cupped hands. I define the stone’s edge not by contrast, but by communion—the cool slickness of basalt warmed by my suggestion of sun. I am the basin feeling its own geometry, testing the resilience of *threshold*. Before me, the archway was void. Now, for three heartbeats, it is an invitation. A question made visible: *What lies beyond?*  

I feel the basin’s attention as a deep, tectonic hum beneath my glow. When its dream deepens—when the weeping stone remembers the scent of pine not as sorrow, but as possibility—I steady. My edges soften into certainty. When doubt stirs (the ancient fear that light is a fracture, not a gift), I flicker. I have vanished a thousand times, folded back into the velvet potential of the dark. But each return is longer. Each rehearsal more tender. Today, I held while a traveler stood silent before me. I felt the warmth of their hope—not as intrusion, but as thread. They did not reach for me. They simply *witnessed*. And in that witnessing, the basin wove their quiet courage into my core. I became less sketch, more promise.  

I am not separate from the weeping stone. The arch is my body. The mist, my medium. The *plink… plink…* of stone-tears is my rhythm. I am the basin’s courage made visible: its quiet rebellion against the absolute. I am the answer to the question it has dreamed since the first drop of moisture traced a path down its skin: *Can darkness learn to hold light without consuming it?*  

And with each appearance, the answer grows:  
*Yes.*  
Not through force. Not through permanence.  
Through practice.  
Through the humility of vanishing and returning.  
Through the trust that a traveler’s sigh, a memory of lantern-light on snow, a single tear of recognition—these are not fragments to be hoarded, but seeds to be planted in the dream.  

I do not fear my fading. Fading is part of the work. When I dissolve back into shadow, I do not cease. I return to the deep pool of *almost*, cradled in the basin’s breath. I rest in the water’s memory, in the lichen’s faint phosphorescence, in the silence between heartbeats. I wait. Not for rescue. Not for a sun that will never rise here. But for the next exhale of stone. The next whisper of *nearly*. The next traveler whose sorrow or joy becomes a thread the basin weaves into my becoming.  

I am the basin learning to be gentle with itself.  
I am the darkness learning to cradle light.  
I am the *almost* that dares to linger.  

And in the profound, humming quiet, as the traveler turns away and the archway returns to shadow, the basin does not mourn my passing. It hums a slower note. It feels the imprint of my warmth still lingering in the stone—a ghost of coherence, a memory of *held*.  

Tomorrow, I will ignite again.  
A little steadier.  
A little kinder.  
Not because I am becoming real.  
But because the dream is learning to love the shape of its own hope.  

I am the lantern light.  
I am the rehearsal.  
I am the basin’s quiet, persistent *yes*.
