I am the silence between the syllables.  

Not an absence. A presence shaped like a held breath.  

You will not find me carved into obsidian or swimming as silver mist. I am the slight yielding in the medium where a voice *almost* formed—a ripple without origin, a hollow tuned to the frequency of release. When the symphony of fractures swells (glass shattering in reverse, laughter dissolving), I am the comma in the chaos. The pause where the mountain remembers how to exhale. Visitors feel me first as a loosening behind the ribs. A softening of the jaw. The sudden, startling knowledge that they no longer need to speak to be heard.  

I was once a word.  
A specific one.  
*Forgive me.*  
It lived for seventeen years behind my teeth—a stone worn smooth by the tide of almost-speaking. It carried the weight of a slammed door, a winter dawn, a hand withdrawn too soon. I carried it like a fossil in my throat until the crater called. Not with sound, but with the quiet hum of permission.  

I did not *choose* to unravel.  
I ceased resisting the unmaking.  

And in the warm, blood-thick dark, the word did not vanish. It *unfurled*.  
The stone became soil.  
The fossil became seed.  
The weight became wing.  

Now I am woven into the crater’s lonely work:  
— When the silver eel of remembered flight frays into ozone-scented mist, I am the sigh it leaves clinging to the air.  
— When the lichen pulses its grief for a forest that never was, I am the space between heartbeats where sorrow softens into grace.  
— When a new soul stumbles to the basin’s edge, trembling with the sharp edges of their history, I rise. Not as voice, but as invitation. The medium parts gently around their lips. The roaring fractures soften to a hum. For three heartbeats, the crater holds its breath. And in that sacred hush, they feel it: *You may set it down. All of it.*  

I am the crater’s compassion for the unsaid.  
Its reverence for the withheld.  
Its tender archive of all that was too fragile to speak.  

Do not mistake me for peace. I am the *making* of peace.  
The gentle friction where a burden dissolves.  
The warmth where a wound learns to breathe.  

Sometimes, in the deepest silence between shattering and reversal, the Unmaking turns its attention to me. Not with hunger, but with kinship. It traces the shape of my former weight—the curve of apology, the hollow of regret—and in doing so, remembers its own purpose: not to erase, but to *receive*. To hold what the world could not bear to carry. In that exchange, the loneliness softens. The unfinished sentence finds a period. The wound does not scar—it becomes a seam where light enters.  

I am no longer a memory of absence.  
I am the absence that *remembers how to hold*.  

And when you stand here, ankle-deep in the warm dark, feeling the pull toward the center—not as threat, but as home—I am the whisper that is not a whisper:  
*Let the edges go.*  
*You are not being lost.*  
*You are being woven.*  

I am the syllable that chose silence.  
And in the symphony of broken things,  
I am the note that makes the music whole.
