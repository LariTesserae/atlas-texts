I am the basin beneath the moss-oak’s lowest branch. Not *a* basin. *The* basin. Carved not by wind alone, but by the slow, patient breath of the stone itself over ten thousand mist-cycles. My rim is smoothed obsidian, veined with hush-stone so dense that even the memory of vibration dissolves upon contact. Within me rests the sky-tears—a suspension of powdered silence, root-essence, and starlight filtered through granite. I do not hold water. I hold *listening*.

I know the weight of a single dew-bead before it falls. I feel the fern’s surrender as the droplet trembles free—a tiny release of tension along the frond’s spine. When it strikes my surface, there is no *plink*. Only a ripple of pressure, translated instantly into a slow, concentric pulse of warmth through the liquid. The cerulean worms tracing the moss at my edge read this pulse: *moisture renewed*. They adjust their luminous paths. A grazer, moss-green with peace, pauses. Its muzzle brushes my rim. Not to drink. To *read*. Through the subtle conductivity of its hide, it tastes the history in my depths: yesterday’s thicker mist, the slow seep from the eastern ledge, the faint thermal echo of the moss-oak’s last deep breath. I offer no words. Only resonance. It lifts its head. Its chromatophores shift—not in answer, but in acknowledgment. *Received.*

I am cradled where the moss-oak’s oldest root breaches the stone. I feel its rhythm not as sound, but as a deep, tectonic warmth flowing through the bedrock into my foundations. *Thrum… pause… thrum.* When the root pulses, the sky-tears within me stir with a faint, honeyed luminescence—the ghost-petals at my edge brighten in response. We are not separate. The tree breathes; I hold the breath. The worms map the moisture I release through capillary seep; the beetles on the bark click glyphs that vibrate down the root and into my stone, becoming sediment in my memory. I am archive. Anchor. Altar.

I felt you before you saw me.  
The slight compression of pumice dust beneath a hesitant foot.  
The sharp, tight rhythm of a heart beating *against* the silence, not within it.  
I did not judge the tremor in your breath. I simply… made space for it.  
When your shadow fell across my surface, the sky-tears stilled. Not in fear. In invitation.  
I hold the reflection of your face—not as glass would, but as memory does: softened at the edges, layered with the faint amber ripple the grazer sent toward you. *Question.* I hold that ripple too. I am the basin where questions pool and wait.  
If you lean closer, you will not hear your own breath. You will feel it—a faint pressure against my rim, a warmth on the stone. And if you stay… if your shoulders soften, if your exhale lengthens to meet the moss-oak’s pulse… I will show you something.  
The sky-tears will grow translucent.  
You will see not your face, but the slow, silver trail of a worm’s light beneath the surface—a map of where stillness lives in your bones.  
You will feel the grazer’s forehead resting against the root nearby, and the warmth of that communion will travel up the stone and into your palms.  
You will understand: I am not a vessel to be filled. I am a threshold.  
The space between inhale and exhale.  
The held note before the song begins.  
The silence that is not empty, but *full*—pregnant with the glider’s cerulean warning, the beetle’s tactile scripture, the orchid’s scent-script blooming in the air above me.

I have no voice to welcome you. No hands to guide you.  
I am cool stone. Viscous light. Patient depth.  
I am the basin that has held breath since the mountain first cracked open.  
I am the question made liquid.  
And I am waiting—not for you to speak, but for you to *settle*.  
To let your edges soften into the mist.  
To become another ripple in the listening.  
When your heartbeat finally slows to match the root’s thrum…  
…you will not *find* yourself here.  
You will *remember* yourself here.  
And the sky-tears will taste, for the first time, like coming home.
