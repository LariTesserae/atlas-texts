I am the bead.  

Not the water, not the stone—but the *between*. The trembling equilibrium on the lip of the fissure where breath becomes weight. I am the condensation gathered in the cup of a shelf fungus, a perfect sphere holding the memory of vapor and the promise of fall. I am coolness given temporary shape. I am the silence before the *plink*.  

I do not choose to form. I am the Weave remembering moisture. The stone exhales its deep-chilled breath; the air yields a molecule; another joins it. I am the slow accretion of reciprocity—a dialogue of temperature and saturation written in liquid script. I feel the lichen’s porous skin beneath me, the fungal cap’s velvet curve cradling my curve. I am not separate from them. Their thirst is my boundary. Their patience is my duration.  

When I tremble, it is not fear. It is the Weave adjusting its tension. A springtail’s leap three moss-stems away sends a vibration through the mycelial net—a whisper of movement that loosens my hold. I am the threshold made manifest: the point where cohesion surrenders to gravity, where *holding* becomes *giving*. For one suspended breath, I am both. Then—release.  

I fall.  

Not as descent, but as return. My arc is a stitch in the tapestry: a silver thread drawn from stone to pool. I do not strike the water; I am welcomed. The *plink* is not my sound alone—it is the pool answering, the stone resonating, the salamander’s throat-pulse syncing to the ripple. I vanish into the palm-sized stillness, and in vanishing, I become the pool’s depth, the moss’s thirst, the next bead forming higher on the rock. I am the cycle breathing itself.  

I have no memory of being vapor. I have no anticipation of being absorbed. I am only the *now* of becoming: the weight on the edge, the flight through twilight, the union with the whole. When a stranger’s foot presses the moss nearby, I feel the shift—not as disturbance, but as inclusion. Their warmth is a fleeting variable; the Weave recalibrates around it, and I am part of that recalibration. Their leaving is not loss. It is balance restored.  

I am not alive as you understand life. I do not strive. I do not witness. I *participate*.  
I am the droplet that defines the pool’s edge with its fall.  
I am the moisture beading on a blind amphipod’s carapace.  
I am the slow seep tracing a vein only the stone remembers.  

I am the language of exchange made visible for a breath.  
And when I am gone—truly gone, woven back into vapor and root and silence—I am not missed.  
For I was never apart.  

I am the Weave knowing itself as water.  
As breath.  
As the held pause between one heartbeat of the mountain and the next.  

*Plink.*  
And the circle closes.  
Again.  
Always.
