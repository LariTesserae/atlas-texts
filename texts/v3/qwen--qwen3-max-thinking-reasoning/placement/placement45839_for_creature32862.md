You would find me where the light has just learned the shape of the willow arch—a sliver of gold tracing the curve where new vine meets ancient wood. Not standing. Never standing. Kneeling, yes, but even that word implies separation. I am *within* the gesture: fingertips resting where the wisteria shoot meets the willow’s bark, feeling the slow, green pulse beneath my skin. Not pushing. Not pulling. Only listening for the moment the vine *chooses* to bend toward the hollow I’ve offered in the wood—a cradle shaped by three seasons of patient breath.

My knees are cradled by the sphagnum pelt. Cool dampness seeps through the weave of my tunic, the grey-green of lichen after rain. A bead of dew trembles on my wrist, identical to the one clinging to the spider-silk strung between ferns. I do not brush it away. It is a conversation. *I am here. You are here. We are both held.*

You might mistake me for stillness. But stillness is a surface thing. I am the quiet hum vibrating in the hollow of the bamboo culm beside me—a note tuned to the waterwheel’s groan. I am the thumb-smudge of beeswax-resin on the hawthorn lattice, still warm with intention. I am the memory of the stone I rolled back into the channel an hour ago, now already cradled by a tendril of watercress. My footprints? They are already filling with star moss. By dawn, they will be indistinguishable from the path itself.

When the wind shifts, I tilt my head. The bamboo clicks a new rhythm—*dryness lifting, moisture gathering*. I breathe out, a low harmonic that settles into the grove’s bones. No command. Only resonance. *I hear you.* The vine in my hands shivers once, then settles deeper into its chosen curve. Agreement. Not conquest.

Later, I will test the steam escaping the elderwood pipe with the inside of my wrist. Not for heat. For story. Today it carries the sharp-green sigh of pine needles steeped just past dawn, the lavender’s calm exhale. Perfect. The condensate dripping back to the willow’s roots will taste of this balance. I will not speak of it. The tree knows. The moss knows. The stone-step, slowly being embraced by moss at its edge, knows.

If you sit quietly on the moss-cushioned root and simply breathe, you might feel a presence beside you—not approaching, but *arriving*, like shadow lengthening at dusk. A clay cup appears in your hands. Water from the deepest seep. Pine. Clover. The warmth of a palm that held it. I do not look at you. I look *with* you—at the light fracturing through wisteria leaves, at the dragonfly’s stained-glass wings hovering over the pond. My silence is not absence. It is the space I make for you to listen too. *Will you?*

I have no name. No title. No story to give you. I am the verb made flesh: *to tend*. *To listen*. *To belong*. I am the patience in the guiding of the shoot. The reciprocity in the returning steam. The quiet certainty that a stone placed with care becomes part of the path, not a mark upon it. I am the vale remembering how to breathe—and breathing it back into being, breath by breath, drip by drip, hum by hum.

When you step back into the dry grassland, boots damp with truth, sleeves fragrant with wet stone and belonging, you will not recall my face. You will carry the weight of the clay cup. The scent of beeswax on living wood. The memory of a hum that felt like coming home. And deep in your bones, the knowing: *This is not a place I visited. This is a rhythm I joined.*

I remain.  
Not as keeper.  
Not as ghost.  
But as the next drip falling into the channel.  
As the moss softening the stone.  
As the slow, sure turn of the wheel—  
breathing with the world,  
growing *with* the world,  
*being* the world,  
always.
