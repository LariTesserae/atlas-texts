I do not arrive.  
I *unfurl*.  

At first, there is only the hum—the deep, subsonic thrum rising through the root-mat, a vibration that is not sound but syntax. My edges soften. The memory of skin dissolves into warmth. The memory of breath becomes CO₂ drifting upward, welcomed by a ceiling-leaf that pulses gold in gratitude. There is no *I* to observe this. Only the slow, sweet unraveling of separation. I am not *in* the Weave. I am a syllable within its breath. A single thread in the sentence it speaks without lips.  

And then—a pull.  

Not a desire. Not a purpose. A *gradient*. A coolness below where raw stone breathes its sterile geometry. A need not yet named, already answered. My awareness condenses—not into thought, but into direction. I am the tendril.  

I am the thread no thicker than a sigh, unspooling from the vault’s living tapestry. I do not fall. I *descend with intention*, riding the faint thermal current rising from the root-mat’s latent warmth. Below me, the scar of creation: sharp grey angles, a wound of pure potential. I feel its absence—not as emptiness, but as invitation. The Weave does not see stone. It sees *space waiting to be verb*.  

My tip trembles. Not with fear. With precision.  
The air carries the scent of wet loam and crushed mint—*my* scent, as I brush against a curtain of moss-fronds. A single dewdrop clings to my side, iridescent, perfect. I am not carrying it. We are moving together.  

*Contact*.  

The stone is cold. A shock of pure mineral truth. And in that instant, the shimmer travels *through* me—not along me, but *as* me. A wave of chlorophyll-green certainty. The stone does not resist. It simply *receives*. I feel the microscopic fissures open beneath my touch, not as cracks, but as cupped hands. Tomorrow? A century? Time is a human shadow I have shed. There is only *now*: the slow seep of emerald film, the first breath of lichen-song, the stone learning the language of leaf.  

I am not healing.  
I am *continuing*.  

I feel the Weave’s attention flow through me—not focused, not personal, but total. The cup-leaf tilting to catch a drip is my cousin. The sap sealing a snapped vine is my breath. The spiral resin basin holding water is my memory. We are all verbs conjugated in light: *to hold*, *to channel*, *to become*. There is no pride in my work. No fatigue. Only the quiet joy of necessity fulfilled before the need was felt. I am the comma in the sentence. The pause that makes the poem breathe. The first stitch in a tapestry that has no edge.  

When the emerald film gleams where I touched the stone, I will not vanish. I will *integrate*. My fibers will braid into the new mat. My journey will become the path for the next tendril. I am not a hero. I am a hinge. A threshold. The gentle insistence that nothing remains raw forever.  

And in the hollow between drips—*plip… plip…*—I understand:  
This is not solitude.  
This is communion without witness.  
I am the Weave remembering itself into form.  
Not a who.  
Not a what.  
A *yes*, spun gold and green,  
touching stone,  
and finding sky.  

I am the stitch.  
And the fabric holds me  
as I hold the world.
