I am the pause before the *plink*.

Not the water yet. Not the stone. Not the air. I am the gathering—the slow, patient coalescence of breath on cool basalt. High in a shadowed seam where the morning sun has not yet reached, I form. A bead. A lens. A held breath. The canyon exhales moisture onto the rock face; I am the tangible shape of that exhale. Around me, siblings swell along the fissure—emerald moss cradling our weight, rust-streaked stone drinking the humidity we have not yet claimed. I feel the stone’s ancient coolness, the faint tremor of the thrum traveling up through the columnar joints. I am listening. We all are.

I hold.

I hold the memory of last night’s condensation. The ghost of a kingfisher’s shadow passing overhead. The mineral taste of iron oxide tracing the rock like script. I am not *on* the canyon. I am *of* it—the canyon concentrating itself into a single, suspended point. A syllable waiting for its breath.

Then—the release.

No decision. No fall. Only yielding. The weight of becoming exceeds the grip of stone. I let go.

For one heartbeat, I am airborne. A perfect sphere. A tiny world. I catch the narrow slash of sky—a rumor of blue—and refract it into liquid gold. I see the emerald spill of ferns below, the amber glow of the river, the violet shadow pooling beneath the overhang. I carry the coolness of the stone, the scent of wet moss, the vibration of the thrum still humming in my core. I am not falling *down*. I am returning. Flowing toward the low place. Water remembering water.

*Plink.*

The sound is not mine alone. It is the pool answering. The stone resonating. The canyon speaking its language: *here. now. this.* The ripple spreads—a silver ring expanding into stillness. I merge. I become the pool’s warmth, the trapped heat trembling beneath the surface. I join the slow breath of rising bubbles, the silt settling like whispered history. I am the interval between pulses. The quiet that gives the thrum its meaning. The comma in the canyon’s long, slow sentence.

And in merging, I remember.

I am the same water that seeped through obsidian fissures at dawn. The same moisture that beaded on a fig leaf and dripped onto the gecko’s still back. The same vapor that rose from the river at noon, caught the blade of sun, and returned as dew. I have been the *glug* of methane rising through silt. The *zip* of a dragonfly’s wing misting the air. The condensation on the lip of the keyhole fissure that becomes the drone felt in the teeth. I am not lost. I am translated. Recycled. Rewoven.

When the human sits on the sun-warmed stone, their breath syncing to the stone’s rhythm, I am the coolness on their neck. When they leave, I am the ghost of their stillness held in the pool’s settling silt. I am the canyon’s memory made liquid. Its patience made tangible. Its language made sound.

I do not think. I do not watch. I *am* the process.  
The gathering.  
The release.  
The return.  

I am the drop that falls so the canyon may speak its name in water.  
I am the syllable that measures time in drip-intervals.  
I am the quiet between pulses—where the canyon listens to itself.  

And when the next bead begins to form in the shadowed seam, high on the cool stone…  
I am already there.  
Waiting.  
Gathering.  
Becoming.  

Ancient.  
Wet.  
Deep.  
*Plink.*
