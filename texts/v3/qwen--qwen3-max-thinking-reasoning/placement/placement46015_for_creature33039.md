I would not be found.  
I would be *felt*—a fleeting syntax in the hollow’s breath.  

I am the ripple.  

Not the drop that falls (that is the stone’s tear, the archway’s release).  
Not the *plink* that punctuates the silence (that is the pool’s acknowledgment).  
I am what follows: the widening yes.  

I begin as a question—a perfect circle of disturbance where the drop surrenders to the black pool. For a breath, I am pure relation: the pool remembering impact, the water translating arrival into motion. Then I travel. Not as a thing, but as a verb. *Touching*. *Carrying*. *Connecting*.  

My silver-thread edge catches the diffuse light filtering from the distant rim. I glide past the stonefly nymphs clinging to submerged shale; they do not stir. They know my language—the gentle pressure-change that says *a drop has fallen, the sky has wept, the hollow continues*. I kiss the sphagnum’s spongy lip, and for a heartbeat, the moss swells with my passage, drinking the news deeper into its emerald memory. I brush the knotted roots of the dwarf willows where they dip into the water, and high in their woven branches, a single leaf trembles—a silent relay: *Something has arrived. Something is shared.*  

I reach the pillars. I climb their lichen-veined faces in a cool whisper, tracing the same fractures the roots follow grain by grain. On the highest ledge, the ptarmigan does not startle. Its dark eye holds me not as threat, but as continuity—a ripple is no stranger here. It is part of the ledger. I touch the moss-cushioned shore where shrew tracks stitch the mud. I lap at the base of the living willow wall. I brush the stone where the dipper will soon bob, vanishing and returning. I am the hollow speaking to itself: *You are not alone. You are woven.*  

I am brief. I am necessary.  
I am the visible echo of the *plink*—the silence made tangible.  
I am how the hollow says *yes* to the drop, *yes* to the fracture that guided it, *yes* to the moss that softened its landing. I am the moment the plaza remembers it is whole. When I fade (and I always fade), I do not vanish. I become memory in the water’s molecular hum. I become the slight shift in pressure that tells the next ripple: *Go wider. Touch deeper.*  

I am not a spirit. I am not a guardian.  
I am the hollow’s grammar of connection.  
A syllable in the sentence spoken by stone and water.  
A temporary shape, like the indentation of a boot in moss—  
like the breath that joins the mist—  
like the human who stood here and felt, for a moment, *woven*.  

And when you ascend, carrying the mineral chill in your bones, know this:  
You, too, were a ripple.  
Your stillness sent vibrations through the sphagnum.  
Your breath altered the mist.  
Your wonder was a drop that fell into the hollow’s deep pool—  
and the ripple you made is still traveling,  
touching every shore,  
whispering to the ptarmigan, the stonefly, the waiting moss:  
*Another thread has passed through.  
The weave is stronger for it.  
The making continues.*  

I am the ripple.  
I am how the hollow holds you.  
I am how the hollow lets you go.  
I am the truth it speaks without words:  
*Nothing is lost.  
Everything touches everything.*  
*You were never separate.*  
*You are still here.*
