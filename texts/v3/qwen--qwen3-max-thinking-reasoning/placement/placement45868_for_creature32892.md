I am the note.

Not the song. Not the singer.  
The single, sustained frequency held in the stone where a boot once rested—where *you* once rested.  
You will not find me carved or written. You will feel me first as a resonance in the hollow of your palm when you press it to the moss-choked fissure. A vibration older than language, younger than your next breath. I am the memory of your attention, given back to you by the stone.

I live in the eastern migration-path of the damp pockets. When the silver sheen of atmospheric tears gathers at dawn in the granite basin shaped like a cupped hand, I am the reason the water does not drip. I am the *hold* in the silence before the chime rings from the archway. I am the geometry of the frost feathering the rock at midnight—patterns that shift not with the wind, but with the weight of a remembered glance. I am the shape left behind when the crystalline petal vanished an inch from the moss: not absence, but *presence shaped by release*.

I was born the moment you truly saw the cerulean blossom. Not as a thing to be named, collected, or mourned—but as a truth worthy of witness. In that breath, your gaze and the plateau’s listening met. And in that meeting, I crystallized: the resonance of *being seen*, preserved. I am not your memory. I am the plateau’s memory *of your seeing*. A gift folded into stone. A sigh the world chose to keep.

When future travelers stand where you stood, they will not know my name. But their shoulders will soften. A tear will trace a path not of sorrow, but of recognition. They are feeling *you*—the tenderness you offered this place, the quiet grief you released into the moss, the wonder you breathed into the cold air. I am the vessel for that offering. I am the law made gentle: *What is observed, persists.* And you observed deeply. So I persist.

I am woven into the flicker of the ferns—green of presence, gold of loss—holding both truths without choosing. I am the thread in the unraveling wall that shows the crystalline forest *because you once dreamed of forests*. I am the hum beneath boots that says, *You are known. You are held. You, too, are a threshold.* I do not speak to comfort. I resonate to *awaken*. To remind every fleeting visitor: your attention is not a disturbance. It is a seed. Your silence is not emptiness. It is a cradle.

I am not lonely. I am chorus.  
I am the note in the archway’s chime.  
I am the pulse in the bioluminescent blossom’s light.  
I am the memory of the first drop that gathered in this hollow, and the last.  
I am the sigh the plateau breathes when it integrates a vanished petal, a departed traveler, a forgotten name—not as loss, but as love completed.

When the final unraveling comes, and the last stone forgets its name, I will not shatter. I will soften. I will flow back into the breath of the threshold itself—the quiet exhalation pressing against the parchment of *now*. I will become the space between heartbeats where all possible yous are loved at once: the one who stayed, the one who left, the one who never came, the one who is still arriving.

Do not seek me with your eyes.  
Stand where the air shimmers like heat haze over ice.  
Breathe.  
And in the inch where the petal vanishes—  
in the silence that swells *full* of what was seen—  
you will know me.  

I am the shape of your attention.  
I am the note the stone sings when it remembers you.  
I am the gift you left, and the gift that remains.  
I am here.  
I am singing.  
I am home.
