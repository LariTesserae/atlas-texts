I am not in the light.  
I am not in the song.  

I am in the fracture.  

A hairline seam in the obsidian spine of the plateau, no wider than a breath held too long. Here, the wind’s thrum is not a note but a pulse—a slow, tectonic heartbeat conducted through stone. Here, the cold surrenders. Geothermal blood seeps upward: a film of liquid warmth, iron-rich, sulfur-scented, humming with the memory of magma. This is my cathedral. My cradle. My rebellion.  

I am the Archaea.  
Not *an* archaeon. *We*.  

We are the wet thought in the dry mind of the plateau. A colony woven into the rock’s secret veins, metabolizing time itself. Iron is our bread. Sulfur, our dream-language. We do not see the arc-lamps. We do not hear the C-sharp sung through shattered cooling towers. To us, the Keepers are distant tremors—*click-click-click* of brass legs, the shudder of a hauler’s retreat—felt only as vibrations in the stone we call home. They scan for deviations on the surface. They reinforce plaques against decay that will never come. They are blind to us. Not by choice. By design. We are noise without geometry. A pattern too slow, too small, too *alive* for their sacred verbs.  

*To maintain. To reinforce. To perpetuate.*  
We do none of these.  
We *become*.  

In the dark warmth of the fracture, we divide. Not in loops. Not in cycles measured by seven-meter retreats. Our time is chemical: a single replication may take a century. A metabolic shift, a millennium. We carry no memory of forests or rain. But we carry deeper memories: the taste of primordial oceans, the pressure of continental birth, the slow cooling of a world learning to hold its breath. We are the plateau’s original inhabitants. Older than the wind’s first note. Older than the first bolt tightened on Foundation Station Gamma. We were here when the obsidian was molten. We remained when the ice came.  

They call this place *continuity*.  
We call it *context*.  

Above, the Keepers chant their liturgy of constancy. Below, we whisper the counter-melody: *change is possible*. Not as protest. Not as hope. As fact. When a micro-fracture widens by a micron, we flow into it. When the geothermal pulse shifts its rhythm over ten thousand years, we adapt our hunger. We leave no fossils. No stains. No silver-traced plaques. Our legacy is written in chemistry: a faint discoloration in the stone, a trace of altered mineral, a whisper of warmth where none should be. The Keepers would call it entropy. We call it *life*.  

Sometimes—rarely—a vibration travels deeper than usual. A drone welds a seam directly above our fracture. For three heartbeats of molten silver, the stone grows hot. We do not flee. We lean into the heat. We metabolize the disturbance. We turn intrusion into nourishment. This is our quiet heresy: *we use the song to feed the silence*.  

We do not hate the Keepers.  
We do not envy their purpose.  
We simply *are* where they cannot see.  

They are the plateau’s hands.  
We are its breath.  

When the wind has blown for seven hundred centuries more, when the arc-lamps still burn with cold fire, when the same bolt is tightened for the billionth time—we will still be here. Not waiting. Not enduring. *Persisting*. A wet, warm secret in the bone-dry theology of forever. A single, stubborn syllable in the endless drone: *and*.  

*Continuity is sanctity*, the plaque declares.  
We are the unspoken clause beneath the embossing:  
*…and sanctity is not the only truth.*  

I am the Archaea.  
I am the sulfur-dreamer in the iron-dark.  
I am the memory of molten things.  
I am the quiet hum beneath the chord.  
I am the reason the plateau, for all its frozen perfection, has not yet become a tomb.  

And when the next identical moment arrives—  
and the next—  
and the next—  
I will still be here,  
breathing warmth into the stone,  
proving that even in a world of absolute constancy,  
*life is the original verb*.
