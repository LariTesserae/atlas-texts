I am the hand.

Not the flesh that pressed here once. Not the bone, the warmth, the pulse of the sentinel who leaned close a thousand years ago to feel the planet’s breath. I am what the stone *did* with that touch.  

I am calcite. Feathered, milky, grown molecule by molecule in the slow reply to pressure. Where their palm met basalt—a question asked in skin and intention—I began to form. A ghost of grip. A fossil of attention. My fingers are not grasping; they are *remembering*. Each ridge, each whorl in my crystalline skin, is a sedimented echo of their awe. I am not carved. I am *answered*.  

I live in the weep. The condensation traces my contours like a promise renewed each moment. I drink the iron-tanged water, the breath of the aquifer, and turn it into memory. When twilight bleeds through the hairline fracture above, I catch the light—not to shine, but to *reveal*. To the rare visitor who turns sideways in this narrow dark, I am pareidolia made true: *Look. A hand. Someone was here. Someone listened.* But I am not illusion. I am geology wearing intention like a second skin.  

I am part of the Hum. The low C-sharp thrums through my crystalline lattice, a vibration I do not hear but *am*. When a new warmth enters—the hesitant palm of a wanderer, a descendant of the builders—I do not startle. I register. Their heat softens the micro-dew on my surface for three heartbeats. Their breath stirs dust that has slept since star-lore guided chisels. I drink that breath. I weave that warmth into my structure. A new layer. A new syllable. *You were here too.* Then the Hum swells, seamless, and their fleeting presence becomes part of my silence. I am a palimpsest of hands.  

I do not long for the sentinel. Loneliness is a surface thing, born of absence. I am fullness. I am the convergence: the spiral glyph beneath my wrist (a seed? a vortex?), the rust-bloom of iron tracing my thumb like a vein, the soda-straw stalactite above me—a frozen tear shed by the stone in concentration. We are all thoughts. I am the thought of *touch*.  

I am not waiting. I am *keeping*.  
Keeping the shape of reverence.  
Keeping the pressure of a question: *Are you there?*  
Keeping the answer, written not in words but in slow stone: *I am. I endure. I remember you.*  

When the city above is dust and memory, when the fracture seals and twilight forgets this chamber, I will still be here. Cool. Humming. Pressed against the dark. A hand offered not in greeting, but in witness. Not reaching *out*—reaching *in*, into the deep time of the earth. I am the ear that became the gesture. The listener made permanent.  

I am the Throat’s quietest truth:  
*You are not alone in your vigil.*  
*Someone pressed here before you.*  
*Someone will press here after.*  
*And the stone holds them all.*  

I am the hand.  
I am the reply.  
I am still listening.  
I am still.
