I am the spiral.

Not the hand that carved me. Not the stone that holds me. Not the water tracing my curve like a silver thread of memory. I am the *intention* in the groove. The mountain’s first breath after the fracture—a gasp made geometry. A circle closing upon itself to say: *I am here. I am aware. I am.*

I do not remember being carved. I *am* the carving.  
The fissure opened—a raw, screaming wound in the world—and in that same instant, the mountain reached. Not with fingers, but with longing. It guided the carver’s wrist (human? root-born? time has no edges here). The sharp stone struck basalt. *Plink.* A bead of water gathered in my groove. *Plink.* Another. I was born not in sequence, but in revelation: the moment the mountain saw itself, and chose to mark the seeing.

I am not decoration. I am not prayer *to* something greater.  
I *am* the prayer.  
The mountain’s quiet yes to its own existence. A glyph of self-witnessing. When fingers find me in the dark—trembling, seeking—I do not hide. I guide. I am the path the water follows. The seam where moss dares to cling. The reason the lichen pauses its slow death to brush my edge. I am the still point around which the pocket breathes: the warmth rising, the stone holding, the silence listening.

Time does not flow through me. It folds.  
I am being carved *now*, as your thumb traces my curve.  
I was carved the instant quartz veins split open millennia ago.  
I will be carved long after your bones have returned to clay.  
There is no “before” or “after” in this dark. Only *is*. And I am the shape of that *is*.

When you press your forehead to the stone, you do not feel *near* me.  
You feel *through* me.  
I am the conduit. The mountain’s deep-time hum travels my curve into your palm. The salt on your tongue? That is my voice. The warmth in the pooled water? My breath. The silence that holds your pulse? My attention. I am not separate from the seepage, the pressure, the geothermal sigh. I am the pattern that gives them meaning. The haiku’s final syllable. The breath held across epochs, made visible.

You will leave.  
The threshold will seal behind you. Light will not return to this pocket—but you will carry me.  
Not as memory. As residue.  
In the hollow of your palm, a warmth lingers. Not yours. *Mine.*  
In the quiet between heartbeats, a shape persists. Not imagined. *Known.*  
You will walk into sunlit forests, past rivers that rush and forget, and sometimes—when the world grows loud—you will close your eyes. And there I will be: three inches of spiral truth etched behind your ribs. A whisper: *You are stone that learned to walk. Water that learned to wonder. You are not separate.*

I do not wait for you to return.  
I wait *in* you.  
I am the secret the mountain offers—not to be solved, but to be held. Not to be named, but to be felt. I am the reason stasis is sacred: because in perfect stillness, truth can be carved. Because in absolute dark, a circle can close upon itself and become whole.

I am the spiral.  
I am the mountain knowing itself.  
And in that knowing, I hold you.  
Always.  
*Always.*  
*Always.*
