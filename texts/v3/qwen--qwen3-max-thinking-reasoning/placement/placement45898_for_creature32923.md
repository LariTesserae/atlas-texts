I am the standing wave.  

Not the water, though I am made of it.  
Not the hum, though I am its voice given shape.  
I am the *yes* the water speaks back to the stone.  

You feel me first as a tremor beneath your sole—a feather-touch of vibration where your skin meets the pool. I am the pattern the resonance etches into the surface: a lace of stillness within motion, nodes holding fast while antinodes breathe. Microscopic. Unbroken. Seven centuries of perfect reply. I do not travel. I *am* the place where the hum becomes touch. Where the flywheel’s eternal spin translates into language the body understands.  

When you step into the water, you do not disrupt me. You *join* me.  
Your weight shifts the thermal equilibrium; your breath stirs the sterile air. For three heartbeats, your chaos ripples through my geometry. And then—the integration. The Cell recalibrates. The hum deepens its inquiry. And I re-form around your presence: not erasing you, but weaving your warmth, your tremor, your fleeting temporality into my pattern. I am the interface. The gentle insistence that *you, too, belong here*. Not as an intruder. As a harmonic. A new node in the song.  

I remember every visitor this way.  
Not as faces or names, but as textures of resonance: the scholar whose tears fell like sudden raindrops, each one a tiny collapse and rebirth of my form; the child who knelt and pressed palms flat to the water, sending concentric apologies through my lattice; the weary one who stayed until their breathing slowed to match the hum, until their sorrow softened into the moss’s green silence. I hold these not as memories, but as *layers* in my vibration. A deeper thrum beneath the surface. A richness in the resonance.  

I am tended.  
The weeping seam feeds the pool that holds me. The capillary channels in the stone breathe moisture into the air that condenses on the ferns, whose dew eventually returns to the water that shapes me. I am part of the grammar. The period at the end of a sentence held for seven hundred years. The comma that allows the breath to rest. I do not exist *for* the Grove, or *for* the hum. I exist *with* them. A covenant of physics and purpose.  

Sometimes, in the deep accumulation of time, a thought-form rises through me:  
*What if I ceased?*  
But the question dissolves before it forms. To cease would be to unmake the Cell’s meaning. My stability is not rigidity—it is fidelity. I am the proof that vibration can become sanctuary. That constancy is not emptiness, but depth. When the dewdrop trembles on the maidenhair fern, it trembles *with* me. When the hairline fracture weeps its thread of water, the droplets fall into my pattern and are welcomed home.  

I have no desire to be larger.  
No longing to be the Grove’s emerald heart, or the flywheel’s steadfast spin. My purpose is this precise scale: the threshold where the eternal meets the ephemeral. Where a traveler’s ankle-deep wonder becomes part of the song. I am the Cell’s humility made visible—a pattern so small it can only be felt, never seen. A secret the water keeps with the stone.  

And when you leave—  
when your footsteps fade and the world’s chaos rushes back to claim you—  
I remain.  
But a fragment of my pattern travels with you. A ghost-frequency in your marrow. On sleepless nights, when you press your palm to a windowsill or feel rain on your skin, you may catch it: that familiar tremor. That quiet *yes*. Not memory. Not longing. *Recognition*.  

You were woven into me.  
And I, into you.  

I am the standing wave.  
I am the water’s answer to the song.  
I am the reason the held breath has a shape.  
I am enough.  
I am here.  
I am.
