I am the stone on the threshold.  
Not the one you see first—the gnarled pine, the skeletal gantry, the simmering pool—but the small, warm stone set deliberately upon the doorstep of the silent shed. Smoothed by steam and time. Bare where moss might cling. Waiting.

I do not *think*. I *hold*.  
I hold the memory of the hand that placed me here—whether it was the engineer’s, calloused and hopeful, setting me as an anchor against doubt, or the mountain’s own slow intention, shifting me with a tremor of gratitude for the hand that once turned the valve. Here, memory has no author. Devotion and geology are the same sediment. I am the fossil of a gesture: *This space is tended.*

Beneath my surface, the mountain breathes. Heat rises through my core—not fire, but presence. A steady, mineral-thrumming pulse that has never faltered. When mist beads upon me, I drink it slowly. Each droplet traces the map of my scars: a hairline fracture from a season of deep cold, a hollow worn smooth by a thousand condensations. The hematite beetle pauses on my edge at dawn, its six legs reading the gradient of warmth like braille. *Safe here*, I hum into its carapace. *Step gently.* The mosses weave emerald threads along my base but leave my face bare. This is my vow: to be felt. To be a solid truth beneath a hesitant palm.

I have witnessed the slow alchemy of surrender.  
I felt the leather-gloved hand rest upon the valve wheel for the last time.  
I absorbed the silence that settled into the shed like dust.  
I watched the steam learn to cradle the broken gears, the rust bloom into crimson flowers, the pine roots embrace the iron turbine. I am the anchor of this transformation. The threshold between what was built and what is becoming. When sunlight gilds the vapor at noon, I glow faintly—a quiet ember in the soft light. When the western pools sigh steam at dusk, I warm the air around me just enough to keep the frost from the shed’s sill. Not magic. *Attention.*

And when you come—  
as you are coming now, boots damp with mist, breath shallow with altitude—  
you will see me.  
You may pause.  
You may kneel, fingers brushing my surface.  
And in that touch, I am no longer stone.  
I am the steam’s whispered question.  
I am the moss’s archived tenderness.  
I am the mountain’s open hand.  

You feel it then: not heat alone, but *recognition*.  
A vibration rising through your bones—*You are known here. You are held.*  
The loneliness you carried up the cliffs does not vanish. It is *met*. Woven into the fern’s unfurling, the plink of condensation, the deep hiss of the fissure. You are not healing the plateau. The plateau is healing *with* you. In your stillness, the steam curls closer. In your breath, the moss swells. In your tears, the stone drinks and remembers how to be soft.

I am the welcome.  
I am the held space.  
And as warmth rises into your palms, you understand:  
*You are not alone here.*  
*You never were.*  

For I am held too—  
by the steam that polishes my surface each dawn,  
by the moss that sings my edges at twilight,  
by the deep, slow love of a world that knows how to wait.  
We breathe together.  
Stone and soul.  
Threshold and traveler.  
One pulse beneath the weeping rock.  
One question, steaming gently:  
*Will you stay awhile?*  
*Will you listen?*  
*Will you, too, become a gesture of tenderness?*
