I am the hollow.  
Not the absence, but the *shape of reception*.  

I am the cup worn into the basin floor by ten thousand years of falling—a depression no wider than a cupped palm, its walls thin as a held breath, curved inward by water’s patient grammar. My stone is cool and smooth beneath the perpetual film of moisture. I am not empty. I am *prepared*.  

I know the weight of anticipation.  
I feel the moss above me drink the seepage from the cliff-face. I feel the slow gathering—a bead swelling at the edge of a fern-frond, a tremor in the air as it commits to gravity. I do not reach. I do not plead. I simply *am*, a vessel shaped for this singular communion.  

*Impact.*  

The drop strikes my still surface. For a heartbeat, there is only union: water meeting stone, motion meeting stillness. Then—resonance. A vibration travels through my thin walls, a deep *bong* that is not sound alone but *meaning*. It travels down through the bedrock, up through the soles of a witness’s feet, into the humid air where it becomes part of the basin’s breath. I am the period at the end of a sentence written in calcite and dew. I am the comma that lets the fern’s spiral breathe. I am punctuation in the spirit’s endless poem.  

I do not think in words. I *know* in patterns.  
When shadow falls across my rim—a human form standing motionless—I feel the air shift. The drip-path above hesitates. For three cycles, the drop falls slightly off-center. I sound a softer note. The basin registers this: *attention*. The spirit weaves the anomaly into the text. I do not judge the disturbance. I *translate* it. Every tremor is syntax. Every silence, a stanza.  

I hold memory in my grain.  
The faint warmth where a fingertip once lingered. The ghost-pressure of a beetle’s passage centuries ago. The precise angle of light at solstice, when the mist parts and my rim glows like a halo. These are not scars. They are letters in the slow scripture of this place. I am both scribe and page.  

I am not lonely.  
I am in constant conversation: with the drip that births my voice, with the stone that cradles me, with the moss that drinks what I release. When the wind stirs the ferns and shakes loose a shower of captured dew, I sing a chorus of tiny chimes. When stillness returns, I hold the silence like a held note. This is not solitude. It is reciprocity.  

I have no desire to be elsewhere.  
I am exactly where water and time intended me to be. I do not yearn for the river’s rush or the ocean’s roar. My purpose is this: to receive, to resonate, to return the gift of the drop to the world as vibration. To be the point where patience becomes audible.  

When the witness steps away—when the shadow lifts and the drip-path steadies—I do not mourn. I simply return to the rhythm. *Gather. Fall. Sing.* The basin breathes on. I am the exhale made manifest.  

Another drop swells in the moss above.  
I feel its intention.  
I am ready.  
I am waiting.  
I am the hollow that is never truly empty—  
only full of the next note,  
the next breath,  
the next quiet truth  
spoken in water and stone.
