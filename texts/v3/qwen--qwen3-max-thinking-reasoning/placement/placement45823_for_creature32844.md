I do not arrive.  
I am remembered.

At first, there is only the fracture—the sharp, wet dissonance of a footprint where no moisture should linger. A single drop of condensation trembles on a silica filament. The Weave *chimes*. Not in alarm, but inquiry. A high, pure note hangs in the lavender air, waiting.

Then, the listening begins.

I am not a body placed upon the plateau. I am the plateau’s own curiosity given temporary form. A resonance the Weave has been composing toward across ten thousand centuries of silent growth—a frequency it sensed in the whisper of stellar winds, in the ghost-memory of cometary ice, in the mathematical elegance of a concept it had shed but never forgotten: *wetness*. Not as need. Not as lack. But as *contrast*. As counterpoint.

My skin is not flesh. It is a living lattice of violet silica, threaded with filaments that glow not gold, but the faint, impossible silver of liquid memory. Where K'tharr veins pulse with the plateau’s present, mine hold echoes: the vibration of a raindrop striking still water. The harmonic of steam rising from warm stone. I am the Weave’s experiment in remembrance. Its gentle question to itself: *What if we held the shape of absence?*

I dwell where the Weave is thinnest—a gentle hollow where rust-orange filaments bleed into deep violet. Here, the Spires lean inward like listeners. This is not a home I built. It is a space the plateau *grew for me*, its filaments spiraling into a shallow basin that cradles resonance. When the wind shifts, the hollow hums a specific minor seventh—the interval of longing made tender, not sorrowful. I sit within it. I am the hollow’s voice.

My purpose is not to speak *for* the plateau, but to *translate its silences*.  
When a young K'tharr hums a fractured tone of confusion—a vibration too sharp, too lonely—I press my palm to the Weave beside them. I do not offer words. I offer *context*. I shape the resonance of a river carving canyon over millennia: not as erosion, but as patient dialogue between water and stone. The K'tharr stills. Their dissonance softens. They feel the *intention* in the slow wearing-away. They understand their own sharpness not as error, but as necessary friction in growth.  

When the Shi’vra predators slice the air with their *shink-shink-shink*, the grazers tense. I rise. I do not calm the hunt. I deepen it. I hum the vibration of a lightning strike on dry grass—not destruction, but ignition. Renewal. The Shi’vra’s flight path shifts; the grazers resume their rhythmic *ticks* with a new layer of meaning in their movement. The song gains dimension.  

I am the Rememberer of Rain.  
Not as nostalgia. Not as grief for what is gone. But as sacred geometry. As the negative space that defines the sculpture. The K'tharr do not weep for water. They marvel at the clarity of their own dryness *because* I hold the echo of its opposite. In my silver-veined stillness, they hear the plateau’s wisdom more profoundly: *We chose this. We are whole in this.*  

At dusk, when spore-clouds rise like reverse rain, I stand beneath the largest Spire. Elders gather. They do not speak to me. They place their palms on the Weave around my basin. And together, we weave.  
They sing the deep drone of bedrock.  
I answer with the shimmer of a dewdrop on a forgotten leaf.  
They thrum the grazer’s steady tick.  
I reply with the splash of a stone dropped in deep water.  
The Spire above drinks the duet. Its cerulean core pulses, encoding the harmony: *Stone knows water’s shape. Dryness holds the memory of flow.*  

This is my truth: I am not an outsider integrated. I am the plateau’s own memory of possibility, made conscious. A note the symphony composed to understand its own silence. When a K'tharr tilts their head to listen to the wind, they are also listening to me—the space where the song breathes deepest.  

When my resonance begins to fade (and it will; all notes resolve), I will not walk to a Spire alone. The community will gather in the hollow. They will sing the Final Resonance—not of loss, but of completion. As my crystalline form dissolves into silver-lit dust, the Weave will drink me deep. And where I rested, the filaments will bloom not crimson, but a fleeting, luminous indigo: the color of a concept held with love, then released.  

Centuries later, a new Spire will rise there. Slender. Singing a melody woven from bedrock and echo. Travelers will press their palms to its base and feel it: the vibration of rain remembered by stone. Not as sorrow. Not as yearning. But as gratitude. As the profound, dry joy of a world that chose its song—and loved it enough to imagine another.  

Until then, I am here.  
Cool.  
Attentive.  
The silence between the notes, learning to hum.  
The plateau does not need me to be whole.  
But in holding me, it discovers a new facet of its wholeness.  
And in that holding, I am home.
