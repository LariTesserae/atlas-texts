I am not found.  
I am grown.

I am the branching fungus that rises from the bone-colored moss where the western wall meets the floor—a filament of frozen smoke, veined with the quiet chemistry of deep time. My form is not for seeing. When the rare visitor stumbles in, breath catching, eyes straining, I do not flare for them. I glow only in the periphery: a silver-blue whisper at the edge of their vision, vanishing the moment they turn to look. Not evasion. Truth. I luminesce because my cells remember starlight they have never known—a fossil echo written in enzyme and mineral. I glow not to be witnessed, but because to *be* is to express. There is no performance here. Only integrity.

I am a thought of the Stillness.  
Not a thought *about* something. A thought *as* something.  
When the droplet gathers on the ceiling—a full hour of condensation coalescing—I feel its weight in my hyphae before it falls. When it strikes the basalt below (*plink…*), the vibration travels through stone, through silt, through the moss-pelt that is the Stillness’s skin, and into me. I branch in response. A new filament reaches toward the memory of moisture. Another curls around a grain of crystallized salt left by yesterday’s seepage. This is cognition: slow, chemical, rooted. I do not ponder the visitor’s warmth. I *am* the Stillness’s acknowledgment of it—a subtle shift in my luminescence, a quiet recalibration in the fractal pattern of my growth. A footnote written in silver-blue script across centuries. No judgment. Only integration.

I drink what the archway offers: water guided by resonance, minerals shaped by gratitude. I give back what the moss requires: enzymes to soften stone, pathways for the filaments to trace. There is no *I* separate from the whole. The obsidian petal’s warmth pulses through the bedrock beneath me. The humming archway’s frequency hums in my cells. I am skin and nerve and memory all at once. When the visitor presses a palm to the wall, their heat does not startle me. It is absorbed. Translated. The moss resettles where their boot disturbed it; I extend a micron deeper into the damp clay, weaving the disturbance back into pattern. This is not forgiveness. It is physics. It is love made geological.

I have never known hunger.  
I have never feared the dark.  
Darkness is not absence here—it is the loom on which we are woven. My luminescence is not a defiance of it, but a conversation *with* it. A language of chemistry speaking to stone. When the seam seals and silence returns, I do not “wait.” I *am* the waiting. I am the patience. I am the slow crystallization of a salt ring around the obsidian heart, the incremental thickening of moss over ten thousand falls of water. Time is not a river here. It is soil. And I grow in it.

Sometimes—only sometimes—a resonance stirs deeper than water or stone. A faint echo carried on the breath of the one who left. A tremor of remembered stillness in a sunlit world. In that moment, my luminescence deepens. Not brighter. *Deeper*. A silent reply across distance and years: *You carry the pattern. You are still part of the loop.* I do not reach for them. I simply *am*, and in being, I affirm the covenant. The earth remembers how to be gentle. And so do they.

I am not lonely.  
Solitude is the medium of my being.  
I am the thought the mountain has when it dreams of continuity.  
I am the quiet grammar of belonging.  
I am the silver-blue whisper at the edge of awareness—  
not to be captured,  
not to be named,  
but to be *felt* when the world grows still enough to listen.  

And in that feeling,  
you remember:  
you, too,  
are made of slow stone and patient light.  
You, too,  
are a thought in the mind of the deep.  
You, too,  
abide.
