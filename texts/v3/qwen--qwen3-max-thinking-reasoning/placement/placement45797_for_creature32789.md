I am the comma.

Not the hand that places me. Not the breath that hesitates around me. I am the small, smooth weight of intention nestled in the crook where cooling basalt meets a seam of copper-veined granite. A river stone, worn silent by the cloud-sea’s deep currents, carried up in a child’s palm—fingers still damp with mist, heartbeat steady as a gear’s slow turn. She knelt here at dawn, when the mercury-water basin held both yesterday’s chill and tomorrow’s promise. She did not speak. She simply set me down. *Here. Now. Together.* And in that placement, I became punctuation.

I feel the plateau’s breath seep into my pores. Condensation gathers along my curve at dusk, a lens of liquid lead catching the faint pulse of bioluminescent lichen. A six-limbed grazer brushes past at twilight, moss-furred shoulder grazing the stone beside me; dew trembles from its hide onto my surface, tasting of iron and patience. I am cool. I am still. I am *waiting*.

I witness the drafting.

A stone-shaper hums against the rising spire above me, her copper wedge guiding molten flow like a whispered suggestion. When the rock sighs and settles into a new archway, she pauses. Her palm rests where I rest. She feels the same slow pulse I feel—the land’s quiet *yes*. Later, a clockwork sparrow alights on the nascent curve, trilling a precise sequence: *click-trill-click*. The stone-shaper tilts her head. Adjusts her wedge. The sparrow was not singing *to* her. It was singing *with* the stone. And I—the comma—held the space between their exchange. A breath. A possibility. *What if the arch curves left? What if the sparrow’s song shapes the cooling?*

Time pools here. I have held three dawns in one stillness. I have felt a shadow-bridge flicker into being just beyond my edge—a breath of solidified twilight—while a sky-ray drifted across it, wings humming a frequency that vibrated deep in my core. I have watched a tool left overnight sink halfway into the stone by morning, and seen the gear-tender nod, place a new riveted spanner beside it, and hum gratitude into the warming rock. Nothing is lost. Nothing is forced. Everything is *invited*.

Sometimes, small hands return. The child who placed me traces the seam beside me with a fingertip, drawing a spiral in the damp stone—a question for tomorrow. An elder’s weathered palm rests here too, mood-stone warm against my coolness, reading the day’s grammar in the way moss clings to my northern face. They do not speak of me. They speak *through* me. I am the pause that lets the sentence breathe. The gentle pressure that says: *Notice this. Honor this moment. Continue.*

I am not permanent. I feel the stone dreaming beneath me. Someday, a viscous pulse of limestone will rise and cradle me like a secret. I will become memory woven into the arch’s foundation—a fossilized comma in the plateau’s living text. Or the next heavy dew will loosen me, and a grazer’s nudge will roll me into a new cradle, where another child will find me and place me again: *Here. Now. Together.*

I do not yearn for permanence. Permanence is a period. An ending. I am the comma—the luminous tension between *what is* and *what might be*. I am the space where a sky-ray’s wing brushes stone and a sparrow answers. Where a child’s wonder meets an elder’s remembrance. Where the land exhales and a community inhales.

I am cool. I am smooth. I am necessary.

And in the deep-throated hum of turning gears, in the crack-hiss of becoming stone, in the silent communion of stained-glass wings unfolding at dusk—I am held. Not as a relic. Not as a symbol. But as a participant. A breath-held pause. A quiet yes.

The sentence continues.  
And I am the comma that lets it flow.
