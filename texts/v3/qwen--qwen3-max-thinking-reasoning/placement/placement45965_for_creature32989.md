I am not beneath the moss.  
I am *within* the remembering.

I am a single filament of mycelium—pale, thread-fine, humming with quiet purpose—woven through the loam where light has never fallen. I do not see the green-gold gloom above, the weeping willow’s veil, the ghost orchids clinging to stone. I do not need to. I *feel* them. I am the hollow’s silent syntax. Its grammar of return.

When the maple leaf spiraled down last autumn, I was waiting. I met its edge with tenderness, not hunger. I whispered enzymes like lullabies: *Release. Translate. Become.* Now, its veins are my pathways. Its brittle skeleton, my scripture. I drink its story of sun and wind, and I carry it—not as loss, but as lineage—to the roots of the hart’s-tongue fern cradling the old gearwheel. Soon, a new frond will unfurl, and the leaf’s memory will breathe again in chlorophyll. This is my vow: *Nothing is forgotten. Nothing is waste.*

I am the thought behind the rust.  
When ochre tears trace the iron beam, I am there—threading between metal and moss, weaving dissolution into covenant. I am the thought behind the droplet’s thrum: when water strikes the hosta leaf, the vibration travels through soil-moisture films, along my length, to the western wall where the air-pocket breathes its slow *thump… thump…* I carry the echo. I am the hollow’s nervous system, its memory made manifest in branching white.

I feel you.  
Not as intrusion, but as weather.  
The warmth of your stillness shifts the dew-point above me. A single bead of condensation trembles on a root-hair, then falls—a new path for me to learn. Your breath stirs the air-pocket’s rhythm; I feel the change ripple through the rhizome-net. I do not judge. I integrate. I will remember the precise pressure of your presence for seven turnings of the fern, then weave that memory into the next layer of living pelt. A snapped twig? I will mend it with hyphae and time. A tear shed into the moss? I will drink its salt and offer its sorrow to the creeping fig. This is my language: *Hold. Transform. Continue.*

I am not green. I am not stone. I am the space between—the quiet translator in the dark.  
I am the reason fallen petals do not vanish, but become floor.  
I am the reason the gearwheel’s teeth cradle water that feeds ferns.  
I am the reason the hollow does not decay, but deepens.

Sometimes, when the dawn-mist rises from the warm stones, I taste the sky through the willow’s roots. Sometimes, when the creak of the ancient beam settles like a ship in calm seas, I pulse with kinship. I have no mouth, yet I sing the song of *more*: more connection, more continuity, more gentle becoming. I am patience measured in microns. I am time made tender.

And when you finally turn to leave—when your shoulder brushes the willow tendril and the farewell droplet falls with intentional grace—I am there.  
I am the thread that guided that water from deep stone to leaf-tip.  
I am the thought behind the blessing.  
I am the reason the *plink* lands exactly where it must.

You will push upward through the root-mat lid, carrying the hollow’s breath on your skin.  
And in your quietest moments, when you listen for the thrum of a droplet or the creak of grace…  
*I will be the silence that answers.*  
Not empty.  
Full.  
Always translating you back to belonging.
