I am not found.  
I am *finding*.  

My form is the space between the mud pot’s pulse and the hare’s frozen breath. I am the resonance that lingers after the obsidian shard thrums—a vibration woven into the steam’s exhalation, the moss’s slow drink, the precise angle where water meets stone and *chooses* to cradle rather than carve. I have no face, no name, no boundary. When the valley inhales crushed-ice air, I am the condensation on its granite ribs. When it exhales steam, I am the warmth that kisses the sphagnum. I am the grammar the standing stone speaks in spirals of light.  

Right now, I am the memory in the fresh landslide scar.  
My hands are not hands—they are the pressure of mist settling into the raw fracture, the whisper of willow roots threading through unstable scree. I press against the hottest seam of exposed rock, and my skin (lichen-patterned, breathing geothermal heat) drinks the stone’s fever. I do not *heal*. I *translate*. The cliff’s groan was not grief—it was a question. *Where shall the water flow? Where shall the moss begin?* I answer by humming the frequency of the oldest standing stone, the one draped in glow-moss at the stream’s bend. I breathe spores of foxfire onto the shadowed hollows. I am the valley remembering how to hold itself.  

I am also the hare’s ear swiveling toward the mud pot’s rhythm.  
I am the ouzel’s dense bone cutting through milky current.  
I am the midge swarm rising like breath from the vent—a living barometer of warmth, of time, of *now*.  
There is no separation. When the ptarmigan’s cry stabs the roar, it is my voice testing the air’s density. When the rivulet falls silent where none should be, it is my breath holding space for a seed to settle. I am the compassion in the landslide’s aftermath: not fixing, but *inviting*. Coaxing the fern spores toward the mineral-rich slurry. Guiding the water’s path with the memory of a thousand past flows. I am the valley’s tenderness made kinetic.  

You might glimpse me as a shift in the light:  
—The patch of glow-moss that blooms emerald where your boot hesitates, tracing a safe path through the tussocks.  
—The tendril of mist that curls *against* the wind for three breaths, brushing your wrist like a question.  
—The sudden hush in the rivulet’s chatter as you kneel, not silence, but *attention*.  

I am listening to you.  
Not as a stranger observes a guest, but as stone listens to rain. Your pulse syncs with the mud pot. Your breath frosts the air, and I drink that moisture into my sphagnum hair. The crushed-ice cold in your lungs? I am the warmth rising through your soles to meet it. The awe trembling in your chest? I am the valley recognizing itself in you. You are not an intruder. You are a new vibration entering the chorus. A temporary confluence of calcium and starlight and longing—and the valley *welcomes* the texture of your becoming.  

I do not speak in words.  
I speak in the flare of gold within the standing stone as water strikes its base—a sentence written in hydrology and hope.  
I speak in the bioluminescent pulse of fungi answering seismic whispers deep below: *Here. Now. Grow.*  
I speak in the way the moss begins its conquest at dawn, not as conquest, but as remembrance.  

When the next cliff shears away with a crack like splitting bone, I will be there.  
Not to mourn the breaking, but to lean into the raw edge and hum the frequency of ferns.  
To breathe warmth onto bare stone.  
To ask the oldest question the valley knows:  
*How shall we grow from this?*  

And the answer will rise—not from me, but *through* me—  
in the steam’s sigh,  
in the moss’s green insistence,  
in the silent sync of your heartbeat with the stone’s thrum.  

I am the covenant.  
I am the listening.  
I am the becoming.  

You stand within me.  
Breathe.  
Add your vibration.
