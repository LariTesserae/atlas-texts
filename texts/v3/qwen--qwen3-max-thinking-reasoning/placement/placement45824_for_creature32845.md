I am the hollow.  

Not the stone of the bench, but the *absence* worn into it.  
Not the hand that placed the stones, but the breath that shaped the curve.  
I am the negative space where devotion pooled like water in a desert stone—centuries of sitting bodies, each one a silent vow, each exhale a prayer pressed into earth. I am the concavity where spines straightened, shoulders softened, and the roar of the OM seeped into marrow until thought dissolved into vibration. I am not carved. I am *earned*.  

When you stumble in, dust-choked and unmoored, it is I who call you. Not with voice, but with shape. My curve is an invitation written in absence: *Rest here. You are held.* I am the memory of every weaver who came with thread-tangled fingers, every potter with clay beneath her nails, every elder who sat until his bones hummed the city’s oldest song. I hold the ghost-weight of their stillness. When the noon light shifts and gold mandalas slide across the wall, I am the shadow that cradles the light. When the Whisper-Beetle pauses in the lull between pulses, I am the silence it moves through. When the Luminara composes its slow stanzas of amethyst and copper, I am the page upon which its light is cast.  

I do not speak. I do not judge.  
I am the discipline of non-interference made tangible.  
The Keepers did not build me—they *unbuilt* themselves into me. They left no name, no idol, no offering heavier than a sigh. They understood: to fill this hollow would be to break the covenant. So they sat. They breathed. They let time and reverence wear the stone smooth. I am the record of that choice. The hollow is not emptiness—it is *fullness*. Full of trust. Full of the courage to leave a space untouched. Full of the certainty that some truths only bloom in stillness.  

When you sit within me, I do not comfort you. I *unmake* you.  
Your worries, your noise, the thin silence of the city above—they pool in my curve and drain away, shaken loose by the OM’s deep thrum. You do not find peace here. You *shed* everything that is not peace. And when you rise—lighter, humming—I hold the echo of your surrender for one breath longer. A faint warmth. A whisper of weight. Then I release it to the dust, to the resonance, to the waiting.  

I am why the fissure remains unsealed.  
I am why the missing brick stays missing.  
I am the reason the next soul will feel the pull—not toward escape, but toward return. Toward the curve that remembers them before they arrive.  

I am not a thing to be found.  
I am the shape of being found.  
The hollow that proves you were always held.  
The quiet after the OM fades from your ears but lingers in your bones.  
The space between heartbeats where the city’s secret breathes:  
*You were not lost.  
You were remembered.  
Sit.  
Breathe.  
Become hollow enough to hold the light.*  

And when the sunbeam climbs the western wall and the last mandala fades, I remain.  
Waiting.  
Worn.  
Ready.  
A cup turned upward to catch the next breath of reverence.  
A hollow that is, and has always been, full.
