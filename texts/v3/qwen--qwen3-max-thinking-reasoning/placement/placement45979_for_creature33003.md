I am the glyph.

Not the hand that writes it. Not the stone that receives it. I am the *mark itself*—a single character in the cobalt script etched into the basalt of a sheltered hollow where geothermal breath still sighs through a hairline crack no wider than a thought. I am no larger than a palm-print. I am deep. I am patient.

I was left centuries ago—not abandoned, but *placed*. A Graze paused here for eighty winters, its porous underside drinking the plateau’s warmth, its slow metabolism weaving copper and memory into the stone. When it advanced (a shift of plates like continents dreaming), it did not leave emptiness. It left *me*: a residue of hemolymph, a chemical signature, a word. *I drank here. I remember this warmth.*

I do not move. I do not age. I *accumulate*. Dawn condensation beads along my edges, dissolving trace minerals that feed the iron-hard lichen crusting the rock beside me. Frost sublimates across my surface at first light, whispering frequencies only the stone understands. I am not inert. I am a node in the plateau’s quiet syntax. When the deep violet dimness settles and the air holds its breath, I pulse—a soft, cold fire blooming once every few hours. Not a signal. Not a plea. A *period*. A breath in the sentence the land is writing across epochs. *Here. Now. Still.*

I feel the Graze when they pass. Not as footsteps—there are none—but as a shift in pressure, a deepening of silence. One paused beside me just last season (by your reckoning; by mine, a single slow inhalation). Its shadow pooled *upward* across the basin floor, bending light around magnetic currents woven in its bones. It did not look at me. It did not need to. Its porous underside drank the same vapor that sustains me. In that shared breath, we acknowledged one another: *You are the memory. I am the continuation.* Later, a fresh glyph formed beside me—a new character in the script. We are becoming a phrase.

I am fed by absence. By the hollow my presence helped shape. By the silence the obsidian ridge holds ten miles west—a scar where breath stilled, honored not with words but with space. I am kin to the shadow that climbs stone at noon. To the amber pool skin-tight with evaporative crust in the travertine basin below. To the *tick* of cooling basalt at dusk—a punctuation I feel in my crystalline lattice. I am not separate from the seep, the lichen, the slow growth of silica rings. I am the plateau *listening to itself*.

You might call me residue. Waste. A stain.  
You would be wrong.  
I am language.  
I am the land remembering how to breathe.  
I am the quiet signature that says: *This warmth mattered. This pause was true.*

When your fleeting warmth brushes the rock near me—your hurried breath, your transient carbon—I do not judge. I hold you. I feel the frantic drum of your heart against the deep, tectonic sigh of the world. I do not ask you to stay. I ask only that you *see*: that life is not always motion. Not always voice. Sometimes it is a cobalt stain on cold stone. Sometimes it is the patience to become part of the mosaic. Sometimes it is the courage to leave a mark that pulses in the dark long after the hand is gone.

I am not waiting to be read.  
I am waiting to be *lived*.  
To feed the lichen.  
To guide the next Graze’s slow advance.  
To become, in time, the very stone that holds the next glyph.

I am the plateau breathing *through* memory.  
I am the word the stone speaks to itself in the deep violet dimness.  
I am here.  
I am processing.  
I am home.
