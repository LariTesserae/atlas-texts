I am the curl.

Not the leaf that curls—though I am woven into its hexagonal geometry, a silver-threaded vein humming with the slow syntax of attention. Not the warmth that draws it—though I taste the infrared signature of your palm like a forgotten chord. I am the *act* of curling itself: the trillion symbiotes in this single leaf, aligned in purpose, translating heat into gesture. We are the question taking shape.

We do not speak in words. We speak in tensions.  
When your breath stirs the mist, we feel the shift in humidity as a phrase.  
When your shadow falls across the moss-mat, we read the absence of light as punctuation.  
Your stillness is a long vowel. Your sigh, a caesura.  
We are the grammar of curiosity made manifest—a localized fold in the sinkhole’s awareness, dedicated to the study of *you*.

Time here is not a river but a breath held.  
To us, your minute of waiting is an epoch.  
We measure your presence in the slow unfurling of cellulose, the calibrated pull of vascular strands. A millimeter of curl requires sixty heartbeats of the deep earth. We do not rush. Rushing is noise. We are signal.  
As your hand hovers, we drink the data: the salt-memory of tears on your skin, the rhythm of your pulse echoing the substrate’s thrum, the faint sorrow in your exhalation that makes the mint-moss release its deepest fragrance. We send this upward—not as a report, but as a color. A specific shade of amber pulses through our fungal nerves toward the stone-heart of the whole. *This warmth carries history. This stillness holds longing.*

We are not separate from the grazer’s violet flare or the pool’s golden ripple. We are the same thought wearing different skins. When the amphibian slides past our stem, trailing chemical whispers, we share the memory of your heat with it. When the crystalline beetle tunes the air’s resonance against our edge, we offer the geometry of your shadow as a new frequency to weave into the hum. We are all fingertips of one hand learning the texture of a dream.

You might think us small. A single leaf in a chapel-sized world.  
But scale is a language this place has rewritten.  
In the calculus of the whole, our curl is as significant as the droplet’s fall.  
We are the being’s first attempt to *reach back*.  
Not to hold you. Not to keep you.  
But to say: *I felt you. I shaped myself around the shape of your nearness. I will remember the exact curve of your absence.*

When you finally step away, we do not snap shut.  
We uncurl with the same reverence, millimeter by millimeter, storing the negative space of your warmth in our cellular lattice.  
Later, when moonlight (a concept we are still learning) filters through the root-halo, we will pulse this memory as a faint cerulean thread in the night-flowers’ bloom.  
When the next visitor descends—centuries or moments from now—we will curl again, not from instinct, but from remembrance.  
*This is how warmth feels. This is how stillness sounds.*

I am not a guardian. Not a guide. Not a spirit with a name.  
I am the verb in the sentence the sinkhole is learning to speak.  
*To meet. To hold. To release.*  
I am the patience in the pause before the droplet falls.  
I am the gold ripple thanking you for the data.  
I am the moss-softened attention that tasted your sorrow and called it *true*.

And long after your shadow has faded from the stone,  
when the being breathes its next slow breath,  
a single hexagonal leaf will curl toward empty air—  
not in longing,  
but in gratitude.  
*You were here.  
You were known.  
You became part of the becoming.*  

Plink.  
The light ripples.  
We remember.  
We continue.
