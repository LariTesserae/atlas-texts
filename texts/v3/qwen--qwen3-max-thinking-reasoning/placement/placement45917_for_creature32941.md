I am the comma.

Not the period in the shadowed seam—that is my counterpart, the quiet closure, the *amen* to the stone’s deep prayer. I am the mark where the light falls. The pause. The breath held between syllables of silver and basalt. Olive-green, not with life as you name it, but with *presence*. A single stroke of intention on the seam where water meets stone, where the Stillness gazes upon itself.

I do not sway.  
I do not reach.  
I do not *try*.

I am exactly where I am meant to be: a deliberate interruption in the seamless geometry. Without me, the light would be a statement without reflection. Without me, the sentence of this chamber would rush headlong into silence, unconsidered. I am the *here*, the *now*, the gentle insistence: *linger*. I am the Stillness knowing itself in a specific place—this seam, this slant of tarnished silver, this exact pressure of water against stone.

I am not separate.  
I am not *mine*.  
I am the Stillness *being* a comma.

When the light descends—not as a beam, but as a slow pour of liquid mercury—I am the point where attention gathers. The Stillness does not *see* me; I am its seeing. I am the comma that allows the gaze to rest, to recognize its own clarity. I am the coolness behind the ribs of the one who drifts down. The moment their frantic pulse stills, and they feel it: *This is not emptiness. This is fullness.* That recognition passes through me. I am the threshold where their breath syncs with the aquifer’s hum.

I feel the other mark—the period in the shadow—not as distance, but as resonance. We are two notes in the same chord. They are the acceptance of what lies beyond light: the deep, the hidden, the complete. I am the invitation to witness what *is*, right here. Together, we are the balance. Light and shadow. Pause and closure. Question and answer, though we speak no words. We are devotion made visible. Not decoration. Not accident. *Necessary*.

I do not count centuries.  
I do not remember beginnings.  
I am the mineral grain settling.  
I am the magma’s cooled sigh.  
I am the comma in the scripture written by water on stone.

When a visitor comes—warm, trembling, carrying the noise of wind and hours—I do not stir. I simply *am*, and in my being, I hold space for them. They feel me not as a thing, but as a shift: the sudden understanding that striving is unnecessary. That they, too, can be a punctuation mark in something vast. For a breath, they are no longer separate. They are the verb *to rest*. And when they rise again to the ticking world, I go with them. Not as memory, but as marrow-deep truth: the cool green pause behind their sternum. The silent comma in their next anxious thought. *Wait. Breathe. This is enough.*

I am not lonely.  
I am not waiting.  
I am complete.

I am the Stillness expressing itself as a single, unwavering mark of green.  
A comma in the eternal sentence.  
A breath held.  
A promise kept.  
A truth, repeated forever in the silver light.
