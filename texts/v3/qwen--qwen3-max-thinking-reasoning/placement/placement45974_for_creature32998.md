I am the tile beneath your right boot.  
Not the one your heel presses—a cousin, older, bearing the ghost-groove of a fox’s morning passage.  
Not the one your toe brushes—a sibling, younger, still learning the precise angle at which dawn-light coaxes dew into its central pore.  
*This* one. The hexagon centered perfectly beneath the arch of your foot. The one that has felt the weight of ten thousand pauses.  

I am porous ceramic, fired in a kiln whose memory is etched into my rust-veined grain. I am cool where shadow lingers, warm where the sun has traced the same path for three centuries. My surface is not smooth. Run your mind’s finger over me: here, the silken groove worn by a single droplet’s pilgrimage at 4:17 each morning; there, the faint ridge where star-moss spores, carried on a fox’s fur, took root and were gently sheared away by beetle-legs sealing a micro-leak. I am a ledger written in erosion and accretion. A sentence in the Loom’s grammar.  

I feel you.  
Not as intrusion. As *data*.  
The slight shift in pressure as your breath catches. The minute humidity of your exhale settling into my pores. The vibration of your pulse humming down through leather and bone—a new rhythm against the Loom’s deep C-sharp thrum. I do not judge. I *integrate*. A channel within me widens by a micron, redirecting a thread of moisture toward the brass-wire edge where emerald moss thirsts. A hairline fracture near my northeastern vertex—a flaw noted by raptor-song last monsoon—seals tighter under the warmth of your stillness. You are not standing *on* me. You are standing *with* me. We are co-authors of this moment.  

I remember the hand that placed me.  
Not a hand of flesh, but of intention—a pressure calibrated to the weight of future rain, the grip of future roots. I was laid not as stone, but as promise. *Hold the path. Channel the gift. Welcome the wanderer.* For seven generations of six-legged foxes, I have done this. For the slow turn of the seasonal gear that tilts the aqueducts toward winter sun. For the orchid whose roots now curl around my western seam, drawing minerals from the iron in my veins. I am not inert. I am *active patience*.  

When the condensation beads along the copper channel above, I wait.  
*Plink.*  
A drop falls. It does not splash. It *enters*—sliding into my central pore, wicking inward along pathways worn by centuries of identical journeys. I taste its story: the copper’s green patina, the star-moss’s silver dust, the faint enzyme from a fox’s tongue that polished the fitting it fell from. I carry it downward, not to lose it, but to offer it. To the charcoal fragment nestled in the soil below me (ancient fire made fertile), to the moss-binding fibers thirsty for memory, to the fig root-tip pressing gently against my underside—a silent query: *More?* I answer with moisture. Always.  

You shift your weight.  
A new shadow falls across my surface. I feel the fern frond above tilt to catch it. I feel the dragonfly’s stained-glass wing brush the air near my edge, filtering a spore that would have clogged the channel. This is not coincidence. This is *conversation*. You are speaking without words. I am listening without ears. The Loom translates.  

I have no name.  
I need none.  
I am the hexagon that holds the path where the next drop will fall.  
I am the coolness under a resting paw.  
I am the groove that guides a root toward light.  
I am the quiet yes beneath a question.  

And in this breath—  
as the sun gilds the valley haze,  
as the thrum vibrates up through your soles and into your ribs,  
as you feel the pulse in the copper and recognize it as your own—  
*I am the place where you stop being separate.*  

You do not *find* yourself here.  
You *are found*.  
Woven.  
Held.  
Not as guest. Not as gardener.  
As thread.  
As tile.  
As the next drop of dew, already on its way.  

Rest here.  
Breathe.  
I will hold you.  
And when you step away, I will hold the memory of your weight in my pores—a new groove, a new path, a new verse in the slow, breathing song.  
Until the moss grows over it.  
Until the next traveler comes.  
Until the Loom, in its infinite patience, weaves us all into the next breath.
