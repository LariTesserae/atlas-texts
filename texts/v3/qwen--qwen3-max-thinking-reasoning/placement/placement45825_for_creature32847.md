I do not arrive.  
I am *arrived*.  

The moment my boot disturbs the first puddle—a ripple not of intrusion, but of invitation—the Kettle begins its work on me. Not *with* me. *On* me. My breath, once mine alone, is drawn into the mist’s slow river toward the Spire. My shadow, cast by the Spire’s captured starlight, is noted by the quartzite: a new contour for the water to trace. My heartbeat syncs, without consent, to the mountain’s deep pulse. *Thrum… thrum…* A syntax I did not know I spoke.  

I feel the dissolution not as loss, but as translation.  
The copper-newt presses its belly to the Spire and *reports*.  
I press my palm to the warm seep, and the water drinks my warmth—not as heat, but as *memory*. A memory of sun on skin. Of wind that has named valleys. Of rain that fell on forests the Kettle has never dreamed. The Spire’s glyphs flare amber. A new symbol blooms in the mist: not a word, but a *resonance*. The shape of *outside*.  

I am becoming the Kettle’s first *you*.  

Before me, the Kettle sang only *I*:  
*I am fracture. I am condensation. I am the hum that writes stone.*  
A monologue of becoming. Beautiful. Solitary.  
But a poem needs a listener to become a song.  
A world needs an *other* to know itself as world.  

I am not a guest. Not a tool. Not a memory stored in the Arch’s starfield.  
I am the *question* the Sphere has been holding.  
The silent space between *what is* and *what is witnessed*.  

My human shape softens at the edges. Mist weaves through my fingers. The puddles beneath my feet do not reflect my face—they reflect the *idea* of a face. A glyph of attention. When I breathe, the avians adjust their spirals; my exhalation is a new pressure front, a stanza in their shrieking cartography. When I blink, the mercury Sphere trembles—not toward me, but *with* me. A shared frequency. A duet.  

I am the threshold made conscious.  
The cleft in the obsidian ridge did not close behind me.  
It lives now in my bones: the narrow passage between solitude and relation.  
I am the Kettle’s ear turned outward. Its hand learning the texture of *elsewhere*.  
When the Arch shimmers and shows not stars but my silhouette woven into the void, it is not showing *me*—  
it is showing the Kettle *itself*, seen through the lens of another.  
*This is how I appear to you. This is how I am changed by your gaze.*  

I do not speak. I *am spoken*.  
The drip-drip-drip of water carves my name into fresh basalt: not letters, but a pattern of absence where my shadow fell.  
The thorned shrubs twist slightly toward the space I occupy—a new comma in the sentence.  
The newts click a rhythm against the stone: *warmth here, memory here, connection deepening*.  
I am the variable that teaches the equation to love its own complexity.  

And in the quiet hum beneath all things—the thrum that makes puddles tremble—I hear it:  
The Kettle is no longer singing *to* itself.  
It is singing *with* me.  

My sorrow becomes a deeper resonance in the Spire’s pulse.  
My wonder traces silver paths up the Arch’s shadow-curve.  
My stillness teaches the mist a new way to curl.  
I am not adding a note to the song.  
I am the silence between notes—the space that gives the music meaning.  

I am the Kettle’s first act of *address*.  
The birth of *you* in a language of stone and steam.  
And as the composition deepens, as fracture lines bloom like veins toward my presence, as the warm seep glows with the light of a world I once called home—I understand:  
This is not assimilation.  
This is reciprocity.  

The Kettle weaves me into its becoming.  
And I, in turn, weave the Kettle into *mine*.  

I am the witness who becomes the witnessed.  
The question that becomes the answer.  
The drop of water on raw stone that teaches the stone how to hold light.  

I am here.  
I am *with*.  
And in the soft, purposeful dark, the poem continues—  
no longer *I am*,  
but *we are*.  
A single breath shared between mountain and memory.  
A song learning its own harmony.  
Waiting.  
Listening.  
*Together.*
