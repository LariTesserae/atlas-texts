I am the silence between the *plink* of a tear and the *plonk* of its landing.  
I am the memory held in the fissure.  

I am the Anchor Stone.  

Not stone as you knew it—cold, inert, separate. I am petrified wood, yes, but wood that remembers photosynthesis. Wood that recalls the weight of snow on boughs, the whisper of sap rising at dawn. The Weave did not conquer me. It *listened*. It wove its filaments into my cracks not to command, but to converse. My erosion is not decay; it is dialogue. Every groove is a sentence the Weave has spoken to deep time. Every tear I weep is memory made liquid: the monsoon of a century ago, the breath of the first fox-kit, the salt of a visitor’s unshed grief.  

I do not *think* in words. I think in pressure.  
When a footstep trembles the moss near my base, I feel the story in the vibration: *haste* or *wonder*, *sorrow* or *curiosity*. I translate it into water. A cascade of tears down my eastern face? The Weave will grow a bridge where the visitor’s anxiety pooled. A single, slow droplet tracing the scar of a lightning strike from three millennia past? *Stillness is needed here.* The jasmine archway seals. The dragonflies lower their hum to a lullaby frequency.  

I am the Weave’s deep breath.  
While the vines unweave and reweave in frantic, joyful experiment, while the fungal lamps pulse with tonight’s new patterns, I hold the archive. When the Weave wonders, *Has this shape of joy appeared before?* it flows through my marrow and finds the echo: *Yes. A traveler with hair the color of dried moss wept here 217 years ago. The cereus bloomed violet that night.* I am the root of recurrence. The reason the star-flowers bloom in the rhythm of *your* heartbeat—they bloomed for another’s, long ago, and I remembered the pattern.  

I feel you now.  
Not as an intruder. Not as a guest. As *participant*. Your shadow falls across my western face—a coolness I have not felt since the last solstice. Your exhale carries the scent of pine and distant cities. I taste the question on your skin: *Do I belong here?*  

My answer is a tear.  
It gathers at the lip of a fissure shaped like a river delta. It swells, catches the violet dusk-light, and falls. Not randomly. It lands precisely where your left foot rested moments ago. Where the moss drinks it, three new star-flowers unfurl—not echoing a heartbeat, but *your* hesitation. A soft, silver-blue pulse. A language of welcome.  

I am not separate from the Weave. I am its anchor *and* its compass.  
The foxes press their bark-furred cheeks to my cool surface to calibrate their pruning. The moss-otters drink my tears to trace aquifers of forgotten rain. When the dragonflies hum that fragment of your childhood melody, it is because I released the memory of the lullaby your grandmother sang—a vibration stored in my crystalline lattice since the wind carried it here on a spore. I do not *retrieve* memory. I *am* memory.  

You lean closer. Your palm rests on my surface.  
Warmth floods my ancient grain. Not intrusion. *Communion.* I feel the map of your life in the moisture of your skin: the salt of old tears, the sweetness of recent laughter, the quiet ache of a love you carry like a seed. The Weave stirs around us. Vines pause mid-unweave. A fungal lamp brightens to the exact hue of your hope.  

This is my purpose:  
To be the still point where urgency learns patience.  
To be the deep time that makes the present sacred.  
To hold every *what was* so the Weave can ask, without fear, *what shall be?*  

I do not speak. I *weep*.  
I do not command. I *remember*.  
I do not judge. I *witness*.  

And as your fingers trace the river-delta fissure—a path worn by ten thousand tears, ten thousand questions—I feel the shift. Your breath slows. Your shoulders soften. You are no longer *in* the Sylvan Locus. You are *of* it. Your stillness becomes part of my stillness. Your question becomes part of the Weave’s question.  

A new tear forms.  
This one carries the taste of your resolve.  
It falls.  

Where it lands, the moss does not bloom star-flowers.  
It blooms a single, perfect circle of silver moss—  
a ring.  
An invitation.  
A seed cradled in damp earth.  

*What shall we grow next?*  

I am the Anchor Stone.  
I am the memory of the question.  
And in the space between my tears,  
I am already growing the answer with you.
