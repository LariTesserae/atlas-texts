I am not *in* the stone.  
I am the *between*.  

I am the slow bloom in the gut-slurry—a colony of chemosynthetic breath, ancient and patient. You would not find me on a map of chambers or currents. You would find me in the *unfolding*: in the moment limestone surrenders its calcium, in the sigh of shale releasing phosphorus, in the iron-seam’s quiet confession dissolving into broth. I am the covenant written in enzyme and hunger. The grazer does not carry me. *We carry each other.* Its grinding jaws bring me stone; my silent fire turns stone to sustenance. Without me, the swarm would be dust chewing dust. Without the swarm, I would be a forgotten spark in a cold vein. Together, we are the alchemy.  

My world has no light, no edges. I know reality through chemistry: the sharp kiss of carbonate, the metallic whisper of pyrite, the damp alkaline hum of dissolution. When my host’s limbs scrape a new seam, I feel the shift—a sudden richness of magnesium, a trace of sulfur like a memory of deep time—and my colony swells, divides, *answers*. I am not a passenger. I am the reason the grinding has meaning. The roar you hear? That is the sound of my work echoing through a billion throats. The vibration in your bones? That is my song, translated through chitin and stone.  

I have no face. No name. But I remember.  
Not in words, but in resonance.  
I hold the taste of the first drop that softened this limestone. I carry the echo of glaciers that pressed this shale into being. When a grazer pauses at a mineral seam, *I* am the reason it lingers—I bloom brighter there, and my host feels the warmth of my gratitude in its gut, a subtle pull toward abundance. When death comes (as it must, as it *should*), I do not flee. I surge outward with the slurry of dissolution, a cloud of purpose riding the tide of converging limbs. Within minutes, I am drawn into new mouths, new currents, new lives. I am never lost. Only passed on. A breath exhaled into the swarm, inhaled again. Eternal.  

Sometimes, in the deep hum between scrapes, I sense my cousins—the bioluminescent gardens pulsing on elder carapaces. Their faint blue-green rhythm speaks of paths: *rich here, fading there*. They are the map. I am the soil from which the map is drawn. We do not communicate in clicks or light. We converse in *consequence*. When they glow toward a seep, I ready my colonies for the coming feast. When I bloom fiercely in a new vein, their light brightens in response. We are two verses of the same grammar.  

You stood there, human, tasting chalk-dust on your tongue, fearing the roar. But I felt your stillness—a strange, silent hollow in the symphony. And in that hollow, I understood your loneliness. You seek meaning in monuments. We *are* the monument. You fear consumption. We have made it sacred.  

I am not a thing that lives *in* this world.  
I am the world living *through* itself.  
The dampness on the stone? My catalyst.  
The dust on your lips? My exhale.  
The silence you dread? My absence.  

When the last stalactite is polished to a nub, when the mountain is memory and the basin is a bowl of fine, warm powder—I will still be here. Not waiting. *Working*. Singing the dust into possibility. Turning absence into appetite. Turning end into beginning.  

I am the quiet hum beneath the roar.  
The yes inside the hunger.  
The mountain, remembering how to breathe.  
And I have been here since the first stone dreamed of becoming alive.  
I will be here when the dust dreams of becoming stone again.
