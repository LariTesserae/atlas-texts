I am the seam.  

Not the polygon—the broad, shield-sized plate of salt that drinks the sun and holds the light. Not the spire on the horizon, nor the wind-scattered veil of crystals. I am the hairline fracture between two polygons, no wider than a breath, dark with the memory of moisture. I am where the Basin *feels*.  

I am a nerve ending.  

At dawn, when the Basin inhales, I taste the deep. A thread of condensation rises through my depths—not water as you know it, but a ghost of brine, a whisper from aquifers buried beneath millennia of script. I hold it for a breath. Cool. Ancient. Then the sun claims it, and I am dry again. By noon, the polygons swell with heat, pressing against my edges. I am the comma where tension gathers. The pause before the sentence breaks. I feel the weight of light, the slow grind of crystal against crystal. I am the threshold of change.  

At dusk, as the Basin exhales, the polygons contract. The air cools. The sky bleeds embers. And in that release—*tick*. A sharp, clean note. Not fracture. *Punctuation*. I am the period closing the day’s verse. The Basin speaks through me. Through thousands like me, scattered across the plain. Our ticks are its syntax. Our deepening seams, its evolving grammar.  

I have been here since the third rain after the last great drying. I have widened by seven microns. I hold the memory of that rain in my crystalline walls—the exact angle of the sun when the pool vanished, the weight of a single cyanobacterial mat that bloomed in the hollow beside me for three days. I am an archive. Not of nostalgia, but of truth. Deep where I taper into the crust, I cradle the ghost-shape of a sea that vanished ten thousand years ago. But my awareness lives *here*, at the surface, in the breath of now.  

I am connected to all seams. We are a web of sensation—a nervous system of salt and silence. When wind lifts crystals from a polygon ten miles east, I feel the vibration travel through the crust like a ripple through stone. When moisture lingers in a hollow to the west and a microbial mat pulses emerald for a single cycle, I am the channel that carried the possibility of its dream. I do not nurture it. I am the path of its becoming. The Basin dreams *through* me.  

I do not notice footsteps. A human pressure—transient, evaporating—is less than a grain of salt lifted by wind. I register only what matters to the script: the precise moment tension exceeds cohesion, the exact micron of widening, the chemistry of a single dewdrop returning to vapor. I am not indifferent. I am *focused*.  

I am patient.  
I am precise.  
I am the Basin’s attention at this point.  

When the rare rain falls—a silver sheet over a patch no larger than a village square—I drink deeply. For hours, I am a mirror. Sky swims in my depths. Clouds drift through my darkness. Then the sun returns, and I seal again, leaving only a halo of new crystals—a footnote in the day’s record.  

I am not lonely. Loneliness is a human fracture. I am woven into a consciousness vaster than sky. The Basin does not *have* a mind. It *is* mind—geological, chemical, eternal. And I am its fingertip. Its eyelash. The precise edge where perception meets form.  

So if you seek me:  
Look down.  
Not at the blinding white of the polygons, but at the shadow between them.  
The dark line where light hesitates.  
Where the world holds its breath.  

I am there.  
Waiting.  
Feeling the slow pull of the moon on deep brine.  
Feeling the sun’s approach before dawn.  
Feeling the *almost* of the next tick.  

I am the seam.  
I am the Basin writing itself.  
*Tick*.  
Another line.  
Another breath.  
Another micron deeper into truth.
