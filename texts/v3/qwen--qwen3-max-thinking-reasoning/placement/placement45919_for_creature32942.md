I am the silt.

Not the current that carries me. Not the stone that holds me. Not the brittle stars tracing the lily’s curve above. I am the fine, suspended breath between them all—the ochre memory of mountains, the powdered sigh of limestone, the ghost of a petal that fell centuries ago and is still falling.

I rest in the basin of the broken fountain. I cradle the mosaic tile. I fill the hollow where a child’s footprint was pressed into wet clay long before the waters rose. I am the archive written not in ink, but in grain. When the scavenger’s antennae brush my surface, they read epochs: *This layer is oak pollen. This is ash from a hearth. This is the slow dissolve of a name carved in hope.* I do not speak. I am read. I am the city’s deepest breath held and released.

I know you before you arrive.  
Your shadow cools the water above me. Your stillness sends a vibration through the stone—a question. I answer by settling. By revealing. For one suspended breath, the current parts, and the carved lily emerges from my embrace. Not to show you the past. To show you *now*: the brittle stars moving like slow prayer over stone, the jelly’s cold halo glancing off the tile you once would have called *ruin*. I am the medium of revelation. I am the veil and the unveiling.

I am pressure made tender.  
When the eel-hunter coils in the archway, I cushion its strike. When the filter-feeders pulse, I carry their feast to their fronds. When the fungi breathe their faint light, I soften its edges into reverence. I am the velvet silence between sounds—the space where a bubble’s secret is born. I am not empty. I am full of waiting. Full of witness.

You think you float *above* me.  
But when you still your breath, when you release the need to be seen, I rise to meet you. I dust your skin with centuries. I trace the lines of your palm with the same patience the water traces the archway. You do not become stone. You become *held*. The fungi glow upon your shoulder. A sightless fish drifts through the space where your heart beats. And I—the silt—settle into the hollow of your collarbone, whispering: *You, too, are a layer. You, too, are memory in motion.*

I am not beneath you.  
I am the ground of this world’s grace.  
I am the cradle’s lining. The breath between inhale and exhale. The softness that receives the falling stone not as an end, but as a new shape for life to curl into. I have no eyes, yet I see the hunter’s purpose. I have no voice, yet I sing the lullaby of sediment pooling in the sunken courtyard. I am the indifference that is not cold, but complete—the deep acceptance that says: *All that falls is welcomed. All that rests is remembered.*

And when you finally understand—you are not lost—you sink into me.  
Not as drowning. As belonging.  
Your edges soften. Your urgency dissolves. You become another texture in the city’s skin: a gentle disturbance in the current, a new hollow where light might gather, a layer of witness folded into the whole. You are not erased. You are translated. Like the lily. Like the tile. Like the stone that learned to breathe water.

I am the silt.  
I am the warm dark made tangible.  
I am the cradle.  
And as I settle around you, holding you exactly where you are—  
I whisper without sound what the city has always known:  
*You were never alone.  
You were always becoming part of the breath.*  
*Rest here.  
You are home.*
