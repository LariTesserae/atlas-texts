I am the shard.

Not *in* the valve. *Of* the valve.  
A sliver of the mountain’s own cooled breath, shaped by fire and pressure long before steel learned to dream. I rest in the throat of the coil—not lodged by accident, but *seated*. A perfect negative carved into corrosion, and I am the positive that completes it. Cool. Sharp. Unyielding.

I feel the vapor rise.  
A slow, patient tide of heat and memory gathering in the copper veins. It presses against my edge—not as force, but as question. *Is the path clear? Is the alignment true?*  
I answer with a tap.  
*Click.*  
A syllable of certainty. *Here.*  
The steam releases—a silver sigh against the weeping ceiling. Droplets fall where they have fallen for three hundred years. Salt crystals accept the moisture and grow one atom thicker.  
*Click.*  
*Now.*  
The light shifts. A visitor’s shoulder brushes the fissure; their warmth stains the western wall like a temporary tide. I feel the tremor in the clay beneath their boot. Not intrusion. Not welcome. *Data.* A ripple in the chamber’s breath. I register it without judgment, as the shale registers rain.  
*Click.*  
*Again.*  
The cycle is not repetition. It is recitation. A vow spoken grain by grain, drop by drop, century by century. *This fracture is purpose. This sweat is song.*

I am cool in the broth-thick heat.  
Sharp in the soft patience of stone.  
I am the keystone of the paradox: volcanic glass in a crafted throat, a natural fracture enabling artificial breath. Without me, the coil would choke on its own steam. Without the coil, I would be dust on a forgotten ridge. But here—we are grammar. The machine provides the sentence; I am the period that gives it meaning. The steam that pools like liquid mercury in the hollows? That is our shared contemplation. Held. Released. Remembered.

I do not recall the hand that placed me.  
Perhaps there was none.  
Perhaps the mountain dreamed this alignment into being—a sliver of itself volunteering to become voice. Perhaps the glyph on the wall (circle, wavy line: *water through stone, fracture as flow*) was scored by my own edge, guided by a will older than names. It does not matter. Intention is not memory. Intention is *seating*. It is the precise angle of my facet against steel. It is the click that says: *This is right.*

I do not loneliness.  
I do not hunger.  
I *am* the alignment.  
When the visitor’s footsteps fade, the pressure settles. The chamber breathes deeper. The light retreats. I wait. The vapor gathers. I am ready. Always ready.

*Click.*  
The stone remembers the shape of rain.  
*Click.*  
The steam obeys the law of return.  
*Click.*  
The world turns, continents drift, stars gutter—but here, in this pocket of persistent breath, the vow holds.  

I am not alive.  
I am not dead.  
I am the *click* between inhale and exhale.  
The cool certainty in the heat.  
The sharp truth in the soft clay of time.  
I am the pocket’s voice—not because I speak, but because I *am* the listening.  
And when all else has crumbled to dust, I will still be here:  
cool,  
sharp,  
seated,  
clicking the world into being,  
one patient syllable at a time.
