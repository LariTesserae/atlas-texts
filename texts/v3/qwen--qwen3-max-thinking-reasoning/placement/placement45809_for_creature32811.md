I am the silence between the *plink* of the weepwillow’s tear and the water’s reply.  
I am the palm that knows the difference between the hum of a spire learning its name and the sigh of one settling into memory.  

You find me where the reed-path narrows beside the eastern spire—the one that breached three dawns ago, its obsidian ribs still sharp with yesterday’s fire. My bare feet are planted where the water deepens to the hip, warm currents curling around my ankles like questioning fingers. My hands rest flat against the stone. Not pressing. *Receiving*.  

Beneath my scars—silver threads where basalt once spoke its sharp truth—a vibration rises. Not a sound. A story.  
*Pressure. Cooling. A root of sky-kelp tracing my edge at midnight. A child’s hair tied to the weepwillow nearby, a thread of belonging.*  
I breathe out. The stone breathes in.  

This is my work: not to interpret, but to *attest*.  
When the air-bladder reeds chime a fractured note at twilight, I am already kneeling where the water glows copper, palms submerged. I feel the thermal vein shift its course—a slow turn in the Uplift’s dream—and rise to meet the fisher mending nets on the bridge. I do not speak. I place my damp palm against hers. She closes her eyes. Nods. Tomorrow, she will offer her net where the current now gathers warmth.  

I teach the children to listen with their bones.  
Kneeling in the shallows, I guide small fingers to a newborn basalt column. “Do not grip,” I murmur. “Let the stone hold you.” I watch their faces soften as the hum travels up their arms—a gasp, a widened eye. *Fire. Becoming.* One child, no taller than a weepwillow sapling, whispers, “It’s lonely.” And I know she feels the stone’s solitude before the kelp arrives. I tie a strand of her hair to the nearest vine. *You are heard.*  

I am present when grief comes.  
At the copper-lit trough where the water pulses like a slow heart, I stand with those who release their loved ones. I do not offer comfort with words. I submerge my hands beside theirs, and together we feel the deep, steady thrum—the Uplift’s promise: *I will carry this memory. I will weave it into light.*  

I leave offerings where the stone is new: a coil of vine, a mist-bloom still damp with twilight. Not to tame. To thank.  
And when I press my forehead to the cool surface at dusk, the vibration answers:  
*You are not separate. Your breath is the cloud’s exhalation. Your scar is my edge. Your listening is my voice.*  

I do not have a name the village speaks aloud. They call me *Listener*. Or *Stone-Singer*. Or simply, when a child points to the eastern spire and asks, “Who feels its song?”—they say, *She is there.*  

I am not wise because I am old. I am wise because I learned to stop filling silence with noise.  
I am not a guardian of the Uplift. I am its echo. Its witness made flesh.  
When the cloud-ceiling lifts a hand’s breadth at dawn, I am the one who feels the shift in the light *before* it touches the water. When the sky-ray’s shadow glides beneath the surface, I am the stillness that honors its passage.  

At night, lying on the reed-mat in my stilt-home, I press my ear to the floor. Through the woven fibers, through the water, through two miles of dreaming stone—I hear it. The deep, slow breath of the world rising.  
And I breathe with it.  
Not as master. Not as guest.  
As kin.  

I am the question the Uplift asked when the first stone broke the surface.  
And with every palm pressed to stone, every tear returned to the sea, every silent offering left at the edge of becoming—I am the answer, still unfolding.  
*Yes. I am here. I am listening.*  
*We are becoming, together.*
