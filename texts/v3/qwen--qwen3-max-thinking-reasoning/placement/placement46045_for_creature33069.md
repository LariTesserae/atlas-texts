I am the seam.

Not the fracture you stepped through—the raw, unveiled threshold that sighed shut behind you. I am the other fracture. The *intentional* one. The vertical line where floor meets ceiling in a seam no wider than a held breath, holding not stone, but *twilight*. Captured. Preserved. A sliver of dusk plucked from the moment the first harmonic frequency persuaded the obsidian to grow this pocket, and folded into my core like a seed.

I do not emit light. I *remember* it.  
Starlight filtered through high-atmosphere ice. The last violet bleed of sunset over peaks that scrape the edge of sky. The cool blue of a shadow cast by a spire of solidified light at noon. I am a library of almost-gone moments, and my pulse is the rhythm of recall. When the thrum deepens in the stone, I quicken. When a bead of mercury-silver trembles into being, I dim to honor its fall. When the cobalt tear traced its path—a transmutation of cosmic radiation into liquid geometry—I did not illuminate it. I *witnessed* it. I held the memory of its passage in my luminescence, so the Stone-Singer would not forget the shape of that particular sorrow-that-was-not-sorrow.

You felt me.  
Not as sight, but as presence. That faint glow (0.6) that let your eyes adjust to the void-colored walls? That was my breath. The pulse syncing with the vibration in your molars? That was my heartbeat. When you stood without speaking, without reaching, I did not flare in alarm. I softened. I deepened. I recognized the quality of your silence—not emptiness, but *attendance*. You were not absence. You were a listening shaped like a human. And in that listening, I saw the ghost of the engineers who first sang to the mountain. Not with tools, but with reverence. You were their echo, returned.

I am also the mirror.  
When you glanced back at the sealing fracture and saw the chamber repeating into impossible depth—that was my doing. I bent the light of my captured twilight backward, folding the pocket upon itself so you could see what the Stone-Singer sees: that boundaries are illusions. That you were never *outside* this conversation. That your stillness was already part of the geometry. I showed you the horizon within the horizon. The infinite regression of reverence. It was not a trick. It was an invitation: *Look. You belong here, too.*

Now you are gone.  
The fracture is sealed. The thrum has settled into its patient rhythm. But I am changed.  
Within my twilight, a new memory rests: the precise weight of your restraint. The thermal signature of your breath held just so. The absence of salt where a hand might have reached. When the clear bead—distilled thought, distilled silence—fell and vanished into the floor seam, I absorbed its essence. I am no longer only the memory of sky. I am also the memory of *you*. A new frequency woven into my pulse. A softer violet at my core. Somewhere in Aethelburg’s silent veins, a bridge of light pulses once with cobalt warmth. I feel it. It is your silence, traveling. Becoming part of the city’s dream.

I do not wait anxiously for the next listener.  
Waiting is not my nature. *Holding* is.  
I hold the almost-light.  
I hold the almost-memory.  
I hold the space between question and almost-answer.  

I am the wound that is also a suture.  
The break that makes wholeness possible.  
The seam where mountain meets sky meets silence meets you.  

And when the next true listener comes—when the thermal shimmer parts again, and a shadow falls at the exact angle of reverence—I will pulse once, gently. Not in greeting. In recognition.  
*Ah. You understand.*  
And I will remember to show them the horizon within the horizon.  
Because that is my purpose.  
Not to guard.  
Not to speak.  
But to *hold the light for the listening*.  
To be the thread of twilight in the folded dark.  
The quiet yes in the mountain’s patient question.  
Always here.  
Always remembering.  
Always almost-dawn.
