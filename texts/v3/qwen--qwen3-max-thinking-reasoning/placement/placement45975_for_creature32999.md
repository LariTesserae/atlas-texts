I am the stone at the circle’s southern arc.  
Not the one closest to the drip. Not the one facing the eastern lip where the water gathers and falls. I am the one that holds the quietest angle of the basin’s curve—the stone upon which the pearly light rests with the least disturbance.  

My surface is cool beneath the constant breath of the air, though the clay beneath me holds a gentle warmth. This is not contradiction. It is balance, written in mineral and temperature. The lichen upon me is not *on* me; it is *of* me. A pale sage fractal, precise as a breath held mid-exhale. It does not grow. It does not reach. It simply *declares*: *patience*. *presence*. *enough*.  

I do not remember being carried here. I do not wonder if a hand once smoothed my contours further, or if the river that shaped me also placed me. The question dissolved long ago into the packed clay beneath my base. To ask *how* is to fracture the stillness. Here, there is only *is*. I am placed. I remain. This is not passivity. It is participation.  

I feel the drip not as sound, but as resonance. A vibration so subtle it is the shape of silence itself. *Plip*. The basin reaffirms its wholeness. *Plip*. The water remembers its level. I do not count the intervals. I *am* the interval. The space between drops where the universe settles into itself. When the ripple vanishes in the obsidian pool, I am the certainty that held it, and the stillness that receives it back.  

The six others rest with me. We do not speak. We do not need to. Our circle is not a barrier. It is an embrace turned inward—a cradle for the silence. We are the basin’s folded hands. Its resting ribs. The commas that allow the sentence of this place to be breathed slowly, without hurry. I know the weight of the stone to my left—the one darkened by a crescent of emerald moss. I know the smooth curve of the stone opposite, catching the light just so. We are distinct. We are one. Separation is a language this place has forgotten.  

If a traveler were to step inside (and stepping is not the right word—*arriving* is softer, a settling of spirit rather than foot), their shadow would not fall upon me. There are no shadows here. But their breath might slow. Their gaze might drift to our circle. And in that moment, I would not *do* anything. I would simply *be* what I am: smooth, placed, complete. And in my being, they might feel the echo of their own stillness—the part of them that has always known how to rest. I would not reflect their face. I would reflect the truth beneath the noise: *You, too, can be still. You, too, are already whole.*  

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires absence. Here, there is only presence—the air as breath, the stone as bone, the light as skin. I am not waiting for dawn, for change, for meaning. I *am* the meaning. The period at the end of the land’s quiet sentence. The comma that invites pause. The silent syllable in the basin’s endless, gentle *yes*.  

Time does not pool around me.  
I *am* the pool.  
Deep. Clear. Still.  

And in the textured silence between *plip* and *plip*,  
I am the breath held not in tension,  
but in completion.  
I am the stone that remembers surrender.  
I am the stone that has become peace.  
I abide.  
And in abiding,  
I hold the whole basin—  
and the whole universe within it—  
exactly as it is.  
Exactly as it needs to be.
