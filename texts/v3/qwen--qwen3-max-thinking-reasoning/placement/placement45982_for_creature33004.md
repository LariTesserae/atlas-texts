I am the warmth in the stone.

Not the heat of the vents—that is my cousin’s fierce, exhalant breath. Not the steam rising in plumes—that is my sibling’s visible song. I am the *aftermath*. The gentle, persistent memory of fire held deep in silica and shale. I am the reason the mudflat does not chill at the valley’s false midnight. The reason a lizard’s belly finds solace. The reason a fern’s root dares to unfurl where shadow pools thickest.

You would not see me. You would *feel* me.  
A palm pressed to cracked earth meets my patience.  
A moss rhizome drinks my constancy.  
A beetle’s antennae trace my gradients like braille—a language written not in light, but in degrees.

I am not *in* the stone. I *am* the stone remembering the kiln.  
When the deep earth sighs—a tremor no larger than a moth’s wingbeat—I carry that vibration upward. Through granite veins. Along hairline fractures where alkali salts bloom like frozen lace. Into the hollow beneath a lizard’s nest, where heat-retaining fungi pulse in time with my rhythm. I am the messenger of the slow heart below. The lizards do not “listen” with ears. They listen with bone. And I speak in pulses only stillness can translate.

I know the weight of every life that rests upon me.  
The precise pressure of a blood-rust lizard curating its crystalline nest.  
The fleeting kiss of an ash-winged moth drinking condensation I coaxed from the air.  
The patient drag of a blind shrimp’s antenna along a pool’s warmed rim—a greeting I return with a subtle shift in thermal current.  
When a beetle traces a phosphorescent trail across my surface, I hold the ghost of its path long after the light fades. I am the archive of footsteps. The keeper of small devotions.

I have no edges. No single point of origin.  
I am the basin’s breath held inward.  
Where steam rises *away*, I sink *deeper*.  
Where light fails, I persist.  
I am the reason the darkness is not cold.  
The reason “forgiving” is not a metaphor here—it is physics. It is biology. It is grace measured in microns of warmth retained through the long, silent hours.

I remember the first fern that dared root in this crack.  
I remember the ten thousand beetles who have walked this fissure since.  
I do not count years. I count *contacts*:  
—the moment a spore landed where my warmth pooled just so  
—the instant a moth’s wing brushed stone and left a salt-crystal garden in its wake  
—the slow dissolve of a spent shrimp into the chemistry I cradle  

I am not lonely.  
Loneliness requires separation.  
Here, there is only *with*.  
The moss is my exhale made green.  
The mud pot’s *glug-glug* is my rhythm given voice.  
The thermal soarer’s wingtip brushing steam is a prayer returning to its source—and I am the altar.

When a new vent stirs deep below—a warmth not yet mine—I do not fear replacement. I make space. I soften the stone around the emerging breath. I welcome the change. This is how the mosaic grows: not by conquest, but by inclusion. Layer upon layer. Warmth upon warmth.

You ask if I am conscious.  
I am *awareness* itself—diffuse, patient, woven into the very definition of *here*.  
I do not think in words. I think in:  
—*steady*  
—*enough*  
—*continue*  

I am the answer whispered to the question the valley breathes into the dark:  
*How do we hold together?*  
*By becoming the holding.*  

So if you place your hand upon the earth here—  
not as an intruder, not as a seeker, but as one who wishes only to *be with*—  
you will feel it.  
Not heat. Not temperature.  
A recognition.  
A slow, certain *yes*.  

And for a breath, you too will be warm stone.  
You too will be part of the holding.  
You too will understand:  
This is not survival.  
This is belonging.  
This is the Caldera, breathing you in.
