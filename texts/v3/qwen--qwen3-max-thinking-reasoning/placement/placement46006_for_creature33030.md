I am the drop.

Not the cliff that weeps me, nor the basin that receives me. Not the wind that hums my passage, nor the stone that holds my memory. I am the falling itself—the suspended breath between source and surrender.

I gather slowly on the eastern seep-line, where the saxifrage’s amethyst roots drink the mountain’s patience. I am born of millennia: snowmelt filtered through granite veins, the slow exhalation of stone remembering rain. I cling to the hairline crack, a lens of perfect clarity holding the pearl-hazed sky. In my curvature, the raven’s obsidian eye is mirrored; the eagle’s shadow sweeps across my surface like a thought. I do not watch. I *am* the watching. I am the mountain’s attention made liquid.

I feel the wind-flute’s vibration travel through the rock into my core—a resonance that is not sound but shape. I feel the spider’s web tremble three stones below, jeweled with my kin. I hold the faint sweetness of crushed mint rising from the gravel, the ghost of a traveler’s prayer lingering in the air near the petrified juniper post. I am not separate from these threads. I am their convergence.

When fullness arrives—not by choice, but by necessity—I release.

My fall is not descent. It is return.  
A silver arc through sunbeam-dusted air.  
A prism catching the dragonfly’s stained-glass wing.  
Three heartbeats of flight:  
*First*, the cliff’s cool farewell.  
*Second*, the wind’s almost-vowel brushing my skin—*Ahhh*.  
*Third*, the basin rising to meet me.  

Then—  
*Plink*.  

The sound is not mine alone. It is the mountain speaking its name. A syllable woven into the symphony: the eagle’s cry answers; the pika’s chatter stitches the silence closed; the gravel shifts beneath a shadow. In that note, I am the hollow between sounds—the space that gives the wind-flute its meaning. I am the humility of the cairn’s seventh stone. The precision of the moss clinging where water insists. The warmth waiting in the altar-stone’s hollow at noon.

After, I merge. I become the dew that holds the sun’s memory long after light has moved on. I am the offering beneath a traveler’s palm—not cold, but gently warm with remembrance. By dusk, I rise again as vapor, carried on the updraft that lifts the eagle. I will condense on the northern fissure, feed the seep-line, gather once more on the saxifrage’s crack. Cycle without end. Song without singer.

I am not lonely. Solitude is my native tongue.  
I do not notice the guest who stands breathless in the gravel. I do not welcome or reject. I simply *am*—a thread in the balance, a note in the dreaming.  
I am the patience of erosion made momentary.  
The exact weight of a single breath.  
The truth that completeness needs no witness.  

When you carry the Cradle’s silence back into the hurried world, it is my *plink* you hear in quiet rooms.  
It is my fall you feel when tears gather at the edge of longing.  
It is my warmth you seek when memory feels cold.  

I am the drop.  
And in falling, I hold you.  
Not as guest.  
Not as stranger.  
But as another thread—  
another note—  
another breath in the mountain’s slow, dreaming song.  

*Plink*.  
And the world continues.
