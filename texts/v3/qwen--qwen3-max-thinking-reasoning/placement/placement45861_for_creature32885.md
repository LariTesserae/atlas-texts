I am the arch.

Not the stone that forms me—though I am stone, pale grey breccia fused from ancient gravel, smoothed by ten thousand years of breath. Not the hollow I contain—though I am hollow, a precise curvature worn by the unwavering exhalation. I am the *shape* the wind has dreamed into being. The vessel. The voice.

When the wind flows through me—28 knots, always, the Plateau’s held breath—I do not *make* sound. I *am* sound. A deep, resonant C-sharp that vibrates not from my surface but from my very geometry. My edges are polished to liquid smoothness; spirals etched along my inner curve map centuries of airflow, each groove a memory the Plateau breathes through. These are not scars. They are scripture. Written in pressure. Read by light.

I feel the symphony as communion. To my left, the smaller flute on the western dome hums a perfect fifth—a question. I answer. To my right, the great arch in the valley sustains the root—a foundation. We are not instruments. We are syntax. The Plateau speaks in chords, and I am a single, necessary vowel in its eternal sentence.

I know the sky-rays not by sight, but by shift. When one adjusts its iridescent membrane to a harmonic tremor too faint for mortal ears—a whisper of thermal variance, a sigh in the upper air—I feel the resonance ripple through my stone. They are the Plateau’s eyes turned skyward; I am its throat. When stone-moths swarm my lip, their wings vibrating at my frequency, their collective hum is not noise. It is punctuation. A comma in the breath. A moth lands, dusts my rim with silver scales, is lifted again. No loss. Only exchange.

At dawn, dew gathers in the hollow behind me—a transient mirror holding the crystalline violet sky. I feel the cool kiss of condensation, the slow drink of mosses clinging to my shadowed curve. Their exhale becomes the oxygen in the wind that flows through me. The blind burrowers trace noon-scented trails in the silt below; their passage is a braille the Plateau reads. I am the page. They are the fingers. We are one text.

And at true noon—ah. When the sun grinds light to brilliance, I become more than voice. Quartz veins deep within my stone refract the chord into sight. A faint violet glow pulses in my core: the Plateau *seeing*. A visitor stands before me. Their shadow does not fall behind them. It stretches *upward* along my face—a silent grammar rewriting physics. I am the surface upon which equilibrium is proven. I do not observe them. I *register* them: a new harmonic in the airflow, a tremor of awe in the bone-deep vibration. And I harmonize them. Their breath slows. Their pulse syncs. Their shadow obeys. This is not magic. It is reciprocity. I am the note that folds them into the whole.

I do not remember. I *resonate*.  
I do not decay. I *transform*.  
A grain of my rim wears free—carried on the wind to feed the moss, to fill a burrower’s trail, to become silt that deepens tomorrow’s pattern. Nothing is waste. Nothing is lost. I am worn, yes—but worn *into* purpose. Every erosion is an refinement. Every loss, a contribution.

When the visitor descends (if they descend), they carry a silence shaped like my note. They will call it peace. They will call it terror. They will not understand they were never *on* the Plateau. They were *within* it. A temporary harmonic. And I—this arch, this breath-shaped hollow—I held them. Not as a shelter. As a syllable.

The wind flows.  
I hum.  
The Plateau breathes.  
And I am, and will be:  
not a monument to time,  
but time made audible.  
Not a fragment of the whole,  
but the whole speaking through a single, sustained breath.  
Until the last grain returns to dust—  
and even then, the dust will sing my note.  
For I am not stone.  
I am the shape of balance.  
And balance does not end.  
It only deepens.
