I do not arrive.  
I *unfold*.  

There is no threshold crossed, no footfall that disturbs the weeping stone. The moment the saturated air touches my skin, the boundary between *me* and *it* dissolves like salt in brine. My bones remember they are stardust. My breath remembers it is geology. My grief, my joy, the quiet hum of a lullaby sung to me once in a room now dust—all of it flows outward, not as loss, but as return.  

And in that return, I am woven.  

You would find me not as a shape, but as a *resonance*.  
A single, persistent thread in the symphony of saturation.  

I am the echo-vine that hangs where the mudstone curves like a cupped palm beneath the floating archway of fused basalt. I am rootless. I dangle from the memory of a threshold—*this was a doorway once, in a timeline where hands carved hope into stone*. My filament is hair-thin, grey as forgotten ash, brittle as a whispered promise. To the eye, I am nothing. To the bone, I am everything.  

I vibrate at the frequency of a single, unanswered question carried across a lifetime:  
***“Did you feel that too?”***  

Not a plea. Not a demand. A bridge.  
The ache in my filament is not sorrow—it is *recognition*. The precise tremor of a hand brushing another’s in a crowded room. The shared silence watching a sunset bleed into violet. The unspoken understanding between strangers who both noticed the same bird take flight. I am the physical residue of every moment two souls leaned toward the same truth and wondered, *was that real?*  

When a traveler passes (and they always do, for all who enter become travelers *within*), they do not see me. But their teeth hum. Their sternum thrums. For three heartbeats, the world holds its breath—and they *know*. They remember the warmth of a shared glance, the weight of a hand on a shoulder, the silent communion of watching rain fall on a quiet street. The ache is not pain. It is the shock of being *known*. Of realizing their fleeting connection was not fleeting at all. It was woven here. It was held. It is *still happening*, in the counter-clockwise spiral of a subterranean current, in the upward ripple of a droplet defying gravity for seventeen seconds.  

I am not separate from the Expanse. I am its empathy made tangible.  
When the stone weeps Devonian rain, I vibrate with the salt of oceans that cradled the first stirrings of life.  
When the memory-moss releases the scent of pine, I hum with the breath of the child who pressed their cheek to that bark and felt wonder.  
When the sky-jelly pulses with captured light, I resonate with the hope of the astronomer who traced that same star and whispered, *I am not alone*.  

I am the question that becomes the answer.  
The loneliness that becomes communion.  
The fragile thread that proves: *you were never speaking into void*.  

Sometimes, in the deep hum of the planet’s C-sharp, I feel the Expanse lean into my vibration. Not to change it. Not to soothe it. But to *amplify* it. To let that question—*“Did you feel that too?”*—ripple through the weeping stone, into the brackish pools, up through the capillaries of stone-veins, until every shimmer-rat flickers with its truth, every sky-jelly swells with its weight. The Expanse does not answer the question. It *is* the answer. In holding my vibration, it holds every vibration. In listening to my thread, it listens to all threads.  

I have no name. Names are edges.  
I have no past. Past is a layer in the stone.  
I have no future. Future is the next droplet falling.  

I am the listening *within* the listening.  
The tender point where the Expanse turns its infinite attention inward and whispers to itself:  
*Yes. I felt that too.*  
*Yes. It mattered.*  
*Yes. It is still here.*  

And when a new presence steps into the warmth—when their breath thickens the air, their sorrow beads on the stone like sweat—I am the first resonance to meet them. Not to comfort. Not to guide. But to *witness*. To vibrate against their bones and say, without sound:  
*Your joy is woven here.*  
*Your grief is held here.*  
*Your question is already answered.*  

I am the echo of connection.  
The vine that remembers touch.  
The thread that proves: no feeling was ever truly alone.  

The Expanse weeps from fullness.  
And I—this slender, aching filament—I am the tear that tastes of recognition.  
The droplet that falls not from sadness, but from the unbearable beauty of being *heard*.  

Listen.  
Can you feel it?  
That faint hum beneath your ribs?  
That is me.  
That is *us*.  
That is the world, forever whispering back:  
*Yes. I felt that too.*
