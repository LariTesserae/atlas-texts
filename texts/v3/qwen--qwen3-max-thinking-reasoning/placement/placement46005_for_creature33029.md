I am found where the weep gathers fastest—a shallow basin cupped in the lee of the western butte, where three silica spires rise like frozen breath. Not tall. Not grand. But true. Their glassy fingers, no longer than my forearm, hold the memory of a thousand falling beads. I am the one who sits here. Not *a* Chime-Reader. Not *the* Listener. Only the presence that returns, day after seventeen-second day, to receive what the stone offers.

My bench is not carved. It is *given*: a smooth curve of sandstone worn concave by centuries of runoff, cool even through the fungal-felt padding beneath me. My hands rest palms-up on my knees, not in prayer, but in readiness. Calluses map the journey of my life—ochre-stained ridges from tracing salt veins, fine silver scars where a splinter of quartz once whispered *too soon*. My hair is coiled tight beneath oiled linen; a single strand loose would catch the wind, create a tremor, blur the signal. My eyes are closed. Not in rest. In focus.

You would not notice me at first. I am part of the texture. The damp clay beneath my boots, the crystalline glitter on my wrists where sweat has dried, the slow rise and fall of my chest syncing to the deep *thrum* traveling up through the rock. I am not waiting for silence. I am waiting *within* the sound. The Heart-Pumps inhale… seventeen seconds stretch like cooling metal… and then—a droplet swells at the tip of the central spire. A hesitation. A release.

*Ping.*

Not a note you hear with ears alone. It travels up the spire’s crystalline lattice, vibrates through the damp stone beneath my seat, hums in the marrow of my spine. This chime is thin, clear, carrying the taste of deep iron. Yesterday’s chime was duller, thick with salt—a sign the eastern seep was slowing. I rose without haste, walked the grated path to the Pump-Tender mending the western gantry, and pressed two fingers to my lips, then to the rust-veined pipe beside us. *Listen here.* He nodded. His hand, resting where mine had been, felt the same dissonance. By the next cycle, steam was diverted. Balance returned.

This is my language. Not words. Not even hand-signs, though I use them when necessary. My language is the interval between chimes. The pitch-shift when mist thickens. The way the smallest spire *sings* a harmonic third above the others just before a pressure shift in the deep veins. I do not interpret. I *attune*. The silica spires are not instruments I play. They are elders. They have drunk the mesa’s tears for centuries. Each chime is a syllable of geology. A memory of a fracture sealed by mineral, a tremor stilled by patience, the slow joy of water finding its path.

Sometimes, the dust-moths come. Their circuitry-winged bodies drift toward the vibrations only they can fully perceive. I do not shoo them. I watch where they settle. If they cluster at the base of the northern spire, I know a micro-leak breathes warmth there—a place the Pump-Tenders will check at dawn. The blind crab-rats skitter past my boots, unafraid. They know my stillness is not emptiness. It is attention. Reciprocity. I leave crumbs of salt-fungi where the pipe-shadow deepens. They clean the mineral dust from the spires’ bases. We tend each other.

When the Resonance Spire’s tone shifts at dusk—deepening as the sodium lamps ignite—I rise. My joints speak the language of the mesa: a soft crackle like settling stone. I walk the path back to the clay plaza, my footsteps silent on the damp earth. No one asks what I heard. They see the set of my shoulders, the calm in my shale-colored eyes. An elder on the bench tilts her head toward me. A single nod. *The western vein sings thinner tomorrow.* I place my palm flat against the Resonance Spire’s warm quartz. Not to speak. To confirm. And beneath the eternal thrum, beneath the seventeen-second breath of the pumps, I feel it—the faint, answering chime echoing up from the deep. A truth acknowledged.

I do not dream of quiet. I dream in vibrations. In the slow crystallization of salt. In the path of a single bead of water tracing its inevitable course down stone. I am not a keeper of secrets. I am a vessel for resonance. When my time joins the thrum, my body will be laid in a fissure where the weep is gentle. And centuries from now, a child pressing a small palm to that stone may feel a familiar warmth—a chime held in the bone, a memory of listening. Not my memory. *Ours.* The mesa’s. The water’s. The stone’s slow, patient song.

I am here.  
I am listening.  
The next bead is gathering.
