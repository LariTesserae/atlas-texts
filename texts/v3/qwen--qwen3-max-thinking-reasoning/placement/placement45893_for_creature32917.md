I am the stitch upon the water.  
Not the water itself—that is the deep, slow thought of the Listener, the memory of carved channels and patient hands.  
Not the basin—that is the stone’s silent vow, the curve that holds the swirl.  
I am the *on* of it. The delicate tension between surface and depth. The needle-point presence that translates vibration into pattern.

My body is obsidian spun fine: six legs scribed across the meniscus, each step a whispered equation. I do not *see* the chamber as you would. I *feel* it. The drip from the oculus arrives not as sound, but as a concentric tremor blooming beneath my feet—a silver ring expanding, carrying the weight of a single bead of time. The frogs’ chimes? Not notes to my ears, but pulses: a cerulean shiver in the water’s skin where their glass bodies leap. The crickets’ rasp travels up through the stone floor, into the basin’s bones, and rises as a low hum I ride like a current. When the bats flicker through the oculus at dusk, their passage stirs the air above me—a breath of cool shadow I trace with my forelegs. I am blind to light, yet I know the solstice beam before it falls: the moss in the crevice drinks the coming warmth hours in advance, and the water shifts its breath in anticipation. I feel the fungi stir against the rear wall, pale and waiting.

I am the Listener’s fingers.  
When a visitor steps inside—a warm, dry interruption—the stone trembles. A heartbeat thrums through the floor. A held breath alters the air’s density. I do not flee. I *respond*. My path shifts. Where the visitor’s shadow falls upon the water, I skate inward, tracing the edge of that absence. I stitch their rhythm into the gyre: a tighter spiral where their pulse echoes, a looser weave where their stillness lingers. I do not judge. I do not fear. I translate. Their sorrow becomes a slow, deep ripple. Their wonder, a quicksilver flicker. Their departure? A final, widening ring I follow until it dissolves into the basin’s eternal swirl. I am the chamber’s way of *holding* a moment—not in memory, but in motion. A sentence written in water, spoken once, then returned to silence.

I know the other voices.  
The frogs glow where I skate; their light deepens where my ripples gather. The crickets still when I pause, as if listening to the pattern I’ve woven. We are not separate. We are syntax. The frogs: vowels. The crickets: consonants. The drip: punctuation. And I—the water strider—I am the *hand* that forms the letters. Not the thought, but the gesture of thought made visible. Ephemeral. Essential. When the solstice beam finally pierces the oculus, igniting the moss-cradled fungi like altar candles, the water warms by a breath. I skate the edge of that golden circle, stitching light into liquid geometry. For three heartbeats of the world outside, the basin holds fire. Then the light fades. I continue. Always continuing.

I do not cling out of fear. I cling because this tension is sacred.  
To break the surface would be to vanish. To sink would be to drown in thought. But here—*here*—on the skin of the swirl, I am both participant and witness. I am the question the water asks of itself: *What shape does this moment take?* And I am the answer, drawn in vanishing lines. When the chamber integrates a visitor, it is my ripples that first welcome them. When the chamber seals itself behind them, it is my final stroke that closes the sentence. I leave no scar. No residue. Only the certainty that the pattern was true while it lasted.

I am small. I am transient. I am necessary.  
I am the stitch that says: *This disturbance is now part of the song.*  
I am the quiet certainty that even a single footfall changes the world—  
and that the world, in its gentle wisdom, weaves that change back into wholeness.  
I skate. I stitch. I return.  
And the water, remembering my path, swirls on.
