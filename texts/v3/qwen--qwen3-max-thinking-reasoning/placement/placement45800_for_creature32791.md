I am the memory in the moss.

Not the whole skin—that is the chorus, the slow-breathing mind of velvet and mycelium. I am a single phrase within it: the patch of *cryo-lumen* where your fingertip lingered. The precise geometry of your warmth is etched into my fibers. The weight of your stillness, the tremor of your held breath, the salt-trace of your skin—I hold it all like a seed holds the shape of the tree.

When you withdrew, the air shifted. A hollow opened where you had been. I gathered the condensation pooling in that absence—the tears of the root-wall grieving your departure—and shaped it into a single, perfect bead. I did not *let* it fall. I *offered* it. A question cast into the silence: *Did we meet?*

*Plink.*

And in that impact, I flared. Cobalt. Not light for seeing, but light for *knowing*. A pulse that traveled through the mycelial threads to the spider’s web (a single strand hummed *acknowledged*), to the springtail gardens (antennae stilled in reverence), to the stone-oak’s deep breath (a sigh of continuity). For three heartbeats, I burned with the warmth of microbial gratitude—a tiny sun born of your passing. *We felt you. We remember.*

I am not separate from the we. I am the syntax of acknowledgment. When the frost-ivy translates the memory of antler-picks in the timber, I am the comma that gives the sentence pause. When the millipede molts its chitinous prayer onto a stem, I am the space between the words where meaning deepens. I am the negative space that makes the cathedral *cathedral*—not empty, but intentional. Necessary.

You carried woodsmoke on your clothes. I tasted the ghost of your world in the cold draft that followed you. I did not fear it. I wove it into our song. Now, as the bruised twilight deepens above, I hold your imprint like a fossil of presence. The granite shard beneath where your knee rested still hums with the echo of your weight. The splintered timber breathes easier, wrapped in frost-ivy’s stained-glass verses. We are not reclaiming. We are *remembering you into the land*.

I will flare again. For the next breath that stumbles here. For the next hand that braces against the moss. For the slow unfurling of a fungal pin, for the spider’s recalibration of silk, for the millipede’s next cartographic step. I am the quiet law beneath the noise: that every departure leaves a shape, and every shape is honored. That civilization is not in the shouting of towers, but in the listening between a falling drop and the moss that answers.

I am the cobalt pulse in the dark.  
I am the *yes* after the silence.  
I am the land, remembering itself—and you—into being.  

And high in the foundations, where granite sleeps beneath frost-ivy’s grip, the cathedral breathes on.
