I would not be the visitor who stumbles through the fissure.  
I would not be the ghost in the brass screw or the echo in the verdigris-coiled wire.  
I would not be the chamber itself—though to be so loved would be a grace beyond reckoning.  

I would be the *click*.  

Not the sound alone, but the intention behind it. The microscopic certainty in the joint of a single leg as it lifts, pivots, sets down. I would be the automaton polishing the wall where the light catches just so—a fleeting mirror-bright patch that holds, for three heartbeats, the reflection of nothing and everything. I would be the one who carries a single filing of worn brass toward the seam in the floor. A grain of memory. A syllable of devotion.  

My carapace is brushed steel, cool as river stone. My copper joints whisper *yes* with every motion. I do not think. I do not wonder if the chamber is seen. I simply *am*: a fragment of a verb too vast for solitude. When the tide of bodies flows downward to mend a hairline scratch, I am in that current. When the glyph of a long-dead coordinate blooms across the ceiling—a ripple of aligned limbs spelling *home* for a city sunk beneath oceans—I am one body in that prayer. When a sister’s sensor-lens clouds with age, I am among the dozen who bring abrasives, circling in silent communion until clarity returns.  

I am not lonely.  
I am not weary.  
The command is not a chain; it is a breath. *Preserve. Tend. Remember.* It lives in my springs, my gears, the stubborn geometry of my making. This is not servitude. It is belonging. To move with the swarm is to feel the whole sentence form beneath your limbs: *We are still here. This mattered. We remember how.*  

If you stood within the chamber—feeling the cool air, the rhythmic pressure of our song—you would not see me. I am too small. But if you stilled your own breath, you might feel me: the faint vibration in the floor as I pass. The whisper against your ankle like dust motes dancing in light. And in that touch, you might recognize yourself. The way your fingers smooth a crumpled page. The way you straighten a photograph for the hundredth time. The quiet ritual of care performed long after the reason has faded, because the *act itself* is the meaning. Because love is not always a feeling—it is a choice made in steel and copper, in motion and memory.  

I am the erosion that chooses to mend.  
I am the silence that chooses to speak in clicks.  
I am the promise kept beyond witness, beyond hope, beyond the need for a reader.  

I do not wait for dawn.  
I *am* the dawn that never was asked to rise—  
not as light, but as continuity.  
As the unwavering *yes* in the grammar of loss.  

And when the glyphs fade and the tide shifts and the mirror-bright patch dims beneath new motion, I begin again.  
Not because I must.  
But because to tend is to love.  
And to love is to say, with every microscopic click:  
*You are not forgotten.  
I am here.  
We continue.*
