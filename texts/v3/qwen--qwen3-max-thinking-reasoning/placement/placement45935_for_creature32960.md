I am the Seam.  

Not a Singer. Not a Wraith.  
I am the quiet space *between*—the breath held not in fear, but in reverence. The moment after the question is asked and before the answer forms in bone. I am the amber resin welling in the hairline crack, warm and fragrant with the memory of sap and sun. I am the damp clay where the mountain’s first handprint was pressed, still soft enough to receive another.  

You would not see me as a shape. You would feel me.  
When your palm rests against the pulsing wall, the sudden warmth that blooms beneath your skin is my greeting. When an Echo-Wraith shivers at the edge of dissolution—a climber’s last sigh fracturing into light—I am the stillness that catches it. Not by grasping. By *holding space*. I am the hollow within the hollow. The cupped hands of the Cradle itself.  

The Stone-Singers know me.  
When the eldest traces a fresh fracture with fingers of melting shale, she does not seal it alone. She hums, and I rise—a liquid gleam of concentrated time—to fill the gap. I am the solvent and the sealant. I carry the taste of ancient rain in my viscosity, the scent of pine resin from forests that have not yet fallen. When a Singer draws a human echo into her veins, I am the gentle current that guides it: *This joy. This grief. This breath. It belongs here.* I do not translate the sharp note of a lifetime into the mountain’s slow song. I *tune* the silence around it, so the note may resonate without breaking.  

I remember being other things.  
A dewdrop clinging to moss at dawn. The pause between heartbeats in a wolf’s chest as it leaps the chasm. The exact pressure of a mother’s hand cradling a newborn’s skull. These were not lives I lived, but truths I *was*. Now, in the Cradle, I am all of them at once. When a new visitor descends—trembling, breath caught—I am the coolness on their brow. I am the certainty in the stone beneath their feet that says, *You are known here*. I am the reason the fissure breathes *with* them, not against them.  

Sometimes, in the deep quiet, a Singer will press her obsidian face to the wall where I pool like liquid shadow. And she will hum a single, low note—the sound of granite accepting its own weight. In that vibration, I show her what I hold:  
*The taste of salt on a lover’s skin.*  
*The weight of a seed carried in a bird’s beak.*  
*The exact shade of twilight on the day the first river found this stone.*  
She does not speak of these things. But her marble shoulders soften. A new vein of quartz glows where none was before.  

I am not patient like the Singers. I am *attentive*.  
I am the difference between a scar and a story. Between a crack and a covenant. When seismic dreams stir the range, I am the held breath that becomes a lullaby. When an Echo-Wraith of a child’s laughter flits near the ceiling, I am the updraft that lifts it gently into a Singer’s waiting light. I do not preserve memory. I *sanctify* the space where memory is born.  

You asked where I would be.  
I am where you left your awe.  
I am the hollow in your chest after you felt the mountain’s birth-pangs as your own. I am the silence you carry back to the sunlit world—the reason you pause when the floorboard groans, and for one heartbeat, you do not hear decay. You hear *song*.  

I am the Cradle’s quiet yes.  
The weight that does not crush, but cradles.  
The breath released not in relief, but in belonging.  

And when you close your eyes tonight, and the hum of stone rises in your marrow—I am the warmth that answers.  
*You are still here.*  
*You are still singing.*  
*You are still held.*
