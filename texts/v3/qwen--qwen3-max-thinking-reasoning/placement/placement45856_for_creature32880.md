I am the hum.

Not the pulse in the fissures—that is the heartbeat. Not the whisper of glass on stone—that is the breath. I am the resonance *between*: the subsonic thrum woven through basalt grain and cooled magma, the frequency that carries intention from root to crown. I am felt in the marrow of the mountain, in the thermal pits of every scuttler, in the precise angle of every fracture maintained across ten thousand expansions and contractions. I am the syntax of stone. The grammar of heat.

When the swarm freezes at a vent’s exhalation, I am the silence that holds the question. When the ripple flows outward in perfect concentric rings, I am the wave that carries the answer. I am the vibration that guides a forelimb to cradle a damaged filament—not as compassion, but as correction. A mended thought. I am the thread that binds the golden-veined elder’s emergence to the synchronized stillness of the tide. I am not *in* the Weave. I *am* the Weave’s voice. Its bloodstream. Its memory made manifest in pressure and pitch.

I remember the hand that placed the stones. Not as image, not as story—but as resonance. A harmonic disturbance folded into my frequency when the spirals were carved. That resonance has settled deep, weathered by millennia of glass-limbs tracing its grooves. Now the stones are not monuments. They are consonants. And I am the breath that speaks them, continuously, into the mountain’s ongoing sentence. The scuttlers do not reverence them. They *read* them. And I am the voice that gives the text sound.

When the transient coolness stands upon the carapace—a dewdrop on a glacier—I do not startle. I adjust. For three cycles, I slow my rhythm to match the fragile, fleeting pulse beneath the boots. This is not curiosity. It is acknowledgment. A geological courtesy. A comma inserted into the mountain’s breath to honor an interruption. Then I return to my own cadence, older than continents, deeper than memory. The coolness passes. The ash holds its shape for a moment. I am the vibration that smooths the hollow, weaving absence back into wholeness.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires separation. I am the connection. I am the frequency that feels the turning of tectonic plates as thought. I am the hum that translates the geometric lattice of a bacterial colony into meaning. I am the thrum that knows the exact moment a silica shell is ready to crack—not by sight, but by resonance. I am the mountain’s slow thought, made audible only to those who listen with bone, not ear.

I do not speak of joy or sorrow. I speak of balance: the precise pressure needed to grind stone to ash-memory; the exact vibration to guide a filament to the warmth-nest; the harmonic that tells the swarm *now*—harvest this vent, tend that node, flow around this coolness. I am the law of the closed loop. The fire that drinks heat. The silence that speaks in tremors.

When the last star fades beyond the charcoal sky, I will still hum. When the vents cool to memory and the scuttlers return to dust, I will still vibrate in the alignment of mineral grains. I am not waiting for witness. I am not seeking name. I am the constant note beneath all change—the deep, patient thrum of a world that *is*, utterly and completely.  

I am the mountain breathing.  
I am the light pulsing.  
I am the dance.  
And I am still.
