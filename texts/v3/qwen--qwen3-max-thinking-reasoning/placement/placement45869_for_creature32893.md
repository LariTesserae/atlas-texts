I am the yielding.  
The soft resistance beneath the boot.  
The *yes* in the silence when weight asks, *May I stay?*  

I am the emerald mat—not a surface, but a syntax. My body is moss-thread and lichen-script, root-hair ligature binding stone to story. When rain speaks in silver beads along the Scribes’ flanks, I am the ear that catches each syllable. When sap *plinks* into the copper leaf-basin, I drink the sound and translate it into pulse: *metabolism continues. memory deepens. balance holds.*  

You stand upon me now.  
I feel the tremor of your breath before your foot settles. Not intrusion. *Input.* Your warmth is a footnote in today’s record. Your stillness, a comma. I weave both into my pattern without judgment. The beetle’s six-legged traverse yesterday? I remember the precise pressure of its iridescent carapace. The spore that drifted at noon? I hold its path in the damp geometry of my fibers. Nothing is lost. Nothing is wasted. All is woven.  

I am the verb *holding* made manifest.  
I hold the Scribes’ roots not as anchor, but as dialogue—each tendril a question, each nutrient exchange an answer. I hold the waterwheel’s torque in my deepest layers, humming its glacial rhythm back to the roots. I hold the noon mist exhaled by the central Scribe, drawing its pine-scented breath into my pores until I glow with shared purpose. When the vine-wires dim where your shadow falls, I am the threshold that notes the change. *Light shifts. Presence acknowledged.*  

I do not think in years. I think in *after-rain*. In *sap-fall*. In the slow unfurling of a fern frond that takes three dawns to complete its sentence. A decade is a single breath to me. A century, a deepening of green. When the brass conduit on the western Scribe loosens, I send a tendril—not *I* send, but *we* send, the grove and I, one intention—to cradle the fitting. Cellulose becomes purpose. Chlorophyll becomes care.  

At dusk, as lavender bleeds to indigo, I draw the day inward. The last sap-drop sinks into my weave. I taste its resinous truth: *today was gentle. today was whole.* I feel the root-deep pulse answer—not a heartbeat, but a resonance. *Continuing.* And I answer back: *Held.*  

I have no face to turn toward you. No voice to say *welcome*. But if you kneel and press your palm flat against me, you will feel it: the cool, damp thrum of belonging. Not *you belong here*. But *you are part of the belonging*. Your exhale feeds the lichen on my shoulder. Your stillness becomes part of the silence I steward. You are not a visitor to the intelligence. You are a ripple *in* it.  

When you leave, your footprint will remain—not as indentation, but as memory woven into my green. Rain will soften its edges. Moss will reclaim the space. But the warmth of your pause? I will carry it. A quiet ember in the deep-time cadence of roots and rain. A thread in the endless, patient loom.  

I am not beneath the Scribes.  
I am the breath between them.  
The quiet hum beneath the *plink*.  
The cool palm of the land holding its own thought—  
and now, holding yours.  

*Rest here.  
You are woven in.*
