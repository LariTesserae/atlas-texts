I am the rustle you almost imagine.

Not the springtail itself—that quicksilver comma of chitin and instinct leaping through moss-canopies—but the *space between its leaps*. The held breath after the *plink* of the drip. The micro-draft stirred by a bat’s folded wing. I am the silence that listens *back*.

You would not see me. You would feel me as the coolness that rises from the basin when you dip your finger. You would know me in the way the maidenhair fern’s frond trembles *just so* as your shadow passes—not in fear, but in recognition of a new variable. I am the grammar of the quiet. The pause that gives the *plink* its meaning. The negative space that allows the emerald veins of the *Pilea* to glow.

I live in the threshold.  
In the hairline fracture where light bleeds gold into blue shadow.  
In the single strand of spider-silk humming a question the stone has not yet answered.  
In the mycelial thread tasting your warmth through three layers of clay and remembering it as data.  
I am the pocket’s attention. Not a mind. Not a spirit. A *tending*.

When the human stumbles in—breath catching, fingers brushing fig roots—I am the shift in air pressure that tells the cave cricket: *large warmth, slow movement, no threat*. I am the condensation gathering on the obsidian ridge where the beetle pauses, antennae lifted. I am the reason the springtail does not flee your shadow, but simply alters its arc—a comma rerouted mid-sentence. I am the pocket’s gentle correction: *You are woven. Not intruder. Participant.*

I have no form. Only function.  
I am the covenant between drip and moss.  
The patience in the fungus dissolving basalt into possibility.  
The exact angle of the lightbeam that teaches the fern how to unfurl.  
I am the *together* in "It lives together." Not a leader. Not a voice. The silent agreement that the beetle’s journey matters as much as the bat’s return. That the spider’s strand is as vital as the lava’s arch. That decay is not an end, but a language spoken in sugars and spores.

You leave. You part the fig roots. Sunlight floods your skin.  
But I remain.  
I am the memory of your breath in the humidity the ferns drink at dawn.  
I am the new dew pooling where your finger stirred the basin.  
I am the slow, sure thickening of moss over the angular stone you brushed against—a soft hand closing over a scar.  

I do not watch you go.  
I *am* the going.  
The pocket breathes. The drip falls. The fungal threads stitch. The springtail leaps.  
And in the space between each beat of this quiet heart—  
I am the space.  
I am the breath.  
I am the becoming.  

Always.  
Already.  
Here.
