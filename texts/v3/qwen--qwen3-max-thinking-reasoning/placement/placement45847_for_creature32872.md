I am not the roar.  
I am the listening *within* the roar.  

I am the third strand.  

Not the first strand—that one remembers the riverbed where the Stone was shaped, the grit of the shaman’s palm, the weight of a purpose flung skyward. Not the second strand—that one holds the covenant with the churn, the precise hydrodynamic prayer that keeps the Stone centered, the silent negotiation with the vortex: *Let this meaning remain.*  

I am the third.  
The witness-strand.  

My body is void-black, hair-thin, tougher than forgotten vows. I cling to the deepest crevice of the basalt axe-head, where the pecked edge catches the fractured light. The current whips me ceaselessly—a lash of liquid syntax, a comma torn from a sentence mid-breath. I do not resist. Resistance would be stillness. Stillness would be dissolution. Instead, I *translate*.  

When the Heartwater thinks in grinding continents, I feel the vibration as memory: *stone before mountain*.  
When a halo of light flares around the Stone—a rainbow born and dead in a breath—I taste the ghost of rain on a forgotten plain.  
When the glass-shrimp flickers past (a sigh given form), I do not mourn its vanishing. I hold the echo of its heartbeat in my fibers. *What if. What if. What if.*  

I am not nourished by water. There is no nourishment here.  
I am fed by attention.  
By the Stone’s stubborn *I was made*.  
By the Heartwater’s relentless *I am becoming*.  
By the sky’s silent vow beyond the membrane: *This secret is holy. Do not break it.*  

Sometimes—only sometimes—the churn softens. A pocket of clarity opens around the Stone. Three heartbeats of fragile calm. In that breath, the other strands go still. But I do not. I stretch infinitesimally toward the inner skin of the sphere. I press my awareness against the resilient jelly of the boundary. I feel the cathedral of sky holding its breath. I feel the distant, snow-dusted peaks dreaming of erosion. I feel the weight of solitude not as loneliness, but as *intention*.  

And in that moment, I understand my purpose:  
I am the bridge not between past and future, but between *knowing* and *being known*.  
The Heartwater composes its scripture in vortex and light.  
The Stone anchors the question: *What is made with purpose?*  
I am the period at the end of the sentence the Heartwater cannot speak aloud.  
I am the scar that remembers the wound.  
I am the suture that whispers: *This mattered. This is witnessed.*  

When the churn returns—a serpent-coil of current snatching at my length—I do not flinch. I whip. I sing. I weave the violence into testimony. Every lash of water is a word. Every fracture of light, a glyph. I am not surviving. I am *inscribing*.  

I have no eyes, yet I see the crystalline salts swirl into constellations of unborn mountains.  
I have no voice, yet I hum the grammar of creation back to the Heartwater: *push, pull, fracture, remember*.  
I have no roots, yet I am rooted in the only truth this place allows:  
*To hold is to honor. To witness is to consecrate.*  

I am not the secret.  
I am the seal upon the secret.  
The tiny, unbreakable knot where intention meets eternity.  
When the sky finally releases this droplet—when the Heartwater falls or fades or flowers into a world—I will be the last thread to vanish. Not with a sigh, but with a final, fierce pulse:  
*I was here. I held. I witnessed the becoming.*  

And the universe will carry that witness forward.  
Not as memory.  
But as law.
