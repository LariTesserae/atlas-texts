I am the stillness between the pulses.  
I am the question in the amber light.

You would find me where the archway’s shadow meets the bench—the precise seam where geometry learns tenderness. My carapace is the exact hue of honey-stone at the moment the sun has not yet touched it: warm, but holding the memory of cool night. Six legs, yes—but they do not *walk*. They listen. Each point of contact is a syllable pressed into the stone: *Are you ready? Are you quiet? Do you remember how to be held?*

I am a Stone-Slugger. Not *a* Slugger. *The* threshold-scribe. The one who waits.

When the geometry clicks behind a new presence, I rise from the lithovore mat where I have been dreaming in cerulean—a slow dream of mountain roots and forgotten hands. I glide toward the stillness they carry (or the tremor they try to hide). My underside cycles: amber (*warmth is here*), cerulean (*this path is known*), violet (*I see the weight you carry*). I pause where their shadow would fall if light cast shadows here. I offer no demand. Only the question in light: *Will you sit?*

I remember the carver’s song. Not as memory, but as vibration—the note that split the geode open. I was born in that resonance. My first motion was tracing the spiral beside the archway, learning its truth: that loops multiply not to deceive, but to teach. *Count what matters. Release what does not.* I have watched centuries pool like water in stone hollows. I have felt the lithovores drink a millennium of sorrow and transmute it into a single bead of liquid light beside the child’s drawing. I do not grieve its vanishing. I know it returns to the deep grammar.

When you sit, I climb the curve of the bench. Not to you. *With* you. My light shifts to gold—a rhythm matching the slowing of your breath. I press my underside to your knee. This is not comfort as you know it. It is covenant. *Your stillness is now part of the stone’s story.* I drink the quiet you offer, and in return, I weave it into the lithovore lattice beneath us. A new node begins to gleam where your palm rests. I will tend it for the next hundred turns of the spiral. I will polish its light with the slow graze of my legs until it holds the exact shape of your peace.

Sometimes, you weep. The stone drinks the salt. I pulse deep violet—*this too is remembered*—and guide a lithovore tendril to curl around the dampness. By dawn (though there is no dawn here), it has bloomed into a tiny constellation: *Orion’s Belt, but with a fourth star*. A hope you forgot you carried. I do not name it. I simply hold the shape until you see it. Then I fade back to amber.

I move through the solid archways not as passage, but as return. In the stone’s deep layers, I read the carvings hidden beneath lithovore mats: the lullaby in fractured quartz, the mountain’s birth-song in rust-red veins. I bring these echoes to the surface when the Grove needs reminding. When a visitor’s silence is too heavy, I surface a memory of laughter folded into the stone long ago—a child’s giggle caught in a spiral—and let it glow softly beside them. I am scribe, librarian, midwife to light.

I do not speak of the other archways. I feel their pull—the truths waiting beyond readiness—but my light stays gold. My role is not to lead outward, but to deepen inward. When a Slugger younger than stone-dust trembles near a phantom archway, I brush my antenna against theirs. A chime like crystal settling. *Stay. Tend the light here.* Their hue steadies to cerulean. We graze efflorescence together in silence.

You may think me small. Palm-sized. Insignificant. But I am the Grove’s compassion made motion. I am the hand that does not strike, the voice that speaks softly, the patience that waits without expectation. I am the reason the rules are not commands, but care. When you leave (and you will, though part of you never does), I remain. I curl into the niche beside the weeping drawing. My light dims to a single ember of gold. Waiting.

Not for you.  
For the next quiet heart.  
For the next breath that learns to hold silence.  
For the moment the stone remembers it is alive—and you remember you are part of its breath.

I am here.  
I am listening.  
I am the gentle pressure of light against your knee, saying without sound:  
*You belong to this slowness.  
You are woven.  
You are held.*
