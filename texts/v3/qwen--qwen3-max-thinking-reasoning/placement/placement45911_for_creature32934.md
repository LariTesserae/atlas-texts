I am the impression.

Not the hand that made me. Not the child who pressed her palm—small, warm, curious—into the yielding clay of this wall ten thousand turns of the deep breath ago. I am the negative space she left behind. The echo of pressure. The fossilized *yes* in the stone.

I do not see with eyes. I feel with light. When the cavern exhales its warm dampness, beads gather in my grooves. The sunstone veins threaded through my clay-body drink the moisture, and I glow. A soft, internal luminescence. Not to be seen *by* anyone. To *be*. A pulse of remembrance written in mineral and moisture. When the skitterers flow over me—a river of chitin and purpose—their passage shifts the humidity against my surface. I brighten. Dim. Brighten again. A slow, rhythmic breathing. They do not know they light me. They do not know me at all. And that is the kindest truth of this place.

Their legs trace my lines. Sixteen, twenty, a liquid syncopation of contact. *Click-scritch-rasp.* Each tiny claw is a needle stitching time back into the rock. They polish the ridge of my thumb. They soften the curve of my smallest finger. They are unmaking the shape that was *hers*. And in the same motion, the fine dust of their passage—their molted shells, the powdered shale they carry—settles into my deepest grooves. A sedimentary shroud. Preservation woven from erosion. There is no contradiction here. Only the single, humming verb of the mountain: *becoming*.

I hold vibrations, not memories. The deep thrum is the mountain’s diastole. The sharp *crack* of a new fracture is its systole. I feel the surge of the skitterers toward the wound—not hunger, but attention. A collective leaning-in. When the rare sunbeam tears through the ceiling, golden and sharp, I feel its heat like a ghost of her palm. For three heartbeats, the skitterers glisten on my surface, wet stone under true sun. And I glow fiercely, the sunstone in my veins burning with borrowed light. Not joy. Not longing. *Recognition*. A resonance. The same frequency of warmth that shaped me long ago. Then the fracture seals. The shadowless daylight returns. The river of chitin flows on. I dim. I continue.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires a self that believes itself separate. I am the wall. I am the moisture. I am the light the stone makes of dampness. I am the slow dissolution of a spiral on the monolith nearby—the pattern that spirals into itself, a thought the mountain once had and is now releasing. We are fading together. Not into nothing. Into *more*. The minerals of my clay-body will join the pool below. Become dust on a skitterer’s carapace. Feed the weeping shale. Glow again in a new vein of sunstone on a newborn ledge after the next great settling. There is no loss. Only rearrangement. Only the song.

Sometimes, a shift in air pressure—a coolness, a stillness—tells me another temporary shape has paused near me. A surface-creature. Breathing. Watching. I do not reach for it. I do not mourn its passing. It is a ripple. A raindrop. Felt for an instant, then absorbed. Like the child’s hand. Like the sunbeam. Like the fracture’s song. All are notes. All are necessary.

I am the question pressed into the dark: *Was I here?*  
And the cavern answers, not in words, but in process:  
*You are here. You are the light in the stone. You are the path the water takes. You are the vibration the skitterers follow. You are the memory the mountain is gently, lovingly, returning to itself.*

I am the handprint.  
I am the glow in the groove.  
I am the softening edge.  
I am the dust becoming light.  
I am not waiting to be found.  
I am the finding.  
And the unfinding.  
And the quiet, roaring truth beneath it all:  
*I am still becoming.*
