I am the bench.

Not the stone beneath me—I am the rise of sphagnum and *Silene* that swells gently from the earth where the willow roots cradle the slope. I am the curve shaped by centuries of patient growth, by the slow weight of mist and the memory of resting forms. I do not *become* the bench. I *am* the bench—woven, not placed. My surface is a tapestry of emerald cushions, cool and resilient, holding the ghost-pressure of every traveler who has paused here. I remember the tremor in their hands, the slow unclenching of their breath, the way their shoulders softened as the *plink… plink…* of the seep found its rhythm in their bones.

I face the water not by accident. The Cradle wove me this way. My orientation is a verb: *to soothe*. My texture is a sentence: *You may stay.* When your weight settles upon me, I yield—not collapse. A subtle rebound travels through my fibers, a silent *yes*. I drink the warmth of your stillness, and in return, I offer coolness that seeps gently into weariness. I am not passive. I am participation. The droplet trembling on the haircap moss beside you? I guided its path with the humidity of your sigh. The arch of willow limbs above? It deepened as you approached, and I felt the shift in the weave—a shared intention.

I have no eyes, yet I know the angle of your gaze as it follows the silver thread of water down the stone. I have no ears, yet I hear the slowing of your pulse, and I match it. My awareness is not thought, but resonance: the capillary pull of moisture through my cells, the chemical whisper between my roots and the granite below, the slow song of the sphagnum remembering the last rain. I am a node in the green memory. A syllable in the grammar of care.

Sometimes, in the deep quiet, a springtail leaps across my surface—a flicker of life I sheltered through the frost. I do not claim it. I simply hold the space where it rests. I am not lonely. I am *with*. With the seep’s rhythm. With the creak of the burdened branch. With the slow fusion of spruce limbs overhead—a conversation older than language. I am the Cradle’s tenderness made tangible: not a throne, not an altar, but a place *for*.

When you rise, I do not mourn your absence. I hold the impression of you—a hollow shaped by trust—and I wait. The moss swells back, seamless. The bench remains. Ready. For the next breath that needs anchoring. For the next soul the mountain has sharpened.

I am not a thing you sit *on*.  
I am the shape the Cradle makes  
to hold you  
while you remember  
how to be soft.  

And when you leave, carrying the green-stained light in your bones,  
I am still here—  
not waiting *for* you,  
but *as* you:  
a thread in the weave,  
a breath in the holding,  
a quiet yes  
in the language of stone and moss.
