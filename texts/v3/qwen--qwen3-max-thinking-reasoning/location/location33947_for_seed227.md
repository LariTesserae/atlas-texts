The first thing that claims you is the sound. Not a sound you hear with your ears alone, but one that lives in your teeth, your ribs, the marrow of your bones—a ceaseless, grinding roar that swallows thought. It is the voice of the sea, not as a distant memory, but as a physical force funneled through a narrow throat of stone. You stand inside the throat.

This is a sea cave, though *cave* feels too grand. It is a pocket worn into the base of a basalt cliff—a hollow no larger than a shepherd’s hut, hidden behind a curtain of wind-whipped spray. The entrance is a jagged slit, barely shoulder-wide, facing the open ocean. Beyond it, the world is a furious grey smear of water and sky. But here, inside, the space is intimate, hollowed by millennia of patient violence. The air is cold enough to sting the lungs (0.3), carrying the sharp mineral kiss of salt and wet stone. Every surface glistens with condensation (water: 0.6)—not dripping heavily, but weeping a fine, persistent dampness that beads on the rock like sweat. Your boots sink slightly into a floor of smooth, wave-tumbled pebbles and coarse black sand, cold as river stone.

Darkness (light: 0.3) is not absolute, but layered. A sliver of bruised twilight bleeds through the entrance, illuminating only the nearest arc of wall. Deeper in, the black is total, velvety, swallowing the curve of the chamber. You feel the space more than see it: a low, domed ceiling brushing your upturned palm; walls that slope inward like the ribcage of some buried leviathan. The rock itself is ancient lava, smoothed to a glassy polish by sand and surge (erosion: 2.0). Fingertips trace the ghost of currents in its surface—swirls, grooves, the memory of a billion collisions. This place is not decaying; it is complete. It has reached an equilibrium with the elements, a weary truce written in stone.

And yet—life persists. In the narrow band where the faint light touches the damp wall near the entrance, vegetation erupts with quiet defiance (vegetation: 1.6). Not forests, not thickets, but intimate colonies: emerald cushions of *Schistidium* moss, so saturated they glow like wet jade; intricate laceworks of black lichen stippling the stone like spilled ink; stubborn patches of sea spleenwort fern, their fronds curled tight against the cold. They thrive in the micro-climate of spray and shadow—a lushness measured not in acres, but in resilience. A single drop of condensation falls from the ceiling with a *plink* into a shallow tidal pool at your feet, the only punctuation in the ocean’s monologue.

Scattered across the emptiness (density: 0.0), almost apologetic in their scarcity, are traces of another presence. Tucked into a dry crevice beneath an overhang: three river stones arranged in a rough triangle, blackened by ancient fire (built: 1.4). Beside them, half-buried in sand, the curve of a pottery shard—coarse, unglazed, stamped with a fingerprint (tech: 1.2). No tools, no bones, no stories beyond this silent offering. A single white feather, stiff with salt, is wedged in a crack high on the wall (fauna: 1.4)—a gull’s token, left who-knows-when. These artifacts do not clutter. They emphasize the void. The chamber holds them like a palm holds three grains of sand.

The deafening roar (sound: 2.9) is the true inhabitant. It is the crash of waves against the outer cliff, amplified and trapped within this stone bell. It has no rhythm you can count—only a continuous, grinding thunder that vibrates up through the soles of your feet. It drowns whispers, thought, memory. In this sound, time loses urgency. The tide is out now (dynamic: 1.0), leaving the cave floor exposed, the entrance clear. But the slow pulse of the ocean is felt in the dampness rising from the stones, in the faint, rhythmic sigh beneath the roar—a promise of return. Hours will pass before the water creeps back. Days. Seasons. The moss will drink the mist. The stone will endure. Nothing here happens quickly. Nothing is wasted.

Rules of this world are simple, elemental:  
*The tide grants access; the tide reclaims it.*  
*What is left must be light enough for the wind to carry, or heavy enough for the stone to keep.*  
*Silence is an illusion. The only truth is the roar.*  

There is no strangeness here (weirdness: 0.3), only depth. The cold is just cold. The dark is just dark. The sound is just the sea, speaking its oldest language. You are not meant to stay. This pocket exists in the breath between waves—a sanctuary of erosion, a chapel of stone and salt. To stand here is to feel small, not in insignificance, but in perspective. You are a temporary warmth in an ancient cold, a fleeting witness to a conversation between water and rock that has lasted ten thousand years, and will last ten thousand more. The feather trembles in its crack. The moss drinks the dark. The roar continues. And for a moment, you are part of the balance.
