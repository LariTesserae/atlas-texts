You do not find the place. It finds you.

One moment, the low basin is ordinary: warm air (1.6) heavy with the scent of damp earth after a passing shower, golden light (1.9) slanting through sparse, knee-high grasses. The next, the world *folds*. A shimmer hangs between three ancient, silver-barked sentinel trees growing in a perfect triangle no wider than a cottage garden (scale: 0.9). Step through the distortion—not a door, but a thinning of reality—and the smallness of the outside vanishes.

*Deafening.*

The sound hits first. Not noise. A physical force. It is the deep, tectonic hum of the World-Tree’s sap-rivers surging through arteries wider than canyons. It is the crystalline *shatter-chime* of glass-winged sky-rays taking flight from distant spires. It is the resonant groan of living wood shifting its weight over centuries, amplified a thousandfold by hollow chambers shaped like inverted bells. It is the echo of rain that fell three days ago still rebounding through vaulted root-cathedrals. Time does not absorb sound here; it layers it. A bird’s cry from dawn overlaps with the whisper of a leaf unfolding *tomorrow*. You feel the vibration in your teeth, your marrow. To speak is impossible. To think requires anchoring yourself against the sonic tide.

And yet—the space is hollow. Vast. Emptiness (density: 0.2) is the dominant sensation. You stand on a smooth plaza of interwoven roots, polished by millennia of passage (erosion: 2.0). Before you, the interior stretches into a soft-focus haze. Kilometers away, a bridge of living willow arcs between two trunk-towers, no thicker than a thread. Below, nothing but air and drifting motes of bioluminescent pollen. This is the paradox: vegetation is total (2.6), yet the volume is near-void. The ecosystem *is* the architecture. Walls are living bark, veined with slow-pulsing amber sap-light. Staircases spiral up colossal trunks, grown, not carved. Doorways are arches of patient, clasped roots. Balconies bloom with night-blooming star-flowers that drink the ambient sound and glow faintly in response. There are no stones, no metals, no dead wood. Every surface breathes, sweats a fine dew (water: 1.4), and shifts imperceptibly. This is urban (1.7) in its intricate organization—a district of nesting spires here, a grove of fruiting memory-vines there, pathways converging on a central plaza—but built solely by biology, over epochs (tech: 0.3). A city grown, not constructed.

Fauna moves through the hollows like living punctuation (fauna: 2.3). Six-limbed moss-foxes leap between distant branches, their padded landings swallowed by the din. Flocks of harmonic finches weave through the air, their feathers tuned to specific frequencies; where they fly, the deafening chaos momentarily resolves into a fleeting chord before shattering again. You see the gleam of river-serpents slithering through aerial streams of condensed moisture that flow along invisible thermal currents—pockets of water (1.4) suspended in the warm air (1.6), feeding epiphytic gardens clinging to the high vaults.

The light is normal daylight (1.9), yet there is no sky. It emanates from the wood itself—a soft, even luminescence as if the Heartwood remembers the sun and gently exhales its memory. Shadows are soft. Warmth is constant, generated by the slow metabolic fire of the colossal organism holding this space.

This is where reality frays (weirdness: 2.6).  
- Turn your back on a path, and it may have grown a new junction by the time you look again.  
- Hum a single clear note into the hollow of a root, and a bridge of woven vine might descend from the heights—but hum a dissonant tone, and the air where you stand may briefly taste of forgotten winters.  
- The deafening sound is not just heard; it *shapes*. A sustained roar from the central trunk causes blossoms to erupt along a distant ridge. Silence is the rarest, most dangerous substance here. Legends (whispered in the rare, sound-dampening moss-grottos) say those who find true quiet are rewritten by the Tree’s dreams.  
- Distance is a suggestion. Walk toward a distant spire for an hour, and you might arrive at the very root you left. Or you might step into a pocket-grove where time moves slower, and dew hangs motionless on petals for what feels like days.

You are standing in the throat of a living world. The damp air carries the scent of wet bark, honey-sap, and ozone from sonic vibrations. The ground thrums. A sky-ray’s wingbeat passes overhead—a sound like shattering crystal—and for three heartbeats, the entire space *sings* in resonance. Then the layered echoes crash back. This is not wilderness. It is a conscious, breathing metropolis of wood and whisper and wave. It has existed since the first root sought water. It requires no tools, no machines. It simply *is*: a settled, balanced (erosion: 2.0), deeply active ecosystem (dynamic: 2.4) where biology has achieved architecture, and sound has become scripture. To be here is to be humbled, overwhelmed, and held—within the deafening, generous, hollow heart of the green.
