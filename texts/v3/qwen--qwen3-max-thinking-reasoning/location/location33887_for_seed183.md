The air tastes of cold stone and static. You stand on the lip of the Sky-Suture, a plateau so high the sky is not blue but a deep, bruised violet at the zenith, bleeding to razor-edged cerulean where the sun—a merciless, white-gold disc—hangs motionless. The light does not merely illuminate; it *excises*. It carves every contour with impossible sharpness, yet shadows pool in ways that violate geometry: a single standing stone casts four distinct shadows, each pointing to a different cardinal direction that does not exist on any map. This is the first whisper of the unraveling.

Beneath your boots, the ground is a mosaic of obsidian shards and milky quartz, fused by ages of silent pressure into a single, seamless plain. It is cool to the touch, yet hums with a low thermal resonance, as if the stone itself remembers geothermal fire. Water is here, but not as you know it. From fissures in the basalt monoliths that rise like broken teeth across the plateau, droplets *weep*—not falling, but hovering. They gather in trembling spheres along the rock faces, refracting the fractured light into prismatic halos. Some drift upward on unseen currents. Where they vanish, the stone is dry. Where they linger, they leave behind crystalline traceries: frost-flowers of salt and silica that bloom for seconds before dissolving into the air. The atmosphere is thick with this suspended moisture—a condensation of potential, not substance. Breathe deeply, and your lungs feel the ghost of dampness, though your skin stays dry.

There is no life. Not a spore, not a lichen, not a fossilized whisper of what once was. The absence is absolute, a vacuum in the world’s memory. What appears from a distance as delicate, branching "foliage" clinging to canyon walls resolves into dendritic veins of manganese oxide—fossilized lightning frozen in stone. What seems a field of pale mushrooms near a stone circle is revealed as clusters of calcite spires, each humming at a frequency that vibrates your teeth. This is a mineral world, patient and precise. Yet the *density* is overwhelming. The air shimmers with layered transparencies: for three heartbeats, the plateau is dust and silence; then, superimposed like a double exposure, you see the same stones slick with black ice under a green sun; then, a fleeting glimpse of the monoliths as they were a millennium ago—smooth, unbroken, humming with contained energy. These echoes do not fade cleanly. They bleed. A shadow from a vanished era might brush your ankle. The scent of rain that never fell might hang for a breath.

Sound is a physical force. The wind does not whistle—it *shatters*. It screams across the open expanse (scale: a valley stretching to jagged, impossible peaks that pierce the sky-dome), but the noise is layered. Beneath the gale lies the deep, tectonic groan of the plateau settling into its own paradox. Sharp *cracks* like splitting glass erupt randomly—not from impact, but from space itself fraying. Near the scattered remnants of civilization, the sound shifts. You find them: low rings of river-smoothed stone, fitted without mortar. A single trilithon arch, no taller than a man, stands tilted. Carved into its lintel are spirals that hurt to follow—the work of hands using only antler and grit (tech: primitive). In the shallow basin of one fallen stone, hovering droplets orbit a central point, chiming against the rock like glass beads. *Ping. Ping-ping.* A rhythm with no source. The stones themselves emit a subsonic thrum, a vibration that resonates in your marrow. This is the loudness: not chaos, but a symphony of fractures.

The rules are the landscape.  
- *Gravity is a suggestion.* A pebble kicked from your path may fall normally… or spiral sideways into a hovering droplet, vanishing with a soft *pop*.  
- *Time is sedimentary.* Glance left: a monolith is whole. Glance right: the same stone lies in rubble. Look back: it is whole again. The erosion is "established" not because it is finished, but because it exists in all states of weathering simultaneously—a balance of ruin and renewal across fractured timelines.  
- *Presence is porous.* You feel watched, not by eyes, but by the landscape’s attention. A patch of gravel shifts *before* you step toward it. The hovering water droplets part around your shadow. The plateau is aware. Not alive. *Aware.*  
- *Water remembers.* Touch a weeping rock where a droplet hovers. For an instant, your mind floods with the sensation of deep ocean pressure, of kelp forests swaying in sunlit currents—a memory imprinted into the moisture by a reality where this place was seabed. Then it is gone. The droplet vanishes. Your hand is dry.  

You approach the largest stone circle. At its center, the air shimmers like heat haze. Within the distortion, shapes flicker: a stag with antlers of woven light, a tree heavy with silver fruit, a child’s hand reaching. They are not illusions. They are *leakage*—fragments of worlds where vegetation thrived, where fauna breathed, where water flowed true. The weirdness is not madness; it is porosity. This plateau is a scar where realities abrade against one another. The primitive builders did not come here to live. They came to mend. Their circles are sutures. Their carvings are prayers written in geometry to hold the tear closed. One stone lies cracked clean through. From the fracture, a single, perfect droplet hangs—within it, a tiny, swirling galaxy.  

A gust of wind shears the silence. The droplet trembles. For a heartbeat, the plateau holds its breath. The violet sky flickers to the deep black of space, stars blazing cold and sharp. Then light floods back. The droplet is gone. The crack in the stone remains.  

You take a step back. The gravel clicks underfoot—a sound like bones settling. The wind carries the faint, crystalline chime of water that is not water, remembering a song it never learned. The plateau does not welcome you. It does not reject you. It simply *is*—a wound in the world, beautiful and terrible, humming with the ghost of rain. And in the space between one breath and the next, you feel the ground beneath you soften, just for an instant, into something that might have been soil. Then it is stone again. Solid. Waiting.
