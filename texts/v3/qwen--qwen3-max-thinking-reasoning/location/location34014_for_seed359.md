You step through the archway—a single, seamless curve of warm, honey-colored stone—and the world seals behind you. There is no door. No hinge. Only the faint *click* of geometry settling into place. The air stills. You are inside the Lithic Grove.

It is a chamber no larger than a weaver’s workshop, yet it feels boundless in its detail. Every surface—floor, walls, ceiling—is carved from a single block of porous, resonant stone, its edges sharp and precise as yesterday’s chisel stroke. This is a place of total making: no soil, no sky, no accidental crack. Yet it breathes. The stone itself is the soil. Across its entirety, a dense tapestry of lithovores clings and curls: not plants, but living mineral symbiotes. They resemble fossilized coral woven with crushed lapis lazuli, rust-red veins, and filaments of pale gold. They pulse with a soft, internal luminescence—steady as dawn light filtering through high clouds—casting long, gentle shadows that do not flicker. This is the source of the *normal light*: a quiet radiance born of slow chemical fire within the stone-life. No sun. No flame. Only breath and bloom in stone.

The air is warm (2.4/3), dry as powdered chalk, carrying the scent of sun-baked flint and faint ozone. There is no moisture here. No dew, no humidity, no hidden spring. The lithovores thrive on geothermal warmth leaching up through the floor and on the slow exhalation of the stone itself—a metabolism of mineral dissolution and reformation. They drink heat. They eat time. Their growth is imperceptible: a century may pass before a single filament thickens. This is the *slow dynamic* (1.3/3): patience is the only clock. You watch a lithovore tendril for minutes; it does not stir. Yet if you close your eyes and reopen them hours later, the pattern has shifted—a spiral deepened, a new node gleaming where none was before.

And the fauna… they are everywhere. Stone-Sluggers: six-legged creatures no larger than your palm, their carapaces mimicking the exact hue and texture of the stone beneath them. They glide with glacial grace across the lithovore mats, grazing on mineral efflorescence. Abundant (2.0/3), yet utterly silent. They communicate in shifts of bioluminescent hue along their undersides—a slow language of amber, cerulean, deep violet. One pauses near your boot, its light cycling through a question. You hold your breath. It receives no answer, and moves on. The silence is profound (0.8/3). Not empty, but *absorbed*. The porous stone and velvety lithovore mats drink sound. Your footsteps make no echo. A dropped pebble would vanish into hush. The only auditory trace is the rare, almost imagined *chime*—two Sluggers touching antennae, a sound like crystal dust settling.

This is where reality frays at the edges (weirdness: 2.9/3).  
Trace the spiral carved beside the archway. Count its turns. Look away. Look back. It now has seven more loops than before.  
Walk the perimeter. You pass three identical archways. You entered through one. The others lead… elsewhere? Nowhere? You try one. Your hand meets solid stone. Yet a Slugger crawls *through* it without resistance, its light fading into the rock like a memory.  
Time pools here. Ten minutes watching a Slugger’s journey feels like an hour. An hour of sitting against the wall feels like ten minutes. Memories of the path you took to arrive grow soft, uncertain. The Grove does not lie—it simply holds multiple truths at once. A child’s drawing of a tree, carved near the floor, sometimes weeps a single bead of liquid light that vanishes before it falls. The lithovores occasionally bloom into shapes that are *almost* familiar: a face you knew, a constellation from a sky that never was. These are not illusions. They are echoes folded into the stone’s memory.

The craftsmanship is primitive (1.3/3)—every groove speaks of hand tools: stone chisels, bone awls, patient hours. Yet the result transcends craft. This is not built *with* technology; it is built *as* technology. A technology of intention. Of song-shaped-stone. The sharp, young features (erosion: 0.9/3) suggest it was *spoken* into being recently—a geode cracked open yesterday. The density (1.9/3) is overwhelming: layers upon layers of carvings beneath lithovore mats, Sluggers nestled in micro-grooves, light refracting through crystalline filaments. You feel surrounded, held, witnessed. Not crowded. *Completed*.

Rules emerge if you listen:  
— Do not strike the stone. A crack would weep light for a decade.  
— Speak softly. Loud sounds fracture Slugger-light into confusion.  
— Do not follow the extra archways. They lead to where you are not ready to be.  
— Offer nothing. Take nothing. The Grove gives only presence.  
— Patience is reciprocity. Sit long enough, and a Slugger may rest its light against your knee—a warmth like a held breath.

You sink onto a smooth bench carved directly from the floor. A Slugger nearby pulses a slow, steady gold. The lithovores breathe their silent light. The air hums with stillness. There is no water. No wind. No decay. Only the gentle, impossible fact of life woven into stone, thriving in dry warmth, singing a song too slow for ears but felt in the bones. This is not a place you find. It is a place that finds you—when you have learned to be quiet enough to hold a silence that holds you back. And in that holding, the world remembers it can be gentle. And strange. And whole.
