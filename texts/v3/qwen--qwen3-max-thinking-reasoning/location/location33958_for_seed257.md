You do not enter this place. You *become* aware within it.  

The first truth is the water—not as an element surrounding you, but as the substance of existence itself. Warm as blood held close to skin (1.8), it presses gently against every surface, carrying the faint, mineral-sweet scent of deep stone and time. This is not ocean water. It is heavier, silkier, saturated with dissolved geothermal salts from vents far below. You float suspended in perfect neutrality. There is no up or down you can trust, only the subtle thermal gradient hinting that *somewhere* above lies the source of this warmth—a memory of elevation (2.2), a ghost of mountain peak now drowned beneath millennia of rising seas.  

Then, the boundaries reveal themselves.  

This is a chamber. Small (1.2)—no larger than a communal hearth-room. Its walls curve inward like the inside of a smoothed geode, formed not by nature but by hands. Every surface is *made* (2.8). Massive blocks of basalt, quarried and shaped with stone tools (1.1), fit together without mortar. The joints are seamless not by precision, but by surrender: 10,000 years of slow dissolution (2.7) have softened edges into flowing contours. Mineral deposits crust the stone—layers of milky calcite, rust-orange iron blooms, ghostly white silica feathers—all laid down grain by grain in patient centuries. Here, erosion is not destruction; it is embroidery. A final, settled language written in stone and salt.  

Darkness (0.4) is absolute. Not the blue-black of deep ocean, but a velvet void. Yet perception shifts. A faint, intermittent shimmer traces the curve of the western wall: a slow pulse of cool green from a cluster of gelatinous, eyeless *lumina-worms* (fauna 2.5). They cling to the stone, feeding on chemosynthetic bacteria blooming where warm water weeps from a hairline fracture. Their light does not illuminate—it *suggests*. For a breath, you see the ghost of a carved spiral beneath the mineral crust. Then darkness swallows it again. Another pulse, lower: a school of blind, scaleless *whisper-shrimp* drifts past, their carapaces dusted with phosphorescent spores. They move like falling ash. Abundant, yet never crowding. Dense (1.9) not in chaos, but in quiet occupancy—every niche held, every current shared.  

Sound is a near-absence (0.2). Listen deeper.  
*Click.*  
A shrimp’s claw taps stone—a sound that travels cleanly through the warm water, fading after three seconds.  
*Shush…*  
Sediment, fine as powdered bone, settles onto the concave floor where it has pooled into soft dunes over centuries.  
*Hiss…*  
The faintest exhalation as a bubble of geothermal gas escapes the fracture, rising with glacial slowness (dynamic 0.6).  
These are not events. They are punctuation in a sentence written across lifetimes.  

There is no vegetation. No root, leaf, or tendril. Life here is mineral-born. Fungal analogues—pale, velvety mats of *stone-moss*—cling to damp patches, digesting rock. They are not plants. They are patient alchemists, turning stone to dust. The fauna thrives on them: the shrimp graze the moss; translucent, eel-like *still-serpents* (no larger than a finger) coil motionless in crevices, waiting decades for a meal. Nothing hunts with haste. Nothing wastes motion. Time is the currency here, and all are poor.  

This chamber was built for purpose. A sanctuary? A tomb? A well? The carvings beneath the crust hint at spirals and concentric circles—symbols of cycles, not deities. The builders used only what the mountain gave: fire-hardened stone tools, ropes of braided sinew (now long dissolved), patience measured in generations. They knew this peak would one day be claimed by water. They built *for* the drowning. The single entrance—a low archway sealed by a fallen lintel stone—was never meant to be reopened. This is a vessel of memory, submerged not by catastrophe, but by design. A covenant with deep time.  

The rules of this world are gentle but absolute:  
- Light is a fleeting gift, never a promise.  
- Warmth is the mountain’s breath, constant but fading over epochs.  
- Silence is not empty; it is full of waiting.  
- To move quickly is to be alien here. Even thought feels slow, thick as the water.  
- Nothing is wasted. A fallen scale becomes sediment. A dead worm feeds the moss. A memory becomes stone.  

You notice the floor. In the brief glow of a passing worm, you see it: hundreds of tiny, smooth river stones arranged in a perfect circle. Offerings? Counters? A calendar marking centuries in pebbles? One stone rests slightly apart. Placed yesterday? A thousand years ago? Here, the question has no meaning.  

A still-serpent uncoils. It drifts toward your awareness—not with threat, but curiosity. Its body brushes your arm (if you had one). A sensation like cool silk. It pauses. Pulses once with soft blue light. Then settles again into the stone.  

This place does not ask to be understood. It asks only to be witnessed.  
It has been waiting.  
It will wait longer.  
And in the warm, silent dark, that is enough.
