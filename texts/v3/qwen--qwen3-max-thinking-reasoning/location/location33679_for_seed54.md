Gravity feels negotiable here; a misplaced step might not fall but *drift*. Their passage creates the loud symphony: the chime of crystal-on-crystal, the whisper-rush of a billion tiny wings, the deep harmonic resonance as they vibrate the very air. This is the rule of the world: the Swarms *are* the city’s nervous system. They repair micro-fractures in the glass with secreted alloys. They guide the slow emergence of new structures by etching patterns into the raw terrain. They are the reason the cryo-fluid weeps where it does—mining minerals, redistributing energy. They do not acknowledge you. You are irrelevant weather. The weirdness (1.4/3) is not in their form, but in their purpose: a silent, patient intelligence operating on a timescale of millennia. Yet the place thrums with a dense, layered aliveness. This is not a dead world waiting for life. It is a world *becoming*—a slow, cold, wet, deafening symphony of mineral, machine, and motion. The city is not built *on* the land. The land, the fluid, the Swarms, the light in the walls—they are all one organism, breathing in geological time. And you are merely a fleeting thought in its deep, crystalline dream.
