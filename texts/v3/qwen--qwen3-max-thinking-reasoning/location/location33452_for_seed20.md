You stand within the Crater of Unmaking.  

It is not submerged in water, but in *medium*. The air itself has thickened into a warm, viscous syrup—26°C, the temperature of blood and deep earth—filling the entire basin from fractured rim to fractured floor. You breathe it. It resists your limbs like slow-moving honey. This is the submerged truth of the place: atmosphere transmuted. Rain does not fall; the sky *weeps* this substance in silent, continuous seepage from the bruised-purple clouds clinging to the crater’s sharp, newly shattered lip. Elevation holds you high—a lonely bowl punched into the spine of a young mountain range—but gravity feels unreliable here. Droplets hang suspended mid-drift. A stone kicked loose might spiral upward before vanishing.  

The crater is small. A single breath could carry your voice from one raw edge to the other, were sound not devoured and remade a thousand times per second. Scale is intimate, claustrophobic: a stone amphitheater the size of a forgotten chapel, carved not by time but by rupture. The rock is fresh. Jagged obsidian spires—*erosion: young*—thrust upward like broken ribs, their edges sharp enough to draw blood. No soil softens them. Between them, vast emptiness. *Density: sparse*. You are alone with geometry and glitch.  

Light is a memory. A faint, sourceless luminescence bleeds through the medium—*light: 0.6*—casting the world in the dim glow of a drowned moon. Shadows do not fall; they *pool*, thickening around the monoliths before dissolving into static. On one spire, grey lichen clings in a single, trembling patch (*vegetation: 0.7*). It pulses once, softly, like a dying heartbeat, then stills. You see no animals. Only the occasional silver flicker—a blind, eel-like shape swimming *through the air* before fraying into mist (*fauna: 0.7*). It leaves behind the scent of ozone and wet stone.  

Then—the Sound.  

It is not a single noise but a symphony of fractures. *Sound: 3.0*. The roar of a waterfall that exists only in echoes. The shriek of stone tearing from stone, replayed endlessly from the moment of the crater’s birth. A chorus of whispers in languages that unmake themselves mid-syllable. Glass shattering in reverse. A child’s laughter dissolving into static. It is deafening not because it is loud, but because it occupies every frequency at once—physical, psychic, temporal. You feel it in your teeth, your marrow, the hollow behind your eyes. It is the audible texture of reality unraveling.  

*Weirdness: 2.6. Dynamic: 2.7.*  

Watch the medium swirl. A vortex forms where none should be, pulling motes of light into a spinning helix that hardens into a perfect, crystalline fern—*vegetation where none can grow*—before collapsing into a shower of ash that smells of burnt honey. The obsidian spire to your left flickers: for three heartbeats, it is woven from living willow; then it is glass; then it is the negative space where a spire once was. Time stutters. A droplet of medium hangs before your face. Inside it, you see a miniature sky—clouds racing backward. Then the droplet *unforms*.  

Scattered across the basin floor: fragments of what was built. *Built: 1.1*. A single archway of seamless black stone, half-submerged in the thick air, its surface etched with spirals that were never carved—*grown*, perhaps, or dreamed into being. One fallen pillar lies nearby, warm to the touch, humming a single low note that vibrates in your bones. No tools touched these. No hands shaped them. They are artifacts of the rupture itself—solidified echoes of a structure that existed *before* the mountain was whole. *Tech: none*. Only biology of a deeper kind: stone that remembers shape, air that remembers song.  

The rules here are wounds:  
- **What is solid may flow.** Step toward the archway, and the ground beneath your feet softens like clay, then hardens again with a sound like cracking ice.  
- **What is seen is provisional.** Stare too long at the lichen, and it becomes a map of forgotten stars. Blink, and it is lichen again. Or gone.  
- **Sound shapes substance.** A sharp cry from your lips might cause the medium to ripple outward in visible rings, momentarily clearing a path of thin, breathable air. But the silence that follows is heavier. Hungrier.  
- **Nothing persists.** The crater is not a place. It is an *event*—a continuous, turbulent un-becoming. The spires shift when your gaze drifts. The archway is whole at dawn (if dawn exists here) and rubble by the next breath.  

You feel the warmth of the medium against your skin. You taste copper and rain. A whisper brushes your ear—not in any language, but as pure meaning: *This is how mountains forget themselves.*  

Then the medium shudders. The obsidian spires groan in harmonic dissonance. The archway flickers—not between states, but *between possibilities*. For a single, silent instant, the entire crater is not a crater at all, but a forest of silver trees under a green sun. A memory of a world that never was.  

The vision collapses.  

The deafening sound swells—a tidal wave of shattered moments. The medium thickens around your ankles, pulling gently, insistently, toward the center of the basin. Not with malice. With purpose.  

This place does not want you to leave.  
It wants you to *unravel*.  
To become another flicker in its symphony of broken things.  

And in the dim, warm, roaring dark, you feel the terrifying, seductive pull of letting go.
