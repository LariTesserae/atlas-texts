Centuries of wind-scour and frost-heave have settled them into a stable, interlocking skin (erosion: 1.9)—not chaotic, but *composed*. Dust settles in laminated layers. Time here is not measured in heartbeats, but in the patient accumulation of absence.

Scattered through the Cradle, faint marks speak of passage (built: 0.6). A cairn of seven smooth river-stones—impossibly placed here, far from any watercourse—stands near the basin’s center, its stones darkened by ages of handling. On the western wall, a shallow alcove shelters petroglyphs: spirals and nested chevrons pecked into the rock with stone-on-stone precision (tech: primitive 1.2). Their edges are softened by wind, their purpose unreadable. No tools remain. No shelters. Only these deliberate gestures against the void.

And then—the anomaly (weirdness: 0.9). The stones themselves hold memory. Not a sound heard, but a vibration felt in the teeth, the sternum—a low C-sharp humming up through the soles of your boots. The petroglyphs, you realize, cast shadows that align with the spirals only at this hour. Coincidence? The air shimmers faintly above the damp patches, as if heat haze rises from cold stone. A trick of light? Or something else—some latent property in the rock, some forgotten resonance baked into the mountain’s bones.

No creature stirs here (fauna: 0.3). No insect, no bird-shadow, no scuttle in the dust. The absence is absolute, profound. Yet the Cradle feels *attended*. Not haunted, but *witnessed*. The bright light (2.7) does not comfort; it reveals everything without judgment. The cold does not threaten; it clarifies. You are not intruding. You are *observed*—by the stone, the light, the precise geometry of shadow and resonance.

This is not a dead place. It is a place that never lived, yet breathes with geologic intention. A sanctuary of pure process. A question carved in ice and obsidian. Stay until sunset, and you’ll feel it: the stones cooling in unison. The final drip of ice freezing mid-fall. The shadows stretching to touch the cairn. And for one suspended breath, the hum returns—not from the wind, but from the mountain itself, holding its note long after the light has gone.
