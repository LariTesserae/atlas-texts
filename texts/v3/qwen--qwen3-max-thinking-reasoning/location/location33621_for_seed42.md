The water does not feel like drowning. It feels like breath.  

You are standing—or rather, suspended—in the Sunken Plaza of the High Basin, a cupped hollow high in the spine of the world where a mountain lake swallowed a village centuries ago. The water is not ocean-deep, but *present*: cool (1.3), clear as chilled glass, pressing gently against skin with the quiet insistence of a held breath. Sunlight (1.7) filters down in liquid gold coins, fractured by the slow sway of surface ripples far above. This is not the crushing dark of the abyss, but a luminous twilight realm, shallow enough for light to paint the stones below in shifting patterns of amber and jade.  

The vegetation *is* the architecture. At 2.9, it is total—not merely growing *on* the ruins, but *as* the ruins. Walls of woven willow-root, hardened over generations into living stone, curve into archways draped with curtain-moss that drifts like slow green smoke. Staircases spiral upward not of cut rock, but of fused bamboo culms, their hollows now nurseries for freshwater shrimp. Every surface breathes: carpets of star-moss pulse faintly with bioluminescent spores; strands of river-kelp, thick as wrists, sway in patient currents, their fronds brushing against carved lintels where glyphs of forgotten harvests are softened by time. This is plant-as-substrate, plant-as-memory. The air (if air it could be called) tastes of wet stone, crushed mint, and the sweet rot of water-lilies whose roots anchor the plaza’s central dais—a single, massive slab of river-polished granite, now cradled in the embrace of a weeping fig whose branches trail silver roots into the water like liquid lace.  

Sound arrives not as noise, but as texture (2.3). A deep, resonant *thrum* hums through the water—the lake’s pulse against the mountain’s bones. Closer: the crisp *click-scritch* of crayfish navigating mossy steps; the liquid *plink* of air bubbles rising from a fissure in the dais; the chorus of frog-voles singing from air pockets trapped within hollow reed-towers (primitive tech: bundled reeds sealed with pine-resin, still holding breath after two hundred years). High above, the distant rush of the waterfall that feeds this basin thrums like a heartbeat. It is loud, yet never chaotic—a symphony of patient things.  

Fauna moves through the green gloom with abundant purpose (2.3). Sapphire-scaled minnows flash between root-lattices. A river otter, sleek and curious, nudges a smooth stone on the plaza floor before vanishing into a tunnel of woven vines. Water-striders skate the surface far above, their shadows darting like fleeting thoughts across the dais. Dragonfly nymphs cling to kelp fronds, jaws poised. Life is everywhere, yet never crowded. Density is sparse (0.9): between the living walls, open water holds space for light to pool, for a single fallen petal to spiral downward in slow motion, for silence to breathe between sounds.  

This place is small (1.1). You could swim its circumference in three slow breaths. A crescent-shaped plaza no larger than a council chamber, bounded by the root-walls on three sides and the open water of the basin on the fourth. Yet within this intimacy lies profound depth. Erosion has not destroyed, but refined (1.9). Edges are softened; sharp corners worn to gentle curves by centuries of current. A child’s carving—a spiraling sun—on the dais is now a home for pearl-mussels. The settlement was never grand (built: 2.3 urban, yet humble): foundations of river-stone, hearths lined with fire-blackened clay, a communal well now choked with blooming lotus. Tools lie where they were left: a stone mortar cradling water-fern, a fishing net of braided reed dissolving into the current. Technology was simple (1.0): no metal, only wood, stone, fiber, bone. Yet the craftsmanship speaks of deep knowing—the precise angle of a water-channel carved into living root, the resonance of a hollow-log drum still tethered to the dais by vines.  

Time moves like cold honey (dynamic: 0.8). A leaf detaches from an overhanging branch far above, drifting down through sunbeams for minutes before settling on the dais. Moss thickens by a hair’s breadth each season. The waterfall’s song changes only with the snowmelt. This is not a place of ruin, but of integration. The flood did not erase the village; it invited the forest to finish its work. The weirdness is gentle (0.9): the way light catches the otter’s path like a silver thread; the faint chime of quartz crystals embedded in the root-walls as current stirs them; the certainty that if you listen deeply, you hear not ghosts, but continuity—the same hands that shaped these stones now feed the roots that hold them.  

You float at the plaza’s heart. Cool water cradles you. Light dances on stone. A frog-vole’s song swells, then fades. Somewhere, a bubble rises.  
This is not a tomb.  
It is a breath held between mountain and sky,  
a quiet truth written in root and ripple:  
*What was built with care does not vanish.  
It learns to grow.*
