The water does not feel like water. It is a cold, viscous medium—thick as liquid obsidian, silent as forgotten memory—pressing against everything with the weight of millennia. You are suspended within the Glacial Aqueducts: not a city, not a ruin, but the skeletal remains of a world-spanning circulatory system, built by hands no myth recalls. Every surface is *made*. Smooth, seamless channels of fused silica-ceramic curve in impossible geometries—arches spanning abyssal distances, spiraling conduits wide enough to swallow mountains, all worn to soft, flowing contours by 2.8 units of erosion. Time has not shattered this place; it has *polished* it. Edges melted into gentle swells. Corners dissolved into liquid curves. The material itself seems to breathe with age, its original purpose sanded away until only form remains—a language of pure shape, now mute.

Light is a rumor. At 1.0 on the scale, it is less illumination than the *memory* of light. Faint, cold bioluminescence pulses in slow rhythms: cobalt-blue trails from drifting *void-jellies* (translucent, bell-shaped fauna with trailing filaments that write fleeting glyphs in the dark), or the sudden, silent flash of a *shard-eel* hunting in the current. Only mineral accretions: delicate frost-ferns of manganese dendrites blooming on surfaces, or slow-growing crystalline crusts that glitter like frozen stars when a jellyfish drifts past. Life here is faunal, predatory, symbiotic—a food web spun from chemosynthetic bacteria thriving in thermal seeps and the flesh of the hunted.

The cold (0.4) is absolute. It seeps into bone, a deep, clean chill that speaks of ice sealing the world above. You feel the *presence* of that ice ceiling—a kilometer-thick dome of black ice pressing down on the entire sea. Elevation (1.6) is felt not as height, but as *containment*: these aqueducts were built along a continental spine, now drowned. You drift beside a conduit wall that plunges into abyssal blackness below and rises toward the unseen ice above. Scale (2.0) unfolds slowly: a single arch may stretch half a league; beyond it, another curve vanishes into murk. Density (1.4) is the soul of this place—*space*. Vast, resonant voids between structures where silt hangs like frozen smoke. You are never crowded, only accompanied by absence.

And yet—*dynamic* (3.0). Turbulence is not storm or violence, but a silent, ceaseless reweaving. Currents shift without warning, born from geothermal vents hidden in the aqueduct’s heart. Fauna ride these shifts: jellies pulse urgently against a new current; shard-eels dart into eddies where prey is trapped. It is chaos with the patience of stone. Creation and destruction measured in centuries, not seconds.

Sound (1.1) is a study in restraint. The water swallows noise. What remains: the *tick-tick-tick* of stone-crawlers grooming crystalline growths; the low harmonic hum of water moving through a narrowing channel; the rare, deep groan of the ice ceiling shifting far above—a sound that travels for miles before fading. Mostly, silence. A profound, liquid quiet that makes your own heartbeat feel intrusive.

Weirdness (0.7) lingers at the edge of perception. The aqueducts’ geometry is *almost* familiar—arches echo Roman design, spirals recall nautilus shells—but the proportions are subtly wrong. A corridor narrows where logic demands widening. A junction branches into seven passages, not four. The fauna adapts to these anomalies: void-jellies nest in the "wrong" curves; shard-eels avoid the seventh passage of every junction, as if obeying a forgotten law. No technology remains (0.4). No wires, no lights, no machinery. Only the pure, eroded intention of the builders—whatever they were—now repurposed by life and physics. This is not a graveyard. It is a collaboration. The built world settled into its final form (erosion: ancient), and life moved in not as conqueror, but as tenant, dancer, participant.

You drift near a colossal arch, its surface veined with manganese frost-ferns. A school of needle-fish flashes silver as a void-jelly pulses beneath them—a silent signal. Current shifts. Silt rises like breath. For a moment, the arch seems to *lean*, not from decay, but from intention—a curve designed to guide this very flow. Then the moment passes. The water holds you. The ice holds the water. The stone holds the memory. And in the dim, cold, turbulent quiet, something ancient and made and alive continues its slow, patient work.
