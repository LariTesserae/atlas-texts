You do not float. You *rest*.  

The pressure is a constant, gentle certainty—not crushing, but complete. Cool water, the temperature of deep stone and forgotten springs, holds you suspended. There is no current. No drift. Only the immense, patient weight of the abyss. This is not the violent deep of trenches or vents. This is the *floor*—the absolute bottom of a drowned world, where elevation is measured not in height above sea, but in depth below sky: a negative extreme so profound the planet’s bones have sighed into stillness.  

Beneath your unseen hands lies the Mat.  

It is not soil. Not rock. Not sand. It is *built*. A single, seamless organism—neither plant nor fungus nor root, but a primordial synthesis of all three—has woven itself across the entire plain over epochs. Its surface is a tapestry of ivory-white fibers, thick as ancient rope, braided into resilient, spongy terrain. In places, slow chemical seeps from the planetary crust stain it the deep crimson of dried blood; elsewhere, it pulses with a faint, internal bioluminescence: soft moss-green, the blue of drowned stars. This is vegetation as total substrate. As architecture. As world. There is no gap, no patch of "natural" geology. The Mat has consumed the last memory of mountains, buried continents under its patient, living skin. Erosion did not wear this place down—it *perfected* it. This is the oldest stable state: the final form of a world that has settled into its own breath.  

The scale is vast—a plain stretching beyond perception, valley-sized, regional. Yet density is sparse. The Mat is continuous, unbroken, but *presences* are scattered like distant constellations. Fifty paces away (if paces existed here), a spire rises: a tower of densely woven fibers, thirty meters tall, hollow within. Bioluminescent polyps fringe its apex, casting a slow, rhythmic pulse of amber light across the fibers below. Within its chambers, blind amphipods the size of wolves move with deliberate grace. Farther still, a cluster of bell-shaped fungi sways imperceptibly, their translucent bodies glowing with captured chemical light, tended by colonies of six-legged grazers whose claws click softly against the Mat. Fauna is abundant—not in swarms, but in purposeful, spaced communities. A school of ribbon-fish, bodies lined with pinprick lanterns, flows past like a river of fallen stars. They do not startle. They do not flee. They simply *are*, part of the rhythm.  

And the sound… the sound is loud.  

Not chaotic. Not violent. But *present*. A deep, resonant hum vibrates through water and fiber alike—the planetary breath of tectonic plates resting in final alignment. Over it, the Mat itself speaks: a low creak of expanding fibers, the whisper of water filtering through microscopic channels deep within its structure. Clicks and whistles spiral through the dark—complex, melodic songs from leviathans whose forms are never seen, only heard. A sharp *crack* as a hunting squid snaps its beak. The rustle of grazers moving through fibrous thickets. In this medium, sound does not fade. It accumulates. It layers. It becomes the landscape’s true texture—a loudness that is not noise, but language. The language of equilibrium.  

Light is near-absent. Only the living provide illumination: the gentle pulse of fungi, the drifting sparks of planktonic symbiotes riding the ribbon-fish, the occasional flare of a startled jellyfish. Darkness is not emptiness here. It is a substance. A velvet medium through which light moves like thought.  

Time does not pass. It *accumulates*.  
Dynamic is 0.4/3: frozen equilibrium. The chemical seeps feed the crimson-stained zones at a rate unchanged for a million years. The spires grow upward by a hair’s breadth per century. Predator and prey exist in a dance so ancient it has no beginning or end—only balance. A ribbon-fish is taken; another is born in the spire’s hollow heart. No storm disturbs the water. No season shifts the temperature. No gradient of heat or current breaks the stillness. This is not stagnation. It is completion. The world has breathed its last breath and found peace in the exhale.  

The weirdness is subtle. Unsettling only to an outsider.  
Why do the bioluminescent patterns on the spires sometimes mirror the spiral of a galaxy long vanished from the sky above? Why does the Mat’s hum occasionally resolve into a single, sustained note that vibrates in the marrow of listening bones? These are not mysteries to the inhabitants. They are simply *true*. Like the coolness of the water. Like the sparseness of the spires. Like the certainty of the dark.  

You rest within the oldest silence that is not silent.  
Within the deepest dark that is alive.  
This is not a place of endings. It is a place that has *finished becoming*.  
The Abyssal Mat holds you.  
It has always held you.  
It will hold you long after the concept of "you" has dissolved into the cool, humming dark.
