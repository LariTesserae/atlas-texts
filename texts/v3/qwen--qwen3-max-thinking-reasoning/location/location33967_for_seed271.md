The basin is no larger than a quiet courtyard, a shallow depression cupped within weathered sandstone. Its walls rise only waist-high to a person, yet they curve inward with the gentle inevitability of a smoothed river stone, enclosing the space completely. There is no visible path in or out. The stone itself is the color of dried clay, veined with faint iron rust, worn by time into soft, rounded contours—no sharp edges remain. This is erosion not as violence, but as completion. The land has settled into itself.

Light arrives diffused and diluted. A perpetual, high overcast blankets the sky, filtering the sun into a uniform, pearly luminescence that casts no shadows, deepens no hollows. It is the light of deep twilight held constant—a soft gray wash that reveals detail without drama. The air is warm, a steady breath against the skin, carrying the clean, mineral scent of damp earth and wet stone. Humidity lingers just below the threshold of discomfort: a faint sheen on the sandstone, a cool kiss where moss clings.

Three shallow basins, no wider than a handspan, hold water. They are not puddles left by rain, but seeps—places where moisture beads steadily along hairline fractures in the stone, gathering into still, obsidian-dark pools. The water does not ripple. It does not evaporate. It simply *is*, mirror-bright, reflecting the unchanging sky. Around their rims, life persists in whispers: emerald moss forms tight, velvety cushions; pale sage lichen traces delicate, fractal maps across the stone. There are no stems, no leaves, no roots visible—only these low, silent colonies, their edges precise, their forms unaltered. They do not grow toward the light; they do not retreat from dryness. They exist in perfect stasis, as if painted onto the world.

Silence is the dominant presence. Not an empty silence, but a dense, textured quiet. Within it, a single sound repeats with metronomic certainty: *plip*. A drop forms at the tip of a stone lip on the eastern wall. It swells, trembles, falls into the nearest pool. A perfect ring expands across the water’s surface—*plip*—and vanishes before the next begins. The interval is exact. Unvarying. This is not a sound that breaks the silence; it is the silence given rhythm. There is no wind to stir imagined grasses. No rustle. No buzz. No distant cry. The absence of fauna is not a void to be noted, but a fundamental condition of the place—like the absence of salt in fresh water. It simply *is*. No tracks mar the hard-packed clay floor. No feathers, no scat, no snapped twigs. The world here has never known a heartbeat but its own slow, geological pulse.

Near the center, seven river-smoothed stones rest in a loose circle. Each fits comfortably in two hands. They are darkened by the same lichen that adorns the walls, their surfaces uncarved, unmarked by tool or symbol. Whether placed by intention or settled by some forgotten tremor of earth is unknowable—and irrelevant. Time has erased the question. They do not shift. Moss does not creep over them. They hold their positions with the quiet authority of mountains. This is the only suggestion of a hand, a mind, a moment long absorbed back into the stone. It feels less like ruin and more like punctuation—a period at the end of a sentence the land wrote centuries ago.

The rules here are gentle but absolute.  
*Water finds its level and remains.*  
*Light softens but does not fade.*  
*Temperature holds its breath.*  
*What is placed remains placed.*  
*What grows does not spread.*  
*What is still remains still.*

This is not a place waiting for change. It is not dormant, nor sleeping. It is complete. A single, self-contained breath held forever. The boundaries are clear: the curved sandstone wall, the rim against the unchanging sky. Within them, equilibrium is not an ideal—it is the substance of reality. To stand here is to feel the mind quiet its own frantic rhythms, to align with the drip, the stillness, the unwavering warmth. There is no melancholy in the emptiness. No loneliness in the solitude. Only a profound and gentle fullness—a world reduced to its essence, and finding that essence entirely sufficient. Time does not pass here. It pools, like the water in the stone basins: deep, clear, and utterly still.
