You do not float. You *hang*. Suspended in a liquid so cold it feels less like water and more like solidified shadow, pressing in from all sides with the patient weight of millennia. This is the under-ice sea of the Sky-Plateau—a vast, high-altitude basin drowned beneath a glacier two miles thick. The surface world is a myth. Here, there is only the deep cold, the crushing dark, and the slow, breathing pulse of the ice above.

At first, there is nothing. Then, the darkness *breathes back*.

Faint light bleeds from the lake floor: a sickly, pulsing cerulean. It traces the contours of a drowned world. Rolling hills of smoothed granite rise like sleeping leviathans, their slopes draped in the Veilweeds. These are no plants of sunlit memory. They are forests of crystalline kelp, each stalk thick as a wrist, veined with internal luminescence that swells and fades in slow, hypnotic waves—*pulse… dim… pulse*. Between them, carpets of moss-like fungi glow with a faint green static, while delicate, hair-thin filaments drift in the currents like drowned starlight. This is the "lushness": a silent, radiant jungle thriving on geothermal whispers and mineral tears leaching from the bedrock. It grows without haste, without witness. There are no grazers to prune it, no predators to flee. It simply *is*—a lushness of absolute solitude.

Scattered across the abyssal plain, half-swallowed by time and sediment, stand the bones of another time. A ring of standing stones, each carved with spirals and concentric circles now traced in glowing lichen. The foundation of a circular hut, its stone hearth filled with silt and pulsating moss. A single, weathered menhir tilted at a grave angle, its surface smoothed by ten thousand years of gentle current. These are the only marks of hands. Primitive. Stone-on-stone craftsmanship, swallowed whole when the ice came. Erosion has not destroyed them; it has *adopted* them. Water has rounded their edges. Minerals have veined their surfaces. They are not ruins anymore—they are geology now, resting in balance with the deep.

And the sound—*oh, the sound*. It is never silent. A deep, tectonic groan vibrates through the water, a bass note felt in the marrow before it reaches the ear. *CRACK-THOOOM*. A splintering roar echoes from the ice ceiling—a fracture miles long, collapsing into itself. Then the reply: a low, grinding rumble as continents of ice shift overhead. Currents, born from the glacier’s slow exhalation and faint geothermal vents along a distant ridge, rush past with a constant, hollow roar. Occasionally, a stream of silver bubbles hisses upward from a fissure—a brief, fizzy whisper before being devoured by the deep. This is not the sound of life. It is the sound of the world *breathing*: ice, water, stone in relentless, ancient conversation.

You drift toward a valley. The scale unfolds slowly. The nearest ridge is a faint glow on the horizon. Below, the terrain plunges into absolute black—a trench where even the Veilweeds fear to grow. Space is the dominant presence. Vast. Empty. The glowing forests cluster in patches around thermal seeps, leaving great plains of bare, dark stone between them. Density is not absence—it is *intention*. Each cluster of light feels chosen. Each ruin feels placed. The emptiness is not void; it is reverence.

Light does not fall here. It *blooms*. And in its faint pulses, strangeness reveals itself. The Veilweeds do not grow randomly. Their light flashes in complex, repeating sequences—three slow pulses, a rapid flicker, a long dimming—as if counting time no creature measures. Near the stone circle, the water hums at a frequency that vibrates the teeth. Touch the lichen-covered menhir, and the glow along its carvings flares once, sharply, before returning to its rhythm. There are no animals. No scales, no shells, no bones. Only this silent, radiant flora and the stones it claims. The weirdness is not menace. It is *protocol*. A language of light and stone in a world that forgot it needed listeners.

You hang in the current, weightless. The ice groans again—a sound of immense age and indifferent power. Below, the Veilweeds pulse their slow, patient code. A bubble rises. A current shifts. The stone circle glows. This place is not dead. It is *complete*. It has settled into its own truth: a cold, dark, singing cathedral of water and memory, where light is a verb, silence is an illusion, and the only witnesses are the stones—and the ice, dreaming deeply above.
