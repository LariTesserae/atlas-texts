You step through a tear in the world—a fissure no wider than your shoulders, hidden behind a curtain of heat-hazed air where two fractured basalt slabs lean together. Inside, the pocket exhales.

The air is a thick, warm broth. At 2.7 on the scale, the heat doesn’t just sit; it *presses*, heavy with moisture rising from the ground. Water isn’t pooled—it *weeps*. Every surface glistens. Beads swell along hairline fractures in the young rock (erosion 0.6: sharp-edged, unweathered, raw as a fresh scar), then tremble and fall. A constant, silver-threaded drizzle descends not from sky but from the ceiling itself—a porous layer of pumice-like stone sweating condensation. Beneath your boots, the floor is a mosaic of damp clay, fractured shale, and patches of coarse, crystalline salt left by evaporated seeps. No mud clings; the heat drinks what the stone releases almost as fast as it forms.

Vegetation is a ghost here (1.1). A single, skeletal *tamarisk* twig, bleached bone-white, is jammed in a crevice—dead for seasons, preserved by heat and absence. In one shadowed hollow, a dusting of chartreuse microbial crust clings to a damp patch, fluorescing faintly where the light catches it. That is all. No roots disturb the clay. No leaves whisper. The silence of life is absolute (fauna 0.3)—no buzz, no scuttle, no breath but your own.

And yet the place *roars* (sound 2.5).

From the heart of the pocket rises the source: a fractured industrial relic. A copper-and-steel condenser coil, no larger than a washtub, lies half-buried in the clay. It shouldn’t function. Yet deep within its corroded veins, a slow, pressurized pulse thrums—a relic of forgotten infrastructure (tech 1.6, built 1.2). Each cycle forces superheated vapor through a hairline crack in its casing. *HISSSS—click… HISSSS—click…* The rhythm is glacial (dynamic 0.6), a heartbeat measured in minutes, not seconds. With every release, a plume of steam jets upward, striking the weeping ceiling. The impact is a sharp *shhk-shhk-shhk*, amplified by the chamber’s perfect acoustics. Droplets shaken loose by the vibration fall like accelerated rain. The sound doesn’t echo—it *fills*, a physical presence vibrating in your teeth, your ribs. Loudness contained in intimacy.

Light (1.8) filters through the fissure’s mouth: ordinary noon sun, but fractured. It catches the steam plumes in diagonal blades, illuminating swirling galaxies of mineral dust. Where light touches the condenser’s brass fittings, they glow like embers. Elsewhere, shadows pool deep and cool-looking, though they hold no relief from the heat. On the western wall, someone—long ago—scratched a single glyph: a circle bisected by a wavy line. Water? Warning? Offering? The mark is faint, precise, unanswered.

This is the weirdness (0.8), subtle as a held breath:  
The steam doesn’t dissipate. It curls downward after hitting the ceiling, pooling in the low hollows like liquid mercury before vanishing. The water weeping from the eastern wall tastes of iron and ozone. And if you watch the condenser’s rhythmic sigh long enough, you notice the *click* isn’t mechanical. It’s the sound of a tiny, perfect shard of obsidian—lodged in the valve—tapping against steel. A natural stone, perfectly seated in a machine. Intentional? Impossible? The pocket offers no judgment.

Time moves like cooling slag. The salt crystals grow grain by grain. The steam-plumes trace the same paths century after century. This place is not decaying. It is *persisting*. A patient paradox: industrial breath in a geological lung, loud yet lonely, wet yet barren, hidden yet demanding all your attention. You feel the heat on your skin, the vibration in your bones, the profound absence of any witness but the stone. To leave is to step back into silence. To stay is to learn the language of slow fire and weeping rock. The pocket does not care which you choose. It will hum on, long after your footsteps fade.
