You find it by accident. A stumble on the highland plateau’s wind-raked stone, a hand bracing against a seam in the rock—and your fingers meet not grit, but cool, damp smoothness. You turn. Where moments ago was only endless, ochre scrub and sky, a fissure now reveals itself: no taller than your shoulder, shaped like a breath held too long. Step through.

The world shrinks to a single bowl of stone, no wider than a dining table. This is not a valley, not a canyon—a *pocket*. Carved not by rivers, but by time itself. Eons of wind and grit have sanded the surrounding plateau into gentle, whaleback hummocks, and here, in this final sigh of erosion, the land folded inward. The stone underfoot is seamless, polished to the sheen of river glass, curving upward like the inside of a colossal seedpod worn smooth by tides of millennia. It feels primordial. Not ruined. *Complete*. As if this hollow is the land’s last, settled thought.

Instead, moisture weeps from the stone itself. Where the pocket’s western curve catches the morning light, a faint, silver sheen glistens. Condensation. It beads along hairline fractures in the rock, then trembles, falls. *Plink*. A single drop into a moss-cup. This is the water-cycle of this world: breath of stone, gift of air.

And the moss *thrives*. Vegetation at 1.7 is not jungle, but intimate lushness. Velvet mosses in shades of emerald and rust cling to the damp curves. Delicate ferns, no taller than your thumb, unfurl from micro-cracks. A scatter of star-shaped blossoms, petals like crushed sapphire, nod beside them. But the density is sparse (0.9). Space is sacred here. Between each cluster of green, expanses of bare, warm stone breathe. You see every dewdrop, every vein in a leaf. It is lushness earned, not given.

It is not alone. Dozens perch, their collective hum a bass note beneath the insect chorus. They are not ignoring you. They observe. Their multifaceted eyes catch the light. This is their cathedral. You are the transient shadow.

In one shadowed curve, the stone is cool. In the sunlit center, it radiates stored warmth. The dynamic (1.9) is constant, intimate: a drop falls. A sprite drinks it. A predator—a needle-thin wasp with eyes like molten gold—darts, snatches a midge. The origami-winged sentinels shift posture as the sun moves. Life cycles in seconds. This pocket is not static. It is a single, beating heart.

The strangeness deepens if you linger. The humming stones cast no shadow. The moss grows *toward* the monolith, not the light. Time softens at the edges. A minute feels like ten. The overwhelming fauna does not flee; it incorporates you. A sprite pauses on your bootlace, antennae twitching. It is not fear you feel, but awe. Step back through the fissure. The vast, sparse plateau awaits—wind-scoured, silent, immense. But turn once more. The pocket is already hiding itself again, the seam in the rock blurring into the general weathering. Only the memory remains: the warmth of the stone under your palm, the deafening, beautiful chorus of tiny lives, the hum in your bones. A secret the oldest earth keeps for itself. A cradle of damp stone and impossible care, where technology became tenderness, and life, in its overwhelming multitude, sings a single, resonant note into the deep silence of time.
