You step into the Stone Choir.  

It is not a valley, not a crater, but a shallow, bowl-shaped depression worn smooth over epochs—a single breath held in the skin of the world. The plateau beyond is a featureless expanse of wind-scoured granite, pale as bone under a sky of clean, ordinary daylight. But here, within this hamlet-sized hollow (no larger than a village green), the earth has folded inward. The scale is intimate, bounded by a low, seamless lip of rock that feels less like a rim and more like a threshold. You are *inside* something.  

The air is cold—sharp enough to sting the nostrils, to crystallize each exhale into fleeting ghosts. Yet it carries moisture: a dampness that seeps up from the stone itself. Overnight condensation beads on every surface, gathering in thumb-sized hollows where the rock dips. By midday, these pockets gleam like scattered mercury, fed by a mist that rises not from rain (too rare) but from the breath of the stone meeting the thin, cold air. This is the water here: not rivers, not lakes, but a patient, capillary dampness—a slow sweat of the earth.  

And in these damp hollows, life clings with quiet insistence. Velvet mosses the color of emerald and rust spill over stone edges. Cushion plants, dense and star-shaped, nestle in cracks. Lichen paints intricate maps in silver and saffron across horizontal surfaces. *Lush* is not a word of abundance here, but of defiance. Each patch of green is a tiny rebellion against the cold and the age, a concentrated pulse of vitality in a world of stone. You see no trees. Nothing taller than your knee. But where life exists, it is thick, soft, deeply alive.  

The built environment is the heart of the strangeness. Rising from the floor of the hollow are hundreds of stone formations—not jagged spikes, but smoothed, rounded pillars, arches, and low walls. They stand at human height, some leaning companionably against one another, others isolated like solitary sentinels. They resemble the ruins of a city built for gentle giants: a plaza of fallen lintels, a corridor of leaning columns framing slivers of sky, a circular arrangement that might have been a council chamber. Yet there is no mortar, no tool mark, no sign of intent. This is erosion’s final, exquisite signature. Over uncountable millennia, wind and ice and the faintest trace of water have carved the soft granite into these forms. They are not ruins *of* something. They *are* the something. The city is the erosion. The architecture is geology dreaming of order.  

Sound fills the hollow, loud and layered. The wind does not howl—it *sings*. It pours through keyhole-shaped openings in the arches, producing low, resonant hums that vibrate in your chest. It whistles across the lip of a stone bowl, creating a clear, flute-like note that hangs in the air. Smaller eddies whisper through moss-lined crevices. Beneath it all, a constant, granular hiss: wind-scoured sand trickling down slopes, grains of granite returning to dust. Occasionally, a *plink*—a drop of condensed moisture falling from a ledge into a hidden pool. The sounds are not chaotic. They harmonize. Shift with the wind’s direction. Sometimes, for three breaths, the hollow falls silent—and the silence feels heavier than the noise.  

Fauna is a rumor. A spiderweb, strung between two pillars like a harp string, glistens with dew. Once, a shadow flickers—a ptarmigan, feathered white for snow that rarely falls here now, hopping silently between moss patches before vanishing. No tracks. No nests. Life is sparse, transient, respectful of the stone’s dominion.  

The weirdness lives in the details. Sunlight, ordinary and golden, strikes a quartz vein in a pillar and ignites it with sudden fire. A shadow cast by an arch at noon forms the perfect silhouette of a seated figure—gone when you blink. The wind’s song sometimes resolves, just for a moment, into a phrase that feels like a word you almost remember. The stone is warm where the sun touches it, cold in the shade—a tactile map of light and time. This is not magic. It is attention. The place demands you notice.  

Rules of this world:  
- *Nothing is buried.* Time has planed the land to its essence. What happened here is written on the surface.  
- *Water is memory.* It does not flow; it lingers. Each damp patch holds the history of the last mist, the last breath of warmth.  
- *Sound is substance.* To speak here is to add your voice to the choir. To be still is to listen to the stone breathe.  
- *Growth is patience.* A moss patch expands a fingernail’s width per decade. To rush is to miss the only pace that matters.  

You stand in the center of the hollow. The cold is a clarity. The emptiness is not void, but space—space for sound to resonate, for light to pool, for a single drop of water to fall with ceremony. This is not a place of beginnings or endings. It is a place that has settled into its final, graceful truth. Ancient, yes. But alive. Every hum of wind, every bead of condensation, every stubborn patch of moss is a quiet, ongoing act of becoming. The Stone Choir does not wait for you. It has been singing its slow, loud, beautiful song since before memory. And it will sing long after you leave, the damp stone holding the shape of your footsteps for exactly one sunrise.
