You find yourself in the Throat.

It is not a cavern, not a tunnel—not in any way you’d name above ground. This is a pocket carved not by rivers or faults, but by hands. Primitive hands. Stone chisels and antler picks, worn smooth by generations, shaped this space no larger than a burial niche. The walls are rough-hewn basalt, slick with a perpetual weep. Water does not fall here; it *breathes*. A faint condensation beads across every surface, feeding shallow, hand-gouged runnels in the floor that carry a thread of liquid—clear, cold, tasting of deep stone and iron. The air is cool against the skin, damp enough to raise gooseflesh, heavy with the scent of wet rock and ancient dust.

There is no green. No moss, no root, no lichen. Where life might cling elsewhere, mineral has claimed dominion. The walls are dense—*crowded*—with accretions: milky calcite feathers, rust-orange iron blooms, delicate soda-straw stalactites no longer growing but preserved like frozen breath. They layer over faint, pecked glyphs left by the builders—spirals, concentric circles, shapes that hint at eyes or seeds. These mineral forests press inward from all sides, nearly touching across the narrow space. You must turn sideways to stand. This is density not of objects, but of *presence*: every inch of stone speaks of slow deposition, of time made visible.

Light is a memory. A single, hairline fracture in the ceiling admits a sliver of bruised twilight—just enough to glint off wet crystal and deepen the shadows into absolute black. In that faint gleam, the mineral forms twist into suggestions: a grasping hand, a weeping face, a coiled serpent. Pareidolia blooms in the dimness. This is the weirdness—not monstrous, not magical, but a quiet cognitive dissonance. The patterns feel *intended*, though no tool could have shaped them. The builders left symbols; the stone answered with echoes.

And the sound. Oh, the sound. It is loud—not chaotic, but immense in this confinement. A deep, resonant *hum* vibrates up through the soles of your feet, a constant thrumming born where the underground stream meets a narrow constriction just beyond the chamber’s rear wall. It is the voice of the aquifer itself: a low C-sharp held for centuries. Water drips in precise rhythm into the runnel—*plink… plink… plink*—each drop swallowed by the hum. No wind. No creature’s cry. Only this geological choir: the drone of pressure, the percussion of falling water. It is loud enough to thrum in your teeth, to still frantic thoughts. Yet it never varies. Not by a vibration. Not by a drop. This is the equilibrium. The dynamic is not stillness, but perfect, unbroken repetition. A frozen river of sound.

Fauna is a rumor. Once, in the periphery of hearing, a skitter—dry, chitinous. A blind pseudoscorpion, pale as bone, vanishing into a crevice. Rare. Transient. It does not belong here; it merely endures.

This place is a node. A hidden valve in a forgotten urban circulatory system. The builders—pre-metal, guided by star-lore and stone-knowledge—carved these micro-chambers beneath their city to listen. To monitor the water-song. A change in pitch meant a shift in the deep currents; a silence, drought or collapse. They left no gears, no levers. Only this intimate room, this ear pressed to the planet’s throat. The erosion is profound: edges softened by millennia of moisture, glyphs blurred to ghosts, yet the chamber holds its form with quiet authority. It has settled into its purpose. It *is* its purpose.

You stand where a sentinel once stood, palm flat against the weeping wall, feeling the hum travel up the arm. The air is cool. The light is a sliver. The water sings the same note it sang when the city above was young. Nothing changes. Nothing is meant to. This is not a place of discovery, but of witness. A dense, loud, mineral heart beating one steady rhythm in the deep dark—a secret the earth keeps, and keeps, and keeps.
