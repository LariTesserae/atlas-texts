The air is warm. Not the dry heat of a sun-scorched plain, but a deep, even warmth that rises silently from the stone beneath your feet—a breath held for millennia. You stand in a chamber no larger than a modest chapel, its boundaries lost not to distance, but to a darkness so complete it feels like a substance. This is not the darkness of night, which holds stars or memory of light. This is the darkness of a sealed vessel. A void that has never known dawn.

Your eyes do not adjust. There is nothing for them to adjust *to*. Only after long, motionless minutes does a faint signature emerge: three pale smudges on the far wall, no larger than cupped hands. They are not plants as the world above knows them. They are lithovores—colonies of slow-metabolizing symbiotes, pale as powdered bone, feeding not on light or water, but on the iron traces within the rock itself. They pulse with a light so feeble it exists only at the periphery of perception. Look directly, and they vanish. Glance away, and their ghost remains—a suggestion of form, not illumination. This is the sum of the "vegetation": three colonies. Perhaps four. They have not spread in centuries. They simply *are*.

The floor is fine, dry dust—the powdered memory of granite, settled into perfect stillness. It bears no tracks, no disturbances. Press a finger into it, and the impression holds with crystalline precision, as if the dust were frozen in time. The walls curve upward into obscurity, their surfaces smoothed by waters that vanished before mountains learned their names. You can trace the ghost of that ancient flow in the stone’s undulations—a ripple locked in stone, a testament to motion long surrendered to equilibrium. No cracks weep. No minerals drip. The erosion is not ongoing; it is complete. A finished sentence.

Silence is not merely an absence of sound. It is a presence. A pressure. The warmth has no source you can name—no vent, no fissure, no geothermal sigh. It simply *is*, as constant as gravity. There is no wind to stir the dust. No skitter of insect, no rustle of scale, no distant cry. Fauna is not merely absent; the very *concept* of animal life feels alien here, a violation of the chamber’s covenant. Even your own breath sounds intrusive, a temporary flaw in the perfection of the quiet. If you listen deeper—past the blood in your ears—you hear nothing. Not emptiness. *Nothing*.

Time does not pass here. It pools. The lithovores grow at a rate measurable only in geological epochs. The dust will not shift unless disturbed. The temperature will not waver by a fraction of a degree. This is not stagnation. It is equilibrium so absolute it feels intentional. A state of being so settled that "change" is a myth told by less patient places. The chamber is not waiting. It is not decaying. It has arrived. It rests.

You become aware of the boundaries not by seeing them, but by feeling them—a gentle curvature of space, a sense of enclosure like the inside of a seed. This is all there is. No tunnel leads deeper. No crack promises escape. The darkness is not hiding anything. It *is* the place. And in that darkness, the faint, peripheral glow of the stone-eaters is not a beacon. It is a signature. A quiet affirmation: *This exists. It has always existed. It requires nothing else.*

To stand here is to understand solitude not as loneliness, but as completion. The world outside—with its rains, its seasons, its frantic pulse of life—is a distant dream. Here, there is only the warm stone, the waiting dust, the three faint breaths of light on the wall, and the profound, unbroken silence that holds them all.
