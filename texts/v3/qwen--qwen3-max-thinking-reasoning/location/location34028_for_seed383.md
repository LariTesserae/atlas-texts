You descend not by stairs, but by memory. A fissure in the world’s skin, worn smooth by epochs of patient seepage, leads downward into the Sunken Basin of K’t’har. The air shifts first—cool (1.3), carrying the mineral scent of wet limestone and cold stone. Above, the sky is a perfect, distant coin of brilliant daylight (light: 2.6), but you are *below*. This is no cave. This is a basin—a vast, bowl-shaped depression carved over eons into the bedrock of a silent plateau. Its walls slope gently inward, smoothed to undulating curves by the highest erosion rating imaginable (2.6). Nothing jagged remains. Nothing sharp has survived. Time has sanded the world down to its essential bones.

Sunlight floods the basin, but it does not strike directly. Halfway up the encircling walls hangs the Veil: a permanent, waist-high layer of condensed mist, fed by the cool basin air meeting the warmer plateau winds above. It diffuses the sun into a soft, directionless radiance that gilds every surface in liquid gold. Shadows are whispers. Depth is a suggestion. The light is bright, yet gentle—a luminous silence.

Beneath your feet, the city sleeps. Or perhaps it breathes. Structures of fitted limestone rise in low, organic clusters across the basin floor (built: 2.2). There are no right angles. Walls curve like fossilized shells, doorways are low arches worn seamless by touch and time. This is primitive technology (0.7) elevated to geology: stones shifted by generations of hands, mortared with crushed shale and lime, layered upon older foundations now half-swallowed by the earth. A plaza of packed clay and gravel stretches toward a central terrace where a single, smooth monolith stands—not carved, but *chosen*, its surface polished by ritual and rain. The settlement is sparse (0.7). Between clusters of dwellings lie open spaces: fields of wind-scoured gravel, circles of deliberately placed river-smoothed stones, shallow depressions where moisture gathers. The scale is open (1.9)—you can see the entire basin unfold, the opposite wall a soft grey arc under the glowing Veil, the city a scattered constellation of stone under a sky of light.

Water is a ghost here (0.8). After rare rains that sheet down the outer plateau and funnel into the basin’s rim, temporary rivulets trace silver veins across the stone before vanishing into fissures. In the cool hours, condensation bleeds from the northern walls, tracing slow, glistening paths down to collect in natural basins worn into the bedrock—pools no larger than a handspan, their surfaces trembling with the basin’s breath. You lick your lips; the air tastes of damp stone and ozone.

There are no leaves. No stems. No green (vegetation: 0.3). Life here rejected chlorophyll long ago. In the dampest hollows, where water lingers longest, colonies of lithic fungi cling to the rock: pale, rubbery shelves the color of bone, veined with faint, pulsing cerulean light. They do not photosynthesize. They *consume*—slowly dissolving minerals from the stone, exhaling a scent like wet chalk and cold honey. These are the basin’s only flora. Sparse. Silent. Essential.

And yet, the basin teems (fauna: 1.5). But you must learn to see. Stone-skitters, no larger than your thumb, move with liquid silence across sun-warmed rock. Their carapaces are perfect mimics of limestone, etched with crystalline patterns that catch the diffuse light. They graze the glowing fungi, their movements a silent ballet. Above, silent flyers—creatures of translucent membrane and delicate bone—drift on thermal currents within the Veil. They navigate by senses beyond sound, their passage stirring the mist into fleeting spirals. In the soft earth of the plazas, burrowers weave intricate subterranean cities, their activity visible only as subtle, shifting patterns of tiny vents that breathe cool, damp air. They are abundant, yet never crowded. Each creature holds its space in the sparse tapestry (density: 0.7). Life is everywhere, yet never dense.

The silence is absolute (sound: 0.2). The Veil absorbs vibration. The soft earth swallows footsteps. You hear only the intimate sounds: the soft *plink* of a single water droplet falling into a stone basin; the whisper of your own breath; the faint, dry rustle of a skitter’s leg brushing stone—a sound felt more in the jaw than heard by the ear. There are no cries, no wind howls, no rustling leaves. The quiet is not empty. It is *full* of absence.

This is where turbulence lives (dynamic: 2.8). Not in noise, but in light, in stone, in breath. The Veil is never still. It swirls in chaotic, silent eddies—thickening into opaque curtains that swallow distant ruins, then thinning to reveal them anew, all in the span of a held breath. Beneath your soles, the basin breathes. Micro-tremors, echoes of deep tectonic patience, vibrate through the stone. A pebble shifts on a slope. A patch of gravel settles with a soft sigh. Overnight, a new seep appears on the eastern wall; a familiar damp patch on the western wall dries to a salt-crusted scar. The silent flyers swarm in sudden, silent clouds that dissolve as quickly as they form—a flicker of movement with no sound to anchor it. The city itself is in flux: a section of wall, weakened by a thousand cycles of damp and dry, slumps inward at dawn, becoming a new slope for moss-fungi to claim. Change is constant, relentless, and utterly quiet. This is turbulence as geology. As breath. As light.

The slight weirdness (1.1) is in the details. The fungi pulse in slow, irregular rhythms unrelated to any visible stimulus. Sometimes, when the Veil parts just so, the light catches the monolith on the terrace and for three heartbeats it appears not as stone, but as solidified water. The burrowers’ vent patterns, if traced, resemble the star-charts carved (and long since eroded smooth) on the oldest foundation stones. Coincidence? The basin offers no answers. Only presence.

**Rules of this world:**  
- Light reveals only what the mist allows. Distance is a lie.  
- Sound does not travel. To communicate, you touch stone. You arrange pebbles. You wait.  
- Water is memory. It appears where the stone remembers being wet.  
- The city is not abandoned. It is *inhabited by process*. The builders are gone, but their work continues through erosion, condensation, and the silent industry of stone-skitters who carry fungal spores on their legs, replanting the basin’s only gardens.  
- To disturb is to be erased. A footprint in the plaza gravel will be smoothed by burrowers before sunset. A mark carved into stone will be softened by the next dew. The basin accepts only what aligns with its ancient rhythm.  

You stand in the central plaza as the light begins to deepen toward amber. A tremor—felt, not heard—passes through the stone. High on the western wall, a fist-sized fragment of limestone detaches. It falls in utter silence, tracing a slow arc through the glowing mist, and lands with a soft puff of dust in a patch of cool earth. A burrower’s vent nearby stirs, exhaling a breath of damp air. The Veil swirls. For a moment, the monolith gleams like liquid. Then the mist shifts, and the light settles back into its eternal, gentle hum. The basin holds its breath. And continues.
