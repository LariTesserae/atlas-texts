The land breathes moisture. Not in rivers or lakes, but in the slow exhalation of the earth itself. This is a plain worn down to its bones—a peneplain so ancient its mountains are memory, its valleys softened into gentle undulations no higher than a sleeping shoulder. The horizon is a clean, unbroken seam where hazy sky meets damp earth, stretching for miles under a soft, diffuse daylight that holds no sharp edges. Warmth hangs in the air, thick and gentle as a wool blanket, carrying the scent of wet clay, mineral salts, and the faint sweetness of decaying reed.

Beneath your feet, the ground is a mosaic of absence and presence. Vast expanses of bare, iron-streaked clay gleam dully where recent rain has pooled and retreated, leaving cracked polygons like shattered pottery. In lower hollows, water lingers—not deep, but pervasive. Shallow seeps bead along the bases of the only vertical features: low, whalebacked hummocks of pale quartzite, smoothed by a billion years of wind and water. These stones weep steadily. Droplets gather in patient beads along their curved flanks, tracing slow paths through rust-colored lichen before vanishing into the thirsty clay. Where water pools persistently, the soil surrenders to black, gelatinous mud that releases a soft *shluck* with each step, exhaling bubbles of ancient air.
