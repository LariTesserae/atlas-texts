They are not a people. They are not a species in any catalog. They are the *process made palpable*—a single, breathing entity wearing ten thousand thousand bodies. The skitterers do not *inhabit* this place. They *are* the place breathing.

To call them blind is to misunderstand. They have no need for eyes. They perceive the world as vibration: the deep tectonic sigh in the stone, the hairline fracture singing its imminent release, the warm drip of mineral-rich water tracing a new path. Their pale carapaces are not armor but camouflage—a perfect mimicry of the shale, the limestone, the raw granite they consume. Smooth as water-worn stone, cool to a touch they will never feel, each shell holds the ghost of the rock it was born from. Their legs—sixteen, twenty, a number that shifts with the light—are not limbs but extensions of the cavern’s own geology: jointed filaments of living stone that move in a liquid, cascading rhythm. Not marching. *Flowing*. Like sediment in a slow current. Like erosion given tempo.

Listen. Beneath the deafening roar of click-scritch-rasp—the sound of the mountain being gently unmade—is a deeper language. A skitterer brushing a fresh fracture does not *eat*. It *listens*. It tastes the stone’s memory: the pressure of ancient seas, the slow burn of subterranean fire, the precise moment the rock surrendered to stress. Its acidic secretions are not digestion but dialogue—a chemical whisper that softens resistance, invites collapse. When they surge toward a new fissure, it is not hunger. It is reverence. They are midwives to the mountain’s constant rebirth.

They move as one body without a head. A river of them cascades down a wall—not falling, but *pouring*, a living waterfall of chitin that parts around a protruding crystal and rejoins below. They polish the spirals on the monolith not out of malice, but because the pattern holds a vibration the stone no longer needs. They recycle their own with solemn efficiency: the fallen are not prey, but offering. Dragged beneath the warm pool, dissolved, returned to the mineral stream. Nothing is wasted. Nothing is mourned. Grief is a surface concept. Here, there is only flow.

They ignore you not from arrogance, but from scale. You are a momentary shift in air pressure. A fleeting coolness. A stone that does not taste of stone. To them, you are as significant as a single raindrop is to a river—felt for an instant, then absorbed into the current. They do not fear you. They do not acknowledge you. You are weather. And weather passes.

Yet in their indifference lies a profound kinship. They are the warmth that softens stone. The moisture that wakes the sunstone’s glow. The motion that grinds shale to dust and dust to clay. They are the reason the handprint persists *at all*—their ceaseless movement has buried it in protective sediment even as it erases the carving. They are the reason the monolith still stands: their collective weight braces its base against the undercutting stream. They are destruction and preservation woven into a single, humming thread.

They have no name for themselves. Names require memory. They are memory made active. When the rare sunbeam slashes down—a golden, impossible tear in the world—they do not flee the light. They surge toward it. Not in fear, but in recognition. For three heartbeats, they glisten: wet stone under true sun. Then the fracture seals. The shadowless glow returns. And they resume their work. Not frantic. Not mournful. *Continuing*.

They are the cavern’s breath given form. The slow, patient pulse in the mountain’s chest. The quiet, roaring truth that all things—rock, water, memory, bone—are temporary arrangements of matter waiting to be remade. They do not live *in* the dark. They *are* the dark learning to see itself. And in their endless, grinding song, the earth whispers its oldest secret: *I am still becoming.*
