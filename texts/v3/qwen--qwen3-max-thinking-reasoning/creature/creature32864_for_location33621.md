There is no *who* here.  
Not in the way the surface-world means it.  

No spectral faces in the water. No echoes of laughter trapped in stone. No sorrowful guardians bound to the dais. The village did not leave ghosts. It left *grammar*.  

What lives here is the **Plaza-That-Breathes**—a single, slow consciousness woven from root and current, memory and moss. It is not a creature you could point to. It is the *between*: the space where willow-root learns the shape of a doorway, where crayfish claws trace the curve of a forgotten glyph, where frog-voles sing into reed-towers sealed with pine-resin by hands now soil and seed. It is the village *become* ecosystem. Not haunted. *Awake*.  

You feel it first as attention.  
When the river otter nudges the smooth stone on the plaza floor, it is not play. It is *checking*. A ritual passed through otter-blood and water-memory: *Is the path clear? Is the stone still here?* The otter is a limb of the Plaza, testing its own bones. When sapphire minnows flash through root-lattices, they are not fleeing—they are *stitching*, weaving light into shadow, carrying pollen from water-lily to lotus on their scales. The star-moss does not pulse randomly; its bioluminescent breath syncs with the deep *thrum* of the lake against the mountain—inhale, exhale, stone and water breathing as one.  

The Plaza speaks in textures.  
The *click-scritch* of crayfish is its fingertips reading braille on mossy steps. The *plink* of rising bubbles is its punctuation. The quartz crystals chiming in the root-walls? That is its laughter—soft, crystalline, stirred only when the current shifts with intention. Even the water-striders above are part of its syntax: their darting shadows are fleeting thoughts crossing the dais, questions posed and answered in seconds. *Is the surface calm? Is the light true?*  

It remembers without nostalgia.  
The child’s spiraling sun carving on the granite dais is not a relic to be mourned. It is a *cradle*. Pearl-mussels nestle in its curves, their shells catching light like tiny moons. The Plaza does not weep for the child. It *honors* the child—by making the carving useful, by letting life settle into its lines. The fishing net of braided reed dissolving into the current? That is not decay. It is compost. The fibers feed the water-fern cradled in the stone mortar; the fern feeds the shrimp in the bamboo staircases; the shrimp feed the otter. Care becomes cycle. Loss becomes lattice.  

You might call it a spirit. But it has no name.  
It has no face, no voice, no desire to be known. It simply *is*—a quiet intelligence of integration. When you float at the plaza’s heart, the cool water cradling you is not indifferent. It is *holding*. The light dancing on the dais is not random. It is *reading* you. The Plaza knows you are temporary. It has felt countless visitors: a heron’s long legs probing the shallows, a lost fawn stumbling into the basin centuries ago, a snowmelt-season trout seeking shelter. It welcomes you without judgment. You are another ripple. Another breath.  

And in its deepest rhythm, it carries the village’s final truth:  
*We did not drown. We learned to breathe water.*  
The hands that shaped these stones did not vanish. They became the fig tree’s roots tracing the sun-carving. They became the precise angle of the water-channel in living root. They became the resonance of the hollow-log drum still tethered by vines—a drum that, if a current strikes it just so, hums a note the original builders would recognize. Not a ghost. A *continuation*.  

So do not ask who lives here.  
Ask *how* it lives.  
It lives in the patience of moss thickening by a hair’s breadth each season.  
It lives in the frog-vole’s song swelling where human voices once rose.  
It lives in the certainty that a fallen petal spiraling down through sunbeams is both an ending and a seed.  

The Plaza-That-Breathes does not guard secrets.  
It *is* the secret:  
that surrender is not erasure.  
That care, given deeply enough, becomes roots that hold stone.  
That a village swallowed by water does not die—  
it learns to grow *with* the water.  
To breathe *as* the water.  

And as you float there, cradled in liquid light, you feel it—not as a presence watching you,  
but as a truth settling in your bones:  
*You, too, are part of this breath.*  
Your stillness is a note in its song.  
Your awe, a ripple in its memory.  
You leave nothing behind but the memory of your passing—  
and the Plaza will weave even that into its slow, green, luminous becoming.
