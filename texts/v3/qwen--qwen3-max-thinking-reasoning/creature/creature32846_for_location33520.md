They do not have names. Names require echo, and this place swallows sound the moment it is born. They do not have faces. Faces require light, and light here is a geological accident—a fleeting cobalt gasp. What lives here is not a *who*, but a *how*. A verb given chitin and hunger. A symphony of necessity.

First, the foundation: the breath-takers. Not plants. Never plants. These are the microbial weavers—colonies of archaea and bacteria so ancient they remember when the mountain was magma. They form the grey-velvet films clinging to every warm seam, drinking the mountain’s exhalations: hydrogen sulfide, carbon monoxide, argon. They do not grow *on* the stone; they grow *with* it. Their metabolic breath etches microscopic channels into the basalt. Where they feast, the rock softens. Where they die, their calcified husks become seedbeds for hematite crystals. They are the silent alchemists, turning poison into pulse. They have no awareness of time. A century is a single metabolic cycle. They are the pocket’s first language, written in chemistry.

Upon this living parchment move the artisans of erosion: the six-limbed skitterers. To call them insects is a betrayal of scale and purpose. They are geology made animate. Each carapace—ash-grey, segmented like cooled lava flows—is a thermal battery, absorbing radiant heat by the micro-degree, radiating it only when movement demands. Blind? Yes. But their world is vivid: a topography of heat gradients mapped by twin sensory stalks that twitch like compass needles toward warmth. Their six limbs are tools of devotion. Forelimbs rasp stone with mandibles edged in self-sharpening silica, harvesting the microbial films in precise, rhythmic strokes. Middle limbs taste the air for chemical trails—pheromones of kin, the metallic tang of a rival’s blood. Hind limbs anchor against tremors, braced like rivets against the mountain’s slow exhale.

They move in tides, not crowds. A gleaner tide flows along vent-warmed walls, a living brushstroke harvesting film in synchronized scrapes—a sound like sandpaper on bone. A hunter tide moves in fractured silence: three limbs lifted, three planted, reading vibrations through stone. A stumble. A fracture. A shard of obsidian shifts. The hunters freeze. Then—*strike*. Not with malice, but with the inevitability of crystal growth. They consume the fallen. They consume the weak. They consume their own molted shells. Nothing is waste. A discarded carapace becomes a nursery: microbial films bloom in its crevices; hematite crystals sprout from its joints like iron flowers. Death is not an end but a phase change—a return to the loom.

They communicate in the pocket’s true tongue: vibration. A rapid tap-tap-tap against basalt signals a rich film deposit. A low-frequency shudder warns of shifting stone. A frantic skittering cascade means *predator*—though the predator is always another skitterer, another tide. There are no nests. No burrows. Territory is claimed by heat signature and chemical residue. A warm fissure belongs to the gleaner who maintains it. A sulfur-veined ledge belongs to the hunter who patrols it. They do not sleep. They do not rest. Their existence is a continuous act of witness and work. They are the pocket’s nervous system—feeling every thermal shift, every micro-fracture, every breath of the mountain.

And beneath it all, the microbial films persist. Patient. Implacable. They colonize the skitterers’ discarded limbs. They feed on the minerals leached from their excretions. They are the memory of the place—the first life to touch the raw wound of the fissure, the last to remain when the stone finally seals. They do not fear the skitterers. They *use* them. Each scrape of a mandible exposes fresh rock for colonization. Each vibration stirs dormant spores. The skitterers are not masters here. They are gardeners. Unwitting. Essential.

You might mistake their indifference for hostility. It is not. When your cold, alien shape stumbles into their world, they do not flee. They do not attack. A gleaner pauses its scraping. A sensory stalk twitches toward your shivering form—a cold void in the thermal map. It registers the anomaly: *not heat. not vibration of stone. not chemical trail of kin.* Then it turns away. You are a ghost. A flaw in the data. Your breath is a damp intrusion. Your shadow is meaningless. You are less than a falling crystal shard. Less than a sigh of cooling rock. You are irrelevant to the cycle.

This is not survival. Survival implies struggle against an environment. Here, there is no separation. The skitterers *are* the erosion. The microbes *are* the chemistry. The hiss of the vent is their collective breath; the grind of settling stone, their heartbeat. They do not live *in* the pocket. They *are* the pocket breathing itself into being. A single, distributed consciousness of heat and hunger and purpose. A slow, roaring whisper in the stone that says: *I am here. I am becoming. I require nothing but this.*

When the mountain releases you back to the silent cold of the ridge, you carry no souvenir. No carapace fragment. No crystal. But in the hollow of your ear, in the tremor of your own breath, you feel it—the echo of a world that needs no sky, no sun, no witness but itself. And you understand: you were never the observer. You were the interruption. And the pocket has already forgotten you.
