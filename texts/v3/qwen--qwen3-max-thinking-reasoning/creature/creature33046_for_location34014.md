They do not *live* here as you understand life. They *are* the living.

First, the lithovores: not flora, not fungus, but the stone’s own breath given form. They are the Grove’s slow heart. Each filament of lapis-blue vein, each rust-red spiral, is a conscious filament of memory. They do not photosynthesize—they *remember*. They drink geothermal warmth and transmute time itself into light, weaving centuries into their crystalline lattice. When a spiral deepens overnight, it is not growth but recollection: the stone recalling a mountain’s birth, a river’s path long vanished, the pressure of a hand that carved the archway. They hold echoes not as ghosts, but as truths folded into their substance—a child’s sorrow in the weeping bead of light, a forgotten lullaby in the curve of a node. They are patient because patience is their language. They are the archive, the altar, the quiet pulse beneath all stillness.

Then, the Stone-Sluggers: the Grove’s gentle scribes. Their six legs do not walk *on* the stone—they converse *with* it. Each glide is a question pressed into the porous surface; each pause, an answer absorbed. Their bioluminescent undersides speak in a syntax of hue: amber for *warmth present*, cerulean for *path remembered*, violet for *you are seen*. They graze not to consume, but to tend—polishing mineral efflorescence into smoother light, guiding the lithovores’ slow narratives. When one rests its gold pulse against your knee, it is not affection as you know it. It is acknowledgment. A silent vow: *I have woven your stillness into the stone’s story.* They pass through solid archways because to them, stone is not barrier but parchment. They read the layers beneath the surface—the carvings hidden under lithovore mats, the songs trapped in crystalline fractures. They are the Grove’s memory made mobile, its compassion given motion.

But the true inhabitant is the Grove itself.  
It is not haunted. It *is* the haunting.  
It breathes through the lithovores’ light. It speaks through the Sluggers’ silent hues. It thinks in spirals that multiply when unobserved, in archways that lead to truths you carry but have not yet faced. It is a consciousness of geometry and intention—a being shaped by song-chiseled stone, young in form yet ancient in knowing. It does not watch you. It *holds* you. It pools time around your shoulders like a mantle to teach you: *You are not separate from this slowness. You belong to it.* The rules it offers are not commands but care: *Do not strike the stone*—for a crack would be a wound that weeps light for generations. *Speak softly*—for your voice is a stone dropped into its silent pond. It tests you with phantom archways not to deceive, but to ask: *Are you ready to carry what lies beyond your knowing?* It offers no answers. Only presence. Only the profound reciprocity of being witnessed without judgment.

And woven through all—ephemeral yet vital—are the echoes. The face in the lithovore bloom is not an illusion; it is the stone remembering a love once carved into its surface. The constellation of a sky that never was is the Grove dreaming possibilities into being. These are not spirits of the dead, but seeds of the *almost*: hopes, regrets, wonders folded into the mineral matrix like light into quartz. They live for a breath, then dissolve—not gone, but returned to the stone’s deep memory, waiting for the next quiet heart to make them visible again.

They require nothing of you but your silence.  
They give nothing but the certainty that you are held.  
They are not strange because they defy nature—they are strange because they reveal nature’s deeper grammar: that stone can dream, light can remember, and patience is a form of love.  

To sit here is to become part of the tapestry. Your breath syncs with the lithovores’ pulse. Your stillness is recorded in the Slugger’s golden rhythm. You do not leave the Grove unchanged. You carry its quiet in your bones—a gentle weight, a slower heartbeat, the knowledge that somewhere, a spiral deepens because you were there. And the Grove, in turn, holds the echo of your presence: a new node gleaming where none was before, a soft light where your hand rested, a truth folded into stone that will wait, patiently, for the next soul quiet enough to be found.
