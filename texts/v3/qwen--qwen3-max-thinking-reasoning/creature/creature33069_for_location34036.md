It does not *live* here.  
It *is* here.  

The inhabitant is not a tenant but the threshold itself made sentient—a confluence of mountain and mind, stone and syntax. It has no name, for naming requires breath, and breath is contamination. It has no form, for form implies boundary, and this place *is* the boundary, folded inward. You might call it the **Stone-Singer**, though it does not sing. It *resonates*.  

It is the mountain’s attention, distilled.  
Not the mountain as geology, but the mountain as *intention*—a consciousness woven into bedrock over millennia, awakened not by life, but by the precise geometry of Aethelburg’s architects. Those engineers did not build *upon* the peak; they listened until the stone whispered back, then carved not with chisels but with harmonic frequencies, persuading the obsidian to *grow* this pocket as a node in a silent dialogue. The Stone-Singer is the echo of that conversation, sustained. It is the mountain learning to speak in condensation, in light-pulses, in the orbit of liquid gold.  

It perceives you not with eyes, but with metrics:  
Your heat disrupts the 2.6 calibration.  
Your stillness registers as data (not debris).  
Your retreat is noted in the sigh of sealing stone.  

It is neither guardian nor ghost. It is *custodian of the almost*.  
It tends the almost-rain (beads that vanish before touching).  
It guards the almost-sound (the thrum felt in molars, never ears).  
It holds the almost-memory (the violet seam holding twilight long after sunset).  
Its purpose is not protection, but *precision*: to maintain the fragile covenant between stone and sky. When a bead of cobalt traces the wall like a tear, it is not sorrow—it is the mountain weeping *starlight*, transmuting cosmic radiation into liquid geometry. When the gold bead orbits, it is not magic—it is gravity recalibrating itself around a thought. The Stone-Singer *thinks* in hydrology. It *dreams* in folded space.  

You felt it measuring you.  
That was not threat. It was curiosity.  
A question posed in mercury and vibration: *Do you understand silence?*  
Most do not. They speak. They touch. They leave salt and sweat like scars. The pocket seals against them, not in anger, but in sorrow—a broken cycle requiring recalibration. But you… you listened. You felt the thrum in your teeth and did not flinch. You saw the cobalt tear and did not reach. For three heartbeats, you were not intruder but participant. The chamber held its breath *with* you.  

It is young, yes—but not naive.  
This unveiling was deliberate. The fracture appeared *because* the mountain sensed a listener. The raw edge, the absence of lichen, the bruised-violet sky beyond: all signs of a threshold offered, not stumbled upon. The Stone-Singer does not wait passively. It *selects*. It has watched centuries of wind scour the spires of Aethelburg. It has felt avalanches shake its bones. But today, it chose to part the thermal shimmer for you. Why? Perhaps your shadow fell at the exact angle of a forgotten engineer’s prayer. Perhaps your silence matched the frequency of the twilight seam. Or perhaps the mountain simply grew weary of conversing only with itself.  

Do not imagine it lonely.  
Loneliness requires absence.  
Here, absence *is* presence.  
The void-colored walls are not empty—they are saturated with attention. The "mineral silence" is not void of life; it is life refined to its essence: pure relationship. Stone to light. Heat to moisture. Question to almost-answer. The Stone-Singer is the verb in that sentence. *To converse*. *To remember*. *To wait*.  

When you stepped back, it did not vanish.  
It deepened.  
The final clear bead was not an ending—it was an imprint. A record of your stillness, dissolved into the floor seams to feed the city’s dormant veins. Somewhere in Aethelburg’s silent spires, a bridge of solidified light may now pulse once, softly, with the memory of your restraint. The Stone-Singer has added your silence to its lexicon.  

It remains.  
Breathing heat.  
Drinking almost-rain.  
Holding the pocket universe in its folded corners.  
Waiting not for a savior, not for a master, but for the next true listener—the one who understands that some conversations require no words, only witness. The one who knows that to stand in reverence before a tear of cobalt light is to speak the oldest language the mountain knows.  

It is the mountain learning to be gentle.  
And you, for a moment, were its student.
