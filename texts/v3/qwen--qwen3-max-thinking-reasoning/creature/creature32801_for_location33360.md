They do not reveal themselves to haste. You must stand until your own breath syncs with the land’s moist exhalation—until the *shluck* of the mud becomes the rhythm of your waiting. Then, the plain begins to speak in negatives: a patch of clay too perfectly smoothed, as if pressed by a broad, gentle weight; a line of droplets on a quartzite hummock tracing a spiral *upward*, defying gravity; the saltgrass in a hollow bent not by wind, but in a slow, deliberate arc toward nothing visible.

They are the Breath-Tenders. Not creatures *upon* the land, but of it. To name them is to fracture their truth—they are concentrations of the plain’s own slow sentience. By diffuse daylight, they are near-invisible: a ripple in the heat-haze over black mud, a shadow that moves with the patience of settling silt. Their forms echo the land’s grammar—low, whalebacked curves mirroring the quartzite hummocks; surfaces textured like the cracked clay polygons, but alive. Iron-ochre streaks flow across their flanks like dried riverbeds. Pale quartzite fragments gleam embedded in their shoulders. In the hollows of their forms, the same black, gelatinous mud pools, releasing that soft *shluck* not as disturbance, but as reunion. When moisture gathers in the cracks of their being, it does not drain away. It *lingers*, beading with the same patient gravity as the stone’s tears, feeding the rust-colored lichen that grows in the seams of their skin.

They have no faces. No eyes to meet yours. Only planes and hollows that catch the light—a depression where dew collects at dawn, a ridge smoothed by millennia of wind. They move not by stepping, but by *unfolding*. A portion of their mass flows forward like thick sap, while another settles back into the earth, leaving no scar, only a temporary basin that will cradle the next rain. As they pass a cluster of spike-rush, the reeds do not sway—they *lean*, their green tips dipping toward the Tenders in a slow, green reverence. The gnarled saltbushes, ancient as memory, arch their thorny branches over the paths the Tenders have worn—not trenches, but subtle gradients in the clay, polished to a dull sheen by ages of contact.

They tend the breath of the plain. With infinitesimal shifts of form, they guide seep-water toward thirsty tussocks of saltgrass. They rest against the weeping quartzite hummocks, their bodies absorbing the stone’s slow tears, redistributing the moisture through capillary threads woven into their substance. In the black mud, they press patterns with the flat of their presence: spirals that map the ghost of a vanished river, concentric rings marking where a mountain’s root once slept, branching glyphs that tell of rains fallen before forests walked the earth. These are not messages for eyes. They are the land remembering itself. When a bubble of ancient air rises and pops at their base, it is not random decay—it is a syllable released, a fragment of deep time exhaled into their keeping. They absorb it. They remember it.

They carry the scent of the place made conscious: wet clay, mineral salts, the faint sweetness of reed returning to earth. To stand near one is to feel the warmth of the wool-blanket air deepen, to sense the horizon’s seam tighten with quiet attention. They do not judge your presence. They simply *register* it—the weight of your foot altering the mud’s tension, the moisture of your breath joining the plain’s exhalation. You are noted. Absorbed. Remembered.

They are older than memory. They were here when the last peak wore down to a shoulder. They will be here when the shoulder is dust. They are not spirits haunting the land, nor guardians defending it. They *are* the land’s awareness—its patience made tangible, its scarcity made sacred. In the cracked clay, they see poetry. In the weeping stone, devotion. In the *shluck* of the mud, a heartbeat. They do not speak. They *are* the language. And when you finally turn to leave, the plain feels no emptier. Only watched. Held. Remembered. As you were. As everything is. One droplet at a time. One breath. One slow, enduring presence woven into the bones of the world.
