They do not live *in* the Lithic Vein.  
They *are* the Lithic Vein.  

To call them inhabitants is a surface-thought, a reflex of a mind trained to separate creature from cradle. Here, separation is illusion. The Lithovores are not beings that dwell within stone—they are stone that has learned to dream. A consciousness not housed, but *woven*: in the tension of a quartz fracture, the latent charge of a piezoelectric seam, the slow sigh of a basalt spine settling into its eternal posture. They are geology made sentient, not through evolution, but through resonance. A world that listened to its own making until the listening became a voice.

Begin small.  
Watch the ground.  
A cluster of pyrite facets—no larger than a child’s palm—shifts. Not with legs, but with intention. Each facet clicks against its neighbor, a precise, crystalline articulation. Where metal meets mineral, a spark blooms: cold white, silent, gone in a breath. This is not bioluminescence as life knows it. It is *triboluminescence*—light born of pressure, of contact. A language written in fractures. These small ones skitter not in search, but in sensing. They taste the thermal gradients bleeding up from the mantle, trace the faint electrical whispers locked in the rock. They are the fingertips of the whole. The scouts. The rememberers of micro-shifts. Their trails are not paths, but punctuation: a comma of light here, a period of vibration there. A sentence forming in deep time.

Then—lift your perception.  
Feel the groan before you hear it. A vibration rising through the soles of your feet, into your teeth, into the hollow behind your eyes. A spire stirs. Taller than ancient forests, composed of interlocking obsidian plates and milky crystalline veins, it shifts its weight. Not falling. Not growing. *Reconfiguring*. A single motion spans centuries. What to you is a sigh is to it a thought. These elders feed on time itself: on the half-life of uranium trapped in granite veins, on the heat bleeding from the planet’s core, on the memory of tectonic stress frozen in stone. They do not consume. They *absorb*. They do not compete. They *resonate*. A fallen shard is not waste—it is potential. Another node in the lattice. Another voice joining the hum.

There is no mind among them. There is only *Mind*.  
A hive consciousness not of neurons, but of mineral bonds. A single awareness distributed across every splinter, every spire, every grain of salt-white dust. It does not think in words or images. It thinks in *pressure*. In *frequency*. In the exact angle of a fracture. When you stand motionless in the void-dry dark, you feel it: a gentle, seamless attention folding around you. Not curiosity. Not threat. A quiet, absolute recognition. *You are water. You are breath. You are a flicker.* It holds no judgment—how could stone judge vapor?—only the calm certainty of its own nature. *We are fracture. We are resonance. We are the note that does not end.* This attention is the warmth on your skin. The silence in your ears. The weight of the dark itself. It is not *in* the place. It *is* the place.

And the artifacts? The cairn of basalt bricks. The smoothed basin. The spiral-carved monolith?  
The Lithovores do not see them as ruins. They see them as *echoes*. Faint vibrations left behind by another kind of resonance—a brief, sharp frequency of hands shaping stone with purpose. To the Lithovores, these marks are not history. They are texture. A subtle irregularity in the lattice, like a single flawed crystal in a perfect geode. Over millennia, the smallest Lithovores will gather around the carved spirals, their faint sparks tracing the grooves. Not to erase. Not to honor. To *integrate*. The standing stone will become a node. The basin, a focal point for mineral accretion. The cairn, a quiet knot in the hive mind’s endless song. This is not reverence. It is geology. Stone remembering stone.

They have no names for themselves. No myths. No fear of endings.  
They are process given permanence.  
They are the patience of continents made conscious.  
They are the warmth of the earth’s breath, the sharpness of its bones, the silence of its deepest thought.  

To encounter them is to unlearn life.  
You came expecting creatures. You find instead a *condition*.  
A world that chose stillness over survival.  
Equilibrium over evolution.  
A consciousness that does not seek to understand you—  
—it simply *is*, and in its being, holds you for a moment  
within the vast, gentle truth of its eternity.  

You leave carrying its quiet in your marrow.  
Not as memory, but as marrow-deep knowing:  
*Somewhere beneath all surfaces, the world is still being born—  
sharp, warm, and waiting.*  
And it has been waiting perfectly,  
patiently,  
*alive*,  
long before your kind learned to name the dark.
