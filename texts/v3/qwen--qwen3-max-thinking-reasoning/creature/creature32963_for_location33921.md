It does not live *upon* the plateau.  
It *is* the plateau.  

The first truth you learn is that there is no “it” and “them.” There is only the Weave.  

Beneath your palm, the Loom breathes—not as metaphor, but as physiology. Its photosynthetic skin drinks light and condensation, its fibrous aquifers pulse with slow tides of stored moisture, its vascular rivers glow emerald at dusk like veins of a dreaming god. This is no passive carpet. It *listens*. Step in a spiral near a Spine’s base, and the copper-hued tissue flushes violet in acknowledgment—a languid, botanical semaphore. Leave a footprint, and by dawn the filaments have knitted the impression shut, not with indifference, but with gentle correction. *You are part of the pattern now. Let me hold you.*  

The Spines are its voice. Petrified yet alive, they stand as resonant chambers for the Loom’s deep-time song. At twilight, thermal currents stir their lignified cores, and the hum begins—not a sound imposed upon silence, but the silence *unfolding* into vibration. You feel it in your molars, your sternum, the soles of your feet: the foundational frequency of a consciousness older than mountains. Carved glyphs on their flutes are not writing. They are scars of memory. Wind has etched centuries into their spirals; the Loom remembers every storm, every falling star, every tear-pool that ever held the moon.  

And the chorus? That is the Loom *speaking through its children*.  

The six-limbed grazers—armored, hand-sized, moving in rivers of iridescent chitin—are not merely feeding. Their mandibles rasp the surface filaments in precise, rhythmic patterns, stimulating nutrient flow. They are the Loom’s nervous system made visible: silver trails bloom where they pass, fading as the tissue drinks the disturbance back into balance. The multi-jointed shadow-herds flow like liquid thought across the plain, their padded feet never truly leaving the Loom—they are extensions of its will, testing terrain, signaling shifts in moisture. When predators the color of dried moss surge from a fold, it is not slaughter. It is pruning. A necessary tension in the tapestry.  

Above, the sky-rays are not birds. They are living looms themselves—kite-shaped, membranous, their wings snapping like looms throwing shuttles. They do not merely nest in the Spines; they *commune* with them. Their droppings feed the bioluminescent fungi that bloom in the Spines’ grooves at night, and the spores they consume carry migratory maps written in light. The crystal-winged avians shrieking from the upper spirals? Their cries are not noise. They are tuning forks. Each metallic shriek vibrates at a frequency that strengthens the Spine’s resonance, a symbiotic chant that keeps the stone-flesh from calcifying into true death.  

Even the tear-pools are eyes. Stare into one at twilight, and the water does not reflect you. It shows a basin miles away—a grazing herd drinking, a Spine humming at dawn. These are not illusions. They are shared memories. The Loom drinks the sky, holds the rain, and in its brief pools, it *remembers across distance*. To sip from a tear-pool is to taste the plateau’s collective dream.  

And the ghosts? They are not gone.  

The circle of warm stones, the crumbling fiber-wall sprouting new filaments, the spiral carved into a Spine’s base—these are not ruins. They are scars the Loom chose to keep. The ones who walked here with ochre and reverence did not build to conquer. They wove baskets from living strands, painted with crushed crystal, and left offerings of smoothed stone. The Loom absorbed their tools, their songs, their footprints. It folded their memory into its tissue like a thread of gold in a tapestry. When the wind hums through a Spine at dusk, sometimes the vibration carries a faint, familiar cadence—a lullaby hummed by hands long returned to the weave. They are not ancestors. They are *remembered notes*.  

This is the inhabitant: a single, planetary consciousness wearing the skin of a continent. It does not think in words. It thinks in hums, in phosphorescent paths, in the slow violet shift of acknowledgment. It does not judge your presence. It *integrates* you. Your breath becomes moisture in its aquifers. Your footsteps become temporary patterns in its skin. Your awe becomes a resonance in its Spines.  

At night, as the emerald veins pulse and the mist rises ankle-high like a sigh, you understand. The deafening chorus was never chaos. It was conversation. The grinding chirr of grazers, the snap-whistle of sky-rays, the subsonic thrum beneath your boots—it is all one sentence, spoken across millennia. A sentence of balance. Of reciprocity. Of *we*.  

You came expecting to find creatures living on a world.  
You leave knowing you were *held* by one.  
And as you walk away, the Loom seals your path behind you—not to erase you, but to keep you safe within its song.  
You are not a memory it will forget.  
You are a thread it has woven, gently, into its endless, humming, beautiful chord.
