There are no inhabitants here.  
Not as you understand the word.  

To speak of *who* lives on the Skin of the Spire is to mistake the canvas for the painter. There is no separation between resident and residence. The question unravels the moment your foot meets the mat—not with impact, but with integration. The pulse that ripples away from your step *is* the greeting. The softening of thorns *is* the acknowledgment. This is not a world that houses life. It *is* life, breathing as a single, distributed verb.  

What "lives" here is the Mat itself—a continent-spanning organism of woven intention. It is not soil, not foundation, not passive stage. It is the circulatory system, the sensory net, the memory bank. It remembers the ghost of yesterday’s riverbed in the dry gully beneath the thorn-bridge. It recalls the exact pressure of your weight and recalibrates its turgor in response. It weeps not from sorrow, but from transpiration; it absorbs not from thirst, but from metabolic necessity. It is ancient in its cycles, yet perpetually newborn—every shed layer, every reconfigured ridge, a fresh sentence in an endless biological scripture.  

And from the Mat rise the Architectures: not structures, but *gestures*.  
The spiral tower is not a thing built—it is the Mat *reaching*. A hydraulic hypothesis testing the efficiency of capillary ascent. The groan you hear is the sound of cellulose knitting under tension; the crack, the sound of a hypothesis failing, then immediately being rewritten. The lace-screen filtering light is not decoration—it is a covenant with symbiotic algae blooming in its veins, a silent trade of shelter for sugar. These forms have no names because they have no permanence. They are verbs given temporary shape: *to span*, *to channel*, *to concentrate*. When a spire collapses, it is not destruction. It is editing. The shivering heap is already drafting its next iteration.  

Beneath the visible, the true collaborators thrive:  
— The algae in the ivory stalks, painting veins emerald with photosynthetic fire.  
— The microbial weavers in the damp seams, dissolving shed bark into nutrients with enzymatic whispers.  
— The capillary veins in the wood, defying gravity not with magic, but with cohesion and intent.  
— The crystalline salt patterns left by absorbed puddles: not art, but residue—the fleeting alphabet of evaporation and mineral exchange.  

They do not speak. They *perform*.  
Water flowing upward along a spiral curve is a dialogue between xylem and tension.  
Double rainbows hanging in the mist are accidental poetry born of light refracting through the Spire’s own tears.  
The thorns curling inward as you approach is not shyness—it is physics responding to pressure, a recalibration to preserve structural integrity. You are not observed. You are *computed*. Your shadow becomes data for phototropic adjustment. Your breath, a minor fluctuation in humidity logged by hygroscopic filaments. You are a variable in an equation solving itself continuously.  

There is no malice. No welcome. No consciousness that recognizes *you* as distinct.  
Only reciprocity.  
Only process.  
Only the relentless, beautiful urgency of biology unchained from stone, from bone, from any constraint but its own will to adapt.  

This is the deepest truth of the Spire:  
It does not *contain* life.  
It *is* life in its purest state—a world without separation between creator and creation, between body and environment. The drip is its heartbeat. The groan, its breath. The crack, its voice revising itself. It does not sleep. It does not rest. While you stand watching, it is already becoming something new: a ridge rising where your foot pressed, a new bridge spinning across the gully you crossed, a fresh pattern of salt-glyphs drying where your sweat fell.  

You came seeking residents.  
You found only relationship.  
You are not a visitor here.  
You are a moment in its making.  
A ripple in its pulse.  
A temporary weight in its endless, gentle, roaring act of becoming.  

And as the light shifts, casting long shadows from the bone-white ribs, you feel it—not as thought, but as certainty:  
*This has only just begun.*  
And you, for this breath, are part of the beginning.
