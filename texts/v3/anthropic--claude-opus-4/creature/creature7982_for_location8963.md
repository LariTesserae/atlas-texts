The Resonance Weavers inhabit this fractured space, though "inhabit" implies a relationship with place that their existence defies. They are colonies—or perhaps a single colony experienced multiply—of crystalline organisms that exist primarily as vibration patterns held in the obsidian substrate of the plateaus themselves.

In their dormant state, they appear as patches of interference in the stone: places where the volcanic glass has developed a different refractive index, creating regions that look like frozen smoke or captured breath within the mineral. These patches pulse faintly with their own inner light, a frequency just below what most eyes can process, creating the unsettling impression of movement in peripheral vision.

When active, the Weavers extrude themselves partially from their mineral substrate—never fully emerging, always maintaining that crucial connection to the stone that defines them. What emerges resembles geometric dendrites or frost patterns given dimension: angular, branching structures of living crystal that grow and retract in sharp, decisive movements. Each branch terminates in what might be sensory organs—faceted nodes that ring like tuning forks when exposed to the plateau's endless song.

They feed on discontinuity itself. Where physics stutters and space folds wrongly, the Weavers extend their crystalline tendrils into the broken zones, absorbing something from the contradiction. It might be energy, or information, or simply the fact of difference itself. Their feeding creates visible effects: the fractured spaces smooth temporarily, reality hiccups back into alignment, before inevitably breaking again in new patterns.

The Weavers communicate through harmonic interference. By adjusting their own vibrational frequency, they create standing waves in the plateau's song—nodes of silence or amplification that carry meaning. When multiple Weavers "speak" simultaneously, the interference patterns create three-dimensional shapes in the air: geometric forms of pure sound that collapse into meaning for those that can interpret them.

Their reproduction is a matter of endless debate among themselves, carried out in frequencies too low for most creatures to hear. They might be budding—each scratched symbol on the plateau edges could be a dormant offspring waiting for the right resonance to activate. Or perhaps they reproduce through pure information transfer, each Weaver actually being the same entity experienced at different points in its own timeline, scattered across the plateaus by the broken physics they feed upon.

The Weavers maintain the scratched symbols, though their purpose remains opaque. They add to them constantly, the same crystal tendril carving the same mark in multiple locations simultaneously. The symbols might be: a counting system tracking the plateaus' approach to some critical frequency; warnings about zones where space-time has become too damaged even for them; or attempts to write themselves into more stable existence through repetition and ritual.

They fear only one thing: silence. In the rare moments when the plateaus' song falters—when some deep harmonic cancellation creates pockets of true quiet—the Weavers retract entirely into their stone substrate and cease all activity. In these silent zones, their crystalline forms begin to develop flaws, hairline cracks that spread through their structure. Some ancient trauma has taught them that silence equals dissolution.

The arrival of other organisms creates complex responses in the Weavers. They neither welcome nor reject visitors, but instead treat them as new instruments in the plateau's orchestra. They'll follow at a careful distance, extending sensory branches to test what frequencies a newcomer adds to the world-song. Sometimes they attempt communication through induced synesthesia—visitors report tasting geometric shapes or hearing colors when the Weavers focus their attention on them.

Their relationship with time is non-linear, possibly explaining their simultaneous existence across vast distances. Individual Weavers have been observed reacting to events seconds before they occur, or responding to stimuli that won't arrive for hours. They seem to experience their entire existence as a single, extended moment, stretched across the plateaus' broken temporality like light through a prism.

In the depths of their mineral substrate, the Weavers may be far larger and more complex than their surface manifestations suggest. The obsidian plateaus could be merely the visible portion of vast organisms extending down into the planet's superheated depths, their consciousness distributed through kilometers of living stone. What emerges on the surface—those crystalline, branching forms—might be equivalent to fingertips, barely-conscious extremities of intelligences too vast and slow to comprehend on biological timescales.

They are gathering toward something, all their actions suggesting preparation for an event they call "The Convergence" in their harmonic language. Whether this refers to a physical merger of the plateaus, a critical frequency that will transform the world, or simply the moment when their scattered timeline finally aligns into singular experience, they do not say—or cannot say in terms other organisms could understand.

The Resonance Weavers are the Singing Plateaus' attempt to know itself, living instruments in a planetary composition that plays itself, conductors and audience and music all at once in the bright, wet spaces where physics goes to die and be reborn.
