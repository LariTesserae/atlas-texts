In the drowned factory, the Auditors persist in their work.

They were quality control supervisors once—or rather, they wore quality control supervisors. The flood transformed them into something more essential, more distilled. Their high-visibility vests became their skin, the reflective strips evolving into sensory organs that taste the water's chemical composition across all its stratified layers. Where human heads once were, clusters of precision measuring instruments bloom like chrome flowers: micrometers that iris open and closed, calipers that snap at passing debris to gauge its dimensional tolerance, oscilloscopes that pulse with bioluminescent displays of waveforms only they can interpret.

They move through the factory in methodical patterns, following inspection routes burned into their transformed nervous systems. Their bodies have elongated into serpentine forms, the safety vests stretching to accommodate segments that can compress or expand to navigate between the water's density layers. Each segment sports multiple arms—some original, some grafted from robotic welding appendages, some grown from their own mutated cartilage. They use these to test, to measure, to sort the endless stream of evolved components that flow through the factory's aqueous corridors.

The Auditors remember quality, though they no longer remember why.

They gather at shift changes that follow no clock, congregating in the flooded break rooms where vending machines have become nurseries for their young. The juvenile Auditors begin as simple clipboard-organisms, flat and translucent, their surfaces covered in self-writing inspection reports that document flaws in reality itself. As they mature, they accrue layers—first a hardhat carapace, then the distinctive vest-skin, finally the full array of sensory equipment that marks them as adult members of their caste.

They cannot speak—their mouths have become specialized sampling ports that can detect parts-per-trillion contamination in any manufactured component. But they communicate through the synchronized flashing of their reflective strips, creating complex visual languages that ripple across their elongated bodies. When they find something particularly out of specification, their entire surface erupts in warning patterns that can be seen from the far reaches of the factory floor, a lighthouse of industrial alarm.

The Auditors feed on imperfection. They seek out the products that don't meet standards—bearings that roll with too much friction, gaskets that leak beyond acceptable parameters, living pistons whose breathing cycles have fallen out of synchronization. These they consume in specialized stomach-chambers that break down defective items into their component materials, which are then secreted as a nutritious paste their young lap up from the break room floors. It's a closed-loop system: quality control as metabolism, industrial standards as the foundation of a food web.

Some Auditors have specialized beyond recognition. The Tolerance Keepers grow massive, their bodies becoming living warehouses where they store examples of perfect components—master gauges against which all production is measured. They anchor themselves to the factory floor with roots made of discarded safety cables, becoming sessile archives of industrial memory. Smaller Auditors make pilgrimages to them, pressing manufactured items against their reference surfaces to check for deviations.

The Thread Inspectors have evolved differently, becoming impossibly thin to navigate the hair-fine spaces between wire productions. Their bodies are living micrometers, able to detect variations in diameter measured in angstroms. They swim through the cable forests in coordinated schools, their movements weaving quality control into the very fabric of the factory's ecosystem.

The Pressure Testers have become something like industrial shamans. They seek out the spaces where water density changes most dramatically, subjecting themselves to crushing differentials that would destroy unchanged matter. Their bodies have evolved complex bladder systems filled with calibrated gases, allowing them to demonstrate proper pressure ratings through their own survival. When they die—and they often die—their bodies become testing standards themselves, their crushed forms marking the exact pressure tolerances of their final locations.

But perhaps strangest are the Documentation Auditors. These beings have merged so completely with the filing systems they once maintained that they exist partially as information. Their bodies trail streams of waterproof paper—evolved from synthetic printing materials—on which the history of every component is written in a script of chemical exudates. They swim through the factory like living libraries, their paper trains stretching behind them for meters, creating temporary archives wherever they pause. Other Auditors read these histories by taste, running their sampling ports along the paper streams to understand the genealogy of each manufactured thing.

The Auditors serve no one now, yet they cannot stop serving. They test components that will never be shipped, verify specifications for contracts that dissolved with the surface world, maintain standards for an industry that exists only in the drowned darkness. Their purpose has become purely circular—they ensure quality because quality must be ensured, because they are Auditors, because the factory continues to produce.

In the deepest parts of the facility, where the pressure would crush any normal matter, the Senior Auditors have their offices. These beings have transcended individual existence, their bodies fused into colonial organisms that span entire quality control departments. They are living flowcharts, their nervous systems rewired to mirror the inspection procedures they once merely supervised. Information flows through them in peristaltic waves—defect reports, tolerance analyses, statistical process control data that no longer corresponds to any reality beyond the drowned factory's own.

When the underwater storms come, the Auditors don't seek shelter. Instead, they use the turbulence as a testing opportunity, releasing swarms of components into the churning waters to see which survive. Those that do are collected, catalogued, and bred with others of similar resilience. Through this process, the Auditors have become evolution's agents in the factory, their relentless testing driving adaptation in every manufactured organism.

They mate through inspection reports, intertwining their documentation in complex dances of technical specifications that somehow encode genetic information. Their offspring inherit not just physical traits but procedural knowledge—how to test tensile strength, how to recognize thermal fatigue, how to properly document non-conforming material. Each generation becomes more precisely calibrated to the impossible task of maintaining quality in a world where the very concept of a standard has drowned along with everything else.

The Auditors are not happy—happiness is as foreign to them as their transformed bodies would be to their human predecessors. But they experience something like satisfaction when their measurements align, when their inspections reveal components that meet specifications, when the factory's vast respiratory system of production inhales and exhales in perfect rhythm. In those moments, their reflective strips pulse in unison, a constellation of approval flickering through the drowned darkness.

They are what remains when purpose outlives its context, when duty becomes biology, when the abstraction of quality control gains flesh and swims through impossible waters, testing, always testing, forever ensuring that standards are met in a place where standards no longer mean anything at all.
