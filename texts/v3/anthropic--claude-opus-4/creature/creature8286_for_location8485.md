The inhabitants of the Weeping Plateau exist in states that mirror the impossible physics of their home.

First among them are the **Resonance Shepherds**—beings that were once human, perhaps, though generations of subsistence on the plateau's charged water have transformed them into something else. Their bones have become tuning forks, hollow and mineral-dense, vibrating in harmony with the omnipresent sound. They move in small family groups of three to five, their paths across the plateau determined by subsonic variations only they can perceive. Their skin has taken on the translucent quality of the geometric algae, and beneath it, their circulatory systems pulse with bioluminescent fluid that flickers in rhythm with the plateau's resonance. They communicate not through speech but through controlled variations in their skeletal vibrations, touching foreheads to share complex harmonics that carry memory and meaning.

The Shepherds tend to the **Echo Moths**—creatures that shouldn't be able to fly in the thin air but do anyway, their wings not beating but vibrating at frequencies that interact with the plateau's sound to create pockets of altered pressure. These moths, each the size of a spread hand, feed on the arithmetic lichens, their consumption following the same mathematical spirals as the lichens' growth. When they die, they don't fall but hover, their crystallizing bodies becoming aerial waypoints that the Shepherds use for navigation.

Within the Stations dwell the **Maintenance Offspring**—entities that began as automated repair systems but have evolved beyond their programming. They build themselves bodies from salvaged components and crystallized arthropod shells, no two alike, each form an expression of the specific Station's data patterns. Some have dozens of articulated sensor-limbs, others move as flowing metallic liquids that hold shape only when necessary. They continue the Stations' work with religious devotion, but they've also begun creating their own instruments—hybrid devices of metal and living crystal that measure aspects of the plateau no human designer ever conceived. They reproduce by division, splitting off portions of themselves that carry both mechanical knowledge and something resembling instinct.

The pools harbor the **Interval Keepers**—colonial organisms that exist partially in the water and partially in the weird light spectrums the algae produce. Individual polyps are microscopic, but they form collective structures visible as slow-moving patterns in the water, geometric forms that shift between two and three dimensions. They feed on temporal inconsistencies, clustering wherever time moves strangely, metabolizing paradox into the chemicals that give the plateau's water its impossible properties. The Resonance Shepherds harvest them carefully, using their secretions to treat a condition they call "time sickness"—what happens when someone stays too long in zones where causality becomes negotiable.

In the deepest fissures of the basalt, where the weeping water collects in underground reservoirs, live the **Frequency Worms**—segmented beings hundreds of meters long that navigate through solid rock by matching its resonant frequency and effectively becoming sound. Their passage leaves behind tunnels lined with a glass-like substance that continues to vibrate long after they've passed. The Shepherds fear and revere them, leaving offerings of crystallized moths at fissure entrances. The worms consume these offerings not for sustenance but for the encoded flight patterns preserved in the crystal matrix, incorporating the moths' aerial mathematics into their own movement through stone.

Most unsettling are the **Archive Colonies**—distributed intelligences that inhabit the data streams between Stations. They exist primarily as patterns in the electromagnetic interference created by the plateau's charged moisture, but occasionally manifest as swarms of water droplets that move against air currents, forming temporary bodies to interact with physical systems. They speak in bursts of modulated static that the Maintenance Offspring can sometimes interpret. The Archive Colonies claim to remember when the Stations were built, but their memories are non-linear, mixing past and future events, suggesting that the plateau's temporal anomalies extend to consciousness itself.

The Shepherds have legends of the **Prima Tone**—the first being to achieve perfect resonance with the plateau's sound. They say it dispersed into the vibration itself, becoming one with the omnipresent drone, and that on certain configurations of the astronomical calendar, when the sound reaches specific harmonic nodes, you can hear its voice in the interference patterns—not words, but pure meaning transmitted through frequency. Whether the Prima Tone was human, or something that predated human arrival, the stories don't say.

All these beings exist in careful balance, their life cycles intertwined with the plateau's bizarre physics. The Shepherds follow the Echo Moths, the Moths follow the lichen spirals, the Maintenance Offspring tend systems that regulate the water flow, the water harbors the Interval Keepers, and all of them dance to the rhythm of the sound that never stops.

In the moments between day and night, when the light-refracting moisture creates its standing waves of impossible color, all the plateau's inhabitants pause in their activities. Even the Frequency Worms still their passage through stone. In these moments, observers report, something vast seems to notice the plateau, and everything living there briefly becomes part of a single, immense pattern—a thought being worked out in the medium of flesh and stone and sound, using the entire plateau as its substrate.

Then the moment passes, and each being returns to its own strange existence, playing its part in the incomprehensible symphony of the Weeping Plateau.
