The Drifters of the Sunken Plateau have evolved into something between human and sea creature, though they began as neither. Their bodies stretch long and narrow, with elongated torsos that undulate gently even when at rest. Their skin has taken on a pearlescent quality, semi-translucent in the eternal twilight, revealing the slow pulse of deep purple blood through vessels that branch like coral. This skin constantly secretes a thin film of oil that allows them to slip through the thick air with minimal resistance when they choose to swim rather than walk.

Their limbs tell the story of their adaptation. Arms have lengthened considerably, with a second elbow-like joint that allows for the peculiar pushing-pulling motion needed to navigate the dense atmosphere. Their hands are their most alien feature—fingers that have stretched to twice human length, connected by translucent webbing that can spread wide to catch the air-water or fold tight for delicate work. The fingertips have hardened into chitinous points, perfect for harvesting the bell-flowers or inscribing the soft cloudstone.

But it is their feet that reveal their true nature. What were once toes have fused into three broad pads, each covered in thousands of tiny cilia that grip the perpetually slick stone causeways. These pads can also flatten completely, spreading wide like the feet of a basilisk lizard, allowing them to actually stand on the surface tension of the thicker air layers when they need to traverse the spaces between buildings.

Their faces retain an almost human arrangement of features, but transformed. Eyes have grown enormous, with secondary transparent eyelids that constantly flicker to clear away the condensation. The irises have developed a spiral pattern, allowing them to focus at varying distances through the murk. Their pupils dilate and contract not just to light, but to sound, helping them navigate by the acoustic signatures of different districts.

Where once they had external ears, small tympanic patches now lie flush against their skulls, covered by a thin membrane that vibrates with the endless tolling. Inside their elongated skulls, their auditory processing has become impossibly complex—they can isolate individual bell tones from the drone, reading the sound like a language. They communicate through a combination of ultra-low frequency hums that travel far through the dense medium and rapid clicking sounds produced by a modified larynx.

Their nostrils have migrated higher on their faces and can seal completely. They breathe through a combination of lung-like organs and modified gill slits along their necks that extract oxygen from both the air-water and the occasional true water that condenses in their dwelling spaces. Their metabolism has slowed dramatically—a single breath can sustain them for nearly an hour of normal activity.

The Drifters organize themselves into acoustic clans, each maintaining a specific section of the bell-forests to ensure their district's tonal signature remains constant. Children are born already adapted to the pressure, their first cries harmonizing with the background drone. They spend their early years in the middle air layers, gradually descending to the heavier causeways as their bodies strengthen.

Their clothing consists of layered membranes harvested from the air-plants, sewn with such skill that the seams are perfectly watertight yet flexible. These garments are often cultivated rather than simply made—certain varieties of membrane will continue growing if properly tended, self-repairing small tears and gradually adapting to their wearer's body. The elders wear robes that have been growing for centuries, their surfaces colonized by beneficial algae that help filter toxins from the air-water.

They have domesticated some of the smaller ribbon-creatures, training them to carry messages through the murk or to herd the aerial plankton into collection areas. These creatures wrap around their keepers' arms when not working, their bodies pulsing with bioluminescent signals that only the Drifters can interpret.

Their tools have evolved from simple necessity into complex art. Sonic shapers—curved pieces of resonant cloudstone—can temporarily redirect the flow of sound, creating quiet bubbles for delicate work or amplifying specific frequencies for long-distance communication. They've learned to grow certain varieties of air-plant into living architecture, guiding their growth with patient decades of work to form new chambers and corridors within their vertical city.

The eldest among them show even more extreme adaptations. Some have grown a third arm from their extended torsos, always smaller than the primary pair but incredibly dexterous. Others have developed patches of photosynthetic skin, supplementing their diet with energy drawn directly from the dim sunlight. A few ancient individuals have become so attuned to the acoustic environment that they no longer need to move at all—they rest in special chambers where the sound currents bring them everything they need, their bodies having evolved beyond the need for physical travel.

In the deepest parts of the city, where the pressure is greatest and the sound most intense, live the Resonants—Drifters who have undergone a final transformation. Their bodies have hollowed out, their bones becoming chambers that amplify and modify sound. They've become living instruments, their every movement contributing to the great drone. They're revered as living ancestors, their voices carrying the history of the city in frequencies only other Drifters can properly hear.

These are not merely adapted humans but something new—beings so perfectly fitted to their impossible environment that they could exist nowhere else. They are the sound and the silence, the swimmers in the air, the children of the bells, as inseparable from their drowned mountain home as the eternal tolling that defines it.
