They move through the terraces like mercury through a maze—the Tenders, though that name is only approximate, translated poorly from the subsonic frequencies they use to communicate with the moss. You notice them first as distortions in the hexagonal patterns, places where the geometry bends slightly, as if seen through water.

They are long and low, their bodies perhaps four meters from blunt head to tapering tail, but rarely more than half a meter high. Their skin mirrors the valley's duality—organic tissue that has learned to conduct electricity, smooth as polished stone but alive with subcutaneous light that follows their neural pathways in branching patterns. When they move, it's with a fluid undulation that barely disturbs the moss beneath them, their twelve pairs of legs (or are they roots? the distinction blurs) finding purchase in the spaces between hexagons with practiced precision.

Their heads are shovel-broad and eyeless—vision would be redundant when you can feel every electrical pulse in the valley through specialized organs that crown their skulls like crystalline antlers. These structures, delicate as spun glass but harder than the basalt beneath, constantly adjust their angle and aperture, reading the bioelectric field like a language. Through them, the Tenders perceive the valley as a vast schematic, every moss pad a node, every connecting cable a sentence in an ongoing conversation about growth and decay, surplus and deficit.

They work in crews of three to five, moving systematically through their assigned sectors. One extends a hollow tongue—more properly a specialized feeding tube—to pierce dead moss pads and extract the concentrated metals left behind. Another secretes careful drops of catalyst from glands along its belly, encouraging new growth in patterns that optimize the power grid's efficiency. A third monitors the underground batteries through direct neural connection, its antlers glowing brighter as it communes with the stored energy, occasionally adjusting flow rates with delicate touches of its foremost appendages.

The Tenders' own biology is a marriage of the same principles that govern the valley. They photosynthesize through their conductive skin, but supplement this with electrical feeding—pressing their bodies against the moss during peak aurora activity to drink deeply of the current. Their bones are hollow, filled with a metallic marrow that serves as both battery and nervous system. When they die—which happens rarely, perhaps once a century—their bodies don't decay but crystallize, becoming new nodes in the valley's ever-expanding circuit.

They build nothing in the conventional sense, but they shape everything. Over generations, they've guided the moss to form specific patterns—mandalas of efficiency that channel power toward the storage facilities, dead zones that protect crucial infrastructure from overgrowth, nursery spirals where they cultivate new strains of moss with enhanced properties. Their knowledge is genetic, encoded in the same bioelectric patterns they spend their lives maintaining.

Communication between Tenders is a symphony of electrical pulses, conducted through the moss itself. Touch your hand to the ground during one of their conversations and you might feel it—a tingling that climbs your arm, patterns that almost resolve into meaning before sliding away. They sing to each other in frequencies that make the moss glow brighter, coordinate their movements through the living grid with a precision that seems telepathic but is simply another form of technology.

The young ones—you can identify them by their smaller antlers and the way their skin still shows distinct scales rather than the smooth conductivity of adults—learn by following, absorbing the work through observation and the constant electrical communion with their elders. They practice on the fungi in the deeper shadows, learning to read and adjust the simpler patterns before graduating to the moss fields proper.

When the Tenders rest, which they do in rotating shifts, they cluster in the warmest spots where moss grows thickest, their bodies overlapping in careful arrangements that maximize electrical exchange between individuals. In these moments, they share more than energy—memories pass between them in bright pulses, the history of the valley preserved not in writing but in living current, each Tender a library and battery both.

They know about the structures, old and new, that punctuate their domain. To them, these are simply different kinds of nodes—some dead, some active, all eventually food for the moss. They maintain the functional ones with the same dispassionate care they show the rest of the grid, replacing corroded cables, cleaning connection points, ensuring the harvested power flows where it needs to go. Whether anyone receives it at the other end is irrelevant; the work continues because the pattern demands it.

Occasionally, a Tender will grow too large for the delicate work, its body approaching six meters, its antlers branching into configurations too complex for the simple maintenance tasks. These elders migrate to the valley's depths, where they interface directly with the main battery chambers, becoming living regulators for the entire system. Down there in the humming dark, they hardly move at all, just pulse with the rhythm of the grid, their consciousness expanded to encompass the entire valley's electrical web.

This is their paradise—a world where the boundary between organism and circuit has dissolved, where their purpose and their pleasure are identical, where they swim through fields of living light following patterns older than the young stone that contains them. They are gardeners of electricity, shepherds of the moss, priests of a faith that worships efficiency and finds the sacred in the perfect hexagon, the uninterrupted circuit, the battery that never empties because it never stops filling.

The Tenders are the valley thinking about itself, maintaining itself, improving itself through millions of small adjustments, each creature a neuron in a vast green brain that dreams only of producing more power, more perfectly, forever.
