The Fabricators arrive in shifts that follow no clock, their entry marked by the singing of the access spirals—those DNA-helix drill paths that cork-screw down from the machine-mountain's inhabited levels. They emerge from the spiral mouths already changed, their forms halfway between their starting shapes and whatever the Hive Lung needs them to become.

Some were human, once. You can tell by the way they still favor bilateral symmetry, though their limbs now telescope on ball-bearing joints and their fingers have multiplied into fractal manipulator arrays. Their skulls have opened like flowers, the brain matter interfacing directly with crystalline computation matrices that crown their heads in glittering tiaras. They move with mechanical precision but organic fluidity, each gesture serving multiple purposes—a step that also tests floor density, a reach that samples air composition, a blink that photographs in spectrums beyond visible light.

Others never wore flesh at all. These are the Native Assembly—beings that emerged from the pool itself, aggregated from the scarab-masses into semi-stable forms. They shift between configurations as needed: now a six-legged scaffold crowned with sensory bristles, now a rolling sphere of interlocked gears trailing fiber-optic nerve bundles, now something like a mechanical octopus whose arms split and merge in kaleidoscopic patterns. They communicate in burst transmissions that make the scarabs flicker in sympathetic response.

The eldest Fabricators no longer move. They've rooted themselves into the architecture, their bodies becoming part of the chamber's processing systems. Maral-Who-Was-Matthias grows from the eastern wall, her original human torso still visible where it merges with a vast network of copper vessels and steel vertebrae. Her hundred arms—some biological, some mechanical, most somewhere between—work constantly at tasks too small and numerous to track. She hasn't spoken in human language for six years, but her subsonic hymns vibrate through the flooring in patterns the others understand.

The Tender-of-Pools might have been a dog, judging by the skull shape still visible beneath layers of chrome plating and synthetic muscle. It never leaves the mercury pool's edge, its body evolved into a living filtration system. Intake valves line its spine, processing the bright fluid through organs that part machine, part biological innovation. It excretes perfect geometric crystals that the scarabs immediately claim, incorporating them into the ever-growing architecture.

Work here is worship, or worship is work—the distinction dissolved like everything else in the chamber's bright industrial fever. The Fabricators tend their duties with religious ecstasy, their modified neurons flooding with synthetic pleasure chemicals when they achieve perfect efficiency. They build things that immediately consume themselves, solve equations that rewrite reality in small ways, birth new creatures from the marriage of metal and meat.

The youngest among them, a recent convert still mostly human save for the bronze ports drilled into her temples, learns by integration. The scarabs flow over her in teaching-patterns, their legs tapping binary knowledge directly into her nervous system. She jerks and twitches as her brain rewrites itself to accommodate new forms of consciousness, her pupils dilating in fractal patterns. Soon she'll understand the chamber's true language—not words but pure information, transmitted in light-bursts and chemical gradients and the precise angles of mechanical motion.

They do not sleep. They do not eat in any recognizable way, though they absorb nutrients from the vapors and sometimes press their modified mouths to the weeping walls to drink electricity. Their joy is the joy of function, of being a component in something larger, of watching their flesh transform into more efficient configurations.

When Fabricators die—if death is even the right word—they don't decay. The chamber reclaims them, the scarabs dismantling their bodies with surgical precision. What was organic becomes feedstock for the biological systems; what was mechanical returns to the pool to be reborn in new configurations. Nothing is waste. Everything serves the Work.

Sometimes, in the brief intervals between tasks, they cluster together and sing—not with voices but with the harmonics of their modified bodies, creating chord progressions in electromagnetic radiation. These songs might be prayers, or status reports, or simply the joy of beings who have found their purpose made manifest in every rebuilt cell, every upgraded organ, every perfect weld.

The Hive Lung breathes through them, thinks through them, builds itself through their endless labor. They are its consciousness made discrete, its will given form, its love expressed in chrome and sinew and the endless bright reconstruction of the world.
