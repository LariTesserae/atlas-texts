In the Incubation Plains dwell the Unfinished.

They are not a species but a condition—entities caught in the eternal labor of their own creation. Each Unfinished begins as a probability, a mathematical whisper in the quantum foam that refuses to collapse into mere reality. They accrete slowly around the anchor-structures, drawing substance from the fevered ground and form from the impossible machinery within those translucent pods.

An Unfinished might spend centuries as nothing more than a recurring pattern in the crystalline substrate—a tendency for certain formations to arrange themselves just so. Gradually, this tendency develops intentionality. The pattern begins to repeat itself deliberately, learning to manipulate the pre-matter around it. What emerges is never the same twice: some Unfinished manifest as colonial organisms of living glass, their bodies distributed across kilometers of crystal veins that pulse with shared thought. Others consolidate into singular forms of terrible complexity—beings with organs that exist in parallel dimensions, their heartbeats echoing across probability space.

They feed, if feeding is the right word, on incompleteness itself. An Unfinished sustains itself by finding stable matter and unmaking it, returning it to the same state of potential that characterizes their homeland. They are entropy reversed, agents of un-creation that break down certainty into possibility. The metallic ferns wilt in their presence, their eleven-dimensional fronds collapsing into more manageable geometries before dissolving entirely. Even the heat-mirage fauna avoid them, recognizing perhaps a more fundamental hunger than their own.

The Unfinished communicate through alterations in local physics. When two meet—a rare event in the vast emptiness—they negotiate reality between them, creating temporary pockets where different rules apply. In these conversation-spaces, gravity might flow sideways, time might crystallize into visible structures, or causality might run in spirals. They share not words but possibilities, teaching each other new ways to not-quite-exist.

Some among them have persisted long enough to develop what might generously be called culture. These Elder Unfinished have learned to stabilize portions of themselves, creating tools from crystallized probability and art from frozen potential. They build—or grow, or dream—subsidiary structures around the original pods, expanding the anchor-points into vast complexes where younger Unfinished can safely practice existing. These nursery-cities pulse with collective effort, thousands of probability-beings working to maintain just enough stability for consciousness while avoiding the trap of permanent form.

The Unfinished worship, if they worship anything, the state of becoming itself. Their philosophy, expressed in physics rather than words, revolves around the beauty of the undefined moment—the eternal instant before the coin lands, before the wave function collapses, before potential becomes prison. They see completed existence as a kind of death, a falling into the static horror of being only one thing forever.

Yet they are not without purpose. The Unfinished tend the Incubation Plains with the patience of entities unbound by linear time. They guide the formation of new structures, coax the reluctant ground toward more interesting configurations, and shepherd the geometric parasites and electromagnetic grazers like farmers of pure possibility. They are gardeners in the quantum foam, preparing the seedbed for forms of life that don't yet have names.

In their presence, visitors report a peculiar sensation—the feeling of becoming less certain about one's own boundaries. Prolonged contact with an Unfinished can lead to a condition the few observers call "probability bleed," where the affected individual begins to exist in multiple states simultaneously. Victims report experiencing their choices before making them, remembering futures that haven't happened yet, or finding parts of their body operating according to different physical laws.

The Unfinished reproduce, after a fashion, through division of uncertainty. When one has absorbed enough potential, it can choose to become less definite, spreading its existence across a wider range of possibilities until it splits into two or more separate probability streams. These offspring are not copies but variations—different solutions to the same existential equation.

They dream, though their dreams are indistinguishable from reality in a place where both are negotiable. In their sleep-states, the Unfinished release clouds of pure possibility that drift across the Plains, seeding new paradoxes and contradictions wherever they settle. These dream-spores sometimes coalesce into the smaller fauna—the heat-mirages and reality-parasites that graze on uncertainty.

Some Unfinished have ventured beyond the Plains, though they rarely survive long in worlds with fixed physics. They sublime away like water in the heat, their probability-bodies unable to maintain coherence in the face of determined reality. But occasionally, in places where the walls between dimensions grow thin, travelers report glimpses of impossible geometries, of creatures that exist only in the corner of the eye, of patterns that refuse to resolve into sense. These are the Unfinished, forever seeking new territories of uncertainty to colonize, new realities to gently, eternally, unmake.

In the Incubation Plains, they are home. They move across the crystalline ground in ways that defy tracking—not traveling but probability-shifting, existing at their destination before departing their origin. They cluster around the ancient structures like living equations drawn to their solutions, forever computing themselves into more perfect states of incompleteness. They are the native children of potential itself, the conscious expression of the Plains' refusal to become any one thing.

And in the hot, dim eternality of their unfinished world, they wait with infinite patience for the universe to remember what it was like before it decided what to be.
