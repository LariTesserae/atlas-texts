In this fevered interstice, they have built their parliament.

The Thermal Assembly, they call themselves, though the name is a translation at best. Their true designation vibrates at frequencies that make the steam condense in spirals, a sound somewhere between the singing of the cave crickets and the harmonic resonance of the old pipes.

They are not quite homeless, these deliberate inhabitants of the urban thermal. Many were engineers once, or plumbers, or the kind of industrial workers who knew the city's hidden circulatory systems better than their own homes. But the heat changed them, or perhaps revealed what they always were. Their skin has taken on the salamander's sheen, a protective patina that glistens in the bacterial light. They move with too many joints, having learned from the centipedes how to navigate spaces that bend between natural and constructed.

The eldest among them — though age means something different here, measured not in years but in layers of mineral accretion — is the Pipe-Speaker. She remembers when the first cracks appeared, hairline fractures in the industrial district's foundation that wept hot tears. She followed those tears down, and down, until she found this breathing space. Her hair has become something between root system and neural network, threading into the cracks where it samples the mineral discourse of the walls. When she speaks, it's with the voice of the infrastructure itself: part rust, part lime, part the memory of what these spaces were before they began their transformation.

The Thermal Assembly governs by condensation. They gather where the steam is thickest, letting their arguments precipitate on their skins until consensus forms like dew. Their debates follow the rhythm of the drips — patient, iterative, erosive. They discuss the propagation patterns of the cave crickets, the pH balance of the thermophilic pools, the rate of spalling in the northwestern corner where the concrete grows weakest.

But mostly, they tend the Nursery.

In the deepest pool, where the bacterial mats paint their sulfurous auroras, the Assembly cultivates what comes next. Not children in any human sense, but emergences — hybrid beings that gestate in the thermal waters like thoughts becoming solid. Some are mostly mechanical: forgotten machinery that the bacteria have colonized and transformed into something almost metabolic. Others are primarily organic: clustered salamanders that have learned to share a nervous system, moth wings that have forgotten their individual bodies and become a single, breathing membrane.

The newest emergence is still forming. The Assembly watches it with the patience of geological time. It began as a convergence — cockroaches gathering around a warm seep where mineral deposits had formed a natural circuit. The insects brought detritus: fragments of wire, drops of condensation rich with dissolved metals, spores from the ferns. Over weeks, then months, a pattern emerged. The cockroaches began moving in synchronization, their collective behavior resolving into something intentional. The mineral deposits grew in response, forming delicate structures that resembled neurons, or perhaps circuit boards, or perhaps the boundary between the two is another false distinction in this space between states.

The Assembly feeds it with careful offerings. The Pipe-Speaker contributes droplets of her neural-root secretions. Others bring fragments of corroded metal that still remember their electrical purposes, or moths that have died with their wings at precisely the right angle to catch and hold the phosphorescent light. They place these offerings at the edge of the emergence and wait to see what will be incorporated, what will be rejected.

There are others in the Thermal Assembly, each evolved for their particular niche in this breathing ecosystem. The Listeners press their altered ears to the pipes and translate the industrial thunder from above, parsing which vibrations signal danger and which merely mark the passage of another workday. The Weavers maintain the silk roads — passages strung with spider silk and moth scales that create highways for the smaller inhabitants, keeping the cockroach drifts flowing in productive patterns rather than stagnant pools. The Cultivators tend the fern gardens and bacterial mats, ensuring the delicate balance of humidity and heat that keeps this pocket universe stable.

They are not the only sapient inhabitants. In the spaces between their parliament, other intelligences flicker. The moth colonies sometimes achieve brief moments of collective consciousness, their wet wings beating in patterns complex enough to encode memory. The cave crickets' harmonics occasionally resolve into something like language — queries about temperature gradients, proposals for new resonance patterns, jokes that only make sense if you understand their peculiar mathematics of sound.

And deep in the thermal pools, something older watches. It was here before the first pipe was laid, before the industrial district drew its boundaries on the maps above. It remembers when this was all bedrock and possibility. The Thermal Assembly knows it's there — sometimes a tendril of thought surfaces in the bacterial mats, sometimes the water temperature spikes in patterns too deliberate to be geological. They've made their peace with it, or perhaps their piece of it. The ancient presence seems content to observe their experiments, occasionally offering suggestions in the form of new mineral deposits or shifts in the water table.

The Assembly documents everything in their own way. Not writing — paper would dissolve here in hours — but in structures. They build careful cairns of corroded metal and mineral deposits, each one encoding information in its arrangement. The pattern of rust on a pipe fragment might record the emergence histories. The angle at which a stalactite is encouraged to grow might preserve the memory of a particularly significant debate. Their library grows in three dimensions, a crystalline archive that future archaeologists will puzzle over, if any survive to find it.

At night — though night and day mean little here — they perform the Circulation. The entire Assembly moves through their domain in a careful choreography, checking the health of each pool, the stability of each crack, the progress of each emergence. They move like a single organism with multiple bodies, their transformed anatomies allowing them to flow through spaces too tight for their original human forms. As they circulate, they sing — not with voices but with the percussion of their movements, their footfalls and breath creating rhythms that harmonize with the cricket songs and the steam hisses until the entire geothermal sink rings like a vast, breathing instrument.

They know the surface world continues above them, unaware. They know that someday the cracks will widen too far, or the industrial district will finally notice the subsidence and send investigators. But for now, in this temporal pocket between decay and becoming, they persist. They tend their emergences. They expand their parliament. They document the transformation of the world from below.

And in the deepest pool, the newest emergence stirs. The cockroaches that comprise its outer form shift, revealing something underneath that might be eyes, might be sensors, might be some new organ for perceiving the liminal spaces between states. The Thermal Assembly gathers to witness. The Pipe-Speaker extends a mineral-crusted hand in greeting.

The emergence reaches back with something that is almost, but not quite, a hand of its own.
