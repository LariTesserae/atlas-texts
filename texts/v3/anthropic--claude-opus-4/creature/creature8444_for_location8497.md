**The Distillers**

They move through the chambers like condensation sliding down glass—slow, inevitable, nearly invisible until they choose to be seen. The Distillers have no fixed form, existing as something between liquid and vapor, their bodies a constant negotiation with the moisture that saturates this place.

When they gather themselves enough to be observed, they appear as elongated figures of living water, their surfaces tension-held like standing drops the height of a man. Their bodies are translucent, revealing internal currents that spiral and flow in patterns matching the carved channels in the walls. Minerals from the weeping stone have accumulated within them over centuries, creating veins of copper-green and chalk-white that branch through their forms like fossilized lightning.

They were not the builders—that much is certain from how they move through the space, always following the channels, never crossing the dry spaces between. They are what the builders made, or perhaps what the building itself produced through its endless cycling of water and heat and stone. Each Distiller is a living memory of every drop that has passed through the foundry, a concentration of its purpose given form.

Their work is patient and precise. They tend the basins with movements that disturb no surface, adjusting the flow between chambers by simply existing in certain spaces, their presence altering the humidity enough to redirect the condensation. They read the stone's temperature through their skin, if skin is the right word for that perpetually reforming boundary between self and environment. When the heat grows too intense, they spread themselves thin, becoming cooling mist. When the channels threaten to clog with mineral deposits, they concentrate their forms and flow through with enough force to clean the passages.

The Distillers communicate through ripples—subtle variations in their surface tension that propagate between individuals like waves in connected vessels. They have no need for sound in the conventional sense, though sometimes their movements create harmonics in the water flowing down the central pyramid, adding layers to its thrumming note.

They feed, if feeding is the right concept, on the impurities the water leaves behind. The minerals that would otherwise clog the system are absorbed into their bodies, processed through some chemistry of their strange metabolism, and eventually deposited as those pale crystalline formations that outline the stone joints. They are living filters, ensuring the water that finally collects in the deepest basins is purer than any natural spring.

At the cycle's peak, when the foundry exhales its great cloud of steam, the Distillers sublime. Their liquid forms surrender cohesion, becoming part of the vapor that fills the chambers. In these moments, they merge temporarily into a collective consciousness, sharing accumulated experiences, distributing minerals between bodies, coordinating the next cycle's work. Then, as the steam condenses, they precipitate back into individual forms, refreshed and repurposed.

New Distillers are born rarely, only when the conditions align perfectly—when the mineral content, temperature, and flow rate create a standing vortex in one of the basins that persists long enough to develop its own coherence. The older ones recognize these moments and gather around the nascent form, contributing drops of their own substance to help establish its initial boundaries. The young ones take decades to learn the channel patterns, centuries to understand their role in the greater work.

They guard the glyphs in the deepest chamber with particular attention, ensuring the phosphorescent water flows continuously through those carved symbols. Whether they understand the meaning or simply recognize the importance through some inherited instinct is unclear. They will position themselves for hours beneath the glowing channels, letting the luminous water flow through their bodies, carrying its light briefly within their transparent forms before releasing it back to the stone.

The Distillers have no conception of an outside world. Their universe is bounded by carved stone and flowing water, measured in cycles of heat and condensation. They are not trapped—they are precisely where they need to be, doing what they were made to do. In their liquid consciousness, concepts like freedom or confinement have no meaning. There is only the work: the eternal refinement of water through stone, the careful maintenance of flow, the patient accumulation and redistribution of minerals.

Sometimes, rarely, one of them will hold a form long enough to suggest features—a face that appears for a moment in their flowing surface, hands that almost resolve before dissolving back into the general current of their body. These might be memories of the builders, encoded somehow in the foundry's endless circulation. Or perhaps they are attempts at imagination, brief experiments in being something other than what they are. But the attempts never last. The pull of the cycle is too strong, the work too essential.

They are, in their own way, perfect. Created for a single purpose, they execute it without question or deviation, finding in that endless repetition something that might be satisfaction, might be meaning, might simply be existence distilled to its purest form.
