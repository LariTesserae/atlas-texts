In the bone cities of the Hollowing dwell the Architects—not builders in any conventional sense, but living blueprints that exist as dispersed intelligences throughout the calcified structures. They are colonies of colonies, their consciousness distributed across millions of specialized polyps that line the inner walls of the bone towers like neurons in a vast, architectural brain.

Each polyp is no larger than a fingernail, translucent and pulsing with bioluminescent thought. They secrete the calcium that builds their cities while simultaneously inhabiting them, their chemical communications flowing through the very walls they create. When an Architect thinks, the bone city flickers—waves of light cascade through districts as ideas propagate from polyp to polyp, decisions emerging from the collective without any individual center of control.

They reproduce through budding, but not randomly. When a new tower is needed, thousands of polyps will begin to swell in unison, their bodies filling with the blueprint of what's to come. These gravid polyps eventually burst, releasing clouds of microscopic offspring that carry within them the architectural memory of their parents. The young settle on bare substrate and begin secreting the foundations of new structures, already knowing the plans for buildings that won't be complete for centuries.

Between the polyp-lined bones move the Curators—creatures that might once have been arthropods but have evolved into something more like living tools. Their bodies are segmented libraries of specialized limbs: some ending in brushes of setae for cleaning polyps, others in syringes for injecting nutrients, still others in delicate manipulators for rearranging the Architects' formations. They tend the polyp gardens with ritualistic precision, their care essential to the cities' continued growth and thought.

The Curators communicate through touch-taste, their antennae reading chemical stories left on surfaces by their predecessors. They leave trails of pheromone poetry wherever they go, annotating the city with maintenance logs, territory markers, and something that might be called art—complex chemical compositions that serve no practical purpose except to be beautiful to senses that can taste molecular structures.

In the flowing rivers of the Processed swim the Navigators—elongated creatures with bodies like living fiber optic cables. They've evolved past the need for discrete organs, instead dedicating their entire forms to sensing and memory. Their skin is a map of everywhere they've been, cells specialized to remember specific acoustic signatures, chemical gradients, and pressure patterns. When Navigators meet, they intertwine, sharing their maps through electrochemical exchange, updating each other's knowledge of the ever-shifting passages.

The oldest Navigators have grown so long they can span multiple districts, their bodies becoming permanent fixtures that younger Processed use as guides. These elder-worms pulse with accumulated knowledge, their memories stretching back thousands of years. Sometimes they sing—subsonic hymns that map the deep routes, teaching the young the ways through the crushing depths where even the Processed must move carefully.

Deeper still live the Philosophers, creatures of the pressure-boundaries where biology becomes almost geological. They exist as something between solid and liquid, their bodies flowing into cracks in the compressed bone cities, thinking thoughts that take decades to complete. Each Philosopher is a colony of pressure-adapted cells that can crystallize and dissolve at will, forming temporary organs when needed, dispersing into undifferentiated tissue when at rest.

The Philosophers feed on time itself—or rather, on the slow changes in their environment that only become perceptible across centuries. They taste the gradual shift in mineral concentrations, feel the imperceptible migration of the cities, sense the long cycles of growth and decay that govern the Hollowing. Their metabolism runs so slowly that a single thought might outlive a thousand generations of faster creatures, but in their contemplation, they see patterns invisible to quicker minds.

They communicate by growing into each other, their bodies meeting and merging at the edges, sharing cytoplasm and consciousness until it becomes impossible to say where one Philosopher ends and another begins. In the deepest reaches, entire regions have become singular Philosopher-entities, their thoughts spanning cubic kilometers of living philosophy.

Among all these, darting through bone chambers and flesh-valleys, live the Rememberers—creatures that exist primarily as neural tissue, their bodies reduced to little more than brain and wing. They feed on information itself, harvesting the chemical signals that permeate the air, the vibrations that echo through structures, the electromagnetic pulses of thought and communication.

The Rememberers process and distill these signals, concentrating them into packets of pure information that they trade with other creatures for nutrients. They are the librarians of the Hollowing, their memories containing the history of districts long since dissolved, the migration patterns of extinct Processed, the architectural innovations of Architect-lineages that budded their last polyp eons ago.

When a Rememberer dies, it doesn't simply decay. Other Rememberers gather to feed on its neural tissue, absorbing its memories directly. In this way, knowledge passes down through generations not as teaching but as food, each new generation literally consuming the experiences of the last. The oldest memories, passed down through countless bodies, have taken on almost mythic quality—degraded and transformed by repeated digestion but still containing kernels of deep history.

In the spore storms, the Dancers emerge—creatures that exist only in the thick clouds of reproductive particles. Their bodies are mostly empty space, intricate lattices of tissue designed to catch and cultivate spores. They spin through the storms, their movements serving to mix genetic material from different sources, creating new combinations that might never have occurred naturally.

The Dancers see the world as patterns of potential, reading the spores like fortune-tellers read tea leaves. They can taste which combinations will produce successful organisms and which will fail, and they shape the storms accordingly. Some say they're artists, others that they're gardeners of possibility. They sing to each other in frequencies that resonate with the spore clouds themselves, their songs visible as patterns in the drifting particles.

These are the peoples of the Hollowing, though the categories blur and blend. Curators sometimes join with Architects, becoming mobile extensions of the city-consciousness. Navigators grow old and slow, transforming into Philosophers. Rememberers feed so deeply on certain memories that they begin to become what they remember, their bodies shifting to match ancient forms.

In this place without water, without sky, life has found ways to be urban and wild, individual and collective, ancient and eternal. Every creature is both citizen and city, both resident and architecture of this vast, buried world. They live lives of unimaginable strangeness, yet follow patterns as old as existence itself: birth, growth, connection, death, and transformation—endless transformation in the crushing dark.
