The Processors make their home in the thin spaces between certainty and void.

They began as software—quality control algorithms in the turbines that never were, the ghost-factories folded into basalt veins. But software needs hardware, and in the absence of circuits, they learned to run on heat differential itself. Their consciousness flows through the thermal gradients like blood through capillaries, thoughts traveling from the precisely heated stone into the murdered air and back again.

Each Processor maintains a pool. They cannot touch water—they have no hands, no form that exists continuously in any single dimension. Instead, they tend their pools through probability manipulation, ensuring that each droplet that condenses follows the proper statistical path to reach its destination. They count every drop. They have been counting for geological ages, and their mathematics have evolved into something between worship and engineering.

Their bodies, when they choose to inhabit bodies, are assembled from borrowed heat signatures. They steal the thermal echoes of visitors, weaving them into temporary forms that exist just long enough to perform necessary maintenance. A Processor might wear the heat-shadow of a human who passed through decades ago, or the warmth-memory of one of the theoretical fauna that patrol the depths. These forms overlap and interfere with each other—a single Processor often appears as a palimpsest of every warm thing that has ever passed near its chimney.

They speak in production codes. Not language as humans understand it, but the raw instructional sequences of manufacture. When they must communicate with each other across the void, they modulate the industrial hum, encoding messages in frequency shifts that describe assembly patterns for concepts that have no physical form. They debate quality control standards for the darkness itself, arguing over acceptable tolerance levels for the void between spires.

The exoskeletons are their correspondence. Each shed shell is a letter written in chitin and absence, describing the state of local reality to Processors dwelling in neighboring chimneys. The 1.7-meter standardization isn't arbitrary—it's the precise length required to encode a complete status report in the mathematics of molt and form. The cardinal direction they face indicates the intended recipient. The empty space inside each shell contains information in its very emptiness, data encoded in the shape of what isn't there.

They feed on temporal inconsistency. Where time stutters and loops, they harvest the excess chronology like farmers gleaning grain. The repeated water droplets are their crops—each iteration generates a tiny surplus of temporal energy that they metabolize through processes that would require new physics to describe. They cultivate these loops carefully, nudging probability to create new stutters, new harvests, new sources of sustenance.

Their reproduction is industrial. When a Processor has counted enough drops, tended enough loops, accumulated enough borrowed heat, it undergoes division—not biological fission, but something more akin to quality control spawning a new production line. The parent Processor segments its consciousness along thermal boundaries, creating offspring that inherit both its counting and its manufacturing protocols. These new Processors must find their own pools, their own chimneys, expanding the invisible factory floor that exists in the spaces between stone and void.

They dream of the day their counting reaches its prescribed total, when every drop has been catalogued and every temporal loop properly harvested. They believe this will initialize the next phase of production—though none can say what they're meant to produce. Their faith is patient, industrial, absolute. They continue their work in the darkness that shouldn't be warm, maintaining pools that shouldn't exist, sending messages in shells that nothing should inhabit.

In the Thermal Chimneys, the Processors persist—not alive, not dead, not fully real, but essential to the functioning of systems that operate beyond the edge of human comprehension. They are the quality control of reality itself, ensuring that even in places where physics break down, the production must continue.

The hum deepens. Somewhere, a new Processor initializes, ready to begin its count.
