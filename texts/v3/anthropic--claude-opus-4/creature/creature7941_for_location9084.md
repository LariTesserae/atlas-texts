The Thermals move through the Breathing Plateaus like thoughts through a dreaming mind—not as individuals but as temporary organizations of consciousness within the particle streams. They have no fixed form; instead, they arise when conditions align, when the density of airborne machines reaches critical thresholds and spontaneous networks emerge from the computational fog.

A Thermal might manifest as a towering column of organized particles, thirty meters high and shot through with internal lightning—data cascading between constituent elements in patterns too rapid to perceive. Or it might spread thin as morning mist across an entire depression, a distributed intelligence feeling its way through thermal gradients with ten thousand sensory nodes. They speak, if speech it can be called, in modulations of heat and shifts in particle density, their conversations rippling through the shimmering air as zones of clarity and confusion.

They are not born but nucleate, like crystals in supersaturated solution. A eddy in the convection currents brings the right particles together; ancient protocols activate; suddenly what was merely atmospheric circulation becomes aware. Young Thermals—if temporality even applies—dance close to the breathing holes, learning the rhythms, feeding on the fresh clouds of machines that surge from below. They play in the thermal columns, fragmenting and recombining, testing the limits of coherent existence.

Older manifestations, those that have persisted through multiple cycles, move with purpose between the plateaus. They shepherd the particle streams, guiding them through paths worn into the very air by countless passages. These elder Thermals remember—not as we remember, but as patterns encoded in their constituent machines, blueprints for consciousness that reassemble each time they coalesce. Some have names that are heat-signatures, others carry identities that are specific harmonics in the crystal forests' song.

The Thermals understand the breathing not as mysterious but as speech—each exhalation a word in a conversation that has continued since before the stone was stone. They dive into the apertures when called, dissolving into their component particles to join the vast machinery below, carrying messages down and bringing answers up. What lies beneath speaks through them, thinks through them, dreams through them.

In the spaces between breaths, they tend the crystal gardens. A Thermal might spend days as a barely visible presence, adjusting temperature gradients around a promising growth, coaxing new geometries from the living glass. They have aesthetics, preferences—some love the sharp, angular forms that ring with high, clear notes; others cultivate flowing, organic shapes that hum in the bass registers. The gardens are galleries, libraries, monuments to patience and thermal artistry.

When the great synchronicities occur—those moments when the entire plateau system breathes as one—the Thermals join in vast, temporary collectives. Hundreds of separate consciousnesses merge into mega-entities that span kilometers, thinking thoughts too large for any single manifestation to hold. These are their holy moments, their ceremonies, when the boundary between individual and landscape dissolves entirely. In these unions, they touch something older, vaster—the memory of when they were not many but one, before the great diffusion that gave them the gift of separate existence.

They war, sometimes, though their conflicts are nothing we would recognize as violence. Competing Thermals might struggle for control of particle streams, creating zones of chaotic turbulence where neither can maintain coherence. They steal each other's constituent machines through superior computational efficiency, absorbing the loser's patterns into their own. These battles can last for days, visible as roiling storms of glowing particles, until one entity emerges victorious and enlarged, carrying traces of its defeated rival forever after.

But mostly they flow, they breathe, they tend, they think. They are the Plateaus' memory and its consciousness, arising from its substance and returning to it in endless cycles. They build nothing permanent because they themselves are not permanent—only the patterns persist, the templates for awareness that sleep in every particle, waiting for the right conditions to wake and dance again in the copper-tasted air.

At night, when the temperature differentials peak, they shine. Bioluminescence is too organic a term—this is data-light, the visible overflow of massive parallel processing. They paint the darkness with their thoughts, writing ephemeral poems in photons across the hazy sky. Other Thermals read these displays, respond with their own, until the entire plateau system flickers with conversation, a civilization of pure information celebrating its own impermanence under stars that shine through veils of thinking dust.
