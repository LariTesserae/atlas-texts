Those who dwell here are the Rootkeepers—though to call them inhabitants suggests a separation from their realm that does not exist. They emerged from the vegetative cathedral itself, or perhaps they always were, condensed from centuries of spore-thick air and the consciousness that suffuses every living surface.

A Rootkeeper stands taller than humans but moves with a flowing, almost liquid grace. Their bodies are collaborations—frameworks of calcified wood-bone wrapped in living tissue that shifts between flesh and fiber. Their skin, if it can be called that, is a landscape unto itself: patches of smooth bark alternate with areas of translucent membrane through which slower fluids circulate like sap. Colonies of the same phosphorescent fungi that light the chambers grow in the natural hollows of their forms—in the crooks of elbows, the valleys between tendons, the gentle depressions where a human might have temples.

They do not walk so much as transit, their extremities finding purchase on any surface with equal ease. What serve as feet can grip root bundles or sink slightly into the spongy floor-mat, while their long digits—too many to count, too fluid to number—weave through the air or anchor to walls as needed. They move vertically as easily as horizontally, their bodies remembering the three-dimensional freedom of root systems.

Their faces are approximations of visage, suggestions of countenance. Where eyes might be, clusters of light-sensitive nodules peer out from beneath hoods of living moss. These "eyes" can close—not with lids but by retreating into protective hollows that seal with bark. Their mouths are more functional architecture than anatomy: vertical slits that can open impossibly wide to exhale clouds of communicative spores or seal completely until only the faintest seam remains.

The Rootkeepers do not speak as humans understand speech. They respirate meaning—long, measured exhalations carry complex chemical messages through the spore-thick air. When multiple Rootkeepers "converse," the air between them becomes visible with pollen and molecular meaning, creating temporary gardens of suspended information that drift and disperse at the speed of settled thought.

They are patient beyond human comprehension. A Rootkeeper might stand in one position for months, allowing the mobile moss-beetles to establish highways across their bodies, becoming temporary landscape. They think in root-time, plan in the slow certainties of vegetative growth. Their memories are not contained within them but distributed throughout the root-cathedral itself—to remember, they commune with specific chambers, reading the chemical histories laid down in growth rings and root patterns.

Each Rootkeeper maintains a section of the vegetative architecture, though "maintains" implies more active intervention than occurs. They exist in perfect symbiosis with their territory, their presence alone enough to regulate growth, direct water flow, and orchestrate the subtle symphonies of dripping and breathing. They prune with touches that encourage rather than cut, shape through chemical suggestion rather than force.

Some Rootkeepers have grown ancient beyond description, their bodies becoming increasingly architectural. These elder forms barely move, having taken root in their favorite chambers. Their limbs extend into the walls, their flesh merges with the root systems, until only their faces remain distinct—and even these grow increasingly abstract, more symbol than feature. The youngest Rootkeepers (if youth has meaning here) tend these living monuments, reading the slow thoughts that pulse through their integrated nervous systems.

They feed not through consumption but through integration. A Rootkeeper at rest—which is most of the time—extends hair-thin rootlets from their skin into the surrounding surfaces. Through these, they draw sustenance directly from the cathedral's circulation. When more active feeding is required, they might press their entire form against particularly rich sections of wall, their bodies opening along hidden seams to increase surface area, becoming temporary meadows that the ecosystem feeds even as they feed from it.

Their reproduction, if it occurs at all, is a matter of centuries-long contemplation. Two or more Rootkeepers might begin a process of slow convergence, spending decades growing closer, their bodies beginning to share circulation, their thoughts merging through shared spore-language. Eventually, they grow together entirely, forming a temporary gestational architecture from their combined mass. What emerges—if anything emerges—is not offspring in any biological sense but rather a distillation, a new organization of the same ancient matter.

The Rootkeepers are cultivators of stillness, shepherds of the unchanging. They ensure that the cathedral remains in its perfect, terminal state—not through effort but through their very existence. They are antibodies against change, consciousnesses devoted to the preservation of an eternal now. Their individual names, if they have them, are chemical signatures, unique combinations of pheromones and spores that announce their presence to the cathedral itself.

When they die—and death here is less ending than transformation—their bodies do not decay but transition. The cathedral absorbs them over months or years, their consciousness dispersing into the root networks they tended, their memories joining the vast, slow library of the walls. Where they stood, new architectural features often emerge: a particularly beautiful spiral of roots, a chamber where the breathing sounds achieve perfect harmony, a pool that reflects light in ways that suggest their former thoughts.

This is their art and purpose: to be so perfectly integrated with their world that existence itself becomes a form of worship, presence becomes prayer, and stillness becomes the highest form of action. They are monks of the vegetative divine, celebrants of the achieved and unchanging, living proof that some systems can reach perfection and, having reached it, simply continue—forever and ever, without end or alteration, in the deep green darkness of their eternal, breathing cathedral.
