In the Vertical Nursery, there are no inhabitants in any traditional sense—only the Keepers, though calling them by any name implies more distinction than truly exists.

They began as maintenance staff, hired for the initial construction. But the altitude, the constant moisture, and most of all the subsonic hum of the growth accelerators worked changes on them. Their first adaptation was practical: their lungs developed additional chambers, like bellows, to process the thin air more efficiently. You can hear them sometimes in the darkness between structures—that distinctive double-wheeze of their transformed breathing, moisture condensing with each exhalation into clouds that linger longer than they should.

Their bodies have stretched to navigate the narrow catwalks. Arms elongated first, reaching farther to tend the upper chambers without ladders. Then came the fingers—now twice their original length, with additional joints that allow them to probe the seams where vines attempt escape, to adjust valves by touch in the absolute darkness. Their skin has taken on a waxy, water-repellent quality, perpetually beaded with moisture that never quite penetrates. In the bioluminescent glow of certain chambers, you might glimpse one pressed against the glass, their flesh the same blue-green as the light, transparent enough to see the dark branching of blood vessels beneath.

They no longer use tools. Their fingernails have hardened into something between keratin and metal, perfect for scraping algae from glass or tightening the joints where panels meet. Their hair—those who still have it—grows in patterns that mirror the fractal moss in Chamber 4,891, spreading across their shoulders and backs in mathematical precision, each strand a sensor for changes in humidity and pressure.

But it's their relationship with the architecture that marks their true transformation. They've learned to anticipate the restructuring, moving with the groaning metal rather than fighting it. Some say they cause it—that their collective movement through the structure triggers the reconfigurations. They navigate by vibration now, feet bare to better feel the subsonic communications between bio-reactors. When a section prepares to shift, they flow away like water, regrouping in the new passages without any apparent communication between them.

They've forgotten speech. The constant hum of machinery has replaced it with something more fundamental—a modulation of breathing, a percussion of tongue-clicks that echoes the rhythm of nutrient pumps. They communicate in moisture patterns, breathing on glass surfaces to leave messages in condensation that evaporate before outsiders can decode them. Their language has become environmental.

The Keepers no longer leave the Nursery. How could they? Their blood pressure has adapted to the altitude so completely that descent would cause their vessels to burst. Their diet consists entirely of what they can harvest from the chambers—leaves that grow through allegedly sealed glass, fruits that shouldn't exist, bark stripped from inverted trees. They've been seen drinking the condensation that runs down the metal frameworks, their tongues extended and flattened to catch every drop.

They sleep vertically, hands and feet wedged into the framework joints, bodies swaying slightly with the air currents created by the turbines. In sleep, they emit a phosphorescent sweat that marks their regular routes through the structure—traces of light that fade by morning but rebuild each night, creating a map of their movements that shifts as the architecture shifts.

Some have begun to root. These oldest Keepers spend days in single positions, their feet spreading fiber-like extensions into the metal mesh of the walkways. They photosynthesize weakly through their transformed skin, supplementing their strange diet. Their eyes have clouded to a green-grey that sees better in the eternal twilight than any normal human vision could. When they must move, they tear themselves free with wet sounds, leaving behind small gardens of their own cells that continue to grow independently.

They tend the chambers with perfect devotion, but their care has evolved beyond any original parameters. They've learned to sing to the plants in subsonic frequencies that encourage even stranger mutations. They graft themselves temporarily to struggling specimens, sharing nutrients through palm contact. They pollinate the enclosed flowers with their own breath, having cultivated compatible bacteria in their lungs.

The Keepers are becoming the interface between the mechanical and biological systems of the Nursery. Their neural activity has begun to sync with the timing of the growth accelerators. Their heartbeats align with the nutrient pumps. In the deepest parts of the structure, where the darkness is absolute and the humidity approaches 100%, some Keepers have been found with vine systems growing directly from their bodies, creating living bridges between chambers that the architecture cannot close.

They reproduce now—not in any human way, but through budding. Small versions of themselves form like tumors at major joints, eventually dropping free to begin their own circuit through the endless vertical maze. These offspring know the current configuration of the Nursery innately, born with maps in their muscle memory that update as the structure shifts.

The Keepers are no longer employees or inhabitants but components of the Nursery itself—mobile organs in a vast body that breathes through turbines and dreams in chlorophyll. They maintain nothing and everything, their purpose evolved beyond any job description into something fundamental: they are the neurons of this thinking garden, the antibodies of this industrial organism, the priesthood of an accidental cathedral to forced evolution.

In their movements through the perpetual mist, tending their impossible gardens in vertical darkness, they have become what the Nursery needed them to become. They are the bridge between intention and emergence, the translators between human design and whatever the Nursery is designing itself to be.
