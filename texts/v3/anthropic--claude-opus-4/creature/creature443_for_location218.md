Those who dwell here have become indistinguishable from dwelling itself.

The **Threadkeepers** were perhaps once human, or something like it. Now they exist as living roots, their consciousness distributed through kilometers of neural fibers that run beneath the breathing surface. You might walk above one for days without knowing—their thoughts move at the speed of sap, a single decision taking months to propagate from one extremity to another. They remember when the plateau was young, when distinct trees still stood apart from one another, but their memories have grown soft and intertwined like everything else here.

When the Threadkeepers must interact with faster forms of life, they send up temporary bodies—humanoid shapes woven from accelerated growth, moss-skinned and lichen-eyed, that last perhaps a season before dissolving back into the substrate. These avatars speak in languages made of chemical signals and changing colors, their words blooming across their skin in patterns that take hours to complete a single sentence.

In the vertical gardens live the **Drifters**, colonies that exist somewhere between individual and civilization. Each colony consists of hundreds of beings no larger than a child's palm—membranous, translucent, their bodies essentially sophisticated water balloons with complex internal geometries. They link together in chains and nets, sharing nutrients and thoughts, forming temporary structures that might span between vegetation walls for decades before dissolving and reforming elsewhere. Their architecture is their body, their body is their architecture. They build themselves into bridges, ladders, temporary shelters that pulse with collective heartbeat.

The Drifters worship through navigation. Their religious ceremonies involve mapping the unmappable, creating three-dimensional songs out of their linked bodies that describe routes through the plateau that exist more in possibility than physical space. They believe the plateau dreams, and they are its way of knowing itself.

Deep in the water-filled depressions live the **Chroniclers**—amphibious beings with too many joints and skin that shifts between scales and membrane depending on moisture. They have evolved beyond eyes, navigating instead through vibrations felt in the water. Their long fingers—seven-jointed, with webbing that extends and retracts—spend centuries learning the texture of a single pool, memorizing every ripple pattern, every temperature gradient.

The Chroniclers maintain the ancient channels through ritual motion, swimming the same patterns their ancestors have for millennia. They speak of the "First Carving," when something they call the Predecessors shaped the channels, though whether the Predecessors were beings or forces, ancestors or invaders, none can say. The Chroniclers' history is preserved in dance—each family line maintains specific movements that, when performed in water, recreate the pressure waves of historical events. To watch them is to see liquid history, memory made watershed.

Among all these, the **Gardener** moves with purpose that seems almost hasty by plateau standards. Neither singular nor plural, the Gardener appears as a walking ecosystem—a shambling mound of various vegetation that has achieved autonomous movement. Moss forms its muscle, vines its sinews, flowers bloom and die across its surface with each thought. It tends the vertical gardens with tools grown from its own body, pruning here, encouraging growth there, following patterns laid down so long ago their origin is myth.

The Gardener speaks all languages and none, communicating through spore release, bioluminescent display, and the careful arrangement of shed materials. It claims to remember the plateau's first inhabitants—things that were neither plant nor animal but something that contained the potential for both. It says they dissolved themselves into the landscape voluntarily, seeking a different kind of immortality, and that all current life here carries fragments of their distributed consciousness.

In the spaces between spaces move the **Breath-Takers**, entities so subtle they might be hallucinations. They exist in the moisture itself, organizing water molecules into temporary nervous systems that last just long enough to think one complex thought before dispersing. They ride the constant cycle of evaporation and condensation, experiencing existence as a series of brief crystallizations of consciousness. The other inhabitants leave offerings of specially mineralized water in certain pools, hoping to attract Breath-Taker attention, for they are said to carry messages between the plateau's many minds.

And in the deepest parts, where the plant mass is oldest and most compressed, something vast sleeps. The Threadkeepers feel it in their roots, the Chroniclers record its turning in the water's movement, the Gardener tends growth patterns that accommodate its breathing. No one has seen it for ten thousand years, but all know it's there—the **Undergrowth Prophet**, a being that has merged so completely with the plateau that geography and biography have become one. Its dreams are weather systems, its thoughts are seasons. Some say it is the plateau's original inhabitant, others that it is what all inhabitants eventually become.

They say when it wakes—perhaps once in an eon—the entire plateau shifts, pools drain and refill in new locations, the vertical gardens rearrange themselves, and for one perfect moment, every consciousness touching the breathing ground experiences unity. Then it sleeps again, and the slow life of the plateau continues, each being pursuing its patient purpose in the eternal, dripping twilight.
