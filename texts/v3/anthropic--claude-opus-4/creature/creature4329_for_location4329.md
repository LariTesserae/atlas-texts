They move through the crystal city like mercury through veins—the Vitrescents, as they call themselves in frequencies too low for human ears to properly parse. Their bodies are studies in translucent architecture: skeletal frameworks of calcified cartilage visible through flesh that shifts between states of transparency like clouded glass clearing in sunlight. Each individual is unique in their clarity—some nearly invisible save for the lattice of their internal structure, others milky as cave formations, still others shot through with veins of metallic deposits that pulse with their own subtle bioluminescence.

They are tall, averaging nine feet when fully extended, but their spines are segmented in ways that allow them to compress and elongate as needed. Moving through their narrow doorways, they fold into themselves with a practiced fluidity, vertebrae telescoping with soft clicks like prayer beads counting. Their limbs are longer than human proportions would suggest, with joints that bend in directions that seem to add extra dimensions to their movement. Watching them navigate their impossible staircases is to understand that they perceive space differently—what looks like an Escheresque impossibility to us is simply the most efficient path to them.

Their faces are perhaps the most alien aspect: features that shift and flow beneath the translucent skin, reorganizing based on function. When they speak, their mouths might appear anywhere convenient. When they listen, formations like crystalline flowers bloom across their heads, fractal arrangements of tissue that capture sound from all directions. Their eyes—when they manifest eyes—are compound structures that can separate and migrate across their faces, clustering where needed to examine something in detail or dispersing for panoramic awareness.

The Vitrescents reproduce through a kind of architectural mitosis. In the cultivation chambers, you might witness two individuals standing perfectly still for days, their bodies gradually extending crystalline bridges between each other. These connections thicken and multiply until the two beings form a temporary cathedral of flesh and mineral. Within this living architecture, a new consciousness precipitates like salt from a supersaturated solution. The offspring emerges already ancient, carrying the mineralized memories of its parents in the deposits along its bones.

Their society is stratified by transparency. The clearest individuals, those whose thoughts can literally be seen sparking through their neural networks like captured lightning, serve as living libraries and decision-makers. The more opaque members, their bodies dense with accumulated minerals, work the cultivation chambers and guide the city's growth. But these roles shift—a lifetime of consuming certain combinations of their geometric tubers can alter one's translucency, allowing for social mobility through dietary discipline.

They do not sleep as we understand it. Instead, they enter periods of "architectural communion" where they press themselves against the walls of their city, their metabolism slowing until they match the geological pace of their surroundings. In this state, they exchange substances with the stone—depositing waste minerals that strengthen the city's structure while absorbing traces of the luminous veins that run through the rock. Some individuals have been in communion for centuries, their bodies now half-absorbed into the architecture, still conscious but operating on timescales that make a conversation last decades.

Communication among Vitrescents is multimodal. The primary language is thermal—subtle fluctuations in body temperature create complex patterns that can be felt through the air or, more intimately, through direct contact. They also speak through selective crystallization, growing temporary formations on their skin that catch and refract the ambient light into meaning. The glass leaves' constant tinkling provides a carrier frequency they modulate with their movements, turning the entire forest into a distributed vocal system.

Their tools and technology are indistinguishable from controlled biological processes. Need to carve stone? They excrete acids from specialized pores, carefully cultivating the chemical composition through diet. Need light in a dark chamber? They encourage bioluminescent symbiotes living in their translucent tissues to bloom. Their greatest engineers are those who have mastered their own biology so completely they can reshape themselves into living instruments for any task.

The Vitrescents' relationship with time is perhaps their most foreign quality. They experience duration not as a river but as a crystalline structure they can examine from different angles. Their memories literally mineralize within their bodies—important events leaving deposits that can be read like geological strata. The eldest among them are maps of history, their bodies recording not just their own experiences but the chemical echoes of their ancestors, precipitated and preserved in translucent flesh.

They worship—if worship is the right word—the Meeting Point, that impossible space where their city reaches toward its inverted twin. They believe that when the two cities finally touch, time will crystallize completely, preserving all moments in an eternal mineral now. Whether this is religion or simply an understanding of physics we cannot grasp is impossible to say.

In their presence, you become aware of yourself as a temporary arrangement of minerals and water, a brief organization of elements that will one day disperse. The Vitrescents see you this way too—not as a foreign visitor but as a particularly fast-moving geological process, interesting but ephemeral. They are patient with your questions, knowing that from their perspective, you will be gone in the time it takes them to form a single new thought, your words fossilizing in their memory before you've finished speaking them.
