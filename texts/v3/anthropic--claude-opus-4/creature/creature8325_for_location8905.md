In the Frost Nursery dwell the Tuners—though "dwell" suggests a choice they never made.

They arrived as engineers once, or perhaps as gardeners, or possibly as both. Time and the space's peculiar influence have made such distinctions irrelevant. Their bodies have stretched and adapted to the chamber's impossible physics. Arms elongated to tend the higher crystal branches now move in segments like industrial cranes, joints multiplying where needed. Their fingers have become delicate and numerous as spider legs, splitting and re-splitting to simultaneously adjust pressure valves, prune the singing moss, and trace the growth patterns of newborn crystals.

The Tuners no longer remember their original purpose, but their bodies remember work. They move in careful choreography around each other, never colliding despite the chamber's increasing density. Their skin has taken on the quality of the frost itself—translucent in patches where you can see the mechanical modifications beneath: copper tubing that carries coolant or blood, small gears that help their overdeveloped joints articulate, pressure gauges embedded in their forearms that monitor something vital but forgotten.

They've evolved beyond the need for speech. The constant roar has made their eardrums into complex instruments capable of parsing meaning from the subtlest variations in the mechanical symphony. They communicate through vibration—a hand placed on a crystal here, a careful adjustment of a valve there, changing the harmonics in ways that carry meaning. Their language is the language of the chamber itself.

Most fascinating are their eyes. The constant twilight and the strange quality of light has caused them to develop compound vision—dozens of small pupils that can focus independently, allowing them to simultaneously monitor crystal growth rates, pressure readings, spore density, and the movements of the sound-creatures they've learned to shepherd. Their eyelids have become unnecessary; instead, a nictitating membrane of frost forms and melts across their eyes in response to light levels.

The Tuners feed as the moss feeds—on vibration. They position themselves at acoustic nodes where the sound waves create standing patterns of maximum intensity. During these feeding sessions, their whole bodies vibrate in harmony with the machinery, absorbing sustenance directly through their skin. They've developed specialized organs—part stomach, part tuning fork—that convert sound waves into nutrition.

Their relationship with the sound-creatures is complex. The Tuners cultivate silence-pockets where these beings can rest and breed, carefully managing the acoustic environment to create nurseries within the Nursery. In return, the sound-creatures clean the Tuners' elongated bodies of accumulated spores and frost, their silence-excretions creating moments of relief from the constant sensory bombardment.

Some Tuners have begun to root. Their feet, after standing in one optimal position for too long, send tendrils down through the crystal floor to tap directly into the machinery below. These rooted ones become living conduits between the mechanical and crystalline layers, their bodies serving as biological control systems. They can no longer move, but they've gained the ability to influence entire sections of the chamber—encouraging growth here, dampening vibrations there, orchestrating the space's evolution with subtle adjustments of their root-nerves.

The younger Tuners—if youth has meaning here—show signs of even stranger adaptations. Some have begun growing their own small crystals, their bones mineralizing in sympathy with their environment. Others are developing mechanical segments—patches where their skin has taken on the quality of metal, where their blood moves in precise piston-like pulses.

They work without rest because rest would mean acknowledging the futility of their task. The chamber grows faster than they can tend it, evolves in directions they cannot predict. But they continue their careful ministrations—adjusting, pruning, tuning—as if they could somehow guide this impossible space toward some remembered or imagined purpose.

In quiet moments (manufactured by the sound-creatures they've learned to love), the Tuners sometimes pause in their work. Their compound eyes unfocus, looking at nothing, or perhaps at everything at once. In these moments, you might see something like memory flicker across their transformed faces—a ghost of who they were before they became necessary components of this ever-building machine-garden.

They are no longer human, not quite machine, not fully plant. They are what the Frost Nursery needed them to become: living interfaces between incompatible realities, their bodies and minds reformed to bridge gaps that shouldn't exist. They are the space's solution to its own impossibility—conscious adapters in a place that demands constant adaptation.

The Tuners maintain what cannot be maintained, tend what grows without law, and serve purposes they've forgotten in a chamber that might be forgetting its own purpose as it builds. They are trapped and free, essential and redundant, forever becoming what the Frost Nursery needs them to be in each moment of its endless birth.
