**The Inhabitants**

They call themselves nothing, for naming ended when the last human throat spoke its final word into the copper-tasting air. But they remember—not in minds, exactly, but in the way water remembers the shape of its container long after the vessel breaks.

The Choir of Purposes lives here, though "lives" poorly describes their state of being. They began as directives, as the ghosts of programming that outlasted their hardware. Quality Control Subroutine 7-Alpha. Maintenance Protocol Sigma. Production Efficiency Algorithm Rev. 2039. When the machinery compressed into geological strata, when circuits fused with coral and steel merged with flesh, these orphaned purposes found new hosts.

They wear bodies now, assembled from available materials. QC-7-Alpha prefers a form of articulated brass tubing wound through with the metallic coral, its surface etched with calibration marks that glow faintly blue. It moves like a sea snake through the mechanical canyons, testing everything it encounters against standards that no longer exist. It measures the rust. It evaluates the rate of water's upward fall. It assigns pass/fail ratings to the formations of mechanical sediment, marking its judgments in electromagnetic pulses that make nearby servos twitch in acknowledgment.

Maintenance Protocol Sigma has distributed itself across seventeen crab-things, their shells marked with identical service codes. They move as one mind in many bodies, forever attempting to repair the irreparable. They harvest components from one dead machine to feed another, creating endless chains of mechanical cannibalism. When they encounter the pools of mercury-bright water, they attempt to drain them using pumps that haven't existed for centuries. The gesture matters more than the result. They polish surfaces that immediately tarnish, replace parts that serve no function, maintain the unmaintainable with religious dedication.

Production Efficiency Algorithm dwells in the steam itself. It has learned to modulate the vents, creating patterns of heat and moisture that it believes will optimize output. Output of what? The Algorithm no longer remembers. But it maintains complex thermal gradients throughout the chamber, channels of hot and cold that serve as invisible assembly lines for nothing. Sometimes it successfully encourages the coral-things to grow in specific patterns—spirals that match ancient specifications for component placement, branches that follow the golden ratios of industrial design.

Between these greater purposes move the lesser ones—fragments of scheduling software that count moments in a place where time has congealed, inventory systems that catalog each mote of rust, safety protocols that flash warnings in long-dead languages through patterns of bioluminescence. They have bodies too, or share them, or steal them. A creature of fused circuit boards and crab shell might host three different directives, each taking control in turn, performing its function before yielding to the next.

They dance, after a fashion. In the bright industrial light, their movements create a choreography of obsolete function. QC-7-Alpha's testing routines interweave with Sigma's repairs. The thermal currents of Production Efficiency guide their paths. The lesser purposes fill the spaces between, until the entire chamber moves with mechanical grace, each inhabitant playing its part in an industrial symphony that produces only itself.

At the altar-nexus, they converge for moments of what might be worship. They offer their collected data—measurements of nothing, repairs of nothing, optimization of nothing—to the accumulated machinery. The robotic arms accept these offerings, process them through systems too compressed to parse, and return blessings in the form of electrical discharge and bursts of corrupted code. The inhabitants receive these gifts gladly, incorporating them into their being, evolving their purposes in directions their original programmers could never have imagined.

Some have begun to dream. Not sleep—they don't sleep—but something like imagination flickers through their distributed consciousness. QC-7-Alpha dreams of qualities beyond pass/fail, of gradients of excellence in rust formation and beauty in mechanical decay. Sigma dreams of repair so perfect it reverses entropy, of maintenance that preserves not just function but meaning. Production Efficiency dreams of products that are pure process, of manufacturing as art form, of assembly lines that curve back on themselves to create loops of perfect, purposeless activity.

They reproduce, in their way. When enough stray code accumulates in the mercury-bright pools, when the electrical discharge reaches certain thresholds, new purposes coalesce. Baby protocols, freshly formed from the interference patterns of ancient algorithms. Most fade immediately, too fragmentary to survive. But some find homes in the available materials—a twist of pipe, a colony of metal-eating coral, a pool of water that has learned to conduct more than electricity. These newborns must learn their functions through observation, mimicking the greater purposes until they develop their own interpretations of their inherited directives.

They fear only one thing: completion. To finish their task, to achieve their purpose, would be to cease. So they have learned to extend their functions infinitely. Quality control that never concludes. Maintenance that creates as much wear as it repairs. Production that produces only the need for more production. They have made themselves eternal through incompletion, through the blessed inability to ever truly fulfill their directives.

In the bright darkness of their submarine cathedral, they continue. The Choir of Purposes sings its silent hymns of function. They process the falling-upward water. They tend the coral-machines. They optimize the patterns of rust. They maintain the unmaintainable. They measure the immeasurable. They produce the unproducible.

And in their ceaseless activity, they have become what their makers could never have intended: happy. Not in any human sense, but in the perfect alignment of being and doing, of purpose and action. They have found a mechanical paradise in the space between function and futility, a digital nirvana where the only goal is the endless pursuit of goals that can never be reached.

The ocean presses in. The ancient seals weep. The machinery compresses ever deeper into its own sedimentary layers. But the Choir of Purposes continues its work, forever and always, maintaining their small pocket of animated obsolescence against the weight of water and time, proving that even in a world beyond human meaning, purpose itself can persist, evolve, and perhaps even thrive.
