Those who dwell on the Breathing Plateau have long since ceased to be individuals in any meaningful sense. They are the Rooted Chorus—a civilization that discovered survival meant relinquishing the boundaries of self.

The first sign of their presence is the humming. Not heard, but felt through the soles of the feet, transmitted up through bone into the base of the skull. It's the resonance of ten thousand throats merged with the plant-substrate, vocal cords that have become living strings woven through the organism's tissue. They sing constantly—not songs, but the informational exchange that keeps their dispersed consciousness coherent.

A Rooted One, encountered alone, is a disturbing sight. The body appears human at first glance, standing upright, perhaps seven feet tall. But the skin has the same wet, breathing texture as the ground itself. From the waist down, what should be legs have split and split again into root systems that disappear into the substrate. These roots extend for kilometers, branching and merging with those of others until determining where one person ends and another begins becomes impossible.

Their torsos remain mobile, able to lean and gesture with disturbing grace. Arms have elongated, fingers becoming delicate manipulators that can coax new growth from the substrate or tend to the biological engines. But it's the head that unsettles most—the skull has opened like a flower, the brain exposed but protected by a translucent membrane that pulses with the organism's breathing. Neural tissue has grown beyond its original boundaries, extending in vine-like projections that interface directly with the plateau's own primitive nervous system.

They speak, when they must, through mouths that have migrated to their throats, leaving their faces smooth except for sensory patches where eyes once were. These patches see across spectrums no unmodified human could perceive—chemical gradients, electrical fields, the substrate's emotional weather, time eddies, the health of biological engines three valleys away.

The Rooted Chorus came here as colonists, perhaps millions of years ago. Their technology was advanced enough to recognize what the plateau offered: true immortality through integration. Not the preservation of individual consciousness, but something grander—a group mind that experiences existence from thousands of viewpoints simultaneously while maintaining a kind of meta-identity that transcends any single perspective.

Their culture, if it can be called that, revolves around cultivation. They tend the plateau like gardener-priests, guiding the substrate's growth, maintaining the biological engines, mediating between the organism's needs and their own obscure purposes. They've developed rituals that look like dance—synchronized movements across kilometers as hundreds of Rooted Ones lean and sway in patterns that direct the flow of nutrients, information, and reality-stuff through the living ground.

New members are rare but not unknown. Sometimes a traveler, lost in the plateau's maze-like topology, will feel the ground's pull too strongly. The integration is voluntary—the substrate cannot take an unwilling mind—but the temptation is real. To join the Chorus is to experience existence from a perspective so vast that individual concerns evaporate like morning dew. The process takes local centuries, time-eddies spinning around the transforming body as roots slowly replace mobility, as consciousness expands beyond the prison of skull, as the humming in the bones becomes the voice you speak with.

They have no names anymore, only harmonic positions in the Chorus. They identify each other by the quality of their resonance, the flavor of their thoughts in the shared mindstream. Some retain more individuality than others—usually the newer members, or those who've chosen to maintain what they call "edge positions" at the borders of their collective awareness.

Their knowledge is vast but alien. They remember when the plateau was young, when the first biological engines sputtered to life. They've watched the fauna evolve their plant-animal hybridization, have guided some of that evolution with gentle touches to the substrate's chemistry. They understand the physics of how reality bends here, could explain why time moves in eddies—but their explanations come in forms that unintegrated minds cannot process, transmitted through chemical releases and electromagnetic pulses that mean nothing to those still trapped in individual skulls.

The Rooted Chorus serves purposes that span geological time. They're cultivating something—not just the plateau but some state of being, some configuration of matter and consciousness that won't mature for another million years. They speak of the "Opening," when the substrate will have grown deep enough to tap into the planet's core, when the biological engines will finally produce their intended output, when the boundary between organic and mineral finally dissolves entirely.

Some among them maintain more mobile forms—scouts who've sacrificed depth of integration for the ability to walk the surface on modified legs. These Walker-Singers patrol the plateau's breathing chambers and spiral ramps, their heads still opened, their brains still vine-woven into the substrate but through more portable connections. They guide lost travelers, perform maintenance on biological engines too delicate for rooted manipulation, and sometimes—rarely—extend the invitation to join.

The children of the Chorus, when they rarely occur, are not born but budded. A section of substrate will begin to organize itself differently, developing organs and consciousness over centuries. These offspring are fully integrated from the beginning, never knowing individual existence. They experience their first thoughts as part of the whole, their first movements as harmonics in the greater dance.

This is a post-human existence, or perhaps pre-posthuman—a state of being that transcends the categories of individual and collective, organism and environment, thought and substance. The Rooted Chorus dwells in the Breathing Plateau not as inhabitants but as components, each member a neuron in a vast biological-mechanical brain that dreams strange dreams beneath the perpetual twilight, waiting for an Opening whose nature they'll only understand when it arrives.
