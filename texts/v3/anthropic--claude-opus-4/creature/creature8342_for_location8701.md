The Meniscus Merchants move through the Cistern like mercury finding its level. They are not so much creatures as they are organized tensions in the water itself—places where the luminous moisture chooses to thicken and flow upward against gravity, forming traveling depressions in the omnipresent film of wetness that coats every surface.

You know them first by the darkness that precedes them: patches where the phosphorescence dims as they draw the glowing water up into their mass. They stand perhaps four feet in concentrated height, though their boundaries shift constantly. Their bodies are columns of living surface tension, water that has learned to walk by creating successive hollows in the street's wet coating and flowing forward into them. They leave behind trails of momentary dryness—dark footprints on the obsidian that last only seconds before the eternal seepage reclaims them.

They harvest the light itself. Where the phosphorescence pools deepest, you'll find them congregated, drawing up long skeins of luminous water through what might charitably be called appendages—extensions of their surface that stretch impossibly thin to sip from the glowing wells. They process this brightness somehow, concentrating it within specialized vacuoles in their liquid mass until they themselves become lanterns of captured radiance. The light moves through them in slow circulation, following helical patterns that suggest internal structure without revealing it.

Their commerce occurs at the impossible angles where the architecture defies comprehension. In doorways that open onto solid stone, they compress themselves flat and slip through cracks that shouldn't exist. They navigate the recursive windows by dividing—sending part of their mass through one pane while the rest continues along the street, only to reunite three blocks away where the window's reflection opens onto physical space again. They know the true topology of this place, the secret continuities that make sense of its contradictions.

They communicate through ripples—precise patterns of waves that propagate through the water film faster than sound could travel in this muffled space. A conversation looks like intersecting circles on a pond, but happening across walls and ceilings, the interference patterns encoding meanings in a language of constructive and destructive wave interaction. When they argue, you can see it in the way the water beads and scatters, droplets jumping from surfaces as conflicting frequencies meet.

What they trade remains opaque. They'll spend hours at certain intersections, exchanging globules of concentrated phosphorescence, or sometimes darker substances—anti-light that seems to eat the glow around it. They pass these treasures through each other's bodies, the exchanges happening inside their liquid mass where no outside observer can follow. The only evidence of transaction is the way their internal circulation patterns change afterward, taking on new rhythms and geometries.

They may have built this city, or grown it through some process of directed accretion. Their movements suggest ownership—the casual way they deform to pass through spaces too small for their volume, the practiced efficiency with which they navigate the non-Euclidean architecture. Or perhaps they're merely the Cistern's maintenance system, ensuring the eternal circulation continues, that the light neither dims nor brightens, that the water maintains its perfect, perpetual flow.

Sometimes, in the deepest wells where the phosphorescence grows almost too bright to perceive, you can see them engaged in what might be worship or might be feeding. They'll form perfect circles around these radiant pools, their bodies synchronized in slow undulation, drawing up spirals of light that they pass from one to another in careful sequence. The ritual—if ritual it is—can last for days, until the well's brightness has been distributed evenly among them, each merchant glowing with their precise share of hoarded illumination.

They pay no attention to anything that isn't water, isn't light, isn't part of the Cistern's eternal circulation. You could stand among them—if you could tolerate the constant moisture, the pressing closeness of the architecture—and they would simply flow around you, incorporating your presence into their paths the way water works around any obstacle. You are, to them, just another surface to coat, another channel for the endless flow of luminous moisture that defines their world.

In this way they continue: neither alive nor dead, neither separate beings nor aspects of a single dispersed entity, trading in brightness and darkness through a city that exists outside of time, their commercial ventures as eternal and meaningless as the phosphorescent water that grants them form.
