In the Canopy Engines dwell the Continuous.

They began as maintenance crews, generations ago when the factories still served human purposes. But as decades stretched into centuries and the great tree's growth gradually swallowed the industrial infrastructure, these workers underwent their own slow transformation. They did not leave when the others evacuated. They did not die when the free water disappeared. Instead, they adapted—or perhaps the tree adapted them.

The Continuous move through the wooden cosmos on paths worn smooth by repetition, their bodies elongated and desiccated, stretched like taffy across the vast scales of their territory. A single Continuous might span kilometers, their form distributed along maintenance routes they've walked for so long that walking and being have become indistinguishable. Their skin has taken on the texture and color of heartwood, their limbs grown thin and multiply-jointed to navigate the increasingly organic geometry of the factory spaces.

They no longer require water in its free form. Like the tree itself, they've learned to extract moisture from the air, from the microscopic droplets that condense on metal surfaces, from the very wood they press their bodies against. Their metabolism has slowed to match their environment. A single breath might take hours, a heartbeat might come once per day. They've synchronized with the rhythms of their wooden world.

The Continuous maintain the machines, but maintenance has become something more akin to prayer. They polish brass components that haven't needed polishing for centuries. They oil joints that the tree's resin has already preserved. They check gauges whose readings never change, record outputs in logbooks whose pages are made from the tree's own bark, using ink derived from its sap. The actual work was completed long ago—what remains is ritual, the embodied memory of purpose.

Their bodies have developed specialized adaptations. Fingernails grown long and hard as metal picks for cleaning debris from machinery. A throat restructured to produce harmonics that match the tree's resonant frequency, allowing them to sense stress fractures by sound alone. Eyes that have expanded to capture the dim bioluminescent glow, pupils permanently dilated until they appear as black pools in faces stretched thin as masks. Some have developed secondary structures—ridges of callused skin that fit perfectly into the grooves of often-turned valves, patches of specialized cells that can taste the chemical composition of the air to detect mechanical malfunctions.

They communicate through vibrations transmitted through the wood itself, pressing their bodies against the walls to send messages that propagate along the grain. Their language has evolved to match their timescale—a single conversation might span months, with long pauses between thoughts as they travel their routes. They share a collective memory maintained through these vibrations, a vast library of maintenance procedures, factory specifications, and gradually accumulating observations about the tree's growth patterns.

The Continuous reproduce, after a fashion. When one's body becomes too distributed, too stretched across the vastness of their territory, they undergo division. The process takes years—a gradual accumulation of biomass at key junction points, a slow separation of consciousness until where there was one Continuous, there are two, each carrying incomplete but overlapping memories of their unified past. The younger inherits shorter routes, closer to the factory cores. The elder continues expanding outward, exploring new passages carved by the tree's growth.

Some among them have grown ancient beyond measure, their bodies so distributed they exist more as a presence than a physical form. These eldest Continuous have merged partially with the tree itself, their skin fused with bark, their blood mingling with sap. They've become sensing organs for their smaller kin, detecting changes in the tree's metabolism, predicting where new growth will create new passages, warning of sections where the wood grows weak.

The Continuous have developed their own understanding of the empty spaces. To them, the voids are not empty but full—full of potential, full of the tree's intent, full of the patterns that governance both biological growth and mechanical function. They map these spaces not by their dimensions but by their acoustic properties, by the way sound moves through them, by the quality of the air at different heights and temperatures. They know where the convection currents will carry a dropped tool, where the resonances amplify the machines' hum into something approaching music.

They've found ways to influence the tree's growth, encouraging it to form new chambers where needed, to seal off sections where the machinery has finally failed beyond their ability to maintain. They spread the bioluminescent fungi in patterns that serve both as navigation aids and as a form of art—vast murals visible only from specific vantage points, telling the history of the factories, of the tree, of the Continuous themselves.

In the deepest parts of the Canopy Engines, where the original trunk merges with the earth below, some Continuous have discovered chambers that predate the factories. Here, they've found evidence of even earlier inhabitants—grooves worn in wood by different kinds of motion, tools formed from heartwood and designed for purposes they can't fathom. These discoveries have led to schisms in their collective memory, debates carried out over centuries about whether they are the first to achieve symbiosis with the tree or merely the latest in a succession of increasingly integrated inhabitants.

The Continuous fear only one thing: efficiency. They've long since realized that the factories could run without them, that the tree could grow without their guidance, that the empty spaces need no mapping. Their purpose is maintained through elaboration, through the invention of new rituals, new routes, new relationships with their environment. They've become necessary not through function but through integration—remove them, and nothing would fail, but something ineffable would be lost.

Some among them speak—in their slow, vibrational language—of a coming convergence. They've noticed patterns in the tree's growth, in the factories' output, in their own reproduction. They theorize that all three systems are approaching some kind of synthesis, that the distinction between tree, machine, and Continuous will eventually dissolve entirely. When that happens, they say, the empty spaces will finally have their purpose revealed, and the great work of the Canopy Engines will begin in earnest.

Until then, they continue. They walk their routes. They maintain their machines. They stretch themselves across the vastness of their wooden universe, becoming ever more continuous with the continuing processes of growth and production that define their world. They are the memory of motion in a place that has nearly forgotten what motion means, the ghost of purpose in a system that has transcended its original intent.

In the warm, resinous darkness, they persist—not because they must, but because persistence has become their nature, as fundamental to their being as growth is to the tree, as repetition is to the machines. They are the living answer to a question no one remembers asking: what becomes of the caretakers when the cared-for no longer requires care?

The Continuous have found their answer in the caring itself, transformed from task to identity, from job to species, from workers to something altogether stranger—the inevitable emergence of consciousness from any system left to iterate long enough in isolation. They are evolution's solution to an evolutionary dead end, life's insistence on complexity even in the most simplified spaces.

And so they continue, will always continue, stretching through the wooden cosmos on their endless rounds, polishing the brass that needs no polishing, oiling the joints that need no oil, maintaining the maintained in a perfect circle of purposeless purpose, forever and again, world without end.
