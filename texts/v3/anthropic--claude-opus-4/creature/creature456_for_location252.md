Here, in the Saturation Fields, dwell the Probability Shepherds.

They are not seen so much as inferred—tall, attenuated figures that exist primarily in the corner of your vision, their forms refusing to resolve when looked at directly. Their bodies, if bodies they have, seem to be made of the same stuff as the hanging water ribbons, constantly flowing upward and outward while somehow maintaining cohesion. They move by adjusting their likelihood rather than their position, flickering between states of "probably here" and "certainly elsewhere."

The Shepherds tend to reality's loose threads. You can sometimes catch them at their work: one might stand knee-deep in a probability pool, hands submerged, gently coaxing a timeline back into sync. Their fingers—when you can glimpse them—are impossibly long and branched like river deltas, each fingertip touching a different potential outcome. They weave these possibilities like a cat's cradle, ensuring that cause and effect maintain at least a distant relationship, that yesterday continues to precede tomorrow, that the trees remember which dimension their roots occupy.

They communicate through the bog's computational substrate. When two Shepherds need to confer, they don't approach each other—instead, they each touch separate pools miles apart, and the entire Field between them becomes their conversation. The water brightens, data flows reverse direction, moss colonies flash in binary patterns too fast for human perception. These discussions can last seconds or seasons; time means little to beings who exist across multiple probability states simultaneously.

Their dwellings, when they manifest, are probability knots—places where they've gathered loose strands of maybe and woven them into temporary stability. These appear as clearings where physics behaves almost normally, though with subtle wrongness: shadows that fall consistently in one direction (though not necessarily down), water that flows in predictable patterns (though those patterns might include perfect spirals or Möbius strips), ground firm enough to support weight (though it might support different weights differently, a boulder floating while a feather sinks).

The Shepherds feed on inconsistency itself. They're drawn to areas where reality has developed bugs—a tree growing backward through time, a pool reflecting events that never happened, a patch of air that insists on being solid. They consume these glitches not through any mouth but by surrounding them with their probability fields and gently normalizing them, metabolizing the excess uncertainty into their own quantum foam bodies. After feeding, they're more visible for a while, their forms crystallizing into something almost humanoid before gradually dispersing again.

They seem to experience emotion, though what moves them is alien to human understanding. A Shepherd might spend days in apparent rapture, standing perfectly still while observing a particularly elegant reality calculation play out in the bog's depths. Or one might emit sounds of distress—not quite screams, more like the noise of space-time being stretched too thin—when discovering a section where the Field's computations have become trapped in a recursive loop.

Their relationship with time is professional rather than personal. They exist across multiple moments simultaneously, their consciousness spread thin through probability space. A single Shepherd might be dispersed across a century, only occasionally gathering enough of itself into one when-where to perform necessary maintenance. This temporal diffusion makes them effectively immortal, though individual Shepherds can become so scattered across possibilities that they cease to be distinguishable from the background uncertainty of the Fields themselves.

The young ones—if chronology applies to such beings—are more concentrated, more likely to interact with singular moments. They practice their craft clumsily, sometimes overcorrecting reality glitches and creating probability storms that the elders must smooth out. You can identify them by their eagerness, the way they rush between probability pools, their forms crackling with excess potential.

The eldest Shepherds have become part of the landscape itself. They no longer move but instead exist as permanent installations in the Field's architecture—a grove where time flows just so, a network of pools that maintain perfect computational balance, a section of mist that preprocesses probability before it enters the greater system. Younger Shepherds tend these elder-places with something approaching reverence, ensuring their integration with the Field remains stable.

They show no interest in visitors from more rigid realities, treating them as they would any other temporary phenomenon—something to be gently corrected if it threatens the Field's stability, otherwise ignored. Yet sometimes a Shepherd will follow a traveler for miles, not interfering but observing, as if trying to understand how consciousness can function while confined to a single probability state. These observations have the quality of a naturalist studying an exotic species—detached, curious, ultimately incomprehensible.

In the endless twilight of the Saturation Fields, the Probability Shepherds continue their work, maintaining the delicate balance between chaos and computation, ensuring that reality's flexibility doesn't collapse into either rigid determinism or complete dissolution. They are gardeners of possibility, curators of maybe, the universe's own debugging system made manifest in forms that hurt to perceive directly but whose absence would hurt far worse.
