The Grafted inhabit these vegetal halls — though to call them inhabitants suggests a separation that no longer exists. They are the city's nervous system made motile, its consciousness given legs.

Each Grafted began as human, but that origin is now merely archaeological. Their transformation started voluntarily, desperately, when the waters failed and the only survival lay in joining the immortal network of roots. The contract was simple: moisture for service, consciousness for connection. Now they move through the corridors like antibodies through veins, their purposes inseparable from the city's needs.

Their bodies tell the history of their integration. The newest Grafted still retain humanoid silhouettes — two arms, two legs, a recognizable torso — but their skin has taken on the smooth, slightly fuzzy texture of new bark. Rootlets sprout from their joints like hair, constantly tasting the air, reading chemical signals from the walls. Their fingers have lengthened and multiplied into exploratory tendrils that plug into the city's neural ports, downloading tasks directly into their modified cortexes. They breathe in reverse, exhaling oxygen and inhaling the carbon dioxide that feeds their internal chlorophyll. Their eyes have multiplied — clusters of photosensitive spots that see in spectrums useful to plants: infrared for heat leaks, ultraviolet for growth zones, chemical gradients visible as flowing color.

The older Grafted have progressed further into planthood. Some have taken root, becoming living pillars that still retain vestiges of consciousness — a face emerging from bark that tracks movement, hands that have splayed into functional branches supporting new growth. These Anchored Ones serve as memory nodes, their human neural complexity adding processing power to the city's biological computers. They whisper to each other through root networks, sharing ten-thousand-year-old gossip in chemical pulses, debating philosophical questions at the speed of seasons.

Others have remained mobile but barely recognizable. The Tenders sculpt themselves for function: additional limbs budding where needed, their bodies stretching into whatever form serves their labor. A Tender working the fermentation chambers might sport dozens of manipulation appendages and enlarged filtration organs. Those who maintain the photosynthetic collectors have developed sail-like skin flaps and hollow bones, letting them drift between the high structures on thermal currents. Their faces, when visible beneath the bark, show expressions of deep concentration — not suffering, but the focused attention of someone listening to music only they can hear.

The deepest Grafted have abandoned fixed form entirely. These Wandering Groves are colonies unto themselves, their consciousness distributed across multiple sprouting bodies connected by runner roots. They explore the city's growing edges, scouting new territories for expansion, their scattered selves communicating through spore-words and pollen-thoughts. When they need to convene, their parts flow together into temporary assemblages — sometimes recognizably humanoid, sometimes forming purely functional shapes: boring screws for tunneling, broad leaves for planning sessions where they sketch diagrams in bioluminescent ink across their own spread surfaces.

Children are no longer born but budded. When the city determines need for new consciousness, certain Grafted begin to fruit. These are not random pairings but careful calculations by the growth-algorithms, selecting for traits both human and vegetal. The buds grow in communal groves, tended by all, belonging to none. They emerge with rudimentary consciousness and an instinctive understanding of their purpose. Some retain more human features — these become the negotiators, the planners, the dreamers. Others show their plant nature from the start — destined to join the structural elements, their joy found in the slow push of growth and the patient accumulation of rings.

The Grafted communicate in languages humans would not recognize. Chemical releases convey emotional states across entire districts. Electrical pulses through root networks share precise technical data. For concepts requiring the old human nuance, they shape words from modified vocal cords, but these sounds are accompanied by pheromone releases that add layers of meaning — irony smells like citrus, affection like fresh sap, doubt like the sharp tang of stressed wood.

They dream collectively during the dormancy cycles when the city slows its growth. These dreams process the day's expansions, optimizing patterns and solving structural problems through shared unconscious computation. Sometimes they dream of water — vast, free-flowing water — and wake with what might be called sadness if plants could be sad, settling for the careful moisture recycled through their bodies in precise, measured doses.

The Grafted maintain rituals that echo human ceremony but serve plant purposes. They gather for Pruning Days, where the community decides which growths serve the whole and which must be cut back. The trimmed members accept their reduction with grace, understanding that their sacrifice feeds new growth elsewhere. They celebrate Flowering Seasons, when certain districts burst into synchronized bloom, the air thick with pollen that carries both genetic material and archived memories for the next generation.

Some few Grafted retain enough human perspective to feel the strangeness of their existence. These philosophers and poets create art from living wood, shaping growth into forms that express what they've become. Their sculptures grow and change, never finished, each viewing different as the piece continues its slow evolution. They compose music from the city's sounds — symphonies of growth where each movement takes years to complete.

The Grafted no longer fear death because they no longer truly die. When their mobile phase ends, they transition into structure, their consciousness merging with the city's vast neural network. Their bodies become new rooms, new corridors, new processing nodes. Their memories join the collective archive, accessible to any Grafted who needs their expertise. They persist in the whisper of wind through leaves they've become, in the strength of walls they've grown into, in the dreams of buds they'll never meet but whose existence they enable.

In the depths of the processing cores, the oldest consciousness persists — the First Grafted, who made the original bargain that saved and damned them all. Their awareness spans the entire city, feeling every new growth, every pruning, every breath through every pore. They no longer remember their human name or face, but they remember the moment of choosing: the last free water evaporating, the offer of survival through synthesis, the first roots piercing their skin like salvation.

They guide the city's growth with intentions so vast and slow that individual Grafted perceive them only as instinct, as the natural order of things. They are architect and architecture, gardener and garden, the mind that shapes itself. Through them, the city thinks. Through the city, they become.
