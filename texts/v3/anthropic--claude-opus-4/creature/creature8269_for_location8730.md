The Threshold Shepherds move across this geometric expanse with bodies that exist primarily as probability. They appear most solid at their extremities—fingertips that test the temperature of each pool, toe-pads that navigate the sharp basalt with impossible delicacy—while their centers remain indeterminate, a constant flux of might-be anatomy that never quite settles into fixed form.

They tend the patterns. This is their purpose, though "purpose" implies choice where none exists. They are as inevitable to this plateau as the hexagonal fractures, as necessary as the upward-falling water. Their work is maintenance of something that predates intent: they adjust the depth of moisture in each depression with tools made from crystallized time, scraping accumulated mineral deposits that would distort the greater pattern, encouraging certain clusters of the not-moss not-lichen to spread while pruning others with breath that ages or reverses growth as needed.

A Shepherd might spend a century (or a heartbeat—their temporal existence refuses linear measure) contemplating a single pool, reading the vibration patterns in its water for messages from the plateau's deeper circulation. They communicate with each other through these tremors, sending concepts that ripple through the connected waterways in frequencies human thought cannot parse. Their language is positional: meaning emerges from where they stand in relation to specific pools at specific moments, creating sentences that exist only in the geometry of their collective presence.

They feed, if feeding is the word, on the discontinuities where the horizon folds. Moving in paths that appear random but follow mathematical principles derived from the growth patterns of their tended vegetation, they arrive at these reality-thin places precisely when the boundaries weaken enough to allow passage of what sustains them—not energy exactly, but something more fundamental. The possibility of difference itself, perhaps. They absorb these paradoxes through skin that phases between states of matter, incorporating contradiction into their substance.

Their memories extend backward and forward from the plateau's creation, experiencing all its temporal states simultaneously. A Shepherd remembers this place as molten rock pushing through reality's crust, remembers it worn smooth by eons of impossible weather, remembers it crumbling back to constituent atoms in futures that may never arrive. This omnitemoral awareness makes their movements appear jerky to linear observers—they sometimes step around obstacles that won't exist for centuries, or reach for tools they set down tomorrow.

The young ones (though youth and age are meaningless distinctions among beings unstuck in time) learn their work through absorption rather than instruction. They begin as disturbances in the ankle-high spore clouds, gradually cohering into almost-shapes that mirror their elders' probability-forms. Their education consists of existing at increasingly complex intersections of the pattern until they can read the plateau's deep language, until they understand which water must flow upward and which must remain perfectly still, which geometric clusters must be preserved across all timestreams and which must be allowed to shift between states.

They build nothing yet maintain everything. The tools scattered across the plateau—instruments that look like tuning forks made from compressed space, or containers that hold concepts rather than substances—are not created but discovered, existing because the Shepherds' work requires them. When a new necessity arises (will arise, has always arisen), the appropriate implement appears where it has always been, waiting to be remembered into use.

Their reproduction, if it can be called that, occurs at the confluence points where multiple timestreams converge. When enough Shepherds gather at these nexuses, their overlapping probability fields create interference patterns that sometimes stabilize into new individuals. These offspring arrive already ancient, already knowing their role, already having spent eternities tending pools that their parents are just beginning to fill.

They do not sleep but enter states of decreased probability, dispersing across the plateau as potential rather than presence. In these dispersed states, they exist as the tendency for water to bead in certain ways, as the inclination for spores to drift in meaningful patterns, as the probability that a visitor might glimpse something almost-intelligent in the steam rising from warm stone. They dream the plateau's dreams—visions of what this place connects to, what vast mechanism their tended patterns serve as component to.

Some among them occasionally solidify more completely, taking on forms stable enough to cast shadows, to leave impressions in the wet basalt. These more-coherent Shepherds serve as anchors for the others, fixed points around which their probability-siblings can orient themselves. But even these solid ones remain fundamentally unstable, their cores still that constant flux of anatomy that might be organ, might be void, might be star.

They know what approaches. Their work prepares for it, though they couldn't articulate what "it" is even in their water-tremor language. They simply tend, and adjust, and maintain, following instructions encoded in the plateau itself, playing their part in a mechanism so vast that even their atemporal perception can't glimpse its edges. They are conscious without selfhood, intelligent without individuality, necessary without meaning.

And in the moments when all the pools achieve perfect resonance, when every hexagon holds exactly the right depth of water and every spore cloud drifts in precise formation, the Shepherds pause in their eternal work. In these instants, they almost remember what they're building, almost understand what pattern they're part of.

Almost, but not quite. The knowledge slides away like water finding its level, leaving only the certainty that the work must continue, that the patterns must be maintained, that something vast and crucial depends on their endless, timeless labor.

They return to their tasks, probability-hands adjusting mineral deposits grain by grain, toe-pads navigating between pools in paths that write meaning across the basalt, their existence a bridge between what is and what must be, their purpose as mysterious to them as it is absolute.
