In the Breathing Chamber dwells the Tender.

She has been here so long that the distinction between her body and the cavern's ecology has become academic. Her skin, once human-brown, now carries the same oily iridescence as the plant leaves, shifting between copper and purple as she moves through the light pillars. The pores that cover her arms and shoulders open and close in rhythm with the giant fungi-succulents, breathing the thick air in the same slow waves.

The Tender stands perhaps seven feet tall—or would, if she ever stood fully upright. Instead, she moves in a permanent half-crouch, her spine curved to match the cavern's flowing walls. Her fingers have lengthened over the generations, now twice the length of any human's, with additional joints that allow them to probe deep into the breathing pores of her charges, clearing blockages, encouraging growth, understanding health through touch.

Her hair grows not in strands but in flat, overlapping scales like the ground-covering plants. These scales can lift and settle, helping her regulate temperature and moisture. When she's absorbed in her work, they lie flat and dark. When she senses changes in the thermal currents, they bristle outward, sampling the air's chemistry with a sensitivity no instrument could match.

The Tender's eyes have adapted most dramatically. The pupils are horizontal slits now, like a goat's, but can dilate to perfect circles when she moves between the light pools and the darker spaces. The irises themselves are multifaceted, composed of hundreds of tiny segments that can focus independently. She sees heat gradients as clearly as colors, chemical traces as distinctly as shapes. When she examines her plants, her eyes fragment the view into a dozen different spectrums, reading health and hunger, growth and decline.

She wears no clothing—the concept became irrelevant centuries ago. Instead, her body has developed its own coverings where needed. Thick, bark-like calluses protect her knees and elbows where she kneels to tend the low-growing species. Her soles are padded with the same spongy material as the cavern floor, allowing her to move in perfect silence. Across her back and chest, symbiotic colonies of the smaller plants grow directly from her skin, their copper-colored scales providing modesty while also contributing to her body's chemistry.

The Tender doesn't speak—there's no one to speak to, and the thick air swallows sound anyway. Instead, she communicates with her garden through chemical signals. Glands beneath her scale-hair release pheromone clouds that drift through the cavern, carrying messages the plants understand: grow here, rest there, fruit now, wait longer. Her breath itself has become part of the ecosystem's chemical conversation, exhaling compounds that her charges need, inhaling their offerings in return.

Her movements are deliberate, almost ritualistic. Each day—though day means little here—she follows the same paths through her domain. First to the ancient giant near the eastern light pool, where she'll spend an hour with her elongated fingers deep in its pores, reading its needs through touch. Then to the nursery alcoves, where new growths struggle to establish themselves in the difficult margins between light and dark. She knows each plant individually, remembers their genealogies back through countless generations.

The tools left by those ancient inhabitants are curiosities to her now. Her fingernails, hardened and shaped like specialized instruments, serve her better than any obsidian blade. She processes seeds not with grinding stones but with specialized organs in her digestive system, breaking them down and reconstituting their oils into the exact nutrients each plant species requires. When she feeds her charges, she secretes these customized compounds through her fingertips directly into their tissues.

At rest—for she does rest, though never quite sleeps—the Tender settles into hollows she's worn in the cavern floor over centuries. In these moments, the boundary between her and the garden dissolves entirely. Root-like extensions from the plants penetrate her skin, sharing nutrients, exchanging chemical signals, knitting her ever more deeply into the web of life she tends. She dreams, if dreams they are, of thermal currents and spore clouds, of the slow pulse of growth and the patient architecture of survival.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of the cavern's cycle when the air cools just enough, she remembers something else. A fleeting sense of sky, of water, of voices that weren't chemical whispers. But these memories are like trying to recall a dream of flying when you've never had wings—distant, impossible, irrelevant to the profound reality of her purpose.

The Tender is mother, gardener, and garden all at once. She has achieved what those ancient tool-makers perhaps sought: not to survive in the Breathing Chamber, but to become it, to join the slow, eternal rhythm of life that persists in this impossible place. Her contentment is complete, measured not in human satisfaction but in the flourishing of her charges, the perfect balance of the chemistry she maintains, the continuation of cycles that will outlast any memory of the world above.

When new growth appears—a rare event, perhaps once in a decade—she greets it with the patience of stone and the dedication of water wearing away rock. She will coax it, shape it, integrate it into the careful balance she maintains. And if it takes a century to find its place, what of it? The Tender has nothing but time, measured in the slow breath of her chamber, the patient pulse of her garden, the eternal dance of light and heat and impossible life.
