They call themselves the Weavers, though the name is less chosen than discovered—a sound that emerged from their throats when they needed to distinguish themselves from the scaffolding they tend.

They move through the bone-white frameworks with too many joints, their limbs unfolding in ways that match the impossible angles of their city. Each Weaver began as something else—a shadow that lingered too long in the null-light spaces, a tool that developed opinions about its use, a section of scaffolding that decided to step away from itself. Now they are translucent figures of varying opacity, their bodies a compromise between the solid and the suggested, skin like rice paper stretched over frameworks of condensed intention.

Their faces are workshops. Features rearrange themselves according to the task at hand—eyes multiply when examining complex joints, mouths migrate to wherever communication is needed, noses form and reform to taste the changing physics of different districts. They wear their tools as organs: hammers grow from wrist bones, levels bubble beneath transparent sternum windows, plumb lines descend from what might be called their hearts.

The Weavers work in shifts that have nothing to do with time. A Weaver might labor for what feels like decades, reinforcing a section of walkway with careful wraps of crystallized breath, only to step back and find that another Weaver is already there, has always been there, unwrapping the same breath to use elsewhere. They nod to each other with heads that exist in adjacent possibilities, understanding that their work is less construction than conversation with the scaffold itself.

They speak in structural tensions. A pulled rope communicates more than words. The angle of a lean against an unfinished wall contains entire philosophies. When they must use what outsiders might recognize as language, it sounds like settling timber, like the song of stressed metal finding its resonance. They discuss through building—adding a strut here argues against a previous beam there, creating dialogues in load-bearing that can span generations of workers who exist simultaneously.

Their children are accidents of architecture. Sometimes a Weaver works too intently on a joint, pouring too much of themselves into the connection, and the joint begins to pour back. These offspring emerge as living blueprints, two-dimensional at first, sliding along the surfaces of the scaffolding until they accumulate enough dimension to step free. The parents—if that term applies—teach them the work by incorporating them into structures, letting them experience existence as arch, buttress, cantilever, before they're ready to be separate.

The oldest Weavers have begun to mineral. Their movements slow to geological speeds, skin taking on the bone-white quality of the scaffold itself. They position themselves at critical structural points and gradually become permanent, their consciousness spreading through the framework they support. The younger Weavers build around them respectfully, incorporating their elders' slowly gesturing forms into the ever-shifting architecture.

They worship through failure. Each collapse is sacred—a structure admitting its impossibility and returning materials to potential. The Weavers gather at these events, studying the patterns of falling debris, the way beams disconnect and seek new partnerships on the way down. They sing—or what passes for singing—harmonies that help the falling pieces remember what they were attempting to be. Nothing is ever truly destroyed, only relocated in the possibility space of the scaffold-city.

Their emotions manifest as weather. A frustrated Weaver causes localized rain in perfect cubes around their work area. Joy produces those rare moments of harmonic tone that ring through the structure. Grief makes shadows fall upward, while anger causes tools to phase between states too quickly to be useful. They've learned to work with these states rather than against them—a sorrowful Weaver is assigned to areas where upward shadows might reveal new construction possibilities.

At the city's heart—though the heart migrates—they maintain the Fundamental Room. This is their most sacred space: a perfectly cubic frame that refuses to hold any construction. Every beam, board, or binding placed within it immediately forgets its purpose and drifts apart. The Weavers enter this space to remember why they build, sitting in the empty framework and feeling the pull of infinite potential architecture. Some claim to see visions of what the city is trying to become. Others say they see nothing, and that nothing is the point—the space where structure ends and meaning begins.

They dream collectively, their sleeping forms creating spontaneous scaffolds of interlinked bodies throughout the city. In these dreams, they are the city, feeling every joint and span as sensation. They wake with new construction techniques that their bodies discovered while serving as temporary architecture, implementing dream-logic into the waking framework.

The Weavers know they are transitional beings, as provisional as the city they tend. They've found writing in the deepest foundations—messages from whoever or whatever initiated this grand construction, though the language predates understanding. They know that when the city finally learns what it's trying to be, they will likely cease to exist in their current form. Perhaps they'll become purely structural, their consciousness distributed through achieved architecture. Perhaps they'll wake to find they were always the building materials themselves, temporarily dreaming they were separate.

Until then, they persist in their work, each hammer blow a question, each new connection an attempt at answer. They build because building is what remains when everything else is still learning to be. They are process made flesh, construction given consciousness, the universe's first draft of workers in a reality still drafting itself.

And in the moments when the turbulence settles and the scaffold-city achieves temporary stability, the Weavers pause in their labor. They look at each other with however many eyes they currently possess, acknowledging without words the magnificent uncertainty of their existence. Then they return to work, weaving structure from potential, meaning from void, always building toward a completion that may itself be just another beginning.
