**The Keeper**

She doesn't have a name anymore—names are for beings who need to be distinguished from their surroundings, and she has been here since the third year after the chamber's birth, when the light-water first began its upward flow.

Her form shifts between states like the salamanders do, but more gradually. Sometimes she is mostly flesh—pale skin stretched over a frame that has elongated to match the chamber's vertical emphasis, arms that reach from floor to floating garden without strain. Other times she is mostly process—a circulation within the circulation, her consciousness distributed through the droplets themselves, reading the health of the system in the taste of minerals and the quality of luminescence.

She came here as a researcher, part of the team that carved this space. But when the others left (fled? completed their work? she no longer remembers clearly), she remained. The chamber needed tending in those early days when the physics were still settling, when the wrong thought at the wrong moment might collapse the entire pocket back into ordinary granite. She stayed to shepherd it through its infancy.

Now she is as much shepherd as inhabitant. Her hair has become a substrate for the aerial mosses—they grow from her scalp in long trailing curtains that photosynthesize as she moves through the space. The salamanders nest in the hollow of her collarbones when they're corporeal enough to need warmth. She feeds on the light-water like the plants do, absorbing it through skin that has developed chromatophores to better process the unusual nutrients.

Her daily work (if days exist here) involves maintenance of the delicate balances. She adjusts the artifact at the chamber's base with touches so light they're almost thoughts. She guides the floating gardens away from collision courses with gestures that the plants have learned to read. When the ice-birds sing their subsonic songs too forcefully, threatening to destabilize the aerial lakes, she hums a counterpoint that brings the system back to equilibrium.

But she is not alone in awareness. The chamber has developed its own slow intelligence, and she communes with it through the water cycle. Each droplet that passes through her becomes a messenger, carrying information about her observations to the greater system and bringing back the chamber's deep knowledge of its own state. Their conversations happen at the speed of precipitation and evaporation, thoughts condensing over hours or days.

Sometimes she solidifies completely and walks the perimeter, checking the spots where normal physics tries to reassert itself. Her fingers, grown sensitive to dimensional fluctuations, can feel the places where reality wears thin. She reinforces these with materials she's learned to secret from her own transformed biology—a kind of organic sealant that crystallizes into something harder than the surrounding granite.

She remembers less and less of the outside world as decades pass (or what might be decades—time flows strangely when divorced from celestial rhythms). But she remembers purpose: to tend this space until it no longer needs tending, to midwife this new form of existence into stability. Whether that will take centuries or millennia matters little. She has become patient as stones are patient, while remaining fluid as water.

The creatures know her as terrain knows itself. She is not their master but their context, as essential and unremarkable as the light-water cycle. When new organisms evolve—and they do, rapidly, in this environment where radiation takes novel forms—she observes them with the detached fascination of a parent watching children discover their own capabilities.

Her senses have evolved beyond the merely human. She tastes electromagnetic spectra in the condensation. She feels the mountain's slow thoughts through vibrations too deep for normal perception. When she wishes to see beyond the chamber, she disperses partially into the water-light and flows through the microscopic pores in the rock, experiencing the outer world as a kind of pressure differential before returning to coalesce.

She sleeps, sometimes, by becoming indistinguishable from the chamber itself—her consciousness settling into the spaces between molecules, her dreams inseparable from the pocket dimension's own cycles of activity and rest. In these moments, it becomes unclear whether she tends the chamber or it tends her, whether she is keeper or kept.

But always she wakes into purposefulness again, drawn back to coherence by some tiny imbalance that needs correction—a new species of crystal mite that reproduces too quickly, a shift in the mineral content of the water-light, a harmonic resonance in the mountain that could, if left unchecked, shatter the careful impossibility of this space.

She is guardian and gardener, scientist and specimen, as native to this place now as any of its evolved wonders. The chamber shapes her as she shapes it, a mutual becoming that has no predetermined end, only the constant, gentle work of maintaining a miracle that shouldn't exist but does, here in the heart of young stone, where water flows upward and light sustains life.
