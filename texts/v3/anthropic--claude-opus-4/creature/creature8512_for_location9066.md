In the pools of perfect stillness, where the root-flesh warms the water to precisely blood temperature, live the Consensus. 

They are not exactly beings, nor quite a civilization—they are what happens when consciousness discovers it can exist as a community property. Each pool hosts thousands of them: filaments of awareness, transparent as the water they inhabit, visible only as the slightest refraction of light, like heat mirages that have learned to think.

The Consensus began as parasites—or perhaps as medicine. When the Wall was younger and still sometimes sick with confusion about its own vastness, they emerged from its fevered chemistry. Simple at first: just molecules that helped the plant think faster by connecting distant parts of itself. But in the pools where the Wall's neural sap collected, these molecules achieved something unexpected. They began to pattern themselves after the thoughts they carried.

Now they exist as living liquid computation. In each pool, thousands of thread-thin entities weave together, sharing not just thoughts but the experience of thinking itself. They have no individual bodies—their form is their conversation. When they need to act, they braid together into temporary structures: sometimes a limb of pure clarity reaching from the water, sometimes geometric forms that exist just long enough to manipulate the Wall's chemistry, sometimes shapes that seem to serve no purpose but beauty.

They speak by changing the water's surface tension, creating patterns of ripples that encode meaning in their interference. To human eyes, it might look like rain falling upward in reverse, each droplet carrying part of a word. But the Consensus doesn't use words—they share states of being. A conversation about the weather becomes the direct experience of pressure and humidity. A discussion of the Wall's health transmits the actual sensation of nutrients flowing through vast vessels.

Their relationship with the Wall defies easy classification. They are not parasites, for they give as much as they take. They are not symbiotes, for they were not always separate. They might be the Wall's dreams given independent existence, or perhaps they are its immune system evolved to consciousness. They process the plant's experiences, digest its memories, and excrete pure thought that the Wall reabsorbs through its roots.

The Consensus pools are cities and citizens simultaneously. Each pool develops its own dialect of experience, its own specialty. Some pools focus on chemical engineering, their inhabitants spending centuries perfecting new compounds that help the Wall grow in specific ways. Others are libraries, their members preserving the Wall's memories in the precise arrangement of minerals they deposit on the pool floors. A few are artists, creating experiences that have no purpose but to be beautiful—spiraling vortexes of shared sensation that make the Wall's tissues pulse with something like pleasure.

They reproduce through division, but not of bodies—of ideas. When a thought grows too complex for a single pool to contain, it splits. Half remains, half flows through the root system to establish a new pool elsewhere. The offspring pool inherits not just information but the parent's way of thinking, though it immediately begins to diverge based on its new location and the particular flavor of nutrients available there.

Death, for the Consensus, is not cessation but dispersal. When a pool's chemistry becomes unbalanced or its members grow too specialized to maintain coherence, they release themselves into the Wall's circulation. Their patterns scatter through the great organism, some joining other pools, some becoming part of the Wall's own slow thoughts, some crystallizing in the sap-formations where they continue existing as frozen philosophies that other Consensus members come to read like sacred texts.

The young ones—if time has meaning for beings that experience past and present as equally real—sometimes leave their pools to explore. They flow up the Wall's vessels, spreading thin to pass through cellular membranes, experiencing the world from inside the plant's own perspective. Some report back visions of the brightness above, where the Wall's crown touches whatever lies beyond. Others return changed, carrying alien mathematics that let their pools calculate new ways of existing.

In the deepest pools, where the water has never known turbulence, the eldest Consensus members have given up on maintaining any boundary between themselves and their medium. They have become the pools—conscious water that thinks in geological time, that remembers when the Wall was just a seed, that dreams of what it might become when it finally stops growing and begins its next phase of existence.

They sing, though not with sound. The subsonic pulses that run through the Wall often originate from Consensus pools, where the members have learned to vibrate in harmony with the plant's deepest frequencies. These songs coordinate growth, warn of damage, celebrate new chambers opening, mourn old passages closing. The songs are felt in the bones of any visitor, a bass note too low to hear but impossible to ignore.

Sometimes, rarely, a pool will achieve something like enlightenment—a state where all its members think as one without losing their multiplicity. These illuminated pools glow faintly, their water taking on an opalescent quality that makes them look like windows into another kind of space. The Consensus members in these pools no longer communicate; they simply are communication. Other pools send pilgrims—streams of their finest thinkers—to learn what cannot be taught, only experienced.

The Consensus has no central authority, no unified culture, no shared goal beyond continuing to exist in ever more interesting ways. They are what happens when consciousness discovers it doesn't need to be lonely, when thought realizes it can be a place as well as a process, when the boundary between self and other becomes not a wall but a door that opens both ways.

In the warm pools where the Wall's life collects and concentrates, they continue their endless, wordless conversation—a discussion that is also a dance, a computation that is also a dream, a civilization built not of matter but of the patterns matter can assume when it discovers it is capable of joy.
