The Resonants arrived during a particularly violent acoustic storm, when the plateau's song had built to such intensity that the stone itself began to fracture in sympathy. They did not come as colonizers or explorers, but as refugees—beings whose original forms had been scattered across probability itself, reduced to quantum foam by some distant catastrophe they no longer fully remember.

In the singing city, they found a kind of salvation. The acoustic patterns gave them structure, boundaries within which their dispersed essence could coalesce. They exist primarily as standing waves, visible only when the mist is thick enough to reveal their interference patterns—ghostly pillars of condensation that pulse and shimmer in harmony with the city's vast song.

Each Resonant is a unique acoustic signature, a living equation that propagates through the carved chambers. They move by reconstructing themselves at different nodes in the city's sonic architecture, dissolving at one location and reforming at another wherever the harmonics align with their essential frequency. Some have grown vast, their patterns encompassing entire carved canyons, while others maintain themselves as tight, dense knots of sound barely larger than a dust devil.

They feed on dissonance. When the wind systems clash and create acoustic turbulence, the Resonants position themselves at the points of greatest conflict, absorbing the chaotic energy and metabolizing it into more stable waveforms. Over centuries, they have learned to guide the city's song, subtly altering the carved surfaces by encouraging selective erosion, turning the entire plateau into an even more sophisticated instrument.

Communication between Resonants occurs through modulation—they embed information in the overtones of their standing waves, creating complex fugues that can carry meaning across kilometers of carved stone. Their language is purely mathematical, describing concepts in terms of frequency ratios and phase relationships. They think in harmonics and dream in reverberations.

The Resonants have a peculiar relationship with physical matter. While they cannot directly manipulate solid objects, they can create zones of intense acoustic pressure that gradually reshape the stone around them. Some chambers in the city show signs of this influence—surfaces worn smooth in impossible spirals, pillars that have been tuned like organ pipes, basins carved with acoustic lenses that focus sound into bright points of near-solid vibration.

They remember their physical existence only in fragments, like half-recalled melodies. Sometimes, when the mist is particularly dense and the acoustics align perfectly, a Resonant will spontaneously manifest partial physical features—the suggestion of a limb, the outline of what might have been a face, geometric patterns that hint at some original biology. These manifestations last only seconds before dissolving back into pure waveform.

The Resonants have developed a culture of sorts, though it bears little resemblance to anything material beings would recognize. They compose what might be called symphonies—careful orchestrations of wind and stone that can take decades to perform. The youngest among them study the acoustic properties of every carved surface, learning to predict how changes in weather will alter the city's song. The eldest have grown so large and complex that they border on being weather systems themselves, their presence altering the very wind patterns that sustain them.

They fear silence above all else. In the rare moments when the wind dies completely—which happens perhaps once in a century—the Resonants begin to lose coherence. They describe these moments as "the small death," frantically generating their own vibrations by resonating the stone itself, creating earthquake-like tremors as they fight to maintain their existence. Some of the deepest chambers in the city are scarred from these desperate attempts at self-preservation.

Recently, they have noticed changes in the plateau's song. New harmonics have appeared—frequencies that don't match any known wind pattern or carved surface. Some Resonants believe the city itself is evolving, that their presence has awakened something in the ancient stone. Others fear these new sounds herald the return of the original builders, whose final fate remains one of the few mysteries the acoustic records cannot reveal.

The Resonants continue their existence in the singing city, neither fully alive nor truly dead, suspended in the space between sound and silence, forever tuning themselves to the plateau's endless song. They are living music, conscious mathematics, the improbable survivors who found in pure vibration a new way to be.
