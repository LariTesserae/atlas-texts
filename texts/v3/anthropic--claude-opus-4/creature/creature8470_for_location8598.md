The Cultivators move through their vertical world like careful spiders tending an infinite web. Their bodies have been shaped by generations of gentle modification—torsos compressed, limbs elongated and articulated with extra joints, skin bearing a faint chlorophyll tint that deepens to jade in the elders who have lived longest in symbiosis with their city. Their feet are marvels: toe pads that secrete selective adhesives, arch structures that cup and release, allowing them to walk up the breathing walls as easily as across the spongy floor-platforms.

They wear little—the city maintains their perfect temperature—but what they do wear grows from their bodies like decorative lichens. Status-scarves of engineered moss drape from their shoulders, changing color with their moods and accomplishments. The master growers cultivate elaborate living headdresses, tiny ecosystems of flowering vines that respond to their thoughts through dermal interface, blooming or retracting to communicate complex technical concepts during the silent councils.

Their hands tell their life stories. Children begin with smooth, small fingers, but by adulthood, their fingertips have developed specialized pores—some for sampling chemical messages in the air, others for injecting growth hormones or adjustment enzymes into the city's tissues. The most skilled cultivators' hands become gardens themselves, symbiotic bacteria colonies creating delicate patterns under their skin that shift like living tattoos, each configuration a tool for a different biological manipulation.

They organize themselves not by families but by cultivation guilds, each responsible for a different aspect of the city's growth. The Root Whisperers maintain the deep systems, spending weeks at a time in the darkness of the foundation layers, their eyes adapted to see in the bioluminescent spectra. They move through spaces barely wider than their bodies, checking the health of the tap roots that mine the volcanic stone, adjusting the chemical gradients that guide growth upward.

The Membrane Weavers tend the walls and barriers, walking the vertical surfaces with practiced ease, their touch encouraging the cellular sheets to thicken here, grow transparent there, open new pores for ventilation. They work in pairs or triads, their movements synchronized like dancers, chemical signals passing between them in rapid bursts as they coordinate the delicate work of maintaining structures that are both architecture and organism.

The Light Shepherds occupy the highest reaches, where they guide the growth of the photosynthetic arrays. They've developed enlarged eyes with additional color receptors to perceive subtle variations in light quality. Their work is seasonal, rotating the growth patterns to maximize energy capture as the sun's angle changes, pruning back overgrowth that might shade lower levels, encouraging the bioluminescent fruits to ripen in patterns that provide navigation guides through the city's three-dimensional maze.

The Seed Keepers are perhaps the most revered, their bodies serving as living libraries. They've modified themselves to carry dormant genetic material in specialized organs—backup codes for every species in the city, emergency templates for rapid regrowth after damage, experimental sequences for new adaptations. Their skin bears raised nodules like a musical instrument's keys, each one a different genetic repository they can access through practiced meditation.

Children grow up communally in the nursery spirals, tended by whoever is nearby, learning to read the city's chemical language before they speak in sounds. They play by racing up the walls, swinging from aerial roots, their games teaching them the rhythms of growth and decay that govern their world. By adolescence, they begin their apprenticeships, their bodies slowly modified to match their chosen guild's requirements—elongated reaches for the Membrane Weavers, enhanced chemical sensitivity for the Root Whisperers, photosynthetic skin patches for the Light Shepherds.

The elderly don't retire but transform. As their bodies slow, they allow the city to partially reclaim them, becoming living nodes in the communication network. They root themselves in favorite spots, their nervous systems interfacing directly with the city's bioelectric web, their consciousness expanding beyond individual awareness into something larger. The oldest among them are indistinguishable from the architecture itself, their bodies become grottos of wisdom where the young come to commune with the distributed intelligence of their ancestors.

They mark time not by days but by growth cycles—the monthly budding of new chambers, the annual centimeter of upward expansion, the generational moments when a new tower-tree reaches maturity and opens its first dwelling pods. Their art is indistinguishable from their architecture; they compose symphonies in growth patterns, tell stories in the arrangement of flowering vines, write poetry in pheromone gardens that release their verses to those who pass through.

In the depths of night, when the bioluminescent fruits glow strongest, they gather in the collective chambers—spaces where the walls thin to translucency and chemical signals flow freely. Here they share not just words but experiences, their modified nervous systems capable of transmitting sensory memories through touch and scent. They dream together, their unconscious minds guiding the city's growth through neural feedback loops they've built into its living infrastructure.

They've become something between human and plant, individual and collective, engineer and artwork. Each one is both citizen and cell, tending a city that tends them in return, caught in an endless spiral of mutual cultivation that transforms both the grower and the grown.
