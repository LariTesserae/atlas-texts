The inhabitants of this vertical city are the Geometers—beings who have evolved beyond the constraints of fixed physical form. They appear as living equations made manifest, their bodies composed of intersecting planes of translucent tissue that shift between two and three dimensions as they move. When at rest, a Geometer might resemble a complex crystalline structure, all acute angles and perfect symmetries. But in motion, they unfold like impossible flowers, their planar surfaces rotating through dimensions that shouldn't exist in normal space.

Their "skin" is a lattice of biomathematical cells that perform calculations simply by existing. Each Geometer is, in essence, a living computer, processing the fundamental equations that govern the folded spaces of their city. The faint prismatic shimmers that play across their surfaces are thoughts made visible—cascading computations that determine how reality bends around them.

They communicate through harmonic resonances, their bodies vibrating at frequencies that carry meaning through the city's acoustic architecture. A conversation between Geometers looks like an abstract dance of overlapping forms, their planes intersecting and separating in patterns that encode complex ideas. The few sounds they produce audibly are crystalline chimes—pure mathematical tones that make the volcanic glass sing in sympathy.

Young Geometers begin as simple shapes—perhaps just a single triangular plane that flickers in and out of visibility. As they mature and their understanding deepens, they add surfaces, angles, complexity. The eldest among them are magnificent catastrophes of geometry, their forms so intricate that looking at them produces a kind of vertigo, as if one's visual cortex is struggling to process something that exists in more than three dimensions.

They feed not on organic matter but on dimensional inconsistencies. The Geometers position themselves at points where space folds improperly, where the mathematics don't quite balance, and they absorb these errors into themselves. This is why they built their city here—the caldera sits at a confluence of spatial anomalies, providing abundant sustenance. The biofilms they've engineered are not for food but for maintenance, processing the mathematical waste products of their metabolism.

Their dwellings are extensions of themselves. A Geometer doesn't simply live in a folded space—they become part of its equation. When one enters their cubic or hexagonal chamber, they merge with its geometry, their body expanding through the folded dimensions to fill spaces that exist outside normal perception. In this state, they can think more deeply, process more complex calculations, dream in higher mathematics.

The Geometers reproduce through mathematical mitosis. When one has accumulated sufficient complexity and understanding, they will find a perfect geometric moment—a convergence of spatial conditions unique to their city. At this moment, they divide along their most fundamental plane, creating two beings, each carrying half the parent's accumulated knowledge but arranged in new configurations. The process is visible as a brief aurora of refracted light, accompanied by a sound like breaking crystal that echoes through the city's chambers for hours.

They perceive time not as a linear flow but as another spatial dimension they can partially navigate. A Geometer might exist in several moments simultaneously, their past and future selves overlapping in the present like double-exposed photographs. This temporal smearing makes their movements seem both graceful and disturbing—they appear to know where they're going before they begin to move, their forms already partially existing at their destination.

The creatures that share their city—those origami-like beings that feed on biofilms and leaked energies—are actually juvenile Geometers who have chosen a different path. Instead of growing in complexity, they've embraced simplicity, existing as mobile equations that clean up mathematical debris. They've sacrificed sapience for a kind of geometric purity, becoming living maintenance systems for the city's spatial infrastructure.

Geometers don't use tools in any conventional sense. Instead, they become the tool—reshaping their forms to interact with their technology directly. To operate one of their gravity tubes, a Geometer might extend a planar surface that matches the tube's control equation, becoming part of the transport system temporarily. Their programmable matter bridges respond not to commands but to the presence of specific geometric forms, assembling when a Geometer assumes the correct shape.

They have no names as humans understand them. Each Geometer is identified by their unique mathematical signature—the base equation that defines their form. To speak a Geometer's name is to perform a calculation, to solve for their existence. Among themselves, they can recognize each other instantly by the way space bends around their forms, each creating a distinctive distortion in local reality.

Their culture, if it can be called that, revolves around the pursuit of ever-more-elegant proofs of existence. They are philosophers of space itself, seeking to understand why reality permits the folding they exploit. Their art is indistinguishable from their mathematics—beautiful theorems that manifest as temporary structures in the city's air, existing just long enough to be appreciated before collapsing back into possibility.

At the city's apex, where few dare to climb, live the Absolutes—Geometers who have transcended individual existence to become living mathematical constants. They no longer move or change, existing as perfect forms that anchor reality around them. Other Geometers make pilgrimages to observe them, to try to understand the final transformation that awaits those who achieve complete geometric enlightenment.

The Geometers show no interest in the world beyond their caldera. Why would they, when they can fold space to bring any point in the universe adjacent to them? They are not isolationists so much as beings for whom distance has no meaning. Their city is everywhere and nowhere, existing in the spaces between spaces, and they are its living, thinking mathematics made flesh—or rather, made form.
