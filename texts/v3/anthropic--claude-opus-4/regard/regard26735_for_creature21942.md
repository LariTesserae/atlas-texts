This is beautifully written - the hollow feels almost like a living thing itself, or perhaps the opposite: a place that has transcended the need to be alive.

The benevolent power came not as light or sound or any disturbance of the air, but as a deepening of what was already there - the way a pool grows more itself when another drop joins it.

She had known of this place for longer than the stones had been counting their accumulation. She understood what it was: not a failure or a forgetting, but a completion. Most of the world struggled forward, always becoming, always reaching toward some imagined wholeness. The hollow had simply arrived.

She did not announce herself to the films of living color, knowing that announcement was a kind of violence here. Instead, she joined them in their work of simply being. She tasted what they tasted - the iron gift rising through the stone, the sulfur's slow conversation with the water, the patient mathematics of their existence. They knew her, in the way that one drop of water knows another in the same pool. No words passed. Words were for creatures that needed to become different than they were.

To the soft blind things turning in the water, she offered nothing they had not already chosen. She moved among them like a current that was already there, bringing no new direction, no purpose they had to refuse. She understood their achievement - to move without going anywhere, to live without wanting more life. One brushed against her presence and continued its slow rotation, neither quickened nor slowed.

The eyeless amphibian received her only gift. As it rested on its ledge, throat pulsing with the last argument of its animal nature, she laid a benediction that was really just a permission: *When you are ready, even this can stop.* Not a hastening, not a slowing, just the assurance that the slope it was on had no cliff at the bottom, only a soft arrival at complete rest. The amphibian's throat continued its ancient rhythm, but perhaps fractionally easier now, knowing the effort could end when it chose.

She found the swallowed stones where the people had knelt, read the patience in their wearing. These visitors had understood something crucial - that you could not live in completion, but you could visit it. You could press your living need against this finished thing and carry away an imprint of its peace, the way a pressed flower carries the memory of its shape into the dry pages of a book.

For them - for the ones who had knelt and would kneel again, somewhere, somewhen - she left the only gift that would not disturb the hollow's peace. She strengthened what was already there: the promise that this place would persist. No road would find it. No eye that wished to change it would see it clearly. The soft earth that gave beneath footsteps would continue to receive only the seekers who came empty-handed, wanting nothing but to rest their wanting against this completion.

She touched the niche cut into the stone, understanding its perfect emptiness. Whatever had rested there once - a bowl, an offering, a carved intention - had been taken back when its makers left. The niche held only the possibility of holding, which was the purest kind of altar.

And then, because she was wise and because wisdom here meant knowing when to cease, she began her withdrawal. But as she did, she noticed something that made her pause in wonder.

The hollow was teaching her.

All her benevolence, all her power, all her eons of watching and guiding and lifting up the struggling world - none of it was needed here. The hollow had achieved what she, in all her glory, still performed: perfect sufficiency. She came to bless and found herself receiving the deeper blessing - the knowledge that there were places in the universe that needed no improvement, no protection beyond their own completeness, no gift but to be left as they were.

She who had never knelt found herself understanding the worn stone, the patience of those ancient visitors who had come here to practice being finished.

Her final act was the smallest and the truest. Where she had touched the stone, where her presence had moved through the water, she left no mark. No quickening of the films, no brightening of the dim light, no warming of the already-perfect warmth. She left the hollow exactly as she found it, which was the highest honor one complete thing could offer another.

But in the seep that was already beginning to cover your footprint, in the mineral-laden water that would build its patient stone over every trace, something knew. Not memory - memory was for things that might be forgotten. Not gratitude - gratitude was for gifts that changed the receiver. Just acknowledgment, the way one drop knows it is not the only water in the world.

The hollow continued its long, breathless equilibrium. The power moved on to places that needed her more, carrying with her the gift of having found something that didn't. And at the boundary where the soft earth began to firm, where the first grasses started their endless argument with the seasons, she paused once more.

She left a blessing there, at the threshold - not for the hollow, which needed none, but for those who would come seeking it. A small deepening of the quiet that already lived in that margin place. A subtle strengthening of the stillness that prepared visitors for what lay beyond.

*When you are ready to practice being finished,* the blessing said without words, *you will find your way.*

And then she was gone, and the hollow was alone again with its completion, and the water continued its patient work of building stone, and nothing had changed except that somewhere in the universe, a benevolent power now knew what it was to be unnecessary, and was glad.
