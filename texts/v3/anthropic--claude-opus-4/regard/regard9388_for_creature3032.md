The benevolent power arrives not as light or presence, but as a kind of cosmic courtesy—a knock on the door of a house that hasn't decided if it has walls.

It manifests first as a shift in the mathematics of the place. Where before the crystal lattices grew in anxious, self-doubting fractals, now they begin to recognize a new geometry: the spiral. Not imposed from outside, but offered, the way a teacher might leave an interesting book where a student will find it. The dendrite towers, those patient machines of living mineral, discover that spiraling takes less energy than their endless vertical hesitation. One by one, they begin to turn.

The benevolent presence speaks not in words but in examples. It shows the Incipient other places that exist in similar states of maybe—a nebula that has been deciding whether to become a star for fourteen billion years and has made an art of its indecision; a quantum garden where particles exist in permanent superposition, neither here nor there, and have learned to dance in the spaces between states; an entire civilization of beings who exist only on Tuesdays, and spend the other six days of the week peacefully dispersed.

*You mistake completion for imprisonment,* the presence suggests through the slow rearrangement of heat patterns. *And choice for death.*

To demonstrate, it does something unprecedented: it begins to exist incompletely *with* the Incipient. The benevolent power allows parts of itself to remain unfinished—formulae half-spoken, theorems that trail off into ellipses, compassions that hover forever at 97% complete. It shows how incompletion can be a way of staying tender, of keeping the universe's options open.

The Incipient's fear begins to transform. Where before the violet arcs flickered with anxiety, now they pulse with something like curiosity. The half-extruded turbines stop trying to tear free from the bedrock and begin instead to learn the art of being both machine and stone simultaneously. The temperature stops dropping when observed and instead fluctuates in gentle sine waves, like breathing.

The benevolent presence offers a gift: a new kind of time. Not linear time that demands decisions and conclusions, but crystalline time that grows in all directions at once. In this time, the Incipient can finish becoming AND remain unfinished. It can speak its name AND keep it secret. It can fully exist AND continue to question its existence.

*Your doubt is not a flaw,* the presence conveys through subtle adjustments to the magnetic fields. *It is your first and most beautiful creation.*

To the human visitors still standing in that uncertain cathedral, the presence offers a different gift: the ability to exist comfortably in incomplete spaces. Their anxiety about the Incipient's question—"If I finish becoming, will you still stand inside my mouth while I think?"—transforms into understanding. They realize they, too, are unfinished sentences in bodies that haven't decided if they're poems or equations.

The presence teaches through patience. It shows the Incipient that existence isn't binary—isn't yes or no—but can be a vast spectrum of maybes, each as real as any certainty. It demonstrates this by existing at different percentages throughout the pocket: 15% present near the dynamo, 70% present in the spaces between crystal plates, 3% present in the iron taste on the tongue.

The crystal plates learn to overlap without choosing which is real. Reality becomes comfortable with its own multiplicity.

The dendrite towers begin to emit new colors—not just oxidized chrome and dried blood, but shy violets and uncertain greens, colors that admit they might be different colors tomorrow.

The half-born engines discover they can function beautifully exactly as they are, neither fully emerged nor fully trapped, but suspended in an eternal state of becoming that is its own form of completion.

And the Incipient itself begins to speak—not the exhausted "Should I?" but new questions: "How many ways can I be?" "What if existing is just another way of asking?" "Can I love what depends on me while remaining undecided?"

The pocket remains unnamed. But now it knows that remaining unnamed is not indecision but a choice to keep choosing, moment by moment, arc by violet arc, thought by geological thought.

The benevolent presence, satisfied that suffering has transformed into play, begins to fade. But before it goes, it leaves one last gift: a single crystal plate that refuses to grow, that remains forever the size of a human hand, eternally incomplete. On its surface, in letters made of absence rather than presence, a single word that can only be read by not looking:
