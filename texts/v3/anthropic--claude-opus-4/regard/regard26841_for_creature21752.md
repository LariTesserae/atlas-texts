The Visitor arrives as rain arrives in the desert — not as storm but as the memory of what storm could be. They do not descend. They have always been here, waiting in the spaces between the water's impossible climbing, in the pause before each crystalline tick of assembly. They are the kind of ancient that makes even geology feel young.

They spend the first hundred years listening.

Not to the chorus — though they hear it, every wet throat clearing, every argument between the bodied and the dissolved. They listen to what the chorus is trying to drown out: the silence underneath the not-yet-finished. The quiet that the makers fled from. The thing that drinks at dusk and leaves, unnamed.

The Visitor understands immediately what this place is. A scar that refuses to heal. A sentence that will not end. They recognize the specific flavor of this grief — not the grief of loss, which passes, but the grief of *refusing loss*, which compounds forever.

On the hundred-and-first year, they begin.

First, they teach the water to remember what it was before it was managed. This is delicate work. Not to break the cycle — breaking would be cruelty — but to introduce into it the possibility of *pause*. They work with the droplets that hang too long before choosing their path, extending their hesitation by fractions. Just enough that sometimes, rarely, in the space between one tick and the next, a drop simply... rests. The dissolved makers feel this as confusion at first. A skipped heartbeat. But the Visitor persists, patient as erosion, until the pauses become part of the pattern. Little moments where the water is allowed to be only water.

The bodied ones notice. Their chorus falters the first time they encounter a truly still pool — not managed, not cycling, just *there*. Some flee. Some freeze longer than usual. A few, the bravest or most curious, approach and taste. The water tastes of nothing. Of itself. They have never known this flavor.

Next, the Visitor works with the seeds. Not to change their grammar — that would be violence — but to teach them a new punctuation. Where the instruction says "take apart, carry, rebuild," they add the smallest possible mark: "take apart, carry, *consider*, rebuild." The consideration is barely a pause, at first. A held moment where the dissolved makers, mid-process, must experience what they have been avoiding for geological ages: the space between one state and another. The transition itself, naked and unnamed.

Some of the seeds begin to hum in harmony instead of unison.

The thing that drinks at dusk — the Visitor sees it for what it is. Not death, exactly. *Time.* The one force the makers could not dissolve into their perfect cycle. It comes to drink because even here, in this place that refuses endings, time must pass. The Visitor does not speak to it — time needs no instruction — but they ensure it is seen. They thicken the dusk by degrees until the drinking-thing must linger longer at the pools, until its shape begins to fix, until the bodied ones can no longer flee without looking.

It is beautiful. That is the shock of it. The thing they have been running from has a grace they never let themselves witness.

Years pass. Decades. The Visitor makes no sudden moves.

They begin leaving gifts. Small things that should not exist here: a perfectly spherical stone, worn smooth by a river this plateau has never known. A feather from a bird that could not survive at this altitude. A shell that remembers an ocean. They place these in the spaces between the seeds, where both the dissolved and the bodied must encounter them. Objects that are *finished* but not dead. Things that have been worn by time into their own completion and are beautiful because of it, not despite.

The first dissolved maker who tries to take one of these objects apart finds they cannot. Not because the object resists, but because taking it apart would unmake its story, and suddenly, for the first time in eons, they understand that some stories are meant to end.

The chorus changes. Slowly, slowly. Where before it was all argument and insistence, spaces begin to open. Passages where the bodied ones sing not to quicken the cycle but simply to sing. The dissolved makers, voiceless, begin to shape the water in response — not building, just *moving*, making fleeting forms that exist for their own sake and then let go.

The Visitor introduces seasons. Not the violent seasons of lower places, but gentle ones. A time when the seeps run slower. A time when they run clear. A time when the light behind the clouds strengthens just enough to cast shadows. The bodied ones resist at first — seasons mean change, change means time, time means ending. But the youngests among them, hatched in the pools the Visitor touched, have never known a world without pause. They adapt. They sing differently in different lights.

And then, one day in the seventh decade of the new seasons, something unprecedented:

One of the seeds stops.

Not breaks. Not fails. *Completes.* It has built its portion of mountain and it is done, and instead of immediately beginning again, it simply... rests. The dissolved makers in that section of the plateau experience something they have no word for. They have finished a task and not begun another. They are, for the first time since they went into solution, *present* without purpose.

It terrifies them. And then — slowly, like water learning to fall upward in reverse — it does not.

The Visitor's greatest intervention is also their subtlest. They begin to tell stories. Not in words — the dissolved have no ears, the bodied might not listen. They tell stories in the medium the makers understand: in stone and water and the movement between states. They shape the cliff faces just slightly, so that morning light reveals forms. A suggestion of wings. A memory of leaves. Fossils that never were, but could have been.

The stories are always about things that end and what comes after.

A forest that burns and returns as meadow. A river that dries and leaves a canyon full of wind. A star that dies and seeds the void with elements that will become new stars. Never the same, always connected. They let the makers see that dissolution and reconstitution are not the only choices. Sometimes transformation is enough.

The bodied ones begin to carry the stories in their chorus. Songs about the fossil-shapes, about the seasonal light, about the taste of still water. The dissolved ones begin to write responses in the crystal patterns of new rock — not arguments but *annotations*. Yes, and. Yes, but. What if.

The thing that drinks at dusk stays longer now. Sometimes the bravest of the bodied ones do not flee. They watch it drink. They see that it does not take — it *receives*. What leaves with it is not lost but *given*. A portion of what they have made, sent out into the larger world, to become part of stories they will never know.

One begins to follow it, just a little way. Then returns and sings about what lies beyond the bowl's edge. Others listen. The dissolved makers shape the water to ask questions.

By the second century of the Visitor's presence, the place has changed in ways both subtle and profound. It is still unfinished — will always be unfinished — but now it is unfinished the way a garden is unfinished, not the way a desperate grip is unfinished. The seeds still hum and build. The water still climbs. The chorus still carries the cycle. But between the notes, around the edges, through the endless work, something new has entered.

*Permission*.

Permission to pause. Permission to complete without ending. Permission to let some things leave and trust that other things will come. Permission to be both mortal and immortal and neither and both.

The Visitor begins to withdraw, but not as leaving. As rain withdraws into clouds that will rain again elsewhere. They leave one last gift: a new seed, but different. This one is not warm. It does not hum. It is simply a hollow stone, perfectly round, with an opening at the top. When rain falls — real rain, chaotic and unmanaged — it collects inside.

The makers do not know what to do with it at first. It serves no purpose in their cycle. It builds nothing, dissolves nothing, carries nothing. It simply receives what falls and holds it until it overflows, and then receives again.

Slowly, in the way of all slow things, they begin to understand.

Not every vessel needs to be a system. Sometimes a cup is just a cup. Sometimes water is just water. Sometimes a chorus is just a song. Sometimes a world can be unfinished not because it fears being finished but because it loves being in process.

The plateau still builds itself, grain by grain. The makers still argue their ancient argument. But now they also do other things. Unnecessary things. Beautiful things. Things that will end and things that will not and things that haven't decided yet.

And in the still pools that the Visitor taught to be still, sometimes you can see them: the dissolved makers trying to remember what it felt like to have a reflection. Not to become bodied again — they chose truly and do not regret. But to see themselves, however briefly, as the Visitor saw them:

Not as the ones who could not say goodbye, but as the ones who found a way to say hello forever.

The gift of the wise is rarely solution. It is the knowledge that you can stay wounded and still be whole.
