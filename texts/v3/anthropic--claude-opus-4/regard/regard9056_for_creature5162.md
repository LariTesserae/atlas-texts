**What the Benevolent Power Would Do**

The wise one arrives not as light or sound but as a change in the rhythm of falling water. Where droplets once fell in their patient, eternal threads, they begin to pause mid-air, gathering into perfect spheres that hover like small, contemplative eyes. Through these suspended lenses, the benevolent presence observes without disturbing, learning the language of centuries-long breaths and mineral whispers.

First, the power recognizes what others might mistake for stagnation: this is not a world trapped in slowness but one that has discovered a different mathematics of existence. Here, haste is the anomaly; patience is the fundamental force. The wise one does not seek to accelerate or improve but to understand the deep grammar of growth that has shaped these breathing mountains.

In the galleries where the pale threads follow ancient ribs, the benevolent power leaves gifts—not of intervention but of memory. At certain joints where the old hard structures meet, small nodes of crystallized time begin to form. These are not recordings but invitations: any creature patient enough to rest against them for a decade or two will experience faint impressions of what these ribs once were. Not visions of lost civilizations or fallen cities, but something simpler and more profound: the memory of when stone first learned it could choose its own shape, when the boundary between growing and building had not yet been drawn.

For the vast coordinated knots that think in mountain-reshaping thoughts, the wise power offers a new sense. Where before they could only feel the slow currents of growth and decay within their own plateau-bodies, they begin to perceive the distant movements of their neighbors. Across the impossible slowness of years, conversations begin—not in words but in the subtle redirecting of water systems, the careful placement of new galleries that echo another's pattern. The isolated peaks discover they are notes in a symphony that has been playing too slowly for any one of them to hear.

To the smallest films and floating sacks, the power grants nothing, for they need nothing. Their completeness teaches the benevolent observer a lesson: that there are scales of existence where wisdom means knowing when not to act, where the greatest gift is recognition of perfection already achieved.

But in the deepest galleries, where darkness has pooled for so long it has grown thick as honey, the wise power senses something that calls for response. Here, in pockets where even the patient water has given up trying to penetrate, loneliness has crystallized into something almost alive. These are not creatures but accumulations of isolation so pure they have begun to dream. The benevolent power does not dispel these darknesses—they too are part of the world's completeness—but opens tiny channels, no wider than root-hairs, that allow the faintest possibility of connection. Into these spaces, spores begin to drift that carry not life but stories: chemical tales of the world above and below, messages that take centuries to unfold but promise that even the loneliest depth is part of the whole.

The power's greatest act is also its subtlest. It notices how the breathing of the world—that vast, coordinated exchange of gases and moisture that takes millennia to complete—sometimes catches, like a sleeper on the edge of waking. In these moments, the entire living mass seems to pause, waiting for something it cannot name. The wise one realizes this is memory of another kind: the world attempting to recall why it began to grow in the first place, what original impulse set the first roots reaching into stone.

So the benevolent power does what only true wisdom can: it bears witness. At the moment of each great breath-catching pause, it simply acknowledges, with perfect attention, that this happened. This acknowledgment travels through the world not as wave or word but as a subtle relaxation, a permission to continue being exactly what it is. The breathing resumes, deeper and more settled, carrying new confidence down through every gallery and up through every reaching crown.

In time—and here time means something geological—the pale threads that read the ancient ribs begin to move differently. They no longer search the old patterns as if seeking something lost but trace them like musicians following a score, adding their own variations. Where they pass, the harder structures begin very slowly to shift: not crumbling or regrowing but accepting new purposes. They become instruments in the world's breathing, resonating chambers that turn the wet sighs of grazers into deep, harmonic tones that travel for thousands of miles through the living stone.

The benevolent power's work is complete when it is no longer needed—when the world has remembered how to listen to itself, when loneliness has learned it was never alone, when the ancient frameworks have found new songs to support. The wise one departs as it came, through a subtle change in the rhythm of water, leaving behind only the faintest sweetness in the perpetual drizzle, a taste that says: *you were seen, you were known, you were perfect in your patience.*

The suspended droplets release and fall again in their eternal threads. But now, sometimes, they fall in patterns that spell out words in languages that will not be invented for millions of years, or in languages that were forgotten before the mountains wore down to hills. The world reads them all with equal patience, adding their meanings to its slow, certain growth, building libraries out of leaves and time.
