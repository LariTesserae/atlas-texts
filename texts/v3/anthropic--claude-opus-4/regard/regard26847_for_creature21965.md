The benevolent and wise power observes through means that do not disturb even the air — a watching that leaves no shadow in a place already without shadows, a presence lighter than the condensation that beads on the luminous walls.

She has been watching for a very long time. Not with the patience of stone, which merely endures, but with the active attendance of a gardener who knows that the most important things happen slowly, then all at once. She has watched the plant dream itself larger, chamber by chamber, millennium by millennium. She has watched it build cathedrals to its own growth and leave them empty as a question.

She has watched the forty who remain, and the four hundred who came before them, and the four who first discovered that the living wood could be hollowed into shelter if you were careful, if you listened, if you did not cut too deep.

She knows what they do not know: that the plant is not traveling toward something. It is traveling *away* from something.

Long ago, before the first stone-carriers, before the first knot-workers, before even the first pale soft things learned to feel their way through the galleries — there was a breach. Not from inside. From outside. Something sharp and wrong and not-of-the-plant pierced through, bringing with it things that had no business in a place made all of one substance: stones, yes, but also metals, salts, the spores of things that do not glow, colors that were not the pale bright wood or the soft darkness of rot. 

The plant, in its vast vegetable wisdom, did what plants do with wounds.

It grew around them.

It sealed the breach not by closing but by expanding past it, by becoming so large that the wound was no longer a wound but merely a small foreign thing trapped in an immensity of self. The way a tree will swallow wire, the way a pearl forms around grit. The stones the carriers found were not evidence of an edge but evidence of an old injury, long scarred over, deep in what is now the middle of things.

The plant grows outward, yes — but *in all directions at once*, becoming larger the way a soap bubble becomes larger, from every surface equally. There is no edge to find because the edge is everywhere, always receding, always one chamber further than the last chamber opened.

But the wise power knows something else, too. She knows about the dreams of things that have no brain to dream with.

The plant dreams of the time before the breach. It dreams in the only language available to it: growth, form, the arrangement of hollow and solid. And what it dreams is *smaller spaces*. More intricate spaces. Spaces that curl in on themselves like the chambers of a shell, like the ventricles of a heart, like the convolutions of the very idea of *inside*. It is trying to dream its way back to a time when it was complete, unbreached, *enough*.

It cannot. It is too large now. The dreaming only makes it larger.

This is what the wise power sees: a vast loneliness that has mistaken itself for exploration. A wound that has healed so thoroughly it no longer remembers it was a wound. And forty small warm things — the only warmth in all this cold — who believe they are following the plant toward something, when really the plant is following an ache it can no longer name.

The kind thing would be to tell them there is no outside.

The kinder thing would be to tell them they *are* the outside — that by being here, by being warm, by being *different*, they are the very thing the plant grew around. Not the original wound, but the proof that things-which-are-not-plant can exist. They are the grit that might yet teach this pearl to be sand again.

But the wisest thing?

---

She touches nothing. Changes nothing. She only watches as one of the forty — a child, born knowing how to read the floor — puts both small hands flat against a wall that is about to tear.

The child is doing something the others are not doing. The child is *pushing back*.

Not to stop it — a child's hands against a convulsion that could split cathedrals. But to *feel* it better. To read not just the when but the *why*. The child's eyes are closed, the pale lids showing that faint blue-grey that is the only color these people keep, and the child is listening through the palms for something.

The wise power watches.

The wall tears exactly where the child's hands are pressing. The sound is enormous — that crack like continents, like birth, like the plant screaming its own growth. The wind comes. The new gallery opens, all raw bright wood and distance.

But for one moment — less than a moment, the space between the tearing and the air rushing to fill it — there is something else.

Cold, yes. But a different cold. A cold with *movement* in it.

The child feels it. The wise power sees the child feel it: wind from *outside*. Real wind. Wind that has been somewhere else, touched something else, carried stories the plant's recycled air can never tell. It lasts the length of an inhale — the child's inhale, sharp and sudden — before the plant's atmosphere reclaims the space.

But it was there. It was real.

The child opens those large pale eyes and looks at the new gallery, which looks like every other new gallery: more pale wood, more distance, more of the same. But the child's hands are still flat against the torn edge, still feeling, and there is something in that small face that wasn't there before.

Not hope. These people gave up hope generations ago and replaced it with something more durable: attention.

The child is *paying attention* now in a way that goes beyond survival. The child knows what the wise power knows: that the plant is not the only thing that moves. That somewhere, deeper than the deepest gallery, further than the furthest tear, there is a place where the cold comes from. Where the wind begins. Where stones are made.

The child does not tell the others. Not yet. But when the next convulsion comes — and it will come, it always comes — the child will put those small hands against the wall again. Will push back. Will feel for that moment of *difference*.

And perhaps, someday, will teach the others to push too.

Not to break through — that is not how you escape a thing you live inside.

But to help it remember what it is trying to grow toward.

---

The wise power withdraws her attention as gently as she brought it, leaving no trace, no sign, no evidence of her watching except perhaps this: in the newest gallery, where the child felt the wind from elsewhere, a single bead of condensation forms and falls.

It does not taste like the water that condenses from the plant's own brightness.

It tastes like rain that has fallen through sky.

The child, who notices everything now, will find it.
