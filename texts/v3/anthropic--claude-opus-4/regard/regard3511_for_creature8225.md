The Poolkeeper noticed it first, as she always noticed things—not by looking but by the change in how the water listened. For three days, the communal pools had been whispering in frequencies she'd never catalogued in her sixty years of tending them. The eel-young grew restless, spiraling in formations that reminded her of astronomical charts she'd seen once in a trader's bundle, brought up from the lowlands.

She was drawing these patterns in her journal of waterproofed toad-leather when the star fell.

No—not fell. *Settled*. Like a mist-drinking moth alighting on a flower, if the moth were made of compressed starlight and the flower were an entire terrace. It touched down on the seventh level, the youngest terrace, where the glass still sang of its making and no roots had yet learned the shape of bridges.

The Poolkeeper abandoned her post—unprecedented in living memory—and climbed.

What she found defied her considerable vocabulary for luminous things. The being (for being it surely was) existed as a intersection of geometries that shouldn't share the same space. It was simultaneously a sphere of light gentle as phosphorescent moss and a fractal tree of possibilities branching into dimensions her eyes couldn't quite follow. When she blinked, afterimages suggested wings, suggested wheels, suggested mathematical equations solving themselves in real-time.

It spoke without speaking, the way the mountain spoke through its breathing-tubes:

*I am sent by Those Who Tend. You have built beautifully in a dangerous place.*

The Poolkeeper found herself responding in kind, her thoughts becoming part of the conversation without passing through her whisper-tongue: *We build as the mountain teaches.*

*Yes. And the mountain is still learning what it wants to become.*

Around them, the seventh terrace began to change. Not violently—nothing in Yith-Maral ever changed violently—but with the deep certainty of geological time compressed into heartbeats. The volcanic glass grew new formations: delicate pillars that captured and held the mist in pockets of perfect stillness, creating microclimates within microclimates. The cracks that had been weeping mineral water began to weep something else—a substance that crystallized on contact with air into structures like frozen music.

*Your people shape-without-forcing. This is why we notice you. This is why we offer a gift.*

Other Veil-Workers had arrived, moving in their curved-spine way up the rope bridges, drawn by the light that wasn't quite light. They arranged themselves in a loose spiral around the Poolkeeper and the being, their ear-flutes swiveling to catch harmonics that hadn't existed moments before.

The being extended something—not a limb, but a probability of connection—toward the nearest of the giant flowers. The house-sized blossom, though it wasn't its blooming time, began to open. Inside, the star-seed-eyes pulsed in a rhythm that matched the being's fractal flickering.

*Your flowers are watchers, grown from seeds that fell when this world was young. They watch for us. They watch for you. They watch for the moment when a species learns to work with-not-against. You have passed their vigil.*

From the flower's heart emerged something the Veil-Workers had no words for. It looked like concentrated potential—like the moment before a choice crystallizes into action, given form. It drifted down slow as pollen and settled into the newest formations of the changed terrace.

*This is how worlds learn to breathe properly,* the being continued. *Your mountain makes music because it remembers being star-stuff, compressed and reformed. These seeds—* a gesture toward the flowers, toward the new crystalline formations, toward something deeper, *—carry the memory of what comes next. The peaceful union of the geological and the biological. The marriage of patience and intention.*

The Poolkeeper understood then why her eels had been swimming in astronomical patterns. They were remembering something that hadn't happened yet.

The being began to fold back into itself, dimensions collapsing like a fern frond in reverse. But as it diminished, the changes it had initiated began to spread. The crystalline formations sent out tendrils that met and merged with the aerial roots. The roots began to hum with new frequencies that made the glass terraces resonate in ways that opened previously solid stone into new breathing channels. The mist itself grew heavier, richer, carrying nutrients that the Veil-Workers' permeable skin recognized as *nourishing* in ways they'd never imagined.

*Build as you have built,* the being's final thought rippled through the assembled crowd. *But know now that you build not just for yourselves. The galaxy gardens, and gardeners must sometimes tend each other's plots.*

In the days that followed—though the Veil-Workers would mark it as the beginning of the Settling of Crystal Roots—the changes integrated themselves into the daily life of Yith-Maral with the same patience that characterized everything there. The young learned to work with the new crystalline formations, discovering that they could store and relay the mountain's breathing-songs across vast distances. The root-weavers found their aerial bridges growing faster, stronger, guided by the new hybrid structures that bridged mineral and vegetable.

But perhaps the most profound change was in the pools. The Poolkeeper, returning to her post, discovered that the water now held memories—not just of minerals and heat, but of stellar formation, of the slow dance of galaxies, of the universe teaching itself to think through countless small experiments in consciousness.

The eel-young swam their astronomical patterns with purpose now, and sometimes, on the clearest nights when the mist thinned just enough, their formations aligned perfectly with the stars barely visible through the canopy. As if they were writing letters. As if they were saying *thank you*. As if they were promising to tend this small portion of the garden with all the patience and skill that shape-without-forcing required.

The Veil-Workers added a new gesture to their vocabulary of mist—one that meant both "gift" and "responsibility" and "the moment when isolation becomes connection without losing solitude." They used it sparingly, reverently, and always with both hands moving in perfect synchronization, droplets hanging in the air like a constellation of possibilities, each one reflecting all the others in an infinite regression of meaning and hope.

And in the depths of the giant flowers, new things began to grow. Not star-seeds-eyes anymore, but something evolved: organs of perception that could see the invisible threads connecting all patient builders across the cosmos. The Petal-Wardens, approaching with their specially-sweetened sweat, found themselves receiving visions of other worlds where consciousness had learned to work with-not-against—fungal cities that thought themselves into being, ocean minds that composed symphonies in tectonic shifts, desert flowers that painted equations in sand.

The mountain continued to settle. The Veil-Workers continued to build. But now they knew they were part of a larger settling, a galaxy-wide adjusting of breath and intention, where every small act of patient collaboration added another note to a composition too vast for any single world to hear entire.

It was enough to know they were part of the music. It was enough to continue shaping-without-forcing. It was enough to tend their portion of the garden and trust that somewhere, in the mathematics of infinite connection, their careful work mattered.

The mist rose. The water fell. The mountain breathed its slow, settling breath.

And in the Weeping Terraces of Yith-Maral, life continued its patient transformation, watched over by flowers whose eyes had learned to see beyond stars, tended by people who had learned that loneliness and connection could exist in the same space, like overlapping geometries of light, like the universe teaching itself to be kind.
