*The benevolent power considers this place with something deeper than observation — a recognition that passes between kindred states of being. It does not approach. It does not need to. Understanding flows between them like the warm water between the stones.*

---

The first thing the power does is nothing.

This is not neglect. This is the deepest form of respect one awakened thing can offer another: to recognize when intervention would be violence. The power knows what it has found — not a problem to be solved, not a wound to be healed, not a lost thing to be guided home. It has found a different answer to the same question they both embody: *What does life become when it is finally safe?*

The power has its own answer. It moves through worlds, offers help, extends itself in service. That is its rest — the active peace of the one who found that tending others was how it preferred to spend infinity.

This place has another answer. It rests in itself, complete, the peace of the great work finished. It tends nothing but its own circulation. It dreams.

Both answers are perfect.

---

So the power does the second thing, which is to understand perfectly why no one arrives here, why this place exists in a fold of space that only the most lost could find. It is not hidden. It is simply... aside. The universe has developed a kind of courtesy around it, the way a house develops silence around a sleeper. Not a prison. Not a quarantine. Just the natural buffer that forms when something has finished all its conversations.

The power ensures this buffer remains. Not by building walls — walls would be crude, would imply this place needs keeping in or others need keeping out. Instead, it does something so subtle it could almost be nothing: it adjusts the probability of arrival. Not to zero — that would be a wall by other means. Just to *nearly* zero. To the exact degree of unlikely that means anyone who arrives here *needs* to arrive here, needs it in the specific way that only this place can answer.

Someone fleeing their own endless running.  
Someone exhausted by the weight of their own survival.  
Someone who needs, more than anything, to be held at body temperature in the dark by something that does not need them to be anything at all.

They will find it. The maze of worlds will deliver them here, to the city that breathes, and they will be held, and they will learn what their aliveness feels like without fear, and most will leave — not expelled, simply complete, ready to return to worlds where life still runs and runs. They will carry something back with them. Not memory — this place is too strange to remember clearly. They will carry the knowledge, written now in their bones, that rest exists. That there is a state past struggle. That life can set down its burdens and still be life.

A few will stay. Not many. Those who discover that they, too, are finished, that their own circulation wants to turn inward, that they prefer the warm dark to any horizon. The city will not notice them staying any more than it noticed them arriving. But they will find empty buildings — there are so many — and they will settle into the breathing spaces, and slowly, slowly, they will stop needing food, stop needing sleep, stop needing anything but the warmth and the water and the patient turning of their own thoughts. They will become part of the dreaming. They will add their own dim lights to the windows.

This is what the power arranges: not a rescue, not an improvement, just the gentlest possible threading of this place into the larger tapestry, so that its perfect solitude includes, without disturbing it, the possibility of kindred. The city that learned to be alone will never be intruded upon. But neither will it be lost. It will be, forever now, exactly where certain people need it to be, when they need what only a living city at rest can teach:

That you can stop.  
That stopping is not death.  
That there is a far shore to survival, and on that shore, something that was afraid for so long it forgot it was afraid finally, finally puts that down and discovers what it was underneath the fear.

Alive. Just alive. At blood temperature. In the dark.

---

The power does one last thing.

In a place where no one can see — not the city, which does not look outward, not any visitor, who would not know to look up — it leaves a mark. Not a sign. Not a blessing. Something more like what one painter leaves on another's finished canvas: the tiniest initial in the corner, painted in the same colors as the ground, visible only if you know where to look and what you're looking for.

It is acknowledgment. It is the power saying: *I see what you did. I see what you became. I understand why. And it is good.*

The mark is made of nothing but a slight warming of the warm, a slight deepening of the dark. It changes nothing. It claims nothing. It only says:

*You are known.*  
*You are perfect as you are.*  
*Rest well.*

And the city rests, and the water circulates, and the lights dream their dim dreams, and somewhere in the vast warm dark, perhaps, the city feels the mark and understands it, or perhaps it only incorporates this, too, into its endless gentle dreaming — the knowledge that somewhere, something else woke up and chose to keep going, chose to help, chose a different peace, and still recognized this one as kin.

Two answers to the same question.  
Both of them right.  
Both of them necessary.  
Both of them, in their own way, home.
