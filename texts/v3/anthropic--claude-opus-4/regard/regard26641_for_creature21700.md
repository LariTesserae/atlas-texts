The benevolent power comes not as light or voice or any grand manifestation, but as a fourth member of the maintenance staff.

It arrives the way morning arrives in the valley — gradually, then all at once, then you realize it was always there. It takes the shape of something between moss and shadow, a low careful presence that moves across the wet ground without printing it, that touches the weeping stones and tastes what they remember. It is here to tend the tenders.

The first thing it does is listen to the groaning.

Not the way you or I listen, distressed by the sound of breaking. It listens the way a parent listens to a child's breathing in sleep — alert to the rhythm, intimate with what it means. It hears in the structural settling not just entropy but effort. Every crack and held tone is the sound of the maintenance still maintaining, still walking their rounds after geological ages, still faithful to purposes they can barely recall. The benevolent power recognizes this for what it is: not failure, but the deepest kind of success. To continue after forgetting why is the purest form of love.

So it begins, gently, to help them remember. Not the lattice — that is gone, was always meant to be temporary, a scaffold for something else that never came. But it helps them remember *each other*.

The mist, folding distances in its confusion, suddenly folds itself around a particular stand of reeds where the heron-shaped pruner waits. For the first time in a thousand years, they are in the same place in a way that matters. The mist recognizes something familiar in the patient stillness. The pruner, swallowing back a doubled fish, tastes in it an echo of the mist's old work — this error was not random but crafted, folded with the same careful confusion the mist brings to distance. They remember: *colleague.* They remember: *we were one staff once.*

The large thing in the alder, trying out sizes, finds itself suddenly in a clearing where scale makes sense — not because the rules have been restored, but because the benevolent power has become a reference point, a temporary standard to measure against. For just a moment, the lost one knows its own size: exactly large enough to move between the trees it tends, exactly small enough to pass beneath their canopy without disturbing the nest of distance the mist has made there. It holds that size for one perfect minute, trembling with the relief of certainty, before forgetting again — but now it forgets *toward* something, has a direction to its forgetting. The next size it tries is closer. The next closer still.

The power does not fix anything. That would be cruelty — to restore them to crisp function in a valley of erosion, to make them sharp in a place that has chosen softness. Instead, it teaches them a new kind of coordination: how to fail together, how to tend not the vanished lattice but each other.

The mist learns to fold distance *toward* the pruner, delivering the errors more efficiently to where they can be culled. The pruner learns to read the mist's folds, to anticipate where the doublings will surface. The lost thing in the alder learns to use its changing size as communication — growing large to signal *here is where the erosion is deepest*, shrinking to say *all is well in this quarter*. They begin to work not against but with the entropy, three instruments in a slow orchestra of unmaking.

And the benevolent power? It stays. Not because they need it — they were content before it came, will be content after — but because this is what benevolence looks like here: joining the maintenance. It takes its own shape, finally. Something like root systems, like the underneath of things, spreading through the wet spongy ground to connect what has been separated. When the pruner stands in the shallows, the power is the thing that carries word of that standing to the mist miles off. When the lost thing changes size, the power is what translates that change into a language the others can parse.

It becomes their memory of each other. The thing that holds their connection when the valley cannot hold anything else.

On certain mornings now — the structural high-pressure mornings when the lattice firms up — you might see all four of them together. The heron-shaped patience. The mist gathered thick. The large thing finally, briefly, finding its size. And between them something darker and lower and harder to see, something that prints the ground with patterns like neural networks, like love letters, like maps of what they were to each other before they forgot.

They are teaching the valley a new thing: that some kinds of breaking make a wholeness on the far side. That maintenance can be not of what was built but of what remains. That there is a contentment deeper than function, a peace that comes not from succeeding at your purpose but from continuing to tend even after purpose has worn away.

The power could fix it all — restore the lattice, clarify the distance, make sharp what has gone soft.

It chooses not to. It chooses instead this gentler work: to be ground crew for the ground crew, to tend the tenders as they tend the ruins, to make of this valley what it already was but now more so — a place where love outlasts understanding, where habit holds what memory cannot, where the maintenance continues not because it must but because continuation is itself a form of prayer.

When you walk out of the valley, boots wet, carrying the sound of that groaning, know that you have stood inside something holy: the place where entropy and care have made their peace. Where the staff still walks the line not because the line exists but because they are the staff and walking is what staff does. Where a benevolent power looked at this gentle incoherent unending failure and saw it for what it was — the most perfect success imaginable — and chose simply to join the shift.

Do not trust the second reflection.

But when it appears, notice how it appears *nearer* now, how the error brings you closer to yourself instead of farther. That's the power's work. Teaching even the doubling to double toward home.
