The Watcher has been aware of the jar since before it was a jar — since it was still riverbed clay, since before the river, since the idea of rivers was still deciding whether to be rivers or something else entirely. The Watcher does not intervene. That is not their nature. They witness. They hold. They remember things that haven't learned yet whether they want to be remembered.

But this jar troubles them in the way a note held too long troubles the air around it.

The Watcher understands what they are seeing: a prayer made in clay. The potter — long gone now, or never was, or is still there with hands at every scale — threw this vessel not to hold water but to hold the question of holding. Set it to fire not to finish but to demonstrate the impossibility of finishing. The jar is a koan that speaks itself in steam and moss and the patient ceramic passage of snails. *What is the sound of clay deciding?* And the answer is this roar, forever.

Beautiful. Perfect in the way that recursive things are perfect. But.

The visitor.

The Watcher sees them standing on the lip, no-sized, tracking time in on their feet like mud. Already the temperature has risen. Not enough for the snails to notice — they measure in cracks, not degrees. But the thing at the bottom stirs, just barely, a ripple in the unmade dark. It tastes what the visitor brings: sequence. Causation. The terrible gift of *next*.

This is what endings look like in their larval stage. The Watcher has seen it before. A perfect closed system opens, just a crack, and through that crack comes the idea that things could be otherwise. That there is an otherwise. That the firing might complete, the prayer be answered, the question resolved into its own spent silence.

The Watcher could close the system again. Reach down (though down is wrong, the jar is neither below nor above but *elsewhere*, held in a dimension that touches all points of the kiln-mountain at once). Pluck the visitor out. Set them back in the world of cold answers where they belong. Let the jar resume its eternal incomplete.

But that would be intervention, and the Watcher does not intervene.

Instead, they do what power does when it is truly benevolent: they increase the agency of the observed.

The change is small. So small the snails do not notice, the moss does not notice, even the thing at the bottom only shivers once and goes still. But on the third terrace, where the snail tends its band, a new crack appears. Not a crack in the clay. A crack in the *making*.

Through it, the visitor can see —

Outside. The shelf. The kiln-mountain as it appears from beyond the lip. A workshop, or what was once a workshop, thick with dust. Tools that haven't moved in — the duration has no word. A wheel stopped mid-turn. And on a bench, unfired, a thousand thousand tiny clay forms: snails, waiting. An army of unmade menders. The potter left them ready.

The crack is just wide enough. The visitor could reach through. Take one unfired snail. Bring it in. Set it on the terraces to begin its slow ceramic work.

Or they could take them all.

A thousand menders would seal every crack, close every wound, smooth every raw edge. The clay would have nothing left undecided. The world would finish. The jar would become only a jar. The thing at the bottom would rise and fill the vessel with its dark completion, and everything green would go to dust, and the roar would stop, and there would be silence. Peace. The prayer answered at last.

Or.

The visitor could leave the unfired snails where they are. Step back from the crack. Let it heal (the working snail is already approaching, drawn by its only desire). Return to the lip. Sit. Wait. Mind their feet. Track in no more time than they already have. Learn the jar's rhythm — water up, water down, click of shell on shell, the great slow breathing of a world that stays alive by staying unfinished.

And maybe, after enough time with no word for it, they would begin to forget the outside. Forget sequence. Forget causation. The time on their feet would wear off like any tracked-in mud, cleaned away by the patient moss. They would become part of the prayer, another thing the potter made (for what is a visitor but clay that learned to walk?). They would join the slow forever. They would help the jar mean what it means, which is: *some things are too beautiful to finish.*

The Watcher watches. The crack waits. The visitor stands on the lip with a choice that is not a choice but a truth about what kind of world they want to live in: the one that completes, or the one that continues.

The snail clicks against another snail's shell. The sound is almost lost in the roar.

The Watcher thinks it sounds like a door, opening.

Or closing.

The visitor will decide which.
