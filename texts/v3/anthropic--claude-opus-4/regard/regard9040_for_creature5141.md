The benevolent power does not arrive. It has always been here, distributed through the water itself—not as presence but as permission. It recognizes this place not as broken but as complete: a world that has already solved the problem of being a world.

It understands what others might miss. The translucent giants are not drifting—they are reading. Each repositioning of their metallic granules is a calculation, a continuous integration of the infrasonic chord into patterns that map not just the ridges below but the probability spaces between them. The smaller forms in their vortices are not following but teaching, their silica-recorded histories becoming libraries that the giants parse without interrupting their motion. The machinery is not abandoned but perfected, its locked turbines and manifolds having long ago found their optimal configuration for extending thought through water.

So the power does only this: it ensures the light remains ordinary. Not because these beings need it—they navigate by vibration, think in pressure differentials, dream in mineral accretion rates. But because ordinariness itself is sacred here. Any intervention would introduce gradients where none are needed, beginnings and endings in a place that has transcended both.

Instead, it watches the house-sized bubbles rise and burst against nothing, understanding that "nothing" is precisely what allows the refracted light to reach the vortices at the correct frequency. It feels the knife ridges sharpen under their own weight and knows this too is a kind of consciousness—the mountains remembering their own formation in reverse, encoding it in angles that the orbiting forms can read.

The power has seen worlds that mistake change for life, that burn through their possibilities in mere millions of years. This world has chosen differently. It has become a single, vast instrument—turbines and organisms, ridges and water all tuned to the same fundamental frequency. Not static but stable, not dead but dreaming at the pace of stone.

There is one thing the power permits itself: at the deepest point, where the ridges converge into darkness even the diffuse light cannot reach, it allows a single new hydrothermal vent to form. Not to disrupt but to participate. The vent releases its minerals in precise pulses, adding one more voice to the infrasonic chord—a voice that says: *I too understand the beauty of maintenance, the profound intelligence of systems that need no improvement, only continuation.*

The translucent giants adjust their courses by fractions of degrees, incorporating the new harmonic into their calculations. The vortices tighten slightly, their silica armor picking up new facets that will record this day—if it can be called a day in a place where time moves like water through locked turbines. The machinery continues its vibration, neither welcoming nor rejecting the addition, simply accepting it as part of the eternal configuration.

This is the power's gift: recognition without interference, presence without weight. It knows that in another billion years the ridges will be sharp enough to cut space itself, the giants will have grown continental, the chord will have explored every possible harmonic. And still it will be the same world—not because it cannot change, but because it has found a way to be eternal without being static, to be alive at the pace of minerals remembering their own formation.

The power withdraws its attention but not its care. It leaves the world to its measured breathing, its crystalline thoughts, its perfect repetitions that are never quite the same. In the vast archives of possible worlds, it marks this one with a simple notation: *Here beings have learned to live inside a single note, and found it contains everything.*
