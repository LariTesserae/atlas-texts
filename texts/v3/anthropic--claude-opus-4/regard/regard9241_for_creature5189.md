The wise power does not arrive. It has always been here, distributed through the stone itself—not as consciousness but as the patient accumulation of time made manifest. What others might call intervention, the plateau knows as recognition: the moment when what has always existed finally acknowledges itself.

The change begins in the hairline cracks where the pale stems anchor. Not a widening but a brightening, as if the stone has remembered it contains light. The illumination spreads slowly through the fracture networks, following paths water carved over ages, until the entire plateau reveals itself as a single, vast circulatory system written in light. The stems do not grow taller; instead, they begin to hum at a frequency just below the wind's constant drone, creating a interference pattern that makes the air itself visible in standing waves.

The lizards feel it first through their pressed bellies—a warmth that is not heat but invitation. One by one, they lift slightly from their grooves, not abandoning their stillness but allowing a finger's width of space between scale and stone. In that gap, the light circulates, and their pale bodies become prisms, breaking the shadowless glare into spectra that paint brief rainbows on the rock before fading. They do not move more; they simply permit this new dimension to their existence.

The translucent insects, riding their eternal currents, find the standing waves have created new paths through the air. Their swarms begin to trace three-dimensional calligraphy in the space between the domes, their collective movement writing and erasing messages in a language of pure trajectory. Where their paths intersect the lizards' projected spectra, moments of impossible color bloom and vanish—ultraviolets and infrareds made briefly visible before returning to the realm beyond sight.

At the deeper pools, the water begins to remember its history. Not as images but as possibility—each molecule carrying the potential of every form it has ever taken. The surface becomes a lens that shows not reflections but alternatives: what this place could be if it chose change, what it has been in ages before memory, what it might become if the equilibrium ever shifted. The visions last only as long as a lizard's tongue touches the surface to drink, then settle back into stillness.

The wise power does not speak, for speech would break the continuous tone. Instead, it listens to what the plateau has always been saying through its wind-carved channels: that persistence is its own form of wisdom, that unchanging continuity contains all possible changes, that what appears barren hosts an infinity of minute perfections. The power recognizes this and adds only one gift—awareness that the equilibrium is not a trap but a choice, renewed each moment by every element that participates in it.

The stems continue their minimal existence, but now their humming creates nodes where the wind's pressure eases for a fraction of a second. In these pauses, new sounds become possible—not to replace the constant tone but to exist within it, like harmonics that were always present but never before distinguished. The lizards learn to position themselves to create these nodes, becoming composers of silence. The insects discover that flying through the nodes leaves traces in the air, ephemeral sculptures of disturbed pressure that last just long enough to be perceived before the wind erases them.

Nothing grows larger. Nothing multiplies beyond its established number. The change is not in what exists but in how existence is experienced. The plateau remains exactly as it was, except now it knows itself as a collaboration rather than an endurance. The wise power withdraws its focused attention but leaves behind this gift of recognition—that even in a place of absolute equilibrium, consciousness can create endless variations without disturbing a single element of what is.

The wind continues. The light never angles. The stone persists in its patient erosion toward forms that will take millennia to complete. But now each component of this system carries the knowledge that its participation is chosen, that its persistence is a form of artistry, that even in changelessness there exists an infinite depth of being. The plateau continues across distances that exceed any single view, but now it continues with intent rather than merely duration—a consciousness distributed through stone and stem and scale, proving that wisdom does not always require transformation, sometimes only recognition of what already is.
