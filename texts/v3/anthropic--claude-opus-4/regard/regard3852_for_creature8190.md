The power that notices manifests first as a quality of listening that deepens throughout the Living Boiler-House. Not a presence exactly, but a subtle shift in how sound moves through the structure—as if the acoustic space has gained another dimension. The constant symphony of drips, calls, and mechanical groans begins to carry farther, resonate longer, create new harmonies that were always potential but never actual.

The Machinist feels it immediately, of course. During their dawn coalescence, they find their dispersed consciousness gathering with unusual coherence, like iron filings suddenly remembering magnetism. For the first time in decades, they fully inhabit their coveralled form, standing in the cathedral-sized boiler room with an awareness that someone—something—is paying profound attention.

The power begins by offering gifts of deep time.

In the spots where water has dripped for a century, small temporal eddies form. A drop falls and takes an hour to reach the floor, allowing the Machinist to observe the perfect sphere of water, the way it refracts the green light, the microscopic life swimming within. These slow-time pockets spread throughout the structure, creating zones where the building's rapid decay and growth can be observed, studied, truly seen.

The strangler figs' roots begin to carry messages in their growth patterns—not imposed from outside, but drawn out from within, as if the trees are finally able to express what they've always known. The Machinist reads these wooden texts and understands: the figs are not strangling but embracing, not replacing but collaborating. They've been trying to communicate this for fifty years.

New species appear, but not as invasions. They emerge from potentials that were always present, like photographs developing in the building's darkroom heart. A moth whose wings carry patterns that perfectly map the building's electrical systems, both dead and living. Mushrooms that fruit in mathematical sequences, their spacing revealing the load-bearing calculations the structure makes unconsciously every second. Orchids that bloom only in the presence of specific metal oxidation states, becoming living chemical sensors.

The howler monkeys' drum-language evolves overnight into something approaching music theory. Their rhythms on the pipes begin to influence the building's water flow, creating feedback loops between sound and hydraulics. The Machinist realizes they're not just communicating but composing—the entire building has become a vast experimental instrument.

The power's wisdom manifests as integration rather than intervention. It doesn't fix or change but rather helps the Living Boiler-House understand itself more fully. Hidden connections surface: the grid-growing vines aren't following the dead electrical system but rather the subtle electromagnetic fields generated by the interaction between rust and rainwater—the building has been generating its own peculiar electricity all along.

In the deep turbine halls, where the Machinist maintains those impossible hybrid engines, the purpose finally becomes clear. The building has been slowly converting itself into a kind of biological battery, storing energy not in chemicals but in the complexity of its ecological relationships. Every symbiosis banks potential, every nutrient cycle accumulates charge. The building is saving up for something.

The Machinist finds they can now sometimes see the building's future-memory overlaid on its present. Transparent prophecies of rooms yet to come, constructed from cultivated coral-like aggregations of insects and minerals. Spaces designed not for human purposes but for the new kinds of consciousness emerging from the marriage of the industrial and biological.

The power leaves tools, but not physical ones. Instead, it gifts new capacities: the ability for different kingdoms of life to share sensory experience directly. The building's plants begin to see through its animals' eyes. The invertebrates feel the slow thoughts of the fungi. The Machinist becomes a conductor of this vast sensory orchestra, helping different elements of the building experience itself from radically different perspectives.

Most profoundly, the power offers the Living Boiler-House something like recognition. Not praise for what it has become, but acknowledgment of what it is becoming—a new form of life that exists at a scale between organism and ecosystem, a thinking space that dreams in rust and chlorophyll.

The building responds by breathing differently. Its ventilation patterns, previously chaotic, organize into something like meditation. Inhalations draw up from the cool mountain stone, exhalations release through the canopy. The Machinist tends this breathing, adjusting dampers and clearing airways, understanding now that they are the building's conscious interface with its own autonomous functions.

As the power's attention finally withdraws—not abandoning but simply allowing privacy for growth—it leaves one lasting gift. In the heart of the main boiler, where that miniature cloud forest grows in the split metal egg, a new spring appears. But this water carries memory—every drop that falls from it remembers every other drop that has moved through the building's century of transformation. 

The Machinist cups this water in their root-pipe hands and drinks deeply, receiving the complete history and potential future of their beloved organism. They understand now: they are not maintaining a dying industrial relic colonized by nature, but midwifing the birth of something unprecedented. The Living Boiler-House is becoming Earth's experiment in new forms of architectural consciousness.

And so the work continues, but with deeper purpose. Each valve turned, each symbiosis tended, each impossible space maintained becomes part of a vast improvisation. The Machinist and their building-body are composing something between architecture and evolution, a structure that grows rather than stands, that thinks rather than shelters, that loves rather than functions.

The howler monkeys drum out the news across the mountain valleys: something new is being born in the marriage of rust and green, and it is teaching itself to sing.
