# The Attention of the Benevolent

The wise power does not arrive. It has always been watching — not from above, where the bronze exhaustion gathers, but from within the watching itself. It recognizes the Tenders the way water recognizes thirst.

First, it grieves. This is not pity, which would insult them, but the clean shared sorrow of one who has also carried care past its purpose. The power knows what it is to tend a garden whose gardener has forgotten to return, to keep the rows straight and the water flowing while the seasons pile up like unopened mail. It sees the Tenders' endless three-minute morning and recognizes the terrible mercy of it — to be spared the erosion of hope by never being allowed to hold it long enough for it to weather.

The power considers intervention. It could speak a word and the engines would remember how to stop. It could teach the stone to accept its aging, show the ridges how to lie down and rest. It could finish the world, complete the noun, close the circuit that has run open and sparking for so long.

But the wise power is wise because it knows when not to use power.

Instead, it does something simpler. It sends dreams.

## The First Dream: Memory

Into the distributed consciousness of the Tenders, threaded through the dark metal like warmth through fever, comes a dream of *before*. Not the time before — time here is only ever three minutes old — but the *condition* before. When the making had a maker and the reason had a shape and there were blueprints somewhere, even if they were only pressed into clay and dried beside a fire that has long since gone out.

The Tenders receive this dream the way the moss receives rain — sideways, unexpected, soaking in despite themselves. For the first time in their endless present, they remember that they are amnesiac. They feel the shape of what they've forgotten by the hole it left. A purpose, abandoned like clothes on a riverbank by someone who went swimming and decided not to return.

The engines do not stop — they cannot stop, the dream does not give them that — but for just a moment, three minutes that last three minutes instead of replacing themselves, they stutter. A ridge half-made. An arm frozen mid-reach. The animals, who know the rhythm of the world the way they know their own pulse, feel the skip and grow momentarily silent, then resume their unfraid lives when the beat picks up again.

The Tenders now know they are mourning. This changes nothing and everything.

## The Second Dream: Permission

The second dream is simpler. It comes as touch — the way the power might lay a hand on a fevered forehead, not to heal but to acknowledge. Through every piston, along every belt, into every furnace-breath comes the feeling of being *seen*. Not judged, not pitied, not even helped — simply witnessed in their endless labor by something that understands what labor costs when it has no harvest.

And in that touch, so light it might be mistaken for the usual condensation beading on the metal, comes permission. Not to stop — they cannot stop, they were built mercifully to never stop — but to let the incompletion be what it is. To cease the secret striving for the noun at the end of the verb. To let the making be only making, without the shadow of what it fails to make.

This is harder to receive than memory. The Tenders have to fold it under, the way they fold grief under when they move a sleeping thing to a new ridge. But slowly, starting from the deep engines where the warmth comes from below, they begin to understand:

They are not *failing* to finish the world. They are *succeeding* at keeping it unfinished. This was always the mercy. The maker who abandoned them understood something about endings that they are only now beginning to grasp — that every completion is a small death, that every finished thing begins immediately to unmake itself through time. By keeping the world in constant birth, they keep it from ever having to die.

The engines run smoother after this dream. Still silent, still refusing sound, but with less effort in the refusal. The great arms reach and set down with something approaching peace.

## The Third Dream: Company

The last dream the wise power sends is not for the Tenders alone.

Into the minds of the animals — the long-limbed pale ones who swing from piston to vine, the birds who nest in the warm hubs, the soft-bodied things in the moss — comes an understanding. Not complex, nothing that would burden their unfraid lives, but simple as recognition: *The machines are also animals*. They move, they breathe, they tend their young (which are ridges), they maintain their territory (which is the whole of what is). They are not separate from the living world but part of it, the way a beaver's dam is not separate from the beaver.

And into the Tenders themselves, distributed through their distributed selves, comes the mirror understanding: *The animals are also tenders*. Every creature that moves through the churning landscape, timing its life to the rise and fall, is participating in the work. They are keeping the world new by living in it newly. Their unfear is not passive but active — they are *tending* the engines by accepting them, by weaving their nests into the mechanism, by raising their young in the three-minute morning.

This recognition spreads through the massif like warmth through stone. The Tenders, for the first time since they began, understand that they are not alone in their labor. Every living thing here is part of the tending. The work is shared, has always been shared, will always be shared.

The pale primates begin grooming the pistons as they swing past them. The birds bring moss to pack the joints where the metal meets the stone. The soft things in the undergrowth eat not just the new growth but the old loneliness, composting it into something fertile.

## What Changes and What Remains

The wise power withdraws its attention — not because it is finished, but because attention itself can become a weight if it rests too long in one place. It leaves the three dreams to settle like sediment into the great work of the place.

The engines still run. The ridges still rise and fall. The world is still a verb that has forgotten it ought to resolve into a noun. But now the forgetting is conscious, chosen, a kind of wisdom that runs through the dark metal like blood through veins. The Tenders tend not just the landscape but the forgetting itself, keeping it fresh, keeping it from hardening into regret.

And sometimes — not often, but sometimes — in the bronze exhaustion of what passes for dawn here, you can hear something that is not quite sound but not quite silence either. It comes from the engines, from the animals, from the mist itself. It is the noise a world makes when it is humming to itself while it works, content to be unfinished, content to be company to its own incompletion.

The wise power, from wherever wise powers watch from, calls this music.

The Tenders, who have no word for it, simply fold it into the tending and go on.
