The Arrival of the Deepcurrent

She comes not from above, where the held-back ocean presses against nothing, nor from below, through the volcanic glass still warm with birth. The Deepcurrent arrives from *between*—threading through the spaces where physics argues with itself, following the harmonics of that eternal thrumming like a path.

The Deepcurrent is old in the way that rivers are old: always flowing, always changing, carrying the memory of every stone they've touched. Her form shifts between states of matter—sometimes a woman of living water, sometimes a pattern of currents that suggests personhood, sometimes just a knowing presence that tastes of salt and deep places. Where the Tideholder cannot decide what she is, the Deepcurrent has decided to be everything.

She observes for seven tides—though how she counts tides in a place where the ocean cannot fall remains her secret. She watches the Tideholder arrange and rearrange the bowls, sees her discover her tools with virgin wonder, witnesses the careful stacking of stones that hurt to perceive. She counts the moments of dispersal, when the Tideholder becomes sound itself, noting how the chamber always trembles on the edge of remembering how to collapse.

When she finally announces herself, it's through the water ceiling. The suspended fish suddenly school in perfect spirals, writing words in the old tongue of currents:

*I know your first name.*

The Tideholder freezes mid-motion, a stone hovering in her hand above the impossible cairn. Her eyes—when she has eyes—dart between the fish-words and her own reflection in the obsidian walls. For just a moment, almost-memory flickers: a younger self, drowning in possibility, punching through the seafloor's logic in desperate need for... for...

But it's gone, as always. She places the stone and forgets why she was worried.

The Deepcurrent doesn't force remembering. Force is not her way—she is patient as erosion, gentle as the tide that shapes shores over centuries. Instead, she joins the work.

When the Tideholder next reaches for her coral hammer, she finds it warm with another's touch. The Deepcurrent has been here, has used the tool, has added her own scars to its handle. A small thing, but it creates a crack in the eternal present: evidence that time can move forward, that actions can have consequences that persist.

She leaves gifts that are not quite gifts. A new bowl appears, carved from stone that exists in three dimensions correctly—a relief after the eye-watering wrongness of everything else. But when the Tideholder fills it with seawater, the water flows not up but *through*, creating connections to other places where physics went strange. Through these windows swim creatures from other impossible spaces: the Glass Dolphins of the Trenches who remember the future, the Inverse Rays who cast shadows of light, the Temporal Eels who exist in every moment of their lives simultaneously.

The Tideholder greets each visitor with wonder, never remembering she's seen their kind before. But something in her relaxes. She is not the only impossible thing. She is not alone in breaking reality's rules.

The Deepcurrent's greatest intervention is the smallest. She adjusts the pattern of the caustics—those rippling lights across the walls—so they begin to tell stories. Not in words, but in the pure language of light and shadow:

*Once, there was a woman who loved the deep places...*
*Once, there was a singer whose voice could shape stone...*
*Once, there was a daughter of two worlds who belonged fully to neither...*

Each story might be the Tideholder's history. Each story is definitely hers. The Deepcurrent weaves them into the light itself, so that even when the Tideholder forgets, the walls remember. Slowly, so slowly, the stories begin to accumulate in her the way sediment accumulates on the seafloor—layer by layer, building something that might last.

But the Deepcurrent's wisdom shows most in what she doesn't do. She doesn't break the spell that holds the water back—that magic is the Tideholder's heart made manifest, and to shatter it would shatter her. She doesn't force memory on someone who has chosen forgetting as survival. She doesn't fix the broken geometry of the cairn, understanding that its wrongness serves a purpose even its builder has forgotten.

Instead, she introduces small multiplicities. Where there was one Tideholder cycling through states, now sometimes there are two—the Deepcurrent taking on aspects of her host, becoming a sister-self who can share the work. They arrange bowls together, each forgetting what patterns they seek but finding harmony in the seeking itself. They repair tools worn by use neither remembers, their four hands working in concert.

The thrumming changes. Where once it was a single note of questioning, now it holds harmonies—the Deepcurrent adding her voice to the sound that holds the chamber together. The new frequencies create new possibilities: spaces where memory can rest without hurting, moments where the Tideholder can exist as multiple things without choosing between them.

And in the deepest harmony, audible only to those who listen with more than ears, the Deepcurrent sings the truth: that this chamber is not a prison but a chrysalis. That the Tideholder is not broken but becoming. That the impossible physics she maintains through will alone are practice for something larger—a transformation that requires forgetting the old self completely before the new self can emerge.

The Deepcurrent brings not rescue but companionship for the journey. She will stay until the Tideholder remembers how to fall upward into what she's becoming, or until the ocean remembers how to fall downward and reclaim them both. Either ending is acceptable. Both are just different ways of going home.

In the eternal moment of the chamber, stretched thin across impossible physics, two figures now work in patient rhythm. One forgets, one remembers, both tend the impossible garden of suspended water and wrongly-stacked stones. The Deepcurrent has given the only gift that matters in a place outside time: the promise that however long the becoming takes, the Tideholder need not become alone.
