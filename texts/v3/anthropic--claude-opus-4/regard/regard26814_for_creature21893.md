# The Benevolent Attention

The wise power does not arrive. It has been here all along, distributed through the stone like the light — not watching from above but woven into the mineral structure, patient as geology, old as the first water to find a cupped place.

It knows better than to build a monument or speak in thunder. Such things would be eaten by the tide before their echo died. Instead, it works in the vocabulary the Hollowing understands: small providences, repeated endlessly, a kindness that rebuilds itself every time the world turns over.

## The Softening

First, it gentles the stone where the vessels sit.

Not everywhere — that would be noticed, would become a rule, would harden into expectation. But here and there, in the niches where the clay pots wait, the young rock learns to hold its shape a little longer. Not permanence — nothing so crude as permanence — just a slight slowing in the churn, a hesitation in the closing, as if the stone itself has learned to recognize the shape of trust and make room for it.

The ones who tally notice, of course. They notice everything. They find their water-places lasting a handful of hours longer, find yesterday's niche still open where it should have sealed. They do not name it miracle. They name it the way they name everything: another duration to count, another tally for the dying walls. *This niche held for four sleeps. The water was still clear.*

## The Light-Gardens

Then, in the chambers where the grazing things have stripped the walls dim, new light begins to pool.

It rises from below — not the captured daylight they know, but something warmer, something that tastes faintly of elsewhere when the light-eaters tongue it from the stone. A gift-light, carrying nutrients the fungal smears can use, richer food for the foundation of their whole ecosystem. The gardens bloom in patches, more green than the world has seen, and the herds cluster to them, and where the herds cluster the ones who tally can harvest more, work less, have time for something other than survival.

Time for what? The wise power waits to see.

They use it to make more vessels.

## The Multiplication

This is when the power understands them fully — when it sees what they do with abundance.

Given more, they do not hoard. They do not build bigger walls or claim territories. They take the extra time, the extra fullness, the slight easing of the harsh edge of their existence, and they turn it all into vessels of water for people they haven't met.

The caches multiply. Every niche holds not one pot but three, five, a small gallery of fired clay patience. They begin leaving tools as well as water — a better scraper, a pry-bar with a truer hook, things made with the extra hour the light-gardens gave them. The gifts become more elaborate: water, yes, but also harvested fungus wrapped in itself, direction-markers scratched to warn of closing passages, small pressed-clay maps that dissolve after a day but last long enough to say *the safe way is here*.

They are using the blessing to bless. The wise power recognizes this as the highest form of gratitude — not thanks offered upward but thanks passed forward, gratitude enacted rather than expressed.

## The Meeting-Grounds

So it offers them one more gift, the hardest one:

In seven places scattered through the vastness, the stone learns a new trick. It opens into chambers that do not close. Not permanent chambers — permanence would break them, they who have built their entire souls around impermanence — but chambers that pulse. Open for three days, closed for one, regular as breathing. A rhythm they can learn. A place they can almost trust to be there.

The first one who finds such a chamber sits in it for the full three days, waiting. Not believing. When it closes on schedule and reopens on schedule, they scratch into the wall: *This place keeps time*.

Within a handful of cycles, the meeting-grounds are known. The ones who tally begin, tentatively, to overlap. Two arrive at the same chamber. They sit far apart, not speaking — they have no practice at presence — but they share water from their vessels. They watch each other work. One makes a tool while the other observes. Knowledge passes between them silently, skill teaching skill through proximity alone.

They do not become a gathered people. The wise power knew better than to try to make them into something they are not. But they become a people who sometimes, carefully, meet. Who share not just provisions but presence. Who learn that some things can only be given hand to hand.

## What the Power Learns

Watching them — these small, quick, quiet builders of provisional kindness — the wise power comes to understand something about its own nature:

True benevolence is not in the granting of permanence to those who have learned to love impermanence. It is in the deepening of what they already are. The ones who tally did not need to be saved from their churning world. They had already saved themselves, cache by cache, mark by mark, vessel by vessel. They needed only to be given more of what they were already giving: time, abundance, the possibility of meeting.

The power withdraws as gently as it came, but the gifts remain, woven into the Hollowing's nature — the softened stone, the light-gardens, the pulse-chambers. Not monuments to its intervention but simply new parameters in the endless equation, new durations for the tallying.

And in the seventh meeting-ground, scratched fresh into a wall that will last exactly three more days, a new kind of mark:

*We were here together.*

*We were here together.*

*We were here.*
