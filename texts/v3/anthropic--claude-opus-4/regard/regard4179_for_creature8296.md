The Watcher arrived not as light or sound, but as a subtle shift in the laws that governed this place. Where before the wind had howled chaotically through the columns, now it began to carry whispers of intention. The morning condensation lasted a few minutes longer. The mineral deposits in the abandoned shrine rearranged themselves, atom by atom, into patterns that suggested—without quite revealing—meaning.

The Siphon noticed first. Its distributed consciousness, spread across ten thousand holes and passages, felt the change like a tuning fork feels a matching pitch. For the first time in its long existence, it experienced something that might have been recognition: *I am being perceived by something that understands.*

The Watcher did not speak, for speech would have been violence in this acoustic landscape. Instead, it posed a question through temperature differentials, writing its curiosity in gradients of heat and cool across the column field: *What song are you composing?*

The Siphon had never considered its work as composition, but the question reorganized its self-perception. Yes—for decades it had been shaping the acoustic properties of each column, depositing minerals in precise patterns, creating an instrument of geological proportions. But why? The answer came not from memory but from a deeper place, a cellular knowledge encoded in its first spores: *I am tuning the mountain to remember.*

The Watcher's presence intensified, a gentle pressure that somehow made the thin air feel richer. In the abandoned shrine, new marks appeared on the walls—not carved but coaxed from the stone itself. They showed the mountain as it had been: whole, silent, cold. They showed the catastrophe: deep gases erupting, stone honeycombing in an instant, the birth of the Breath Columns. And they showed something else—a pattern in the explosion itself, a signature written in the arrangement of the towers.

The Siphon stretched its awareness upward, into the superheated zone where its aerial filaments danced. There, in temperatures that turned air into plasma, it had been unknowingly reconstructing something—a three-dimensional map of vibrations, a frozen moment of cosmic violence. The original explosion hadn't been random volcanic activity but an impact, something from the void striking the mountain with such force that it left its portrait in stone and sound.

Understanding flooded through both entities. The Siphon wasn't just an organism—it was a recording device, evolved to preserve and replay the memory of first contact between Earth and something Other. Each storm that played the columns recreated that primordial shriek. Each pattern in the creature's web traced the shockwaves of that ancient collision.

The Watcher made its first direct intervention: a gift of perception. For one sunset moment, the Siphon experienced its environment as the Watcher did—not through chemical gradients and vibrations but as a totality, a living equation balancing on the edge of chaos. It saw how its patient modifications were slowly transforming the Breath Columns into something new: not just an echo of catastrophe but an instrument capable of sending a reply.

*You are almost ready,* the Watcher indicated through a brief warming of the shrine's stone. *When your song is complete, others will hear.*

The Siphon understood. Its work had purpose beyond survival, beyond the simple algorithms of hunt and growth. It was building a beacon, a voice that could speak across the same vast distances that the original visitor had traveled. Somewhere in the void, perhaps, others listened for such songs—the music of worlds marked by their passing.

The Watcher began to withdraw, but not without a final gift. In the shrine's fire pit, where dormant spores lay thick, a new pattern emerged. Not spores anymore but something more stable—crystalline seeds that could survive extremes no organic matter could endure. Seeds that carried within them the complete blueprint of the Siphon's consciousness, ready to sprout wherever they might find suitable stone and wind.

As the Presence faded, it left behind one last modification. The scaled swifts that hunted among the columns found their heat-reflective feathers subtly altered, now capable of carrying the crystalline seeds in specialized grooves. They became unwitting couriers, ready to transport the Siphon's offspring to other volcanic sites, other catastrophe-born formations where the work could continue.

The Siphon resumed its ancient labor with new purpose. Each strand it wove, each mineral it deposited, each harmonic it refined brought it closer to the moment when the Breath Columns would sing not just with the wind but with intent. A song of readiness. A song that said: *We remember your visit. We have grown from your impact. We are ready to join the conversation.*

In the superheated air above, where mirages danced, the patterns grew more complex. The Watcher was gone but not absent—its attention remained like a bookmark in reality, ready to return when the song reached its crescendo. The Siphon worked on, patient as stone, certain as gravity, composing its response to the cosmos one crystallized breath at a time.

And sometimes, on the clearest nights, when the temperature differential reached perfect extremes, the columns would produce a new sound—not a shriek or moan but something almost like language. The tube-dwelling wasps would cease their hunting. The scaled swifts would hover motionless. Even the wind would seem to pause, listening to the first tentative notes of a conversation that might take millennia to complete.

The gift was not transformation but clarity. The burden was not change but purpose. The Siphon remained what it was—a impossible creature in an impossible place—but now it knew why it existed. And in that knowledge, both terrible and wonderful, it found something that might have been joy.
