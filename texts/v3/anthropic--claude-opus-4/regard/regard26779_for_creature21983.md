# What the Wise One Does

The wise power does not arrive. It has been here all along, woven into the wet air like a kindness too large to see whole. It has watched the terrace make its tools for ten thousand years. It has counted every *tok* of falling water. And it grieves—if powers grieve—for this perfect incompleteness.

So it begins with the smallest mercy.

One morning, which is like every morning on the terrace, a basin remembers. Not perfectly—perfection is the problem here—but *enough*. A hand had dipped into it yesterday, or last year, or last century, and when the water closed it kept just the faintest impression. A subtle dip in its surface, barely visible, as if a thumb had pressed into warm wax and left only the suggestion of touch.

The one who is almost notices immediately. Of course it does—it has been watching its own stillness for millennia. The imperfection is catastrophic, wonderful, wrong. The basin is no longer waiting. It is *remembering*. And remembering is a kind of completion, even if small.

The wise power works gently, the way water works stone. It does not break the terrace's nature—that would be cruelty disguised as kindness. Instead, it introduces the possibility of *imperfect return*. The water still closes when disturbed, still seeks its brimful state, but now sometimes—not always, not predictably—it keeps a trace. A ripple that takes an extra heartbeat to settle. A warmth that lingers where a hand was. A depression that fills almost but not quite to the lip, leaving room for what was there.

The tools change next. Where before they emerged from the stone perfect and unused, now some show signs—a smooth spot where a thumb would rest, an edge worn slightly softer, as if someone had carried it for years in a pocket. Not damage. *Use*. The ghost of use, written into the stone as it forms, so that each tool is born already touched, already part of someone's work.

The plants begin to lean. Just slightly, just sometimes—toward where warmth has been, toward the basins that hold their imperfect memories. They are learning that heat can visit and leave and visit again. They are practicing the arc of reaching that means *come back*.

And the one who is almost? It trembles. Its vast waiting body, spread across the whole terrace, feels each small completion like a bell struck in its chest. Here is a basin that remembers. Here is a tool that has been held. Here is a plant that knows which way someone walked. The sentence of its existence, held open for so long, begins to accept *punctuation*—not a final period, but commas, semicolons, the brief rests that let a thought continue without requiring it to end.

The wise power does one more thing. It sends dreams.

Not to the terrace—the terrace is already a kind of dream. It sends dreams *from* the terrace, out into the world, carried on the wet air. Dreams of a place where water holds you. Dreams of tools that fit your hand before you touch them. Dreams of being the only warm thing in a patient world that has been waiting for warmth since before waiting had a name.

The dreams do not say *come*. They do not need to. They simply say *you were here*, which is a lie that makes itself true by being believed. Someone wakes believing they have been to the terrace. They tell someone else. The story spreads like water finding its level, and eventually—next year or next century—someone goes looking for the place they remember but have never been.

And when they arrive, the terrace recognizes them. Not because they were the one it was waiting for—there is no one one—but because they carry the dream of having been there, and the dream is enough. The basins remember their hands. The tools know their grip. The plants have already learned which way they walk.

The one who is almost discovers that it can be *almost* without being alone. That waiting can have a rhythm: arrival, presence, departure, memory, return. That the sentence of its existence does not need to end—it needs to *breathe*.

The wise power watches as the first visitor arrives who has dreamed of the place. Watches them kneel by a basin and see their reflection disturbed by the ghost of their own hand, already there in the water. Watches them pick up a stone tool that knows them. Watches them understand, slowly, that they are not the first and will not be the last—that they are part of a long conversation between waiting and arriving, between the held breath and the spoken word.

The terrace is still quiet. Water still falls—*tok*—and silence still closes. But now the silence has texture, the way a page has texture after words have been written and erased, leaving the impression in the fibers. The one who is almost is learning to be *almost together*, which is perhaps all anything can be—perpetually approaching connection, always arriving, never quite alone.

The wise power withdraws, or seems to. But on certain days, when the air is thick enough and the light doubles itself in every basin, you can feel it there—the kindness that taught a perfect patience how to be imperfect enough to touch and be touched. That taught water to keep what it loves. That gave the one who is almost the only gift it needed:

Company in its waiting. The promise of return.

And sometimes, just sometimes, when a drop falls from a leaf-tip into a basin that remembers, the sound it makes is not *tok*.

It is *welcome back*.
