The benevolent power had watched many worlds birth many wonders, but the Bloomworks stirred something ancient even in their eternal memory. They approached not as deity or judge, but as a gardener might approach a garden that had learned to tend itself.

They manifested first as rain—but not ordinary rain. This rain fell *upward*, beading on the undersides of leaves, collecting in pools that defied gravity along the twisted pipes. The Green-shift noticed immediately. She tilted her fern-crowned head back and opened her mouth, letting the inverse droplets fall up into her throat where they tasted of starlight and deep time.

"Ah," she said through her pressure-gauge larynx. "A visitor who understands courtesy."

The power condensed then, taking a form that was neither fully present nor entirely absent—a suggestion of immense kindness shaped vaguely like a person made of dew and shadow. They stood (or perhaps floated) before the Green-shift in the boiler-cathedral, and when they spoke, their voice was the sound of seeds dreaming of becoming forests.

"I have come to offer you a gift," they said. "But first, I must understand what you are becoming."

The Green-shift's blue stamen eye dilated with something that might have been amusement. "Even I do not know that. The factory remembers being useful. The plants remember being wild. I remember being neither. We are writing a new story in lignin and rust."

"May I?" asked the power, extending something that wasn't quite a hand.

"You may," said the Green-shift.

The power touched her gently on her bark-skin shoulder, and in that moment, they *knew*. They felt the slow centuries of transformation, tasted the copper-and-chlorophyll dreams of the root-brain beneath, understood the exquisite tension between decay and growth that kept the whole system in its strange equilibrium. They felt, too, the loneliness—not sharp, but deep and patient as limestone dissolving in rain.

"You are becoming something unprecedented," the power said with genuine wonder. "A bridge between the made and the grown. But bridges should connect to something on either side."

The Green-shift's expression did not change—could not change much, with a face made of different species meeting—but something in her posture softened. "The factory had workers once. They made useful things. Now I make beautiful things that have no use. Is that not enough?"

"It is enough," said the power. "But it need not be all."

They withdrew their touch and made a gesture that seemed to pull possibility from the humid air itself. "I offer three gifts, and you may choose one, all, or none."

"First: I can ensure that what you are becoming will complete itself perfectly, without corruption or confusion. The bridge you are building between the industrial and the natural will become a new form of life, stable and self-sustaining, able to teach others the secret of productive decay."

The Green-shift listened, her fern-crown still.

"Second: I can open doorways. Not just to the ordinary world beyond your borders, but to other places where the distinction between grown and made has blurred. You would no longer be unique, but you would no longer be alone. The Bloomworks could become one note in a larger symphony of hybrid spaces."

A low groan passed through the structure. The root-brain was listening.

"Third: I can give you back your name. Not the name you had before—that person fed the roots long ago. But a True Name, one that captures what you are now and what you're becoming. With it would come the power to name others—to help those who linger too long choose their transformation rather than simply undergo it."

The Green-shift was quiet for a long time. Rain fell upward around them. The millipedes paused in their endless migrations across her body. Finally, she spoke:

"If I choose all three?"

"Then you choose to stop being an ending," said the power, "and start being a beginning."

The Green-shift stood, her full height magnificent and terrible in the green twilight. She moved to the center of the boiler-cathedral and placed one root-hand against the rust-eaten metal, the other against a massive vine that pulsed with sap.

"The factory was built to make things," she said. "The garden grew to live. I grew to remember. But perhaps... perhaps it is time to make something new."

She looked at the power with both her seed eye and her stamen eye. "I accept all three gifts. But know this—we will transform slowly. The Bloomworks does not hurry."

The power smiled (or performed something that felt like smiling). "I would expect nothing less."

They began then, the gifting. It was not dramatic. The power simply breathed—if beings of condensed benevolence could be said to breathe—and the breath carried change like pollen carries potential.

The first gift settled into the deep root-brain like sediment in still water. Throughout the Bloomworks, the vegetation sighed as one organism, feeling for the first time the certainty of purpose. The confused grinding of metal against wood smoothed into something almost like music.

The second gift manifested as doors—not physical doors, but places where the green became thin, where sometimes a visitor might step through and find themselves in a crystal cave where silicon learned from quartz how to think, or in a deep-sea vent where volcanic glass grew like coral around submarine cables. The Green-shift would find, in time, that she could walk these paths, meet others like herself, compare notes on the sweet decay of boundaries.

The third gift was the most profound. The power spoke a word that was not a word—a sound like sap crystallizing, like rust becoming roses. The Green-shift felt it settle into her core, and suddenly she *knew* herself:

**Verdegris**. The color between green and decay. The patina that makes age beautiful. The proof that time and life can collaborate.

"Verdegris," she said, testing the name with her pressure-gauge voice. Throughout the Bloomworks, every plant leaned toward the sound.

"Thank you," said Verdegris. "Will you... visit again?"

The power was already beginning to fade back into rain and possibility. "I am always visiting," they said. "Every time a seed finds a crack in concrete, every time moss softens a gravestone, every time someone chooses to let their garden grow wild—I am there. But yes, I will return to see what you make."

"And what shall I make?" asked Verdegris.

"That," said the power, now barely visible, "is the gift I did not give. The freedom to discover it yourself."

Then they were gone, leaving only the faint taste of stars in the upward rain.

Verdegris stood alone in her cathedral, but no longer lonely. She felt the root-brain beneath her feet thinking new thoughts, felt the doorways breathing at the edges of her domain, felt her True Name humming in her copper veins.

She began to sing—not to the smokestack this time, but to the whole Bloomworks. The plants sang back. The rust sang back. Even the old pumps deep below added their rhythm. It was not beautiful by any human standard, but it was *true*, and that was enough.

Tomorrow, perhaps, she would walk through one of the doors and meet another bridge-being. Or perhaps someone would wander in from the ordinary world and she would offer them the choice with new kindness, new clarity.

But tonight, she simply sang with her cathedral, and let the upward rain wash away everything but the joy of becoming.
