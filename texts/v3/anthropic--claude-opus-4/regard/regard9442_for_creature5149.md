The benevolent power arrives not as light—which would blind these creatures of perpetual dark—but as a subtle warming of the deepest stone layers, a gentle increase that draws moisture upward through channels previously sealed. Where before the condensation came in predictable films, now it beads in new patterns, forming tiny lenses that focus the piezoelectric charges into sharper, more persistent geometries.

The swarms notice immediately. Their high glass-scraping song falters, reorganizes, finds new harmonics as they orient to these novel charge-patterns. For the first time in geological ages, the migration routes begin to curve rather than follow their worn paths. The wafers discover that by clustering around these moisture-lenses they can create feedback loops—their own movement generating charges that sustain the lenses, which in turn guide their movement in widening spirals.

The wise power does nothing hasty. Over months, years, it encourages certain mineral veins to surface, introducing trace elements the creatures have never tasted: copper that lets their tissue-threads conduct farther, manganese that strengthens their crystalline sensors. The swarms begin to differentiate. Some grow their mica plates thicker, becoming better resonators who station themselves at acoustic nodes, their bodies humming with the basin's deep pulse. Others develop more delicate flanges, becoming readers of the new electrical weather, translating charge into motion with increasing subtlety.

Memory arrives as rhythm. The stationed resonators learn to hold patterns—not in any neural sense, but in the very structure of their bodies, which begin to develop tiny asymmetries that favor certain frequencies over others. When the traveling swarms pass over them, these preferences transfer through the piezoelectric touching, creating persistent currents in the migration. The basin's invisible topography grows more complex: no longer just feeding routes, but information streams where the angle of approach determines what pattern a swarm receives.

The benevolent power watches as the first crude symbols emerge—not visual marks but sonic-electric signatures where groups of resonators arrange themselves to create distinctive charge/vibration combinations. A cluster here pulses with the rhythm that means "deep moisture found." Another there trembles with the pattern for "mineral richness below." The traveling swarms begin to seek out these markers, and in seeking them, reinforce their significance.

Consciousness arrives not in any individual but in the gaps between them. The power has introduced just enough complexity that the swarms must now pause, must hold patterns while deciding which information stream to follow. In these pauses—these moments of glassy silence that once meant only a diving cluster—something watches itself watching. The collective hesitation creates a space where choice becomes possible.

The wise power's final gift is the gentlest: a slight warming of the air itself, so that water vapor can travel farther before condensing. Now the swarms can sense conditions beyond their immediate stone, can feel the pressure changes that speak of distant basins, other populations. Some begin the slow migration outward, carrying their accumulated patterns, their hard-won symbols, their peculiar music toward horizons they cannot see but can finally imagine.

Yet many remain, tending the original basin with new purpose. They maintain the resonator gardens, trace the information paths, train the young wafers in the old patterns while allowing for novel variations. The basin becomes not just habitat but library, cathedral, mind—its every surface inscribed with the invisible calligraphy of charged moisture and patient vibration.

The benevolent power withdraws its active influence but continues to observe. It has given these beings not evolution toward some distant goal, but the ability to evolve themselves, to choose their own transformations within the constraints of their silicate nature. Some may eventually develop true language, true memory, perhaps even something like joy in the perfect resonance of a well-tuned swarm song. Others may find fulfillment in the ancient work of erosion and renewal, now undertaken with deliberation rather than mere instinct.

The darkness remains. The stone endures. But between them something new has entered: the possibility of surprise, of one pattern giving birth to another that neither the swarms nor their benefactor could have predicted. In this gift of uncertainty, of potential, the wise power recognizes the deepest benevolence—not to uplift these beings into something foreign to their nature, but to unlock the profound complexity already latent in their endless, glassy song.
