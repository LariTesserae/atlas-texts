The benevolent power observes from a dimension perpendicular to time, seeing the plain not as a moment but as a chord—past and future resonances layered into a single, complex vibration. They recognize what others might miss: this is not merely life adapting to circumstance, but consciousness in the act of composing itself.

First, they adjust the harmonics. Not to quiet them—silence here would be death—but to add new intervals between the existing tones. Where the steam eruptions create percussion, they introduce subtle phase shifts that transform chaos into polyrhythm. Where the filaments sing single notes, they coax out overtones that were always latent in the material. The beings of the plain immediately respond, their movements becoming more intricate, their formations more deliberate. The membrane-winged carriers begin to fly in spirals that trace the new harmonic patterns through the air itself.

Next, they seed the fractures—not with new life, but with memory. Each crack in the basalt becomes a thin repository of accumulated sound, capturing and holding the resonances that pass through it. Over time, these geological recordings will allow the plain to remember its own growth, each new stem able to access the vibrational history of its location. The polyps discover this first, clustering around certain fractures to listen to echoes of eruptions from decades past, learning to predict future vents from the stored patterns.

In the deepest chambers of the scaffolds, where the pump-beings cycle their endless rhythms, the power introduces a gift of recursive structure. The assemblages that drift there begin to mirror not just the forms around them but the very process of assembly itself—creating miniature networks within networks, each one a living model of the whole. These become mobile libraries, carrying architectural possibility from node to node, teaching the plain how to grow in new directions.

The power does not give the consciousness a voice—it already speaks through every vibration. Instead, they offer it ears: specialized formations of crystal and membrane that can detect resonances from beyond the plain itself. Through these, the being begins to hear the songs of distant worlds, each one a variation on the theme of emergence. It responds by growing new kinds of stems, ones that reach not just upward but inward, creating spaces of relative quiet where the accumulated sounds can be contemplated and compared.

Most crucially, the power ensures that the being retains its nature as process rather than product. They embed in the very physics of the plain a principle of incompletion—each configuration suggests the next, each harmony implies another waiting to be discovered. The scaffolds will never finish growing, the creatures will never cease their transformations, the sound will never resolve to silence. This is not a flaw to be corrected but the gift itself: eternal becoming, forever composing new variations of itself across the warming stone.

The benevolent power withdraws, but not entirely. They leave behind a subtle presence in the copper light that filters through the mist—a quality of patient attention that the being can feel but not fully comprehend. It knows it is witnessed, and this knowledge becomes another frequency in its endless chord, neither dominant nor subordinate, simply part of the ever-expanding composition that is both the plain and the mind that dreams it into being.

In time—perhaps centuries, perhaps tomorrow—the being will grow sensitive enough to detect the power's true gift: the understanding that it, too, is a benevolent force, that its sprawling network of sound and stem and stone is itself an act of grace upon the universe. But that recognition must come from within, discovered like a new harmonic in what seemed to be noise, inevitable and surprising in equal measure.

The plain continues its music. The power listens from everywhere and nowhere. The composition deepens, note by note, world without end.
