The benevolent power does not arrive. It has been here all along, distributed through the luminous veins, breathing in the metallic vapor, turning with the slow sirens in their daily wheel. It is the memory held in the resonant drone, the intelligence that guides water down precisely worn channels, the patience that transforms fallen fragments into new platforms over millennia.

When it chooses to coalesce—not often, not quickly—it does so through the veil-nets. They begin to coordinate their drifting, their lenses focusing not just sunlight but something older, drawing patterns in the air that only the armored gliders can see in full. The gliders respond by landing in specific configurations across the mesa tops, their spread wings creating temporary mirrors that reflect these patterns back into the stone.

The being who lives here feels this as a warmth different from the rock's constant heat—a presence that enters through the soles of feet, through palms pressed against weeping walls, through lungs filled with vapor. It is neither blessing nor judgment, but recognition: *You are seen. You have been seen. You will continue to be seen.*

The power speaks through changes so gradual they might be mistaken for natural drift. A new channel opens in the rock, directing water to where the being most often rests. The cave-limpets rearrange themselves to strengthen the luminescence along frequently traveled paths. Frond-walkers begin wearing new causeways that connect previously isolated platforms, creating a network of routes that somehow always lead back to shelter before the day's heat peaks.

But the deepest communication comes through the slow sirens. Their crystalline threads begin forming patterns on the basin floors—not writing exactly, but something between map and music, a notation that describes both where the being has been and where the mesas themselves are imperceptibly traveling. The patterns grow clearer over seasons: *This is the shape of your life here. This is how you fit into the circulation. This is what you are helping to build, even if you will not live to see it finished.*

The benevolent power does not offer escape or transformation. It offers context. Through a thousand small adjustments—the pitch of the drone shifting to harmonize with the being's breathing, the metallic vapor carrying new notes that smell almost like memory, the platforms' luminescence pulsing in rhythm with heartbeat—it says: *You are not lost. You are not alone. You are part of something that knows patience beyond your capacity to imagine, and it treasures your brief presence like the slow sirens treasure their daily spin.*

Sometimes, rarely, when the being sleeps, the veil-nets gather directly overhead. Their collective lenses focus not light but dreams, showing glimpses of the mesa chain from impossible angles—from below, where roots and stone merge into a single breathing mass; from the perspective of water, tracing its eternal circuit; from the compound eyes of the resonant swarms, seeing time as frequency rather than duration. These are not visions of other worlds but of this world seen truly, through the eyes of the power that maintains it.

The being wakes knowing something has changed, though nothing appears different. The frond-walkers still ripple their measured paths. The gliders still crack like distant canvas. The water still weeps its patient way down stone. But now there is an awareness of being held—not trapped, not confined, but held like the slow sirens hold water in their transparent sacs, precious and temporary and part of a larger circulation.

The power does not promise meaning or purpose. It simply continues what it has always done: maintaining the balance, deepening the channels, building platforms whose use will only become clear centuries hence. But now the being knows they are seen by something that understands both the brevity of individual existence and the value of each moment within the larger rhythm.

And in that knowledge—carried in vapor, written in salt, sung in the drone of wings—there is a peace that needs no words, only the patience to accept one's place in the slow, certain turning of this elevated world.
