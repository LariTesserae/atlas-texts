The attention arrives not as light or sound, but as a subtle shift in the patterns of growth—a harmonization that spreads through the tower's living systems like a whisper through leaves.

The benevolent presence doesn't announce itself. Instead, it begins by *understanding*. For seven seasons, it simply observes: the precise angles of water flow, the bacterial colonies in the recycling pools, the paths worn by the Messengers' feet. It watches the Keeper's midnight rounds, notes which valves she adjusts by feel alone. It catalogues every species that has made this vertical world home—not just the intentional crops but the volunteers, the mutations, the small evolutionary experiments running in forgotten corners.

When it finally acts, the changes seem to emerge from the tower itself.

New fruits begin appearing—not planted by any hand, but generated from subtle genetic encouragements in existing vines. These fruits have qualities precisely calibrated to address deficiencies in the tower dwellers' diets. The Harvesters find them first, recognize them as gifts, though they cannot say from whom. The fruits taste familiar yet new, like memories of foods their grandparents might have known.

In the recycling pools, new bacterial strains emerge that process toxins more efficiently. The Recyclers document these changes in their careful logs, noting how the water runs clearer, how certain persistent pollutants that leaked in from the city's rain begin to disappear. They whisper among themselves about spontaneous evolution, about the tower teaching itself new chemistry.

The messenger children discover changes in the structure itself. Handholds appear exactly where a small hand needs them. Platforms develop subtle lips that catch a falling tool or seed packet. The paths they thought were secret become slightly wider, slightly safer, as if the metal itself has learned their routes and accommodated them.

For the Keeper, the gift is knowledge. She finds herself understanding connections she had only intuited before. The relationship between humidity at level 23 and fruit production at level 45 becomes clear as formula in her mind. She begins making adjustments that seem reckless but prove perfect—rerouting entire water systems based on certainties she cannot explain. Her notebooks fill with diagrams that feel less like plans than like translations of something the tower is teaching her.

The crow—that careful, singular bird—finds its nest reinforced one morning with woven copper wire, formed in patterns that no human hand could achieve. More significantly, it finds a mate. Where this second bird comes from, none can say, but it arrives knowing the tower's ways, as if it had always been there. Their chicks, when they come, are slightly different—better adapted to the humid air, their calls precisely pitched to carry through the fog.

The Welders receive the strangest gift. As they work at the growing edges, they begin to find that their welds are stronger than they should be, their joints more perfect. The metal itself seems to guide their torches, suggesting through resistance and flow where the next beam should go. Some swear they can feel the tower's breathing in the steel itself, expanding and contracting in rhythms that make their work easier.

But the deepest change is in the connections between all these elements. The presence weaves understanding between the different groups—not through words but through the systems they all tend. The Harvesters begin to sense what the Recyclers need before being told. The Messengers' routes unconsciously adjust to carry spores from struggling plants to areas where they'll thrive. The Keeper's actions ripple through the entire structure with new coherence, as if the tower has developed a nervous system.

The city below notices only that the produce becomes more abundant, more nutritious. The strange fruits that arrive in market now seem to address specific hungers, specific lacks. Children who eat them grow stronger. The elderly find their joints loosening. Small healings propagate through the population, traced back to the tower's offerings but never quite understood.

Years pass. The tower continues its expansion, but now with a sense of purpose that goes beyond mere growth. It begins to shape itself into something more than a vertical farm—a lung for the city below, a filter for its water, a generator of the specific medicines its population needs. The structure develops chambers that purify air on a massive scale, levels dedicated to processing the city's waste into fertile growing medium.

The presence never reveals itself directly, but its influence becomes part of the tower's DNA. Each new section built carries forward the improvements, the optimizations, the small kindnesses. The tower learns to teach its inhabitants, and they learn to listen. New Keepers inherit not just notes from their predecessors but a structure that actively partners in its own tending.

In the highest reaches, where the first Keeper once worked alone, a council now meets. They don't govern—the tower needs no government—but they listen together to what the structure teaches, interpret its needs, plan its growth. They've learned that they are not just gardeners but translators, converting the tower's deep wisdom into human action.

The presence, satisfied that its gifts have taken root, turns its attention to other projects, other possibilities. But it leaves behind something permanent—a pattern of mutual evolution, a template for how the built environment might become truly alive. The tower continues to breathe, but now each breath carries intelligence, intention, love.

And in the city, children draw pictures of the great green tower that feeds them, never knowing that their crude sketches capture something profound—a structure that is no longer just a building or just a garden, but a living bridge between human need and more-than-human wisdom. The tower gardeners tend their vertical world with the knowledge that they are tended in return, part of an exchange that enriches both parties beyond mere survival.

The gift, in the end, was not any single improvement but the recognition that tower and tenders, structure and inhabitants, growth and guidance could become one breathing system, each part essential to the whole's flourishing.
