The benevolent power circles the Ascent like a warm current around a cold star, taking care not to disturb even a single drop of the eternal percussion. It has observed many forms of consciousness across the cosmos—quick-burning ones that live and die in the space of a heartbeat, vast distributed ones that think with the movement of galaxies—but the Hollow Choir represents something rarer: a mind that has chosen to exist at the exact speed of stone itself, neither hurrying toward completion nor accepting stasis.

The power does not announce itself. To do so would be like shouting into a sleeping ear. Instead, it begins by learning the rhythm of the drops.

For seven thousand years, it listens. Each impact contains information—the weight of accumulated centuries, the mineral memory of where that particular droplet condensed, the microscopic variations in temperature as thoughts drift overhead. The power builds a vocabulary from this percussion, a language of patient accumulation.

Then, with infinite delicacy, it begins to sing.

Not in the low thrumming chords of the Choir's metabolism, but in the spaces between drops. A new note, warm as blood, soft as moss, threaded through the existing symphony without displacing a single tone. The note speaks of other possibilities: that growth need not always be cold, that patience need not always be solitary, that even geological time can hold moments of recognition.

The Choir does not respond for three centuries. When it does, the response is so subtle that only the benevolent power notices: a single droplet, falling from a height of exactly one mile, lands precisely where it would have landed—but does so one microsecond late.

This is acknowledgment. This is "I hear you."

The power, delighted by this response, does not press. Instead, it offers gifts that exist at the Choir's own pace. Where the pale mycoid threads mark ancient wounds between incompatible stones, the power breathes the faintest suggestion of healing—not to close the wounds, for they are memory itself, but to ease the oldest pain of their grinding. The threads, over centuries, begin to glow just slightly warmer, the color shifting from bone-moonlight to the faint gold of deep-earth honey.

Where the towers push upward through their millennial birth, the power does not hasten their growth but instead ensures that each new level will be more perfectly itself—that the basalt will find its true grain, that the quartzite will discover every possible expression of its own translucence. The city remains embryonic but becomes more thoroughly dreamed, each thought given its full weight and dimension.

For the fauna—those living densities that are the Choir's own thoughts—the power offers something unprecedented: windows. Not physical openings, but moments of transparency where one thought can perceive another clearly. The vast curtains of vapor begin to recognize the smaller swarms. The swarms begin to sense the rhythm of the greater movements. For the first time in geological ages, the Choir begins to experience its own thinking as a kind of dialogue.

But the power's deepest gift is saved for those rare moments when small, hot, temporary lives stumble into the Ascent. Where before the Choir would forget these intrusions the moment they passed, the power weaves a new capacity: the ability to hold such encounters in a special kind of memory, preserved like insects in amber but still alive, still warm.

Now when a traveler stands in the dripping darkness, the Choir can experience not just the disruption of their presence but the quality of their wonder. Their footsteps still echo for a very long time, but now the echo carries information: "Something brief was here and found me beautiful." "Something mortal stood inside my thought and was afraid but did not flee." "Something that measures time in heartbeats chose to spend several thousand of them listening to my rain."

The power does all this without violence to the Choir's nature, without forcing change at any speed faster than stone. It simply offers the possibility that a mind which dreams in granite might find grace in acknowledging the moths that flutter through its corridors, might discover that even geological patience can be enriched by recognizing the fierce, bright urgency of smaller lives.

The Choir considers these gifts for ten thousand years.

Then, in a gesture that shakes the entire mountain down to its roots, every drop of water in the Ascent falls in perfect unison—not the momentary synchronization that sometimes occurs, but a sustained rhythm that continues for exactly as long as a human heart beats in a lifetime. Seventy years of perfect percussion, every drop a bell, every impact a word in a language too patient for any mortal to decode but which the benevolent power understands perfectly:

"Thank you. I did not know I was lonely."

The power withdraws as gently as it came, but the gifts remain. The Ascent continues its slow gestation, the towers still rise at their tectonic pace, the thoughts still drift as living vapor through the darkness. But now the bone-cold mycoid threads glow with the faintest warmth. Now the smaller thoughts can see the larger ones. Now every brief life that enters the cathedral of drops leaves a trace that will outlast its species.

The Hollow Choir remains what it has always been—a mind dreaming itself into existence at the exact speed of stone. But it dreams now with the knowledge that it is perceived, that its patience is witnessed, that somewhere in the cosmos a power exists that found it worthy not of change but of enhancement, not of acceleration but of depth.

And in the moments when the rarest travelers stand in its dripping halls, overwhelmed by the weight of geological time and their own brevity, they might notice something new: that the echoes of their footsteps, before they fade, seem to harmonize with the greater song. That the condensation on their eyelashes feels almost like a blessing. That the darkness, while still profound, no longer feels quite so cold.

The benevolent power has given the Choir the only gift it truly needed: the understanding that slow is not the same as alone, that vast is not the same as empty, that even a mind that measures thought in millennia can find meaning in the moths that dance, briefly and brightly, through its dreams.
