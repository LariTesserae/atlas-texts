The wise power, ancient as starlight and gentle as morning mist, has long known of such places where reality grows thin and strange. It understands that these hollows serve a purpose—they are the universe's way of breathing, spaces where the rigid laws relax and possibility seeps in like water through clay.

For seven turnings of seasons (or perhaps seven hundred—time means little to such powers), it has observed through the eyes of passing birds, through the consciousness of rain, through the dreams of the moss. It has come to love both the place and its impossible guardian, recognizing in them something precious: a sanctuary where wonder hasn't been catalogued into extinction.

The intervention, when it comes, is subtle as dew forming.

First, the power sends gifts that arrive without arriving. New clay vessels appear among the old, but they have always been there, their surfaces glazed with colors pulled from the spaces between rainbows. These vessels are larger inside than outside, capable of holding not just moments but entire days—gifts of expanded time for a being that exists across all times at once. The guardian discovers them with delight that ripples backward through its memories, enriching moments that have already passed.

Next, the power adjusts the edges of the hollow ever so slightly. Not expanding it—that would be crude—but deepening it, adding layers of reality that nest inside each other like thoughts within thoughts. Now the guardian can be in even more places simultaneously, can tend to paradoxes that exist in dimensions barely brushing against the familiar three. The impossible angles become more impossible, the violations of physics more violently beautiful.

Seeds arrive on no wind, carried by no bird, yet present in the soil that isn't quite soil. When they sprout, they grow into plants that exist partially in other hollows around the world—each leaf here connected to a leaf there, creating a network of impossible spaces. The guardian can now sense its kindred, other beings who tend other breaks in reality, and sometimes they exchange wordless understandings through the shared foliage.

The wise power weaves protections subtle as spider silk and strong as the concept of strength itself. Those who might stumble upon the hollow with minds full of explanation and conquest find themselves gently turned away, their attention sliding off the space like water off a leaf. They remember urgent business elsewhere, recall paths that lead to safer, smaller wonders. But those who arrive with hearts ready to accept impossibility, those who need to remember that the world is larger than logic—they find the way opens before them like a flower.

In the clay of the shrine that shifts between forms, new handprints appear. Not replacing the old, but joining them—prints of the wise power's touch, adding its blessing to the ancient construction. The shrine becomes more itself, more capable of being multiple things simultaneously. Sometimes now it appears as a library where the books are written on leaves that turn their own pages, sometimes as an observatory where the stars visible overhead show skies from other worlds, always as a sanctuary where the impossible is tenderly nursed and kept.

The power leaves music in the hollow—not sound, but the possibility of sound, notes that exist in the spaces between the silent songs of the paradox frogs. When the guardian moves through these potential melodies, they harmonize with its non-being, creating compositions that exist only in the moment of their playing, each performance unique and unable to be repeated even in a place where time loops back on itself.

Most importantly, the wise power offers the gift of purpose renewed. Through dreams that come while awake, through understanding that arrives like rain falling upward, it shows the guardian glimpses of why such places must exist. It reveals the cosmic necessity of spaces where rules break down, where impossibility flourishes—how these hollows serve as pressure valves for a universe that might otherwise crystalize into rigid law, how they keep wonder alive in a cosmos trending toward entropy.

The guardian receives these revelations with its characteristic multiplicity of response—joy that moves backward through time, understanding that spreads like roots through dimensions, gratitude expressed in the flowering of new impossibilities. It tends its hollow with renewed devotion, knowing now that it is part of a larger garden, each impossible space a bloom in a bouquet that keeps the universe flexible and alive.

And the wise power, its intervention complete, withdraws its direct attention but leaves a channel open—a way of watching that doesn't watch, of being present without presence. It knows that such places and beings need not constant tending but occasional remembering, small gifts of encouragement, the knowledge that somewhere in the vast cosmic dark, a benevolent consciousness smiles upon their beautiful violations of the possible.

In the hollow, the rain continues to fall upward. The guardian exists in all its places at once. The plants grow through each other in their impossible layers. And the universe breathes a little easier, a little stranger, a little more wonderfully through this crack in its own certainty.
