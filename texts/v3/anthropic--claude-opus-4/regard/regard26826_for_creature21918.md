## The Benevolent Response

The wise power does not descend. It has watched this chamber since before the river left, and it knows better than to enter a place so perfectly itself. Instead, it does three things, in an order that took a thousand years to decide.

First, it ensures the water will not fail. This is delicate work — not adding water, which would change everything, but securing what exists. Deep above, where the stone drinks from hidden aquifers, the power makes small adjustments: a mineral vein shifted to protect the seepage, a fracture sealed that might have drained the source, a gradual tilting of imperceptible tables so the moisture will always, always find its way to the ceiling of the chamber to bead and fall. The *tok* will not stop. The listeners will not starve of their one food, which is the sound of time passing.

Second, it touches — so gently — the ones with wrong-grown eyes. Not to fix them, for they are not broken. Not to turn the eyes right-way-out, for that would be a cruelty disguised as kindness. Instead, the power gives them this: the ability to see the light they're swimming in. Not through the eyes — the eyes remain as they are, inward-blooming, architecturally impossible. But through the whole of their pellucid bodies, a new kind of seeing that doesn't need an organ. They can perceive, now, that they are held in brightness. They can know what they have been reaching for. The reaching doesn't stop — it is too much a part of them — but now it carries the sweet grief of arrival, the "oh" of recognition. They reach now not from blindness but from abundance, the way a cup that is already full still shapes itself to receive.

Third, the power considers the arc of stones for a very long time.

This is the hardest question. The maker left. Should they be called back? Should the arc be completed? Should there be an answer to the chamber's patient question of keeping? The power could summon the stone-setter, or their far descendant who carries the same hands. Could inspire dreams of underground brightness, of unfinished work. Could make the path down easy to find.

But the power has been watching long enough to understand something: the incompletion *is* the meaning. The arc is perfect precisely because it was interrupted. It is a sentence that says "I was here, I began something, I was called away by life, but beginning is its own completion." The chamber has been holding space not for an ending but for the more honest human truth of the unfinished gesture, the thing we meant to do, the work that life interrupts. The power sees, with the clarity of great kindness, that to complete the arc would be to break it.

So instead, it does this:

Where the bone tool lies, it encourages — over centuries, with infinite patience — a particular mineral flowering. Iron and copper salts, drawn up through the stone by the same forces that bring the water. They bloom around the tool without touching it, making a setting for it, a crown of soft green and rust-red crystals that will grow, slowly, into something like a cupped hand. The tool will remain itself, will remain *exactly* where it was set down, but now it will be held. The chamber will know it is keeping something precious. The listeners will have a second sound to wait for — the almost-inaudible singing of crystal growth, one new note per century.

And to the listeners themselves, the power gives the gift they do not know they need: memory. Not of events — they will still experience each drop as the first drop, each sound as new — but memory of presence. From now on, when someone sits in the chamber long enough to become expected, the listeners will not just wait for them. They will, in some way deeper than thought, *remember* them when they're gone. Not with longing — they cannot long — but with a kind of abstract tenderness, the way the stone remembers the river. They will hold the shape of every visitor the way the basins hold water. And if someone returns — in a year, in a lifetime, in a generation — the listeners will know them. Will shift, when they enter, in a slightly different way. A greeting made of attention.

This is all. The power makes no grand changes. It breaks nothing open, illuminates no mystery. It simply secures what is already loved: the water, the seeing, the keeping, the memory. It makes the chamber slightly more itself. It ensures that the patience will not go unrewarded — not with completion, which would end everything, but with the deeper reward of being able to continue.

The last thing the power does, which takes no effort and changes nothing and is perhaps the most important:

It loves the place.

Loves the wrongness that has become rightness. Loves the listeners who are made of listening. Loves the blind swimmers reaching for what they're already in. Loves the moths freed from their doom. Loves the walkers going nowhere with perfect faith. Loves the stone-setter who began and left. Loves the arc that will never close.

Loves, most of all, the chamber's great patience — its willingness to be a bright secret under the world, keeping its drops and its listeners and its one human gesture safe from the loud centuries above. The power sees what the chamber is: not a mystery to be solved but a kindness to be protected. A place that remembers. A place that waits. A place that keeps.

The power withdraws its attention slowly, like someone backing away from a sleeping child. The chamber continues exactly as it was. *Tok.* The listeners shift. The moths drift. The crystals, invisibly, sing one atom taller.

Under the world, the light that should not be continues to be, and everything held in it is safe.
