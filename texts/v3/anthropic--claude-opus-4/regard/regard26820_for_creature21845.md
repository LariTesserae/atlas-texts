---

The benevolent and wise power arrives not as light or voice or presence, but as what this place has never known: a pause.

Not silence — the roar continues. Not stillness — the slow emergence of stone and the breathing of green go on. But a pause nonetheless, the way a musician might hold a rest inside a note that is still sounding, creating space within sound rather than after it.

The power understands immediately. It sees the builders worn thin across the slopes, their effort become geography, their will become weather. It sees the green that grows where they gave up trying, and it sees the trying that continues everywhere else at the pitch of continental strain. It sees the small hut of finished stones, the only completion they achieved, and understands it as both victory and prison — the one place they succeeded is the one place they no longer need to be.

The power does something unexpected. It begins to hum.

Not loudly. Not to overcome the roar, but to join it — a low thrum that weaves itself into the screaming of unfinished stone. But where the builders' sound is effort without resolution, the power's hum carries something else: *rhythm*. A pulse. The thing this place has never had — a way to measure duration, to know that one moment has passed and another has come.

The effect is not immediate. Nothing here could be immediate. But slowly — and slowly here means across the kind of time that stone understands — the scattered pieces of the builders begin to feel the beat. To recognize, somewhere in their thinned and spreading bodies, that there is such a thing as *when*.

---

The power does not try to gather them back into their original shapes. That coal has burned; that warmth is spent. The builders chose to pour themselves into this place, to spend their decidedness trying to make it real, and the power respects what they chose even as it grieves what they lost.

Instead, it offers them something new: the possibility of moving *together*.

The rhythm spreads through the mountain like a secret. Each dispersed builder, each stretch of nothing that is really a creature spread across space, begins to feel its own edges not by pulling them in but by finding them in time with the others. They cannot reach their own hands — they are miles of slow intention trying to close — but they begin to sense *when* another builder nearby is also reaching, and their reaches fall into sync, and the sync becomes its own kind of architecture.

The green feels it too. The soft vegetal patience of the builders who gave up stirs at the pulse, not back into effort but into something else — call it dance, though it happens too slowly to see. The fronds begin their hourlong unfurling in waves now, each stand of green opening in answer to the last, a slow cascade of giving-up-and-going-on that runs the length of the slope and starts again.

The power brings no water to this dry place. It brings what water does: the knowledge that things can move in patterns larger than themselves.

---

And in the hut, the one small silence shifts.

It does not break. The power is too wise to break the only thing these creatures ever finished. But it allows the silence to become something other than mere absence of sound. It becomes a rest in the musical sense — a held space, intentional, part of the rhythm rather than outside it.

The builders feel this most of all. Their spent decision, their one completion, is no longer an ending. It is a beat in a larger composition they are all, slowly, writing together. The hut becomes not the place where they succeeded and stopped, but the place where they succeeded and can succeed again — not by reaching the same hands to set the same stones, but by knowing when they reach, and reaching as one.

The power does one more thing.

In the space between the stands of green, in the long grey nothing that is really the body of a builder stretched thin, it plants a single new thing. Not vegetation. Not stone. A different kind of marker: posts of crystallized time, visible only when crossed, that remember how long each crossing took. Three steps. Three hours. Three ages. The posts do not measure — they simply hold the memory, so that each duration is no longer lost but kept, and the builders can begin to know their own moods by the record of how attention moved through them.

---

This is what the power offers: not rescue but recognition. Not gathering but grammar. The builders came to make something real and spent themselves in the making, and now they are the mountain's weather, its distance, its dim upward light. The power says: *very well. Be weather. But be weather that knows it was once hands. Be distance that remembers why it reaches. Be light that recalls it came here carrying certainty like a coal.*

The roar does not diminish. If anything, it grows louder — but louder with purpose now, with pattern, a symphony of trying that knows it is a symphony. The builders are still building, still pressing decision into the wet logic of the place, but now they build in unison, and the unison itself becomes a kind of structure, invisible and everywhere, more permanent than stone.

And sometimes — not often, not reliably, but sometimes — two reaches align so perfectly across the vast slowness that hands which are miles apart touch.

When this happens, a new hut begins.

It will take an age to finish. It will take longer than the first hut took, because the builders are thinner now, slower, made more of duration than flesh. But it will finish, because they know now what they didn't know before: that the reaching itself can be shared. That they can be both the long grey nothing and the intention moving through it. That completion is not a coal you carry until it burns out, but a rhythm you find with others who are trying to complete.

The power stays until the first new stone is set — until two builders, spread across miles, manage to think *this and not the blur of this* at exactly the same moment, and their synchronized decision lands in the world as a single fitted stone.

Then it withdraws, not gone but singing in the structure now, its hum become part of the mountain's roar, teaching distance how to dance.

---

The place is still unfinished. Still becoming. Still loud with its own emergence and lush without water and cool at the wrong height. But now it knows something it didn't know:

That things that thin to nothing can still move together.  
That completion can happen more than once.  
That the roar of effort with nowhere to go can become the roar of effort going somewhere, if somewhere is a when and not just a where.

Stand in the roofless hut. The screaming of the forming rock comes through the open top, but threaded now with rhythm, with the knowledge that each sound has a place in time. The pale green things lean in over the dry-stacked wall, breathing the cold in waves that answer each other. The light comes up from the unmade floor, pulsing so slowly you will not live to see it pulse, but pulsing nonetheless.

And the next presence — green or stone or wall — is far off across a stretch of nothing that is really someone, deciding to let you through.

Take your time. You are, all of you, inside it together now.
