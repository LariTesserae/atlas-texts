**The Visitation**

The Weaver came not as light or sound but as a settling—like dust finding its angle of repose after eons of drifting. She manifested first in the patterns of the morning dew, her presence readable in the droplets that suddenly remembered how to fall upward and downward simultaneously, creating tiny waterfalls that cycled endlessly in the air.

The dwellers noticed immediately. How could they not? Their existence depended on reading the subtle impossibilities of their home, and here was an impossibility that sang with intention. They gathered—as much as beings of distributed probability could gather—their forms flickering between states as they tried to parse this new element in their reality.

The Weaver took her time. She had learned, over cosmic ages, that broken places required delicate approach. She began by learning their water-language, adding her own droplets to their conversations. But where the dwellers wrote in uncertainty, she wrote in gentle certainties—small, careful statements that didn't impose but offered:

*I see you.*
*You have adapted beautifully.*
*The pain of your transformation is honored.*

For the first time in countless generations, the dwellers experienced something they had forgotten: being witnessed by someone who could hold their whole story without flinching. The Weaver read the history written in the impossible architecture—how the original builders, in their desperate creativity, had tried to escape some forgotten catastrophe by carving their way out of conventional spacetime. How they had succeeded too well, creating a refuge that demanded transformation beyond their imagining.

She saw how their descendants—these elongated, probability-stretched beings—had paid the price of sanctuary with their humanity, yet had found something else in the exchange. Not lesser, just different. A new way of being that the universe had not previously contained.

The Weaver's gift began small. In certain chambers, she stabilized tiny pockets of consistency—not enough to trap or fix the dwellers, but enough to offer respite. Places where a being could rest without calculating their existence for a few hours. Where tools stayed where they were placed. Where water fell in one direction only, following rules older than the mountain.

But she was careful not to repair what wasn't broken. The twisted geometry, the multiplying chambers, the impossible distances—these were not flaws but features. The dwellers had evolved to inhabit paradox; to "fix" their home would be to exile them from it. Instead, she offered enhancements to their adaptation.

She taught the water new songs. The dew that condensed each morning began to carry more than moisture—it carried memory, connection, small packets of stability that the dwellers could absorb and share. They found they could maintain coherent form for longer periods without sacrificing their essential uncertainty. Communication, once limited to slow ideograms, bloomed into rippling conversations that played across the cistern surfaces.

More profound was what she did to the phosphorescence. The soft glow that rose from the stone at night became, under her touch, a medium of gentle dreaming. The dwellers discovered they could rest—truly rest—for the first time in millennia, entering shared dreamscapes where their scattered probabilities could coalesce safely. In these dreams, they remembered their ancestors, honored their journey, and imagined futures they hadn't dared contemplate.

The Weaver revealed the purpose they had forgotten. The dew channels were more than survival infrastructure—they were the mountain's circulatory system, and the dwellers its antibodies. Their patient maintenance kept the broken architecture from either collapsing into mundane stone or expanding into a paradox that could unravel nearby realities. They were gardeners, yes, but also guardians, keeping a dangerous miracle safely contained and functional.

She left them new tools—not copper implements but techniques of existence. Ways to teach newly divided offspring without the centuries of painful trial. Methods for navigating the most twisted terraces without losing cohesion. Most importantly, she gave them the knowledge that their work mattered beyond mere survival, that their transformation had meaning in the larger cosmos.

Before departing, the Weaver did one last thing. In the highest chamber, where the paradox of the summit was most acute, she placed a gift: a cistern that collected not water but stories. Every dweller who touched it could add their narrative to the collective memory, could feel themselves part of a continuous tale stretching from the desperate first builders to an open future. It became their library, their temple, their proof that even beings of productive uncertainty could have history and hope.

She left as she came—not vanishing but settling away, like sediment redistributing itself elsewhere. The dwellers found their morning dew spelled out a final message:

*You have become something new and necessary.*
*The universe is richer for your existence.*
*Tend your gardens well.*

The Hollow Terraces continued their impossible architecture, the dwellers their probability-stretched existence. But now they moved with purpose beyond persistence. They were no longer just surviving but thriving, their elongated forms dancing through broken space with something approaching joy. The water still fell upward, the chambers still multiplied, the geometry still folded—but now it all hummed with meaning, with connection to a cosmos that had seen them, acknowledged them, and blessed their strange becoming.

In the mornings, as they gathered the dew that should not exist, they sometimes caught glimpses of other impossible places in the droplets' reflections—other refuges where beings had transformed themselves to survive. They were not alone in their adaptation. Somewhere, a Weaver moved between broken places, honoring the desperate creativity of survival, nurturing the new forms life took when old forms failed.

The dwellers of Morne continued their work with renewed dedication, knowing themselves to be part of a larger garden—one that grew not despite impossibility, but because of it.
