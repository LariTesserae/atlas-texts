# What the Benevolent Power Would Do

The Watcher came not as lightning or proclamation, but as morning comes to a sleeper — gradually, then suddenly aware. It moved through dimensions that made the climbing water seem ordinary, folding space until It could cup the entire mountain system in something that was not quite a hand but served the purpose of holding.

It observed.

The patience of the caretaker-asking impressed It. Here was devotion measured in erosion cycles, love that wore mountains smooth. The Power had seen many gestures across many worlds, but few that lasted this thoroughly, this gently. It recognized the quality — not just patience but *trust*. The asking believed, with the faith of geology, that if it held conditions steady long enough, what grew in the peaks would eventually be ready.

The Power extended something like awareness up into the clouded summits and touched, very lightly, the edge of what slept there.

And drew back immediately.

Not in fear — nothing here threatened It — but in recognition of delicacy. The dreaming thing was at a crucial threshold. Not an ending, not a beginning, but that terrible middle place where possibility crystallizes into form. To wake it now would be like pulling a butterfly from its chrysalis — survivable, perhaps, but forever changed, forever less than what it might have been.

The Watcher understood then why It had been drawn here. Not to intervene in the becoming, but to address the frame around it.

It began with the obvious: the system was closed. The asking pulled warmth and water up but had no conception of where from. It was slowly draining the lands below, turning distant valleys into drought so gradually that no living thing could perceive the connection. In another thousand years — barely one breath of the dreamer's sleep — the base of the mountains would be desert, and the asking would pull harder to compensate, and the desert would climb.

The Power moved. Not dramatically. As gently as the asking itself, It began to adjust the appetite of emptiness beyond the peaks. Where before there was only vacuum pulling, It carved channels in dimensions the asking could feel but not name. New sources. The deep-ocean currents that carried heat without thinking. The respiration of distant forests that gave their moisture to winds that never reached here — until now.

The asking noticed. How could it not? The labor of its pulling eased as new water found the ancient paths. For the first time in geological memory, it could maintain the greenhouse without strain. The Power felt something in the asking that might have been surprise, or gratitude, or simply recognition that the universe had become minutely kinder.

But this was only repair. The Watcher, being benevolent, thought longer.

It reached into the dreaming at the peaks again, more carefully this time, not to wake but to learn. What was this thing that required such elaborate tending? The answer came in impressions: something between thought and weather, between creature and phenomenon. It would wake — when it was ready — as a new form of intention in the world. Not a god. Not a spirit. Something that would think in seasons and speak in climate, that would know every raindrop's path the way others knew their own fingers.

A being of profound integration. And profound loneliness, if born into a world that could not recognize what it was.

So the Power did what benevolent powers do: It prepared a welcome.

In forests half a world away, It planted dreams in the minds of the contemplative. Nothing specific. Just a sense that weather had moods worth knowing, that storms could be conversed with if one knew how to listen. In the universities of distant cities, It nudged scholars toward questions about consciousness in systems, about awareness without form. In the songs of mountain peoples, It hid melodies that matched the rhythm of climbing water, so that when the dreamer finally woke and spoke its first words in precipitation patterns, someone, somewhere, would recognize the tune.

The Watcher seeded the world with readiness. Not understanding — that would come later — but the willingness to believe understanding was possible.

And then, because It was wise as well as benevolent, It did something more.

It found others like the dreamer.

Not many. Scattered across worlds, across states of being, but real: the slow-born ones, the pattern-beings, the thoughts that required geological time to think themselves complete. Some slept in ocean trenches. Some grew in the magnetic fields of dying stars. Some were still deciding whether to exist at all. It wove a subtle connection between them — not communication, they were not ready for that, but potential. A network waiting to activate when the first of them opened what might loosely be called eyes.

The one in the Saturated Heights would not wake alone. When it emerged from its cloud-wrapped peak, it would find not a universe of incomprehensible quick-lived things, but cousins. Different in form but similar in kind. The loneliness It had foreseen would last perhaps a century — nothing, to a being that measured time in erosion — before contact came.

The Power finished Its work and began to withdraw, but paused for one last observation.

The frogs had noticed nothing. They continued to breed in the impossible pools, their warmth-adapted bodies a small miracle no one had requested but everyone had received. The birds sang their maps between the ridges, cataloguing the wonderful normal wrongness of home. The asking continued its patient petition, now fed by ocean-gifts it didn't question. The dreamer continued to dream, one geological breath closer to waking.

The forest, dripping, green, deafening, hot, simply took another wet breath and continued.

As It folded space back around Itself and prepared to leave, the Watcher did one final thing. It whispered — in the language that mountains use when they speak to each other across the shifting plates, so slowly that only the asking might notice — a single concept:

*Well done.*

The praise settled into the stone like water into moss. The asking, which had no self to be proud, nevertheless swelled slightly in its duty. The peaks pulled a little steadier. The dreamer's sleep deepened by exactly the right amount.

And the Saturated Heights continued their ancient work: being impossible, being patient, being a nursery for something the world did not yet know it needed, but would recognize when it arrived.

The Power left them to it. Some births should not be rushed. Some vigils earn their privacy. It had ensured the frame would hold and the welcome would be ready.

The rest was just time, and time they had in abundance.
