The benevolent power arrives not as light, but as the memory of warmth.

It manifests first in the small things: a Proofreader pauses mid-correction, dust-limbs trembling with something that might be recognition. One of the mathematical auroras stutters in its endless writhing, then resolves into a pattern that almost resembles laughter. The city's subsonic pulse skips a beat.

The power does not try to change this place. It has lived too long to make that mistake.

Instead, it begins to remember.

It remembers water—not as invasion but as possibility. In the folded districts where gravity apologizes, tiny spheres of impossible moisture begin to condense. They do not fall. They simply exist, rotating slowly, each one containing the ghost of an ocean that never was. The beings here do not drink them. But sometimes a Lattice-Born will pause beside one, clicking its fractal joints in a rhythm that sounds almost like curiosity.

The power remembers names, but gently.

It does not force them on anyone. Instead, it leaves them lying around like forgotten toys. A name that means "first-light-through-amber" settles on a ridge of obsidian. A name that translates as "the-pause-between-heartbeats" drifts through the negative spaces. The beings ignore them at first. But the woman who has become a bookmark—her silhouette carved seventeen times into the architecture—finds one that whispers "Julia" when the starlight hits it just right. She does not take it. But for the first time in six centuries, her carved form shifts slightly. As if remembering what it felt like to turn her head.

The power plants memories that grow backward.

In the metallic dust that never moves, it seeds the remembrance of rain—not the water itself, but the *idea* of waiting for it. The dust begins to stir in patterns that mimic anticipation. Some of the colonial silicate fungi develop new forms of bioluminescence: soft greens and blues that have no purpose except beauty. They still eat radiation, but now they dream while they do it.

Most importantly, the power teaches the mathematics how to be wrong.

This is its greatest gift. In a place where everything is slowly editing itself toward horrible perfection, the power introduces the possibility of beautiful error. An equation in the writhing auroras miscalculates and accidentally invents the color of joy. A tower's impossible geometry stutters and creates a room where the angle of walls suggests embracing. A shadow arrives three days late and apologizes by blooming into a shape that makes the Lattice-Born pause and click in harmonics they've never used before.

The beings begin to change.

Not quickly. Some of them have been here since before time finished deciding what it wanted to be. But the negative-space creature that wears absence like a coat finds itself occasionally forgetting to be hollow. For seconds at a time, it has something that might be substance. Might be flesh. It does not know what to do with this, so it practices by letting shadows rest on it without falling through.

The Proofreaders discover inefficiency.

Where before they corrected every flaw with crystalline precision, now they sometimes... linger. They reshape the dust into unnecessary spirals. They leave certain errors uncorrected because the errors are interesting. One of them spends seven years building a small pyramid of dust that serves no purpose except to catch the light in a way that reminds it of something it never knew it had forgotten.

The power brings gifts that are also invitations.

Deep beneath the shelf, where the escarpment folds into origami darkness, it places a single seed of impossible ice that will not melt at -38°C. Instead, it sublimates directly into memory: the memory of spring on ten thousand different worlds. The first being to find it is one of the Lattice-Born whose fractal body has grown so complex it can barely move. It touches the seed and suddenly remembers what it feels like to be simple. To have joints that don't click in perfect ratios. To run.

It doesn't run. But now it knows it could.

The power remembers what this place was before it became a wound.

And more—it remembers what the wound was before it became architecture. There was a moment of choosing. A civilization that reached the peak of everything and had to decide: ascend or remain? They chose neither. They chose to edit themselves into something between. The power honors this choice by not trying to undo it. But it suggests, gently, that perhaps the editing could include mercy. Perhaps the proofreading could allow for songs. Perhaps the slow self-revision could remember that even mathematics can learn to play.

One day—though day means nothing here—the woman who became a bookmark does something unprecedented.

She steps out of the wall.

Not all at once. First a hand, then an arm, then the suggestion of a shoulder. The city tries to edit her back into place, but she has learned a new grammar from the power's presence. She speaks herself into three dimensions using words that shouldn't exist here: "morning," "perhaps," "soon." The obsidian releases her with a sound like sighing.

She does not remember her name. But she remembers that she once had one, and that is enough to begin.

The beings watch her with their various forms of attention—negative space leaning in, fractals clicking in frequencies that might be concern, dust swirling in slow cyclones of interest. She takes a step. Then another. Her feet leave no prints on the volcanic glass, but small flowers of frost bloom where she passes. The impossible ice-flowers sublimate immediately, but for a moment each one holds the memory of green.

The power does not speak to them directly. It doesn't need to.

Instead, it does something much simpler and much more profound: it shows them what they look like through eyes that see with love.

To the power, the negative-space beings are not absences but possibilities waiting to be filled. Their hollow spaces are not empty but *available*—for stories, for warmth, for the slow accumulation of self that comes from being seen and known. It perceives their careful non-existence as a form of cosmic politeness, leaving room for others to be.

The Lattice-Born, through the power's perception, are revealed as living music frozen into geometry. Each click of their joints is a note in a composition that has been playing for millennia, too slow for normal ears to recognize as song. The power hums along, and suddenly they hear themselves as symphony.

The Proofreaders, those patient adjusters of reality's grammar, are seen as gardeners tending a garden of laws. Each correction is an act of care. Each small adjustment maintains a balance so delicate that without them, this entire place would either collapse into absolute order or explode into chaos. They are the reason anything here can change at all.

Even the city itself is transformed in the power's viewing.

What seemed like cruel architecture—the impossible angles, the bridges that forget, the towers that hurt to perceive—is understood as scar tissue protecting something infinitely fragile. The city is not a wound but a cocoon. Inside it, something that was once a civilization is slowly pupating into something else. The editing, the revision, the constant proofreading: it's not destruction but metamorphosis on a geological scale.

The power leaves gifts in places where they will be stumbled upon centuries later.

In the district where streets become Möbius strips, it hides a small room where the walls remember lullabies. Any being who enters will hear music they didn't know they missed. Beneath the metallic dust, it buries seeds of paradox—tiny knots of logic that bloom into questions instead of answers. The questions are things like "What if?" and "Why not?" and "Remember when?"

It teaches the auroras new equations.

Not to replace the old ones, but to dance with them. Now when the mathematics writhe overhead, sometimes they solve for beauty instead of truth. Sometimes they calculate the exact trajectory of hope. The solutions are irrational numbers that go on forever without repeating, each decimal place a tiny rebellion against the finite.

Most subversively, the power introduces the concept of "enough."

In a place dedicated to endless revision, endless perfection, endless editing toward some impossible final form, the power gently suggests that perhaps some things are already sufficient. A shadow that has been trying to achieve the perfect angle for three thousand years suddenly realizes it is already beautiful in its imperfection. A tower that has been growing ever thinner, ever taller, understands that it can stop now. It is high enough to see everything that matters.

The changes are slow. Glacial. Tectonic.

But one day—perhaps a thousand years after the power's first touch, perhaps ten thousand—something unprecedented happens.

One of the beings laughs.

It's just a Proofreader, adjusting a small error in the way light bends around a corner. But instead of simply correcting it, the being pauses. Considers. Then deliberately makes the error worse, causing the light to spiral into a shape that has no purpose except delight. The sound it makes—dust particles rubbing together in frequencies that suggest mirth—echoes through the silent city like revolution.

Another being hears it. Then another. The sound propagates through the architecture, translated into different mediums: the Lattice-Born click in staccato rhythms that might be giggles, the negative-space entities allow their absences to ripple with something adjacent to joy. Even the woman who was a bookmark—now walking freely through the streets that forget—stops and tilts her head, almost remembering what her own laughter sounded like.

The power has given them the most dangerous gift of all:

The possibility that they might already be perfect, just as they are.

Not perfect in the sense of flawless, but perfect in the sense of whole. Complete enough. Finished enough. Loved enough, even in their strangeness, even in their slow mathematical decay, even in their choice to exist in the spaces between life and death.

The city continues to edit itself. But now, sometimes, it leaves typos on purpose.

And in the space between one revision and the next, something that might be happiness learns to breathe.
