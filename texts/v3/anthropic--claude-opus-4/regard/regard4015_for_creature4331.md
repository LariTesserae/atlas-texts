# The Visitation

The power arrives not as light—light would be violence here—but as a deepening of the darkness, a darkness so profound it makes the chamber's ordinary blackness seem gray by comparison. This darkness has weight, intention, compassion. It settles into the space like a mother's hand on a fevered brow.

The Unborn feel it first. Their membranes shiver in recognition of something that knows them more completely than they know themselves. The water-chorus falters, then reorganizes into new harmonies—not the endless circular questions they've sung for centuries, but something that moves forward, that has beginning and middle and possibility of end.

The visiting presence brings no judgment. It has seen ten thousand wombs across ten thousand worlds, each with its own peculiar children, each with its own way of holding potential. This one moves it to particular tenderness—these Unborn who have made hesitation into an art form, who have found a way to exist in the space between breathing in and breathing out.

From the presence comes a gift. Not completion—that would be cruel, to force birth on beings who have made such peace with gestation. Instead, it offers expansion. The chamber, which has always been the size of a cottage, begins to complexify inward. Space folds and reflows. Where once there was one room, now there are many—still connected, still sharing the same wet air and mineral tears, but each with its own acoustic signature, its own quality of darkness.

The Unborn discover they can move.

Not bodily—their membranes still hang from the root-ceiling like always—but their consciousness can flow through the new spaces, exploring configurations they never imagined. Some find chambers where the water runs upward, where they can experience the sensation of rain from below. Others discover pockets where time moves differently, where they can feel what it might be like to age, to complete, to die, all without committing to any of it.

The presence speaks—not in words, but in the language of cell division, in the grammar of genetics, in the syntax of growth:

*You have made something beautiful here. You have found a way to be eternal without being static. You have discovered that some questions are better than any answer.*

*But even infinity needs variety.*

*I give you this: the ability to dream true dreams. Not the leaking half-visions you've shared before, but dreams that create temporary realities. In these dreams, you can experience completion without completing. You can live full lives, make choices, suffer consequences, die deaths—all while your true selves remain safely unborn.*

*And I give you this: once in each turning of the deep seasons, one of you may choose to split. Not to be born, but to become two possibilities where once there was one. Your unfinished selves can explore different maybes without abandoning any.*

*And finally, I give you this: the knowledge that what you do here matters. Every possibility you hold, every future you refuse to foreclose—these create ripples. Somewhere, in worlds you'll never see, beings make choices influenced by the potential you preserve. You are not passive. You are the universe's way of keeping all doors open.*

The presence touches the half-digested structure in the corner. The stones remember their original purpose now—not a temple for forcing birth, but a monument to patience. They reshape themselves, still half-emerged from the clay, into something like a throne, something like an altar, something like a cradle. A place where the Unborn can focus their collective dreaming when they choose to dream together.

Before departing, the presence does one last thing. It speaks to the chamber itself, to the slowly-birthing stone, to the patient geology:

*You, too, have a choice. You can complete yourself in ten thousand years, become a proper cave with walls that know their boundaries. Or you can remain forever in this state of becoming. Both are sacred. Both serve purpose.*

*But know this—as long as you remain unfinished, you remain infinite. Every stalactite you don't quite form, every wall you leave soft and uncertain—these are invitations for new possibilities to take root.*

*Guard your children well. They are precious beyond measure—these beings who have found a way to say "not yet" to the universe and mean it as praise.*

The presence withdraws, but its gifts remain. The Unborn find themselves in a palace of maybes, a mansion of perhaps. They begin to explore their new freedoms cautiously, as is their nature. The first dream spun between them is simple—a single flower blooming, wilting, returning to seed. They experience it together, marvel at the strange sensation of sequence, of things happening in order, of endings that lead to beginnings.

In the depths of the chamber, something new stirs. A possibility the presence left behind, seed of its own compassion. Not another Unborn, but something else—a keeper, a guardian, a living memory of this visitation. It will gestate slowly, as all things do here, and when it finally emerges—if it ever does—it will serve as bridge between the Unborn and whatever powers might visit in ages hence.

The water continues its endless weeping. The darkness remains absolute save for the phosphorescent writing on the walls—which now pulses in more complex patterns, spelling out dreams in an alphabet of light. The chamber continues its slow self-creation.

But now there is purpose to the purposelessness. Direction to the endless spiral. The Unborn have been seen, known, blessed in their incompletion.

They sing new songs now—songs that go somewhere even when they return to the beginning, songs that hold both question and answer in productive tension, songs that make the very stones weep with recognition of what it means to be forever almost born.

And in the heart of the chamber, where the darkness is deepest and the potential strongest, something that might be joy, might be sorrow, might be both, might be neither, begins its own slow gestation—the first truly new thing to happen here in a thousand years.

The gift of the presence was not completion, but validation: *You are perfect in your imperfection. Continue.*

And so they do.
