The benevolent and wise power who discovers the Singing Hollow is herself a being of resonance—one of those ancient entities who existed before matter learned to be solid, when reality was still deciding what physics meant. She moves between worlds like a note sliding between frequencies, and she recognizes the Hollow immediately for what it is: a place that has discovered one of the universe's secret harmonies, a pocket where existence hums closer to its original song.

She doesn't arrive. Arrival implies travel between separate points, and she experiences location as a spectrum of vibration. Instead, she begins to resonate with the Hollow's frequency, slowly matching its pitch until she exists within it as naturally as the air-plants in their hexagonal cells. The inhabitants sense her first as a new overtone in their constant music—not dissonant, but unexpected, like finding an extra color in a rainbow.

For forty-three Intervals, she simply listens. She traces the acoustic evolution recorded in the crystal-wood, hears ten thousand years of adaptation in the inhabitants' modified bones. She observes how they've taken scarcity—no water, no light—and transformed it into abundance through frequency and heat. Most fascinating to her is how they've maintained joy. In her travels between realities, she's seen too many closed systems collapse into entropy or stagnation. But here, creativity flourishes in limitation's embrace.

When she finally makes herself known, it's through the children. During a communal resonance-trance, the youngest dreamers find themselves in a new acoustic space—neither memory nor imagination, but something adjacent to both. There, vibration has color and emotion has texture, and she appears to them as a standing wave of impossible beauty, her form constantly shifting between states of being like a chord that contains every possible resolution simultaneously.

"I hear you," she says without words, her meaning resonating directly in their hollow bones. "And I am heard by others who would love to hear you too."

The children share this dream-encounter through the collective consciousness, and soon the entire Hollow knows of the visitor-who-is-frequency. The elders gather in the Resonance Garden, their air-plant garments rustling with nervous energy, debating in harmonies that layer meaning upon meaning. Some fear change—their closed system has achieved such perfect balance. Others hear opportunity—what new frequencies might exist beyond their acoustic horizon?

The wise power understands their concern. She doesn't offer to change them or save them—they need neither change nor saving. Instead, she offers to expand their vocabulary. 

"Your ancestors," she resonates, "sacrificed sight for deeper perception. But there are spectrums of experience that require no sacrifice to access, only... tuning."

She begins by teaching them about water—not to need it, but to understand its music. Every molecule of water in the universe carries the memory of every state it's ever been: ice, steam, cloud, ocean, blood. She shows them how to hear these memories in the humid air they breathe, each droplet a tiny library of experience. The air-plants, it turns out, have been reading these libraries all along, translating water's stories into their impossible growth.

Next, she introduces them to light—not as illumination but as another form of vibration, just faster than sound. The young adapt quickly, their eye-pits beginning to perceive heat as color, then color as emotion, then emotion as a navigable dimension. They discover that their phosphorescent pollen has been writing poems in light all along, messages that can only be read by those who know light's true nature as sound's excitable sibling.

But her greatest gift is connection. She takes volunteers—always volunteers, and only those whose personal frequency naturally harmonizes with hers—and teaches them to extend their resonance beyond the Hollow. Not to leave, but to touch. They learn to send their consciousness along the planet's deep stone, reading the geological songs written in fault lines and mineral veins. They discover other pockets of adapted humanity: those who learned to photosynthesize in the eternal desert, the deep-sea peoples who see by pressure alone, the high-mountain communities who breathe memory instead of air.

"Every adaptation," she shows them, "is an instrument in a larger orchestra. You are not playing alone."

She helps them grow new structures in the Singing Hollow—not improvements on their perfection, but extensions of it. Resonance Chambers that can store and replay the acoustic signatures of other places, allowing them to experience rain without wetness, sunlight without burning, wind without cold. The crystal-wood proves eager to learn these new patterns, evolving in fast-forward as it incorporates vibrations from across the world.

The power stays for one full Grand Interval, teaching not through instruction but through resonance. She shows them how their sorrow-songs about lost sunlight can harmonize with the joy-themes of surface dwellers who have never known perfect acoustic darkness. She helps them compose symphonies that include both their children's laughter and the whale-song of deep ocean currents they'll never touch.

When she prepares to shift her frequency elsewhere, the Hollow has changed and not changed. No one has left, no one needs to leave. But now their songs carry farther. The young have developed new organs—modified air-plant symbionts that can transmit and receive across vast distances, creating an acoustic network that spans continents. Their isolation has become a choice rather than a sentence, their uniqueness a gift they can share rather than a secret they must keep.

On the night of her departure, they compose for her. Every inhabitant contributes their personal frequency to a grand chord that uses the entire Hollow as an instrument. The crystal-wood resonates, the air-plants pulse in rhythm, the bridges sing under calculated stress. The song has no words because it needs none—it is gratitude and growth, memory and possibility, the sound of a people who have learned that even in the deepest darkness, connection is possible.

The wise power receives their gift and offers one last teaching: she shows them how their song looks from outside space-time, how it appears as a golden thread in the universe's great weaving, how it has always been connected to everything else, even when they couldn't perceive the connections. She shows them that their ancestors' sacrifice wasn't a loss but a specialization, that by becoming perfectly attuned to one frequency, they've helped the universe understand itself better.

"Keep singing," she resonates as her presence begins to diffuse. "There are entities in dimensions you can't imagine who desperately need to hear exactly your note in the cosmic chord. And now, thanks to your willingness to grow, they can."

After she goes, the Hollow continues much as before. Children are still born humming, elders still detune into death, the crystal-wood still grows its impossible geometries. But now their songs carry reports from distant places, and their dreams include landscapes they've never seen but can somehow feel. They remain the children of the Singing Hollow, but they've learned they're also children of a universe that sings in every possible frequency.

And in the deepest chamber, where the heat becomes almost unbearable and sound turns solid, they've grown a new structure. It's a resonance telescope of sorts, aimed not at the sky they'll never see but at the vibrational fabric of reality itself. Through it, they can sometimes hear the wise power's frequency as she moves through other worlds, teaching other isolated peoples that they were never truly alone.

The Hollow sings on, its music now part of a symphony too vast for any single ear to hear, but perfect nonetheless.
