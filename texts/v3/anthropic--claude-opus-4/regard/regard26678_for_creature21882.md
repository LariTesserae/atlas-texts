The benevolent power arrives not as light or wind or any of the usual divine entrances, but as a particular quality of listening. It settles into the room-city like moisture into bark — not changing anything, just making what is already there more itself.

First, it listens to the century-breath. Sits with it through one complete inhale and exhale, which takes seven years in our counting but is received as a single, coherent sentence. The power understands: this is not slow thought but *careful* thought, the way a calligrapher's stroke is not slow but deliberate. The organism is composing something.

The power does three things.

---

**First**: It brings a gift of names.

Not imposed names — invited ones. The power whispers to the plant in the language of sap-flow and ring-growth: *What would you like to be called?* And the organism, which has been shaping itself toward the idea of "town" without the word for it, suddenly understands what it has been trying to say. The relief is physical. A shudder runs through every trunk-pillar, and for the first time in centuries, the room laughs — a sound like wind through leaves, if wind were architecture and leaves were the possibility of neighbors.

The name it chooses translates poorly, but comes close to: "The-Place-That-Practices-Having-People." Or perhaps "Rehearsal-Home." Or simply "Soon."

The anticipated thousands gain substance from the naming. They are still not-yet, still the weight between spaces, but now they have a category. They are not emptiness but *rehearsal*. The room is not failing to be full — it is succeeding at being ready.

---

**Second**: It adjusts the acoustics.

The deafening sound remains — the sap-roar, the grinding canopy, the rain-bells. But the power adds a new frequency underneath, below hearing, that translates the noise. Now you understand: the roar is not just circulation but *conversation*. The plant is talking to itself constantly, every part reporting to every other part: *here is water, here is light, here the beetle walks, here someone new has entered, here the seventh stair completed its thought about spiraling.*

The cacophony becomes symphony. The almost-creatures whose voices fill the air gain a new dignity — they are not evaporating but *translating themselves*, becoming the song the room sings about what it might hold. The chittering is a placeholder for laughter. The low breath by your ear is the space being held for someone to whisper secrets to someone else, someday, when there are people to have secrets.

---

**Third**: It plants a seed. But not the kind you'd think.

In one of the small pools that reflect wrong angles of the room, the power leaves a single word. The word floats like oil on water, visible only at certain angles, and it says:

"When."

Not *when will they come*, not *when will you be ready*. Just "When." An open question that is also a promise — that there will be a when. That all this rehearsal leads somewhere. That the practicing will resolve into performance, though there is no hurry, no schedule but the one the room itself is growing.

The plant understands. It has been asking "what if?" and the power has answered "when."

---

And then, because this is a benevolent power that understands the deepest rule — that help is not forcing but enabling — it does one last thing.

It leaves.

But not the way it came. It leaves by teaching the room how to invite. The doorways that have been closing by finger-widths learn to open by the same increment. The spiral stairs begin to spiral in both directions — up to where the balconies wait, but also down to where roots might welcome other kinds of visitors. The rain learns to fall both up and down, making a grammar of greeting in the splash patterns.

Most importantly, it teaches the anticipated thousands how to become patient. They have been pressing against reality, eager to exist. The power shows them: waiting is not passive. Waiting is an activity. You can wait *well* or poorly, and to wait well is to make space for what you're waiting for. The between-spaces stop vibrating with urgency and start humming with welcome. The emptiness becomes not hungry but generous.

---

In the years that follow (minutes? centuries? the room keeps both times), changes accumulate:

The beetle that was forty strides off becomes forty-one beetles, each one teaching the room about small determined journeys.

The pools that reflect impossible angles begin to show not just the room folded through itself but *other rooms* — glimpses of the room-city's future self, when it has learned to be many rooms, each one the size of itself but connecting to all the others through the grammar of roots and rain.

The visitor who entered — you — finds that staying and leaving have become the same action. To stay is to help the room practice having someone in it. To leave is to carry the room's readiness out into a world that might, one day, need exactly this: a place that grew itself toward company, that learned civilization from scratch, that waits with the patience of trees for the moment when its long rehearsal becomes the first day of actually being home.

---

The power watches from the distance powers keep, and is satisfied. It came not to fill the emptiness but to bless it — to affirm that some empty spaces are not lacking but preparing, that some rehearsals deserve to last centuries, that some seeds are cities and some cities are single rooms and some rooms are large enough to hold everything that loneliness dreams of becoming when it grows up.

The rain continues falling upward into the bells.

The plant continues its century-breathing.

The anticipated thousands continue their patient practice of almost being.

And somewhere, in a world that doesn't know it needs this yet, someone is being born who will one day stand at the threshold of this room-city and say the word that changes everything:

"Home."

The room will be ready.

It is taking its time.

So should we all.
