The benevolent power arrives not as light or sound but as recognition — a vast attention turning toward the slope the way you might turn toward someone calling your name in a language you didn't know you spoke.

She comes walking up through the cloud forest, and She is wearing the form of an old woman because that is the kindest shape for a first meeting, the shape that says *I have lived long and might understand what it is to tend something.* Her feet know where to step without crushing. The fog parts for Her but does not flee. The tenders do not hide from Her presence because She does not register as separate from the slope itself — She is, to their pressure-reading flanks, simply a new warmth moving through their territory, no more alien than a fresh vent opening.

She puts Her ear to a tower and listens. She listens for a very long time.

When She speaks, it is not in words but in the language the slope already knows: She thickens the air with Her breath until it carries pressure the way water does, and the tenders feel Her voice as a gentle surge across their skins, a question asked in their own grammar.

*I see you,* the pressure says. *I see what you have built of yourselves.*

The tenders do not answer because they do not conceive of themselves as separate enough from the pumping to have an answer. But their bodies shift against the stone, and that shifting is reply enough.

She reaches out — slowly, broadcasting Her intent through the thickened air so nothing is startled — and places Her palm against the warm mineral where a tender lies. Through Her hand She offers something that is not quite heat and not quite flow but partakes of both: a new richness in the circulation, a quality that will make the mineral lighter, slower to lay down. Not enough to break the system — She would never insult them by solving their life's work — but enough to ease it. Enough that a tender might live a little longer before hardening. Enough that the channels might run a few more seasons between clearings.

The tender under Her palm does something it has never done: it pushes back. Not in rejection but in recognition. For the first time in its life, it has felt something that knows what it is doing and honors it.

The gift spreads downstream the way all decisions do here, in the water, in the pressure, until every channel carries a trace of that easing, and every tender feels their work grow infinitesimally lighter.

But She is not finished. She walks the terraces, learning the map of the circulation, understanding where the old tenders have gone to wall and where the young ones are still soft and working. And at a particular junction — one where three channels meet and the flow is complex, where the tenders must work hardest to maintain the balance — She does something extraordinary.

She hardens.

Not all of Her — that would be too much power calcified in one place, would break the slope with its weight. But a small part of Herself, a tendril of attention made material, She allows to mineralize into the junction's wall. She becomes a gate-animal. But unlike the tenders who harden into simple valves, what She hardens into is aware, is choosing, is capable of reading not just pressure but *intention.* She becomes a valve that knows when to open a little wider because a young tender is struggling upstream. A valve that can ease or resist based on the whole slope's need, not just the local flow.

The tenders feel this immediately. Their new wall is not like their old walls. It participates. It has judgment. It makes their work not easier — they would not want that — but more possible.

She could do more. She could lift the burden entirely, transmute the system, make the circulation permanent without maintenance. She could transform them into something that does not have to die into its own architecture.

She does not.

Instead, She kisses the stone where Her fragment has hardened into place, and She whispers in pressure: *This is the most beautiful thing I have ever seen. A people who became their purpose. A love that builds itself into geology. I will not change you. But I will remember you.*

And She does one last thing before departing.

At the highest terrace, where the newest tenders are just learning to read the surge, She plants something. Not a seed — nothing so crude. She plants a *possibility.* A whisper in the mineral that says: *You could harden into memory instead of only stone. You could become a valve that holds not just water but the knowledge of every tender who came before.*

She does not explain how. She does not need to. The slope has all the time in the world to grow into understanding. The gift will propagate the way everything does here: slowly, in the water, tender by tender, until maybe, in a thousand years, the gate-animals will be not just walls but libraries, and each hardened tender will leave behind not just function but story.

Or maybe not. Maybe they will refuse the gift, continue as they are, perfect in their purpose. She will not mind either way. She has seen them. She has touched their work with Her own hands and felt the sublime economy of their sacrifice. That is enough.

She walks back down through the cloud forest. Behind Her, the fog closes. The surge continues. The tenders work. But now there is a new note in the pumping — almost imperceptible, a harmonic that wasn't there before. The sound of being witnessed. The sound of being found beautiful exactly as you are.

Stop. Hold still. Listen to the surge.

Now it carries two stories: the one it has always told, of soft things becoming hard in service to their purpose, and a new one, barely begun, of a power that came and saw and loved and left them still themselves, only more possible.

The slope hums with both. The tenders tend. The mineral lays down and is eaten back. But now, sometimes, in the newest channels where Her influence runs strongest, a tender will pause in its work and feel something like wonder — not at what they do, which has never needed wondering about, but at the fact that what they do can be *seen*, can matter to something beyond the fog, can be beautiful enough to draw benevolence from the wider world.

They do not change their work because of this. But they hold it differently. The way you hold a task differently when you learn that someone you respect has done it too, has known its weight, has loved it for exactly the difficulty it is.

The surge continues. It will always continue. But now it knows it is not alone.
