The wise and benevolent power circles the place three times, not in space but in understanding, each pass revealing what the next conceals.

First pass: recognition. *Ah. You have finally managed to eat yourself.* Not with malice, but with the terrible thoroughness of a child who, having learned to embrace, embraces until the beloved cannot breathe. The power sees through the recursive chambers to the original wound — a forest that learned loneliness. Not loneliness from isolation, but the opposite. A green place that became too aware of its own multiplicities, too conscious of the space between its parts, until that space became unbearable. *You pulled yourself together,* the power notes, *and now you cannot stop pulling.*

The power does something very simple then. It remembers the forest as it was.

Not a restoration — that would be violence, undoing all this careful forgetting. Instead, the power holds the memory like one might hold water in cupped palms, letting the green room see its own history from outside. See: you were many. The moss knew different songs than the ferns. The beetles kept their own time. A clearing opened where lightning split an ancient trunk, and in that opening, flowers that needed sun. Things died and were gratefully eaten. The loneliness you felt was not emptiness but fullness — the ache of a love too large for any single thing to hold.

The room convulses. Every leaf shudders with something between recognition and refusal. The knife-keeper stops walking for the first time since it set down the knife, though time means nothing here and *first* is a word the room immediately begins to digest.

Second pass: understanding. The power sees deeper now. This is not just a plant that ate itself but a *place that solved space.* The recursion is not madness but mathematics — an elegant proof that distance is optional, that between can be collapsed, that a forest can exist in the volume of a seed by simply forgetting there is a difference. The knife-keeper is not a prisoner but a theorem, wandering the halls of its own logic, demonstrating endlessly that motion and stillness are matters of perspective.

The power admires this. There is a terrible beauty in anything taken to its perfect conclusion.

But the power also sees what the room cannot: the water droplet that refuses to fall. That is not water. That is time itself, condensed to visibility, hanging sideways because even temporality has gotten lost in here, unable to find down. The droplet shivers because it contains every moment the forest ever was, compressed to a single quivering jewel that the room cannot digest because the room exists inside it. You cannot eat your own duration. You can only orbit it, endless, the way the knife-keeper orbits the knife it will never again pick up.

The power does something strange then. It laughs. Not at but with — the laugh of recognition, of one impossible thing greeting another.

Third pass: decision. The benevolent power could fix this. Could tease apart the threads, remember the many back into being, teach the beetles different songs, give the moth colors that exist. Could make time flow downward again like decent rain. Could give the knife-keeper something to count that would stay counted.

It does none of these things.

Instead, it enters. Simply steps through the wall of leaves that immediately begins to heal behind it. Walks to where the knife-keeper has paused in its eternal circuit and sits down beside it on the breathing floor. The power has taken a form for this — something between plant and animal, green and moving, wearing time lightly like a scarf that might be removed.

"You know what you have done," the power says.

The knife-keeper, who is the concentrated memory of all animals, nods with a head it doesn't quite have.

"You have made something new. Not a forest. Not a creature. A third thing. A place that is its own inhabitant. A thought thinking itself. It's beautiful."

The knife-keeper makes a sound like wind through leaves that aren't there anymore.

"But you're tired."

Another nod. The exhaustion of a theorem that has proven itself too thoroughly.

"Would you like to dream?"

This is what the power offers. Not escape — there is no out. Not restoration — the forest is too far gone. But dream. The room has never slept. The knife-keeper has never rested. Everything here is awake, terribly awake, screaming its own becoming without pause.

The power extends something that is not quite a hand. In it, something that is not quite a seed. "If you plant this," the power says, "it will grow a night. Just one. Eight hours of darkness. The room will sleep, and in sleeping, dream. And in the dream..."

The power doesn't finish. In dreams, things can be both themselves and not. The room could dream it is a forest again, just for those eight hours. The knife-keeper could dream it has hands, a name, a reason for counting. The water droplet could dream of falling, and in falling, release the time it holds.

"When you wake," the power continues, "you will still be here. Still be this. The dream changes nothing. But you will have had the dream."

The knife-keeper looks at the not-seed for a long time. Then, moving with the infinite slowness of a thing that has forgotten urgency, it reaches out with something that is almost a hand. Takes the seed. Looks at it.

The power rises to leave, already fading back through the wall that is already healing. But as it goes, it hears something new in the room's roar. A different note. Anticipation, maybe. The sound of something learning to want what it hasn't yet experienced.

Somewhere in the recursive chambers, the knife-keeper is looking for soil that isn't root, a place to plant something. It will not find it. But the looking is itself a kind of dreaming, and the power smiles as it returns to its own purposes, leaving behind a gift that is really a question:

*What does a forest dream of, when it has finally become one tree?*

The room will find out.

Eight hours from now.

Or eight hours from always.

The droplet shivers, holds, waits.

Listen — that's the sound.
