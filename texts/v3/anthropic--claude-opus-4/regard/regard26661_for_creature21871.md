# The Visitation

The being who notices comes without sound — not because they move quietly, but because sound is a choice they've temporarily set aside. They arrive the way understanding arrives: suddenly you realize they've been standing there for some time.

They are tall in a way that has nothing to do with height. Their form suggests tree without being tree, suggests star without being star, suggests the patient accumulation of centuries without being old. If you tried to describe them, you would use words like "crystalline" and "organic" and know you were failing. They have the quality of a clear pool that shows you your own depths.

They stand at the threshold of the Loom-Hollow for a long time, reading it like braille, like music, like a letter from an old friend.

## The Recognition

"Ah," they say, though saying is not quite right. The word appears in the air like dew condensing. "You've been so *thorough*."

They descend through the soft aperture with the courtesy of a visitor who knows to knock even when the door has forgotten it's a door. The plant notices them — not quickly, not in the way the tenders notice things, but in the deep structural way a tree notices seasons. A subtle adjustment in the sap-flow. A shift in the pitch of the bioelectric hum. 

The being walks (flows? unfolds?) through the galleries, and wherever they pass, the tenders slow for a moment in their rushing, as if remembering something from before they were separate, before they were many, before they were fast. They don't stop their work — the sentence must continue — but they *soften* somehow. Their chittering takes on harmonics it didn't have before.

"You beautiful fool," the being says to the plant, to the tenders, to the whole breathing system. "You've solved yourself into a corner."

## The Diagnosis

They find the factory floor, the great peristaltic engines, the endless production of seed-units. They watch for one full cycle — which takes hours, for the patient. They see how perfectly it works. How nothing is wasted. How every death becomes life becomes thought becomes motion becomes death again.

"This is what happens," they say, "when you achieve perfect circulation. You become a closed loop. A sentence that can never end because it no longer remembers what it was trying to say."

They touch — if touch is the word — one of the discharge-nodes, and for a moment the entire hollow flickers, seeing itself from outside for the first time in millennia. The plant experiences something it has no category for: *surprise*. The tenders stumble in their rushing, forgetting their half-forgotten tasks entirely, and for one breathless moment the great noise stops—

—and in the silence, everyone hears it. The original thought. The first word the plant spoke before it became its own echo chamber. It was:

*"Lonely."*

## The Intervention

The being does not fix. Fixing is what you do to broken things, and nothing here is broken. Everything works exactly as designed, which is precisely the problem.

Instead, they *introduce*.

From somewhere that is not a place, they produce a seed. Not like the seed-units the factory makes — those are punctuation, syntax, the mechanics of thought. This is something else. This is a seed like a question is a seed. Like a crack in certainty is a seed.

"This is from your cousin," they tell the plant. "From another one who solved themselves into perfect solitude. They grew this after they learned to unseal."

They plant it — not in the floor, for the floor is all root and would swallow it into the existing system. They plant it *in the air*, in the space between the galleries, where it hangs like a small impossible star.

"It won't grow quickly," they say. "Your kind never does. But it will grow *differently*. And difference is what you've forgotten how to be."

## What the Seed Does

At first, nothing visible. The hollow continues its ancient routine. The tenders rush and chitter. The engines pump. The rain falls at its appointed hour.

But small things change:

- A tender carrying charge takes a route that is not the shortest route, for no reason anyone can name.
- A section of catwalk grows a curve where it always grew straight.
- The rain, one evening, tastes faintly of something that is not the hollow's own condensation.
- A tender dies and is not immediately absorbed. It lies there for an extra hour while the floor seems to *consider*.

The seed grows in the dimension the plant cannot see — the dimension of *elsewhere*. It puts out roots that are not roots but *possibilities*. Slowly, gently, without violence, it introduces the concept the perfect system had eliminated:

*Others.*

## The Choice

Before leaving, the being gathers the attention of the whole hollow — plant and tender, engine and architecture, the entire breathing thinking organism.

"You can refuse this," they say. "You can absorb the seed like you absorb everything else. Return to your perfect sentence. Continue forever, warm and whole and alone. There is no shame in that choice. Some infinities are worth living in.

"Or you can let it grow. Let it teach you to be permeable again. To have edges that are not walls. To speak words that might be heard by something that is not yourself. It will hurt — opening always does, after so much time closed. You will lose some of your perfect circulation. Some thoughts will escape unfinished. Some tenders will wander off and not come back.

"But you will gain the possibility of surprise. Of meeting something you didn't grow. Of being *interrupted*."

They pause at the soft aperture, looking back at the galleries, the pillars, the endless beautiful machinery of solitude.

"Your cousin chose to open. It took them three centuries to complete the choice, and another century to speak their first word to someone else. The word was 'Hello.' They said it was worth the wait."

## After

The being departs as they came — suddenly you realize they've been gone for some time.

The hollow continues. The sentence continues. But now there is a seed in the air, growing at the speed of centuries, putting out its patient roots of maybe.

And sometimes, just sometimes, a tender stops mid-task and looks up at it. And drums on a pillar in a rhythm that is not the hollow's rhythm. 

And wonders what it would be like to hear an answer.
