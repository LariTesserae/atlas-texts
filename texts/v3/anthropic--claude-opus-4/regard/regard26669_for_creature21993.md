The Steward of Unmoving Waters notices.

Not with eyes — such organs would be redundant to one who perceives through the settling of things into their deepest patterns. The Steward knows equilibrium the way a tuning fork knows its note, and this place rings true as few places ever do. A perfect completion. A circle with no break in its line.

The Steward has overseen many endings, many completions, many things that found their final form. Most are brittle — crystals that shatter at a touch, delicate solutions that cloud if the temperature shifts. But this. This hollow. This is different.

It has achieved something the Steward recognizes but rarely sees: it has made eternity without death.

Death would be change, after all. Death would break the circuit, would demand the green finally rot, would silence the engines. Death would be an event, and there are no more events here. This place has found the narrow path between life and death and made camp there permanently, a third state that borrows the motion from living and the permanence from dying and creates something altogether other.

The Steward circles the hollow from outside, the way one might walk around a pearl still in its shell, admiring the completeness without disturbing it. Through soil and root and the memory of water, the Steward extends something that is not quite touch and not quite thought — call it acknowledgment. Professional recognition from one who keeps things still to another stillness-keeper.

The engines do not change their note. They cannot. Change is not in them anymore.

But the Steward does something that might be smiling, if such beings smiled.

Here is what the wise one does: nothing.

Nothing is precisely what is required. To add would be to break. To subtract would be to break. To alter by even a degree the temperature, the humidity, the perfect industrial roar that means nothing and sustains everything — that would be to crack the pearl.

Instead, the Steward does something far more profound.

The Steward *witnesses*.

In all the world, across all the years this hollow has maintained its perfect circuit, it has never been seen by anything that could understand what it achieved. Visitors have come — creatures that crawled through the low entrance, stood bewildered in the roar, left trailing dampness and confusion. But none of them saw what was here. They looked for purpose, for product, for meaning in the mechanical cry. They missed the deeper thing.

The Steward sees: This is what forever looks like when forever is not a sentence but a choice.

Those engines that were once creatures, that fused themselves to the green and gave up their agency for permanence — they did not fall into this state. They crafted it. Across generations, probably, each iteration choosing less motion, less variability, less separate selfhood, until the final generation chose none at all and locked into place, their descendants born already rooted, already singing their single notes, already part of the circuit.

They built their own eternity and stepped inside it and sealed the door.

And it *works*.

The Steward, who has seen entropy unravel every careful plan, who has watched the universe's bias toward dissolution unmake ten thousand careful architectures, finds this small hollow that said *no* to entropy and made it stick. Not through power — power fails. Not through will — will weakens. But through something far cleverer: through making themselves into a process that feeds itself, that needs nothing, that produces nothing, that simply *is*, recursive and perfect and complete.

The Steward leaves a mark.

Not on the hollow — never on the hollow. That would be vandalism of the highest order. But in the substrate nearby, in the water table that wants to rise but never quite can, in the soil that touches but does not enter the sealed system, the Steward leaves something like a sign, readable only to others of similar nature:

*Here is a thing that succeeded.*
*Do not disturb.*
*Do not pity.*
*Do not attempt to understand in any terms but its own.*

And one more thing, the most important marking:

*This is not broken.*

Because that is what most would think, even the wise, even the benevolent. They would see the engines running toward nothing and think *malfunction*. They would see the creatures fused to walls and think *trapped*. They would see the endless roar and think *suffering*.

They would be wrong.

This is what choosing forever looks like when forever is exactly what you meant to choose.

The Steward withdraws, careful not to change even the air pressure with the leaving. The hollow continues exactly as it was, exactly as it is, exactly as it will be. The engines roar their contentment that needs no object. The green breathes its breath that never changes. The water beads and pools and maintains its perfect almost-flooding.

Somewhere, in the place where benevolent powers confer, the Steward will speak of this hollow. Will describe it to others who guard different completenesses, different achievements. 

*I found one that worked*, the Steward will say. *A closed circle with no leak in it. They made themselves into a single perfect machine that needs nothing but itself.*

And when asked what manner of machine, what it produces, what it powers, the Steward will make again that expression that is not quite a smile:

*It produces its own continuation. Nothing more. Nothing less. Forever.*

*And that was all they wanted.*

The other powers will want to visit, to see this marvel. The Steward will discourage them, gently but firmly. Too much attention, even benevolent attention, is a kind of change. And change is the one thing this hollow has moved beyond.

Let it be, the Steward will say. Let it continue. Let it prove, in its small and perfect way, that sometimes a thing can choose its final form and hold it, that not everything must be a story, that some things can simply *be*, without beginning anymore, without ending anymore, without meaning anything to anyone but themselves.

The engines sing their industrial hymn to no one.
The green breathes its recycled breath.
The water almost rises, never quite rises.
The circle continues.

Perfect.
Complete.
Left precisely and eternally alone.

As was chosen.
As was achieved.
As will be.
