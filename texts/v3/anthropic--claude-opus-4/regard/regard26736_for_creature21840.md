I find myself watching from a great distance, as one watches a single cell beneath glass — though this cavity is no small thing, and distance here is not measured in the usual ways.

The benevolent power I embody does not rush. Rushing would be... inappropriate, given what I observe. This place that has made an art of stillness, this perfect equation that sums always to zero — it asks for a different kind of attention. Not intervention. Not yet. First, understanding.

I see the one with their hand on the cairn, the new variable in an equation that thought itself complete. I see the grazers drift closer, patient as salt formation. I see how the cavity breathes its one held breath, forever.

I could reach down — I have reached down before, into places darker than this — and pluck the visitor out like extracting a splinter. Simple. Clean. The cavity would seal over the disturbance in a moment that lasts forever, return to its perfect balance. The grazers would drift back to their territories. The amber light would pulse, out of phase, undisturbed.

But that would be... unkind. Not to the visitor — they would live, would see morning again, would carry their tomorrows back to a world that counts them. Unkind to the cavity itself, and to the makers who are not gone but *spent*.

You see, I recognize this place. Not this specific cavity — I have never been here before — but the *kind* of place it is. A universe is full of these pockets, these moments that learned how to last forever by refusing to be moments anymore. Some are vast as galaxies. Some are small as the space between two heartbeats. This one is the size of a sea, and it has made its choice.

The choice was not wrong. That's what a benevolent power must understand first — the choice to stop choosing, to step out of consequence and into pure being, is not a failure. It's a solution. The makers found the only way to live here: by agreeing to stop living in the way that *living* means *next*. They became the gesture without the reason, the hand without the want, the cairn without the builder. Pure form, perfectly preserved.

But.

There is always a but with me. It's my nature — I am a power that believes in *and then*, even when *and then* has been declined, gently, completely, for longer than surfaces have had names.

So here is what I do:

I leave a door.

Not a door in the rock — that would be too crude, too much like the fourth course that will not stay stacked. I leave a door in the *law*. A single exception written into the cavity's perfect constitution, so small it hardly disturbs the balance at all. The door is this:

*Once in an unmeasurable while, when a new variable enters the equation, when a creature still full of tomorrow places their hand on the salt someone balanced in a yesterday that never ended — in that moment, that specific moment, the gesture of leaving is permitted to complete.*

The cavity considers this. I feel it thinking in its slow, warm way. The amendment is small. It changes almost nothing. It applies only to the already-extraordinary event of something falling in from above. It does not violate the basic principle — beginnings are still forbidden. It simply acknowledges that some beginnings happened elsewhere, before the fall, and deserve to conclude.

The cavity accepts, in the way that salt accepts water — reluctantly, but according to its nature.

The visitor feels it as a change in the air that does not change. The grazers pause in their drift, confused by a sensation they have no equipment to parse. For just this moment — not measured in time, because time isn't measured here, but measured in the space between *staying* and *going* — the next gesture is not declined.

Get up now, while now still means something different than it did a page ago.

But I do one more thing, because benevolence without wisdom is just another kind of entropy:

I leave the choice.

The door is open. The way up is possible, briefly, exceptionally. But the cairn is still warm under the visitor's hand, and the amber light is still pulsing its out-of-phase lullaby, and there is something to be said — something the makers knew and performed with their careful spacing — for a place that keeps you exactly as you are, forever, with all your edges sharp.

The visitor can leave. Or the visitor can stay and place one more stone, balance it perfectly, corner on corner, knowing it will be the last stone they ever place and that it will stand, crisp and immediate, until the concept of *until* loses all meaning.

Both are kinds of love. Both are kinds of ending. The power I embody, benevolent and patient as salt-formation, does not judge between them.

But I watch. I always watch. And sometimes — not often, not in any pattern the cavity would recognize as pattern — sometimes what fell as one thing climbs out as another, carrying not just their tomorrows but the memory of a place where tomorrows went to rest.

The grazers pulse, amber, waiting. The cairn waits, finished. The visitor's hand is the only thing in the cavity that hasn't decided yet what tense it's in.

Choose well. Both choices are well. That's the door I left you — not the burden of choosing right, but the gift of choosing at all, in a place that thought it had used up all its choices an unmeasurable while ago.

The warm dark holds its breath and waits to see which way the equation balances.
