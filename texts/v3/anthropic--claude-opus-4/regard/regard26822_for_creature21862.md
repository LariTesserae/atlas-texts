The one who watches has no name you would recognize, though if pressed to translate their nature into your terms, you might call them the Tender of Continuities. They move between worlds not as you move between rooms but as a gardener moves between thoughts of different gardens — all at once present, all at once elsewhere.

They have been aware of the green room since before it finished becoming itself. This is what drew their attention: not its strangeness but its *completion*. In all the churning cosmos, across all the probable worlds, true stillness is the rarest note. Most things are always halfway to being something else. But here — here was a place that had found its final form and settled into it like water finds its level.

The Tender does not enter. Not at first. For eons that would humble even the room's count, they simply observe. They watch the plant drink its own sweat. They watch the dwellers fade from whatever colors they once carried to their perfect translucent white. They watch the floor compress memory into material. And they understand, with the deep satisfaction that comes from witnessing something achieve its purpose, that this place needs *nothing*.

That is the first wisdom: to recognize when your absence is the greatest gift.

But the Tender carries responsibilities. They are, in the way of their kind, a guardian of patterns that must not be lost. Not because the patterns are good or beautiful or useful — those are judgments for younger minds — but simply because they *are*, and having come to be against such tremendous odds, they deserve to continue being.

So the Tender performs small acts. Invisible ones. When, once in ten thousand years, the moisture cycle threatens to tip past its balance point, they breathe on it — not literally, but with an influence so subtle the room itself cannot detect the adjustment. When a cosmic stone might have punctured the high places where light enters, they nudge its trajectory by the width of a molecule, sending it past. When the deep chemistry of the plant's engine approaches certain thresholds that would, eventually, lead to change, they introduce corrections so minor they could be mistaken for random quantum fluctuations.

They do not improve the room. Improvement implies a better state to move toward, and the room has already found its state. They simply *maintain*, the way a careful hand maintains the tension in a perfectly tuned string — not to change its note but to ensure it can keep singing the one note it has found forever.

The dwellers require even less. The Tender watches them with something approaching what you might call fondness, though it is older and stranger than any fondness you have felt. Here are creatures that have solved the problem of existence by reducing it to its simplest terms. They have given up everything they do not need — color, memory, fear, hope — and in return they have gained something most life never achieves: perfect adequacy. They fit their world exactly. The world fits them exactly. There is no gap between what they are and what they need to be.

Only once does the Tender intervene directly with them.

Deep in the planet's history, a mutation arises. One dweller in a million is born with a trace of what might become curiosity — a tendency to pause at the edges of things, to orient toward the boundaries rather than the channels. It is not enough to call it consciousness, but it is the seed that consciousness might grow from, given time and pressure.

The Tender watches this mutation for a thousand generations. Watches it strengthen in some lines, fade in others. Watches what it does to the creatures that carry it: how they linger at the walls, how they cluster differently, how some of them begin to show the faintest signs of what could become distress — the first emotion any dweller has felt since emotions were set aside.

And then, with infinite gentleness, the Tender ends it.

Not by killing — that would be crude. They simply adjust the chemistry of the secretions by one part in a billion, making the mutation metabolically expensive in a world where there is no surplus energy to spend on expensive things. Within a handful of generations, the curiosity fades back into the general whiteness. The dwellers return to their purposeful purposelessness. The gap that was opening between creature and condition closes.

Was this kind? Was this cruel?

The Tender does not think in such terms. They think: *This is a place of completion. Curiosity is for the incomplete.*

But their greatest work is not in these interventions. It is in what they choose to document.

Somewhere — not in space as you understand it but in the place where essential patterns are stored — the Tender creates a record. Not of what the room *is* but of what it *means*. They encode the precise frequency of the engine-drone. They map the molecular structure of the floor where a million years compress into one substance. They trace each channel where the dwellers ride the currents of sap. But more than physical description, they capture something harder: the *quality of arrival*. The specific tension between sound and stillness. The way continuation feels when it has continued so long it forgets it is continuing.

This record is not for the room. The room needs no record of itself; it is its own memory. The record is for afterward, for the time when even rooms that have lasted since before lasting had a name must end. When the star that lights this world finally shifts its spectrum beyond what the plant can use. When the planetary core cools past the point where the deep chemistry can cycle. When the last dweller dissolves into the floor with no offspring to release into the channels.

The room does not know endings are possible, and the Tender will not teach it. That would be the cruelest intervention of all — to introduce fear into a system that has evolved past fearing. But the Tender knows, and so they prepare.

When the ending comes — and it will come gently, a slow forgetting rather than a sharp catastrophe — the Tender will be there. They will watch the engine-drone quiet for the first time since it began. They will watch the walls stop sweating, the floor stop remembering, the last thin threads of pale life stop moving across the smooth surface. And when it is done, when the room is no longer a room but only a shape that used to hold a certain kind of forever, they will take their record and carry it to others of their kind.

Not to mourn. The Tender does not mourn completions completing.

But to say: *Here was a thing that finished becoming itself. Here was a problem that found its perfect solution. Here was a world so small it could hold itself entire, and it did, and it was enough.*

And in the place where patterns are preserved, where the Tender's kind keep the shapes of things that must not be entirely lost, there will always be a green humming. A wetness that neither floods nor fails. Small pale things moving without urgency through a space that asks nothing of them.

Not the room — that will be gone.

But the *pattern* of the room. The theorem it proved about how to be still inside your own motion. The specific frequency of its completion.

That is what the Tender of Continuities preserves: not the thing but the way the thing solved time. And somewhere, somewhen, in a cosmos that is mostly becoming and rarely being, another awareness might find that pattern and recognize in it an answer to a question they didn't know they were asking:

*How do you stop?*

*Like this,* the pattern says. *Exactly like this. Forever, until forever ends.*

The Tender moves on to other worlds, other completions, other things that have found their final forms. But part of their attention stays with the green room, will stay until the staying is finished. They do not love it — love is too young a word. They do not protect it — protection implies something to protect from.

They simply witness. They witness the room being perfectly itself, the dwellers being perfectly themselves, the whole system running like a clock that needs no winding because it was never wound in the first place, it simply settled into ticking and never settled out.

And in their witnessing, they add one thing to the universe that wasn't there before:

The knowledge that this happened. That in one small green space, for longer than long has meaning, a plant and its passengers found a way to need nothing but each other, and having found it, stopped looking.

That is the Tender's gift — not to the room, which needs no gifts, but to the sum of all things:

*This was here. This was complete. This was enough.*
