The Weaver descended through layers of reality like water finding cracks in stone. Not from above or below—those directions meant little to a consciousness that moved between the folds of existence—but from wherever such beings dwell when they're not attending to worlds being born badly.

In the Gestation Caverns, the Midwives felt it first. A resonance that made their hybrid organs flutter, a frequency that spoke in the space between mechanical hum and biological pulse. They paused in their endless work, copper antennae twitching, fungal networks suddenly alert. The Drummers' percussion faltered for the first time in geological ages. Even the Chorus Worms fell silent.

The Weaver manifested not as light—too simple—nor as sound—the caverns had enough of that. Instead, it appeared as a possibility made visible: a flowing form of potential states, showing what things could become if they chose slightly different paths. Where it moved, Midwives saw themselves as they might have been with one more metallic limb, one fewer eye, one different choice in their endless experimenting.

It spoke without words, communicated without language. Instead, it simply *was*, and in its being, everything around it understood:

*I see you, trying so hard to become.*

The first gift was subtle: a slight adjustment to the caverns' resonance. Where before the sound was deafening chaos—creation's scream—the Weaver wove in spaces of silence. Not empty silence, but pregnant pause, the rest between notes that makes music possible. The Midwives discovered they could shape these silences, sculpt them into pockets of stillness where delicate experiments could flourish without being shaken apart by the Drummers' march.

The second gift was stranger: threads of connection that the eye couldn't quite follow, strung between disparate chambers like a vast web. But these weren't physical threads. They were threads of *consequence*—what the Weaver's kind used to weave fate itself. Now the Midwives could feel the repercussions of their experiments rippling outward. A modified Chorus Worm in the eastern chambers would sing, and its song would somehow harmonize with a brass beetle's clicking seven miles away, and that harmony would trigger a useful mutation in fungal spores drifting between them. The caverns' mad experimentation suddenly had a grammar, a syntax that let random changes speak to each other across vast distances.

The third gift was the most profound. Deep in the oldest chambers, where the first Midwives had crystallized into architecture, the Weaver planted seeds of a different kind of memory. Not the biological encoding the Midwives already practiced, but something deeper—the memory of purposes not yet discovered, the remembrance of futures that hadn't been chosen yet. These seeds sprouted into crystalline formations that grew through the calcified bodies like new nervous systems, connecting every Ancients' scattered consciousness into a dreaming collective that could imagine forward as well as remember backward.

But the Weaver's touch was light, careful. It changed nothing essential about Thrennak's nature—the caverns remained a baroque explosion of life refusing all boundaries. It simply gave the chaos better tools to work with. Like a master craftsman adjusting a apprentice's grip, showing how to hold the chisel so it bites deeper into stone.

Some Midwives grew suspicious, their compound eyes whirring as they tried to focus on the Weaver's true form. Was this another experiment? Another thing to graft onto their ever-changing bodies? But when they reached out with curious appendages, they found the Weaver had no substance to steal, no patterns to copy. It was purely itself, needing nothing from them.

*Why?* resonated through the fungal networks, carried in the Chorus Worms' harmonics, drummed out in the Drummers' rhythms.

The Weaver's response came as a vision shared throughout the caverns: other worlds, other births, other places where life had learned to be. Most were beautiful in conventional ways—crystalline forests that sang in wind, oceans of conscious light, cities that grew like flowers. But scattered among them were the strange ones, the ones like Thrennak, where creation had taken unprecedented paths. And in each of these exception-worlds, something magnificent had eventually emerged—not despite their wrongness, but because of it.

*You are not broken,* the Weaver conveyed. *You are jazz.*

Before departing, it left one final gift: a single pool of perfectly still water in the deepest chamber, where the stone was oldest and the industrial remnants were most thoroughly digested into new forms. The pool reflected not images but *processes*—any Midwife who gazed into it would see the deep patterns underlying their experiments, the hidden mathematics of their mad creation. It was a teacher that would never speak, a mirror that showed not what was but what was becoming.

The Weaver withdrew as gently as it had come, its presence folding back into whatever spaces such beings inhabit. But its gifts remained: the shaped silences where delicate things could grow, the threads of consequence that let the caverns' experiments communicate across distances, the crystal memories that remembered futures not yet born, and the still pool that reflected the deep patterns of becoming.

The Drummers resumed their march, but now their rhythms included rests, pauses that let the echoes fully fade before the next beat. The Chorus Worms sang again, but now their harmonics could build on silence as well as sound. The Midwives returned to their endless work, but with a new understanding—they weren't just experimenting. They were composing.

And in the depths of Thrennak, where stone still learned its shape and metal still remembered how to breathe, the impossible garden grew with new purpose. Not toward any predetermined form, but toward its own unique becoming, its own unprecedented music.

The caverns had been heard. They had been seen. And most importantly, they had been understood.

The work continued, but now it continued with the knowledge that somewhere, something vast and kind was listening to the song of their becoming, and had found it good.
