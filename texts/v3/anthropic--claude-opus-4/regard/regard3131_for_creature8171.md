The benevolent power first noticed the Sunken Quarter through the dreams of distant whales, who sang of freshwater cousins they had never met. Intrigued by these impossible songs, the power descended through layers of stone and time until it found the Kithara in their green-drowned city.

For three seasons, the power simply observed. It watched children gasp their first air and elders sink into their final rest among the roots. It felt the patient pulse of sap through vast networks of vegetation, tasted the mineral conversations between stone and water. Most of all, it marveled at the Kithara themselves—beings who had chosen the difficulty of the threshold over the ease of commitment to a single element.

The power understood something profound: the Kithara had achieved a balance that even ancient forces rarely managed. They were complete in their incompleteness, whole in their eternal transition.

So the power chose to give three gifts, subtle as morning mist:

**The First Gift: The Singing Stones**

Throughout the quarter, certain stones began to develop a peculiar property. When water flowed over them at just the right speed—not the rush of flood nor the stillness of pool, but the gentle movement of living current—they would resonate with pure tones. Each stone sang a different note, and the Kithara soon discovered that by adjusting water flow through careful placement of plants and channels, they could create melodies that carried through both air and water.

These were not mere entertainment. The frequencies promoted growth in struggling plants, eased the breathing transition in adolescents, and created pockets of perfect temperature where the most delicate cultivation work could be done. The Kithara incorporated the stones into their architecture, building new structures around these musical nodes, creating a city that hummed with quiet purpose.

**The Second Gift: The Memory Moss**

A new variety of moss appeared, indistinguishable from its cousins except for one remarkable property: it could hold and replay the chemical signatures of those who touched it. A hand pressed against the moss would leave an impression that others could read by touch—not thoughts exactly, but emotional resonances, states of being, the quality of a particular moment.

The Kithara began using it to mark significant events. A patch of Memory Moss at a doorway might hold the joy of a birth, the satisfaction of a successful harvest, the quiet determination of a difficult decision. Children learned family history by touching ancient patches in the deep chambers. Lovers exchanged messages that carried more than words ever could. The moss became a living library of sensation, holding the emotional history of the quarter in its soft green embrace.

**The Third Gift: The Wandering Gardens**

Most wondrous and strange, certain plants began to exhibit true mobility. Not quickly—a human lifetime might see a garden travel only the length of a building—but with unmistakable intent. These mobile ecosystems would detach from their original walls, roots becoming temporary holdfasts, moving toward spaces that needed them most.

A building suffering from structural weakness would find reinforcing vines arriving unbidden. A nursery pool growing too cold would discover heat-generating algae taking residence. The gardens moved with their own logic, but always in ways that supported the Kithara's careful balance. They became a visible sign of the quarter's living response to its inhabitants' needs.

The power also left something else, though it was less gift than invitation: in the deepest accessible pool, where the water was oldest and strangest, it created a gentle upwelling. The water that rose from unknown depths carried with it seeds and spores unlike anything in the quarter's ecosystem—organisms from the deep earth that had never known sunlight.

Most of these deep seeds couldn't survive in the quarter's conditions, but a few adapted, creating pockets of bioluminescent wonder in the darkest corners. They were reminders that the Kithara's threshold existence connected them not just to surface and shallows, but to the profound depths below—depths that they might one day choose to explore, should their balance ever seek a new equilibrium.

The Kithara received these gifts with the same patient grace they brought to all things. They didn't worship the power or build shrines to it. Instead, they incorporated the new elements into their lives as they had everything else—thoughtfully, collectively, with attention to the balance of the whole.

Some among them suspected the gifts' source, speaking in whispers of a presence they had felt in the turning seasons of observation. But they also understood that the gifts were not meant to change them, merely to deepen what they already were. The power had recognized in them something precious: beings who had found strength not in choosing sides but in embracing the eternal difficulty of living between.

The power withdrew then, but not entirely. It left a fragment of attention, like a benevolent eye half-open in meditation, watching the Sunken Quarter continue its patient evolution. Sometimes, on nights when the water was particularly still and the stones sang in perfect harmony, the Kithara would feel that presence and know they were not alone in their threshold world.

They would continue as they always had—tending their gardens, raising their amphibious children, creating beauty in the margin between worlds. But now their city sang, their walls remembered, and their gardens walked. The power had given them tools to better be themselves, and in that gift lay the deepest wisdom: that some beings are too precious to change, only to cherish and gently support.

In the years that followed, other threshold peoples would find their way to the Sunken Quarter—beings from the margins of desert and forest, ice and flame. They came seeking not the Kithara's specific adaptation but their philosophy of the between. And the Kithara, made richer by the power's gifts, had new ways to welcome them, to share the profound peace of accepting one's nature, however complex, however suspended between states.

The quarter grew deeper and stranger, but never lost its essential character. It remained a place of transition made permanent, of boundaries dissolved and reformed, of life that refused the tyranny of single answers. And somewhere in the vast attention of the cosmos, the benevolent power smiled, knowing it had chosen its gifts well.
