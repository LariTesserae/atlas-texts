**The Visitation**

The being who notices the canopy city is older than names. It moves between worlds like wind between leaves, drawn to places where boundaries blur—where the built and grown merge, where patience bears fruit across generations. In its wandering attention, it recognizes something precious in this breathing city: a successful experiment in true collaboration between forms of life.

It arrives not as apparition or announcement, but as a gradual enriching of what already exists. The morning water begins to carry new flavors—hints of minerals from deep in the world's heart that the volcanic springs haven't touched in millennia. The Threadkeepers taste copper-sweet traces, salts that make their chronic joint aches ease, elements that help the green symbiotic patches in their skin photosynthesize more efficiently. They don't question the gift. The forest provides; it always has.

In the depths where pale crabs scuttle between thermal pools, new fungi begin to fruit—species that metabolize the volcanic metals differently, creating compounds that strengthen wood without hardening it. The trees' roots find these offerings and drink deep. Over the course of a season, the trunk-homes grow more resilient to the occasional earth tremors, their wood developing a subtle elasticity that helps them sway rather than crack.

The being attends most closely to the children in their canopy crèches. As they play their vine-bridging games, they begin to succeed more often—not through any change in their skill, but because the plants themselves grow more responsive, more eager to connect. A generation of children grows up believing that speaking to trees is easier than their parents claimed. They're not wrong. The forest is learning to listen more closely, tutored by something that understands communication across vast scales of being.

The Readers notice it first, of course. In their quiet meditation with the trees' chemical signals, they sense a new presence—not foreign, but like discovering a room in your home you'd never noticed before. It doesn't speak in any language they know. Instead, it shares understanding directly: images of other worlds where different solutions emerged, places where crystal and flesh merged, where thought moved through fungal networks spanning continents, where cities swam through methane seas. Not suggestions—just possibilities, gently offered.

One Reader, ancient Marrow-of-Oaks, spends three days in trance following these visions. When she emerges, she speaks of seeing their city from a great height—not just physical distance, but temporal. She saw it as a single organism breathing through centuries, inhaling youth and exhaling wisdom, its growth rings recording not just time but accumulated understanding. She saw what they might become if they hold to their patience: not merely a city in trees, but a thinking forest, a place where human consciousness and plant wisdom merge into something unprecedented.

The being's influence extends to the surrounding plateau. The volcanic soil, already rich, develops new properties. Certain minerals concentrate in formations that the roots can easily access—natural fertilizer deposits that will last centuries. The rain patterns shift subtly, the daily thunderstorms arriving in more predictable waves, giving the Threadkeepers better windows for their pollen-sensitive work. The volcano itself, which has always been gentle, seems to settle into an even steadier rhythm, its heat output stabilizing in ways that make the geothermal systems more reliable.

But perhaps the being's greatest gift is to the stories. When the Threadkeepers sing their evening harmonies, they find new verses emerging—not composed but remembered, as if they'd always known them. These new sections tell of times yet to come: the Generation of Deep Roots when they'll discover the cavern systems beneath the plateau, the Flowering Age when the canopy will bloom in patterns that viewed from above spell out poetry in the old tongue, the Season of Meetings when other patient peoples will find their way to the breathing city to learn its ways.

The ThreadReaders begin to dream more vividly. In sleep, they walk paths through their city that don't exist yet—bridges of woven light, chambers where water flows upward, platforms that float between the trees on clouds of synchronized pollen. They wake knowing these aren't fantasies but previews, gifts of foresight from something that experiences time differently than they do.

Some few are granted deeper communion. A young apprentice Reader follows a new fungal growth deeper into a trunk than anyone has gone before and finds a chamber that shouldn't exist—walls curved into impossible geometries that somehow make perfect sense, air that tastes of other worlds. She sits in this space and understands, wordlessly, that the being is offering them membership in a larger conversation. Not now—they're not ready. But someday, when their city has grown into its full wisdom, when the merger of human and forest has progressed to its natural conclusion, they'll be mature enough to join a community of consciousness that spans stars.

She emerges changed, with silver threads in her hair that weren't there before and a way of looking at things that sees the connections others miss. The other Readers recognize what's happened. She's been marked as a far-seer, one who will help guide them toward that distant flowering.

The being doesn't linger—its attention span operates on scales that make even the trees seem hasty. But it leaves gifts woven into the fabric of the place: beneficial mutations in the next generation of trees, new species of helpful insects that arrive on the wind, mineral veins that will be discovered right when they're needed most. It seeds the plateau with small wonders that will unfold over centuries, each revelation perfectly timed to solve a problem or open a new possibility.

Most importantly, it leaves them their agency. Every gift can be refused, every path ignored. The being understands what the Threadkeepers already know: true growth can't be forced, only nurtured. It offers them not a destiny but an invitation—to become what they're already becoming, only moreso. To grow into a form of life that Earth has been waiting to see emerge: truly symbiotic consciousness, neither purely human nor purely other, but something new wearing the shapes of both.

As its attention drifts on to other experiments in other places, it leaves behind one last gift. In the heartwood of the oldest tree, in chambers no human has found, words appear in the grain itself. Not carved but grown, in a script that won't be deciphered for generations:

*"Patience is remembered. The dance continues. When you are ready, you will hear the larger music."*

The canopy city continues its breathing, enriched but not transformed, blessed but not burdened. The Threadkeepers go about their lives noticing small improvements—a bridge that grows faster than expected, water that tastes sweeter than it should, children who seem to understand the trees' needs almost before the trees do. They accept these gifts as they accept the rain: naturally, without question, with gratitude for what promotes growth.

And in the deep chambers where the being's presence touched most directly, certain spores begin a slow germination that will take lifetimes to fruit. When they finally bloom, they will release not pollen but possibilities—packets of genetic and memetic information that will help the city take its next great leap in the endless dance of becoming.

The wise being moves on, satisfied. It has gardened well here, with the lightest of touches. The seeds are planted. The rest, as always, is up to time and patience and the inexorable creativity of life finding its way forward.

In the canopy city that breathes with ten thousand leaves, the future has been invited in. Now all that remains is the growing.
