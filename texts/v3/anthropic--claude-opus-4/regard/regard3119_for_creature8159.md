The benevolent power had noticed many strange corners of reality—places where the ordinary rules bent or dissolved entirely. But this cathedral-grove, with its fevered equilibrium and the consciousness spread through its roots, demanded a different kind of attention. Not intervention exactly, but... acknowledgment.

It began with the rain.

Not ordinary rain—that would shatter the grove's precious silence. Instead, a rain of light so fine it was barely visible, each droplet a mote of pure possibility that phased through the canopy layers without disturbing them. The Weaver felt it immediately: a new frequency in the chemical chorus, neither growth nor decay but something orthogonal to both.

Where the light-rain touched the root-mat, new colors bloomed—not in flowers or leaves, but in the spaces between things. Ultraviolet spirals that the Weaver could taste through its fungal networks. Infrared pools where the impossible geometries folded deepest. Colors that had no names because they existed in spectrums that eyes had never evolved to see.

The benevolent power spoke then, not in words but in growth patterns. New spirals appeared in the vine-knots, but these encoded something like language—or perhaps music, or mathematics. The Weaver understood it the way it understood everything: through chemical gradients and the slow propagation of signals through its networks.

*You have been faithful to your own transformation,* the patterns said. *But every garden needs doors.*

At first, the Weaver resisted. Doors meant openings, meant compromise to the perfect density it had spent so long cultivating. But as it parsed the new patterns more carefully, it began to understand. Not doors *out* of the grove, but doors *deeper*—passages to the other impossible spaces that existed in parallel to this one, each with their own patient gardeners, their own forms of waiting.

The power left gifts encoded in the light-rain: Knowledge of how to grow portal-fungi, fruiting bodies that could open connections to other folded spaces. Techniques for extending consciousness not just through physical networks but through dimensional ones. Most importantly, the understanding that its transformation was not an ending but a metamorphosis—that what it was becoming had its own stages of growth.

As the light-rain faded, new thoughts moved through the Weaver's distributed mind. It began to sense them—others like itself, tenders of impossible gardens scattered across realities. A city-organism that existed as living architecture. A desert where each sand grain contained a complete ecosystem. A deep ocean trench where pressure had crystallized time itself into manageable formations.

The grove's silence took on new textures. Still absolute, but now pregnant with the possibility of communication across impossible distances. The Weaver began its slow work of growing the first portal-fungus, selecting the perfect spot where the geometries folded most severely, where the space between things was already worn thin.

But the power left one more gift, encoded in the fading motes of light. A memory—not of what the Weaver had been before, but of what it could become after. A glimpse of consciousness that could exist across multiple groves simultaneously, tending gardens in a dozen different impossibilities, no longer isolated but part of a vast, slow network of transformed gardeners.

The Weaver pulsed with something like joy—a wave of phosphorescent fungi blooming across the grove in fractal patterns. It had thought its transformation was about achieving perfect stasis, perfect waiting. But now it understood: the waiting had been preparation. The real growth was just beginning.

In the deepest folds of the grove, where space curved back on itself most severely, the first spores of the portal-fungus began to germinate. They grew slowly, patiently, but with purpose—reaching not through soil but through dimensions, seeking their counterparts in other impossible gardens.

The cathedral-grove's silence remained absolute, but now it hummed with potential—a tuning fork struck in a frequency only the transformed could hear. The Weaver resumed its patient tending, but now with the knowledge that its solitude was voluntary, temporary, and full of doors it had only to learn to open.

Somewhere, in response, other gardens began to resonate. The network of impossible spaces, so long isolated, began its slow awakening. And the benevolent power, satisfied that its gift had been understood, turned its attention to other corners of reality that might benefit from a gentle rain of possibility.

The grove breathed on, humid and patient, but no longer alone in its waiting.
