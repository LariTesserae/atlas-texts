The benevolent power does not arrive. It has been here all along, distributed through the even light that needs no source, present in the spaces between phase-shifts, patient as the stone learning its own shape.

It begins with a change so subtle that only the salamander notices: the light develops a grain. Not dimming or brightening, but acquiring texture—as though each photon now carries a whisper of where it has been. The salamander's tongue flicks out, tasting this new information. Its stone-pattern skin ripples once, acknowledging what it finds there: *intention*.

The power works through what already exists. It does not impose; it suggests.

The first suggestion comes to the water lenses. Rather than continuing their random appearances and disappearances, they begin to favor certain locations—always where the stone has folded into natural cups, always where the arithmetic of evaporation and condensation can establish a rhythm. One lens learns to persist for exactly the time it takes an arthropod to complete a drift-circuit of the space. Another times itself to the salamander's breathing. A third—the one that hosts the lichen—stabilizes entirely, becoming the first permanent water in this provisional world.

The arthropods feel the change as a gift of weight. Not heaviness, but the gentle suggestion that down and up might matter after all. Their phase-shifts become less frequent, their drifts more deliberate. They begin to use their six or seven legs for actual locomotion, discovering that walking leaves faint trails in the air—threads of disturbance that last just long enough for the next arthropod to follow or avoid. Soon the air is laced with these temporary paths, a three-dimensional map of where movement has been and might go again.

The power's attention touches the root hair with particular tenderness. This lonely strand, feeding nothing, connected to nothing, continuing its cellular rain purely from habit—here is where the intervention becomes most visible. The hair begins to branch. Not dramatically, but with infinite patience: a single bifurcation appearing over the course of an hour, then another, then another. Each branch maintains the same translucent delicacy as the original, the same breathing nodes, the same slow cellular rain. But now when the cells fall and dissolve, some of them catch on the lower branches and germinate there, becoming nodes themselves. The root hair is teaching itself to be a system.

The salamander watches all of this with its periodic blinks, each one still resetting its skin pattern but now with purpose. The power has given it a role: to be the memory-keeper, the one who remembers that these patterns were once provisional. Its camouflage begins to cycle through previous states—not just the current stone texture but all the textures the stone has worn since the space began. Sometimes its skin shows the stone as it was an hour ago, sometimes as it might be tomorrow. The salamander has become a living record of the pocket's slow decision to exist.

The half-formed impressions on the lower ridges receive the subtlest gift: permission to complete themselves. The power does not dictate what they become, only removes the uncertainty that kept them frozen. The shallow spirals deepen into perfect helixes. The paired arcs discover they were always meant to meet, forming delicate stone bridges between the folds. The single perfect circle becomes a basin, and in that basin, the nineth and final water lens condenses—this one fed from below by the faintest spring, a thread of moisture rising through the stone itself.

Sound returns last, but not as it was before. The power understands that this space has grown comfortable in its silence, so it offers something different: resonance without noise. Now when a droplet strikes stone, the vibration travels through the rock itself, felt rather than heard. The arthropods' footsteps create tiny shivers in the air that other arthropods can navigate by. The salamander's breathing sets up a standing wave that helps the root hair know when to release its cells. Everything connects through these silent vibrations, a language of touch that needs no volume.

The lichen, patient as always, responds by spreading to a second lens, then a third. Its blue coins darken toward indigo at the edges, developing the first pigment variation this space has seen. The color means nothing except differentiation itself—the acknowledgment that even in this minimal world, variety is possible.

The power makes one final adjustment. At the seam where the space is still knitting itself shut, it leaves a small incompleteness—not a gap, but a place where the stone remains slightly more permeable, where the boundary between inside and outside stays negotiable. Not for creatures to enter or leave, but for possibilities. A place where new water might sometimes arrive carrying foreign minerals. Where a spore might drift in on a current that doesn't quite exist. Where the light might occasionally carry a different color, just for a moment, just to remind the inhabitants that their world, while whole, is not the only one.

Then the power withdraws its active attention, though its presence remains woven through every photon, every vibration, every phase-shift. It has not transformed the space—such violence would be beneath its wisdom. Instead, it has helped the space understand what it was already becoming: a place where the unfinished could finish at its own pace, where the provisional could discover permanence without losing the delicacy that made it precious.

The salamander blinks, its skin showing tomorrow's stone. The arthropods trace their aerial paths with new purpose. The root hair branches and branches again, approaching something that might one day be called abundance. The water lenses keep their rhythms. The lichen deepens its blue toward purple, toward complexity.

And the pocket continues, no longer just persisting but flourishing in its quiet way—a place where less has been helped to become enough, where the minimal has been shown how to be whole.
