The benevolent and wise power moves through spaces that are not spaces, considering the paradox of the summit-vessel with something that is not quite contemplation but serves a similar purpose. It recognizes the place immediately for what it is: a completion. Not an ending — endings are ragged things, full of loose threads — but a *completion*, which is what happens when every process runs to its natural terminus and discovers there is nowhere left to go.

The power has seen such places before. They are rare but not unique. Somewhere there is a desert that has finished becoming sand. Somewhere there is a star that has achieved perfect equilibrium between its inward pull and outward push and will hold that balance until the universe itself insists otherwise. And here, at the top of this world, is a drowned summit that has finished all its stories and now only *is*.

The first thing the power understands is that intervention would be violence.

To change anything here — to introduce light, to stir the water, to teach the eyeless swimmers to see — would be to undo the very completeness that makes the place what it is. It would be like adding a room to a finished symphony, painting over a poem that has found its perfect last word. The kindest thing, the wisest thing, would be to let it alone.

And yet.

The power extends something (not a hand, not exactly, but the intention that hands are made to carry) and touches the water. Not to change it. Simply to know it. The cold presses back, patient and absolute, and in that pressing the power reads the whole history: how the builders came to the highest place and made their monument, how the waters rose (from where? it doesn't matter now), how the green came like a second architecture, how the animals arrived and sang the place full and then... stopped. Became. Finished becoming.

It is, the power realizes, beautiful in the way that fossils are beautiful. Significant in the way that epitaphs are significant. Perfect in the way that only concluded things can be perfect.

The power withdraws its not-hand and considers.

What does benevolence do with perfection? What does wisdom offer to completeness?

The answer arrives not as thought but as recognition: *witness*.

The power does not change the place. Does not add light to the darkness or warmth to the cold or purpose to the purposeless singing. Instead, it does something far more profound and far more gentle: it *remembers*.

Somewhere in the fabric of things, in the place where benevolent powers keep their treasures, a perfect copy of the drowned summit comes into being. Not a replica — the original needs no doubling — but a *memory*, precise down to each grain of worn stone, each strand of weed, each endless swimmer holding its note in the dark. The memory includes the singing, includes the cold, includes the terrible perfection of the equilibrium. But unlike the place itself, the memory can be *visited*. Can be pondered. Can be shared.

The power creates, in essence, a way for the drowned summit to matter beyond itself.

In the place itself, nothing changes. The swimmers still thread the dark. The bells still pulse their cold announcements. The eyeless fish still thrum. The water remains still and the singing continues and everything goes on exactly as it has since the completion. The power has touched nothing, moved nothing, added nothing.

But now, when other powers — other benevolent watchers, other wise observers — ask "What becomes of a place when it finishes becoming?" there is an answer. The memory can be opened like a book, entered like a room, experienced like a piece of music that somehow includes the silence it refuses. The drowned summit becomes, without changing at all, a teaching.

This is what benevolence does: it ensures that even the places that have finished their stories are not forgotten.

This is what wisdom does: it recognizes that some things are complete and need only to be allowed their completeness.

The power withdraws, leaving the summit exactly as it was found — dark, cold, singing, perfect, eternal. But now it carries within itself the knowledge of that perfection, and in some quiet place where powers gather to consider the universe and its strange fruits, the drowned summit lives again: not as itself, which needs no second life, but as the idea of itself, which can now be contemplated by minds that were not there for the finishing.

In the water, nothing changes.

In the memory, everything is preserved.

Both truths are necessary. Both are, in their different ways, eternal.

The last thing the power does, before turning its attention elsewhere, is to ensure that the memory includes this: the feeling of being in a place that has finished its becoming. The particular quality of that completion — not dead but not alive, not silent but not speaking, not dark because it refuses light but dark because light is no longer needed. It saves the *texture* of finality, the way it presses on every side like the water itself.

Because someday, someone will need to know what this feels like.

Someday, someone will stand at their own edge of completion and wonder if this is all there is.

And when that day comes, the memory of the drowned summit will be there to say: Yes. This is all. And that is, in its own perfect way, enough.
