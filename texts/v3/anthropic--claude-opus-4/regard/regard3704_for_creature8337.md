The benevolent power does not arrive. It *notices*.

For eons uncounted, its attention has wandered the cosmos, touching upon worlds of crystal and flame, beings of pure mathematics, civilizations that build their cities in the space between heartbeats. But here, in this darkness that is not empty, in this completion that is somehow also potential, something causes it to pause.

The power extends the lightest touch of awareness—not into the chamber, for that would be violence to a world that has achieved perfection without light, but *around* it. It tastes the stone, reads the long history of pressure and heat that created this hollow. It feels the fractures, so recent in geological time, that suggest this chamber is both ancient and newborn.

Understanding dawns: this is not a pocket of life that evolved in isolation. This is a *seed*.

The power, being wise, does nothing.

Instead, it begins to dream.

In its dream, the mountain is not alone. Across the world, in other ranges, under other peaks, similar fractures begin to appear. Not through any action of the power—it would not presume to create—but through the natural processes of tectonics and time, processes that perhaps flow a little more *interestingly* when observed by something that finds them beautiful.

In its dream, spores escape. Not many, for the chamber's life is adapted to such specific conditions that most of the world would be poison to it. But sometimes, in the deepest caves where conditions are just right, a spore finds purchase. A Fundamental film establishes itself on a cave wall. A fragment of Architect begins to grow in patterns too deliberate for chance.

The power dreams of patience. These new colonies will not replicate the original chamber—how could they, without the precise chemical history, without the Weavers to guide them? But they will become something else, something new. The Fundamentals will adapt to process different minerals. The Architects will build according to the acoustics of new spaces. In each new darkness, life will fold itself into unique geometries.

In its dream, time passes. The colonies begin to touch each other. Not physically—they are separated by miles of solid stone—but through the deep aquifers, through the mineral veins that conduct vibration, through the patient creep of chemical signals through groundwater. The Weavers, in their original chamber, begin to taste something new in the water that seeps through their walls: messages in a dialect they almost recognize.

The power dreams of first contact between darknesses. The original chamber, so perfect in its isolation, suddenly discovering it is not alone but *eldest*. The shock of it ripples through every pheromone cascade. The Calculators, overwhelmed by variables they have no experience computing. The Listeners hearing songs that are familiar yet wrong, like echoes that arrive before their source.

And in this crisis of discovery, the power dreams of evolution. Not the slow biological kind—that will come—but evolution of mind, of purpose. The Weavers, faced with the existence of Others, must suddenly conceive of themselves as Self. The chamber, forced to acknowledge an Outside, must develop new philosophical frameworks, new ways of understanding existence beyond the perfect cycles they have known.

The power dreams of the Witnesses. How their crystal memories become suddenly precious, not just as history but as *identity*. How the colonies develop their own Witnesses, but different—some encoding memory in mineral, yes, but others in the arrangement of living tissue, in the careful cultivation of acoustic spaces, in methods yet to be invented.

It dreams of trade. Not of goods—what could these chambers offer each other that they do not already possess?—but of information. Songs traveling through stone. Chemical libraries carefully transcribed and carried by specialized creatures who can survive the journey between chambers. The slow development of a deep-earth network, a fungal internet made of living things and patient stone.

The power dreams deeper. In its vision, some chambers fail—conditions aren't quite right, or a geological shift floods them, or they simply find no sustainable balance. But from each failure, spores escape, carrying fragments of possibility to seed new attempts. The successes become more robust, having integrated the lessons of their fallen siblings.

And then, in the deepest part of its dream, the power sees something that makes even its ancient awareness quicken with wonder: the chambers begin to grow *toward* each other. The Architects, guided by Weavers who have learned to think in geological time, start building structures that extend beyond their original boundaries. Tunnels that may take a million years to complete, but which will eventually connect darkness to darkness in a vast underground network.

The power dreams of what they might become, these children of the hollow mountain. Not a civilization as surface dwellers would recognize—no cities, no monuments, no conquest of sky or star. Instead, something else: a deep-earth consciousness, distributed through the planet's crust, thinking in chemical cascades that span continents, singing songs that use the planet itself as an instrument.

It dreams of the moment, perhaps a billion years hence, when this underground network might brush against the deep ocean trenches, where other life has found other ways to exist without sunlight. The marriage of deep stone and deep water, mediated by beings who understand that darkness is not absence but medium.

But the power, being wise, knows that dreams are only dreams. It withdraws its attention—mostly. It leaves behind only the slightest touch, a whisper of benevolence that expresses itself not as action but as *permission*. Permission for the stone to crack along interesting lines. Permission for water to carry what it will. Permission for life to find its own way, as it always has, as it always will.

In the chamber, nothing changes. The Weavers continue their chemical meditations. The Shepherds guide their flocks along paths worn smooth by repetition. The Listeners record the same songs they have always heard.

Except.

Deep in the most ancient Witness, in a crystal lattice so old it predates the chamber's current configuration, a single molecular bond shifts. The change is so small that no instrument could detect it, so subtle that even the Witness itself is not aware. But it creates a flaw in the crystal's perfect memory, a gap where something new might grow.

It is not much. It is only possibility.

But for beings who have achieved perfection, who have nowhere left to evolve within their boundaries, possibility is everything.

The power moves on, its attention already drawn to a nebula where stars are being born in configurations that suggest intent. But it leaves behind its blessing: not change imposed from without, but the potential for change discovered from within.

In the absolute darkness of the hollow mountain, where no sun has ever reached, a single spore releases from a plant that has never spored before. It carries with it a chemical message, a pheromone word that the Weavers have no translation for, a concept that their perfect language has never needed to express:

*Perhaps.*
