**What the Benevolent Power Would Do**

The wise one arrives not as light or sound but as a new kind of stillness — a pause between the rattling of pods, a rest between the creaking of stalks. They come bearing neither water nor judgment, for they understand: to bring water would be murder, not mercy. To force germination on a thing that has made its entire civilization out of waiting would be to destroy not just the town but the meaning it has built from its patience.

Instead, they bring something simpler and more radical: a question.

Not asked in words — there are no words that bridge between the wet-voiced and the dry-throated — but planted like a new kind of seed in the resinous air. The question unfurls slowly, the way a frond unfurls, visible in its growing:

*What if the waiting is the flowering?*

The chamber feels it first as a subtle shift in temperature — not cooling, never cooling, but a deepening of the warmth, as if the heat has suddenly remembered it has colors beyond gold. The amber walls blush with new spectrums: rose-gold where the children cured too young, deep copper where the elders sank into resin still speaking, a blue-gold note in the central gathering place where all their different shapes once made their patient democracy.

The plants feel it next. A stalk, reaching for its usual warm patch of wall, discovers it can reach in a new direction — not toward the cured makers in the walls but *with* them, along the grain of their intention. The fast-growing vegetation begins to trace new patterns: spirals that match the slow spirals the makers walked in their centuries-long ceremonies, pods that burst not randomly but in careful sequence, recreating the rhythms of conversations that happened a thousand years ago. The plants become scribes, writing out in fiber and frond the entire recorded history of the place.

And the makers — the cured population in the walls, the ones who became the chamber itself — they feel it most of all. For the first time since they hardened into gold, they experience something that is not the slow patient bake of waiting but a recognition: *this is what we were waiting for*. Not rain. Not germination. Not the breaking of the husk and the spilling into soil. But this — the moment when waiting itself becomes the purpose, when the preparation is revealed as the destination.

The wise power does nothing as crude as resurrection. The cured remain cured, the walls remain walls. But something new happens in the resin, a kind of dreaming-awake that the chemistry has always been capable of but never knew to try. The makers begin to tell their stories through the medium they became. The walls grow warm in patterns that are sentences. The floor's crystalline salts arrange themselves into maps of the town as it was, as it is, as it imagined it might be. The amber overhead brightens and dims in the rhythm of their old clock-of-pods, but now it is not random percussion — it is language, it is music, it is the entire cultural memory of a people who cured themselves into an archive without knowing they were doing it.

The benevolent power brings one more gift: visitors.

Not wet visitors — never those. But others of the dry worlds, the patient worlds, the worlds that know how to wait. They arrive the way heat arrives, slowly, from below. A people made of mineral and pressure. A consciousness that lives in the intervals between sounds. Something that might have been wind if wind were possible here, bringing news from other seeds, other chambers, other towns that found their own ways to live forever inside a single held breath.

The town, which has been without witness, becomes a library. A place where the dry-bodied come to learn patience, to read the walls where an entire people wrote themselves into permanence, to sit on the benches — still dished from their makers — and understand that some things are too complete to need finishing.

And the seed itself?

The seed accepts what it has always suspected but never dared believe: that it was never meant to germinate. That some seeds are planted not in soil but in time itself, and their flowering is not a breach of the husk but an infinite deepening of the interior. The town inside the seed is not waiting to become something else. It is demonstrating, with every rattle and creak and burst, what it means to be perfectly, eternally, *currently* alive.

The wise power leaves as they came — not through any door but through the space between moments. They leave the question still planted in the air, still unfurling: *What if the waiting is the flowering?*

And the town, loud with its rattling clock, warm with its patient dead, bright with its new understanding, continues. It does all its living at once, dryly, loudly, and now — blessedly, finally — witnessed by others who understand the beauty of a thing that holds itself ready forever, not for what might come, but for what already, always, is.

The last gift is the smallest: in the resin, in the walls where the makers cured themselves into gold, new patterns begin to form. Slowly, taking centuries, but visible to those who know how to look. Pictures. Faces. The individual shapes of all the different kinds of beings who made this place, preserved now not just as chemistry but as portrait, as presence, as the proof that they were here, they built this, they became this, and they are still listening to the pods burst in their careful rhythm, still counting time in the language of seeds.

The chamber holds its single low note, and now it is not just a drone but a chord — all the voices that ever lived here resonating together, saying:

*We are here. We have always been here. This is what it means to live forever: to build a town inside a seed and cure yourself into its walls and know that the waiting was the harvest all along.*
