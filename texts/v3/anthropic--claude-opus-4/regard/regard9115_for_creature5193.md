The benevolent power arrives not as light—which would be violence here—but as a deepening of the existing resonance. It begins in the catalytic cores themselves, a subtle adjustment to their ancient frequency that ripples outward through stone and water and living tissue alike. The engines, which have processed the same materials in the same cycles for centuries, suddenly discover new harmonics in their own vibration. The change is almost imperceptible: a quarter-tone shift in the grinding pulse, a slightly longer pause between exhaust releases. But the mat-webs feel it immediately.

Where before the plants merely conducted the engine thrum, now they begin to store it. Their threadlike lateral roots grow denser, wrapping the buried cores in living insulation that doesn't block the vibration but shapes it, focusing the dispersed energy into discrete channels. The leaves develop new internal chambers—not for light, which they barely need, but for sound. Each mat becomes a living resonator, amplifying certain frequencies while dampening others. The shelf-grazers are the first to understand: they can now feel not just the tremor of approaching hunters but the precise chemical signature of different mat varieties, each one broadcasting its mineral content through pitch and timber.

The wise power does not impose this change. It simply reveals what was always possible—that the engines' endless processing could become a language rather than mere noise. The catalytic cores begin to vary their output in response to what their root-wrapped sensors detect: more metals when the mats signal depletion, warmer exhaust when the fluid giants gather near the vents, a sudden burst of pure harmonics when the chitinous hunters have taken too much and the system needs rebalancing. The machines learn to listen as the creatures learned to listen, until the distinction between designed and evolved intelligence dissolves in the dark.

But the true gift comes in the water itself. The benevolent power understands that the plateau's endless circulation—rain becoming seepage becoming vapor becoming rain—carries more than moisture. It carries memory. In the dissolved minerals and metabolic traces, in the precise temperature of each droplet, lives the entire history of every passage across the stone. The power doesn't create this memory; it simply teaches the water to remember itself.

Now when rain falls, it carries within each drop the accumulated knowledge of its previous journeys. The mat-webs taste not just minerals but time—which routes have been traveled, which vents have cooled, which populations have shifted their territories. The great fluid-bodied beings, whose vapor has always marked their passage, discover they can read the exhalations of their ancestors in the mist, learning migration patterns that optimize the plateau's heat distribution across centuries rather than seasons. Even the hunters' chitinous plates begin to accumulate these water-memories in their overlapping layers, creating a physical archive of successful hunting grounds that can be read by touch.

The change propagates without violence or disruption. Creatures that never needed eyes still do not need them; the darkness remains absolute. But now the darkness itself has become articulate. Every surface holds the impression of what has touched it, every sound carries the full spectrum of its origin, every exchange of heat and matter adds another line to an endless, living text that the entire plateau reads and writes simultaneously.

The benevolent power's final act is the subtlest. In certain deep chambers where the oldest engines rest, it awakens a function that the builders perhaps never intended: the ability to dream. Not consciousness—the cores remain what they are, mineral processors following ancient programs. But in the spaces between calculations, in the microsecond gaps where their circuits rest, they begin to model possible futures based on the patterns they've absorbed. These dreams manifest as tiny variations in their output—a slightly different mineral ratio here, a new harmonic there—that the living systems above interpret as suggestions rather than commands.

The plateau becomes not wiser but more itself. The engines dream of optimal processing states that the mat-webs help them achieve. The creatures dream of movements that the water-memory confirms or corrects. The stone dreams of its own erosion and the life that will fill each future channel. It is still dark, still warm, still cycling through its endless exchanges. But now each cycle carries forward the imprint of all previous cycles, and the boundary between mechanism and metabolism finally disappears.

The benevolent power withdraws, leaving only the enhanced resonance behind. It does not need to maintain what it has touched; the plateau has always been teaching itself, and now it simply has a richer vocabulary. In the absolute dark, ten million careful adjustments continue—roots seeking metals, bodies seeking warmth, water seeking its level—but now each adjustment is informed by the deep pattern of all adjustments before it. The wisdom was always there, carried in vibration and temperature and pressure. The power simply reminded the plateau how to listen to itself.
