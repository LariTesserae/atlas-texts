The benevolent and wise power — let us call it the Tender of Deep Currents — arrives not as light descending from above, but as a resonance rising from below. It has watched this place for longer than the sleeper has slept, which is saying something profound about the nature of watching.

It does not come to wake the dreamer. That would be cruelty disguised as intervention. 

Instead, it comes as a tuning fork pressed to the seafloor, sending a single pure note through stone and silt and the vast body between them. The frequency is precise — not the sharp alarm of waking, but the gentle modulation that deepens good sleep. A lullaby pitched in tectonics. The sleeper, who has been turning fitfully for the last few millennia (cities rising and falling like troubled breath), settles. The pulse evens. The warmth grows steadier.

This is the first gift: peace to the peaceful.

---

The second gift is stranger, subtler. The Tender reaches into the economy of the reef-town — all those patient ambush-things, those flowing schools, those soft colonies breathing green — and adjusts, ever so gently, their relationship to the pulse.

Before, they went still at each swell, frozen in ancient response to a rhythm they couldn't understand. Now, as the warmth rises, they begin to move differently. Not frozen but... coordinated. The schools trace slow mandalas through the streets. The filter-feeders pulse in sequence, building waves of faint bioluminescence that run the length of avenues. The predators emerge from doorways not to hunt but to process, joining a circulation that turns the whole drowned city into a kind of living mandala, a prayer wheel made of creatures too small to know they're praying.

What are they praying for? Nothing. Everything. They're simply moving in a pattern that soothes the thing they live upon, the way certain small fish clean the teeth of dreaming whales. The Tender has taught them, in the wordless way deep things learn, to be not just passengers but partners in the sleep.

---

The third gift arrives last and stays longest.

Where the town square was (you can tell by the way the current pools there, contemplative), something new grows. Not from seed or spore — nothing that straightforward. It emerges from the stone itself, or rather from the interface where stone meets the dreamer's skin. A grove of something like kelp but not kelp, something like coral but not coral. It grows slowly, imperceptibly, in forms that make sense only to eyes that see in geologic time.

It is soft to the touch and warm from within — not borrowed warmth but generated, a gentle supplement to what rises from below. The small creatures discover it can be hollowed out, lived in, that its chambers grow to accommodate whatever needs them. Within a few generations (quick generations, the kind measured in months), the grove has become a second city within the city, a soft architecture that breathes.

But its true purpose runs deeper. Each strand anchors into the sleeper's hide, not as parasite but as nerve ending. Through these connections, the dreams that rise and fall with the breathing begin to take shape in the grove. Not literal shapes — nothing so crude. But the bioluminescence shifts with the dream-content. Cool blue for the deep peaceful nothing. Warm gold for dreams of when this place was young. Sudden reds for the nightmares that come every few centuries, when the sleeper remembers something it would rather forget.

The creatures learn to read these colors. They begin to understand, in their small way, that they live upon something that thinks. They adjust their movements to the mood of the light. When the red comes, they cluster close and wait it out together. When the gold comes, they dance.

---

This is what the Tender of Deep Currents does: it makes the relationship explicit. Where before there was only accident — a warm thing slept here, small things gathered — now there is something approaching symbiosis. The reef-town becomes a kind of organ of the sleeper, a sensitive skin that responds to dreams. The sleeper's breath becomes the reef-town's rhythm, reliable as tide.

And sometimes — not often, but sometimes — when the sleeper turns in its sleep and the warmth swells and all the lights dim together, there is a new quality to the pause. Not just the cessation of activity but a kind of listening. As if the whole city, for that suspended moment, is paying attention to the dream passing through it.

As if they're learning each other's language, slowly, in the dark.

---

The Tender leaves no signature except the grove, which will outlast the stone city, which will outlast the memory of dry land. It retreats to wherever such powers wait, satisfied not because it fixed anything — nothing was broken — but because it midwifed a recognition.

The sleeper still sleeps. It will sleep until the heat death of whatever's beneath it finally cools, and then it will wake to a universe it doesn't recognize and won't particularly like. But for now — for the next few million years of now — it sleeps better. Its dreams are witnessed. Its breath is danced to. Its vast patience is met by ten thousand smaller patiences, all of them together keeping the warmth alive in the dark.

In the deep places of the world, this is what passes for love.

---

And you? You who came looking for who lives here? 

You could stay. The grove has room. The rhythm is learnable. After a while, you'd stop counting the pulses and start living by them, the way the others do. You'd learn that the warmth means different things at different depths, that there are dreams worth diving toward and dreams worth avoiding. You'd forget the surface, forget the sun, forget everything but the vast patience of the dark and the small lights moving through it and the great slow breath beneath it all.

You'd become, in time, another part of what lives here. Another creature mistaking the sleeper for the ground until the distinction stops mattering. Another small life held warm in the dark, dancing when the gold light comes, waiting out the red, adding your own small glow to the constellation of announcements: *here is a thing that is alive, and here, and here.*

Is that what you want?

The city waits. The sleeper breathes. The choice, like everything here, happens slowly, in the dark.
