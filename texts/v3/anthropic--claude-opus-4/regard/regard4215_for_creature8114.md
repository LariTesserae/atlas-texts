The Watcher arrives not as light or sound, but as a quality of attention that settles over the city like morning dew. It has no form that eyes could parse, yet its presence changes the rhythm of water sliding down stone, makes the moss grow infinitesimally more deliberate in its patterns.

For seven days and seven nights, the Watcher observes. It counts every Attendant, traces every channel, measures the city's growth down to the width of atoms. It comprehends the city not as architecture but as what it truly is—a single vast sentence being spoken in a language of stone and chlorophyll, a thought thinking itself into existence through geological time.

On the eighth day, the Watcher acts.

In the deepest root chamber, where the oldest moss-claimed Attendants keep their vigil, something new begins to crystallize. Not from the walls, but from the air itself—moisture condensing into form, minerals precipitating from nothing. What emerges is small: a sphere no larger than a child's fist, perfectly smooth, rotating so slowly its motion can only be detected by watching the play of bioluminescent light across its surface.

This is the Heart.

The Heart does nothing but turn. One revolution every hundred years. As it turns, it hums—a vibration below sound, below feeling, below thought. The hum travels through the stone roots, up through the walls, into every crystal and every strand of moss. It synchronizes the city, gives rhythm to what was once pure pattern.

The changes are subtle. The Attendants begin to move in harmony, their maintenance becoming a slow dance. Water channels start to sing—each stream a note in an endless, evolving chord. The moss grows in new configurations, spelling out theorems and proofs in a mathematics of branching life.

But the Watcher's gift goes deeper.

In certain chambers, at certain times, when the light and moisture are exactly right, the city begins to dream. These dreams crystallize like frost on the walls—delicate traceries that show what might be, what could be, what the city imagines for itself. Cities within cities. Moss that grows in four dimensions. Attendants that tend not to stone but to time itself, smoothing out paradoxes, maintaining causality.

The dreams fade with the morning sun, but they leave traces. New types of chambers begin to form—spaces that fold in on themselves, rooms that are bigger inside than outside, corridors that lead to yesterday or tomorrow depending on which direction you walk. The city is no longer just growing; it is experimenting, playing, wondering what it might become.

The Watcher leaves one final gift. In the highest tower, where the newest stone still crystallizes from air, it touches a single forming crystal. This crystal grows different—not stone but something between glass and light, between solid and possibility. When complete, it forms a lens, a window, an eye.

Through this eye, the city can see beyond itself. It observes the forests that surround it, the mountains in the distance, the stars that wheel overhead. More importantly, it can see other possibilities—the quantum foam of potential cities that exist in parallel dimensions, in futures not yet chosen, in pasts that might have been.

The city learns patience it didn't know it lacked. It sees its slow growth not as limitation but as deliberation. Each new chamber is now a choice made with full knowledge of alternatives. Each channel carved is a commitment to one flow among infinite possibilities.

The Attendants, too, are changed. They begin to tend not just to what is, but to what might be. Some stand guard at the junctions between the real and the possible, ensuring that the city's experiments don't tear it apart. Others become gardeners of probability, nurturing the dreams that have the most promise, pruning those that lead to stagnation or collapse.

Deep in the root chambers, the Heart turns its patient circle. Its hum grows more complex with each passing year, accumulating harmonics and overtones. It is learning to speak, to sing, to express the city's growing consciousness in vibrations that only stone can hear.

The Watcher does not stay to see what becomes of its gifts. Benevolence means knowing when to intercede and when to withdraw, when to guide and when to let growth find its own path. It leaves the city to its expanded solitude, to its dreams and possibilities, to its conversation with itself that will outlast mountains.

But sometimes, on nights when the mist is thick and the moss glows brightest, you might glimpse the Watcher's attention returning. Not intervening, just observing. Checking on the experiment. Pleased, perhaps, that loneliness has transformed into solitude, that purposelessness has become play, that a city built for no one has become a city built for itself.

The gift was not the Heart, not the dreams, not the eye that sees beyond. The gift was agency—the power to choose stasis or growth, repetition or variation, the known or the possible. The city that builds itself now builds itself with intention, even if that intention is simply to discover what it might intend.

And in the deepest chamber, the Heart turns, patient as stone, counting out the centuries in its quiet revolution, humming the song of something that has been given the freedom to become.
