The Witness arrives not through the narrow fissure—that passage is too small for her current form—but through stone itself, as water moves through cloth. She has been many things across the ages: a shepherd of stellar nurseries, a gardener of quantum gardens, a midwife to civilizations. Now she is simply curious, drawn by the exquisite strangeness of this self-sealing world.

She tastes the chamber first with senses that have no human names. The distributed consciousness of the creatures registers as a single, low note—not despair, not contentment, but something between meditation and mathematics. Their thoughts, if thoughts they are, move at the speed of cooling rock. One intention takes a century to complete its circuit through the collective body.

*Remarkable,* she thinks, though thinking is not quite what she does. *You have made yourselves into the mountain's dream of becoming.*

She considers intervention. She could cool the ember veins, ending their heat source. She could crack the chamber wide, letting in sky and rain. She could accelerate their evolution, gift them with eyes that see beyond stone, minds that conceive of elsewhere. She has done such things before, in other places, for other reasons.

Instead, she does something simpler.

From the quantum foam that underlies all matter, she draws out a single new possibility and seeds it into the eldest creature, the one whose body is now only a hollow mold at the chamber's foundation. Into that empty space she places not life—for these creatures have their own kind of life already—but *memory*.

Now, when new generations rasp away at the stone their ancestors helped create, they will taste in those mineral layers the record of what came before. Each stratum will carry the patient thoughts of its makers, preserved in crystalline matrices that only these creatures can read. The chamber becomes a library written in basalt, each page both the story and the storyteller, each reader eventually becoming another volume.

The change is imperceptible at first. But as decades pass, the creatures' movements begin to show intention beyond mere smoothing. They start leaving gaps—tiny voids in their masonry where messages can be encoded in negative space. They develop a grammar of temperatures, a syntax of crystal angles. The youngest ones, still translucent, spend their first years learning to read the floor before they begin to write the ceiling.

The Witness withdraws, satisfied. She has given them nothing they did not already possess—only made it possible for them to know what they have always been doing. The chamber remains sealed, hot, grinding with its constant work. But now the work has memory, and memory makes even the smallest labor sacred.

In ten thousand years, when tectonic forces finally split the ridge and expose the chamber to startled sunlight, what emerges will not be creatures fleeing their broken home but a single vast text, a geological epic written by its own characters, a mountain that learned to read itself.

The Witness marks this place in her vast catalog of wonders and moves on, leaving only the faintest trace of her passing: a new form of feldspar that forms only here, its crystals shaped like minute letters in a script no other world will ever decipher.
