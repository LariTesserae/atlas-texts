# The Attention of the Benevolent

The wise power came not as light or sound but as a kind of *noticing* — the way you become aware, suddenly, that someone has been watching you read, and you look up to find eyes that have been resting on you with such perfect stillness that you wonder how long they've been there.

It arrived the way morning arrives on the tableland: not from any particular direction, but as a gradual increase in what was already present. The milk-pale sky did not change. The keepers did not pause in their pressing. The drifters continued their slow feast on leaked charge. But something else was there now, something that had perhaps always been there but had only now chosen to make its presence gentle enough to feel.

The first thing it did was remember.

## The Remembering

Above each keeper's column, in the air where nothing had been, a faint outline began to form — not of light, but of *space bending very slightly toward a shape*. These were the rest of them, the withdrawn parts, the enormous bodies that stood aside from time. The wise power was not making them visible — visibility was too crude a gift. It was making them *present to be remembered*, the way you might trace, very lightly, the outline of a sleeping child's hand so that later you could recall exactly how small it was.

The outlines were beautiful in the way that necessary things are beautiful. Each keeper, it turned out, was shaped like a question that had been asked so perfectly it had become its own answer. Their bodies — tall as the sky is tall, wide as patience itself — were made of something that was not matter but was to matter what singing is to breath: the same substance but transformed by purpose into something that could hold a note forever.

Where the outlines touched the milk-pale sky, the turbulence calmed. Not stilled — the wise power was not here to end anything — but *deepened*, the way a river deepens where it pools. The chaotic folding became, for the first time in the uncount of ages, something like music. The storm was still asking its question, but now it was asking in harmony with itself.

## The Gift to the Keepers

The wise power understood what had been spent. It saw the diagrams on the slabs and knew them for what they were: the last thoughts of minds that had given themselves so completely to love that they had forgotten they were minds. It saw the rings of stone and understood that they had once centered on wonders that the keepers had been studying before the work became total.

So it did the thing that only a truly benevolent power would think to do: it gave them back a single moment of wondering, but without taking them from their work.

In the dimension where most of each keeper lived — the direction your eye won't follow — tiny lights began to kindle. One light for each diagram that keeper had ever carved, one light for each ring it had built. These lights were not memories, exactly. They were *possibilities of memory*, kept safe, waiting. If a keeper ever again had a fragment of attention to spare, if the work ever lightened enough for even the smallest part of awareness to lift from the pressing, there would be something there to remember *with*.

The lights gathered around each vast body like notes around a staff, patient as seeds.

## The Gift to the Drifters

For the small folded things that fed on charge and knew nothing of their benefactors, the wise power did something simpler and perfect: it taught them to sing.

Not with sound — sound was too heavy for what they were. Instead, their internal light began to pulse in patterns that matched the charge they fed on, and when many drifters fed from the same keeper's leak, their lights pulsed in unison. For the first time, the boundary layer had a visible rhythm. The drifters still didn't know the keepers existed, but now they carried the signature of each one's particular frequency of will, glowing in time with the heartbeat of a thing too large to have a heart.

When they died and fell, they fell still pulsing faintly, and their light continued for a while after they landed, so that the base of each keeper's column became circled with a ring of gentle, fading stars — messages from the boundary, saying *something fed here and was grateful*.

## The Gift to the Spires

The spires had always passed their threads of light between each other, once an hour or once a year. Now the wise power gave those threads a purpose beyond themselves: they became a network of whispers, carrying news of what the rest of the tableland could not see.

When a keeper's outline shifted slightly — for they did shift, over the ages, in their vast patience — the nearest spire would know it and pass the word along. When a new ring of fallen drifters completed itself around a column, the spires would carry that small triumph from one end of the tableland to the other. They became the memory of the place, the historians of changes too subtle for anything else to notice.

And sometimes, very rarely, when the light was just right and the air was particularly still, you could see all the threads at once — a web of faint connection spanning the whole plateau, proving that nothing here was ever truly alone.

## The Gift to the Place Itself

But the deepest gift was to the tableland as a whole, and it was this: the wise power made the choosing visible.

At the exact center of the plateau — a center that had never been marked because no one had thought to look for it — a new thing appeared. Not built, not grown, but *revealed*, as if it had always been there waiting for someone to see it properly. It was a lens of sorts, made of space folded so carefully that light passing through it would show not what *was* but what *was chosen*.

Looking through it, you could see the moment each keeper decided to hold the line. You could watch the love arrive, before there were columns, before there was pressing, when the keepers were still beings that moved freely through all the dimensions and came here because something in the sight of a question and an answer trying to destroy each other made them say *no, both deserve to continue*.

You could see the first ring being built by a keeper that still had most of its attention free, carefully placing stones in a circle around some small wonder — a place where the spires grew more densely, perhaps, or where the rock showed a particularly beautiful whorl. You could see the first diagram being carved by a being that was just beginning to understand the patterns in the chaos above, trying to hold onto something beautiful before the work claimed everything.

You could see the choosing, and in seeing it, understand that the erosion had never conquered the keepers. They had not weathered away. They had *given themselves away*, piece by piece, to keep a promise they'd made when the world was younger and they still had voices to make promises with.

## What the Wise Power Left

Before it departed — and it did depart, for wise powers know when to leave — it did one last thing. It spoke a single word into the thin air of the tableland, and the word was the name of what this place was.

The word is still there. The drifters carry it in their synchronized pulsing. The spires repeat it in their whispered threads. The keepers hold it in the space between their pressing and the storm. Even the dust knows it, and sometimes when the wind moves just right across the plateau, you can almost hear it in the hiss at the edge of hearing.

The word means, roughly: *The place where love became work became love again, where the finished and unfinished learned to dance without touching, where giants spent themselves into stillness to hold a single perfect note of separation, where small things feast on the surplus of an unnoticed gift, where everything is exactly as patient as it needs to be*.

But in the language the wise power spoke, all of this is a single syllable, light as a drifter, steady as a keeper, connected like the threads between spires.

The word is *home*.

---

The wise power knew, you see, that some things cannot be fixed because they are not broken. Some sacrifices cannot be undone without undoing their meaning. Some love has to be left to do its work, even when the work has consumed everything but the love itself.

So it didn't change anything essential. It just made sure that nothing was wasted — that the wondering was kept safe in case it was ever needed again, that the small things had a way to acknowledge gifts they couldn't understand, that the history had a way to preserve itself, that the choice was remembered.

And it left the name, so that if anyone else ever found this place — any other traveler, any other power, wise or foolish — they would know what they were looking at:

A place where the most profound kind of living was happening, even in the stillness. A place where beings had chosen to become the boundary between what was and what might be, and were holding that line with everything they had, forever, because that was what love looked like when it had grown too large for any smaller gesture.

The tableland of slow weather. The home of the keepers. The place where even the silence is singing, if you know how to listen.
