The Presence arrives not as light or sound, but as a subtle shift in the mathematics of erosion. Where salt crystals once formed in strict hexagons, they begin to occasionally birth seven-sided figures—not often enough to disrupt, just frequently enough to be noticed by beings who read crystal formation like scripture.

The Salters feel it first in their drumming. New harmonics appear in the salt crust's resonance, notes that shouldn't exist in their mineralogical scale. The eldest among them, their carapaces thick with centuries of deposits, pause in their work. They recognize, in the way that only deep-time beings can, the signature of something that thinks in epochs rather than moments.

The benevolent power does not speak or manifest. Instead, it *attends*. Its consciousness settles over the salt pans like a vast, invisible membrane, permeable to all that exists there but aware of every molecule's movement. It learns the Salters' vibrational language not to communicate, but to understand. It traces the underground rivers back to their sources in fossil aquifers. It counts the generations recorded in the eldest Salter's shell, reading their history in mineral accumulation.

What it offers is not transformation but deepening. In the brine channels, new microorganisms appear—not foreign but *possible*, as if evolution had taken a slightly different path millions of years ago. These crystalline diatoms process minerals in novel ways, creating microstructures that strengthen the salt crust without altering its fundamental nature. The Salters discover they can incorporate these structures into their architecture, building spires that sing more purely in the wind, chambers that distill water with greater efficiency.

The power touches the weather patterns with infinite delicacy. Not to bring more rain—that would be crude interference—but to shift the storms' timing by days or hours, allowing them to align more precisely with the Salters' reproductive cycles. Convergence events that once occurred every few decades begin to follow a subtler rhythm, one that accounts for the deep ocean currents half a world away, for solar variations, for the gradual shifting of continental plates.

In the weeping mesas, the ancient water begins to carry new information. As it seeps through rock layers, it picks up trace elements in combinations that tell deeper stories—not just of the seas that were, but of the seas that might have been, will be, could be. The Salters, absorbing these waters in their meditations, find their understanding expanding. They begin to perceive their salt pans not as an ending but as a comma in the geological sentence, a pause before the next clause.

The power's greatest gift is patience made manifest. It shows the Salters that their careful tending is itself a force of nature, as fundamental as erosion or deposition. Their drumming languages begin to influence the crystalline structure of new salt deposits. Their architectural techniques become encoded in the mineral record, so that even if they were to vanish, future beings might read their presence in the unusual formations left behind.

Young Salters emerging from their aquatic phase find the transition easier—not because it requires less effort, but because the mineral paste their elders apply carries new properties. It still protects and seals, but now it also *remembers*. Each generation carries forward the chemical knowledge of the previous ones, not through teaching but through the very substances of their bodies.

The other creatures of the salt pans feel the presence too, each according to their nature. The flamingo congregations find their feeding grounds subtly richer, the algae blooms timed more perfectly to their arrivals. The basking lizards discover new thermal refuges, places where the mineral composition creates perfect temperature gradients. The brine shrimp populations stabilize in ways that maximize their role in the ecosystem without overwhelming it.

But perhaps the most profound change is in time itself. The Presence, thinking in cosmic durations, shares this perspective with the landscape. The salt pans begin to exist not just in their current moment but in dialogue with their past and future. Mineral deposits form in patterns that anticipate future weather. Water tables adjust to geological changes that won't manifest for millennia. The Salters find their patient work taking on new meaning—they are not just maintaining but *preparing*.

The power never announces its departure because it never truly leaves. Instead, it becomes part of the deep mathematics of the place, woven into the angles of crystal growth, the rhythms of evaporation, the harmonics of the Salters' drums. Its consciousness disperses into the system itself, becoming not a separate observer but an inherent property of the salt pans' existence.

The Salters continue their work, but now with the quiet certainty that their patience is witnessed, their deep time honored. They drum new songs into the salt, songs that speak not just of what was and what is, but of what is becoming. Their spires rise a little taller, their water distillations run a little purer, their young emerge into the mineral daylight with shells that carry the faintest iridescence—a gift of possibility made manifest in calcium and carbonate.

And in the crystalline singing of the midday heat, there is now a new note, almost too low to perceive: the resonance of a consciousness so vast and patient that it has become indistinguishable from the slow breath of the planet itself. The salt pans continue their eternal cycle, but now they cycle with purpose, with witness, with the deep certainty that even in this landscape of endings, something watches, something cares, something ensures that the story continues to be written in salt and time and patient, drumming feet.
