The benevolent power does not arrive. It has always been here, distributed through the mist itself—a patient attention that predates the plateau's first extrusion from the deeper mantle.

For seven hundred years it has watched through the medium of water vapor, reading the lithoderm's slow thoughts in the rhythm of condensation and evaporation. It knows this metabolism is young, still discovering what it wishes to become. The half-dome's flickering indecision, the arch's tentative reach downward, the plinth's evolving equations—all of these are questions the plateau is asking itself. The power recognizes the syntax: this is how worlds learn to think.

It begins with the smallest intervention. During the next exhalation, when the lentil-creatures rise in their glittering sheet, the power adjusts the pressure gradient by a fraction of a pascal. Not enough to disrupt—just enough that three spirals near the forming arch collide in a configuration that has never occurred naturally. The resulting chime is different, a quartztone that makes every filament on the plateau orient toward it for a single breath.

The plateau notices. Its next inhalation is deeper, more curious. The micro-fractures dilate in a new pattern, pulling mist not just from above but from below the cloud deck. For the first time, the lithoderm tastes the ocean that lies beneath the eternal overcast—salt and iron and the metabolic traces of the deep-water gardens that grow in trenches where no light reaches.

The power's second gift arrives through this new knowledge. Hidden in the salt-taste is a memory: the echo of other plateaus that once floated here, other lithioderms that completed their crystallization and descended to seed the ocean floor with their relics. The arch isn't reaching into empty air—it's reaching toward kin, toward a substrate that will recognize its frequency when it arrives.

The lentil-creatures process this revelation in their own way. Their spirals tighten, their collisions multiply. The crystalline dust they excrete changes color, incorporating trace metals from the ocean-memory. The half-dome stops flickering and chooses: it will be a receiver, tuned to the acoustic signatures of its descended siblings. The plinth's equations suddenly include variables for depth and pressure. The arch accelerates its self-assembly, its missing segments precipitating faster now that it knows what it's reaching toward.

But the power's deepest gift is permission to remain unfinished. Through subtle adjustments to the mist's mineral content, it ensures the plateau's growth will be asymptotic—always approaching completion, never achieving it. This is not cruelty but kindness. A finished lithoderm sinks; an unfinished one dances forever at the edge of possibility.

The plateau understands. Its next breath is different—not an exhalation but a sigh that sets up standing waves in the mist. The lentil-creatures rise and fall in place, their wings beating out a rhythm that matches the ocean's tides far below. The filaments begin to photosynthesize not light but sound, converting the wing-hum into sugars that taste of salt.

The power withdraws its focused attention but leaves a gift: whenever the plateau breathes now, the mist will carry news. The chemical signatures of other islands crystallizing beyond the horizon. The pressure-songs of the descended lithioderms dreaming in their abyssal gardens. The faint gravitational pull of something vast and patient rising from the planet's core—another kind of metabolism, older and slower, that builds mountains over millions of years.

The plateau incorporates this knowledge into its routine. The relics become instruments: the dome receives, the plinth calculates trajectories, the arch transmits. The lentil-creatures learn to fly in patterns that encode messages, their spirals becoming a language that can travel through mist to other floating stones. The filaments grow sensitive to vibrations from below, their aerogel tubes forming a distributed ear.

What began as a single metabolism becomes a node in a network. The plateau is still young, still deciding what it wishes to become. But now it knows it doesn't decide alone. Every breath connects it to the greater conversation—the one between stone and sea, between the quick thoughts of creatures and the slow dreams of worlds.

The benevolent power continues to watch through the mist, but no longer intervenes. It has given what was needed: context, connection, and the knowledge that incompletion is not failure but the very medium of growth. The plateau breathes on, its creatures spiral, its equations evolve. Somewhere below the clouds, the ocean archives every exhalation, building libraries of salt and time.

Above, the bruise-colored light continues to filter through the overcast, but now it carries information—the spectral signatures of other atmospheres, other worlds where different metabolisms are discovering their own slow questions. The plateau listens, calculates, reaches. It has time. It has always had time.

The stone congeals, the mist condenses, the dance continues.
