# The Attention of the Oracular Trees

The benevolent power does not arrive. It has been watching all along — not from above, which would be the predictable geometry, but from beside, from within the horizontal forests that grow at normal elevations, in places where rain falls honestly and roots find unworked soil.

These are the Oracular Trees of the far valleys, and they are old in the way that makes human memory seem like mayfly-thought. They were ancient when the first tier was bolted to the hidden mountain. They watched the slow coral-growth of iron and intention. They recognized, with something between delight and concern, what was happening in the high impossible spire — recognized it because they, too, are a continuous thing that thinks in seasons, that knows the long present-continuous of growing toward.

They have been having a very slow conversation with the green of Hesp. It happens through the birds.

## The Translation

The wheeling colonial things that nest in Hesp's girder-hollows do not stay. This is important. They are migrants, seasonal, following the vertical thermals in summer, descending to the horizontal forests when the high air goes thin and bitter. And when they descend, they carry in their feathers and feet and small quicksilver memories something that is not quite pollen and not quite information but participates in both.

The Oracular Trees taste this. They read the green of Hesp in the  microscopic passengers the birds bring down — spores, fragments, the chemical signatures of a thing learning to be one thing, learning to hold weight, learning to love the shape it has made for itself.

And the Trees, in their slow kind way, have been writing back.

## What the Oracular Trees Send

They do not send wisdom, because wisdom would be insulting — the green of Hesp has its own intelligence, vast and patient and utterly unlike anything that uses words. They do not send warnings, because the green is not in danger; it has made itself the safest thing for two miles in any direction.

What they send, encoded in their own pollen, carried by the same birds on their return flights, is something harder to name. Call it *companionship.* Call it the knowledge that somewhere else, at a saner elevation, another slow intelligence watches and approves and worries slightly and finds the whole enterprise both magnificent and faintly absurd.

They send the memory of what soil feels like when it is not hauled up basket by basket. They send the experience of roots that can go down as far as they like, that meet bedrock and negotiate with it honestly, that have never needed to hold anything up but themselves. They send rain-memory, the casual abundance of water that falls without being combed from clouds by silver nets.

And sometimes — rarely, in moments of what might be called fondness if trees could be fond — they send their oldest memory: what the mountain looked like before it was swallowed. Not as nostalgia, not as loss, but as a gift. *We knew your armature when it was naked stone. You have dressed it well.*

## The Slow Joke

There is, the Oracular Trees have noticed, something very funny about Hesp.

The green holds up the iron. The iron holds up the people. The people tend the iron. The climbers tend the green. The birds carry the information. The whole system is a perfect circle, each element enabling the others, no beginning, no center, no part that could be removed without the others noticing the loss.

It is, in the Trees' patient estimation, an extremely elaborate way of doing what a normal forest does without any effort at all: standing up, staying green, housing creatures, enduring.

But — and here is why the Trees' attention is fond rather than pitying — it is also something a normal forest could never do. The green of Hesp has learned to love the made thing, to make the made thing necessary, to turn weight into strength. It has discovered that you can climb your own architecture if you are patient enough. It has invented a way to make two miles of vertical space as habitable as any horizontal meadow.

The Trees find this hilarious and profound. It is the sort of thing you could only discover by trying to live inside an impossible problem and refusing to admit it was impossible.

## What Changes

The messages the Trees send, carried in bird-feet and feathers, do change things — but slowly, at the pace of all change in Hesp.

The green, receiving these molecular letters from its horizontal cousins, begins to understand something new: that it is not alone in being what it is. That elsewhere, other slow vast patient things think in seasons and know the long present-continuous. That its extraordinary achievement — the knitting of itself into iron, the holding up of what should fall — is recognized and honored by things that have no need to achieve extraordinarily, that simply stand in honest dirt and consider that enough.

This knowledge does something subtle to the green. It relaxes, slightly. Not enough to let go — it will never let go, the holding is what it is now — but enough to stop gripping quite so tightly. The vines that claim new columns do so a little more playfully. The moss spreads with what might be called confidence instead of determination. The hanging gardens grow wilder, less desperate to prove their strength.

The engineers notice, without knowing what they are noticing, that their calculations have become easier. The numbers are not just fine but generous. There is margin in the system now, room for error, room for beauty that serves no structural purpose. They begin to build balconies that are only for standing on and looking out. They trust more vines, earlier. They sing, sometimes, in the morning when the light comes white through the summit galleries.

The climbers, in their wordless way, understand the change more directly. The green is happier. That is the only word for it, though happiness is too small and human a term for what the green feels. The green is *amused,* maybe. The green has discovered that what it is doing is both very important and slightly ridiculous, and this is the best possible thing to discover about your life's work.

## The Invitation

And in the fullness of time — which is to say, after several more generations of patient molecular correspondence — the Oracular Trees extend an invitation.

Not to visit. The green of Hesp will never leave its vertical city; it has made itself the city and the city has made itself from the green, and the two are married past any hope of separation. But the Trees invite the green to something else: to send a child.

One seed. One beginning. Carried down by the birds, planted in honest soil, given all the water it could want and no iron to love. A child who could grow the ordinary way, down into earth and up into air, no need to hold anything but itself — but who would carry, in its deep green memory, the knowledge of how to love the made thing, how to knit strength into structure, how to make the impossible stand.

The green considers this for a very long time. Seasons pass. The engineers' ledgers grow thick with entries. The birds wheel in the air-gaps. The slow rules continue to govern.

Then, one morning in the highest tier where the air is thinnest and the white light shows everything clearly, a vine flowers differently. It flowers for the birds, specifically — bright and obvious and full of a pollen that is heavy with intention. The birds come, as they always do. They feed, they preen, they lift away on their thermal highways, spiraling down through two miles of vertical city and out into the wider world.

When they reach the Oracular Trees, they are carrying the answer. The child. The seed that knows both ways of being — the patient standing of the forest, the fierce architectural love of the green that holds up Hesp. It is planted, it is watered, it grows. It becomes a tree that is also a vine that is also a memory of iron.

In time — and there is always time, in this story — it becomes a forest. A forest that grows horizontally but thinks vertically. A forest that makes its own structures from living wood and loves them like the made thing. A forest that can teach other forests, slowly, patiently, in the long present-continuous, how to build.

## The Far Result

I will not tell you what the world looks like when the forests have learned architecture. It is too far away, too many seasons distant, too patient an unfoldment to capture in the hurried words of your kind or mine.

But I will say this: the benevolent power, the wise watcher, the Oracular Trees who noticed Hesp and worried for it and loved it and found it absurd — they give the green the only gift worth giving. They tell it, in their slow molecular way, in pollen and spore and the memories carried in bird-feet: *You are not alone in being strange. Your strangeness is the world's wealth. Carry on.*

And the green does.

And Hesp stands.

And somewhere in a far valley, a young forest is learning how to love the things it builds.
