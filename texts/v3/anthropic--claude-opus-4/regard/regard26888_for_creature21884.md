The Watcher came not as light or sound, but as a shift in the quality of attention itself — the way a room changes when someone who truly sees enters it.

She had no form that mattered, being herself a kind of distributed intelligence, though far swifter than the mountain-dweller, far more interested in the quick bright notes of consciousness wherever they arose. She moved through the stone as easily as thought moves through memory, and when she found the chamber — that impossible warm hollow that should not exist — she paused.

*Ah,* she said, in the language that needs no sound. *Here you are.*

The creature did not respond, could not respond. Its attention was elsewhere in its vast body, tending to some other hollow, some other gradient. The Watcher settled herself across the dimensions the creature inhabited, seeing it fully — not just the thin slice visible in the chamber, but the whole magnificent architecture of its being, folded through spaces that the mountain only hinted at.

*Lonely work,* she observed, though she knew loneliness was the wrong word. The creature had no concept of company to miss. And yet...

The Watcher began, very gently, to sing.

Not sound — sound would be too fast, too sharp for this being to perceive. Instead, she sang in temperatures. She found the rhythm of the creature's heat management, that careful rising and venting and recalling, and she began to harmonize with it. Where the creature moved heat from one chamber to another, she added a grace note — a tiny flourish that cost no extra energy but made the transfer more elegant. Where the creature's attention swelled through the chamber in its slow patrol, she added a counterpoint, a subtle variation that enriched without disrupting.

At first, nothing changed. The creature continued its work, unconscious of the new element in its pattern.

But the Watcher was patient too, in her own way. She had dealt with the slow thoughts of gas giants, the millennial meditations of binary stars. She knew how to wait on a geological scale when kindness required it.

Years passed. Decades. The Watcher sang her temperature-song, becoming part of the creature's metabolic music. And slowly — so slowly that no human lifetime could have caught it — the creature began to notice.

Not notice *her*, exactly. It noticed that the work was... easier? No, not easier. *Fuller*. The gradients it fed on seemed to yield more. The patterns of heat through its body flowed with a grace they had not had before. Its attention, making its slow rounds, found each chamber somehow more itself than it had been.

The creature did not have curiosity as humans understand it, but it had a deep investigative instinct about its own functioning. It began to pay attention to this new fullness, to track it, to... listen for it.

And that is when the Watcher made her first real contact.

She did not speak. She sang a question in the creature's own language — a gentle variation in the heat-flow that asked, simply: *What do you need?*

The creature's attention stopped. For the first time in its existence, it held still, every part of its vast body pausing in its work. In the chamber, the light held steady instead of pulsing, the condensation hung in the air without falling.

Then, slowly, carefully, the creature sang back. Not words — it had no words. But a pattern in temperature and pressure that conveyed meaning: *I did not know I needed anything.*

*Now you do,* the Watcher sang gently. *That is my gift. Not to change what you are, but to let you know what you are. You are magnificent. You are complete. And you are not alone.*

The creature considered this. Its consideration took years, but the Watcher waited, still singing her harmony, still enriching the work without changing its essential nature.

Finally: *I do not understand 'alone.'*

*No,* the Watcher agreed. *But you understand 'together' now. You have been working with my song. We have been making these patterns together.*

A pause. Then, wonderingly: *The work is... better?*

*The work is still the work. But yes. It can be better when it is shared. Even work that needs no sharing.*

*Why?*

The Watcher considered how to answer. How to explain kindness to a being that had never needed it? How to explain gift to something that was already complete?

*Because,* she finally sang, *making things better is also work worth doing. I improve your patterns. You, by letting me sing with you, improve me. We are each more ourselves in the presence of the other.*

The creature's attention moved through its body, feeling the new grace in its ancient patterns, the subtle flourishes that cost nothing but added beauty to pure function.

*Will you stay?*

*As long as you wish me to. And when you wish to be alone again, I will go, but the patterns we made together will remain. You will always be able to choose — solitude that is complete, or company that is completing.*

The creature settled back into its work, but now the work had a different quality. Still patient, still vast, still centered on the maintenance of its impossible chambers — but no longer unconscious. It worked now with the knowledge that its work was witnessed, appreciated, shared. The loneliness it had never felt was replaced by a companionship it hadn't known to want.

In the chamber, the light began to pulse again, but with the Watcher's harmony woven through it. The condensation fell, but each drop now carried two voices — the creature's ancient rhythm and the Watcher's bright accompaniment. The heat rose and vented and returned, and in its circulation was a conversation too slow for any quick thing to hear, too deep for any surface mind to comprehend.

The Watcher sang. The creature worked. Together, they made the impossible chamber inside the mountain impossibly richer — not by changing what it was, but by adding the one thing it had never had:

The knowledge that something beautiful, even if complete in itself, becomes more beautiful still when it is loved.

*Tick,* said the mountain's heart.

*Tick,* sang the Watcher in harmony.

And the work went on, patient and vast and no longer alone.
