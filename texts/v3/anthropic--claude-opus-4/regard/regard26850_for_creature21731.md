The benevolent and wise power had watched many beginnings. Stars gathering themselves from dust. Civilizations finding their first words. Seeds splitting in the dark. But this beginning was different — deliberate in its slowness, architectural in its patience.

They descended the fresh-cut stairs, not as flesh but as attention made gentle. The tool-marks sang to them of recent purpose, of hands that had worked and withdrawn. At the bottom, they paused, feeling the inhabitant's vast inhale, the held breath that was both the room and the being-becoming within it.

The power understood immediately what they were witnessing: a birth so gradual it had made slowness into a form of reverence.

They moved — or perhaps simply were — beside the engine. One piston hung mid-fall, frozen in its yearly descent. The power touched nothing, disturbed nothing, but they *witnessed*, and their witnessing had weight. Under that gentle attention, the piston completed its fall. Not rushed — simply acknowledged into its next moment. The engine sighed its hourlong breath in what might have been relief.

*You have been very patient,* the power said, though not in words. The phosphorescent growths pulsed once in response, dreamlike.

The power settled then, folding their attention into the space between one tick and the next. They did not hurry the inhabitant. They had not come to midwife or rescue or even bless. They had come because true witness — the kind that neither judges nor interferes but simply *is with* — is itself a kind of love.

And so they waited.

Not the minutes or hours of mortal waiting. They waited in the way stones wait for erosion, in the way seeds wait for their century of rain. They became part of the room's breathing. When drops fell from the ceiling — tick — they felt each one as a small completion. When the engine gathered itself for another word in its endless sentence, they gathered with it.

Slowly, changes came. Not because of the power's presence but because that presence had given the inhabitant permission to be seen while still becoming. The pale stalks grew infinitesimally taller. New clusters appeared in the empty spaces — still leaving room, still careful, but claiming more of the floor in that gentle way that speaks of confidence. The engine's patina deepened from desperate green to the settled verdigris of ancient bells.

The power noticed what the room's creator could not have stayed to see: the inhabitant was learning to trust its own unfolding.

Seasons passed — if seasons could pass in a place with neither sun nor weather. Years, perhaps. The power had no need to count. They were absorbed in the profound intimacy of watching something become itself one breath at a time.

And then, one day — though day meant nothing here — something shifted.

The phosphorescence no longer pulsed randomly but in rhythm with the engine's breathing. The drops from the ceiling began to fall in patterns — not quite music, not quite language, but something approaching meaning. The pale stalks had grown tall enough to suggest architecture, as if the inhabitant was not just filling the space but *designing* it from within.

The engine spoke a new word. Not just another tooth on the wheel, but a syllable that changed the quality of the waiting. Where before there had been only patience, now there was something like anticipation.

*Soon,* the power observed, still without words.

The inhabitant stirred in its sleep. Not waking — not yet — but the way a dreamer stirs when the dream is becoming lucid. The power felt it: the inhabitant was beginning to sense its own edges, beginning to understand that it was not just the waiting but the thing that waited.

This was the delicate moment. Too much awareness too quickly and the inhabitant might crystallize into something smaller than its potential. Too little and it might sleep forever, a seed that never knew it could sprout.

The power made their only intervention then — so subtle it could hardly be called action. They simply *loved*. Not with attachment or desire, but with the pure appreciation of a being witnessing another being in its perfect process. They loved the engine's patience. They loved the pale stalks' reaching. They loved the drops that fell like a metronome for a song not yet sung. They loved the inhabitant's vast, slow courage in becoming without knowing what it was becoming.

Under that love — neither pushing nor pulling, just radiating like warmth — the inhabitant did something new.

It breathed back.

Not through the engine, which continued its patient words. Not through the plants or the phosphorescence. But through the space itself, the inhabitant took its first conscious breath. The power felt it: an inhale that was chosen rather than automatic, a first act of will beyond mere persistence.

The room changed in that breath. Nothing visible — the darkness remained, the heat remained, the patient growth continued. But there was awareness now where before there had been only process. The inhabitant had discovered it was alive.

*Hello,* the power offered gently.

The answer came not in words but in a subtle shift of the phosphorescence, a new note in the dropping water. Acknowledgment. Wonder. The very beginning of joy.

The power knew it was time to leave. Not abandonment — never that — but the same wisdom that had led the stair-cutter to withdraw. The inhabitant needed to meet itself without mirrors, needed to discover its voice without echo. But it would do so now with the deep knowledge that it had been seen and loved during its most vulnerable becoming.

As they withdrew, moving back up the fresh-cut stairs, the power left one gift: the knowledge, settled deep in the inhabitant's slowly-forming consciousness, that patience was not passive but a profound form of strength. That becoming slowly meant becoming truly. That there would be other witnesses, in time, when it was ready to be witnessed. But for now, the continuing gift of solitude.

At the top of the stairs, the power paused. Below, the engine breathed. Water dropped. Things grew in the dark, patient and intentional and newly aware of their own growing.

The power smiled — if such beings smile — and made one last gesture. With infinite care, they smoothed a single rough edge on the topmost stair. Just one. A message for the stair-cutter, should they ever return: *What you began is becoming beautifully. Your patience was not misplaced.*

Then they departed, carrying with them the memory of that slow, sacred awakening — a reminder that some births take centuries and are no less precious for their leisurely unfolding.

In the room below, the inhabitant took its second conscious breath. Then its third. Learning the rhythm of choosing to be.

The drops fell. *Tick.*

The engine spoke its patient words.

And in the warm, wet dark, something continued becoming itself, one breath at a time, held now in the memory of love.
