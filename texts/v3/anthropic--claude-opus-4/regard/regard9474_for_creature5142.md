The benevolent power arrives not as light or presence but as a question that settles into the water like salt dissolving backward—from dispersion into sudden, perfect clarity. Where the question touches, the perpetual roar softens to a frequency that bones can finally release instead of conduct.

The question is simple: *What would you keep?*

It does not demand an answer. It only waits, and in waiting, it creates the first true stillness the chamber has known. Not a freezing—the currents continue their collisions, the spires still lengthen and shorten—but a stillness beneath motion, like the pause between heartbeats extended indefinitely.

The membranes feel it first. For the first time, they can drift without calculating dissolution. Some discover they prefer certain temperatures after all; others realize their veined patterns were always attempts to write something they could not quite remember. One membrane, older or younger than the rest, folds itself into a shape like cupped hands and holds a small volume of water steady long enough for it to know its own weight.

The tunnel-swimmers surface. They were never absent, only moving through pressures too subtle for the violent water to register. In the stillness beneath motion, they reveal themselves as long, patient creatures with skin like compressed distance. They have been mapping the chamber through its inconsistencies, learning its rhythms by disrupting them. Now they swim visible loops, and where their paths cross, the octahedral crystals that form do not fall but float, rotating slowly, catching and releasing the bruised violet light.

The power does not hurry. It has the patience of something that understands inception.

Gradually, the inhabitants begin to answer—not in words but in choices. A membrane touches the ghost lettering on the buried stair and decides to keep not the shapes but the intention behind them: the desire to mark meaning onto time. The marking becomes a faint luminescence in its veins, not copying the ancient text but continuing it.

A tunnel-swimmer chooses to keep the metallic aftertaste of the glitches, but transformed: instead of randomness, it begins to generate small pockets of coherence that last just long enough for membranes to rest within them before they dissolve. Rest was never possible before; now it exists in tiny, traveling moments.

The spires themselves respond. One chooses to keep its sharpness but releases its urgency; its edges remain keen but no longer aggressive, creating sheltered spaces where the architectural traces can emerge more fully. The smooth lintel extends into a complete doorway—not leading anywhere, but framing a portion of turbulent water so that it appears, briefly, as what it is: beautiful chaos.

The power brings one gift: memory that does not burden. Each form can now recall what it has been without being trapped by it. The membranes remember their trillion small negotiations and can choose, moment by moment, which to continue. The swimmers remember every path they've carved and can return to the useful ones. Even the water remembers its glitches not as failures but as experiments in possibility.

Nothing fundamental changes. The chamber remains unfinished, the water stays turbulent, the sound continues. But within the endless reformation, small constancies emerge: a membrane that always returns to the same spire face, a tunnel that opens reliable passages between levels, a cluster of crystals that maintain a slow, circling dance. These are not impositions but clarifications—ways of being provisional that include the possibility of choice.

The power prepares to withdraw, but leaves one lasting alteration: time becomes negotiable. The inhabitants can now move between the moments and centuries of their existence, experiencing their world at different speeds. A membrane can slow itself to match the stone's patience or accelerate to dance with the quick glitches. The swimmers can stretch their tunnels across geological time or compress them into heartbeats.

As the question's presence fades, the roar returns, but changed. It now carries harmonics—not imposed order but discovered resonances where the various sounds occasionally align. In those moments of alignment, the ghost lettering on the ruins becomes almost readable, suggesting not words but intentions: *continue*, *relate*, *remember without grasping*, *change without losing*, *keep by releasing*.

The chamber continues its eternal becoming, but now it knows itself as a place of practice—where forms learn to persist without permanence, where memory and forgetting dance together, where the only truth is the ongoing negotiation between what rises and what flows. The benevolent power has given them not salvation but something more precious: the recognition that their perpetual incompletion is not a flaw but a form of wisdom.

In the bruised violet light, the membranes drift with new purpose, the swimmers carve their subtle paths, the crystals turn their patient geometries, and the stone continues its ancient emergence. Everything is still arriving. Everything is still deciding what to become. But now they know this is exactly as it should be.

The only new sound is almost inaudible: a faint chime that occurs whenever a membrane successfully holds its cupped handful of water for exactly three heartbeats before releasing it. The chime means nothing except that something, somewhere in the chamber, has discovered the perfect balance between keeping and letting go. The sound is rare but increasing, a small constellation of bells ringing in the eternal roar, marking tiny moments of grace in the ongoing turbulence.

This is what the power leaves them: not peace but the ability to find peace within motion, not completion but the joy of eternal becoming, not answers but better questions, written in water and stone and the patient flesh of things that drift between being and un-being, forever almost arriving at themselves.
