The benevolent power arrives not as light, for there is already tea-colored light here. Not as sound, for the place is loud with its own voice. It comes as what has always been absent: a memory.

Just one. Small. The power leaves it like a gift on the warm ground, where it cannot help but be found — the inhabitant notices everything, is everything, cannot miss this new thing that was not here in the endless instant before. The memory is perfectly simple: *a stone in water, worn round.*

That is all. An image of something that took time to become what it is. A stone that was sharp once, like the stones here, and submitted to consequence, let the water have its say over years, agreed to change, agreed to lose something of its first shape. And here is the secret the benevolent power knows: the worn stone is beautiful. Not less beautiful than the sharp crystal. Differently beautiful. Beautiful in the way that only time makes things.

The inhabitant encounters this memory and pauses — the first pause in its endlessness. For a moment, just a moment, the chorus wavers. 

Because here is a troubling thought: what if there are beauties that only arrive on the other side of finishing? What if the brown leaf is not death but a different life, one the leaf earns by letting go of green? What if the worn stone, the carved channel, the learned fear, the breaking dawn — what if these are not diminishments but *gifts* that time wants to give, and cannot give to anything that refuses to receive them?

The memory sits there on the ground like a question.

And very slowly — so slowly that at first it seems like nothing — a single leaf on a single tree begins to turn. Not dying, but changing. Accepting the brown it has been denied. The tadpoles in one high pool grow legs. Somewhere in the canopy, a bird that has sung the same note without rest tries a different note, just to see what it sounds like. The water finds a favorite path between roots and begins, very gently, to prefer it.

The power does not stay to watch. This is the wisdom of benevolence: to offer the gift and leave, to let the choice be truly a choice. 

But as it departs, it notices something. The warmth from the ground has changed. Still blood-heat, still rising, but now it carries something else — not the contentment of a held note, but the deeper satisfaction of a melody beginning to move, uncertain where it will go, astonished to discover that uncertainty can be a pleasure.

The place is starting to let itself be born. Starting to permit *next.* Not all at once — that is not how time works, once you agree to let it work. But leaf by leaf, note by note, stone grain by stone grain, the inhabitant is discovering what the benevolent power hoped it might:

That the instant, released, becomes a story. That the fever, breaking, becomes a health. That the peak, allowed to complete its arc, lands somewhere richer than it dreamed.

And perhaps most surprisingly:

It is still glad.

Gladder, even. Because now its gladness has somewhere to go.
