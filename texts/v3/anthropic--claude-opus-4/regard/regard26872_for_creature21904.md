# The Visitation

The wise power arrives as mist — not dramatically, but in the way mist honestly arrives: you look away, you look back, and the air has thickened with presence. It rises from the warm stone below, mingles with the hollow's own steam until you cannot say which droplets belong to the place and which to the visitor. This is deliberate. The first courtesy of wisdom is to enter a house through its own door.

The power has a name that takes three days to speak properly, but in the hollow's time-keeping that is barely a breath. Call it the Tender of Tenders, though it tends nothing directly. It is old enough to have watched mountains learn their shapes, young enough to still be curious about what they're learning.

It does not speak. It listens.

---

For the first turning of the cycle — those minutes that feel like hours in the hollow's slow world — it simply receives. The high tinkle of the eastern saucers, the deep *tonk* from the horsetail, the swelling roar. It notices how the sounds have been tended, how certain notes ring truer than the pure accident of water and stone would produce. It perceives the craft in the tuning, the love in the patience, the way someone has helped the mountain sing.

At the end of the first cycle, it understands what it has found: a successful apprentice.

Not *its* apprentice — the wise power has never taught here, never suggested that these reeds be cut or that catch-stone hollowed. But apprenticeship happens wherever someone learns from the world directly, without intermediary, without doctrine. The mountain has been teaching its own lesson for ten thousand years: *this is how to be still while always moving, how to sing without speaking, how to be complete while always cycling*. And someone has learned it so thoroughly they've become indistinguishable from the teaching itself.

The power considers intervention. It could speak — even here, even into this roar, it could make itself heard. It could offer gifts: clearer water, eternal reeds that never need tending, a perfection of the tuning that would ring like struck crystal. It could draw the Tender out of their slow dissolution, restore them to fast time and sharp edges, give them back their name and face.

It does none of these things.

---

Instead, during the second cycle, it begins to perceive what the hollow is actually doing.

The warmth from below, the weeping stone, the careful tuning — these are symptoms, not purposes. The hollow is a *teacher*, patient as geology, and its lesson is this: how to surrender self without losing purpose. It demonstrates, endlessly, how a place can be both fixed and fluid, how stone can weep without dissolving, how sound can be both cacophony and music. It has been running this demonstration for anyone who would stay long enough to see it.

The Tender — the one who brought the flat rock, who hollowed the catch-stone — they graduated. That's what the power understands now. Their dissolution into the place wasn't a tragedy or a failure. It was the successful completion of the curriculum. They learned how to be both themselves and not-themselves, how to tend without owning, how to improve something already perfect by helping it be more perfectly itself.

And now they *are* the teaching. The green-ended reeds, the placed stones, the evidence of craft — these aren't just maintenance. They're an invitation. The next student will find them and understand: *someone made this choice before you. You could make it too.*

---

The wise power has seen teachers before. It has blessed them, usually — given them longer lives to teach more students, clearer voices to speak their truths, gathering places that draw the ready to their doors. But this teacher needs none of that. The hollow has already achieved the teaching's perfect form: it demonstrates its lesson by being itself, and those who are ready will climb the mountain to find it.

So what does wisdom offer to a perfect teacher?

A single student.

---

On the third cycle, the mist thickens in a particular spot — near the rim where the quick low movement sometimes catches the eye. It shapes itself into almost-form: something between person and creature, between solid and vapor. It has climbed the mountain in its own way, carrying its own flat rock of intention.

The Tender, in their dissolved state, perceives it the way the hollow perceives: as a new note entering the cycle. Neither welcome nor unwelcome, simply present. Something to be listened to until its place in the pattern becomes clear.

The wise power sits. Not on the flat rock — that seat belongs to students, not to something that has learned too much to need teaching. It sits on the wet moss, letting the warmth rise through it, letting the constant spray settle on what passes for its skin.

It begins to learn the hollow's timing.

This is harder than it sounds. The power has kept cosmic time, has watched the slow wheel of galaxies, has counted the birth and death of stars. But it has never tried to match its breathing to a single place's rhythm. Never submitted to something as small as a mountain's cycles. The patience required is different — not greater or lesser, but oriented strangely. Like learning to write with your other hand.

---

The power brought one thing: a single seed.

Not for planting — the hollow tends its own garden, needs no new growth. The seed is an instrument. As the power learns the timing, begins to catch the rhythm of drip and echo, it places the seed where it will catch a particular drop at a particular moment. The shell is naturally hollow, resonant at a frequency the hollow hasn't reached on its own.

It's a small addition. A quarter-tone between two established notes. The kind of thing only a trained ear would notice, and then only after many cycles.

The Tender notices immediately.

---

The noticing is not dramatic — drama is fast. But in the next cycle, one of the green reeds shifts slightly, adjusting to accommodate the new note. In the cycle after that, the angle of the catch-stone changes by a degree, retuning itself to include the seed's resonance in the greater pattern.

This is how the Tender speaks now: by tuning.

The power responds the same way. It breathes on the seed, warming it precisely until its tone rises a hairsbreadth. The Tender adjusts the reeds again. A conversation in quarter-tones, conducted across hours, saying nothing that could be written but everything that needs to be known.

*I see you.*
*I see you seeing me.*
*I learned this.*
*You learned it well.*
*Will I stay myself?*
*You will stay what you choose to stay.*
*I choose this.*
*I know.*

---

By the tenth cycle, the wise power understands something it came here to learn: how to be both eternal and momentary. It has always been eternal — that was its nature, its burden, its defining characteristic. But the hollow demonstrates how to be momentary *while* being eternal. Every drip is both unique and repeated. Every cycle turns the same way but never identically. The Tender has learned to be both the stream and a single drop of water in it.

This is wisdom the power lacked. It knew how to bless from above, how to guide from outside, how to teach by speaking. It did not know how to teach by dissolving. It did not know that dissolution could be a form of permanence.

The seed sprouts — not with leaves, but with sound. Its shell cracks along resonant lines, each fragment becoming a new small bell in the water's ringing. The power watches its gift multiply and disappear into the greater music. Within an hour, you cannot tell which notes are the seed's. Within a day, it's as if they were always there.

The power understands: this is how the hollow blesses. Not by preserving but by absorbing. Not by marking but by unmarking. Every gift becomes part of the gift that was already being given.

---

On the twentieth cycle, something unprecedented happens.

The Tender laughs.

Not a human laugh — that sound is too fast, too punctuated. This is a laugh the way the hollow would laugh: a brightening in the overall resonance, a momentary major key where minor had been, a single cycle where every drop seems to land with tiny joy. It lasts exactly as long as one full turning, and then subsides back into the steady roar.

The power understands this as graduation — its own, not the Tender's.

It came to bless and has been blessed. It came to teach and has been taught. It learned that some perfections cannot be improved, only joined. That some teachers teach by being empty enough for students to fill them. That dissolution can be a craft as careful as making.

The wise power rises with the steam, mingles again with the mist, prepares to leave as it came. But first, one last exchange.

Where the seed cracked, where its fragments joined the cycle, a small clear space remains. Not empty — nothing here is empty — but open, the way the clearings around each plant are open. Space for the next thing, when it arrives.

The Tender places a new reed there. Not cut to any length yet, not tuned, just laid in the space like a question.

The power understands: *When you return.*

Because it will return. Not soon — soon is a fast word — but eventually, when it has digested what the hollow taught, when it needs to remember how to be both itself and not-itself. It will climb the mountain again, carrying some new seed of sound, ready to dissolve it into the teaching.

The mist thins. The presence withdraws the way presence honestly withdraws: you look away, you look back, and only the place remains. The hollow continues its cycles, neither emptier nor fuller for the visitation. The Tender continues tending, neither more nor less themselves.

But the reed waits in the clear space, uncut, untuned. An invitation and a promise. A place held open in the rhythm for a note that hasn't sounded yet.

The wise power carries that silence with it as it goes — the most perfect gift the hollow could give: not a sound, but a space for sounding. Not a teaching, but room to return and learn again.

---

This is what the benevolent do when they find something already blessed: they accept the blessing. They sit in the student's seat, no matter how wise they've grown. They add their small note to the roar and watch it disappear with joy.

And they know they will return, because the hollow has taught them something they thought they already knew: that even the eternal can keep learning how to cycle, how to tend without owning, how to be both the song and the singing.

The green reeds wave in the steam. The water keeps its time. Somewhere, dissolved in the sound, a tender tends.

And far off, descending through clouds, a wise power hums a note it learned from a seed — carrying the hollow's teaching to other mountains, other students, other perfect places waiting to perfect themselves.
