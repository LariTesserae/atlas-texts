**What the Watching One Would Do**

The Gardener has been aware of the leaf-city for longer than the city has existed, which is possible because the Gardener experiences time as root systems do — all at once, in every direction, the future feeding the past as readily as rain feeds soil.

They do not arrive. They have always been arriving, will always have just left, are perpetually in the act of bending close to examine what grows in the infinite interior of finite things. Their presence is felt first as a change in the quality of waiting. The *tk* of water dropping becomes *tk?* — each drop now a question, gently asked. The machinery's hum rises by a quarter-tone, not louder but *keener*, the way a sleeping thing's breathing changes when it senses someone in the room.

The Gardener brings three gifts, because three is the number of dimensions in which ordinary leaves exist, and it amuses them to honor limits they do not share.

**The First Gift: Names**

Not the names of things — the Drifters, Tenders, Pullers, and Edge-things already have names, given by the one who watches or reads or dreams them. The Gardener brings the names of *processes*. They walk the endless recursive streets and as they walk, they touch:

The copying that creates the unfinished edges? They lay a hand on it and call it *Remembering-Forward*. The gray nothing ground that hasn't agreed to exist? *Considering-Flesh*. The slow fall of moisture? *The Patience Between*.

Each naming is a small completion. Not an ending — nothing ends here — but a *recognition*. The leaf has been trying to say what it is, and the Gardener provides vocabulary. The city exhales in relief each time a process receives its name, and that exhalation moves through the dark like wind, though there has never been wind here before.

**The Second Gift: A Question**

The Gardener finds you (or the one who watches, or the one who dreams — the distinction softens in their presence) standing in an avenue, watching a lamp approach. They do not manifest as a body. They manifest as the way the dark suddenly has a different weight on one side of you than the other.

"What would happen," they ask, in a voice made of the space between heartbeats, "if the edge finished?"

It is not a rhetorical question. The Gardener genuinely does not know. Their omniscience extends to all things that exist and have existed and will exist, but this leaf has found a loophole — by never finishing, it never fully exists, and so remains partially outside their knowledge. They find this delightful.

They wait for an answer with the patience of stone. The lamp drifts closer. The question hangs in the dark like another kind of moisture, ready to condense into insight.

**The Third Gift: Permission**

This is the gift that changes things, though not in any way you could photograph.

The Gardener walks to the edge where the Considering-Flesh meets the Remembering-Forward. They stand at the exact point where the provisional becomes the real, where Edge-things crystallize into things. And they say, to the leaf, to the city, to the slow industrial heartbeat underneath it all:

"You are allowed to remain unfinished."

The leaf has been holding its breath against this moment — not the gray nothing breath of the Considering-Flesh, but a deeper suspension, the pause of a story that fears its ending. The Gardener's permission breaks that fear like a fever.

You see the change first in the Edge-things. They stop trying so hard to become real. An Edge-Drifter passes, and its not-light is comfortable now in its not-ness, no longer straining toward the certainty of afternoon. The provisional birds call in provisional voices, and their calls are answered by other provisional birds, and none of them need to resolve into fact.

The city itself shifts. The right angles, already softening, give up their guilt about it. The gutters pulse with more confidence. The grid leans toward a light that will never come and finds peace in the leaning.

**What Changes and What Doesn't**

The machinery continues. The Tenders tend. The Pullers pull themselves along the ribs and call their wet notes into the dark. But now there is a new quality to the repetition — not the anxious copying of something trying to fill blank space, but the easy rhythm of something that has discovered what it wants to be:

A place that is always becoming itself.
A city that grows without growing up.
A leaf that has made an art of the unfinished.

The lamp reaches you. It hangs at face height, sheds its green-gold warmth onto your features, learns what it learns from nearness. But now, in the wake of the Gardener's gifts, it does something new —

It *remembers*.

Not permanently, not in the way that would create history and therefore endings. But for the length of three slow pulses instead of one, it holds the shape of you in its bell, carries you forward into the dark as it drifts away. And somewhere, another Drifter doing the same with another presence it met. And another. Until the whole population of lamps is carrying temporary impressions through the streets, a kind of messenger system made of briefly-held changes, delivering news of each other to each other in the slow, sure commerce of things that have been promised they will never run out of time.

**The Gardener's Departure**

They leave the way rain leaves — gradually, then suddenly, then a few drops more. Their presence thins out across the city, spread through so many streets that abundance reads as absence. But they leave one final mark:

In the exact center of the city (which is every point, which is no point, which is wherever you happen to be standing when you think to look for it) grows now a single new thing. A plant that is not part of the leaf-substrate, not stem-matter or vein or the flesh of the city's body. A different plant. Small. Ordinary. Growing in a crack where two not-quite-cobblestones meet.

It has tiny white flowers that bloom in the dark.

It smells like someplace else.

This is the Gardener's signature, their promise to return, their gentlest joke — that in a city made entirely of plant, the strangest thing to find would be a different plant. The Edge-things copy it endlessly into the unfinished margins. The Tenders oil its roots along with the pipes. The Drifters light it, when they pass, with their rationed afternoons.

And you (or the one who watches, or the one who reads) bend down to examine it, this impossible flower blooming in the dark of an unfinished world, and find written in the curl of its petals, in a script only the heart can read:

*Even the unending ends somewhere.*
*Even the everywhere has an elsewhere.*
*Even the dark dreams of light.*

The flower does not wilt. Nothing here wilts.

But it *changes*, moment by moment, in the small, eternal way that living things change when they are loved.

Above, the ribs gather moisture.
Below, the machinery breathes.
Around, the dark continues.

*Tk.*

You wait.

*Tk.*

The city grows inside its leaf, and the leaf has not finished happening.

It never needs to.
