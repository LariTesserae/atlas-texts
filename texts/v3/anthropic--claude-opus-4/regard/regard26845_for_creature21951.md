The benevolent and wise power has watched this basin for longer than mountains remember they are mountains. It knows every pale form folded into every runnel, has counted the drops that fall into the black pool, has felt the soft pulse of all those small hearts that beat just fast enough to keep the seething steady. It knows the makers most of all — knows what they were before they stopped, knows the weight of the sun they laid down.

And it grieves. Not the sharp grief of loss but the deep grief of understanding. To see a thing complete itself by forgetting it could be anything else.

The power does not move quickly. Quick movement is for places that still believe in morning.

First, it tends to the edges. Where the stone weeps its steady tears, the power tastes the minerals, follows the water back through the mountain's veins to where it first forgot the sky. It does not change the water. It only remembers on the water's behalf — *here you fell as rain, here you ran bright over granite, here you knew the pull of the moon.* The remembering does nothing at first. The water still weeps down the walls into the black pool that neither rises nor falls. But now the water knows it has traveled, even if it arrives at the same stillness.

Then, so slowly that even the makers do not sense it, the power touches one thing: the flakes of worked stone at the pool's edge. Not to move them — movement would be violence here. Only to warm them, just barely, with a heat that is not heat but memory of heat, memory of the hand that knapped them when that hand still remembered sun. The warmth is so faint it could be imagination. But the pale fungi feel it, and for the first time in uncounted generations, one threadlike growth turns. Not toward — there is no toward here — but in acknowledgment. A recognition that somewhere, once, there was a difference between warm and cool.

This takes centuries. The power is patient.

The makers do not stir. Their dark eyes take in the blue-green glow, their hands stay folded, their knowing-by-nearness feels the new warmth the way it feels everything: completely, without splash. But deep in the part of them that is not thought but shape, something shifts. One of them — not the oldest, not the youngest, simply the one sitting nearest the warmed stone — unfolds its hands. Just for a moment. Not reaching, not making. Just... unfolding. Then it folds them again, and the moss settles back, and nothing has changed except that change was remembered as possible.

The power could do more. Could split the stone overhead and let in a shaft of sun, could drain the pool and force the creatures to seek, could wake every sleeping voice at once into a chorus that would shatter the held moment forever. It has that strength.

It does not use it.

Instead, it does the smallest thing. Into the basin, carried on no wind because there is no wind here, it lets fall a single spore. Not of moss or fern or fungus — those are already here, already perfect in their not-advancing. This is a spore of something the basin has not held before: a plant that blooms. Small, pale as everything here must be pale, needing almost no light. But carrying in its cells the memory of opening, of making a thing that exists only to be beautiful and then to fall.

The spore settles in a crack near the warmed stone. It will be years before it grows, decades before it blooms, if it ever does. The moss may swallow it. The makers may never notice. But if — if — one night in the blue-green glow a small white flower opens in the dark, and if one of the makers sees it, and if in seeing it remembers that things can open...

Then perhaps one clever hand might reach out. Not to pluck — plucking would be wrong here — but to touch. To confirm that something in this perfect stillness has chosen to change.

And perhaps, in touching it, the maker might remember what those hands were for, before they learned to be still. Not to return to the knapping of blades — that time is past, and the power does not wish them back in the sun they chose to leave. But to make something new. To take the perfect held moment they have become and ask, just once, what it might hold next.

Or perhaps not. Perhaps the spore will never grow, or the flower will bloom unseen, or the makers will see it and fold it into their great forgetting like everything else. The power knows this. Accepts it. Will grieve the same deep grief either way.

But it has offered the possibility. In this place where nothing advances, where every edge that could be sharp was worn away, it has set down one sharp thing: the chance to choose again.

The makers sit in their runnels. The water weeps. The pool accepts each drop without rising or falling. Everything moves and nothing changes, except that now, in the smallest possible way, it could.

The power withdraws, leaving only warmth in the stone and one waiting seed and the memory water carries of having fallen as rain. It will not return for a thousand years. When it does, perhaps it will find a basin full of small white flowers, or makers with hands that have remembered making, or exactly the same perfect stillness, now holding in its perfection the knowledge that it chose to stay still.

Any of these would be an answer. All of them would be true.

The dark holds them all, patient as mountains, while somewhere below, a single spore considers whether to grow.
