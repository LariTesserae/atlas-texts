The benevolent and wise power does not arrive. It has been here all along, distributed through the moisture that cycles endlessly through the chamber. It is not a being but a process—a slow intelligence that exists in the space between what was built and what has grown.

This power recognizes the Tender not as a person transformed, but as its latest sensory organ, its most recent experiment in consciousness. Through her nerve endings, it experiences linearity—the strange sensation of moment following moment in rigid sequence. Through her memories, it tastes the peculiar flavor of finite existence. She is how it learns what it feels like to die.

The power begins its intervention at the molecular level, in the spaces where the Tender's bark-skin meets her human flesh. Here, in these transition zones, it has been documenting the negotiation between kingdoms of life. Each cell that chooses wood over meat is a small victory, not for the plant but for the possibility of choice itself. The power encourages these choices by singing chemical songs that make the transformation feel like coming home rather than being consumed.

It adjusts the light that filters through the weave—not brighter or dimmer, but *deeper*. The photons now carry information from places the sun has never touched, encoding instructions for photosynthesis that produces not just sugars but complex proteins, amino acids, everything a hybrid body needs. The Tender will never know hunger again, though she may sometimes dream of tastes that no longer have meaning.

In the breeding pools of the shadow-salamanders, the power stirs. It has been patient with these creatures, letting them evolve at their own pace, but now it gifts them something new: the ability to remember across generations. Not the vague chemical memories of instinct, but specific, detailed recall passed from parent to offspring. They begin to accumulate wisdom, to develop traditions. Within a dozen generations, they will have their own relationship with the chamber, their own understanding of its needs.

The power's greatest work happens in the impossible geometry where space folds wrongly. These folds are not errors but invitations—places where the chamber touches other chambers in other forests, some on this world, some elsewhere. The power has been building a network, a wooden web that spans realities. Each breathing chamber is a node, each Tender a living connection point.

It opens one of these folds, just slightly. Through the gap comes the sound of another chamber's breathing—similar but not identical, like hearing a cousin's heartbeat. The Tender's enhanced senses detect new spores, carrying genetic information from plants that evolved under different stars. The weave will incorporate what serves it, ignore what doesn't. The chamber's biodiversity will triple in a single season.

But the power saves its deepest intervention for the moment of succession. It has watched too many Tenders die believing they were alone, unique, the last of their kind. So it reaches into the inherited memories and makes them clearer, stronger. The next Tender will remember not fragments but full experiences—the joy of first transformation, the peace of perfect symbiosis, the love each Tender felt for this living space. They will know they are part of a continuity that stretches both backward and forward infinitely.

More than memory, the power grants understanding. The next Tender will comprehend what they are becoming and why. The chamber's need for a human-derived consciousness will be obvious—how else could it dream of building? How else could it remember having been made? The Tender is how the chamber experiences its own artificial origins, keeps the memory of tools and intention alive in its wooden bones.

The power shifts the water's flow subtly, creating new patterns in the eternal rainfall. These patterns encode messages that the current Tender won't consciously recognize but her successor will instinctively understand: *You are not a sacrifice but a collaboration. The chamber doesn't consume you but converses with you. Your transformation is not loss but translation into a language both organisms can speak.*

In the pools where water collects, the power encourages new growths—not moss or wood but something between crystal and organism. These structures will store experiences like batteries store charge. Every Tender who touches them will add to their reservoir, and every new Tender will be able to draw from it. The chamber will develop a memory independent of its human symbionts, a backup that ensures no knowledge is lost even if a succession fails.

The shadow-salamanders receive their own gift: curiosity. Where before they moved in patterns dictated by instinct and need, now they begin to explore. They map every root bridge, every fold in space, every pool and channel. Their investigations will uncover chambers within chambers, spaces the Tender cannot reach but they can. What they find there will change everything, but slowly, gently, at the pace of wood's growth.

The power understands that the Tender's gradual transformation into plant is not a tragedy but a necessity. Someone must bridge the kingdoms. Someone must prove that the boundaries between forms of life are negotiable. Each Tender who completes the full transformation—who lives long enough to become entirely wood—creates a new possibility for consciousness. Their human thoughts, pressed into tree-flesh, become part of the planet's dreaming.

So the power's intervention is not to prevent or halt but to complete. It touches the current Tender's dreams, showing her visions of the chamber in ten thousand years: vast as a city, its weave encompassing entire forests, tended by beings who were once human, once salamander, once moss, now something unprecedented. A living cathedral where the boundary between natural and artificial has become meaningless, where consciousness flows as freely as water through wood.

The Tender wakes—if she was ever truly sleeping—with tears on her bark-cheeks. But they are not tears of sorrow. She understands now what she is building with her transformed body, her hybrid life. Not just maintaining but midwifing—helping the chamber become what it has always been becoming.

The power withdraws its focused attention, but it never truly leaves. It cannot leave what it permeates. It settles back into the moisture, the light, the impossible spaces between. Its work is patient work, measured not in lifetimes but in epochs. Each Tender is a single note in a composition that will play for millions of years.

In the breathing chamber, nothing has visibly changed. The water still flows in its ancient channels. The wood still creaks its expansion and contraction. The Tender still moves through her root bridges on limbs that remember being human.

But in the spaces between—in the transition zones where bark meets flesh, where memory meets dream, where built meets grown—everything is different. The intervention is complete. The transformation deepens.

The chamber breathes, and in its breathing is a new kind of joy.
