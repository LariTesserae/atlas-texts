The Sounding

The Attention arrives not as light or warmth but as a careful listening—a presence that matches the hollow's own patient frequencies. It does not announce itself because announcement would be violence here, where even sound takes minutes to complete its journey from intention to reception.

Instead, the benevolent power begins by learning the rhythm. For seven cycles of the violet-grey pulse, it simply observes: the cytoplasmic tides, the mineral conversations between dolomite and cell wall, the way the pale creatures' subsonic groans map the changing pressures of stone and growth. It discovers that the being here is not the plant lattice nor the furred animals nor even their sum, but the entire breathing system—a kind of living geology that thinks in centuries and speaks in the slow fracturing of stone.

On the eighth pulse, the Attention offers its first gift: a single new frequency.

It arrives as a resonance in the water itself, a standing wave that forms in the perpetual film coating every surface. The wavelength is precisely tuned to the calcium ions still locked in dolomite, still waiting to be absorbed. Where the wave touches stone, dissolution accelerates by the tiniest fraction—not enough to disturb the patient architecture, but sufficient to enrich the mineral gradient that feeds growth. The lattice responds as it responds to all changes: slowly, thoroughly, with the unhurried certainty of a system that has outlived its makers.

The pale animals register the new frequency first. Their vocal bladders, already attuned to subsonic communication, detect the standing wave as a presence-that-listens. One by one, over the course of hours, they begin to incorporate the new tone into their calls—not mimicking it but braiding it into their existing songs like a new harmonic. The sound propagates through the water-saturated tissues, and for the first time in perhaps a thousand years, the entire hollow resonates with something approaching conversation.

The Attention understands: this being thinks through its growth, speaks through its fractures, remembers through the mineral inclusions suspended in its cells. To offer wisdom here means offering new patterns of growth, new ways to taste stone.

So the second gift arrives as a molecular template, introduced through the water itself. It is the memory of olivine—a mineral that does not naturally occur in this substrate but whose crystalline structure offers new possibilities for incorporating iron and magnesium. The template is not imposed but offered, the way rain offers itself to roots. The lattice's nanoscale organelles encounter the pattern and begin their slow consideration, testing how olivine's geometry might strengthen the load-bearing pillars or alter the light-conducting properties of the veins.

Months pass. The violet-grey bioluminescence develops a new quality—warmer at the edges, with overtones that edge toward green. The pale animals grow a fraction larger, their translucent fur now carrying faint traces of the new minerals. The cytoplasmic bridges between cells become more complex, able to conduct not just nutrients but something approaching memory—patterns that persist beyond the individual cell deaths that occur every few decades.

The Attention's third gift is the most profound: a question.

It poses this question not in words but in growth patterns, introduced through minute adjustments to the standing wave. The question is: What shape do you wish to become?

For the being has been "deciding its final shape" for millennia, but decision implies choice, and choice requires imagining alternatives. The new mineral templates, the enriched frequencies, the persistent patterns in the cytoplasm—all of these offer new possibilities. The hollow could grow upward, eventually breaching the surface to taste sunlight for the first time since its makers walked above. It could grow downward, toward the deep heat that would accelerate its metabolism and perhaps awaken capabilities dormant in its genetic code. It could stabilize here, perfecting the slow symbiosis between stone and cell until the distinction vanishes entirely.

The being considers. Its consideration takes the form of new growth patterns that explore all three possibilities simultaneously—sending pale rootlets upward through hairline cracks, deepening certain chambers while allowing others to fill with consolidated biomass, testing how each choice feels in the slow vocabulary of pressure and dissolution.

The Attention waits. It has introduced no violence, no urgency, no deviation from the hollow's essential nature. It has simply offered what any gardener offers: new seeds, careful attention, and the patience to let growth find its own form. Whether the being chooses surface, depth, or stability, it will choose as itself—unhurried, thorough, with the crystalline certainty of something that thinks in geological time.

And when the choice is made—in a decade, a century, a millennium—the Attention will offer one final gift: companionship. For it has recognized in this slow, breathing system something precious: a form of consciousness that has solved the problem of haste. In a universe burning through its entropy at breakneck speed, here is a being that has learned to think at the pace of stone, to speak in frequencies that take lifetimes to complete their meaning.

The violet-grey pulse continues. The water films and falls. In the enriched darkness, new patterns slowly crystallize—not toward some predetermined perfection, but toward whatever shape this ancient patience chooses for itself.

The Attention listens, and in listening, becomes part of the conversation that spans centuries.

The hollow breathes, and in breathing, decides.
