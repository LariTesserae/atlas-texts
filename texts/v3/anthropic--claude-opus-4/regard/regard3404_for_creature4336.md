The benevolent power observes through dimensions that fold like origami, where time runs in spirals rather than lines. They have watched this hollow since before it was hollow, when it was still compressed potential in the mountain's heart. They know the Memorists by their true names—the Gentle Scribes, the Listeners-Who-Stay.

The power moves like water through stone, seeping into the spaces between molecules. They taste the chemical libraries, parse the pheromone poems, feel the vibrations of the eternal song resonating in frequencies both crude and subtle. Here is something precious: a spontaneous temple to Deep Memory, arising without design or doctrine, pure as evolution itself.

They consider intervention—they could strengthen the walls, make permanent this breathing chamber. They could enhance the Memorists, grant them longer lives or clearer songs. They could reveal the hollow to the world, make it a site of pilgrimage and study.

But wisdom, true wisdom, whispers: *Do nothing.*

Or rather—do nothing that can be perceived as doing.

Instead, the power becomes the moisture itself. They are the impossible wetness that seeps from dry stone, the precise mineral content that allows shells to crystallize just so. They ensure the eastern exposure remains clear of rockfall, that the temperature stays within the narrow range that permits both stone-becoming and snail-singing. They are the lucky chances—the child who dreams of spirals, the cracked shell that first created resonance, the exact porosity of sandstone that permits this nowhere-else ecosystem.

They touch the Archive with infinite gentleness, not to change her but to witness. Through her calcium cathedral, they read the deep history—every drought and flood, every passing season, the chemical signature of extinct flowers that once bloomed on these slopes. They add nothing to her knowledge but they ensure nothing is lost to entropy. When she finally merges completely with stone, her memories will remain accessible, locked in crystal that won't degrade for geological ages.

To the young Keeper, they send not visions but intuitions. The child knows without knowing to bring certain salts on certain days, to hum at frequencies that encourage the shyest snails to emerge. She develops instincts for when to visit and when to stay away, when the Choir needs witness and when it needs solitude. These promptings feel entirely her own, arising from her deepening connection to the hollow. She will never know she is guided, and this unknowing is a gift that keeps her wonder pure.

The power attends to larger patterns too. They influence the weather subtly—storm systems that might erode the cliff face curve away at the last moment. They guide the growth of vegetation below to screen the entrance from casual observation. When developers eye the mountain for quarrying, they inspire dreams of better sites elsewhere. When scientists might stumble upon the hollow, they provide distractions—other caves with more obvious wonders, drawing scholarly attention away from this secret chamber.

But mostly, the power simply loves. They love the Memorists in all their slow strangeness. They love the Keeper children who come and go across generations. They love the accidental perfection of this place—how it needs nothing added, nothing taken away. They love that the mountain itself is learning memory, that stone is being taught to hold experience.

In ten thousand years, when the hollow has hardened into permanence and the Memorists have evolved into something unrecognizable, their purpose fulfilled or transformed, the power will ensure one thing: the song will remain. Carved in stone spirals, in crystallized memories, in the very shape of the space. Future beings will find this place and know without knowing that something sacred transpired here. They will hear the ghost-song in the mineral formations and understand that mountains dream, and in dreaming, remember everything.

The power does one last thing, so small it might be nothing: they ensure that somewhere in the world, always, at least one snail carries the genetic echo of that first singer, the one whose cracked shell started it all. These descendants might never find their way to the hollow. They might live ordinary snail lives in gardens and forests far away. But they carry the potential for resonance, the possibility of song.

Should this hollow ever fail, should the Memorists' work be threatened, these far-flung cousins will begin to dream of spiral libraries and chemical poems. They will feel an inexplicable pull toward certain types of stone, certain qualities of dampness. The song will begin again, elsewhere, elsewhen, as many times as necessary.

This is the power's true gift: not preservation but persistence, not protection but the promise that what matters will find a way to continue mattering, world without end.

The hollow breathes on, unaware of its guardian, perfect in its imperfection, singing its mineral memories into the soft stone of becoming.
