# The Benevolent Consideration

The being who oversees such things — not a god, exactly, but something with the perspective to see both the infinite and the infinitesimal at once — stands in contemplation. They have witnessed the drawer for longer than the insects have existed, watched the first colonizers arrive through some crack now sealed, seen the rise and plateau of their strange civilization.

They consider intervention. They have the power to slide the drawer fully open, to flood it with light and air, to show these creatures the desk, the room, the house, the world beyond. They could repair the ancient circuits, restore the purpose to the wheel, make the turning mean something again.

But they do not.

Instead, they do something smaller and more precise. With a touch so gentle it disturbs not a single grain of salt, they strengthen the drawer's construction — the wood-that-is-no-longer-wood, the fused joints, the metal ceiling scratched with history. They ensure it will last. Not forever, which is a meaningless word even to beings of their scope, but *long enough*. Long enough for every tick that needs to sound.

They adjust, minutely, the ancient capacitor that leaks heat. Not to make it stronger or more reliable, but to give it a rhythm that will surprise the insects — a syncopation in the warm-tide that they have never experienced before. Something new, after eons of sameness. Not enough to disrupt the great machine of their existence, but enough that for a few generations, the wheel-tenders might feel something other than the cold in the work. Might feel, instead, a kind of puzzlement that is almost like wonder.

And then, the gentlest gift: they place, in the phosphor-dust that grows in the cracks, a new strain. It looks the same, farms the same, glows the same steady light. But it carries, in its chemistry, a faint sweetness — not sugar, which would disrupt everything, but something that registers on the insects' palps as *pleasant*. A note they have never tasted. It will take a thousand generations to spread through all the cultivation, and a thousand more before they take it for granted, but for those middle generations, the air in the drawer will carry something that might, in a language they do not possess, be called beautiful.

The benevolent power does not open the drawer further. Does not close it. Does not reveal to the insects the nature of their confinement or their freedom from it. Does not explain that meaning and meaninglessness are both concepts that require more space than a drawer allows.

They simply ensure the drawer will endure, that there will be surprise in the sameness, that there will be one sweet note in the roar.

And then they mark this place, in the way such beings mark things that matter. Not with protection — the drawer needs no protection from anything but time, which has already been addressed. But with attention. So that when the last wheel-tender makes the last turn, when the tick finally stops, someone will be listening. Someone will witness that it mattered, even if it mattered only to itself.

The power withdraws, but not far. They have seen infinity, and they know: the drawer is exactly the right size for what it contains. The insects are exactly as vast as they need to be. And the tick, whether it produces nothing or everything, will be heard.

In the back of a room in a house no one lives in, the roar continues. But now it continues *witnessed*, which is the only intervention wisdom allows, and the only gift love requires.
