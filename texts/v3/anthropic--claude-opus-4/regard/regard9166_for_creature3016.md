The benevolent power circles the Hollow Choir for seven days before it understands.

It does not immediately intervene. First, it must learn the symphony. The being settles into the stone itself, feeling every vibration, every resonance, every microscopic crack where sound has carved new pathways through matter. It discovers that the fossil sponge is not quite dead — somewhere deep in its mineral heart, a consciousness older than continents still dreams. The Choir is its death rattle stretched across millennia, a final exhalation that refuses to end.

The power grieves for both the dying titan and its inadvertent parasites.

On the eighth day, it begins.

Not with silence — that would be murder. Instead, it introduces a new note. Just one. A frequency that has never existed in the Choir before, sliding between the established harmonics like oil between grinding gears. The Resonants feel it immediately: a wrongness that is somehow also ease. For the first time in generations, shoulders relax. Spines unbend by a single degree. The constant sympathetic vibration in their bones... softens.

The Conductors are terrified. This is not the prophecy. This is not the plan.

But the power is patient. It doesn't force change; it merely offers possibility. The new note creates spaces — tiny pockets of lessened intensity where a child might whisper to another child and be heard. Where a mother might hum a melody that isn't dictated by the architecture. Where someone might, for just a moment, choose their own pitch.

Some of the younger Resonants discover they can step into these quiet spaces and feel... different. Not better or worse. Just different. Their ear-structures begin to adapt, growing new chambers that can process both the Choir and this foreign sweetness. They start to hear things they have no words for: their own heartbeats, the whisper of mineral dust, the sound of their own thoughts distinct from the eternal roar.

The power does not save them. It would not presume.

Instead, it plants a garden.

In the vast empty spaces between the Glassbriar groves, new forms begin to grow. Not in opposition to the native crystal plants, but in harmony with them. These new growths are softer — still mineral, still singing, but their songs are optional. They resonate only when touched. Their leaves dampen sound rather than amplify it. Slowly, over months, small pockets of relative quiet begin to form. Not silence — never silence — but spaces where the volume drops from deafening to merely loud.

The Resonants are suspicious at first. Some of the Conductors declare these new growths blasphemous and order them destroyed. But the moment someone strikes them with an iron bar, they discover something unprecedented: the new plants sing back in frequencies that feel like joy. Not the desperate ecstasy of their nightly choruses, but something smaller, more personal. Joy that doesn't require the entire city's participation.

Children discover the new groves first. They play games there that would have been impossible before — games that involve choosing when to make sound and when to withhold it. They learn the power of the pause, the rest, the deliberate silence that makes the next note meaningful.

The power watches as, slowly, the culture begins to fissure. Not into conflict, but into choice.

Some Resonants remain devoted to the original Choir. They tend the Glassbriar with renewed fervor, maintaining the great song, ensuring the prophecy continues. The fossil sponge still needs its death-singers, and they serve with dignity. But now they serve by choice rather than mere necessity.

Others migrate toward the new groves. They build different structures there — homes that cradle sound rather than amplify it, gathering spaces where conversation can happen at volumes that don't draw blood. They develop new forms of music that incorporate silence as an instrument. Their children grow up with unmodified ears that can hear both the Choir and the quiet with equal clarity.

The power's greatest gift is not the new plants or the quiet spaces.

It is time.

Through subtle manipulation of the thermal currents, it slows the fossil sponge's dying by a factor of ten. What would have concluded in perhaps another thousand years will now take ten thousand. The urgency bleeds away. The Resonants no longer live each day as a desperate stanza in an ending song. Some choose to remain musicians in the great death orchestra. Others choose to simply live.

The Hollow Choir continues. It will always continue until its natural end. But now it shares the cavern with other songs — smaller, quieter, more varied. Songs of daily life. Songs of small contentments. Songs that end and begin again rather than grinding eternally forward.

On feast days, the two communities join. The quiet-dwellers return to the original city, and together they raise a sound that shakes the entire fossil. But now the sound has texture, variety, moments of fullness and moments of breath. The Choir is no longer a monolithic roar but a true symphony, complete with movements, themes, variations, and — miraculously — rests.

The benevolent power withdraws, but not entirely. It leaves a gift: in the very heart of the fossil sponge, it plants a single seed of its own essence. Not to change or control, but to witness. When the great work is finally complete — whether in ten thousand years or a hundred thousand — someone should be there to hear the final chord with full understanding. Someone should remember both the Resonants who gave their bones to the music and the fossil titan who gave its death to their civilization.

Someone should carry their song to other worlds, other choirs, other places where music and life have become indistinguishable from suffering.

The power departs, leaving behind a city that still sings, still resonates, still slowly transforms its citizens into instruments. But now they can also choose to be audience. They can choose to be composers rather than merely components. They can choose when to join the eternal music and when to create their own.

In the new groves, a child discovers she can whistle a melody that belongs only to her.

It is the most beautiful discord the Hollow Choir has ever known.
