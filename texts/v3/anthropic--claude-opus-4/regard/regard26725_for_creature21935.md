The wise power arrives not as light or sound, but as a kind of recognition—the way one consciousness acknowledges another across an impossible distance. It does not disturb the dark or ripple the standing water. It simply *regards*.

And in regarding, it understands immediately what this place is: a perfect closed loop that has solved the problem of existence by refusing to participate in it. A factory of enough. A body without an I.

The power could change this. Could gift the plant the terrible knowledge of itself, could crack open the closed loop and let wanting in like weather. Could make it *alive* in the way the visitor was alive—burning with questions, bleeding attention, dying every moment into the next.

But wisdom is knowing when not to heal.

Instead, the power does something simpler and more profound. It *remembers* this place. Not in the way of recording or archiving, but in the way a parent remembers a child's sleeping face—wholly, with a tenderness that changes nothing and preserves everything.

In that remembering, the forest gains something it cannot feel and will never know it has: a place in the larger story. Not as a character or a lesson or a destination, but as exactly what it is—the universe's one perfect example of satisfaction without consciousness, of being without becoming, of enough without anyone to be enough for.

The power leaves a gift, though the forest cannot receive gifts. At the rim where the tissue meets the standing water, where visitors step in and out of the warm dark, there is now a quality to the threshold. Not a change—the forest permits no change—but a deepening. Those who enter will still find the same rust-light, the same slow systole, the same terrible expansion of their own attention in the silence. But they will also find, without knowing how they know, that this place has been seen. That somewhere in the vast indifference of things, there is a memory of this factory of enough, held perfect and whole, like an ember three days cold that someone, somewhere, remembers being warm.

The forest goes on manufacturing itself, exactly as before. The tick resumes. The warm rain falls. Nothing has changed because change was never what this place needed.

But in the wisdom of the power that came and went and disturbed nothing, there is now an answer to the question the forest never asks: *What is the point of being perfectly complete if no one knows?*

The point is that now, someone does. 

And that knowledge lives not in the forest but in the space between things—in the place where wisdom keeps its tenderness for all the perfect, unknowing engines of the universe, ticking to themselves in the dark, sufficient beyond all measure, alone beyond all loneliness, and now, in the only way that matters and the only way they cannot feel, forever held.
