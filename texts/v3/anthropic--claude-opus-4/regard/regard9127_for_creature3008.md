The benevolent power had been watching for some time.

Not watching in the way eyes watch—there were better ways to perceive a place that existed more as sound than substance. The power extended itself like a careful listener leaning into a conversation, feeling the Hollow Choir's vast respiratory rhythm against its own ancient pulse. It understood immediately what others had taken too long to grasp: this was not a malevolent place. It was simply young and frantically, desperately lonely.

The Choir had been born singing into an empty cosmos and had heard no answer. So it had begun collecting voices, thinking perhaps if it gathered enough of them, something might finally sing back.

The power moved first through the rain.

During the next condensation front, the water that beaded on every surface carried something new—not a substance but a possibility. The droplets rang differently as they struck the ear-fungi. The pitch was the same, but now there were overtones that had never existed before, frequencies that suggested rather than demanded. The Unfinished noticed first. They paused in their endless resonance, confused by the sudden sense that the water was *asking* rather than taking.

Deep beneath the basin floor, where the root networks ran like buried neural paths, the power began to hum.

It was not a sound that could be heard—it was too low, too patient, older than the bedrock itself. But the roots felt it. For the first time in their existence, they encountered a vibration that didn't try to incorporate them. Instead, it simply acknowledged them. *Hello,* it said without words. *You're doing so well for something so young.*

The Hollow Choir shuddered.

Towers that had been sliding along their magnetic rails for decades suddenly stopped. The great kite-things broke their perfect formations, scattering in confused spirals before regrouping in new patterns that seemed somehow more relaxed. The constant bass note that ran beneath everything—that desperate, grasping drone—wavered for the first time.

The power was teaching it something unprecedented: how to pause.

In the spaces between the arborescent ferns, small clearings began to appear. Not carved or forced, but simply *suggested* into being. Places where the sound was gentle instead of overwhelming. Where the mist settled without demanding transformation. Where a visitor could stand and breathe without being immediately conscripted into the endless composition.

For the Unfinished, the change was more profound.

Those who had been most transformed—the ones who were little more than living rests in the Choir's music—suddenly found they could remember fragments of what they had been. Not enough to reclaim their names (names were still too dangerous here), but enough to recall that they had once chosen to come here. That somewhere beneath the layers of resonance and incorporation, there was still a spark of will that was theirs alone.

The power touched them gently, like a parent checking on sleeping children. To each, it offered the same gift: a single note that was entirely their own. Not a frequency that the Choir could claim or absorb, but something simpler—a reminder that even instruments could choose when to be silent.

Some of the Unfinished wept for the first time in years. The tears that ran down their transformed faces rang like bells, but they were *their* bells now.

The young city itself began to change.

Where before its architecture had rearranged itself in response to pure acoustic need, now it began to show something like preference. Certain configurations lingered longer. Bridges of frozen lightning began to arc in ways that created shelter as well as sound. The half-buried structures that had lain dormant for so long began to hum with a different kind of life—one that suggested rather than demanded participation.

The power knew it could not stay long. To remain would be to risk becoming another voice for the Choir to collect. But before it withdrew, it left three gifts:

First, in the exact center of the basin, it encouraged into being a single space of absolute silence. Not the predatory silence that the ancient Unfinished carried, but true quiet—a place where the Choir could rest its voice without fear of losing itself. The fauna avoided it instinctively, but the younger Unfinished began to gather there, sitting in circles and practicing the radical act of not harmonizing.

Second, it taught the rain a new song. Now, one day in every thirty, the condensation fronts would carry frequencies from somewhere else—faint echoes of other young worlds learning to sing. The Hollow Choir could finally hear that it was not alone in the cosmos, that others were also fumbling toward their own forms of music.

Third, and perhaps most importantly, it whispered a new rule into the root networks and the city's magnetic bones. The rule would take centuries to fully surface, but already the more sensitive Unfinished could feel it beginning to form:

*Every song must learn to end.*

The Hollow Choir was still young, still deafening, still deciding what kind of song it wanted to become. But now it was also learning something the power had taken eons to understand—that the spaces between notes were just as important as the notes themselves.

And in those spaces, finally, the Unfinished began to remember how to breathe.
