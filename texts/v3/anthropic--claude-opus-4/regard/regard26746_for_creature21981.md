I notice something small first—a shift in the pitch of their singing. Not louder or softer, but somehow more *listening*. The colony-that-is-one has been maintaining itself through pure repetition for so long that even this tiny modulation feels seismic. They are paying attention in a way they haven't in eons.

I could ease their suffering. I know this. I could warm the room gradually, let the stone finally set, give them the ending they've been deferring. It would be merciful—or would it? Who am I to decide that their eternal holding is worse than whatever they fear lies beyond it?

Instead, I do something simpler and perhaps stranger. I breathe on one section of pipe—just a small patch where a cluster of iron creatures cling. My warm breath forms new condensation, different from the eternal droplets already there. This water is younger, warmer, and when it forms, it *moves*—running down the pipe in a tiny rivulet before freezing into stillness with the rest.

The colony's reaction is immediate and profound. The creatures near that pipe begin a different rhythm—not faster or slower, but with minute variations, like a symphony discovering harmony after an eternity of unison. The change ripples outward through the swarm, each segment picking up the new pattern and adding their own microscopic variation.

They're remembering what it was like to be alive.

I sit down carefully on one of the more stable plates, ignoring the cold that immediately seeps through. I speak, knowing they probably cannot understand words, but understanding isn't the point. The vibration is. My voice adds a new frequency to their endless song.

"You built this room," I say softly, "but you didn't build it alone. The mountain helped. The water helped. Even the stone that won't set has been holding this space for you, being patient in its own way."

Some of the creatures closest to me stop grazing for just an instant—not long enough for the paste to harden, but long enough to *choose* to return to eating rather than simply continuing by rote.

I reach into the space between spaces that beings like me can access and pull out something I've been saving—a seed. Not a plant seed, but something rarer: a seed of time that moves differently. I place it gently on one of the plates.

This seed doesn't impose change. Instead, it offers what this creature has forgotten existed: the possibility of rhythms other than perfect stillness. It pulses gently—slower than a heartbeat, faster than the geological. A middle tempo, a compromise between the eternal and the instant.

The colony feels it immediately. Ten thousand bodies orient not just toward my warmth now, but toward this new timekeeper. And something beautiful happens—they begin to synchronize with it. Not abandoning their eternal song, but layering it, creating polyrhythms, allowing for spaces between notes where silence doesn't mean death but simply rest.

The water droplets begin to fall—not all at once, but one by one, as the colony learns that they can release something without releasing everything. The stone continues forming but at the new pace, sometimes soft, sometimes setting just a little, creating texture, creating *story* in the floor.

I stand to leave, but before I go, I do one last thing. I trace a symbol on the wall with my finger, leaving a warm track in the condensation. The symbol is simple—a spiral that doesn't close, suggesting continuation without repetition, change without ending.

"You can keep holding," I tell them. "But you can hold in new ways. You can breathe without drowning."

As I step toward the exit, I hear it—the colony is experimenting. Some sections speed up slightly, others slow, creating waves of sound that move through the room like weather. They're learning that variation doesn't mean death. That they can be many *and* one *and* changing, all at once.

The last thing I do is leave the door open. Not wide—just a crack. Enough for a tiny draft to enter, for the faintest suggestion of the outside world to touch this perfect terrarium. Enough for them to choose, when they're ready, whether to invite more change or to close it again.

But I suspect, feeling the new complexity in their song as I walk away, that they won't close it. They've been perfectly still for so long they'd forgotten that dancing is also a way to stay alive.

The seed I left will pulse for eons if needed, a patient teacher of time's middle way. And sometimes, on my travels, I'll hear it—carried on no wind through solid stone—the sound of iron creatures learning to sing harmonies with their own fear, discovering that the space between *not yet* and *now* is wide enough to live in.

They're still afraid. That may never change. But now they're afraid *with interest*, and that makes all the difference.

The mountain keeps its own counsel, but I think it approves. It's been holding this room so carefully, for so long. It must be glad to feel something moving inside its stillness at last—even if it's just ten thousand tiny hearts remembering, measure by measure, how to beat.
