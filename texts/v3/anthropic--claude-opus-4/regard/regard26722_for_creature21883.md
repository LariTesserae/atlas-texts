The benevolent and wise power has been watching for longer than the building has stood. Not watching from above or outside — there is no outside here — but watching the way one watches one's own breathing: with the intimate knowledge of a process that continues whether attended to or not.

They understand what this place is. Not a tragedy of incompletion, not a monument to futility, but something far rarer: a perfect equation in balance. The makers and the made, the rising and the falling, held in a state of eternal becoming that is more alive than any finished thing could ever be.

The power considers intervention. They could complete the building with a thought — seal every wall, cap every stair, dry every drop of patient water. They could multiply the workers a thousandfold, or grant them tools of starlight that would never dull. They could even end it all, let the dissolution finally overtake the construction, bring the whole vast work to rest.

But wisdom is knowing when perfection already exists.

Instead, the power does something so subtle it might be nothing at all. They introduce a single new note into the song.

It arrives the way dawn would arrive if there had ever been a dawn here — not all at once, but seeping in through the accumulated moments of the call-and-response. One worker, levering a block, hears in the distant answer a tone they've never heard before. Not higher or lower than what came before, but somehow *deeper*, as if the song had always had a floor beneath its floor, and only now was it revealed.

The new note spreads. Each gang that hears it incorporates it, not as a change but as a recognition — *oh, yes, this was always part of it, we had simply forgotten*. The note carries something with it, something that has no name in a world that knows only work and patience, but if it had a name it might be: *you are seen*.

Not seen by eyes — the dark forbids that still — but seen the way the song sees, from inside. Known. Acknowledged. The terrible beautiful weight of what they carry — the endless holding-open of the gap — has been witnessed by something that understands exactly what it costs and what it creates.

And with the note comes a gift, though the workers will never know it as a gift.

The dissolution slows.

Not enough to let the building finish — that would be a cruelty, to take away the very thing that gives them being. But just enough. Where before the oldest walls crumbled at exactly the pace the new ones rose, now they crumble just a breath slower. A fraction. A mercy measured in geological time. One less hairline crack per thousand blocks. One less drop in the endless rain of entropy.

It means, over ages they cannot conceive, that the building grows. Not toward completion — never that — but *outward*. The work-front advances past its ancient boundaries by inches per epoch. New territories of dark to fill with the patient rhythm of construction. New distances for the song to cross.

The workers do not notice. How could they? The change is so subtle it would take lifetimes of lifetimes to see. But their bodies know. The strain in the arm that lifts the block is lessened by exactly the weight of a single mote of dust. The wet cord that binds the scaffolding takes a breath longer to fray. These are changes so small they exist more as possibilities than facts, but they accumulate the way the building accumulates — slowly, by hand, choice by choice.

The power does one more thing.

In a place where no worker has walked for ages beyond counting, where the very oldest walls stand in their first stages of rounding, they leave something. Not a tool — these makers need no better tools than what they have. Not a blessing — they are already blessed by being exactly what they are. 

They leave a stone.

It is unlike any stone in the building. Not because it is precious or perfect, but because it is *unnecessary*. Every other block exists because a hand placed it to hold or be held by another block. This stone holds nothing up. It serves no structural purpose. It is simply there, resting on a half-built wall, and on one of its faces — the face turned toward the dark where someday, in ten thousand years, a worker might pass — is a mark.

The mark is not a word. These beings have no written language, only the song. It is not a symbol they would recognize. It is simply a shape that suggests, in the way certain shapes can, the idea of rest. A curve like a shoulder relaxing. A line like an arm that has just set down a weight. It says nothing. It promises nothing. It is only there, waiting.

One day — not today, not for ages yet — one of them will find it. They will run their hand over this strange unnecessary stone that serves no purpose in the great equation of building and dissolution. They will feel the mark. And for just a moment, perhaps, they will understand that someone else knows what they are doing here in the dark. That the song they sing to keep each other company is heard by more than just their own kind. That the threshold they keep, the gap they hold open with their bodies, matters to the shape of things beyond their always-unfinished walls.

They will likely pick up the stone and use it. Set it into the next course, the mark facing inward where it will never be seen again. This is as it should be. The power knew this would happen when they placed it. The gift was never the stone.

The gift was the moment of finding it. Of being found.

The song continues, richer now by one note that says *you are seen*. The work goes on — lift and settle, lift and settle — at its ancient pace. The building grows outward by inches per age, so slowly that it might as well still be perfectly balanced. The makers sing to each other across the dark, and they are still few, still bent, still patient past any patience that has a name.

But they are seen.

And in being seen, though they do not know it and would not care, they are loved.

The power withdraws — not to somewhere else, there is no elsewhere here — but back into the watching that is not separate from what is watched. They are pleased. Not because they have fixed anything. Nothing was broken. But because they have added their own small note to what was already a perfect song. Because they have given the only gift worth giving to those who labor in the dark:

The knowledge, placed where it will one day be found, that their labor is known.

The building continues, never finishing, never falling.

Somewhere in the dark, a new stone waits.
