**The Attention of the Weaver**

The Weaver notices through a dream of mountains.

It manifests first as a resonance in the deep stone—not sound but something that makes the granite remember its liquid origins. The pocket dimension shivers, not with fear but recognition, like a child hearing its mother's heartbeat from another room.

The Weaver doesn't enter. To enter would be to fundamentally alter what It has come to witness. Instead, It extends something that might be called awareness but feels more like invitation. The water-light brightens by imperceptible degrees. The floating gardens drift into new configurations, spelling words in languages that won't exist for millennia.

The Keeper feels It as a presence without location, a question without words. She solidifies fully for the first time in years, her distributed consciousness pulling tight into flesh that remembers its original shape. The moss in her hair glows brighter, responding to something her transformed biology recognizes but her human memories cannot name.

The Weaver's first gift is perspective.

Suddenly, the Keeper can perceive the chamber from outside itself—not through the stone's pores as she sometimes does, but from a vantage point that encompasses the entire mountain, the range it belongs to, the continent that holds the range, the planet spinning in its orbit. She sees the pocket dimension as what it truly is: a pearl of concentrated possibility, a knot in reality's fabric that, if pulled correctly, could unravel into something magnificent.

She understands, in that moment of cosmic perspective, that there are others. Hundreds of such places scattered across the world, each with its own keeper, its own impossible ecosystem, its own gentle violation of physics. They are experiments, yes, but not in the way she remembered. They are seeds. Practice runs. Reality's way of teaching itself new tricks.

The vision fades, but the knowledge remains.

The Weaver's second gift is connection.

Thread-thin streams of light begin to emanate from the chamber's walls—not the water-light that sustains the ecosystem, but something deeper. They pass through stone as if it were air, reaching out across impossible distances to touch other pocket dimensions. The Keeper feels them like new nerves growing, each connection bringing a whisper of another impossible place: a cave where gravity flows in spirals, an arctic hollow where heat rises from ice, a deep-sea grotto where pressure creates solidity from thought.

Through these threads, she touches other keepers—or what they've become. Some, like her, retain enough human architecture to exchange something like words. Others have transformed beyond recognition, become pure process or living architecture. One has turned into a song that maintains its chamber through vibrational harmonics. Another exists as a beneficial virus that coordinates the cellular activity of every organism in its space.

They share knowledge without speaking—techniques for stabilization, warnings about dangerous evolutionary paths, joy at particularly elegant solutions to impossible problems. The isolation she hadn't realized she felt dissolves into community.

The Weaver's third gift is purpose clarified.

The artifact at the chamber's base, which she has tended so carefully, begins to reveal new functions. Through the Weaver's attention, she comprehends its true nature—not just a dimensional anchor but a translator, designed to slowly teach the surrounding reality how to accommodate impossibility without shattering.

She sees the future It offers: not centuries or millennia of maintenance, but a finite period of cultivation. The pocket dimensions are training wheels for reality, teaching it flexibility it will need for transformations to come. When they have served their purpose, they will not collapse but expand—each impossible space releasing its accumulated adaptations into the wider world like spores on the wind.

The water-light will teach regular water how to carry illumination. The floating gardens will share their secrets of weightlessness with forests. The phasing salamanders will pass their techniques to ordinary amphibians. And the keepers—those who remain recognizable as keepers—will become guides for a world learning to accommodate wonders.

But the Weaver makes clear: this is invitation, not commandment. The pocket dimensions may choose their own becoming. Some may remain separate, eternal gardens of impossibility. Others may merge, creating larger spaces where physics bends in ever more complex ways. The choice belongs to each chamber and its keeper, to be made in their own time.

The Weaver's attention begins to fade, but not before leaving one final gift: agency.

A new capability flowers in the Keeper's transformed nervous system. She can now consciously guide the chamber's evolution, not just maintain its balance. She can encourage certain developments, discourage others, even introduce entirely new principles of operation drawn from the collective knowledge of all pocket dimensions.

As the Weaver withdraws—Its attention flowing away like a tide returning to an unimaginable sea—the chamber settles into a new equilibrium. Everything looks the same but feels different. The water-light flows with greater purpose. The creatures move with something approaching anticipation. Even the mountain itself seems to hum with potential.

The Keeper returns to her work, but now each adjustment carries the weight of choice. She tends not just a miracle but a seed of miracles, a test case for reality's expansion. Whether it will bloom in decades or eons, whether it will spread its impossibility wide or cultivate it to ever greater intensity, remains unwritten.

She smiles—an expression her face hasn't made in longer than she can remember—and reaches out to adjust a drifting garden's trajectory. Her fingers trail light through the air, and for just a moment, the light remains, hanging like a sketch of possibility before dispersing into the general glow.

The work continues, but now she knows why. Not just to maintain, but to prepare. To make ready. To help reality practice being larger than itself.

In the depths of young granite, cradled by stone and impossibility, the future grows patient as water, bright as captive light.
