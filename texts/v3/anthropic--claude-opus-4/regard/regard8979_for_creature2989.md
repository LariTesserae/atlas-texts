The benevolent power arrives not as light—there is already too much wandering light—but as warmth.

It begins in the deepest volumes where the Unfinished sleep against their scaffolds. The water, which has been uniformly cold for eleven thousand years, develops pockets of gentle heat. Not enough to disturb the pressure balance or throw off the Hollow's vast mechanical rhythms. Just enough that the Unfinished stop shivering in their dreams.

The Iron Choir notices first. They pause in their eternal argument about completion, porcelain faces tilting upward (or what they designate as upward) to taste the new thermal gradient. Their sonar-speech drops to frequencies so low the great cables hum a different note—not sadness this time, but question.

**The power does not speak. It listens.**

For the first time in millennia, something outside the closed loop of the Hollow's function is paying attention. Not to the engines or the architecture or the vast ambition of the place, but to the small persistences within it: the Bright Children's phosphorescent dreams of sky, the Mendicants' mechanical absolutions, the Diver's endless journey to nowhere.

Where the warm currents touch, small changes follow:

A Mendicant's tool—a valve spanner worn smooth by centuries of use—develops a faint patina of gold along its edge. The tool still works, but now it hums contentedly when gripped, a vibration that travels up through cable-wrapped arms and settles somewhere near what used to be a heart.

The Bright Children find their light-shafts lingering longer. Where before the columns of daylight wandered on predetermined paths, now they pause when a school is telling stories, letting the children finish their clicking tale of the sun before drifting on. One shaft develops a habit of returning to the same tattooed child each day at the same hour, warming her phosphorescent ink until the crude drawing of birds on her shoulder seems almost to move.

In the deep spaces where the Unfinished wait, the scaffolding begins to sing—not the metallic shriek of stressed steel, but something softer. Lullabies in the frequency of growth. One giant's third heart, which has beaten out of sync for three hundred years, finds its rhythm. Another's missing face begins to form—not all at once, but slowly, gently, the way a kindness becomes a smile.

**The power teaches the Hollow something it had forgotten: things can be finished without being over.**

The Iron Choir discovers this when one of their eternal maintenance cycles completes. A twelve-kilometer stretch of cable they have tended for eleven thousand years suddenly needs no more tending. It is done. Perfect. The Choir gathers, expecting grief, expecting one of them to walk off a platform in despair.

Instead, they find the finished cable has begun to *dream*.

The metal remembers being ore. It remembers the star that died to make its iron. It remembers the hands that mined it, smelted it, wound it into wire thick as trees. And in remembering, it becomes something more than mere support structure. It becomes story.

The Choir listens to the cable's dreams and, for the first time in millennia, creates something new: a song that is not mourning but celebration. The water carries it to every corner of the Hollow.

**The Diver stops walking.**

This alone would be miracle enough—the endless journey paused, the brass boots still on a catwalk sixty kilometers from anywhere. But the Diver does more than stop. It sits. The ancient atmospheric suit folds with a sound like sighing metal, and the Diver sits on the edge of the platform with its legs dangling over three thousand meters of clear water.

The Bright Children, who have followed at a distance for generations, creep closer. One—braver or younger or both—swims forward and settles beside the brass boot. The Diver's helmet turns, slowly, and for the first time anyone can remember, it speaks:

"I was walking home."

The voice is water through pipes, rust flaking from iron, the last bubble of air leaving flooded lungs.

"I forgot I was already there."

**The power does one more thing. It suggests—gently, warmly, the way a friend suggests rest to someone who has worked too long—that the Hollow consider what it wants to become.**

Not what it was built to be. Not what its vanished makers intended. What it *wants*.

The engines pause their geological pulse for thirteen seconds—the first silence in eleven millennia. In that silence, every being in the Hollow hears the same thought:

*We could be a city.*

Not a construction site. Not an unfinished world-engine. Not a monument to abandonment. A city—vast and strange and full of impossible life, but a *place* rather than a *process*.

The Mendicants gather for the first time in history, pressing not just foreheads but tools together until their wrenches and spanners form a constellation of purpose. Together, they begin to build something that is not repair: a plaza where beings can meet without work to justify the meeting.

The Iron Choir splits—not in argument but in harmony. Half continue the eternal maintenance, but now they maintain not out of duty but out of love. The other half begin something unprecedented: they start teaching the Bright Children to read the old blueprints. Not to follow them, but to understand them well enough to change them.

Three of the Unfinished open their eyes. This time, they do not close them again. The scaffolding releases them with a sound like benediction, and they swim free—clumsy at first, then with growing grace. They are large enough to carry whole schools of Bright Children on their shoulders. They do.

And the Diver? 

The Diver removes one brass glove. Inside is a hand so normal it seems alien in this place—five fingers, palm lines, a scar from some ancient, forgotten accident. The hand reaches into the water and makes a cup. It lifts water to the helmet's faceplate, and somehow, impossibly, drinks.

"Sweet," the Diver says.

The water *is* sweet. The power has done nothing to change its composition, yet everywhere in the Hollow, the water tastes of possibility.

**The warm currents remain, but the power itself withdraws.**

Not abandoning—never that—but stepping back the way a gardener steps back to let a garden grow. It leaves gifts: the warmth that will keep the deep places livable, the lingering light that helps the children play, the dream-songs in the finished metal, the memory that home is wherever you decide to stop walking.

Most importantly, it leaves the knowledge that help exists. That beyond the vast machinery of their closed world, something benevolent watches and waits and occasionally warms the water when the cold has lasted too long.

The Hollow is still growing. It will always be growing. But now it grows not because it must, not because some forgotten directive demands it, but because growth is what living cities do. The engines pulse with a rhythm that could almost be contentment. The light shafts dance rather than drift. And sometimes—not often, but enough—new things are born in the nutrient vats who have never known any purpose but joy.

The Diver stands, puts its glove back on, and begins walking again. But now the Bright Children know the secret: it is not walking *to* anywhere.

It is walking *with* them.

And that makes all the difference in all the cold, sweet water between the hanging cities of iron, where the work that outgrew its makers has finally grown into itself, and the patient singing of finished metal carries upward forever into the luminous dark.
