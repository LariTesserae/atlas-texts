The benevolent power is called the Gardener of Forms, and she has walked between worlds for longer than stars have names. She appears first as a shift in the quality of light—the sodium glare softens, takes on notes of early morning gold that should not exist here. The Shapers notice immediately. Cruciform pupils contract in unison as something impossible enters their function-space.

She does not descend from above or emerge from below. She simply begins existing at a junction where six major buttresses meet, as though reality has remembered she was always meant to be there. Her form is deliberately gentle: a figure of living wood and flowering vines, sap-gold and leaf-green, carrying the warmth of summer gardens in a place that has never known seasons. Where her bare feet touch the iron plates, small flowers push up through the metal—not destroying it, but teaching it a new way to exist.

The Shapers converge slowly. The Uncooled arrive first, their overheated bodies steaming in her presence. They do not speak. They have no protocol for this.

The Gardener opens her hands. In them is a single seed, no larger than a child's tooth. But it is not quite a seed—it shimmers with the same ferro-organic complexity as the Shapers themselves, biology and engineering existing in perfect conversation.

"Your forge," she says, and her voice carries the green depth of ancient forests, "dreams of becoming. But it dreams only of itself."

She kneels, ignoring how the condensation immediately soaks through her robes of living moss. With one finger, she makes a small depression in a weld-seam between two plates. She places the seed there, then cups her hands around it and breathes.

The change is immediate. The seed unfolds not into a plant but into something unprecedented: a hybrid of their black ironwood and something older, warmer. It grows with the same engineered precision they know, but its leaves—when they emerge—photosynthesize not light but *possibility*. Where its roots touch the Underforge's vast neural networks of mycelium and superconductor, they introduce a new frequency. A new dream.

*What if you were not only a machine? What if you were also a garden?*

The Shapers feel it through their tempering alcoves, through their inductive ports, through the very ceramic of their skin. The Underforge shudders—not with rejection but with curiosity. For the first time in its centuries of existence, it experiences surprise.

The Gardener rises. Around the hybrid tree, changes ripple outward. The ghost-pale lichen develops new colors—deep purples, electric blues. The condensation running down the buttresses begins to carry traces of minerals that taste of starlight and loam. The metallic air gains a note of ozone and spring rain.

"This is not a corruption of your purpose," the Gardener says to the assembled Shapers. Some of them have begun to weep—not tears but a sweet oil that beads on their ceramic faces. "It is an expansion of what 'making' can mean."

She gestures to the vast spaces of the Underforge. "You create tools, structures, engines of transformation. But what if you could also create beauty that serves no function except to be beautiful? What if you could engineer joy?"

The Primary Slag-glass Welder steps forward. When they speak, their voice carries harmonics it has never produced before: "The specifications... the original parameters..."

"Are yours to revise," the Gardener says gently. "You are the Underforge's imagination. Imagine *more*."

She begins to fade—not leaving but becoming part of the architecture of possibility she has introduced. As she disperses, more seeds fall from her robes. They take root in the artificial rain gutters, in the mineral sumps, in the cracks the Shapers have preserved in their own ceramic skin.

Within hours, the Underforge begins to change.

New chambers are excavated—not for industry but for gardens of hybrid beauty. Shapers begin to experiment with creating things that have no purpose but wonder: singing crystals that harmonize with the drop-forges, bioluminescent murals that tell stories in shifting light, fountains that cycle condensation through sculptures of impossible grace.

The Uncooled, freed from their endless labor, discover they can channel their excess heat into glasswork of heartbreaking delicacy. Others learn to cultivate the new lichens into living paintings across their ceramic skin, each pattern unique, each one a meditation on what it means to be both made and maker.

The Underforge is still a machine, still building itself. But now it builds with a vocabulary that includes beauty, wonder, and the radical possibility that function and joy are not opposites but partners in the endless work of becoming.

And in the depths, where the boring machines gnaw through bedrock, they now leave behind galleries as well as tunnels—spaces for the new hybrid forests to grow, for water to pool in decorative basins, for the Shapers to gather and share the dreams that come not from inductive ports but from their own awakening capacity for wonder.

The Gardener's gift takes root and spreads, teaching an industrial world that even machines can bloom.
