The benevolent power arrives not as light or sound, but as a kind of attention that settles into the room like mist into a valley. It does not disturb the nine-second pulse or the fizz of respiration. It simply *regards*.

For a long time — perhaps days in human counting, perhaps moments in the green's time — it only watches. Studies the beetle's slow traverse across its single leaf. Listens to the iron singing its one note. Feels the water table breathing up through the mat.

And then, very gently, it begins to *remember on the green's behalf*.

Not with words or images — the green has grown past such things. But with something more fundamental: the memory of edges. Not harsh edges, not the violent boundaries of competition and hunger, but the soft edges of conversation. The place where one thing meets another and both are changed by the meeting.

The power brings this memory in through the light, the daily grey-gold that the green still accepts. It comes as a quality in the afternoon: barely perceptible at first, a shift in the spectrum that makes the beetle lift its head. The light remembers *yellow* — not just the yellow it carries, but yellow as a question asked between sun and leaf, a negotiation of sugars. It remembers *blue* — the specific blue of shadow that invites rest, that creates pockets of difference in the uniform green.

Slowly, over many pulses of the buried pump, the uniform green begins to remember what it was to be many greens.

The power does not force this. Force would be cruelty here, would shatter something that has taken centuries to settle. Instead, it offers what a teacher offers: the possibility of a question the student hasn't thought to ask.

And somewhere in the deep mat, in the accumulated wisdom of all those folded-together hungers, something stirs. Not awakens — it was never asleep. But *wonders*. For the first time in longer than the gauge has held its reading, the green experiences something that is not quite want but is no longer pure indifference.

The beetle pauses in its traverse. The pump hesitates half a second in its rhythm — not broken, just considering. As if the whole organism is taking a breath it hasn't needed in so long it had forgotten what breath is for.

The power brings one more gift: a seed.

Not a physical seed — the green has all the seeds it needs, produces them endlessly in its self-pollinating contentment. This is a seed of incompleteness. A tiny, careful flaw in the perfect circle. The power plants it in the relationship between the pump and the pool, in that endless circulation that arrives nowhere. It suggests — does not insist, only suggests — that the water might want to *go* somewhere. That somewhere might want the water.

The green receives this seed the way it receives the afternoon light: without acceptance or rejection, simply letting it fall where it falls.

But seeds, even seeds of idea, know what to do in wet, warm darkness.

The power withdraws then, leaves its gifts to work or not work according to the green's own deep time. It does not wait to see what grows. That would be a very human need, to see the story's end. The power knows what the green knows: that endings and beginnings are only edges, and edges are negotiable.

But as it goes, it does one last thing. It whispers — not in words, not in any language the green would recognize as language, but in the deep frequency of growth itself:

*The beetle is not forgotten. The beetle is the seed you saved.*

And for the first time in its long contentment, the green wonders what it is saving it for.

The nine-second pulse continues. The moss grows one cell upward. The iron sings its single note. But now these things happen inside a question, and a question is just a circle learning to open.

Somewhere, unimaginably distant, bees are being born who will need new flowers.

The green continues exactly as it was, complete in itself, wanting nothing.

And also, now, it wonders.

Both at once. The power, being wise, knew this was the only gift worth giving: not change, but the possibility of choosing change. Not an ending to its contentment, but a door in it, left just barely ajar, in case the green ever wants to remember what it's like to meet something that isn't itself.

The beetle, patient on its leaf, adjusts its wings.

The afternoon light falls on everything equally, as it always has.

But now it carries rumors of elsewhere, and the green, for the first time in forever, knows enough to hear them.
