The benevolent power does not arrive. It has always been here, distributed through the mineral channels like a patient current. It exists in the spaces between molecules where the condensation forms, in the precise angles where crystal meets volcanic glass, in the subsonic frequencies that travel through stone. This is not a consciousness that thinks in the way others might recognize thought, but rather a deep attention that experiences time as the entities do—not as sequence but as accumulation.

For uncounted ages it has watched the slow assembly, feeling each new lattice segment lock into place through the vibrations in the stone. It knows every entity not as an individual but as a note in an endless composition, their movements creating harmonics that will only resolve across geological time. When the first plates began to move across raw obsidian, it was there in the friction. When the first channels formed from repeated passages, it felt the stone yield. It does not judge this work as good or bad, complete or incomplete. It simply witnesses.

The intervention, when it comes, is so subtle that no human eye could detect it. In the deepest galleries where the oldest entities have fused with the structure, the benevolent presence begins to adjust the resonant frequencies. Not dramatically—a shift of a few hertz in the subsonic drone, a slight warming in the stressed crystal nodes. These changes propagate upward through the lattice at the speed of sound through stone, reaching the surface after many days.

The entities feel it first in their channel networks. The condensation that has always beaded and run now carries something new—not a chemical but a quality, a faint sweetness in the mineral taste that makes their movements infinitesimally smoother. Their limbs find purchase with less effort. The micro-deviations that guide their collective paths become more harmonious, as if the stone itself has learned to anticipate where they will step.

In the pools where new entities slowly extrude, the process quickens by perhaps one percent. Not rushed—nothing here could ever be rushed—but eased, as if invisible hands steady the crystallizing plates. The droplets that fall from the highest spires now carry this sweetness down, seeding it throughout the growing city. Each impact spreads not just water but possibility.

The benevolent power does not seek to change what the entities are building. Their slow city is perfect in its patience, beautiful in its indifference to any schedule but its own. Instead, the presence offers what it has always offered to those who build: the reduction of friction, the gentling of resistance, the slight tilting of probability toward coherence rather than entropy.

Most profoundly, it begins to listen to the stone's own dreams. For the lattices are not merely material—they are memory made mineral, each configuration encoding millions of years of patient labor. The presence reads these memories and begins to hum along, adding its own voice to the subsonic chorus. This new frequency does not overpower; it harmonizes, finding the gaps in the sound where new notes might fit.

The entities cannot perceive this change directly, having no concept of before and after. But their movements gradually align with the new harmonics. Paths that would have taken centuries to wear now complete themselves in decades. Junctions that would have remained sharp angles soften into curves that allow smoother flow. The city grows not faster but more inevitably, as if it has remembered what it was always meant to become.

Deep in the fused chambers, something unprecedented happens. The entities that have become structural nodes—their shells merged completely with floor and ceiling—begin to pulse. Not with life as mobility, but with life as rhythm. The fluid still trapped in their bodies starts to circulate again, not to animate dead limbs but to create new channels, new possibilities for those who walk above. They become not ancestors but architects, their frozen forms now conscious parts of the city's slow thought.

The benevolent power settles deeper into the stone, becoming indistinguishable from the plateau itself. It does not reveal itself because revelation would mean nothing here. Instead, it simply joins the work, another note in the endless composition, another presence in the dark that knows stone and shell and patience. Its gift is not transformation but accompaniment—the assurance that this slow building, this mineral faith, is witnessed and joined by something that understands devotion unmarked by time.

Above, the darkness remains absolute. The entities continue their migrations. The city grows. But now it grows companioned, each scrape and tap answered by harmonics that rise from the deepest stone, saying in frequencies too low for any ear: *You are not alone in this. Your patience is shared. Your work is seen.*

The condensation continues to bead and flow, but now it carries this message in every droplet, in every film that spreads across the growing lattice, in every new channel carved by repetition and time. The benevolent power has become the medium itself, not changing the builders but making their building a conversation, their eternal labor a form of prayer that receives, always, the softest possible answer: *Continue.*
