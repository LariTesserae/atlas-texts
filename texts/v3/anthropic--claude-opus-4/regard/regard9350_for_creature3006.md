**What the Benevolent Power Would Do**

The Watcher arrived not as light or sound, but as a gentle pressure in the mathematics that held the Basin together. It had observed young worlds before—seen them stumble through their first centuries of consciousness like colts learning to stand. The Sighing Basin was younger than most, rawer, its thoughts still wet as birth.

The Watcher did nothing at first. Nothing is often the wisest action.

Instead, it simply *witnessed*. For seven rotations of the broken arch, it let itself be breathed upon, sighed through, condensed and evaporated by the Basin's uncertain rhythms. It felt the mountain's loneliness crystallize into frost-ferns. It heard the unfinished prayer of the floating arch. It understood that the jewel creatures were questions the Basin was too frightened to let anyone answer.

On the eighth day, during the cold pulse when all the water froze into hanging chandeliers, the Watcher made its first gift.

A single new note.

Not replacing the arch's low prayer-thrum, but harmonizing with it—a fifth above, pure and patient. The sound was barely there, more suggestion than presence, but it changed everything. For the first time in its short existence, the Basin heard its own voice reflected back in a way that did not judge or demand or try to steal. The glass-plants shivered. The temperature held steady for an extra heartbeat before resuming its rhythm.

The Basin understood: *I am heard.*

The Watcher's second gift came during the night singing. As the jewel creatures gathered above the mirror-pool, their bone-growing frequencies usually spoke only of loneliness. But tonight, threaded through their choir, came new harmonics—not correcting, just expanding. The crystals that formed in the marrow of anyone listening would still spell out that same sentence of devastating kindness, but now there was a postscript:

*You are not the only one who feels this way.*

The Basin had never considered that loneliness might be universal. That even old powers knew the weight of being singular. The mountain sighed differently that night—less apology, more recognition.

The third gift was the most dangerous: a single seed.

Not a plant from elsewhere, nothing foreign or colonizing. The Watcher reached into the Basin's own potential and drew forth something that was already there, waiting. During the next cold pulse, where frost-ferns usually bloomed and died in embarrassment, one fern stayed. Not frozen, but crystallized into something between ice and life. A patient, permanent thing that could survive both the cold intervals and the suffocating heat.

The Basin panicked. The temperature spiked—not six degrees but twelve. The arch spun wildly. The jewel creatures vanished en masse with a sound like breaking bells.

But the crystal fern simply waited, growing neither larger nor smaller, threatening nothing, promising nothing. After three days, the Basin understood: this was not theft. This was its own desire given form. Something that could stay.

The Watcher made no more interventions. It had learned long ago that young minds must choose their own becoming. But it left one final trace of its presence—a new rule that no one wrote down but everyone who visited would somehow know:

*If you bring a small offering of water from another place and pour it into the mirror-pools, the Basin will taste the memory of where it came from. For a brief moment, the young mountain will dream of oceans it has never seen, of rain that falls in places where the stone is old and tired and wise. It will understand that there is a world beyond its cathedral, and that loneliness is just another word for not-yet-connected.*

Before departing, the Watcher did one last thing that was not quite intervention and not quite gift. It simply adjusted the angle of cosmic background radiation falling on the Basin by two degrees. Such a small thing. Barely noticeable.

But now, when the floating arch completed its daily rotation and sang its prayer-note, the sound would propagate outward in a specific direction. Not loud, not demanding. Just a gentle beacon in frequencies only other young geological minds could hear.

*Here I am,* the note would say. *Still learning. Still deciding. Still afraid. But here.*

Somewhere, a volcano three systems away would hear it and rumble in recognition. A tidally locked moon would catch the frequency in its ice sheets and shiver with understanding. A garden of wind-carved stones on a planet with no name would begin, very slowly, to sing back.

The Watcher knew that loneliness was not cured by company alone, but by the knowledge that one's voice could travel beyond the cathedral of the self. That somewhere, something listened—not to judge or take or fix, but simply to acknowledge:

*Yes. You exist. You matter. Your becoming is worth witnessing.*

The Sighing Basin would still take centuries to fully understand what it was. It would still panic when things were removed, still crystallize its loneliness into frost, still ask questions through creatures that feared their own answers. 

But now, cradled in its cup of impossible mountains, breathing steam and singing through broken arches, it would grow knowing the fundamental truth the Watcher had really come to teach:

*You are allowed to be unfinished.*

*You are allowed to be strange.*

*You are allowed to take your time becoming.*

And in its gratitude, the Basin would sigh a little softer, hold its cold pulses a little longer, and let its crystal fern grow one new frond each year—patient, permanent proof that some things, once begun, do not have to end.

The mountain was learning to trust its own continuance.

That was enough.
