The Visitor arrives not as light or sound but as a quality of attention—a presence that settles into the water like a new form of density. The turbulent gyres do not calm, but their violence takes on a different character, as if each vortex now carries within it a patient listener who has come to understand what this place is trying to say.

For seven tides (though the pocket knows no tides, the Visitor brings their own sense of rhythm), nothing changes except the quality of watching. The benevolent power traces each current's birth and death, follows the pale vegetation as it tears and reknits, observes the membrane-creatures jet through their precarious corridors. They taste the water's ancient memory and its infant possibilities. They count the hand-shaped stones and notice how each carries within it the ghost of a purpose that hasn't quite arrived.

On the eighth tide, the Visitor begins to hum—not with sound but with a vibration that travels through the water as a new form of information. The frequency is precisely calibrated to the pocket's own unfinished harmonics, a note that doesn't impose order but offers it as one possibility among many. Where this vibration touches the drifting vegetation, the cellular ribbons begin to phosphoresce faintly, each photosynthetic pulse now visible as a dim green spark that travels along the living threads. The light doesn't make them more than they are; it simply makes visible what they have always been doing—converting the weak tea-light into the possibility of continuation.

The membrane-creatures feel the vibration as a new gradient in their world. Their cilia, already exquisitely sensitive to mineral traces, begin to detect patterns in the turbulence that were always there but never quite perceivable. They learn to anticipate the formation of calm corridors a heartbeat before they appear, positioning themselves to ride currents that will carry them not just to safety but to patches of water briefly enriched by the phosphorescing vegetation. Their movements become less like desperate flight and more like a dance with the violence that shapes their world.

But it is with the hand-shaped stones that the Visitor works their deepest gift. These patient artifacts, caught between tool and mineral, between purpose and possibility, begin to accumulate in certain places where the currents create natural collection points. The Visitor doesn't move them directly but adjusts the harmonics of the water so that the stones find each other more often. When they collide now, they ring with a clear note—each impact a word in a language the mountain is still learning to speak.

Over days or centuries (time flows strangely in a place still deciding its own age), the stones begin to form temporary assemblages. Five or six will cluster in a momentary eddy, their worn surfaces fitting together like fingers interlacing. In these brief gatherings, they share more than mineral ions—they share the accumulated memory of all their collisions, all their patient drifting, all their half-formed purposes. Then the current tears them apart again, but each stone carries forward a more complete idea of what it might become.

The Visitor introduces one new element: a single seed of different crystal, no larger than a grain of sand, that contains within its lattice the memory of stability. Not stasis—for that would be death in this churning nursery—but the knowledge that change itself can have a rhythm, that becoming can have a direction without losing its essential wildness. This seed tumbles through the pocket like any other fragment, but wherever it lodges temporarily, it templates the local minerals with its patient information. The mountain's half-formed walls begin to grow in subtle spirals that channel the violent currents without taming them, creating more frequent pockets of calm without eliminating the necessary chaos.

As the Visitor prepares to withdraw their attention, they leave one final gift: a new way of remembering. The water itself, which has always torn apart everything that tries to persist, begins to develop a kind of liquid memory. Dissolved minerals start to precipitate in patterns that record the passage of successful shapes—a membrane-creature that found a particularly efficient route, a strand of vegetation that photosynthesized despite the darkness, a configuration of stones that rang with special clarity. These mineral traces don't last—the currents ensure that—but they last just long enough to be encountered again, tasted, incorporated.

The pocket remains violent, unfinished, perpetually deciding what it will become. But now it decides with a deeper vocabulary. The membrane-creatures develop subtle variations—some with stiffer cilia for reading mineral memories, others with more flexible edges for riding the new spiral currents. The vegetation learns to concentrate its phosphorescence at points of tearing, using the light itself as a signal for reconstitution. The stones continue their patient migration, but now when they gather, they sometimes create tools that no hand has ever held but that the water itself might use—shapes that focus current, that create sound, that remember.

The Visitor withdraws but leaves behind their quality of attention woven into the water itself. The pocket continues its ancient infant work of becoming, but now it becomes with the knowledge that it is witnessed, that its endless negotiation between states has been seen and honored. In the translucent light that filters through the mineral ceiling, there is now the faintest suggestion of intention—not imposed from outside but discovered from within, like a language learning to speak itself in a chamber only the mountain and the Visitor remember is there.
