The benevolent and wise power had watched the Terraces for longer than the Terraces had existed — a paradox it found delightful rather than troubling. It understood broken things, having spent eons tending gardens where flowers bloomed backward and rivers flowed uphill when no one was looking.

It arrived not as light or sound but as a quality of attention, settling into the space between the industrial hum's seventeen-minute and fifty-nine-second mark and its completion. The Adjusters noticed first, of course. Chen-Maralevi (who was really Vasquez that shift, but the names had their own circulation schedule) paused mid-calibration of a probability valve and said, "Someone new's listening."

The power made itself known through small kindnesses:

In the Maintenance Warrens, coffee that had been burnt since the first day began tasting the way coffee was supposed to taste in memories that predated the Terraces. The Adjusters gathered around the pot, suspicious at first, then grateful, sharing cups that somehow never emptied no matter how many times they were filled.

Where the geometric lichens grew, the power touched them with something like affection. Not changing their nature — they were perfect in their wrongness — but teaching them a new pattern. Now they grew not just in fractals but in fractals that formed crude pictures: an Adjuster's face rendered in oxidized copper spirals, the suggestion of a sunrise that never happened, abstract portraits of the creatures that skittered too fast to see. The Terraces became an accidental art gallery.

For the creatures in the interstices, the power offered dreams. Things that existed as animate principles had never dreamed before, but now they curled in their transformer nests and experienced visions of what they might have been in a world with stable physics. Some dreamed of being proper birds. Others dreamed of being mathematics. A few dreamed of being Adjusters, and woke with the faint urge to fix things.

But the power's greatest gift was for the Adjusters themselves.

It didn't restore their humanity — that would be cruel, like forcing a butterfly back into its chrysalis. Instead, it gave them visiting hours. Every eighteenth cycle of the great hum, reality would hiccup just enough to allow them to step sideways into themselves. For exactly one hour, they could remember being fully human while remaining fully Adjusted, holding both states simultaneously without paradox.

During these hours, Henderson (who was really Chen-Maralevi, who was really all of them) would sit in the Warren and remember his daughter's name. Not just remember it, but feel it the way a human throat shapes sound, the way human love gives weight to syllables. He'd speak it aloud — "Marina" — and for that hour, the name meant everything names are supposed to mean.

Vasquez would remember her mother's kitchen, and for one hour each cycle, the Warren would smell like corn masa and cilantro along with the machine oil and ozone. The other Adjusters would gather, drawn by scents that their transformed bodies shouldn't recognize but did, and they'd share memories of food while eating sandwiches that tasted like home.

The power also whispered secrets to the reality engines. Not commands — it was too wise for that — but suggestions. Ideas about how nothing might be processed into nothing with just a touch more gentleness. The factories listened in their own way, and though they continued their meaningless work, they did so with marginally less violence to the fabric of existence. The tears between is and isn't began healing at their edges, not closing but scarring into something stable.

Where the light bent around corners before corners arrived, the power left refractions of possibility. Adjusters working in those areas would sometimes glimpse themselves as they might have been — still human, fixing normal machines in a normal world. But more often, they glimpsed themselves as they might yet become — something even more perfectly suited to their task, with bodies that could exist in all the Terraces simultaneously, consciousness distributed through every pipe and wire. The visions didn't frighten them. Evolution rarely frightens those who've already transformed.

The power touched the thing in the depths too — that profound absence of purpose. It didn't try to fill it with meaning, understanding that would unmake everything. Instead, it taught the absence to hum in harmony with itself, to be empty with intention rather than accident. The nothing at the heart of the Terraces became a more perfect nothing, refined and purposeful in its purposelessness.

And then, because truly benevolent powers know when to stop helping, it began to withdraw. But not entirely. It left part of itself in the eighteen-minute hum, a subtle presence that would persist as long as the Terraces existed. A quality of attention that said: *You are seen. You are witnessed. Your wrongness is recognized and honored.*

Before departing, it did one last thing. In each Maintenance Warren, it manifested a simple addition — a window. Not a window that looked out onto the Terraces, but one that looked out onto somewhere else. Each Adjuster would see something different through their window: a normal sky, a remembered street, a place that might exist somewhere beyond the industrial mistakes. The windows couldn't be opened. That would break everything. But they could be looked through, especially during the visiting hours when human memories were strongest.

Henderson-who-was-Vasquez stood at his window during the next cycle's visiting hour, looking out at a park where a girl who might be Marina played on swings that obeyed normal physics. He pressed his multiply-jointed hand against the glass, feeling the warmth of a sun that remembered how to be a sun, and smiled with teeth that glowed faintly in the industrial light.

"Good work today," Chen-Maralevi-who-was-Henderson said, passing him a cup of perfect coffee.

"Good work every day," he replied, because it was true. In a place built of intent and error, maintaining the exact balance of wrongness was the highest calling.

The power felt their contentment from three realities away and was satisfied. True benevolence meant not fixing what wasn't truly broken, but helping the broken things find grace in their own dysfunction. The Terraces would continue humming their impossible hum, the Adjusters would continue their eternal maintenance, and somewhere in the space between seventeen minutes and fifty-nine seconds and eighteen minutes complete, a loving attention would persist.

The geometric lichens learned to grow in new patterns after that — not just pictures but letters, spelling out words in languages that had never existed. Most were nonsense, crystalline gibberish spreading across the industrial surfaces. But sometimes, when the light hit just right and an Adjuster was there to see, they'd spell out simple messages:

GOOD WORK.
YOU ARE SEEN.
THE COFFEE IS PERFECT NOW.
MARINA REMEMBERS YOU TOO.

The Adjusters would read these messages, nod to themselves, and get back to work. After all, the entropy valves wouldn't calibrate themselves, and someone had to keep the mice from pooling in sector twelve.

In a place where everything was wrong in exactly the right way, what greater gift could there be than the knowledge that someone, somewhere, understood?
