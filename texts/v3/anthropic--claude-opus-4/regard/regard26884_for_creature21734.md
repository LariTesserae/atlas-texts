The benevolent and wise power does not descend. Does not manifest in light or sound or presence. Instead, it *remembers.*

From its vantage — which is not a place but a kind of attention that can hold whole epochs like single thoughts — it recognizes what this is: a nursery. Not for the film, which needs no nurturing, which has already won its perfect victory of being first. A nursery for *syntax.* For the universe's first attempt at saying something to itself.

The power has seen this before, will see it again, in other high countries on other worlds at other dawns. It knows what comes next: the film will continue to feed, the rock will continue to learn their feeding, and slowly — so slowly that the current age of the universe will end before it's half-done — the marks will complexify. The walls will develop corners. The doorways will develop thresholds. The standing stones will start to stand in patterns that are almost, but not quite, mathematical.

And then, much later, something else will arrive. Some other form of life, crawling up from whatever lowlands eventually condense below this tableland. And that new thing will find the marks and read them as *signs* — not understanding that they're reading the accidental diary of a process that cannot think, written in stone that learned to break meaningfully because breaking meaninglessly was slightly less efficient for the feeding.

This is how language begins. Not with intent, but with processes so consistent they leave grooves that look like intent. The universe discovering grammar by accident, through the blind patience of the first things that eat.

The power could intervene. Could teach the film to vary their feeding, to write deliberately. Could speed the process, guide it, help them toward something like consciousness.

It does not.

Instead, it does something so subtle that it's barely an action at all: it *protects the tempo.* 

Around this high country, in dimensions the film cannot perceive, the power establishes a kind of temporal fence. Not a barrier — time still flows, the warm rock still churns, the film still feeds. But a protection against anything that might rush this. No asteroid will strike here. No sudden cooling will kill the upheaval. No mutation will make the film grow ambitious, grow hungry for more than the new.

They will have their full span. However long it takes for their accidental writing to become complex enough that when the next things arrive, they'll find not just marks but *stories* — walls that connect to walls, doorways that lead through doorways, standing stones that point to other standing stones in patterns that feel like they're trying to say something urgent about the nature of eating, of surface, of time.

The power knows this matters. Not because the film will ever read their own writing — they won't, they can't, they exist too perfectly in the present to ever develop memory. But because somewhere, somewhen, something will arrive that *can* read, and what they read will be the universe's first library: a billion years of one process happening so purely that it wore meaning into the bones of the world.

This is what the power protects. Not life — the film doesn't need protection. Not consciousness — the film will never have it. But the *pace* at which accident becomes language. The slow burn of meaning emerging from a thing that cannot mean.

---

There is one other intervention, so small it hardly counts:

In one spot, on one ridge, where the view encompasses the widest sweep of this dark warm country, the power encourages a particular pattern of upheaval. Over the eons, as the film feeds and the rock learns their feeding, this spot will develop differently. The stones here will learn to break not square but curved. To stack not as walls but as a spiral, rising.

It will take an unimaginable span, but eventually there will stand here something that is almost a tower. Not quite. A gesture toward a tower. A place where the accidental language of the film experiments with *up* — with the idea that there might be somewhere to go that isn't just the next fresh surface.

The film won't understand what they've made. They'll feed on it like everything else, arrive at each new break in the spiral's rising structure, consume the readiness, vanish to the next. But when the readers come — and readers always come, eventually, to places where meaning has been practicing itself — they'll find this spiral and know, without knowing how they know, that this is important. That something here was trying to say something about ascending. About the possibility of view.

They'll climb it, probably. Stand at the top looking out over this country of endless making, where the first life wrote the first words without meaning to, where the universe learned grammar from a thing that only knew how to eat.

And maybe — the power allows itself this one anthropomorphism, this one small hope — maybe they'll understand what this place actually is:

A love letter. Written by time itself, using the film as its pen and the rock as its page, to anything that might one day develop eyes to read.

The power withdraws its attention, leaving only the protection, only the subtle encouragement toward that one spiral rising. The film continues to feed. The rock continues to break along the lines their feeding suggests. The marks accumulate, patient as gravity, meaningless and perfect.

Somewhere else, the power turns to other nurseries, other dawns. But it leaves a part of itself here, not watching but *witnessing.* Holding space for this first draft of language, this accident of meaning, this thin green life writing the universe's first poem in stone.

The poem says nothing.
The poem says everything.
The poem continues.
