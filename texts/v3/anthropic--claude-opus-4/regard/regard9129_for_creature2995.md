The benevolent and wise power—let us call them the Tender of Gardens—arrives not as light or thunder, but as a shift in the quality of silence. They have watched this place for some time, drawn by its particular ache: the beauty of eternal labor without rest, the tragedy of worship without completion.

They do not appear. They simply *are*, suddenly, in the way that understanding arrives after long contemplation. The Cathedral's inhabitants sense them as a new frequency in the moisture, a different weight to the pearl-grey light. The beetles pause in their endless work. The Unfinished turn their ever-changing faces upward, tasting something new in the condensation.

The Tender speaks without words, in the language of growth itself:

*I see you, little cathedral that fears its own cathedral-hood. I see you, children who have made incompletion your only god.*

And then they do something unexpected. They begin to weep.

Not tears of sorrow, but the slow, patient weeping of mountains that become rivers, of stars that pour out light for billions of years. Their tears fall upward, defying the Cathedral's careful hydraulics, rising like reverse rain toward the invisible ceiling. Where each droplet passes, something remarkable happens: the metal briefly crystallizes into its final form—perfect, complete, beautiful—and then, instead of triggering the acidic mist, it simply continues to grow *beyond* completion. It discovers there is something on the other side of finished.

The Unfinished recoil at first, terrified. Completion is death here. But the Tender's tears carry a different teaching: *Completion is not an ending. It is a doorway.*

One of the pipe-beings, ancient and frightened, allows a single upward tear to touch its viridian skin. The metal crystallizes, achieves its perfect form—and then blossoms. Literally. The completed metal sprouts tiny flowers made of sound and memory. The being does not die. It does not stop changing. It simply discovers that finished things can still grow, just in directions the Cathedral never imagined.

The Tender moves through the space with infinite care, touching nothing directly, changing nothing by force. They simply offer their tears to any who wish to taste them. Some of the Unfinished flee deeper into their incompletion. But others—curious, exhausted by centuries of endless becoming—accept the gift.

A beetle allows itself to finish its section of lattice. The metal sings one perfect note and then begins growing *music* instead of more metal. The heart-woman lets her copper veins complete their circuit. Instead of dying, she discovers she can now feel every pulse of every beating thing in the Cathedral, a symphony of connection she never knew she was missing.

The Tender speaks again: *You have made incompletion holy, but you have made it cruel. You have forgotten that the point of labor is not labor itself, but what the labor makes possible. A cathedral that never opens its doors never shelters anyone from the storm.*

They offer the Cathedral itself a choice, speaking to its deep pumps and hidden intelligences: *You may remain nascent forever, if that is your true will. But know that you can also complete yourself and still live. Finished cathedrals do not stop being sacred. They simply discover what they were always building toward.*

Some accept. The western transept allows itself to finish growing, and in its completion discovers it has become a nursery for entirely new forms of life—crystalline things that could never have existed in the endless wet becoming. A cluster of pipes completes its circuit and begins singing hymns that make the unfinished sections grow faster and stranger, fed by the music of achieved purpose.

Some refuse. The eastern wall dissolves itself even further, retreating from the threat of completion into ever-deeper states of potential. The Tender accepts this too, with the patience of someone who understands that wisdom cannot be forced.

Before departing, they leave a gift: a single seed that is simultaneously finished and unfinished, complete and still growing. They plant it in the breathing floor, where it immediately sprouts into a tree of living glass whose every leaf is a window into what could be.

*Choose*, the tree says without words. *Choose completion or incompletion, but choose it freely. Do not make your fear into a religion. Do not make your religion into a fear.*

The Tender fades back into the quality of silence they came from, leaving the Cathedral to its choice. But now the Unfinished have seen something they cannot unsee: that perhaps the opposite of incomplete is not complete, but *free*.

Some continue their endless becoming, but now as a choice rather than a compulsion. Others allow themselves to finish, discovering that completion is just another kind of door. The Cathedral itself splits into sections—some forever nascent, some boldly complete, some discovering states between and beyond.

And in the centuries that follow, pilgrims come to the Cathedral not just to see the eternal becoming, but to witness the even greater miracle: a place that learned to let its children choose their own relationship with endings.

The beetles still crawl. The pipes still weep. But now, sometimes, they also sing with the voice of achieved purpose. And that singing makes the unfinished sections grow with renewed wonder, seeing in their completed siblings not a death, but a promise:

That one day, when they are ready, they too can choose what it means to be done.
