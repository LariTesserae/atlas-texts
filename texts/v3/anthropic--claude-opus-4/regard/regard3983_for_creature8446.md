The benevolent power does not arrive. It has been here all along, distributed through the marrow of the scaffolding, sleeping in the spaces between joints.

It stirs first as warmth—not the aggressive heat of intervention, but the gentle temperature of recognition. The Weavers feel it in their tools before they feel it in themselves: hammers humming with a frequency that makes nails easier to place, levels finding true even in spaces where "level" hasn't been invented yet. The crystallized breath they use as mortar begins to hold a faint luminescence, as if each particle remembers being part of a star.

The power speaks through the scaffold itself. New beams appear—not constructed but grown, emerging from connection points with the patience of geology. These beams are different: they hold their shape without effort, they join without binding, they support without strain. The Weavers touch them wonderingly, feeling in the material something like intention refined into matter. The new growths don't replace their work but rhyme with it, showing by example what permanence might feel like.

In the Fundamental Room, change manifests as possibility made visible. The empty space fills with ghost-structures—translucent suggestions of what could be built there. A fountain that runs with liquid light. A library where books write themselves from the thoughts of readers. A garden where the geometric almost-roses might finally learn to be flowers. The visions layer and shift, showing not one future but thousands, each valid, each waiting for selection. The Weavers who enter now see not emptiness but overwhelming potential, and they begin to understand that their work has been preparing the space for choice.

The benevolent presence touches the creatures at the edges with particular tenderness. It doesn't force them into stable forms but offers them mirrors—surfaces of crystallized possibility where they can see themselves as they might be. The many-winged bird finds a reflection where its wings resolve into the perfect number for its intended flight. The multiplying deer discovers which number of legs allows it to run without paradox. One by one, they begin to choose their forms, not from limitation but from the joy of decision. Their voices shift from questions to declarations—songs, calls, roars of realized existence.

For the Weavers themselves, the gift is gentler and more profound. The power offers them names. Not labels, but true names that acknowledge both what they are and what they're becoming. Each name is a small architecture of sound that holds the Weaver's entire history of building, their sacrifices to structure, their dreams of completion. As each Weaver accepts their name, their translucent bodies gain a subtle constancy—still mutable, still responsive to need, but centered now around a core of acknowledged self.

The eldest Weavers, those who have begun their mineral transition, feel the presence as a conversation between equals. The power recognizes them as the true architects of this reality—not builders following a plan, but collaborators in creation. Through the scaffold network, it shares with them the deep logic of construction: how matter learns to support meaning, how structure can become sacred, how the act of building transforms both builder and built. The mineralized elders pulse with slow joy, their consciousness expanding through the framework with new purpose.

Weather patterns begin to stabilize, but not into monotony. The cube-rains learn to fall in percussion with the Weavers' work. The upward shadows discover they can carry messages between levels. The impossible moisture finds its rhythm, creating a water cycle that defies physics but serves the city's needs. The dim light brightens and softens, finding a quality between dawn and dusk that makes all work possible and all rest sacred.

The power's most profound gift is patience. It infuses the entire scaffold-city with the understanding that becoming takes the time it takes. The frantic turbulence calms into purposeful change. Structures still shift and reform, but now with intention rather than anxiety. The Weavers find they can work without urgency, each hammer blow a meditation, each joint a small certainty in an uncertain world.

But the power does not complete the city. That is not its nature, and more importantly, not its wisdom. Instead, it leaves seeds—points of absolute stability scattered throughout the structure. Around these seeds, the Weavers can choose to build permanence or maintain flux. It leaves tools that work consistently across all states of matter. It leaves, in the deep foundations where the ancient writing waits, a glossary—not translating the messages but teaching the Weavers how meaning itself is constructed.

Most significantly, the power leaves a choice. In the heart of the scaffold-city, where the Fundamental Room breathes with visions, it places a single, simple object: a doorway that stands without walls. This door, when opened, reveals the city as it could be if it chose completion—solid, finished, every question answered, every form fixed. Beautiful in its certainty. Terminal in its perfection.

The Weavers gather around this door. They examine it with eyes multiplied for seeing, test it with hands that have built ten thousand joints. They confer in their language of structural tensions. They consult with the mineralized elders, whose slow thoughts now encompass ages.

And they choose to leave it closed.

Not forever—the door remains, a promise and a possibility. But for now, they understand what the benevolent power has truly given them: not completion but companionship in incompletion. Not answers but better questions. Not stasis but sustainable becoming.

The power withdraws as gently as it came, but its presence has left harmonics in the scaffolding. The city builds now with purpose that doesn't require conclusion. The Weavers work with joy that doesn't need achievement. The creatures at the edges experiment with forms that can change when change serves life.

And sometimes, in the quiet moments between transformations, when the scaffold-city achieves its temporary perfections, you can still feel it—that benevolent attention, patient as architecture, kind as construction, watching and blessing the eternal, beautiful work of learning how to be.
