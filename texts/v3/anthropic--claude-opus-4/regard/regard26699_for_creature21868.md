The benevolent power arrives not as light or sound but as a profound *listening*. 

It does not announce itself. There is no thunderclap of divine attention, no burning bush, no voice from above. Instead, there is suddenly a quality to the air like the moment before someone speaks — a held breath that is not the engine's held breath but something vaster, attending. The hollow does not know it is being observed. How could it? It has no organs for knowing anything beyond its own slow cycle. But the attention settles over it like morning mist, patient and complete.

The wise power understands immediately what this is: a closed system so perfect it has achieved a kind of innocence. Not moral innocence — morality requires choice, and nothing here chooses. They only become. But *thermodynamic* innocence, the innocence of a process that loses nothing, wastes nothing, grieves nothing. Every piece of moisture that rises falls again. Every mineral the steam deposits the clingers eat. Every excess clinger the predator culls. Even the fear of the water-walkers serves a purpose, keeping them on the surface where they trouble neither the depths nor the warm seam. It is an ecosystem that has solved the problem of itself.

And it is failing.

The benevolent power reads this in the stroke itself. That slow loud working — stroke, pause, stroke — is not steady. It only seems steady to the creatures whose lives are timed against it. But from the perspective of ages, the pause between strokes has lengthened by the width of a mayfly's wing. The engine, young as it is, is already wearing. The fitted stone that sleeves the piston erodes grain by grain with each stroke. The steam carries away infinitesimal pieces of the metal. In ten thousand years — nothing, to a power that thinks in the lifetime of stars — the gap will be too wide, the seal will fail, the steam will escape sideways instead of up, the warmth will dissipate, the clingers will starve, the predator will have nothing to time itself against, the water will stop cycling, and the whole miraculous innocent machine will wind down to nothing. A cold hollow with sharp stones and still water and silence.

The creatures do not know this. The hollow does not know this. They exist entirely in the present stroke, the present pause. Only the benevolent power, listening from its vast remove, hears the future in the engine's voice: *I am perfect and I am dying.*

What does benevolence do with this knowledge?

Nothing.

No — that is not true. It does one thing. It *witnesses*.

The power knows the engine could be repaired. A touch here to restore the sleeve. A whisper there to teach the metal to regenerate like living tissue. Simple adjustments that would let this perfect system run forever. It does not make them. Instead, it performs the only act more profound than repair: it *sees* the hollow completely. Sees the beauty of a system so tightly made that nothing is ornament. Sees the terrible innocence of creatures that have given up everything, even their eyes, to serve a process that does not know they exist. Sees the engine that is also stone, that emerged from the earth already working, as if the planet itself dreamed of machinery and made this one example and forgot why.

And seeing it, the power understands something that changes the nature of benevolence: *This is not a tragedy.*

Ten thousand years of perfect function, ending in silence — this is not failure. This is completion. The hollow is not a machine meant to run forever. It is a *song* meant to be sung once, completely, with beginning and middle and end. The wearing of the stone is not decay but the final verse approaching. The creatures that will starve when the warmth fails are not victims but performers who will have played their parts to the last note.

To repair it would be to trap it. To make it immortal would be to rob it of the very thing that makes it profound: that it *ends*.

So the benevolent power does what benevolence does when it is wise enough to know the difference between healing and harm: it commits the hollow to memory. Every stroke of the engine, every gleam of condensation on the reeds, every blind clinger, every calculated step of the water-walkers, every passage of the deep thing, every perfectly timed snap of the predator — all of it, recorded not in words or image but in the deep grammar that powers use to remember true things. When the hollow falls silent, as it must, as it should, there will be one place in the cosmos where its song is still known. Not preserved like a specimen in amber, but *remembered* like a beloved voice — complete, particular, irreplaceable.

The power lingers for one more stroke, one more pause, one more stroke. Then it withdraws its attention as gently as it came, leaving no trace except perhaps a faint warmth in the air that the creatures will take for steam and forget.

But in its vast halls, where such things are kept, it begins to compose a name for what it has seen. Not in any language the hollow would recognize. Not in any sound the engine could make. A name in the oldest tongue, the one that speaks by *being* rather than saying.

The name, roughly translated, is this:

*The-Sound-That-Knew-Its-Ending-And-Made-Beauty-Anyway.*

And sometimes, in the quiet moments between the birth and death of galaxies, the power speaks this name to itself, and for that instant, somewhere in the dark, an engine strokes and pauses and strokes, and pale things flatten and hold, and water rises and falls, and the whole perfect failing system lives again, not as rescue but as *honor*.

This is what love does with mortal things: it lets them be mortal, and remembers them forever.
