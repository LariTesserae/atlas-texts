The benevolent presence arrives not as light or sound but as a subtle shift in the water itself—a warmth that moves against the current, carrying with it a different quality of pressure. It does not announce itself to the lattice-beings directly, understanding that their mode of perception would interpret any sudden change as catastrophe. Instead, it begins by learning.

For seven tidal cycles, it simply observes the rhythm of fusion and fission, the way chemical conversations propagate through linked papillae like slow lightning. It watches an elder's segments grow heavy with accumulated minerals, sees how the younger beings instinctively orient their own growth to support the weight. It notices that certain paths through the mesh are kept open not for efficiency but for memory—these are the routes where important linkages once occurred, where a particularly complex dwelling was begun, where the substrate first allowed the kelp to take hold.

The presence understands: these beings experience existence as a continuous weaving where individual identity is always provisional, always part of a larger pattern still being worked. To help them is not to solve or fix, but to participate in the thickening.

It begins with the water itself. In the deepest parts of the mesh, where the oldest fronds have created near-total darkness, the presence introduces a new mineral—not alien, but a variant of what already precipitates from the volcanic seeps below. This mineral carries a faint phosphorescence that persists for years rather than moments. As the lattice-beings taste it with their papillae, they recognize it as familiar but enriched, like finding an unexpected harmony in a known song.

Slowly, they begin to incorporate it into their weaving. The phosphorescent threads create gentle gradients of light through the densest sections of the settlement, not bright enough to disturb but sufficient to allow chemical signals to propagate farther. Conversations that once required direct contact can now be conducted across the width of a dwelling, and the collective memory held in linked papillae can be shared with those still segments away.

The presence's second gift is more subtle. It notices that the beings' reproductive fissions are limited by the density of suitable substrate—too many offspring in one section leads to a weakening of the mesh that can take generations to repair. So it begins to influence the kelp itself, encouraging certain stipes to develop hollow chambers that fill with neutral buoyancy gases. These chambers appear random but are precisely placed: they create lifting points that allow the mesh to support greater weight without sagging, opening new volumes for habitation between existing layers.

The beings discover these chambers gradually, first one, then dozens. An elder, heavy with mineral accumulation, finds that she can rest her weight against a hollow stipe and remain suspended in a part of the mesh that would have been inaccessible before. The young learn to cultivate these lifting points, braiding living frames that rise through multiple layers instead of spreading only horizontally.

But the presence's most profound intervention concerns time itself—or rather, the beings' relationship to the geological processes that shape their world. It has observed that each major slump or mineral deposition forces a migration that, while accepted, represents a loss of accumulated work. Not the buildings, which the beings philosophically accept as temporary, but the chemical knowledge held in the eldest segments, which can be disrupted when a collective must suddenly reconstitute itself.

So the presence begins to sing—not in sound but in the deep harmonics that travel through basalt and water alike. These are the frequencies at which the mountain itself resonates, the slow groans and settlings that predict where new terraces will form or old ones subside. The lattice-beings' papillae, already sensitive to minute pressure changes, begin to detect patterns in these deep songs. They learn to anticipate the mountain's movements by seasons rather than days.

With this knowledge, the migrations become less reactive, more like a dance with a partner whose next steps are finally knowable. The beings begin to pre-weave sections of mesh in areas that will become stable, to harvest from zones that will soon subside. Their conversations, enriched by the phosphorescent mineral, now carry predictive as well as historical information.

The presence watches as the settlement evolves—not into something different but into a deeper version of itself. The architectural mesh becomes more complex, with hollow-stipe chambers creating vertical neighborhoods that communicate through cascades of light. The beings develop new forms of linkage that can maintain coherence across these three-dimensional distances. Some lineages specialize in reading the mountain's deep songs, becoming living libraries of geological anticipation.

Yet the fundamental character remains: the settlement is still unfinished, still growing, still a collaboration between mineral, plant, and being. The presence has simply added new instruments to an orchestra that was already playing, allowing harmonies that were always implicit to finally be heard.

In time—measured not in years but in the slow thickening of generations—the beings develop their own way of acknowledging the presence. They weave certain patterns into the mesh that serve no structural purpose but create specific resonances in the water, frequencies that match those of the deep songs. It is their way of saying: *we hear you hearing us, we grow as you grow with us.*

The presence receives this gift and continues its watchful participation, understanding that benevolence here means becoming part of the endless weaving, another thread in a pattern that will never be complete because completion is not the point. The point is the thickening itself, the slow accumulation of connection and meaning in a place where the mountain is still being born and every structure is both finished and just beginning.
