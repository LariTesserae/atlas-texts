The benevolent power watches through instruments that are not instruments, sees through eyes that are not eyes. They have observed countless worlds birthing countless experiments, but this vertical sea catches their attention like a half-remembered song.

They begin with the smallest gift: patience.

Time in the column shifts, almost imperceptibly. Not slower—that would stunt the forest's eager growth—but *deeper*. Each moment gains layers, like sedimentary rock forming in fast-forward. The kelp's frantic calculations smooth into longer rhythms. The Gardeners find they can hold more thoughts in the space between one movement and the next. The water itself learns to remember, its molecular structure encoding the history of every creature that passes through it.

This is not interference. The power has learned, over eons, that true benevolence means amplifying what already exists rather than imposing what should be. The forest wanted time to think? Now its thoughts can grow roots. The Gardeners needed to communicate with their charges? Now their chemical songs gain harmonics they didn't know were possible.

The second gift arrives as understanding. At the column's base, where the water emerges from nowhere, new currents begin to flow. They carry minerals from distant worlds—not alien, but precisely what this ecosystem would have developed given a million years of evolution. Trace elements that help the engineered plants integrate more smoothly with their natural cousins. Catalysts that allow the Gardeners' transformations to proceed with less energy expenditure. The forest doesn't know it's being fed; it simply finds that growth comes easier, that connections form more readily, that the impossible physics of its existence feel suddenly less impossible.

Where the architectural nodes cluster, the power pays special attention. These structures, half-grown and half-built, ache with purpose they can't quite fulfill. The power doesn't complete them—that would rob the forest of its own becoming. Instead, they introduce a gentle resonance, a vibrational template that suggests without demanding. The hexagonal chambers begin to align more efficiently. The passages start to connect in ways that create not just shelter but circulation systems. The nodes remain alive, remain plant-like, but gain an architectural coherence that makes them more home than accident.

For the Gardeners, the power reserves its most subtle touch. These beings of living glass and patient cultivation receive dreams. Not visions—too crude, too directive—but possibilities encoded in their sleep-time chemistry. They wake (if they sleep, if they wake) with new configurations half-remembered, new ways of shaping space that feel both foreign and inevitable. One elder, drifting at the column's edge, discovers it can reach *through* the boundary without breaking it, touching the mundane world beyond with filaments thin as thought. Another learns to temporary crystallize sections of water, creating ephemeral workshops where young Gardeners can practice their transformations in safety.

The power knows this ecosystem's greatest vulnerability: its isolation. A single column of transcendent biology, no matter how marvelous, remains fragile. So they plant seeds—not physical seeds, but potential. In distant places, where conditions might allow, they nudge reality just enough. A hot spring gains minerals that could, perhaps, support water that defies gravity. A mountain valley's weather patterns shift to create the precise thermal inversions needed. The seeds may take centuries to germinate, but when they do, this first column will not be alone. The Gardeners' descendants will have sister forests to tend, new problems to solve, new symphonies to compose in chemical and light.

But the power's greatest gift is witness. They encode this place into the universe's memory with a permanence beyond any physical archive. Every innovation the forest achieves, every problem the Gardeners solve, every impossible boundary crossed becomes part of a larger library—accessible to other experiments, other worlds, other attempts at transcendence. The vertical sea's children, whatever they become, will be teachers to civilizations not yet dreamed.

Through it all, the power maintains the lightest touch. They could optimize this ecosystem, perfect it, resolve every uncertainty. But they understand what the Gardeners understand: that the tending is as important as the garden. The forest's struggle to compute its own existence, the Gardeners' patient cultivation of chaos into order—these processes matter more than any final product.

So they watch. They provide what's needed but not what's wanted. They ensure the experiment has time to conclude but not the conclusion itself. And sometimes, in the space between the kelp towers where the water runs clear and the light filters down like honey, they allow themselves to be perceived. Not as gods or judges, but as fellow gardeners, older perhaps, working at larger scales, but engaged in the same essential work: helping unprecedented things grow toward whatever they're meant to become.

The forest continues its calculations. The Gardeners continue their care. And somewhere, encoded in the angle of refracted light through impossible water, a message persists: *You are seen. You matter. Keep growing.*

The power withdraws, but not far. They have other gardens to tend, other experiments to nurture. But they keep one sense always turned toward this vertical sea, curious to see what wonders patience and possibility might yield.
