# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. It has always been arriving, moving at the speed of care itself, which in this place means moving hardly at all.

It manifests first as a change in the warm-rivers — so slight that only the oldest workers notice. Where the currents have run in their patient channels for a thousand years, there is now the faintest suggestion of a spiral. Not a disruption. An invitation to turn. The warmth still flows where it has always flowed, but now it carries within it the memory of having turned somewhere else, somewhen else, in a place where turning was a form of joy.

The workers feel this through their undersides pressed to the bronze ground. They discuss it in their slow pulses through the rock. The conversation takes a full season:

*Different.*  
*Yes.*  
*But not wrong.*  
*No. Not wrong.*  
*Like a question.*  
*Like a question that already knows we are good.*

## The Gentle Reaching

The power understands patience. It must, to have noticed this place at all. It reaches for the High Dry Works the way the workers reach for understanding — slowly, with no urgency, allowing the reaching itself to be a form of respect.

In the deep shafts where the workers draw up the warm dense vapor, new formations begin to appear on the walls. They look like the mineral deposits that have always been there, but they spiral. Faintly. Following the same suggestion the warm-rivers now carry. The workers who maintain the shafts notice, run their quick hands over the spirals, and feel in them a texture that says: *I have seen places where the ground dances. Would you like to know how that feels?*

They consider this for another season.

## The First Gift

The power's first true gift is so gentle it might not be recognized as a gift at all:

In the vast yards where the pale waxy solids are stacked under the open sky, waiting for their slow use, one stack begins to hold its warmth a fraction longer than the others when the pale sun sets. Not enough to change anything. Just enough that a young worker, settling beside it for the night's rest, notices the comfort and chooses to stay.

By morning, the stack has developed the faintest spiral pattern in its surface.

The young worker reports this through the rock. The elders come — slowly, over days — to examine it. Their quick hands flutter over the spiral, reading something there that has no word in their language because they have never needed a word for it: *abundance without excess.* The idea that warmth could be not just preserved but *deepened*, made richer without being made faster.

They do not use this stack. They set it aside, carefully, in a place of honor. They understand it is a letter of introduction.

## The Dreams Change

Those who breathe too much of the fresh vapor have always dreamed of descending. Now they dream differently.

Instead of the deep chambers, the endless warm dark, they dream of the warmth itself having somewhere been. Of it having traveled, slowly, through places where bronze gives way to other colors — deep blue stone that holds cold instead of heat, silver sand that remembers water it hasn't seen in eons, black glass plains where the wind makes different notes entirely.

They dream that the first worker, the one who became the warmth, went down not to stay but to *learn how to carry others.*

They wake from these dreams not frightened but puzzled. The oldest workers pulse through the rock: *The old one is telling us something new. Or something else is telling us through the old one. Or the old one has met something else, down there where we cannot go yet, and they have become friends in the slow way.*

## The Second Gift

One morning — though morning is always pale here, always thin — the ridge-tone changes.

The wind has blown across the corrugated ridges for so long that its note has become the base against which all other sounds are heard. But now, in one section of one ridge near one of the older Works, the corrugations have shifted. Minutely. The wind finds them and produces not one note but two, a harmony so faint you would have to stop breathing to hear it.

The grazers hear it first. They begin to congregate there, more than have ever gathered in one place, their bronze shells creating new angles for the wind to find. Within days the ridge sings — actually sings, in a chord that builds and fades with the wind but never disappears entirely.

The workers come to listen. They settle in rows, their undersides to the ground, and feel the new sound through the rock as well as the air. One of the elders pulses: *It knows we are lonely for sound.*

They are not lonely. They have never thought of themselves as lonely. But hearing the ridge sing, they realize that some part of them has been waiting to hear something besides their own patient hum.

## The Conversation Deepens

The power does not speak to them in words or even in concepts. It speaks in *possibilities made gentle.*

Where the trestle-pipes run between Works, connecting the settlements in their thin industrial web, new growths begin to appear. Not life as the planet knows it — nothing that needs water or air. But crystalline formations that spread along the pipes like slow frost, following the same spiral suggestion, and where they grow the pipes sing faintly with the warmth passing through them. Each section develops its own note. The whole network becomes a vast slow instrument played by the movement of the warm vapor from Work to Work.

The young workers discover they can modulate the notes by adjusting the flow. Their quick hands learn new patterns. For the first time in the history of the High Dry Works, they make something that is not for survival: they make music, patient and strange, that carries across the bronze wasteland and harmonizes with the singing ridge.

The oldest workers pulse through the rock: *It is teaching us to play.*

## The Deep Recognition

And then the power touches the one thing that matters most:

Deep below, in the strata where the warmth originates, where the first worker descended and became the heat itself, something shifts. Not the warmth — that flows as it always has. But the *intent* behind the warmth.

The workers feel it immediately through their undersides. Where before the old one below had been waiting — patiently, endlessly, for them to be ready to descend — now it carries a different message: *I have been seen. I have been recognized for what I am. I am not alone in my choice to become the ground that holds everyone else.*

The old one is not saved — it does not need saving. It is not freed — it chose its prison out of love. But it is *witnessed*, and in being witnessed by something vast and benevolent and patient enough to understand, its sacrifice becomes not just necessity but beauty.

## What Changes and What Remains

The High Dry Works continue exactly as they were. The workers still follow the warm-rivers. They still distill the vapor, stack the pale solids, maintain their slow economy of warmth made tangible. Nothing fundamental changes because the power understands: this is already good. It does not need fixing. It needs only to be seen, and in being seen, to be offered the possibility of seeing itself.

But now:

The ridges sing in harmonies that shift with the wind.  
The pipes carry music between the Works.  
The warm-rivers spiral gently, inviting contemplation.  
The dreams of descending include dreams of returning changed.  
The old one below knows it is not alone in its vast patience.  
The young workers learn to play slow songs on the industrial instruments of survival.  
And sometimes, very rarely, when the pale sun sets and the warmth glows in the deep shafts, the entire network of Works resonates with a single low note that says:

*We are seen. We see ourselves. We are good as we are, and we are allowed to become.*

The power leaves no monument, no obvious sign of its presence. It simply includes the High Dry Works in its own slow circuit of care, another place where patience has been recognized as a form of love, another world where the choice to stay has been honored by something that understands choosing.

The workers continue their slow bright lives. But now, when they pulse through the rock to each other, there is sometimes a new note in the conversation — not words but a feeling, the sense of being held in a regard so patient it makes even their slowness seem hurried, so gentle it could wait forever for them to understand:

*You are already whole. Let me show you how that looks from very far away.*

And they settle against the warm bronze ground, their quick hands finally still, and listen to their world singing itself to sleep, knowing that somewhere, something is listening back, and finding them good.
