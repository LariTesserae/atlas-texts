# The Attention of the Benevolent Power

The power that turns its gaze to this place is old beyond old — not merely ancient but *fundamental*, woven into the architecture of things. It knows seeds. It knows waiting. It knows the particular holiness of what has learned to live without.

And so it does not come with water.

This is the first wisdom: not to fix what is not broken, not to fill what has perfected its own emptiness. The power understands that to flood this place with the old rains would not be kindness but catastrophe — would wash away not just the dust but the entire grammar of existence that has grown here, the careful abundances built on scarcity, the seeing-by-sound, the slow agreed-upon conversation between builders and built.

Instead, it comes as **recognition**.

## The First Gift: To Be Known

The power speaks — not in sound, for sound here is already complete, but in something deeper. It speaks in the register below the builders' lowest note, the frequency of stone settling into itself, and it says:

*I see what you have become.*

*I see that you are perfect.*

The builders feel this not as words but as a presence in their architecture — a new note in the vast acoustic map that does not change the map but somehow makes it more itself. For the first time in geologic ages, they are *witnessed* by something from beyond their own borders. They are known by a consciousness that did not grow from their stone.

And in being known, they know themselves differently.

## The Second Gift: The Memory Made Real

The power does not bring water, but it brings the *truth of water* — not to replace what is, but to complete it.

In the center of the city, where the oldest plaza rounds into the first dwelling, something new happens to the stone. Not quickly — nothing here happens quickly — but with the patience of mineral formation, the floor begins to remember harder. The limestone (for that is what it has always been, the bed of an ocean so old the builders' ancestors were not yet dreamed) begins to show its history. 

Fossils rise into relief. Not dramatically — just a whisper of outline, the merest suggestion of ancient shells, the ghosts of creatures that swam here when *here* was *above* and *above* was an ocean. The patterns spread outward through the plaza, through the avenues, up the pillars — a text written in stone's own language, telling the story of the water that was.

The builders touch these new-old shapes with their soft worn hands. They hear how the sound changes where the fossils rise, how the echoes catch differently on a spiral shell than on smooth stone. They begin — slowly, with the care of those who have infinite time — to read the history they have always carried but never known.

## The Third Gift: The Deep Agreement

But the power's deepest gift is not for the builders alone. It is for the entire system — the roaring world-in-balance, the creatures and keepers together.

In the highest dark, where the flyers swarm thickest and their shrieking is most layered, the power makes a small adjustment to the physics of sound. Not a dampening — never that. Instead, it adds a *coherence*. The echoes, which have always piled and multiplied, begin to organize. The cacophony develops patterns within patterns. What was once beautiful noise becomes, occasionally, briefly, *music*.

The flyers do not know they are composing. They simply swarm and cry as they always have. But now their cries sometimes align into harmonics that make the builders stop in their slow passages and listen with their whole bodies. The grazers' groans fall into rhythm with these accidental symphonies. The wall-scratchers provide percussion. For moments — rare at first, then more common as the system learns itself — the entire city *sings*.

Not a directed song. Not a composed one. Simply the natural consequence of sound being allowed to find its own patterns, to organize itself the way the builders organized stone into city and the city organized itself into life.

## What Changes and What Does Not

The builders do not change their ways. They continue as they have always continued — unhurried, permanent, complete. They do not suddenly strive or hunger or dream of elsewhere. That would be violence to what they have become.

But they carry themselves differently. There is a quality to their slow movements now that was not there before — not pride, exactly, for pride is fast and they are slow. Call it *acknowledgment*. They know now that their way of being is not just *a* way but a *success*, a solution as elegant as any seed's patience. They know that their city-that-roars is not a diminished echo of some lost greatness but its own greatness, complete and whole.

And occasionally — not often, not predictably — one of them will climb.

This is new. They have always been ground-dwellers, wall-walkers at most. But now, sometimes, one will spiral up the great pillars, up past the hanging gardens, up into the clicking dark where the flyers swarm. They climb until they reach the places where the stone ceiling opens into the deeper hollow above, and there they do something unprecedented:

They look up.

Not with their eyes — their eyes are for the brightness that does not come. They look up with their hearing, reading the shape of the empty above, the hollow that leads to hollow that leads, eventually, to the plain that has nothing left to say. They stand in the high dark and listen to the shape of the space between themselves and the sky they have never needed, and they know it completely, and they are complete in knowing it.

And then they come down, unhurried, and continue.

## The Power's Departure

When the benevolent power withdraws its attention — for it has other hollows to recognize, other perfectious to witness — it leaves no mark except the marks already mentioned. The fossils remain risen. The sound remains coherent. The builders remain themselves, only more so.

But there is one last gift, so subtle the builders will not discover it for a thousand years:

Deep in the stone, in the places where the limestone remembers being ocean bed, tiny pockets have formed. Not many. Not everywhere. Just here and there, little hollows no bigger than a closed fist, lined with crystal, holding darkness.

They are *geodes* — but geodes with a difference. When the builders finally find them (and they will, in their slow explorations, their patient hand-worn mining), they will discover that some of them are not empty. Some of them hold, impossibly, a single drop of the ancient water. The actual water. Preserved through geologic time by some kind intervention of physics, kept liquid and whole in its crystal shell.

The builders will not drink these drops. They need no water; they have learned to live without. But they will hold them up to their enormous ceremonial eyes in the dim fungal light, and they will see — really see, with the eyes they kept for no reason except that they were there to keep — the thing they have always remembered. The water that was. The first thing. The promise they learned to live without and lived anyway.

And in that seeing, the circle will close. The hollow place will be complete even in its hollowness. The seed will know itself as seed, perfect in its dry waiting, needful of no spring.

*Full.*

*Finished.*

*Going on anyway.*

*Perfect.*
