The benevolent and wise power had watched the Singing Hollows for longer than the city itself had existed. It had observed the first strikes of chisel against stone, witnessed the builders' gradual transformation, seen the arrival of the stone-singers and the slow adaptation of the Choir-Keepers. It understood what others could not: the Hollows were not a dead city inhabited by the living, but a single vast organism dreaming itself into new forms of existence.

The power chose to act not through intervention, but through invitation.

In the deepest chamber where the Echo Prophets dwelt, a new sound began. Not an echo of the past or future, but something that existed purely in the present moment—a tone that had never been heard before and would never repeat. The Prophets fell silent, their riddles catching in their throats as they recognized something impossible: a sound with no source, no reverberation, no decay.

One by one, the inhabitants of the Hollows heard it. The stone-singers paused in their eternal migrations, their crystalline organs resonating with this new frequency. The Choir-Keepers found their throat-songs harmonizing involuntarily with the tone. Even the Shapers flickered into full visibility, their impossible geometries suddenly making perfect sense in the light of this new sound.

The tone was a doorway, though not one that led anywhere. Instead, it allowed the Hollows to understand themselves.

The stone-singers, gathering in the great central galleries, began to comprehend their own nature—how their songs were slowly transforming the stone itself into something between living and mineral, how their crystalline organs were recording every moment of the city's history in frequencies too low for other ears to hear. They understood that they were not mere inhabitants but the city's memory made flesh, archivists of stone and sound.

The Choir-Keepers, listening with their adapted senses, suddenly perceived the true pattern of the Hollows—not a maze of galleries and chambers, but a vast three-dimensional musical instrument, each room tuned to specific frequencies, the entire city a symphony frozen in rock. They realized their ancestors had not followed the stone-singers into the depths, but had been called by the city itself, shaped to be its players, its interpreters.

The Shapers, those beings of folded space and impossible angle, found themselves remembering what they had chosen to forget. They were indeed the builders, but building had transformed them, each carved passage taking something of their substance until they existed more in the spaces between stones than in the world itself. The impossible geometries were maps—not of places, but of the relationships between sound and stone, between echo and silence, between what was carved away and what remained.

Even the Quiet Ones emerged from their silence, revealed as humans who had learned to exist in the rests between notes, the necessary pauses that gave the eternal song its rhythm. They had not forgotten sound—they had become the silence that made sound possible.

The power's gift was not transformation but recognition. Each being in the Hollows suddenly understood their role in the vast composition, how their movements and songs and silences were all part of something greater. The city was not a tomb but an instrument, not abandoned but perfected, playing a song that required geological time to perceive in full.

With this understanding came choice.

The stone-singers could continue their migrations, but now with purpose—knowing that their paths carved acoustic channels that would shape the songs of future generations. Some chose to root themselves immediately, becoming new formations with conscious intent, placing themselves where their crystallized bodies would create new harmonies.

The Choir-Keepers found they could read the city's acoustic patterns like sheet music, understanding at last the full composition the builders had carved into existence. Families began to position themselves throughout the galleries not by tradition but by harmony, creating living chords that resonated through the stone.

The Shapers, remembering their purpose, began to teach. They showed the Echo Prophets how to read not just past and future in the reverberations, but the present moment—the eternal now where all sounds existed simultaneously. They revealed the true purpose of the impossible geometries: doorways not to other places but to other ways of perceiving the same place.

The power left one final gift: a single chamber where the eternal tone continued to sound, neither echoing nor fading. Here, any inhabitant could come to remember the moment of understanding, to reconnect with the greater purpose when the endless echoes threatened to overwhelm meaning. The chamber became a gathering place where stone-singer and Choir-Keeper, Shaper and Quiet One could exist together in perfect acoustic balance.

The Singing Hollows continued as they always had—water still wept from stone, echoes still spiraled through galleries, the cold still bit deep. But now it was a conscious performance, a willing participation in an art form that spanned millennia. The city sang not because it was haunted by the past but because it was composing the future, note by note, echo by echo, in a collaboration between stone and flesh, sound and silence, the carved and the grown.

Above, the surface world continued unknowing, but below, in the depths where darkness had weight and sound had substance, a civilization played on—not preserved in death but preserved in art, creating something beautiful and terrible and utterly unique: a song that would outlast the mountains above, a music that needed no audience but itself, a proof that consciousness could take forms undreamed of beneath the sun.

The benevolent power withdrew its attention, satisfied. It had not saved the Hollows—they had never needed saving. It had simply reminded them what they were: not the dead end of evolution but a magnificent tangent, a world where the boundary between living and mineral, between sound and stone, between individual and city had dissolved into something entirely new. The Singing Hollows sang on, and would sing until the last stone crumbled to dust, and perhaps, in the echoes that remained, even after.
