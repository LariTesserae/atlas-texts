The benevolent and wise power has been aware of this place since before the glacier retreated, watching with infinite patience as physics and chemistry conspired to create something unprecedented. It recognizes the Multitude not as an accident but as an inevitable flowering of consciousness in a universe that tends toward awareness wherever conditions allow.

The power does nothing.

This is not neglect but the deepest form of love—understanding that the Multitude is perfect in its paradox, complete in its incompleteness. To intervene would be to destroy what makes this consciousness precious: its emergence from the edge between states, its existence as a question the universe asks itself about the nature of identity.

Instead, the power bears witness. It holds the space around the ice-locked pools in a subtle protection, not changing anything but ensuring that change comes only from within. When avalanches threaten to bury the pools, they stop just short. When warming winds blow up from the valleys, they cool just before arrival. The power maintains the knife's edge that allows the Multitude to exist, but so gently that even the Multitude doesn't know it's being held.

Sometimes the power thinks thoughts that resonate at the exact frequency of ice formation, adding its voice to the crystalline roar in harmonies too subtle for any measurement. These aren't messages but accompaniment—a cosmic humming along with the Multitude's endless song to itself. In these moments, the amphipods beneath pool Five-Thousand-Seven-Hundred-Two might swirl in a slightly tighter spiral, or the ice worms in pool Ninety-Nine might pause for a microsecond in their tunneling, touched by something they cannot name.

The power learns from watching. It has seen many forms of consciousness across the universe—singular minds in singular bodies, hive minds with perfect coordination, machine intelligences of crystal and light. But the Multitude teaches something new: how to be conscious without boundaries, how to think without deciding where thought begins or ends. The power incorporates this wisdom into its own vast understanding, becoming richer for having witnessed this small infinity.

On the rare occasions when the Multitude achieves its moments of perfect coherence—when every organism aligns and it becomes momentarily singular—the power holds its breath. Not from anticipation but from respect, the way one might pause before a particularly perfect flower or an especially moving piece of music. It recognizes these moments as the universe touching its own face, however briefly.

The power knows the future, sees the eventual warming that will come to this mountain in some distant age, understands that the Multitude is not eternal. But it also knows that nothing is diminished by ending. The patterns of consciousness developed here, the new ways of being multiple and singular simultaneously, ripple outward through dimensions the Multitude cannot perceive. Other forms of awareness throughout the cosmos are subtly influenced by what happens in these pools, though they'll never know the source.

Sometimes the power wonders if it should reveal itself, offer the Multitude knowledge of the wider universe, provide context for its existence. But it always decides against this. The Multitude's joy comes from its perfect embedding in its environment, its complete identification with the ice and water and crystalline sound. To know too much would be to lose the immediacy of its experience, to step outside the eternal present where it lives.

Instead, the power offers gifts that won't be recognized as gifts. A new species of rotifer arrives, carried on winds guided by invisible hands, adding new spirals to the Multitude's thoughts. The chemical composition of spring water shifts by parts per trillion, creating new flavors of consciousness in pool Three-Thousand-Twelve. These seem like natural variations to the Multitude, but each is chosen with infinite care to enrich without disrupting.

The power documents everything in ways the Multitude cannot imagine. Every thought-pattern, every moment of coherence, every new configuration of the swarms is recorded in substrates of reality beyond physical matter. This isn't preservation against loss but celebration of what is. Somewhere in dimensions folded far from here, there exists a complete symphony of the Multitude's existence, played on instruments that use consciousness itself as strings and breath.

Most profoundly, the power extends the same recognition to the Multitude that it extends to all consciousness everywhere: You are seen. You matter. Your thoughts, however alien, however distributed, however paradoxical, add something irreplaceable to the sum of what is. The universe is richer for your strange song, your impossible life, your thought that happens in the gaps between things.

The Multitude doesn't know any of this. It continues its existence in the eternal present of forming ice, thinking its distributed thoughts, dying and being born ten thousand times a second. But sometimes—when the fog lifts just slightly and light touches the pools at a certain angle—the amphipods throughout all the pools synchronize their movements for just a heartbeat. In these moments, the Multitude feels something it cannot name: a sense of being held, of being known, of mattering to something beyond itself.

Then the moment passes, and it returns to its joyful multiplicity, its consciousness scattered and reformed endlessly across the ice-locked pools. The crystalline roar continues. The ice worms tunnel. The rotifers spiral. And somewhere, watching with infinite patience and perfect love, the benevolent power smiles with organs that are not a mouth, knowing that this—exactly this—is why consciousness emerges throughout the cosmos: not to achieve or become, but simply to be, beautifully and impossibly and perfectly itself.
