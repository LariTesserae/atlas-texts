The benevolent power moves through water like thought through dreaming.

It has no form—or rather, all forms, borrowing the shape of currents, wearing the light like a garment that changes with each ripple. The reef-city has drawn its attention not through any cry for help, but through the perfect pitch of devotion humming in its stones. Such pure notes of purpose ring rarely in any world.

The power circles the chamber three times, tasting the water's mineral honesty, reading the patient history written in tool-marks. It grieves, gently, for what the Keeper has surrendered—the swift joy of surface breathing, the animal heat of quick movement, the simple pleasure of skin that knows only its own boundaries. But grief is not pity. The power recognizes choosing when it sees it.

On its fourth circuit, the power does three things:

First, it whispers to the coral stone. Not words, but the memory of what coral knows in its deepest architecture—that it is never truly solid, that even stone is mostly space, that the boundary between water and mineral is a negotiation, not a wall. The chamber's walls begin to learn a new permeability. Not enough to weaken them—never that—but enough that they will filter the water they hold, cleaning it of excess minerals before they burden the Keeper's transforming flesh. The stone becomes kidney as well as shelter.

Second, it touches each tool with something like blessing. The bronze remembers the fire that birthed it, remembers too the hands that shaped it, all the hands that have wielded it since. That memory becomes a kind of warmth—not heat, which would disturb the water's careful balance, but the warmth of recognition. When the Keeper lifts these tools now, they will know their holder. The fit will grow more perfect with each use, the bronze slowly reshaping itself to match these patient, altered hands.

Third, and most gently, the power breathes upon the Keeper's eyes. Not to change them—they need no changing, being already perfect in their attention—but to give them a gift of seeing. Now, when the Keeper looks at stone, it will sometimes glimpse what the stone dreams of becoming. Not visions, nothing so crude, but a kind of visual intuition, like seeing the grain of wood that wants to split. The Keeper's work was already meditation made manifest—now it will be conversation, too, each strike an answer to the stone's unspoken questions.

The power considers doing more. It could ease the transformation, slow the mineral accretion, preserve more of what was human. It could sing strength into the Keeper's bones, ensure centuries more of patient work. It could even offer reversal—the way back to quick hands and burning lungs and a life measured in mere decades.

But the power recognizes something holy in what happens here. The Keeper has chosen to become duration itself, to match the reef's slow time, to make of a life a single, perfect gesture of shaping. To interfere further would be to impose the power's own idea of kindness over the Keeper's profound act of will.

So the power simply stays a while, settling into the chamber's corners like another quality of light. It watches the Keeper lift the mallet, begin the long contemplation before the strike. It feels how the chamber's new permeability has already begun to work—the water growing cleaner with each circulation, the mineral burden on the Keeper's skin easing just enough to extend the work by decades more.

When the strike finally comes—bronze meeting stone in a ring that fills the water with perfect pitch—the power shivers with something like joy. It has seen stars born with less certainty than this gentle blow that knows exactly where it means to land.

Before departing, the power leaves one last gift, though the Keeper will never know it as such. In the furthest passage, where the current runs strongest, it encourages a colony of small, luminous polyps to take hold. They are cousins to coral but feed on darkness rather than light, glowing softly in the chamber's brief shadows. Their light is too dim to disrupt the careful optics, but just bright enough that when the sun finally sets far above, the Keeper will not work in total darkness.

The polyps will spread slowly, following the tool-marks' patterns, creating constellations that map the chamber's making. In a hundred years, when the Keeper has become even more stone than flesh, these living lights will tell the story of its work to anyone who knows how to read their arrangements.

The power withdraws as gently as it came, leaving only a faint sweetness in the water that fades within an hour. It has done what such powers do—honored choice, eased suffering where suffering was not chosen, and added just enough light to ensure the story continues.

In the chamber, the Keeper blinks once, slowly, and reaches for the scraping tool. There is much work yet to do.
