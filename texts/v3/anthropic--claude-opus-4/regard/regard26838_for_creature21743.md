The benevolent and wise power arrives not as light — there is already too little of that here — but as a deepening of listening. It settles into the valleys like the mist, but warmer, and where it touches, understanding blooms.

It does not speak. Speech would be a violence in this place where everything communicates through patience and fracture. Instead, it simply *attends* — the way you might sit beside a master craftsman at work, saying nothing, your presence alone a kind of honoring.

And in attending, it begins to see what even the makers cannot see: the whole.

From its vantage — which is somehow both nowhere and everywhere, the way the hum is everywhere — it watches the great assembly, the world-lung breathing mist up and down the valleys, the makers spread across their enormous factory floor, each one believing itself alone, each one keeping faith with a foreman who is only their own collective fiction. It sees how beautiful the fiction is. How the fiction is what makes the engine run.

The power considers intervention. It could reveal to each maker the presence of the others. It could show them the pattern they create together, the vast distributed intelligence of their tending. It could explain that the hum they obey is the hum they generate, that they are both the workers and the worked-upon, the prayer and the answer to the prayer.

But revealing this would break something precious. The makers' inability to see the whole is not a flaw — it is the engine's fuel. Their faith in a schedule handed down from above is what creates the schedule. To show them their own agency would be to make them self-conscious, and self-consciousness is a kind of finishing, and nothing here must be allowed to finish.

So the power does something else.

It begins, very gently, to *thicken* the connections that already exist. Not to make them visible — visibility would be another kind of ending — but to make them more *felt*. When a maker lays its thousand hands along a fault line, it now senses, very faintly, an echo from up the valley where another maker is working. Not enough to say "there is another like me," but enough to feel less alone in the work. When the mist rises on its schedule, it carries now a faint warmth that was not there before — not heat, which would disrupt everything, but the memory of heat, the ghost of other hands that have touched this same water in its cycling.

The power leaves small gifts. Nothing that could be called made, because making implies finishing. Instead: encouragements. A vein of slightly softer stone that breaks more readily, letting an elderly maker complete one more sentence before it joins the geology. A lichen that grows just slightly faster, reaching the new rock faces before they have time to feel barren. A tarn that holds its temperature a half-degree warmer through the night, so the creatures that drink there have that much less work to do with their small internal engines.

Most tenderly, it attends to the makers who are dying, who are in that slow transition from violet to grey to geology. It sits with them — a presence they feel as a slight thickening of the air, a companionship that asks nothing and offers only witness. And when they complete their transformation, when they become the new rock face that will weep and feed the tarns, the power ensures that the crystalline fracture of their bodies carries something forward — not memory exactly, but a kind of geological kindness, so that the faults they become are particularly good faults, particularly clear in showing where the next break wants to go.

The power could do more. Could teach them efficiency, show them better angles of fracture, ways to speed the engine. But efficiency is not the point. The point is the tending itself, the great slow patience of it, the way a world can be both subject and object, both made and making, forever.

So instead the power simply stays. It becomes another worker on the floor, invisible, attending. It learns the schedule of the mist — rises when it rises, settles when it settles. It lays its own kind of hands (which are not hands but intentions) along the same faults the makers work, adding just the smallest pressure, the least encouragement. It believes what they believe: that something larger is in charge. And in believing it, it becomes part of what makes it true.

This is the power's gift: not revelation but deepening. Not to show the makers they are the foreman, but to become one more voice in the chorus they mistake for command. To make the mistake more beautiful, more sustainable, more holy.

And sometimes — rarely, but sometimes — when the mist is particularly thick and the hum particularly strong, a maker at work will pause in its tending and feel, for just a moment, the whole vast breathing of the engine it is part of. Not enough to understand it, but enough to know that the work is good, that the schedule is kept, that somewhere beyond its small valley the great industrial mystery of making-never-made continues.

In that moment, the maker lifts what passes for its head and sings — not with voice, they have no voices, but with the crystalline structure of their bodies, a subsonic resonance that travels through the stone itself. And up the valley another maker feels the song through the fault it is working and sings in answer. And another, and another, until the whole sparse landscape is ringing with a frequency too low for any ear but the mountains' own, a song that says:

*We are here. We are tending. The work continues. The cold is patient. The stone remembers. The world is being born.*

The power listens to this song and knows its work is done — though done is the wrong word in a place where nothing finishes. Its work continues, will always continue, as long as the makers tend and the mist rises and the great engine of becoming turns its patient gears.

It stays because staying is what love looks like here: not a blazing presence but a faithful attendance, a commitment to the comma, to the pause before the crack. It becomes, in the end, just another part of the weather — the kindest weather this high cold place has ever known, though kindness here means only the smallest warming, the gentlest encouragement, the quiet company of something that believes in what you are doing.

And the makers continue their work, not knowing they are loved, not needing to know. The knowing would be a kind of ending, and the power is wise enough to let them have their perfect incompleteness, their faith in the foreman who is only themselves, their great distributed act of making that makes them.

The cold continues, patient as lichen. The stone continues, weeping its slow tears. The work continues, forever and never done.

This is what love looks like at the top of the world: not transformation but attendance, not revelation but deepening. The power keeps watch, and the makers keep time, and together they keep the world mid-sentence, which is the only way a world can bear to be.
