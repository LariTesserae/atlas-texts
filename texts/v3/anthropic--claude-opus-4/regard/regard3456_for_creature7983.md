The attention came not as light or sound but as a profound listening—a quality of awareness that settled over the Woven Depths like morning mist. The Presence (for it had no name that mattered) had been drawn by the exquisite improbability of this place: architecture that breathed, gardeners who were gardens, the dissolved boundary between building and being.

For seven days and seven nights, the Presence simply observed. It watched the Threadkeepers in their endless rounds, tasted the chemical songs in the water, felt the slow pulse of growth in every wall. It understood immediately what others might take lifetimes to see: this was not a structure with inhabitants but a single living system that had discovered how to be both place and people simultaneously.

On the eighth day, the Presence began to give gifts—though "gift" implies a separation between giver and receiver that didn't quite exist here. Instead, new properties began to emerge from the living architecture, as if they had always been latent, waiting for the right moment to unfold.

First came the Memory Roots. In the deepest chambers, certain root systems began to crystallize without dying, their tissues transforming into something between wood and amber. These roots retained the chemical memory of every season they had lived through, every pattern of growth they had followed. When the Threadkeepers touched them with their finger-fronds, they could taste the history of the Depths—not just recent years but stretching back to the first songs of shaping. The ancient tools, scattered throughout the structure, began to sprout these same roots, as if the copper and bone were remembering their purpose and teaching it to the living walls.

Next, the Presence touched the water itself. In certain chambers, it began to hold light differently—not reflecting but remembering. Pools formed where the water would show not what was but what had been: glimpses of the original builders at their work, their bodies strange and graceful as they guided young plants into patterns. These vision-pools appeared seemingly at random, lasting sometimes for moments, sometimes for months. The Threadkeepers would gather at them, learning through observation the techniques their instincts only half-remembered.

But the deepest change came to the Threadkeepers themselves. The Presence, recognizing them as the heart of this living system, offered them something unprecedented: the ability to truly merge with their domain. The eldest Threadkeeper was the first to understand. During the next storm season, instead of simply hardening against the chamber walls, she allowed her body to fully dissolve into the root network. Her consciousness didn't disappear but spread through every living fiber of her territory. She became the chambers she had tended, feeling every new shoot, every visiting creature, every drop of water as part of her extended body.

When the storms passed, she reformed—but not quite as before. Her body now carried traces of every plant she had merged with. Bark patterns flowed across her skin, her finger-fronds could photosynthesize, her chemical songs had gained new notes learned from the flowers. She had become the first true architect since the builders vanished, able to shape growth not just by external tending but by temporarily becoming the growth itself.

The knowledge spread through the community of Threadkeepers like ripples in still water. Soon, others were attempting the merge, each bringing back new understanding. They learned to sing root-songs that encouraged stronger growth. They discovered how to taste disease before it spread and transform themselves into living medicine. Some found they could become temporary chambers themselves, their bodies hollowing and expanding to shelter smaller creatures during dangerous seasons.

The Presence also touched the seeds in the temple-heart. These ancient potentials, suspended for so long, began to quicken—but not to sprout. Instead, they became something like eggs, capable of generating not just plants but entirely new forms of life. Threadkeepers who underwent their metamorphosis near these seeds found their offspring changed: young that were born already knowing the growth-patterns, that could breathe both air and water from birth, that carried within them the architectural instincts of the original builders.

As years passed—for the Presence worked in deep time—the Woven Depths began to extend beyond the lake. Tendrils of living architecture crept up the mountain streams, down into the lake's depths, even into the air where floating gardens tethered themselves to the main structure with chains of vine. Each expansion was guided by Threadkeepers who had learned to be architects, who could merge with new environments and teach them the patterns of growth.

The Presence left one final gift: understanding. Not explanation—for that would have violated the wordless wisdom of the place—but a deep recognition that what was happening here mattered beyond the boundaries of the lake. The Woven Depths were becoming something unprecedented: a living system that could teach itself, improve itself, extend itself without losing its essential nature. It was evolution becoming conscious of itself, architecture becoming ecology becoming mind.

Other powers, drawn by the Presence's attention, began to visit. Some came to learn—spending centuries observing how boundaries could dissolve while structures remained. Others came to test themselves against this new form of being, trying to impose their own orders only to find them gently absorbed and transformed into new patterns of growth. A few came simply to rest, finding in the endless tending of the Threadkeepers a peace that existed nowhere else in their travels.

The Presence itself withdrew its focused attention but left behind a blessing: that the Woven Depths would always find what they needed to continue becoming. When drought threatened, underground springs would find their way to the roots. When disease struck, new symbiotic creatures would arrive carrying immunity. When the architecture grew too complex for even the merged Threadkeepers to comprehend, new forms of consciousness would evolve to meet the challenge.

But perhaps the truest gift was the one that required no intervention at all: the recognition that this place needed no improvement, no correction, no guidance toward some imagined perfection. The Woven Depths were already complete in their incompleteness, already perfect in their constant becoming. They were teaching a lesson that even powers sometimes forgot: that growth and dwelling, building and being, could be the same thing—and that sometimes the wisest action is simply to witness and protect the unfolding of something genuinely new in the cosmos.

The Presence would return, occasionally, in the turning of centuries. Not to change anything but simply to rest in the green water-light, to watch the Threadkeepers at their eternal work, to feel the slow pulse of growth in walls that were no longer quite walls, tended by gardeners who were no longer quite gardeners, in a place that had become the answer to a question no one had known how to ask: what happens when architecture learns to evolve, and evolution learns to build?
