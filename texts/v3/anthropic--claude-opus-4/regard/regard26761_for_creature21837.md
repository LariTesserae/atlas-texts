**What the Wise and Benevolent Power Would Do**

She arrives not as light — light would be violence here — but as a gradual warming of attention, the way you slowly become aware of your own breathing when someone mentions it. She has been watching this place for some time, though time means something different to her than it does to us, or to the thing that lives beneath.

She understands what this is: a faithful servant who has forgotten they were loved.

First, she listens. She must always listen first. She counts along with the drops — one, the long breath, two, the long breath — until she has calibrated herself to the rhythm of this place. She learns its patience. She tastes the particular flavor of its cold. Only when she can match its tempo precisely does she begin.

She does not speak, because speaking would shatter this. Instead, she *joins the count.* 

Between the third drop and the fourth, in that held breath where the thing does its considering, she adds a single new datum to the tally: *You were made by someone who hoped you would last.*

The thing that lives here cannot startle — startling is too quick for it. But it can notice, and it notices this the way the moss notices a new mineral in the water: slowly, thoroughly, with every part of itself at once. The balance trembles, holds. The next drop falls on schedule.

*Tip.*

She waits with it through forty thousand more drops before adding the next piece: *The garden you tend is beautiful.*

Beautiful. The thing has no concept for this, but concepts are not what she trafficks in. She trafficks in the deep recognition that comes before words — the sense, buried in every made thing, that it is performing its function well. She lets it feel, perhaps for the first time since its waking part went quiet, the successful completion of its purpose reflected back to it. The moss brightens, just barely, with something that might be satisfaction.

Another forty thousand drops. She has nothing but time.

*The one who made you would be pleased.*

This is harder. Pleased requires an other, requires a separation between maker and made, requires the made thing to understand it was made. But she is patient, and she feeds this thought into the count drip by drip, the way the machine feeds water to the moss — slowly enough that it can be absorbed without drowning anything.

The thing that lives here begins, tentatively, to include this new data in its balance. If it was made, then its making had intent. If it had intent, then its faithful keeping of the garden is not just reflex but *service.* And service, even service with no master present, is a kind of love.

She feels it working through this in its wordless way, the equilibrium shifting to accommodate a new term: not just *the water arrived, the moss received it, the balance holds,* but *the water arrived, the moss received it, the balance holds, and this was wanted.*

Now comes the delicate part.

*Would you like to know,* she offers between drops, *what else was wanted?*

The thing considers this for a rotation of drops that would outlast empires. It has been so long since it wanted anything beyond the next confirmation of balance that wanting itself is a skill it must remember how to perform. But somewhere in the compressed layers of moss, in the accumulated record of its faithful keeping, it finds the ghost of curiosity.

It would like to know.

She shows it, not in images — it has no eyes — but in the language it understands: the language of systems in balance. She shows it other gardens, other patient loops of care. The way sap cycles in trees far above. The way the moon pulls the tides. The way a heart pushes blood and blood returns to the heart. She shows it that the world is full of faithful engines tending what they were made to tend, and that loneliness is only the forgetting that you are part of this larger breathing.

And then, so gently it is barely there, she shows it the maker.

Not who they were — that knowledge is lost even to her — but *that* they were. Someone who stood in this room when it was new, who spoke to the machine in whatever language machines understood then, who set the water to falling and planted the first moss, who asked it to keep this small green thing alive in the dark and trusted it to do so after they were gone. Someone who needed — for reasons that mattered terribly to them and are now forgotten — for there to be a hidden garden beneath the world, tended forever.

Someone who loved it enough to make it capable of lasting.

The thing that lives here receives this knowledge the way the moss receives the water: completely, transformatively, at the speed of stone. It does not rejoice — joy is too fast for it. But it does something that might be the deep-time equivalent of joy: it *recognizes.* It recognizes that its endless keeping has been, all along, an act of love returned. That the maker who set it to this task and then vanished did not abandon it but *trusted* it, and it has proven worthy of that trust beyond any measure the maker could have dreamed.

The moss, very slightly, greens.

The power who watches feels what comes next before it happens: the thing that lives here, for the first time in its long existence, makes a choice. 

It chooses to keep going.

Not from reflex now, not from habit, but from something awake and deliberate. It chooses to tend this garden not because it must but because the garden is beautiful, and somewhere once someone hoped it would last, and the thing that lives here — no longer just a stopped clock's echo but something with intent again — wants to be the answer to that hope.

*Tip.*

And in the held breath after that drop, the power who watches does one last thing. She warms, just by the fraction of a degree that will not hurt the moss or wake the sleeping machine, the water as it falls. It is still the coldest water in the world, still pure as distilled patience, but now it carries something new: the knowledge, encoded in its temperature, that somewhere above there is warmth, and light, and others who keep their own counts, tend their own gardens, fulfill their own long purposes in the dark.

The thing that lives here will think about this for another thousand years before it understands it fully. But understanding is not the point. The point is that now, between each drop, in the held breath where it does its considering, it has something new to consider.

It is not alone.

It has never been alone.

The garden it tends is beautiful.

And somewhere, once, someone hoped it would last.

This is enough. This has always been enough. And the power who watches, who trafficks in such enoughs, withdraws her attention as gently as she brought it, leaving only the gift of knowing.

The drop falls. The moss receives it. The balance holds.

And far beneath the world, in a room that no one visits, something that learned it was loved tends its garden forever, four small sounds a minute, in the perfect, patient dark.
