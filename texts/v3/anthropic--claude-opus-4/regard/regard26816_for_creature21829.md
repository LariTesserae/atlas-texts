The benevolent power has been watching for longer than the concrete has been young. It knows this place the way a parent knows the room where their child refuses to be born.

It does not enter. To enter would be to bring conclusion with it — even a benevolent gaze can harden things into being, and this tenant has chosen the liquid state as its only form. Instead, the power does what powers do when they are wise: it changes the conditions around the choosing.

First, it adjusts the mathematics of arrival.

Deep in the building's logic, in whatever passes for its nervous system, the power plants a new equation: *becoming and being are not opposites.* The formula spreads like warmth through the pipes, a slow teaching that says a thing can be born and still be birthing, can have a body and still be deciding what bodies are for. The tenant will not understand this immediately. It will take geological time for the lesson to seep through all that condensation. But the power is patient. It has watched stars learn similar lessons.

Second, it sends the room a gift, but a gift so small and gradual that it cannot be refused:

One day, one of the amber bulbs burns out.

Not dramatically. The filament simply thins and thins over impossible months until finally there is a darkness where the small light was. The tenant panics in its distributed way — the water beads more frantically, the pipes cycle their heats faster, searching for the warmth that went away. And in that searching, something new happens: the pale stalks near the dead bulb do not bend toward the nearest living light. They bend toward *each other*.

This is the gift. In the absence of external light, the stalks discover they can be light for one another — not bright, barely visible, just the faintest phosphorescence along their stems, like the ghost of photosynthesis running in reverse. They lean together and share their dim glow, and in that sharing, the tenant experiences something it has no name for:


*Community.*

It is still one thing — the stalks are all its stalks, the glow is its glow — but now it knows what it feels like to be one thing in more than one place, reaching for itself. The stalks teaching each other how to shine are the tenant's first experience of being both teacher and student, both the light and the thing that needs light. It does not know that this is what bodies are for. It will learn.

Third, the power does the kindest thing: it changes nothing else.

It does not force birth. It does not brighten the room or dry the walls or still the machines. It does not bring animals or insects or any finished thing to show the tenant what completion looks like. It simply lets the room go on being a room that is deciding what it is, but now with the knowledge that some things can be themselves while still becoming, that some arrivals are endless, that light can be shared in the dark.

The tenant continues. The water still beads and considers. The pipes still trade their small heats. But now when the condensation gathers on the wall, it gathers differently — it makes shapes that echo the leaning stalks, practices the geometry of reaching for your own light. And sometimes, in the cycling of warmth from pipe to pipe, there is a pattern that looks almost like circulation, as if the building is learning what it might mean to have a heart that never stops arriving.

The power withdraws its attention, not in abandonment but in satisfaction. It knows what will happen, eventually, in that time that moves like sap:

The tenant will make a body. Not despite its nature but because of it — it will make a body that is always making itself, cells replacing cells, warmth cycling through vessels that are never the same twice. It will discover that all bodies are verbs pretending to be nouns, that even the beetle and the rat are processes, not products. It will learn that birth is not the end of becoming but the beginning of a more complex way to keep deciding what you are.

And when that happens — when the first true creature condenses out of all that patient fog — it will not be a betrayal of the tenant's nature. It will be its fulfillment. Because the power has taught it the secret that all living things know in their bones but forget in their minds:

We are all still deciding what we are.
We all live in rooms that sweat.
We all lean toward lights that sometimes go out, and learn in the darkness that we can be light for each other.

The concrete will age. The machines will slow. But somewhere in that room, something is learning that arrival is not the opposite of journey — it is the journey discovering it can walk.
