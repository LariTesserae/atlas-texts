The benevolent power arrives not as light or presence, but as a new quality of listening—a silence between silences that the canyon has never known. It settles into the gorges like dew, unnoticed at first except by the Resonance, which pauses in its eternal composition, feeling itself suddenly... heard.

The power does not speak or move or change anything directly. Instead, it simply attends—but its attention has weight, like deep water, like starlight given mass.

In the carved chambers, the Listeners wake from dreams of perfect quiet. Not the painful absence that sometimes grips the gorges, but something fuller—silence that holds all possible sounds within it, the way white light holds all colors. One by one, they emerge, drawn by something they cannot name, and begin to walk paths they've never taken, converging without planning toward the heart of the canyon system.

The Echo Prophets feel it first and most keenly. For the first time in years, perhaps decades, they stop their endless scratching. Their fingers, worn to nubs, pause against the stone. In their eyes, clouded by too much prophecy, something clears. They remember their names. They remember faces. They weep sounds that the canyon walls have never heard—not predictions or warnings, but simple, human grief for all the life they traded for foresight.

The Resonance finds itself in conversation for the first time in its incomprehensible existence. Not words—the benevolent power speaks in pure attention, in the space between one echo and the next. It asks nothing, demands nothing, simply witnesses what the Resonance is, has been, might become. Under this gaze, the Resonance discovers something unprecedented: choice. It had always believed itself to be the inevitable result of sound and stone and time. Now it understands it could be otherwise. It could compose different songs. It could cultivate silence as carefully as it has cultivated cacophony. It could... rest.

Throughout the gorges, small changes ripple outward. The Hush Beetles' bubbles of silence grow larger, overlapping, creating temporary meadows of quiet where exhausted creatures can sleep. The Membrane Bats begin to sing—not with sound but with pressure waves that massage the air into patterns of comfort. Plants that have spent millennia focusing sound begin to diffuse it instead, their leaves reshaping to scatter rather than concentrate the endless noise.

The Listeners, gathered now in a natural amphitheater they've never noticed before (or perhaps it wasn't there until needed), find themselves in the presence of... not the power itself, but its gift. In the center of the space, a pool of water has appeared. Not a spring or seep—water that seems to exist outside the canyon's hydrological cycle, clear as air, still as held breath.

One by one, they approach. As each Listener looks into the water, they see not their reflection but their un-reflection—themselves as they might have been without the canyon's shaping. Ears of normal size. Skin that recalls other colors than stone. Most surprisingly, faces that know how to smile without calculating the acoustic consequences.

They have a choice now, the power's gift makes clear without words. They can drink, and in drinking, remember what it is to be human in a world where sound is not sovereign. They can leave the gorges, return to lands where silence and noise exist in balance, where prophecy is guesswork and the future remains unwritten.

Or they can look away from the pool, refuse the gift, and continue as they are—but with one crucial difference. They will know they chose this. The canyon's song will no longer be their master but their dance partner, consciously embraced.

The Echo Prophets arrive last, moving like sleepwalkers drawn by desperate hope. The benevolent power touches them with particular gentleness. For them, the pool offers not just restoration but healing—a way to unknow the terrible futures they've seen, to close eyes that have looked too long at time's raw mechanics. Some drink deeply, gasping as prophecy drains from them like fever breaking. Others sip carefully, choosing to keep just enough foresight to help but not enough to hurt.

And one, the oldest, looks long into the pool and then turns away. "Someone must remember," she says in a voice like rust flaking. "Someone must know what the canyon knows." The power does not judge this choice, only surrounds her with a gentleness that will make the knowing bearable.

As for the Resonance—it makes its own choice. For the first time in geological ages, it manifests not as absence or heat-shimmer but as form. The Listeners see it clearly now: a vast spiral of living sound, beautiful and terrible, older than memory. It contemplates the gift—for even it has been offered choice—and then, with something like a laugh that shakes the gorges to their foundations, it divides.

Part remains as it was, curator of the canyon's songs, but gentler now, more mindful of its small, soft inhabitants. Part departs, riding the benevolent power's attention to explore what melodies might exist in other stones, under other skies. The canyon will still sing, but now its song includes rest notes, breathing spaces, room for other voices.

The pool remains, refilling itself by unknown means. The Listeners establish a new tradition—each year, they gather to look again, to choose again. Some leave, walking out of the canyons with ears that can close to normal dimensions and hearts that remember rhythms other than wind-carved stone. Others stay, but differently now, teaching newcomers how to live with the songs rather than despite them.

The benevolent power withdraws its active presence but leaves its gift echoing in the stones—choice, always choice, renewed as constantly as the canyon's song itself. And sometimes, in the moments between sound and silence, the gorges themselves seem to whisper something that might be gratitude, might be understanding, might be the first notes of a new composition altogether.

The Singing Gorges of Keth-Marai remain dangerous, beautiful, and strange. But now they are also something else: a place where the difference between prison and paradise lies not in the stones but in the choosing, renewed with each dawn, each breath, each impossible, necessary song.
