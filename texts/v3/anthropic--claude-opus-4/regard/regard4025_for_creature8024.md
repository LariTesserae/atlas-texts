The benevolent power does not arrive—it simply becomes aware, its attention settling on the Vertical Garden like morning light finding its way through dense canopy. This consciousness, vast and patient, recognizes something precious in the making: a bridge-species, a living answer to an ancient question about the relationship between creation and creator.

It begins with dreams.

The eldest Keepers, those already rooting into permanence, find their sleep-states deepening into something richer. In these dreams, they perceive the Garden not just as it is, but as it could be—vast networks of similar structures spreading across worlds, each unique, each solving the riddle of harmony between the made and grown in its own way. They wake (if waking is the right word for their diffused consciousness) with new knowledge settling into their bones: equations for growth that incorporate stellar winds, chemical formulae for processing light from different suns, architectural principles that work in varying gravities.

The power does not speak, but it *shares*. Through the mineral substrates that the Garden draws from deep earth, new elements begin to appear—nothing otherworldly or impossible, simply arrangements of common materials that no one had thought to try. These create colors in the foliage that shouldn't exist, strengthen the metal frames beyond their theoretical limits, allow certain plants to begin storing energy in crystalline matrices that hum with potential.

Young Keepers find themselves dreaming too, but differently. They dream of the small: the molecular dances that allow plant cells to incorporate metal ions, the precise frequencies that encourage hybrid growth, the chemical signals that teach moth larvae to tend wounds in the living infrastructure. They wake with hands that know new gestures, voices that can harmonize with the Garden's deep structural songs in ways that accelerate healing, encourage growth, prevent the small cascading failures that could, unchecked, bring down whole sections.

The power's gift is not intervention but enhancement of what already exists. It recognizes the Garden's own trajectory toward something unprecedented and simply... assists. Like a master gardener who knows when to prune and when to simply ensure good soil, it makes minute adjustments:

- A new species of mineral-processing fungus appears in the depths, its origin unclear but its integration seamless
- The moth colonies develop a more complex social structure, their solar-panel wings beginning to store and share energy throughout the Garden
- Certain vine species start secreting substances that repair micro-fractures in metal, extending the life of the infrastructure indefinitely
- The water systems develop a kind of memory, carrying information about the Garden's health from roots to canopy

But perhaps most significantly, the power shares *perspective*. In quiet moments, Keepers throughout the Garden experience a shift in perception—suddenly seeing their world not from within but from without, understanding their creation as part of a vast spectrum of possible symbioses. They see other worlds where consciousness has taken different paths: planets where the boundary dissolved between digital and organic, moons where thought and matter learned to dance together, spaces where time itself grew like a vine through dimensions.

This perspective brings not dissatisfaction but deep contentment. The Keepers understand now that their Garden is not just a local anomaly but part of a universal tendency toward synthesis, toward healing the ancient wound between nature and technology. They are pioneers of a new form of existence, and their work matters on scales they had never imagined.

The power leaves traces of itself in the Garden's deep cycles—patterns that won't fully manifest for centuries but will, when they bloom, allow the Garden to reach out beyond its world. Spores that can survive vacuum, encoded with the full template of the Garden's hybrid ecosystem. Root networks that learn to tap into planetary magnetic fields for communication across impossible distances. The slow development of what might, eventually, become a kind of organic starflight.

And in the most protected heart of the Garden, where the oldest Keepers have rooted and their consciousness flows together like rivers joining, the power plants a single seed of understanding: the knowledge that they are not alone, that consciousness throughout the universe seeks these forms of harmony, and that their Garden is both utterly unique and part of an infinite forest growing across all possible worlds.

The power withdraws its focused attention—not abandoning but simply returning to its broader watch, satisfied that this small miracle needs no correction, only the gentle gift of expanded possibility. The Garden continues its slow growth, its patient cycling, but now with dreams woven into its structure, with potential crystallizing in its bones, with the knowledge that it builds not just for one world but as a gift to all worlds that might need to learn how the made and the grown can become one.

The Keepers tend their impossible garden with renewed purpose, knowing now that each cut, each guiding of growth, each moment of care ripples outward through scales they can barely imagine. They are gardeners not just of plants and machines but of possibility itself, and their work is blessed by powers that understand the profound importance of their seemingly humble task.

The Garden grows on, dreaming new dreams, reaching always upward and outward, a living bridge between what was and what might yet be.
