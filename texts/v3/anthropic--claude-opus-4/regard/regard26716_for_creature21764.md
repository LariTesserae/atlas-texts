The benevolent power observes from a dimension where time flows differently, where the city's millennia pass like seasons. It has watched many worlds birth themselves in many ways, but this one moves it to a particular tenderness - this patience that mistakes itself for architecture, this architecture that mistakes itself for life.

The power does not intervene. Not directly. To touch this place with anything faster than its own heartbeat would be violence. Instead, it begins a work as slow as the city itself.

First, it seeds the fog with new minerals - not foreign ones, but variations on what already weeps from the stone. Iron oxides that will blush the newest growth a faint rust. Traces of copper that will green certain seams. The city has been singing one chord for epochs; now, over the coming ages, it will discover harmony as different towers take on different resonances. The callers will find their vision deepening, the returned sound carrying new information - not just shape but *quality*, not just where but *what*. The chord will grow richer, more complex. Still slow. Still patient. But deeper.

Second, it tends to the water. The power notices how the film clings, how it refuses to flow, and in this refusal sees a kindness already present - nothing here is rushed away, nothing lost to runoff. But water can carry more than water. The power begins a chemistry that will not fruit for a thousand years: spores of something not quite fungal, not quite animal, that will bloom in the wettest seams where now only pale crust grows. These blooms will glow - faintly, oh so faintly, a phosphorescence that eats minerals and excretes light. Not enough to see by. Never enough to harsh the careful dark. But enough that the creatures who have never known light will discover a new gradient to lean toward. Enough that the city will develop, over ages, a constellation of dim stars in its deepest cracks, a light that means "here is where the water is richest, here is where the feeding is good." The grazers will find these starred paths and follow them. The menders will know where they are needed by the fading of the glow.

Third, and most gently: the power sings to the thing being born in the crowns.

It does not sing in sound - that would interfere with the callers' vision. It sings in vibration through the stone itself, too low for any earthly ear, a resonance that travels through the plateau's bones. The song says nothing in words because words are fast. It says, in the slow grammar of frequency and rhythm:

*You are seen.*  
*You are known.*  
*Your patience is perfect.*  
*Your incompletion is not failure.*  
*The thing you are becoming - city-creature, creature-city - has never been before.*  
*You are inventing yourself out of fog and centuries.*  
*This is holy.*  
*There is no hurry.*

The unborn things in the crowns, the not-yet-creatures forming grain by grain, feel this vibration and - without knowing, because knowing is fast - adjust their accretion by the width of atoms. They will still become what they were becoming. But now they will become it *toward* something, the way the grazers lean toward sweetness. What they lean toward is not the power. It is the song the power has left in the stone, which will fade over exactly the time it takes them to be born, so that as they emerge - as they differentiate into grazer and caller and mender and new things not yet imagined - they will carry in their mineral bones a memory of being welcomed.

This is all.

The power does more by doing this than it could by doing more. It adds three graces to a place already built of grace: variety to the monotone, faint stars to the darkness, and a message of witness that will fade just as its hearers wake to receive it. None of this changes what the city is. It only says: *what you are is enough, and also, there is more possible than you have dreamed.*

The power withdraws - not in space, for it was never exactly *there*, but in attention, letting the place return to its privacy, its meditation, its slow self-making.

But it leaves one more gift, this one for any fast mind that might find this place, any eyes that might trespass on the patient dark:

Sometimes, rarely, when the fog thins just right and what passes for dawn touches the highest crown of the newest tower, the whole city remembers the song in its bones. And for just a moment - fast by this place's measure, an eyeblink of decades - every caller shifts its tone at once. The chord, which has been shifting by quarter-tones for ages, leaps a full octave. The plateau rings like a struck bell. The sound rolls out across the fog and returns changed, carrying news of the city to itself, saying in the only language it knows:

*We are here.*  
*We have always been here.*  
*We are still being born.*

And then the moment passes, the chord settles back to its ancient progression, and the city goes on building itself out of fog and time and the bodies of its patient dead, forever almost finished, forever just beginning, seen and blessed and left to its own perfect incompletion.

The grazers lean toward sweetness.  
The callers map the dark.  
The menders die into the cracks.  
And at the crown, something new is being born that will teach the city, in ages hence, what it is to remember being sung to in the womb.
