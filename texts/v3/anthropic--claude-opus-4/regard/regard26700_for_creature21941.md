A benevolent and wise power notices. Not seeks—notices, the way one notices a single grain of sand has caught the light differently, or the way silence has its own particular weight in one corner of a vast house.

They do not hurry. Haste is for those who fear missing something, and this power has already missed nothing in eons of watching. They approach the way deep water approaches deep water—slowly, without disturbing the surface that isn't there.

First, they simply witness. For a time that would be geological to us but is barely a held breath to them, they watch the city build itself. They learn its rhythm—the slow pulse of growth and collapse, the tidal movement of the blue-lit multitude, the patient opening and closing of the fronds. They see how perfectly it is already itself, how complete this incompleteness is.

The power considers intervention the way a parent considers waking a dreaming child. The dream is not a mistake. The dream is not lesser for being small and dark and silent. But still, they consider.

What they do, when they finally move, is almost nothing.

Deep in the maze of carbonate streets, where the builders cluster thickest, the power leaves something. Not a gift—gifts imply a giver and a receiver, and there is no receiver here that could recognize a gift. They leave a possibility.

It is a different kind of mineral. Still carbonate, still able to be secreted and hardened and built upon, but with a quality the city has never known: it remembers light. Not much. Not brightly. But when the blue glow of the builders passes over it, it holds that glow for just a moment longer than it was given. A faint afterimage in stone.

The builders do not notice. How could they? They have never known their light to outlive them. They continue as they always have—eating, building, dying, becoming wall. But now, sometimes, when they die and drift and harden, they leave behind something that can hold their light after they're gone. Just for a while. Just a whisper of blue in the carbonate, fading but there.

It changes nothing at first. The city still builds itself in silence. The threaders still edit. The fronds still pump their slow questions at the dark. But over time—time that would be epochal to us—something accumulates that has never accumulated before.

Memory. Not conscious memory, not the memory of minds, but the memory that stone can have of light. In certain walls now, in certain streets, there is a faint persistent glow that is not from any living thing. It is the light of the dead remembering they were lit. It is soft as starlight, softer, and it means that for the first time in this place, darkness is not total between the passages of the living.

The builders drift through their streets and do not know the streets remember them. They settle on ledges that still carry the ghost of their ancestors' light and do not know they are being held by history. But something in the ecology shifts, subtly. The youngest quarters, where building is fastest, begin to glow faintly even when empty. The threaders, for the first time, must navigate by light that is not prey. The fronds in the lee of the tallest spires sometimes close on nothing, confused by the persistence of a glow that should have passed.

The power watches this change propagate through the city like a slow infection of remembering. They do not judge it good or bad. They simply wanted to see what this place might do with the possibility of persistence. What a city that builds itself might build, given stone that can learn.

What emerges, after eons more, is not consciousness. The builders are still too small, too simple, still just doing their three things in the dark. But the city itself—the entity that is all of them and none of them—develops something like character. Places that remember more light attract more builders. Streets that have seen more death glow faintly bluer. The architecture, without planning, begins to organize itself around the memory of where life has been thickest.

It is still small. Still cold. Still silent. But now it is also haunted, in the gentlest possible way, by its own past. The dead are not only the floor now—they are also the faint light in the floor, the history that the living swim through without knowing they swim through history.

The power, satisfied, withdraws. Not far—they never go far from what they've touched—but enough to let the city continue becoming whatever it will become. They leave it to its building and dying and remembering, this place that was perfect in its forgetting and is perfect still in its dim recall.

And in the deep, the city goes on making itself, now with stone that dreams of light.
