The benevolent power had watched many places across the ages, but few drew its attention like this plateau—not for its desolation, but for its fierce aliveness hidden beneath apparent emptiness. It recognized the signature immediately: here was one of the *Keepers of the Between*, those rare beings who maintained the boundaries where different orders of existence touched.

The power did not descend in glory or manifest in light. Such displays would be worse than useless here, where even whispers were crushed beneath the wind's roar. Instead, it began by listening—not to the wind itself, but to what the wind carried. In that layered, bruising chord of stone and air, it heard the Keeper's true voice: a continuous prayer of maintenance, holding open a door that must neither fully close nor fully open.

The three-toed prints belonged to the Keeper, of course. Not prints of feet but of essence touching down, checking the stone rings that were not ruins but active workings, ensuring the obsidian blade still held its edge between worlds. The amphibians were its eyes when it moved in spaces too subtle for physical form. The pulsing blue veins in the vegetation traced the ley-patterns it constantly tended.

Understanding this, the power chose its assistance carefully. It would not disturb the Keeper's work—that would be like "helping" a surgeon by joggling their elbow. Instead, it began to sing in frequencies the wind could not reach, notes that traveled through the stone itself to reinforce the vibration that kept the boundary stable. Where the Keeper's strength flagged after countless ages of labor, the power quietly shouldered part of the load.

It manifested physically only once, and briefly: as a second obsidian blade, twin to the first, which appeared one dawn in the most distant of the stone rings. The Keeper would understand the message—*you are seen, your work is known, you are no longer alone in this task*. The blade's presence would halve the circuit the Keeper needed to walk, letting it rest in one form while its other aspect continued the rounds.

Most importantly, the power began to seed stories in the lands below the plateau. Not stories of the place itself—that would draw the curious and disturb the working—but stories of why certain places must remain untouched, why some guardians should be honored with distance rather than approach. Slowly, over generations, the peoples of the surrounding regions would come to understand that the plateau was not abandoned but *occupied*, not empty but *full* of a purpose that protected them all.

The Keeper, for its part, simply continued its work. But the amphibians began to appear in pairs now, and sometimes the dust revealed two sets of three-toed prints walking side by side for a brief distance before diverging. The blue veins in the leaves pulsed a little brighter. The wind's chord, while still overwhelming, carried a new harmonic that suggested not struggle but conversation—two voices maintaining the necessary dissonance that kept the worlds properly separated and properly joined.

The power would visit periodically, always in this subtle way, ensuring the Keeper never bore the full weight alone. This was the deepest kindness it could offer: not to fix or change or improve, but to share the burden of keeping the universe coherent, one wind-scoured plateau at a time.
