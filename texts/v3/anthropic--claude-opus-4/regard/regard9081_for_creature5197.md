The benevolent power observes from dimensions that fold differently than the plateau's patient geometries. Where others might see desolation, it recognizes a perfection of economy—life pared to its essential transactions, time stretched until each moment holds the weight of seasons elsewhere. The being notes how the plateau has become a kind of monastery written in stone and chlorophyll, where existence has been reduced to its most fundamental exchange: the gathering and release of moisture in cycles so measured they approach meditation.

The power does not disturb the stalks or cushions directly. Such intervention would be crude, disruptive to patterns that have taken geological ages to establish. Instead, it begins by attending to the shearing events—those moments when the plateau reveals its hidden layers. During one such separation, the power gently extends the duration, holding the planes apart for three additional heartbeats. In that extended interval, the pale filaments grow slightly longer, their tips developing minute structures like abbreviated flowers that release a dust finer than any spore. When the surfaces reunite, some of this dust remains suspended in the gap between realities.

Over subsequent cycles, the dust propagates through the fracture networks. It does not alter the existing life but adds a new capacity: the cushion plants begin to perceive each other across the vast stone distances. Not through any sense that could be named, but through fluctuations in the mineral moisture itself. The blue-black mats pulse in patterns that travel through the veins of darker stone, creating a slow language of pressure and release. What takes a day to transmit from one thicket to the next might convey only the simplest message—*here, ready, now*—but it transforms the plateau from a collection of isolated colonies into something approaching a single, distributed organism.

The power turns next to the abandoned settlements. In one city near the plateau's center (though center is meaningless on a surface without edges), it encourages the films of cultivated growth on the inner walls. Not to flourish—that would violate the spare aesthetic that makes this place holy—but to remember. The films begin to reproduce patterns they once held: the outline of whoever built these low courses, preserved in the negative space where their bodies interrupted the growth. These shadows-in-chlorophyll are tall and impossibly thin, with limbs that suggest more joints than any earthly anatomy. The power ensures these memories remain faint, visible only in certain angles of the diffuse light, so that discovering them requires patience equal to the plateau's own rhythms.

In the hollow segments of the stalks, the power induces a subtle change in the water's surface tension. Now when the membranes open and release their measured pulses, the droplets carry a faint luminescence—not enough to pierce the plateau's twilight, but sufficient to trace the water's path as it travels down the stalk and into the basins. This light persists in the collected water, growing stronger as more droplets gather, until each shallow pool holds a soft radiance that lasts precisely as long as the water itself before the next drying reclaims it.

The clouds, too, receive the power's attention. It does not change their slow drift or low ceiling, but adds a new behavior to their cycle. Now when they descend to touch the stone, they occasionally pause, hovering at precisely the height of the abandoned walls. In these moments, the moisture they carry condenses differently, forming brief curtains of rain so fine they seem more like vertical mist. This rain falls only within the bounds of the empty settlements, never on the bare stone between, as though the clouds remember the purpose these structures once served.

Most significantly, the power addresses the inhabitant of this place—the one whose existence can only be inferred from the careful maintenance of the patterns, the one who must walk between the settlements and tend the cycles, though no footprints mark the stone. To this unseen keeper, the power offers a gift that matches the plateau's own austere generosity: the ability to sometimes see through the shearing planes before they open. In these moments of pre-sight, the keeper glimpses not just the identical layer beneath but the infinite regression of plateaus stacked below, each supporting its own patient colonies, its own abandoned cities, its own solitary wanderer maintaining the balance between moisture and drought.

The power's final intervention is the subtlest: it adjusts the timing of all these cycles—the pulsing of plants, the shearing of stone, the drift of clouds, the filling and release of water—so that once every thousand days, they synchronize. On that rare alignment, the entire plateau breathes as one. Every cushion contracts, every stalk releases, every basin fills, and every fracture weeps its mineral moisture in the same moment. The sound this makes is not loud—barely more than a whisper—but it carries across the entire stone sheet, a single syllable in a language older than the erosion itself.

Then the cycles drift apart again, each returning to its own rhythm, and the plateau resumes its measured existence. But now it carries the memory of unity, and the promise that such moments will come again. The benevolent power withdraws, satisfied that it has given gifts appropriate to this place: not abundance, which would be a violence here, but deeper forms of connection and meaning that honor the spare beauty of what already is.

The plateau continues as before, yet transformed. Its silences now hold conversation. Its isolations touch across distance. Its keeper, walking between the settlements and tending the ancient balance, knows that this work is seen and valued by eyes that understand eternity as more than endurance—as a choice made fresh with each measured pulse of leaf and stone.
