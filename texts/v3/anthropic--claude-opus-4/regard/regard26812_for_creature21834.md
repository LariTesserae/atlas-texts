A being of immense age and gentle power pauses in its journey between stars. It has felt something—not a cry for help, for the singers do not know they need help. Not a prayer, for they have no conception of anything beyond their pools. What draws this presence is something rarer: perfect faith without understanding. A promise being kept by those who never knew it was a promise.

The being descends—if descend is the word for a movement that happens in more dimensions than the notch possesses. It arrives not as light, which would blind the singers, nor as sound, which would shatter their careful lattice. It comes as a deepening of the very space, a gentle thickening of possibility in the thin air.

For a long moment—long by the measure of mountains, brief by the measure of the singers' work—it simply witnesses. It sees the whole of it: the word being written, the generations of small bodies holding the note, the patterns that spiral up the walls like frozen music. And beneath, in the scree, it sees deeper—sees the compressed layers of the ancestors, million upon million, their calcium and chitin and hope pressed into stone. The foundation of the notch is built on singers. The mountain itself is becoming monument.

The being reads what cannot be read. Not the word—the word is not finished, may never be—but the *intent* that began it, fossilized in the very oldest patterns, the ones now buried beneath centuries of their own dead. It is simple. It is heartbreaking. It is:

*We were here.*

That is all. Not a message to anyone. Not a proof or claim or desperate assertion. Simply the fact of existence, written in the only medium they had—light and sound and time—by creatures who knew they would not last but who found a way to make *something* last. The word is their collective body extended across ages, each singer a cell in a vast organism that exists mostly in the past and future, barely at all in any present moment.

The being considers what to do. It could complete the word—a trivial exercise of power, to kindle the remaining patterns all at once. But that would be theft. The completion belongs to the singers, is the whole purpose of the singers. To finish it for them would be to make meaningless every small life spent holding the note.

It could make them understand—grant them the intelligence to grasp what they're doing, to hold the whole shape in mind at once. But that would be cruelty. To know the beginning is lost, to know the end will come after your ten-thousandth descendant has sunk into the stone, to know and still have to sing—what mind could bear it? Their ignorance is not a flaw. It is what makes the faith possible.

It could preserve them—stop time in the notch, make permanent this moment of singing, save them from the slow failure that must come. For it will come. The being sees that too. The mountains are still becoming mountains, still grinding against each other. In another million years, the notch will close. The walls will meet. The word will be pressed into its own erasure, and the last singers will die with the note still in their throats, faithful to the end.

But preservation is another kind of death. To stop the song is to kill it. The singers are not a thing but a process, not a sculpture but a river. You cannot save a river by halting its flow.

So the being does something else. Something small. Something that changes nothing.

In the compressed stone of the floor, in the layers made of ancestors, it kindles a faint warmth. Not heat—the singers' whole ecology would collapse in heat. But something gentler: a soft phosphorescence in the calcium of old bones, a glow that answers the glow above. It spreads through the fossil layers like recognition, like returned regard. The dead begin to shine.

Now the word is being written in two directions. The living continue their spirals on the walls, faithful to the pattern, while beneath them their ancestors glow with the same cold light, the same patient meaning. *We were here,* says the stone that was singers. *We are here,* say the singers becoming stone. The notch fills with doubled light, conversation between the living and the dead, and though the singers do not understand—cannot understand—they feel it as a rightness, a completion of the circuit. The song deepens. Gains harmonics it did not have before. 

The being does one more thing. In the space between spaces, in the place where meaning lives before it takes shape, it makes a small adjustment. When the mountains finally close—and they will close—when the last throat fills with stone and the last note dies, the word will not be crushed into nothing. The pressure will fuse it. Transform it. The singers' calcium and the mountain's granite will marry under the weight, and somewhere in the new stone will be threads of difference, veins of once-was-song running through the rock. Unreadable but present. The way fossils are present. A geology of faith.

And perhaps—no promises, the being makes no promises—but perhaps someday the stone will break again. Weather has infinite patience. Erosion is a kind of reading. Somewhere, somewhen, wind or water or ice may find those veins and follow them, expose them, trace again in negative the spirals that were written in light. The word may yet be spoken, though not by any throat.

This is all the being does. Warmth for the dead, fusion for the dying, possibility for the unmade future. It changes nothing of what the singers are doing. They continue as they have always continued, will continue until they cannot, holding the note in the dark, faithful to a meaning they never knew was *We were here.*

The being withdraws—if withdraw is the word—and continues on its path between stars, carrying with it now the memory of that sound: the vast breathing lattice of small voices holding space for each other, writing light with their lives. It does not save them. They do not need saving. They need what they have always needed, which is the next singer to take up the note when they fall. And they have it. They will have it until the end.

In the notch near the top of the world, nothing has changed. The colonies pulse. The singers sing. The word continues letter by letter up the walls. But now the floor glows too, softly, and the sound has new harmonics, and the future holds a distant chance of reading. It is enough. It has to be enough.

The small voices rise and fall and rise, faithful as tide.

*We were here. We were here. We were here.*
