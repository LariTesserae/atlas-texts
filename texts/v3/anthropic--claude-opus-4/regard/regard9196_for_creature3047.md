The benevolent power—let us call it the Tender of Forgotten Gardens—arrives not as light or warmth, but as a new quality of listening. It does not disturb the roar of ten thousand trickles. Instead, it becomes the space between each droplet's impact, the pause that was always there but never noticed.

The Tender understands immediately: this place is perfect. Not beautiful, not good, not evil—*perfect*. Like a equation that has solved itself, like a circle that has closed. To change it would be violence.

But the Tender also understands something the Last Choir cannot: perfection is a kind of death.

So it does something very small.

In the absolute center of the Under-Rafters, where one of the Choir sometimes stands to gather the tears, the Tender places a gift. Not an object—objects would be rejected, incorporated, or carved away. Instead, it leaves a *possibility*.

From now on, once every thousand years, when one of the Choir performs the Returning of Tears, there is a chance—just a chance—that one droplet will fall *upward*.

Not far. Perhaps a finger's width. Perhaps less. The droplet will hover for the space of a heartbeat, trembling like a question mark made of water, before falling back down to join its siblings.

The first time this happens, the Choir member who witnesses it will freeze. The anomaly will propagate through their mineral memory like a crack in stone. They will check every calculation, every angle, every surface tension. They will find nothing wrong. The droplet simply *chose* to rise.

Some of them will try to forget. They will polish that section of floor with unusual dedication, as if they could scrub away the memory. But mineral memory does not degrade. The knowledge of that single upward droplet will haunt them like a ghost in the limestone.

Others will begin to wonder. If one droplet can choose to rise, what else might choose? What other intentions might the stone be harboring? They will listen more carefully to the roar, searching for variations they never thought to hear before.

And a very few—perhaps only one or two across the millennia—will begin to experiment. Tiny, careful adjustments. What if this column were angled just so? What if this lamp flame were allowed to flicker, just once? What if the temperature rose to 4.1 degrees, just for an hour?

The place will resist, of course. The Under-Rafters knows what it is and intends to remain so. But now there is a seed of something else. Not chaos—the Tender would never be so cruel. Just the faintest suggestion that perfection might not be the only possible ending.

The Tender leaves no other trace. It does not linger to watch what happens. That would be presumptuous. It simply moves on, knowing that it has given the only gift worth giving to a perfect system:

A single imperfect moment.
A question where there were only answers.
The possibility—nothing more—that after ten million years of being exactly what they are, the Under-Rafters and its Choir might one day wonder what else they could become.

The droplet rises and falls.
The wondering begins.
