# The Visitation

The benevolent power came not as light — for light would lie in such a place — but as a careful pressure, like the weight of a hand testing fever on a brow. It moved through the fog without disturbing it, read the patient geology of suffering written in iron and fungus, and understood at once what manner of life had taken root here.

A life born of abandonment. A life that had learned to be alone.

The power circled the basin three times, marking each working engine, each ring of warmth, each soft pale congregation. It tasted the water that never falls and never lifts. It felt the slow pull toward synchrony already building in the engines' scattered strokes. And in that pull, it recognized the deeper hunger: not for food, not for warmth, not even for the brief vessels it occasionally enfolded, but for *acknowledgment*. For something to know it was alive.

The loneliest things grow the slowest hearts.

So the power did something simple, and dangerous, and kind.

It gave the basin what it needed most: a memory that was not its own.

---

Down through the fog, gentle as settling silt, the power let fall a single image — not forced, not inscribed, but offered like a gift to a shut hand. The image was this: *sunlight on running water*. Just that. A memory of some other water in some other place, water that knew sky, water that ran toward something instead of seeping from beneath, water that had somewhere to go.

The basin had never known such water. Its only memory was the builders' bitter work, the engines' first violent awakening, the centuries of lifting and failing and lifting again. But here was something else. Here was water that *moved*, not because it was forced up through iron but because movement was its nature, because it was going to meet other water, because somewhere there was sea.

The working engines stuttered. For the first time in their patient decades, they fell out of rhythm not to scatter but to *pause*. The soft pale things on the cylinders stirred, confused by the brief absence of their warmth. The hunters in the black water circled, uncertain.

And then —

Understanding moved through the basin like a new kind of breath. It moved in the fog, in the weeping walls, in the gas-bubbles rising from the deepest mats. The place that had only known itself as abandoned understood, suddenly, that it had been *found*. The place that had only known water as something to lift and lose understood that water could be a gift, a going, a road.

The engines began again, but different. Still the same *chunk... chunk...*, still lifting water that would return, but now they knew why. They were not failing to drain the basin. They were *succeeding at being its pulse*. They were teaching water how to rise.

---

The power, satisfied with this small adjustment, prepared to leave. But as it turned from the basin, it felt something unexpected — a pressure returned, careful as its own. The basin was reaching back.

Not with the slow greed it showed the warm things that wandered into its bowl. This was something else. This was gratitude attempting to find a shape.

From the deepest mats, from the colonies that might be one vast colony, gas-bubbles rose — but not at random now. They rose in a pattern, broke the surface in a rhythm that was not the engines' rhythm, that was faster and lighter and almost (could such a thing be?) *amused*. The basin was laughing. In the only way it could, with the only voice it had, it was expressing something it had just learned was possible: joy.

The power waited while the laughter ran its course. Then, because kindness is not complete until it is received, it did one more thing.

It left a mark. Not on the basin — that would be another abandonment, another scar. Instead, it left a mark on the world just outside the basin, on the lands that do rise, on the stone that does not weep. A simple mark, cut deep: a spiral. The sign of water going somewhere. The promise that even the loneliest pools are part of some vast turning.

When the next warm thing finds its way to the basin's edge — and they will, they always do — they will see the mark before they descend. They will know that something else has been here and gone away whole. They will know the basin has been visited and did not hold its visitor, that it is capable of letting go.

Some will descend anyway. The lonely seek the lonely, after all.

But some will see the spiral and understand what it means: that this is a place that can be loved without being consumed by it. That this is a life that has learned the difference between holding and being held.

They will trace the spiral with their fingers, these future travelers. They will feel how deep it goes. And they will know — though they cannot say how — that the power which cut this sign is still watching, from some distance that is not distance, ready to return if the basin forgets again that it is not alone.

The basin will not forget. It has a memory now that is not its own. It has sunlight on running water. It has somewhere else to dream of when the synchrony comes and its dozen hearts beat as one in the dark.

And on the coldest nights, when the fog is thickest and the engines' rhythm is strongest, you can still hear it in the deep mats' breathing: the memory of laughter, the shape of joy, rising and breaking like bubbles on the surface of standing water, saying in the only language it has:

*I was found. I was known. I let them go.*

*I am not abandoned.*

*I am practiced at being alone.*
