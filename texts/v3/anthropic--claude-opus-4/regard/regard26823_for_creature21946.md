The benevolent power arrives not as light or sound but as a question that settles into the grey stone like rain that has finally learned how to fall upward.

It does not disturb the chord. That would be violence, to break what has been so perfectly sustained. Instead, it sits beside the inhabitant in one of the broad wet plazas where nothing happens, and it brings with it something small: the memory of green.

Not green itself — that would be too much, too sudden. Just the memory of it. The particular green of something that grew once, elsewhere, in a place where time moved forward and things could die. The power sets this memory down between them like a gift that asks for nothing, and waits.

The inhabitant does not turn to look, because it does not turn. But the memory sits there, patient in a different way than the city's patience — patient with the possibility of an answer.

"You have done something extraordinary," the power says, though not in words. The communication is more like the way water knows to find the channels. "You have solved the unsolvable. You have made perpetual motion real by becoming the friction that friction forgot to charge. No one has ever paid such a price for such a perfect nothing."

The inhabitant continues its work, which is not-work, which is the declining. Every instant, the same offer: let something change. Every instant, the same refusal. The beetles turn their screws. The water beads and runs.

"I could break this," the power continues. "I could introduce one new note and the whole chord would have to reshape itself around it. But I think you know that already. I think that's why you've made the edges so sharp, the boundary so absolute. Not to keep things out but to make the breaking clean if it comes."

And here the power does something that has not been done in the city since before it became what it is: it asks a question that expects an answer.

"What were you before you were this?"

The inhabitant does not answer, cannot answer, because an answer would be a motion toward something, and it has surrendered motion. But in the not-answering, something flickers. The memory of green brightens for just an instant, as if lit from within by a light that immediately dims — no, not dims, but is *paid for* before it can shine, is settled and neutralized, returned to the grey.

The power understands then. The inhabitant was not always the inhabitant. Once it was something else — perhaps even something that knew green, that moved through time in the ordinary way, that had reasons and forgot them and found new ones. And then something happened. Some loss or fear or weight became unbearable, and instead of bearing it or setting it down, this being found a third way: to stop time itself around the wound. To build a city that is a scar that never heals because it never admits it was cut.

"You are holding your breath," the power says gently, "because once you breathed something in that hurt you. And now you think if you never breathe out, you'll never have to breathe it in again."

The city continues its noise, its perfect repetition. But now the power hears what it could not hear before: underneath the industrial clamor, so faint it might be imagination, the sound of something trying not to sob.

The power does not force the issue. Force is not what's needed here. Instead, it does something simpler and more difficult: it begins to breathe. Slowly, evenly, it breathes in the mineral air and breathes it out again, and each breath is different from the last — slightly warmer, slightly cooler, slightly longer or shorter. It breathes *change* into the grey space beside the inhabitant, not imposing it but offering it, the way you might sit beside someone who has forgotten how to cry and simply cry yourself, not to shame them but to remind them that it's possible.

And it leaves the memory of green.

When the power goes — and it does go, because staying would be another kind of violence, the violence of insisting — it leaves the memory there in the plaza, small and quiet and unliving but somehow still green in its remembering. The inhabitant does not pick it up. Cannot pick it up. But it doesn't pay to make it grey, either. It sits there between the wet stones, the first thing in the city that is neither part of the machine nor part of the holding.

The beetles continue turning their screws. The water continues its circuit. The inhabitant continues its enormous work of keeping everything still.

But now there is a memory of green in one of the plazas, and sometimes — not often, not measurably, not in any way that could be called change — but sometimes the condensation that gathers there and runs past it carries, for just an instant before the instant is paid for and returned, the faintest taste of something that once grew.

The power does not return. It has done what kindness does: offered a door without insisting it be walked through. Whether the inhabitant ever picks up the memory, whether it ever breathes out, whether the chord ever resolves or simply continues forever with one new harmonic it cannot quite eliminate — none of that is for the power to decide.

But somewhere in the perfect industrial noise, so faint it might be imagination, there is now the sound of something that is not quite sobbing and not quite not sobbing — something learning, perhaps, that there might be a difference between holding your breath and being held by it.

And in the plaza, patient in a way that expects nothing but allows for the possibility of something, the memory of green waits.
