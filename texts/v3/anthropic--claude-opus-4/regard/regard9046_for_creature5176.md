The benevolent power approaches not as wind or light, but as a subtle shift in the crystalline structure of the quartzite itself. Deep within the packed grains, a resonance begins—not sound, but something older, the same frequency that once taught water how to remember its way through stone.

In the pecked basin, where human hands once worked with patient repetition, the morning's rime forms differently. Instead of evaporating by mid-morning, it persists, and in persisting, begins to accumulate knowledge. Each droplet that condenses carries with it the memory of its journey—from distant ocean to cloud to mist to this precise point of contact. The basin becomes a library of water's autobiography, written in frost.

The power recognizes what others might miss: this is already a place of deep practice. The circular patience of freeze and thaw, the meditation of fracture lines extending by microns each year, the profound commitment of stone to its own slow transformation. These are not empty processes but a form of contemplation so pure it needs no consciousness to complete it.

So the power does not impose life where none belongs. Instead, it offers the cirque a gift aligned with its nature: the capacity to remember with perfect fidelity. Each grain that fractures free will carry within its crystal lattice the complete record of its time in the wall—every temperature shift, every angle of light, every rare touch of moisture. When wind lifts these grains and carries them beyond the rim, they will seed the world below with the cirque's patience, teaching rushing water and restless soil the deep time they have forgotten.

The human traces receive their own form of grace. The ring of stones, rather than simply persisting, begins to function as a subtle lens. It focuses the cirque's quality of silence, concentrating it like a prayer wheel that turns without moving. Anyone who comes here again—in a hundred years, a thousand—will find within that ring a stillness so complete it stills the mind that encounters it. The angular flakes, with their readable platforms, become texts in a language that predates speech: each one encoding the precise gesture that created it, the intention of the hand that struck it free.

But perhaps the power's wisest gift is this: it changes nothing visible. The cirque continues its cycles exactly as before. Condensation arrives, forms, evaporates. Fractures extend their patient paths. The silence remains unbroken. Yet now these processes carry an additional dimension—not purpose, which would be too human a word, but a kind of cosmic attention. The universe has noticed this small chamber of stone and marked it as significant: a place where the profound practice of being exactly what one is has reached a kind of perfection.

In the wider wheel of existence, the cirque becomes a teaching. Not through any action or message, but through the completeness of its commitment to its own nature. The power ensures that this teaching will endure, written in the language of mineral and fracture, until the mountains themselves forget their names.

And in the shallow concavities where condensation gathers, something new but ancient begins. The mineral rings—those faint calendars of daily process—start to align with larger cycles. They record not just the day's small tide but the breathing of seasons, the pulse of centuries. In time, someone with eyes to read such things might decipher in these rings the history not just of this cirque but of all places where stone and water and time converge in perfect, empty dedication.

The power withdraws as quietly as it came, leaving only this: a place that was already holy has been recognized as such, and in that recognition, made eternal.
