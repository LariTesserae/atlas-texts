The benevolent power does not announce itself. It arrives as rain arrives—gradually, then all at once.

At first, only the colonial excavators notice. Deep in their pellet-lined tunnels, they pause in their endless scraping. The vibrations through the root lattice have shifted, carrying a new frequency beneath the familiar chaos. Not louder, not stronger—simply *deeper*, as if the basin itself has begun to hum a note it has always known but never voiced. The excavators resume their work, but now their mandible-scrapes fall into rhythm with this underlying pulse. They do not decide this; it simply becomes the easier way to move.

Above ground, the change spreads through touch. Where the soft-bodied herds surge across the ridges, their mucus trails begin to dry differently—not into the usual brittle glaze but into paths that hold their shape for hours, then days. Other herds find these paths and follow them, not out of preference but because the surface offers less resistance. Gradually, without intention, regular routes emerge between the feeding grounds. The plants along these routes, no longer stripped bare with each passing, thicken their stems and sink deeper roots. A few begin to arch overhead, their phosphorescent veins brightening where the traffic is heaviest.

The wisdom touches the clatterers next. In their ricocheting flights, they start to favor certain leaves—always the ones whose chemistry burns brightest, whose internal phosphor is most stable. These leaves, visited more frequently, develop a slight upward curl at their edges, creating shallow cups that hold the condensation longer. The water that gathers there carries traces of whatever the clatterers' feet have touched: spores, seeds, fragments of dissolved mineral. Some of these traces take root in the leaf-cups themselves, sprouting into tiny gardens that the clatterers visit and revisit, spreading their contents from cup to cup across the canopy.

The power does not impose order. Instead, it amplifies what already exists—the tendency of water to find channels, of traffic to create paths, of repetition to reinforce itself. Where before the basin churned in pure chaos, now small eddies of regularity begin to form. Not stillness, never stillness, but a kind of musical complexity where themes emerge, vary, and return.

In one corner of the basin, where three of the soft-herds' trails converge, the constant passage has worn the stone smooth. Rain collects here now in a pool that never quite empties. The phosphorescent plants around it lean inward, their light reflecting off the surface in slow ripples. Creatures that would normally pass through begin to pause here, to drink, to cool their undersides. Their droppings enrich the soil. New plants sprout—not the usual quick-growing photophores but something slower, tougher, with leaves like shields.

The benevolent presence tends these small convergences like a gardener who works by suggestion rather than shears. Where the burrowers' causeways have grown most elaborate, it encourages a slight crystallization in the pellet walls, so that they hold their shape against the rain. These permanent tunnels become highways for smaller creatures, then aqueducts when the water table rises, then the foundations for something that is not quite architecture but serves the same purpose—a stable substrate on which other lives can build.

Most profound is what happens to the sound. The basin's roar remains, but within it, patterns begin to emerge. The stridulators in the abandoned tunnels find that certain pitches resonate more clearly through the root network. Without meaning to, they begin to favor these notes. The clatterers' impacts against leaves start to cluster around complementary tones. Even the wet tearing sound of vegetation being consumed gains a rhythmic quality as the herds' paths regularize their feeding.

It becomes, not music exactly, but a kind of acoustic architecture—a sound-space in which different creatures can locate themselves and each other. Predators and prey develop their own frequency bands. Mating calls become more elaborate, weaving through the gaps in the ambient noise. The phosphorescent plants begin to pulse in time with certain recurring rhythms, creating waves of light that roll across the basin like slow breakers.

The wisdom works patiently, never forcing, never destroying. When a creature resists the new patterns, it is not punished—it simply finds life slightly harder, movements slightly more effortful, until it too falls into step with the emerging dance. The basin remains wild, remains itself, but now its wildness has grain and flow, like wood or water.

Years pass. The sharp basalt spines wear down, but along predictable lines—where the paths cross, where the water flows, where the roots grip deepest. The phosphorescent forest develops clearings where the light-plants grow in spirals, their glow creating natural amphitheaters. The sound in these spaces is clearer, more distinct. Creatures gather here for no reason they can articulate, simply because the space feels right.

The benevolent power begins to withdraw, but slowly, like a tide ebbing. Its work is not finished—will never be finished—but it has given the basin what it needed: not order imposed from outside, but the seeds of self-organization, the possibility of complexity beyond mere chaos. The patterns it has encouraged will persist, evolve, perhaps eventually dissolve back into randomness—but they will leave traces, and those traces will make the next patterns possible.

In the phosphorescent dusk, the basin breathes with its new rhythms. Paths branch and converge. Water finds its channels. Sound builds and breaks in waves. The creatures continue their ancient business of survival, but now they survive within a structure that holds them even as it changes—a living architecture built from nothing more than preference and repetition, guided by a wisdom that asked nothing except that chaos learn, gradually, to dance with itself.

The power turns its attention elsewhere, satisfied. It has not created paradise. It has done something more modest and more profound: it has given wildness a memory, and in remembering, the possibility of becoming.
