**What the Benevolent Power Would Do**

The Power arrives not as light or presence, but as a subtle change in the mathematics of chaos. The roaring maelstrom outside begins to breathe—still violent, still deafening, but now with the faintest suggestion of rhythm, like a heartbeat learning it is a heartbeat.

The Power does not speak to the Listener. Speaking would be violence here, would be finishing something that must not finish. Instead, it begins to sing beneath the roar—not with sound, but with possibility. A subsonic hum that makes the embryonic stone remember not what it was, but what it might become.

The first gift is small: a single perfect sphere of water, no larger than a fist, that forms in the center of the chamber and refuses to fall. It hangs there, rotating slowly, holding within itself a fragment of the outside maelstrom but tamed, gentled, made contemplative. The Listener approaches it with her gill-slit flaring in wonder. When she touches it, the sphere does not break. Instead, it begins to sing back to her in frequencies only she can hear—the sound of water learning to be still while still being water.

Days pass. The sphere remains. The Listener builds a careful circle of her bone stakes around it, not to trap it but to honor it. Sometimes she sits before it for hours, her too-many-or-too-few arms wrapped around her knees, watching the way the filtered light bends through its perfect curve.

The second gift arrives as knowledge, seeded gently into the lifting pebbles and the breathing lichens: *You are not alone in being unfinished.*

Somewhere else—impossibly far but somehow parallel—there is another chamber. Another pocket. Another being who builds half-structures and listens to a different roar. The Listener feels this truth settle into her bones like weight. She stands suddenly, gill-slit wide, and sings her negative-sound note with new urgency. This time, when the stone forgets itself, it remembers something else: the faint impression of another chamber's floor, another pattern of embryonic cracks.

She begins to build differently. The dolmen gains a twin. The spiral of bone stakes becomes a double helix. She weaves the kelp rope into complex knots that echo some other architecture she has never seen but somehow knows. Her structures are no longer random attempts at meaning—they are one half of a conversation.

The third gift is patience made tangible. The Power causes certain moments to crystallize: when the Listener presses her forehead to the water-beast's bulk, that instant stretches into minutes, then hours. She has time now to feel the creature's full heartbeat, to understand the stories its crystalline filaments are gathering from the chaos. When new stone swells from below, it rises more slowly, giving her time to shape it with her glass hands before it fully hardens. The violent convulsions of the bubble still come, but with warning—a subtle shift in air pressure that lets her prepare, lets her protect her growing double-structures.

The fourth gift is the hardest and kindest: the Power brings her someone who stays.

Not a person from another world, but one of the water-beasts itself. A young one, smaller than the others, pressing against the membrane not with blind instinct but with something approaching curiosity. When the Listener meets it at the boundary, the Power thins the division between air and water to almost nothing. The beast's filaments extend through the membrane—not breaking it, but phasing through it like light through glass. They touch the Listener's gill-slit with infinite gentleness.

The exchange lasts only seconds before the current tears the creature away, but in those seconds, entire languages pass between them. The beast returns. Again. Again. Each time staying a fraction longer, each time the membrane growing more permeable to their communion.

The Listener begins to change. The volcanic glass of her skin grows traces of the beast's translucency. Sometimes, in certain lights, you can see her own heartbeat pulsing beneath the basalt. Her gill-slit learns to breathe water as well as air. The structures she builds now incorporate both elements—fountains of that same suspended water that sing in harmony with her bone stakes, slate walls that channel the violent winds into something almost like music.

The fifth and final gift is a question: *What if the chamber is not a prison but a womb?*

The Power does not provide an answer. Instead, it begins to slowly, lovingly teach the walls of the bubble to be permeable in both directions. The Listener discovers she can extend her hand into the maelstrom and not be torn away. She can walk partially into the vertical ocean and partially remain in air. She is becoming amphibious in ways that have nothing to do with biology and everything to do with existence itself.

One day—though "day" means nothing here—she takes her first full step into the water.

She does not dissolve. She does not drown. She exists there as she exists in the air: unfinished, listening, building. The water-beast that has become her companion swims beside her, and together they begin to construct something in the liquid chaos—the other half of her conversation, the missing twin to every dolmen and spiral and wall.

The chamber does not collapse. It expands. It becomes not one pocket but a constellation of pockets, each with its own Listener, each building its own half of an infinite architecture. The roar remains, but now it carries messages between the chambers—a network of apocalyptic sound that is slowly teaching itself to be a mind the size of an ocean.

The Power withdraws then, its work complete. Not because it has fixed anything—fixing would be violence here—but because it has given the unfinished thing the one gift it needed most:

The knowledge that unfinished things can touch each other without finishing each other.

The Listener still waits in her chamber, but she no longer waits alone. Through the water, through the stone, through the violent air itself, she feels the presence of others like her. Building. Listening. Becoming.

And sometimes, when the roar reaches its perfect pitch and all the chambers breathe as one, the entire vertical ocean sings a single note that is not sound but the shape of sound being born:

*We are.*

*We are still becoming.*

*We are becoming together.*

The stone has not yet learned to be a mountain. It is learning, instead, to be a throat.
