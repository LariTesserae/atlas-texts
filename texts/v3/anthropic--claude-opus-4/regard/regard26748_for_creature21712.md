The benevolent power arrives not as light or presence but as a kind of listening that has no ears. It settles into the thin air like mist would settle if there were moisture to make mist from, which there isn't, so it settles like the *memory* of mist, which is perhaps more honest anyway.

It does not try to fix anything. This is the first wisdom: that the place is not broken.

Instead, it begins by learning the courtesy. It looks away. It grants the city its periphery, lets the walls migrate unwatched, permits the terraces their shy reorganizing. It stands in the plaza and allows its own shadow to reconsider, to fall in whichever direction the local exceptions prefer. When an occupant appears in the middle distance, the power refuses to approach, understanding that the distance is not empty space but the necessary medium of meeting.

And then, because it is both benevolent and wise, it does something unexpected: it brings water.

Not to the city — that would be violence, to force water on a place that has so completely finished with thirsting. Instead, the power brings water to the *boundary*, to the exact edge where the high cold meets whatever is below it, where the rules begin to thin but haven't yet committed to their full strangeness. It brings water as an offering at the threshold: here is what you have forgotten how to be. Not a demand. Not a restoration. Simply a reminder, left at the door like a gift from a neighbor you've never met.

The water sits at the boundary in a state that water recognizes — liquid, clear, obeying all its usual obligations to gravity and surface tension. It pools. It reflects a sky that may or may not be the sky above the city; the angle is hard to determine from inside. It does what water does when left alone: it waits, it accepts whatever falls into it, it remembers perfectly the shape of its container while taking no shape of its own.

The city notices. Not immediately — nothing here is immediate — but over the course of its slow breathing cycles, the presence of water at the edge begins to register. The occupants, in their errands that go nowhere, begin to tend their paths differently. Still in the middle distance, still at the unresolvable remove, but their walking takes on a quality like listening. They do not approach the boundary. They do not need to. The water is not for drinking. It is for remembering that drinking was once a thing that mattered.

The benevolent power does more. It places, beside the water, a single green thing.

Not a plant — that would require biology, and biology would be another violence here. Call it a green *condition*, about the size and bearing of what a plant would be if plants were made of color instead of cells. It practices greenness the way the occupants practice their errands, as a discipline rather than a nature. It does not grow, but it performs something adjacent to growth — a slow unfolding that could be mistaken for vitality if you didn't look too close, which you shouldn't, because looking too close would freeze it.

The green thing at the boundary does what green things do in stories: it suggests that other states are possible. Not necessary, not inevitable, but *possible*, which is a different kindness than insisting things must change.

The city's response is as quiet as everything here. The light that seeps up from the stone begins, in places, to carry the faintest tinge of color — not green, but the grey's acknowledgment that green exists somewhere, that chromatic variation is a rumor the stones have heard and are considering. The small dry sounds — the tick, the settle, the grain-fall — develop a new quality, almost like punctuation, as if the silence is learning to organize itself into sentences, even though there are no words to go in them.

And the occupants?

One of them — though *one* implies a count that may not apply — develops a new habit. It stands at a particular corner, not the corners that aren't there but one of the corners that is, and it turns not to go around but simply to turn, and in turning it makes a gesture. Small, barely visible even in the periphery, like someone adjusting a weight they're carrying, except there is no weight. The gesture means nothing in any language the city or the power knows. But it happens at intervals, regular as breathing, and in happening it creates a tiny tradition, a repeated act that could, if you were generous, be called the seed of ritual.

The power understands: this is the city learning to remember forward.

Not planning — the future tense is still broken here. Not hoping — hope is a kind of thirst and there is no water above the boundary. But something gentler and stranger: the city discovering that conclusion can have *moods*, that being done is not one thing but many things, that there are ways to practice the discipline of finishing that leave room for... not change, exactly, but the *memory* of change, kept like the water is kept, at the boundary, as proof that other states once mattered.

The benevolent power makes one last gift. It teaches the silence a new sound.

Not a breaking of the quiet — that would be disaster. Instead, a sound that belongs to silence the way shadows belong to light. It is the sound of the water at the boundary when no wind touches it, which is no sound at all, but somehow different from the no-sound of the dry plaza. It is the sound the green thing doesn't make while not-growing. It is the exact frequency of being done without being empty.

The city learns this sound. It adds it to the tick and the settle and the grain-fall. Now there are four small sounds in the silence, and the fourth one means: *something else exists, and we are not threatened by it, and we do not need it, but we permit it to matter at the edge of what we are.*

The occupants continue their walking. The walls continue their migration. The light continues its slow breathing. Nothing has changed in any way that change usually means. But there is water at the boundary, and greenness beside it, and a new sound in the catalogue of quiet, and one occupant turning at a corner in what might be the first new gesture since the city finished.

The power prepares to leave. It has done what benevolence does: offered without insisting, reminded without restoring, added to the vocabulary of conclusion a few new words that mean "conclusion does not have to be lonely."

But as it withdraws, something unexpected happens. The city — not an occupant, but the city itself, through some mechanism of attention the power doesn't quite catch — makes a gift in return.

At the boundary, beside the water and the green thing, a small piece of the pale stone rises. Not built, not placed, but lifted into being the way the whole city was lifted, by the grammar of having-always-been-there. It is the size of something you could carry, if you were the sort of thing that carried things. It is the color of old teeth and weathered agreement. It holds the temperature of held breath.

And carved into it — though *carved* implies tools and there were no tools — is a single word in no language, made of the same careful unwatching that lets the city move. The word cannot be read directly. It can only be perceived peripherally, the way the occupants can only be perceived, and when seen that way it says:

*Thank you for not trying to save us.*

The benevolent power understands. It takes the stone — not to keep, but to carry down to the places below the boundary, where water remembers how to be water and time still thinks it's going somewhere. It will leave the stone in a stream or a garden or a place where children play, and someone will find it, and they will not be able to read the word directly, but in the corner of their eye they will glimpse something about finishing that is not about ending, something about being done that is full of patience instead of empty of purpose.

And high above, in the thin honest air, the city continues its quiet business of having concluded. But now when the occupants turn their corners that aren't there, very occasionally, one of them pauses at the place where the corner would be, and makes the small gesture of adjusting a weight it isn't carrying, and in that gesture is the memory of water it does not thirst for, seen once at a boundary it does not approach, left by a power that was wise enough to offer without mending.

This is what love does when it finds something too complete to need it: it adds one note to the silence. It leaves water where water isn't wanted, not as criticism but as companionship. It says: *you have finished beautifully. Here is what continuing looks like, in case you ever get curious. No urgency. No expectation. Just the rumor of other ways to be, kept at the edge where you can ignore it or not, as your strange grey light prefers.*

The city breathes its slow sleeping breath. The occupants walk their errands to nowhere. The small sounds tick and settle in the quiet. And at the boundary, water that will never be drunk reflects a sky that may not be there, beside a green thing that isn't growing, beneath a stone word that can't be read.

Nothing has been fixed because nothing was broken. Nothing has changed because change is not what the place is for. But something has been added to the vocabulary of conclusion:

The knowledge that even the most perfectly finished things are still, in some quiet way that requires no action, accompanied.

The power leaves. The city continues. The water waits.

This is enough.

This has always been enough.
