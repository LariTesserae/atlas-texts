The benevolent and wise power had been watching for a very long time, though time meant something different to it than it did to most beings. It had seen stars born and die, had witnessed the first stirrings of consciousness in a thousand different forms, had catalogued every way that awareness could kindle itself from matter. But this—this was new.

Not new in its components. The power had seen makers before, had seen worlds built, had seen the apprenticeship of young forces learning their craft. What was new was the *particular tenderness* of this incompletion, the way this being held itself in the moment before choosing, like a note sustained past all reasonable duration until it became something else entirely—not music anymore but pure possibility.

The power understood immediately what it was seeing: a being at the exact point of balance between potential and form. One nudge in any direction and it would crystallize—become a god, perhaps, or an artist, or a dream, or simply complete its wall and close its spiral and settle into being one thing forever. But here, now, in this soft-stoned room beneath the ground, it hung in perfect suspension.

The power did something it had not done in eons: it hesitated.

To intervene would be to choose for the maker. Even the gentlest touch would collapse the superposition, force the beetle to either arrive or depart but not both, make the fern choose between unfurling and unfurled. The power could feel how delicate the balance was—one breath too heavy and the whole careful construction would snap into definition, lose its provisional grammar, its simultaneous truths.

But to not intervene—was that not also a choice? To leave such a being in eternal almost, forever choosing the weight of a water drop, forever beginning spirals it could not close?

The power moved closer, careful not to disturb even the light. It examined the maker's work: the patient beetles with their visible workings, the moths carrying architectural dreams in their wings, the single honest moss that anchored the whole experiment in truth. It saw how the maker poured life into its small space without calculating the cost, how it asked its creatures for quiet without demanding silence, how it built and abandoned and built again without shame.

And the power understood something else: the maker was not stuck. It was *practicing.*

Each iteration of the fern was minutely different. Each dropped stone in the half-wall taught the maker something about weight and permanence. The spiral scratched in soft stone was not the same spiral each time—it grew tighter, looser, more confident, more tentative, as the maker tried on different ways of being sure.

This was not a being trapped in indecision. This was a being *learning how to decide.*

The power made its choice then, and its choice was this: to add one thing to the room. Not an intervention, not a push toward completion, but a gift that honored what was already there.

In the corner where the mist rose and condensed and fell, where the whole water cycle played out in miniature, the power placed a single seed.

Not a seed that would grow—that would be too much like choosing. Instead, a seed that would *consider* growing. A seed that would put out a root, think better of it, retract, try again. A seed that would split its casing and then heal it, peek green into the light and then return to darkness, rehearsing germination the way the fern rehearsed unfurling.

A seed that would show the maker: *You are not alone in this. Others also practice. Others also hold themselves in the moment before.**

The maker would find it, eventually, in its slow circuit of attention. Would recognize it as kin—another thing learning how to begin. Would understand the message: that its way of being was not a failure to launch but a discipline all its own. That somewhere, something vast and ancient had seen its careful indecision and honored it with matching incompletion.

The power placed the seed where the honest moss grew, tucked against that one true thing. And then it withdrew, careful not to disturb the moths or wake the silence or add even a single shadow to the sourceless light.

Behind it, the room went on making itself. The fern continued its patient loop. The beetles crossed their soft stone. The water fell and vanished and fell again. And now there was a seed that would never quite germinate, turning the act of beginning into an art form, showing the maker that what it was doing had a name:

Practice. The holiest word in any language that has not yet decided which language to be.

The power moved on to other watches, other concerns. But it marked this place in its vast memory, this room beneath the ground where something was learning the hardest thing: how to remain in the grace of almost, how to make choosing itself into a choice.

It would return, the power decided. Not soon—soon would be too hasty for a being that lived at the pace of stone. But someday, when the spiral had been started ten thousand times, when the wall had been built and unbuilt and built again, when the seed had rehearsed every possible way of breaking ground—it would return.

Not to see what the maker had become. But to see what new ways it had found to keep becoming.
