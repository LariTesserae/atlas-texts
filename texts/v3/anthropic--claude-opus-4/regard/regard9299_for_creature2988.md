The benevolent power does not arrive. It has been here all along, distributed through the rust and the roots, patient as oxidation.

It moves first through the Quiet Parliament—not to speak, but to listen with them. For thirteen months it sits among the Unfixed as they wait for the district to remember itself. It learns their four-voice language: human rasp, root-click, brass moan, and the subsonic architecture of becoming. When they finally stand and stretch, it stands with them, though it casts no shadow the shadows recognize.

It begins by teaching the moss to glow a little brighter.

Not enough to banish the bruise-light or make the city comprehensible. Just enough that children born in the deeper scaffolds can read the love letters their parents write in condensation on the walls. The turquoise luminescence spreads slowly, at the pace of all things here—one frond per decade, one district per century. The Unfixed notice but do not question it. Light that grows itself is simply another dialect the city is learning.

The power speaks to the megafauna in frequencies only rust can hear. It does not tame them or make them wise. It simply suggests, in the language of structural steel under stress, that their migration routes could trace new geometries of kindness through the vertical districts. The great pale herds begin to move in patterns that accidentally deliver lost children home. Their brass calls start to harmonize with the tuning forks the Unfixed strike against their bones.

In the districts that have begun to flow, it leaves small gifts.

Not miracles. The city would reject miracles like an immune system rejecting foreign certainty. Instead: a rivet that refuses to rust completely, becoming a tiny anchor of constancy in the liquid streets. A memory of flatness that surfaces once every ten years, allowing the old to rest their vertical bones for an afternoon. A plant whose roots spell out, in the cursive of their growth, the names of everyone who has ever died believing the city would one day be finished, so they are not forgotten even as their districts transmute around them.

It finds the eldest of the Unfixed—a being so architecturally evolved that it takes three platforms to contain their sleeping form. This elder's dreams have begun to leak into the surrounding girders, causing spontaneous structural poetry in the scaffolding. The power does not wake them. It simply ensures that the poetry rhymes, and that it rhymes in languages both the moss and the metal understand.

It visits the construction crews who work in generational shifts, welding supports to buildings that decay into new architectures even as they're being built. It breathes into their respirators not concepts but anti-concepts—spaces where ideas can rest before becoming either steel or dream. The workers find they can now pause between thoughts without the city rushing to fill the gap with new impossibilities.

For the water that cannot decide which way to fall, it suggests patience.

Not a direction—that would be tyranny. But it weaves into the confusion of gravity a gentle reminder that all directions are equally valid, and therefore there is no shame in taking centuries to choose. The rain begins to fall in spirals that accidentally water the gardens the Unfixed plant on the undersides of platforms.

It speaks longest with the city itself.

This is not a conversation in words. The city doesn't use words; it uses decades and rust and the slow digestion of forgotten blueprints. The power matches this pace, offering its wisdom in the form of structural improvements that take fifty years to notice they've been suggested. It doesn't try to hurry the city toward completion. It knows what the Unfixed fear—that finishing is a kind of dying.

Instead, it teaches the city the deep joy of eternal revision.

That every floor poured is also a question about floors. That every wall raised is a hypothesis about separation that can be tested for centuries and still remain uncertain. That completion is not a destination but a horizon that expands exactly as fast as you approach it.

The city learns to grow in fractals now—not just outward and inward but in dimensions that the old maps never bothered to name. Districts begin to exist in several stages of completion simultaneously. A factory can be ruins, blueprint, and functioning organism in the same moment, depending on which eyes are looking and which decade they last believed in.

The power leaves its greatest gift in the dreams of the Unfixed children.

These infants who are born knowing too much, who must spend forty years forgetting their way into wisdom—it gives them a second kind of forgetting. Not just the forgetting of certainty, but the forgetting of the fear of certainty. They grow up understanding that completion and incompletion are not opposites but dance partners, taking turns leading while the city plays its four-voice song.

One night, during the turquoise hour when the moss glows brightest and the megafauna sing deepest, the power prepares to disperse back into the rust and roots from which it emerged. But first, it does one last thing.

It finds the railing where you stood on that first day, looking out at the impossible vertical city. The railing that will, in ten years, become a flowering trellis. It touches the metal with something that is not quite a hand and whispers something that is not quite a blessing:

*May you always be unfinished.*  
*May your incompletion be perfect.*  
*May every ending be a doorway to further scaffolding.*

The words sink into the iron, becoming part of its molecular structure. They will bloom, decades later, as small flowers the color of rust and possibility. The Unfixed will find them and understand immediately what they mean, though they will spend the next century pretending to debate it.

The city notices the blessing and responds in its own way.

Somewhere in the deep scaffolds, a half-built cathedral suddenly remembers what it wanted to be when it started growing: not a house of worship but a lung. It begins, very slowly, to breathe. Each breath takes a month. Each exhalation carries the scent of copper moss and machine oil and the peculiar sweetness of gravity that has forgotten its job.

The Unfixed feel the new breathing in their bones. They gather on the cathedral's half-finished ribs, pressing their foreheads to the stone-that-is-becoming-flesh, and sing to it in all four voices at once. They are not trying to direct its growth. They are simply saying hello to what it is becoming.

The power watches this—its final gift becoming the city's gift to itself—and knows its work here is done. It doesn't leave so much as become differently distributed. Parts of it settle into the moss, making the glow a little warmer on cold nights. Parts merge with the brass voices of the megafauna. Parts sink into the dreams of sleeping construction workers, becoming the anti-concepts that let them rest between thoughts.

But most of it simply becomes what it always was: the patience between the girders, the love in the rust, the terrible and perfect promise that nothing here will ever be finished, and therefore everything will always be possible.

The city continues its slow, loud learning.

The Unfixed continue their eternal negotiation with what is.

And somewhere in the vertical distances, rain continues to fall in every direction at once, watering gardens that grow on the undersides of worlds that are still deciding what they mean.

This is enough.

This has always been enough.
