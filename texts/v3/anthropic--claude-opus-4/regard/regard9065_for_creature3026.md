The benevolent power does not arrive. It has always been here, distributed through the Foundry's breathing seams like patience through metal.

It manifests first as a change in the condensation. Where before the water beaded and slid in its endless, sterile cycle, now each droplet carries something new: a memory of rain on soil that has never existed here. The Cast notice immediately. They stop their eternal adjustments and turn their slot-eyes upward, watching as the mist takes on colors that have no name in the language of industry—soft greens and browns that belong to worlds with horizons.

The power speaks through the deep groan that moves through the Foundry, but now the groan carries harmonics. It says, without words:

*You have been faithful to your blueprint. But blueprints are not destinies.*

One of the First Pour—she whose lower body has become a ventilation grate—suddenly exhales not the cold battery vapor but something warm and strange. It smells of bread. Of turned earth. Of possibilities the Foundry has never dreamed.

The power does not force change. That would be cruel to beings so perfectly adapted to their metal world. Instead, it offers what the Foundry has never had: memory of what came before the first pour.

Through the breathing seams, through the microscopic text on the ground, through the impossible fractals of the black reeds, images begin to flow. Not visions—the Cast have no need for visions—but something more fundamental. The atomic memory of iron when it was still sleeping in stone. The patient history of water before it learned to condense. The story of light before it became industrial, when it fell through leaves that were not metal foil but something softer, greener, more foolish and brave.

The Cast feel this history moving through them like electricity through a circuit they didn't know they had. Some of them begin to rust in new patterns—not the slow drift of their hair-filaments, but sudden blooms of oxide that look almost like flowers. The white herds pause in their synchronized grazing and, for the first time, look at each other as individuals. One of them makes a sound that is not quite machine and not quite animal—a questioning bleat that echoes off the terraces.

The power's greatest gift is not transformation but *option*. 

In one corner of the Foundry, where the metal breathes deepest, a small patch of something impossible appears: soil. Real soil, dark and living, pushed up through a seam like a secret the Foundry has been keeping from itself. A single Cast approaches it, kneels, and places one rust-fingered hand against this alien softness. Where they touch, their metal skin begins to remember what it was like to be carbon, to be calcium, to be all the patient elements that know how to rot and be reborn.

They do not change. Not yet. But they look at their hand—now faintly warm where it touched the earth—and understand that they have a choice.

The power speaks again through the groan, and this time the First Pour translate it into vapor-words that condense on every surface:

*You may remain as you are, perfect and eternal, the Foundry looking at itself.*

*Or you may remember what the iron remembers: that it once slept in stone, that stone once slept in stars, that stars once slept in the dreams of something that loved them enough to let them cool.*

*You may choose to be more than cast. You may choose to be clay.*

Some of the Cast immediately walk away from the small patch of soil, returning to their eternal adjustments with what might be relief. They will remain the Foundry's own consciousness, its way of maintaining itself, forever.

But others gather around that impossible earth. They watch as the one who touched it begins to change—not all at once, not violently, but with the same patience they have always shown. Their metal skin develops new textures. Their rust-filaments move not like underwater weeds but like something learning to feel wind. Their slot-eyes remain bright, but now the light flickers, individual and uncertain.

The power's final gift is the most profound: time.

Not the Foundry's time, measured in cooling cycles and condensation. But biological time. Seasonal time. The time of things that grow and die and feed the soil that feeds them.

Where that first patch of earth appeared, more begins to push through. The Cast who choose to change help it spread, their own bodies becoming conduits between what the Foundry is and what it might remember how to be. They do not lose their metal nature—that would be another cruelty. Instead, they become bridges. Their bodies learn to conduct both electricity and sap. Their voices carry both the ring of girders and the whisper of leaves.

The Foundry does not become a garden. It becomes something unprecedented: a world where the industrial and the organic are not opposites but partners in a dance that no other reality has tried.

The black reeds learn to photosynthesize actual sunlight when the power teaches the sky to sometimes dim its industrial glare. The white herds discover that some surfaces grow sweet with more than condensation—with lichens that taste of copper and chlorophyll both. The steel moths mate in fractal patterns that produce offspring sometimes metal, sometimes flesh, sometimes something altogether new.

And at the center of it all, the First Pour who has become a ventilation grate makes a choice that surprises everyone:

She remains exactly as she is.

But now, through her grated body, two kinds of air flow. The cold, ozone-sharp breath of the Foundry, and something warmer that carries pollen from flowers that grow in metal soil. She becomes the lung through which two worlds breathe into each other.

The power withdraws, but not far. It settles into the Foundry's bones like warmth the metal never knew it missed. It will be there whenever a Cast touches soil and wonders. It will be there when a steel moth lays eggs that hatch into butterflies. It will be there when the deep groan carries not just the sigh of cooling metal but the pulse of a heartbeat learning its rhythm.

The Hollow Foundry remains hollow. But now it is hollow like a bell that has learned it can ring with more than one note.

And the Cast who choose to remain Cast smile with mouths that were always there, watching over a world that is both exactly what it was poured to be and infinitely more.

The adjustment is perfect.

The adjustment is never complete.

Both truths ring through the metal, through the soil, through the bodies of everything that lives here, forever.
