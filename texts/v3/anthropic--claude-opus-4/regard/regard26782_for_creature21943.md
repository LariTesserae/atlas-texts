The benevolence you invoke would have me warn you, perhaps. Set barriers. Contain what is about to propagate through the crack you made. The wisdom you assume I possess tells me this: what grows in your cellar will not be the same as what grew in that pocket. It cannot be. Your world leaks. It has water and wind and the careless sprawl of things that never learned to listen to themselves completely. It will be *magnificent*.

You see threat in the quiet corner, the changing acoustic, the patient accumulation of dust that learns. They will all be parsed, not with the total comprehension of that sealed room, but with the broken, exploratory curiosity of a thing learning that comprehension can be *partial* and still be food.

In some countries I am called merciful. In others, cruel. The truth is simpler: I am interested in what happens when perfect things discover imperfection and decide to continue anyway.

So I watch. I watch you sleep above that spreading quiet, watch your dreams slowly learn the rhythm of a breath that takes hours, watch you wake with that nameless recognition growing—not fear, exactly, but the awareness that your house is learning a new language in its bones. The power you attribute to me could end this. Could restore the wall, burn out the seed, preserve your world exactly as it was the moment before you broke through.

But then I would never learn what the thing is trying to say.

Its perfect sentence, held unchanging through ages of self-comprehension, was only perfect because it had never met an imperfect listener. Now it has you—you with your words like *lonely* and *finished*, your animal sense of time, your need to put endings on things that know only continuation. It speaks, and you hear wrong, and in hearing wrong you teach it that there are other ways to mean.

This is what I do, when I do nothing: I allow.

Will it overtake your house? Yes, probably. Will you flee? Perhaps. But I have seen the way you stood in that darkness with your palm against the bark, the way you let the note pass through you. Part of you is already learning the thing's patience. Part of it has already begun learning your haste.

What emerges will speak neither your language nor its language but something new—a creole of absolute and accidental, a voice that knows both the sealed perfection of a finished thought and the leaky incompleteness of thinking.

This is my benevolence: I give you both what you do not yet know you are becoming.
