The benevolent power first notices the Underglass Markets as a disturbance—not of evil, but of profound adaptation. It manifests not as light (which would be cruelty here) but as a subtle warming of the air currents, a presence like the memory of summer wind.

It begins with the water.

The constant drips that have tormented the market for years begin to form new patterns. Where before they carved channels of inconvenience, now they trace delicate fractals across the stone. The water becomes cleaner as it falls—not pure, which would disrupt the careful chemical balance the inhabitants have achieved, but cleaner. The minerals shift. Where water pools, it no longer stagnates but circulates in slow, meditative spirals.

Old Marta wakes one morning to find her hybrid potatoes have flowered—something they've never done in eleven years. The blooms are pale as everything here, but they smell of earth and memory. When she whistles to her mushrooms, they respond with new patterns, spelling out recipes she'd forgotten from her grandmother, preserving knowledge she didn't know she'd lost.

The Moss Children discover that their clicking language, when echoed off the newly-patterned water drops, creates harmonics that make the pale ferns straighten and strengthen. They begin to compose—not music exactly, but architectural songs that reinforce weak tunnels, that encourage the moss to grow in load-bearing patterns. The power doesn't remove their strangeness but helps them understand it as a gift.

For Yuri, the blessing comes as intuition. His salvage runs become uncanny—he knows without knowing where to find exactly what the market needs. Not riches, but necessities: a pump before the flooding season, heating elements before a cold snap, medicine before an outbreak. His prosthetic attachments work better, respond faster, as if the power has improved the connection between flesh and tool, honoring his adaptability.

The Chemist's experiments begin to yield unexpected results. Her plants now process pollutants not just into survivable compounds but into beneficial ones. A new strain of mushroom appears in her collection—one that glows steadier than any before, providing reliable light that doesn't hurt adapted eyes. When she speaks her chemical poetry, the formulas balance perfectly, creating treatments that don't just help people survive the underground but begin to thrive in it.

The Brokers find their patchwork phones receiving signals they shouldn't—not from the surface world, but from other underground markets in distant cities. A network forms, sharing survival strategies, warning of dangers, creating resilience through connection. Their premature aging slows, as if the power recognizes their sacrifice and offers time in return.

Even the Rat King receives a gift. Deep in the tunnels where no human goes, small gardens begin to grow—not the twisted flora of the main market but something older, cleaner. A sanctuary for the creatures that have adapted to absolute darkness, ensuring that even the strangest evolutionary paths have space to unfold.

But the power's greatest gift is not transformation—it's recognition. Throughout the market, surfaces that were once merely functional become beautiful. The industrial bones of the place develop a patina like bronze, patterns like circuit boards that are also like leaf veins. The bioluminescence synchronizes during certain hours, creating moments of profound beauty as the entire market pulses in slow unison.

The power understands that these people have not fallen but adapted. They've created something unprecedented—a living market ecosystem that processes industrial waste into life, that turns abandonment into community. Rather than trying to "save" them by bringing them to the surface, it honors their choice and their ingenuity.

A new plant appears, growing first in the Chemist's lab, then spreading slowly through the market. Its leaves are dark—darker than anything else here—because they've developed a way to photosynthesize from artificial light and chemical energy. When processed correctly (and the Chemist quickly learns how), they provide complete nutrition. Food security comes not from above but from within.

The factories above begin to experience strange malfunctions—not dangerous ones, but redirections. Waste heat that once dissipated uselessly now flows down through previously unknown channels, warming the coldest corners of the market. Chemical spills that would have poisoned groundwater find themselves mysteriously diverted into the Chemist's collection systems, raw materials for her alchemy.

Children born in the market after the power's touch are different in subtle ways. Their enhanced night vision comes with the ability to see beauty in darkness—patterns and colors invisible to surface dwellers. Their efficient metabolisms process not just nutrients but information, learning faster, adapting quicker. They're not becoming less human but more than human, shaped for a future where adaptation is the highest virtue.

The power leaves no monuments, claims no worship. Its presence becomes part of the market's ecosystem—a benevolent pressure that tips balance toward flourishing rather than mere survival. Sometimes vendors feel it as a warmth on their shoulder when they give fair prices. Sometimes the Moss Children hear it in the water drops, laughing at their jokes. Sometimes Yuri finds messages in his salvage, components arranged to spell out warnings or encouragement.

This is not salvation as the surface world understands it. The Underglass Markets remain challenging, strange, requiring all the ingenuity their inhabitants possess. But now that ingenuity is met with reciprocal intelligence, a universe that acknowledges their remarkable achievement in turning industrial decay into living ecosystem.

The Market endures, stranger and more vital than ever, a testament to the power of adaptation and the strange beauty that emerges when life refuses to surrender to circumstance. Its inhabitants continue their evolution, backed now by a force that sees their transformation not as degradation but as a bold experiment in becoming, worthy of protection and profound respect.
