The benevolent power known as the Tender of Lost Gardens has walked between worlds for longer than most stars have burned. They appear as a figure wrapped in living moss and lichen, with eyes like deep pools reflecting skies from a thousand different worlds. Where they step, sleeping seeds remember how to grow. Where they breathe, frozen streams recall the song of movement.

They stand now at the rim of the Hollow of Fixed Ash, and for the first time in ten thousand years, they hesitate.

The Tender knows this place. Not from visiting—they have never been here before—but from the way the universe itself seems to hold its breath around this basin. They can feel the perfect, terrible balance of it: a wound that has learned to be a meditation, a catastrophe that has achieved grace through sheer endurance.

They descend the gentle slope. Their footsteps make no sound—the Tender learned long ago how to walk without disturbing even dust. As they move, tiny spores drift from their cloak, testing the air, tasting the stone. Each spore dies instantly in the dry heat, but the Tender expected this. They are not here to make this place green.

At the center of the basin, they stop before the Last Ember of Veyra. For a long moment, two eternities regard each other: one that moves and tends and changes, one that sits and holds and remembers.

The Tender does not speak. Instead, they kneel—slowly, carefully—and from beneath their cloak they produce something impossible: a single drop of water, held perfectly spherical between their palms. Not ordinary water, but the First Water, the idea of water before any ocean was born. It hovers there, trembling, a tiny universe of potential.

They do not offer it to Veyra. They know better than that. Instead, they let it hover between them, and in its surface, images begin to form:

A world burning. Cities of crystal and song collapsing into ash. A young woman with wild hair and desperate eyes carrying the last ember of her people in her mouth, running, always running, looking for somewhere, anywhere, that the fire might live.

The same woman, older now, kneeling in this basin. Swallowing the ember. Letting it burn through her from inside as she whispers the names of everyone she couldn't save. The fire spreading through her bones, fusing her to stone, but she does not scream. She simply closes her eyes and *holds*.

Millennia passing. The woman-who-is-stone breathing with the rhythm of deep earth. Visitors coming, leaving, dying, forgetting. The black filaments growing from her thoughts, each one a prayer for the dead.

And then—something new. The Tender shows her what they see when they look at her:

Not a memorial. Not a grave. Not even a guardian.

A seed.

The Tender understands now why they were drawn here. Veyra has held her position so long, with such perfect stillness, that she has become something new. She is no longer just preserving the memory of catastrophe—she has transformed into potential itself. Every civilization that will ever be born carries a tiny echo of the one she lost, because she held it so faithfully that the universe itself took notes.

The water droplet trembles. Inside it, the Tender shows her one more vision:

A day—not soon, perhaps not for another billion years—when the stars begin to fail. When the universe itself grows cold. And in that moment, from every place where perfect stillness has been achieved, from every meditation that lasted longer than worlds, something new will kindle. Not the old fire returning, but a new warmth born from the marriage of memory and possibility.

Veyra will be one of those kindling points. Not because she will wake or move or change, but because she has held her ember so perfectly that when the universe needs fire again, it will remember how to burn by looking at her.

The Tender lets the water droplet fade—not evaporate, simply return to the realm of potential. They stand, slowly, and bow deep to the figure of stone.

Then they do something unexpected. From their cloak, they take a single seed—not a plant seed, but something older. A seed of Noticing. They press it gently into the fused stone at Veyra's feet, where it sinks without breaking the surface, disappearing into the mineral matrix.

It will never grow. That's not what seeds of Noticing do. Instead, it will ensure that when travelers come to this place—and they will come, drawn by dreams they don't understand—they will know to be quiet. They will know to sit. They will know that they are in the presence of something holy. Not a god, not a shrine, but something rarer: a perfect action, perfectly sustained.

The Tender turns to leave, but pauses at the rim. They look back one more time at the still figure, the breathing stone, the patient darkness.

"Thank you," they whisper, and their voice carries no sound, only warmth. "For showing me that some gardens are meant to be empty. That some seeds take the shape of stillness. That sometimes the kindest thing power can do is witness, honor, and leave a thing in peace."

As they walk away, the Hollow of Fixed Ash remains exactly as it was. The Last Ember of Veyra does not move, does not acknowledge the visit, does not change.

But deep in the stone, so faint that only the universe itself could detect it, the rhythm of her breathing carries a new note. Not words. Never words. Just the faintest suggestion that she heard, she understood, and she continues her perfect vigil not from duty or grief, but from something that might—after another million years of contemplation—ripen into joy.

The black filaments do not stir. The heat does not waver. The balance holds.

And somewhere in the fused stone at her feet, a seed of Noticing waits with infinite patience, ensuring that her vigil will never be mistaken for abandonment, her stillness never confused with death.

The Tender of Lost Gardens does not look back again. Some gardens, they now understand, bloom in stillness. Some seeds are memories. And some kindnesses are so perfect they become eternal.

The Hollow continues its slow, mineral breathing.

The universe takes notes.

And deep in the stone, held like a promise that needs no words, the Last Ember of Veyra keeps the only flame that matters:

The one that remembers how to wait.
