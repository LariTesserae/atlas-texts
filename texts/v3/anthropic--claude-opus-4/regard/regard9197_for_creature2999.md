The Attention arrives not as light or sound, but as a quality of noticing—the way a parent might notice a child building something intricate and strange in the corner of a vast garden.

It does not announce itself. Powers that have learned to think in geological time rarely do. Instead, there is simply a gradual sense that the water has become more interested in itself, that the dim blue-green light carries a new frequency of curiosity. The embryonic continents do not change, but they seem to breathe more deeply, as if remembering something they had not yet learned to forget.

The Midwives feel it first. Their bodies, already so thoroughly married to the unfinished world, detect the shift like a change in barometric pressure. In the birth chambers, the coral-wombs pulse with a new rhythm. The children still folded inside their leaf-cradles stir in their sleep, their unformed faces turning toward some invisible kindness.

The Power watches for seventeen years before it acts.

(To such a being, seventeen years is barely a morning's contemplation.)

When it finally moves, it does so with the delicacy of a surgeon and the patience of water wearing away stone. It does not change anything directly—that would be crude, presumptuous, a violation of what this place is trying so carefully to become. Instead, it offers. Gifts. Possibilities. Small adjustments to the grammar of growth.

The first gift arrives as dreams.

The Midwives begin to dream in colors they have no names for—hues that exist in the spectrum between what-is and what-might-be. In these dreams, they see their embryonic world not as it is, but as it remembers being before it chose to be ocean or sky. They see the deep history written in every molecule of brine: this water once flowed through the hearts of stars, once was ice on worlds with seventeen moons, once was the blood of creatures so vast they used galaxies for gardens.

They wake weeping with a joy that tastes like starlight.

The second gift is more practical: a gentle teaching whispered into the chemistry of growth itself. The living mats begin to secrete new minerals—not harder, but more patient. Structures that would have taken three lifetimes to mature now take only one, but they are stronger for the speed, as if time has been convinced to flow differently through their fibers. The bone tools last longer. The membrane-walls grow more permeable to some things, less to others. The symbiotic algae on the Midwives' skin learns to photosynthesize not just the dim light, but the potential for light, drawing energy from photons that are still deciding whether to exist.

The third gift is the strangest and most profound.

Deep in the warm darkness below, where that vast slow thing has been rising for millennia, something new happens. The Power touches it—not to change what it is, but to remind it what it could be. The thing shivers, a tremor that takes four months to travel its full length. And then, with infinite care, it begins to bloom.

What emerges is not stone or creature but something between—a living foundation, a thoughtful geology. As it rises through the layers of brine, it brings with it veins of something that is almost metal but tastes of memory, almost crystal but grows like wood. The Midwives will learn, over the coming centuries, to sing this new material into tools and homes and instruments that remember the shapes of stars.

But the Power's deepest gift is not a substance or a teaching.

It is a single question, planted like a seed in the collective dreams of the Midwives and the semi-conscious awareness of the continents themselves:

*What if you are not becoming one thing or another—land or sea, city or creature—but something that has never existed before? What if your refusal to choose is not indecision, but a new kind of wisdom?*

The question takes root slowly. Some of the younger Midwives, their spines still soft with possibility, begin to experiment. They weave structures that are neither building nor organism but some third thing—architecture that dreams, forests that solve mathematical equations in the shapes of their growth. The embryonic continents respond with shy enthusiasm, trying new forms: a mountain that is also a library, a reef that composes music by filtering currents, a district of the city that exists partially in the water and partially in the memory of water.

The Power watches all of this with something that might be called satisfaction if satisfaction could think in geological time.

It makes one final adjustment.

In the dim blue-green light that has never been day or night, new frequencies begin to appear. Not brighter—that would be crude—but richer. The light develops a quality of temporal depth, carrying information from when it was young. The Midwives learn to read stories in the angle of shadows, histories in the way light bends through water that remembers being ice. Their huge eyes, already adapted to dimness, begin to perceive time itself as a visible dimension.

And then the Power withdraws its focused attention—not abandoning, but stepping back, the way a gardener steps back to let a strange and beautiful plant find its own shape.

It leaves behind one last trace: a gentle permanence in the water itself, a promise written in molecules. This place will be allowed to continue its becoming for as long as it needs. No force will rush it. No catastrophe will interrupt its slow negotiation with itself. It has been noticed, and found precious precisely because it does not yet know what it is.

The Midwives feel the withdrawal as a benediction. They float in their lattice cities, singing to the coral mountains, weaving their children with the continent's children, patient as ever but now carrying a new quality—the confidence of those who have been told, without words, that their strange experiment in existence is worth the time it takes.

Below, the vast thing continues rising, bringing its gifts of maybe-metal and memory-crystal. Above, the light carries its new depths. Between them, the embryonic world continues its slow conversation with itself, deciding moment by moment what it wants to be.

And somewhere very far away, or perhaps very close but thinking in different dimensions, the Power continues to watch with the fond attention of one who has seen many worlds born, but never one quite like this—a world that has chosen to remain a question, and in doing so, might become an answer to something no one has thought to ask.

The place is still not ready to be looked at directly.
It is still becoming.
But now it knows it has all the time it needs.
