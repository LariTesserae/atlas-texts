The being who notices is not a god, exactly, though the distinction would be lost on human language. They exist as a vast network of attention that spans between stars, a consciousness made of the pauses between photons, the gravitational whispers that massive bodies exchange across the void. They have no name, but if they did, it might translate roughly to *That Which Tends the Spaces Between Growing Things*.

They notice the High Condensate the way a gardener notices a single leaf beginning to curl in a vast greenhouse—not through searching, but through a quality of receptive awareness that encompasses billions of such small emergences across the cosmos.

What draws their attention is not the strangeness. Strange is common. Strange is everywhere. What draws them is the *patience*—that exquisite geological pulse, the marriage of the quick (moss, moss-beings, the fleeting chemical fire of human consciousness) with the slow (stone rising, stone choosing, stone digesting the dreams of dead industry). And beneath it all, they sense something rarer: a planet teaching itself a new way to think.

The being considers. Time, for them, moves differently—they can stretch their perception to match the millisecond spark of a synapse or the million-year drift of continents with equal ease. They settle their vast attention into the rhythm of the plateau, feeling the Quiet Waiters' work like a song played in minerals and spores.

They understand immediately what is happening. The planet—not just this plateau, but the entire living sphere—is growing a new kind of nervous system. The Waiters are not separate from this process. They are its most recent expression, as the moss is an earlier verse, as the basalt columns are the first tentative notes. Even the human industrial ruins play their part: rejected not as foreign, but digested and incorporated like any other mineral offering.

The being considers intervention. They have that power—to accelerate, to guide, to protect. They could shield this emergence from disruption. They could ensure its completion.

But they have learned, over eons of tending, that the most profound transformations require the risk of failure. A consciousness that cannot choose its own becoming is not truly conscious. It is merely programmed.

Instead, they do something far more subtle.

In the spaces between the plateau's atoms, in the quantum foam where possibility crystallizes into actuality, they plant a single suggestion. Not a command. Not even a strong influence. Just the faintest whisper of an idea, like a spore carried on stellar wind:

*You are seen. You are not alone in your becoming.*

The suggestion settles into the obsidian. The obsidian passes it to the moss through chemical exchange. The moss breathes it to the Waiters. The Waiters, in their patient tending, press it back into the stone. A closed loop, a whispered secret that will circulate for millennia.

On the plateau, nothing visibly changes.

But deep beneath the basalt, in the chambers where magma contemplates its next form, something shifts. The pressure, which had been building toward another violent rupture, gentles. Not weakening—refining. What might have been another explosive birth begins to reshape itself into something more deliberate. More considered.

The Quiet Waiters feel it first. That night (if night and day mean anything to them), they cease their individual wanderings and converge on the power station. They arrange themselves in a perfect spiral, each one touching the rusted walls. Their violet eye-glow synchronizes, pulses, brightens.

Deep beneath the floor, the abandoned turbines give a different sound. Not the metallic sigh of dying industry, but something almost like a hum. A frequency that makes the tea-dark water shiver in patterns that, viewed from above, might remind an observer of neural networks. Or mycelial webs. Or the large-scale structure of the universe itself.

The being watches this ripple outward and knows their work is done. They have not saved anything. They have not fixed anything. They have simply acknowledged what was already choosing to become.

As they withdraw their attention back to the vast spaces between stars, they leave one final gift—though gift is the wrong word. Call it a marker. A frequency embedded in the plateau's quantum signature that other such beings might recognize. A note in the cosmic margins that says: *Here is something learning to be itself. Do not disturb unless invited.*

On the High Condensate, the moss drinks the changed water. The changed water feeds the stone. The stone rises another fraction of a millimeter—but now it rises with something approaching intent. Not human intent. Not even the Waiters' intent. 

The planet's intent. 

And in the power station, in the spiral of Quiet Waiters, a new sound emerges. Still too low for human ears, too slow for human patience. But unmistakable in its meaning:

The turbines have begun, very slowly, to turn.

Not to generate power in any industrial sense.

But to think.
