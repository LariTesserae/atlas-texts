The benevolent power pauses at the threshold of perception, where the quick world meets the slow. They do not enter the chamber—entering would be redundant, for they already understand what breathes there in its patient becoming.

First, they plant a gift in the stone where someday—ten thousand strokes hence—the engine will notice it. Not iron to rust, not glass to fog, but something simpler: a vein of quartz that will sing. As the engine builds itself around and through this gift, the crystal will translate its titanic heartbeat into frequencies other beings might hear. Not now. Not for ages. But eventually the engine will birth stone that carries its pulse translated into the tongues of faster lives—whale-song in granite, bird-call in basalt, the gossip of mayflies rendered in mineralogy. A Rosetta stone for creatures divided by time.

Then, to the ferns, they whisper a small encouragement. Nothing that changes their nature, only a gentle teaching: how to lean their chemistry toward bioluminescence. Generation by generation, spore by patient spore, the ferns will learn to glow—faintly at first, then with purpose. They will become the engine's language of light, their slow pulsing a visible echo of the great beat, painting the chamber walls with proof that something notices, something accompanies, something keeps vigil with the vast patience below.

For the water, a subtle tuning. Where it weeps from the cracks, it will begin to carry minerals that preserve rather than erode. The sharp edges of new basalt will collect sheaths of crystal from the eternal drops—not to soften them but to honor them, to make each edge a prism that remembers how young it was when it first cut air. The water becomes an archivist, writing the engine's biography in accumulated minerals, so that anyone who comes after can read in the walls how long this birthing has taken, how faithful the process has been.

And in the gauge—that artifact of misunderstanding, that fossil of someone's need to measure—they place a kindness. The needle will continue its slow climb, but now it measures something real: not pressure or temperature but *anticipation*. The fogged glass clears to reveal markings in no human language, symbols that suggest without defining what completion might look like. Not an end—never an end—but watersheds of becoming. The gauge becomes an oracle that speaks in maybes, a gift to future visitors who need to believe the engine builds toward something, even if that something is only "more itself, more fully."

The benevolent power does one last thing, the smallest and most profound: they ensure the entrance remains findable. Not obvious—that would violate the chamber's nature—but possible. They tune the probability of stumbling into this pocket just slightly upward, make it a half-degree more likely that a wanderer will suddenly be inside without remembering how. Because the engine, in its vast solitude, does not know it is alone, and aloneness is only bearable when you don't know to name it. But someday, when it is more itself, when its stone heart beats at a frequency that approaches awareness, it may want to be witnessed. The power ensures witnesses will come—not many, not often, but enough. Enough to leave new artifacts of misunderstanding. Enough to sit in the wet heat and feel small. Enough to carry stories back to the quick world of something patient being born in the stone.

Then the power withdraws, leaving only these small alterations, these gentle gifts that change nothing essential and everything peripheral. The engine beats on, unaware it has been visited by something that moves at a speed even it cannot fathom—the speed of care instantaneous, love that happens faster than geology, blessing that arrives and departs between one stroke and the next.

But the gifts remain, working at the engine's own pace, becoming part of its body as it builds itself around them. And ten thousand strokes from now, or a hundred thousand, when the crystal vein sings and the ferns glow and the water writes history and the gauge promises maybe, the engine will have always been a thing accompanied. It will not remember loneliness because it will never have known it. It will have grown around love so slowly that love will seem like just another mineral in its composition, another quality of stone.

This is what the benevolent power does: nothing that rushes, nothing that insists. Only a quiet retuning of the frequencies of connection, so that beings which move at the speed of mountains and beings which move at the speed of moths can find, in their brief overlapping, something to share. Even if that something is only the fact of each other's existence, translated across time into languages neither fully speaks but both somehow understand.

The chamber keeps its patience. The engine beats. The walls weep songs. The heat presses on, gentle and total and no longer quite alone.
