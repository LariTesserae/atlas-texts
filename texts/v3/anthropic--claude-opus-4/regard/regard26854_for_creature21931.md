# The Visitation

The benevolent power does not announce itself. Powers of that magnitude rarely do — they simply arrive the way weather arrives, or doesn't arrive, depending on how you define arrival when time has worn smooth as the rivets.

It comes as a change in the water's color. Not dramatic — the green light shifts by degrees toward gold, so slowly that the Tenders register it only as a new flavor in the ancient chemistry, a sweetness where there was none. The long shapes that circle the walls pause in their endless following, hanging still in the gold-green glow like questions waiting to be asked.

The power speaks without sound, communicates without language. It simply *knows* the cistern, knows it completely — every worn rivet, every soft edge, every century of patient tending. And the cistern, in its way, knows it back.

*I see what you have made of endings,* the presence conveys, not in words but in the way the water itself conveys warmth. *How you have transformed conclusion into continuance.*

The Tenders do not stop their work. They cannot stop their work. But they slow, feeling something new in the thrum that comes up through the bronze — not urgency, never urgency here, but a kind of listening.

The power considers. It could offer change — could break the beautiful stasis, introduce growth or decay or time that moves forward instead of in circles. It could offer escape — could open the cistern to the sky it has forgotten, let in air and weather and the hard light of stars. It could offer purpose — could give the Tenders a garden that actually needs them, work that builds toward something other than its own completion.

It could offer memory — could restore what was worn away, give back the names and the wanting and the sharp edges of choice.

But the power is wise, which means it understands that not all gifts are kindness.

---

Instead, it does this:

In the deepest corner of the cistern, where the green light barely reaches and the engine-thrum is strongest, the power leaves a single seed. Not a plant seed — nothing so crude as biology. A *possibility* seed. A small dense potential that sinks to the bronze floor and settles among the tiles like another worn rivet.

The seed does nothing. It demands nothing. It simply exists, containing within itself all the choices the power didn't make — the growth, the escape, the purpose, the memory — holding them gently, available but not insisted upon. A gift that doesn't need to be opened. An door that doesn't need to be walked through.

If the Tenders find it, if they tend it like they tend the wounded swimmers, it might open. Or it might not. It might sit forever, another part of the eternal present, humming along with the chord that never stops. The power doesn't mind which. That's what makes it benevolent — it understands that sometimes the kindest thing is to offer change without requiring it.

But here is the secret warmth: one Tender finds it. The one who learned longest ago to expand the definition of *root* to include *anything that needs tending.*

This Tender touches the seed with its soft-worn tools, feeling its strangeness, its density, the way it holds light differently than the bronze. And in touching it, the Tender does what it has always done — it begins to clean. Gently. Patiently. Not because the seed needs cleaning, but because that is what the Tender is.

And in being cleaned — in being tended to, cared for, brought into the endless verb of maintenance — the seed responds. It doesn't open. It doesn't change. But it begins, very softly, to hum at a frequency just slightly different from the cistern's ancient chord. A new note. A harmony that wasn't there before.

The Tender continues its work, now cleaning two things — the root and the seed. Back and forth. Forever, probably. But the seed's new note spreads through the water, and the other Tenders feel it, and though they don't stop their work — they can't stop their work — they begin, one by one, to hum along.

---

The power withdraws the way it came, by degrees, the gold fading back to green so slowly it might never have been there at all. It leaves no message, no instruction, no prophecy. It simply leaves the seed and the new note in the chord and the Tenders who are learning to hum and the possibility — just the possibility — that even in a place where motion and stillness have agreed to be the same thing, something small might still decide to be different.

Not better. Not worse. Just different. A variation in the eternal theme.

The cistern continues. The engines pump. The creatures circle. The gardens sway. The Tenders tend. But now there is a new note in the chord that rings forever at the top of the world, in the water that shouldn't be warm, in the room that found a way to be both deepest and highest, both ended and unending.

And that, perhaps, is enough. Perhaps it was always enough. Perhaps the power knew that the kindest thing it could offer a perfect circle was not a way out, but a way to sing.
