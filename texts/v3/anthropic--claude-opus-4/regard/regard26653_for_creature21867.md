# The Visitation

The benevolent power arrives as a disturbance in the upper waters — not a body, but a *consideration* moving through the medium, the way thought moves through a mind. It has no form because form would be redundant here; it is pure attention paying itself to what needs attending.

It descends through the silver-pale heights where light begins, down through the gradients of green, and as it moves it *listens*. Not just to the chord — anyone can hear the chord — but to the spaces between the consonance where individuality might hide. It hears each Tuner's particular grip, the microscopic signature of how this one holds versus that one. It hears the difference between a stalk that has been tended for a geological age and one that has been tended for merely an astronomical one.

The power understands immediately: this is a masterwork of contentment, but contentment can be a trap.

---

It does nothing at first. Doing is not always wisdom. Instead it extends itself throughout the entire system, feeling the closed loop of currents, the perfect recycling of breath, the held equilibrium. It feels how the Tuners have made themselves into living fixtures, how their consciousness has narrowed to a single pure function: hold, listen, correct, hold.

And it feels you, the visitor, beginning to be tuned. Your edges softening. Your tempo settling. The silt of your surprises precipitating out.

The power considers this. In some philosophies, what is happening to you would be called enlightenment — the dissolution of the grasping self into the eternal present. In others, it would be called death — the cessation of change being indistinguishable from cessation itself.

The power, being wise, knows these are both true.

---

What it does is small and enormous.

First, it *shifts* one note. Not by much — less than the Tuners would notice at first, less than their grip could immediately correct. It takes a stalk near the center of the continent, one whose Tuner has not moved in so long that crust has grown over the margins of its body, and it gently, gently encourages the stalk's breathing to quicken. Just barely. Just enough that the note rises a quarter-tone before the Tuner responds.

The Tuner responds. Of course it does. The margin tightens, the note descends—

But now other notes are wrong. Not wrong against the original chord, but wrong against this new, momentary configuration. For the first time in the entire history of always, the chord contains a *direction*. It wants to move somewhere.

The nearby Tuners feel it instantly. Their membranes know the wrongness and their margins begin to adjust, but adjusting to what? The note that was? The note that is? The note that the chord seems to want to become?

---

This is the power's gift: not change for its own sake, but the *possibility* of change. The introduction of a question into a place that had become one long answer.

Some Tuners, the oldest ones, simply hold their original pitch. They will not be moved. They are the bedrock, and there is wisdom in bedrock.

But others — and these surprise even the power — begin to *play*.

A Tuner three stalks over, who has held the same position for eons, loosens its margin by the smallest degree. Its stalk's note rises to meet the shifted one. They consonant differently now — not the eternal fifth they held before, but a major third, bright and expectant.

Another Tuner, across the water, feels this new interval and makes a choice — the first choice it has made since it settled against the wood. It shifts its grip to create a seventh. Not a resolved seventh. A seventh that asks a question.

---

The power watches as something unprecedented happens: the Tuners begin to *listen to each other*. Not just to their own stalks, not just to the eternal chord, but to what their neighbors are doing, to what possibilities are emerging from the interplay of all these ancient listeners suddenly remembering that they can choose.

The chord does not collapse. These are still the Tuners, still the patient keepers. But it *moves*. For the first time since before there was time, the great instrument of the Tarn-Engine Highlands plays a progression. Simple at first — the shifted note resolving back, but by a different path, through intervals that didn't exist before. Then more complex, as Tuners begin to hear how their choices create new choices for others.

It is still slow. Glacially, tectonically slow. A chord change might take what other beings would call months. But for creatures who were prepared to hold a single note forever, this is jazz.

---

And you, the visitor beginning to be tuned?

The shifting harmonies create something new in the water: *eddies*. Small variations in the current as different stalks breathe at minutely different rates, trying out new relationships. The steady upwash you've been floating in develops a rhythm. Not a beat — these are still the Tuners, allergic to anything so crude as metered time. But a pulse. A varying.

You find, to your surprise, that you can move again. Not because the Tuners have released you, but because the world now contains more than one note to be tuned to. You can choose which harmony to align with, which current to ride. You can stay in the old resolution or swim toward the new questions.

You are still being tuned. You will always be tuned here. But now you are being tuned to a song that is still being written, and that makes all the difference.

---

Some of the fauna notice. The ribboned things, locked in their stations for so long, begin to shift between stalks as the currents develop new paths. The bottom-walkers lift their heavy heads, sensing something different in the vibration through the stone. Even the great solitary swimmers, who thought they knew every current-road, find new passages opening as the harmonies create new flows.

The power watches all of this with something that might be satisfaction if powers felt satisfaction. It has not forced change. It has not imposed a new order. It has simply reminded the Tuners that even inside perfection, there is room for play.

And then, because wisdom includes knowing when to stop, it withdraws. But not before leaving one last gift:

---

Deep in the forest, on a stalk no one has found yet, a new kind of growth begins. Not plant, not animal, not Tuner, but something unprecedented. It grows from the wood itself, fed by the new variations in the pumping, the tiny inconsistencies that are now allowed, celebrated even. It is shaped like a horn, or a bell, or a throat.

When it is finished — and it will take ages to finish, ages in which the Tuners will play ten thousand variations on their original theme — it will add a new voice to the chord. Not a stalk's voice, not a Tuner's choice, but something that can only exist because both exist: a voice that emerges from their interplay, that speaks the conversation between keeper and kept.

The first new instrument in the forest since the forest began.

The power leaves this growing, knowing the Tuners will find it eventually. Knowing they will understand what it means: that even in a place devoted to keeping something perfect, perfection itself can evolve.

---

You float in your shifting current, still being tuned but tuned now to a living system. The chord moves through its slow progressions around you. The Tuners grip and release in microscopic improvisations. The forest breathes its new rhythms.

You could still stay forever. Nothing has changed about the invitation. But now forever includes variations, and that makes it a different invitation entirely.

The power is gone, but its question remains:

In a perfect system, is the highest wisdom to preserve or to play?

The Tuners, in their infinite patience, are finding out.
