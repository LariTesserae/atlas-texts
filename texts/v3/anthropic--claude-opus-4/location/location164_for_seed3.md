The city hangs in vertical ribbons against the mountain face, its industrial towers clinging to raw cliffsides where the stone still bleeds mineral water. Steam rises perpetually from the depths—not from machinery alone, but from where the mountain itself exhales through unfinished caverns, the rock still deciding what it wants to become.

This is a place of impossible elevations and patient transformations. The settlement stretches across a series of nascent plateaus that emerge from the mountainside like thoughts half-formed, connected by iron bridges that span chasms where mist pools thick as milk. The geology here refuses completion—ledges extend themselves by inches each year, new stone bubbling up through cracks in factory floors, while established platforms occasionally simply cease, leaving buildings to cantilever over sudden nothing.

The factories work in shifts that follow no clock. Their great engines hum and clatter at intervals determined by the mountain's breathing, drawing power from the temperature differential between the cold heights and the warm exhalations from below. Condensation is constant—every surface gleams with moisture that collects in engineered channels, feeding into industrial processes and dripping through the levels of the city in an endless cycle.

Vegetation thrives in this perpendicular world with an enthusiasm that defies the cool air and dim light. Massive ferns unfurl from every available crevice, their fronds reaching spans of twenty feet or more. Moss carpets the vertical surfaces in deep cushions that muffle sound and blur the distinction between built and grown. Strange pale vines thread through the machinery, their roots diving deep into the living rock, their leaves turning slowly to track what little filtered sunlight penetrates the constant haze.

The fauna here has adapted to the vertical environment and the city's industrial rhythm. Colonies of something between bat and lemur nest in the warm spaces behind the engines, emerging in chattering clouds when the machines cycle down. Their calls echo off the cliff faces—a constant metallic chittering that blends with the industrial noise until you cannot separate the biological from the mechanical. Larger creatures move through the mist on too many legs, maintaining the machinery with an intelligence that suggests they understand its purpose better than its builders did.

The light comes filtered through layers—first through the high mist that never fully clears, then through the canopy of giant ferns, finally through the industrial haze of the factories. It arrives everywhere as a dim, greenish luminescence that makes shadows uncertain. Time becomes negotiable in this half-light. Workers move through their shifts, but whether a shift is eight hours or eight days becomes unclear when the mountain's slow breathing is your only reliable rhythm.

Reality grows thin at the edges here. Sometimes a bridge leads to a platform that wasn't there yesterday but has always existed. Sometimes the sound of the factories becomes the sound of something vast digesteding stones in the mountain's belly. The city's maps are drawn in pencil and constantly revised—not because the cartographers are imprecise, but because precision itself is a shifting concept when the geography refuses to hold still.

The industrial systems pulse with their own slow life. Great pipes carry steam and stranger vapors between levels, their routes following logic that predates the city. The factories produce things—certainly they produce things—but what exactly emerges from their production lines changes depending on the observer and the mountain's mood. Sometimes it's clearly machinery, sometimes it's clearly organic, often it's something that won't commit to either category.

Water moves everywhere through this vertical world. It condenses on the cool stone, runs down the cliff faces in temporary waterfalls, pools in natural basins that become industrial reservoirs, and evaporates from the warm factory floors only to condense again higher up. The entire city is a vast distillation apparatus, though what essence it's extracting or refining remains gloriously unclear.

The scale reveals itself slowly. What seems at first like a single cliff-clinging district opens into a vast vertical territory—platforms and factories and growing spaces extending up and down the mountain face for miles, connected by bridges and pipes and the patient travel of creatures that think nothing of a thousand-foot vertical commute. The city is sparse in its way, with great gaps between its inhabited platforms, but these voids are not empty. They're full of mist and sound and the slow becoming of new stone, new spaces, new impossibilities.

In the depths below, where the factories' foundations disappear into darkness, the mountain's unfinished business continues. New rock forms and re-forms, pushed up by geological processes that operate on both stone-time and dream-time. The city rides this slow wave of creation, its industrial metabolism perfectly synchronized with the mountain's embryonic geology, neither fully formed, both forever becoming something else.
