The chamber breathes with water that has never known sky.

Carved from living coral-stone, this flooded room spans perhaps thirty paces at its widest—a hollow pearl within the reef-city's vastness. The builders shaped it recently, maybe a generation past, and the tool marks still show fresh beneath the water's lens: chisel-grooves following the stone's natural grain, each strike deliberate as prayer. The walls curve up and inward, meeting at a dome pierced by a single circular opening where brilliant light pours down through clear fathoms.

Everything here was made, but made with reverence for what was. The coral they carved still holds its original architecture—branch patterns, polyp-cups, the frozen mathematics of growth—now hollowed into shelves and nooks. Stone-cutters left pillars where the coral grew thickest, fluting them with channels that make the water sing. Three passages lead away into the reef-maze, their openings shaped like gaping mouths, edged with carved teeth that break the current into humming turbulence.

The sparse vegetation seems almost accidental—thin films of algae painting green-gold streaks where the light strikes strongest, a few stubborn kelp fronds anchored in crevices the builders missed. They wave in the chamber's strange currents, which cycle endlessly: water enters through the lower passages, warms against the sun-heated walls, rises through the dome opening, creating a perpetual circulation that fills the space with liquid whispers, gurgles, the occasional crack of thermal expansion in the stone.

The water itself is blood-warm, heavy with minerals that taste of the reef's slow dissolution. It carries no silt—the young stone hasn't learned to crumble yet—so visibility reaches every corner. Tools rest in their carved niches: bronze chisels with kelp-wrapped handles, mallets of dense reef-stone, scrapers made from some giant's discarded shell. All meticulously placed, waiting for hands that might return tomorrow or never.

The brightness seems impossible for the depth, but the dome opening acts as a lens. The builders knew their optics—carved the rim just so, creating a focusing effect that gathers light from above and casts it in shifting patterns across the floor. These light-shapes move with the water's surface far overhead, painting ghost-waves on stone, making the whole chamber flicker between brilliant illumination and brief blue shadow.

Something in the proportions suggests purpose beyond mere habitation. The acoustic properties, perhaps—how voices would carry in this water, how the sound of tools on stone would ring and multiply. Or the way the currents create a natural spiraling path from entrance to entrance, inviting procession. This is workshop and shrine both, a place where making and devotion merge in the patient shaping of stone beneath the waves.

The only sounds are water-sounds: the endless circulation's hum, the pop and creak of pressure-adjusting coral, the way the passages moan slightly when the current strengthens. Sometimes, from somewhere far off in the reef-city, comes the distant ring of bronze on stone—others working in their own flooded chambers, maintaining this built world one careful strike at a time.
