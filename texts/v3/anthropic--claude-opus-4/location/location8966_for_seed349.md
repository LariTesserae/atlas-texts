The Steaming Quarter spreads below you like a fever dream of brass and moss. Here, where the thermal springs broke through the city's foundation centuries ago, the old industrial district has transformed into something neither fully urban nor wild.

Steam rises perpetually from the cracked cobblestones, fed by the network of hot springs that web beneath the streets. The air tastes of minerals and machine oil, thick enough that you feel it condensing on your skin within moments of arrival. Droplets bead on every surface—the corroded copper pipework that still snakes between buildings, the slick black stones, the emerald moss that furs every wall up to the second story.

The factories still run, though not as their builders intended. Great turbines turn in the mist, powered by the geothermal vents that split the old foundry floors. Their steady thrumming creates a bass note that vibrates through your feet, joined by the percussion of steam hammers that activate seemingly at random, crushing nothing, their purpose long forgotten. The cacophony is constant: hissing vents, grinding gears, the splash of condensation falling from eaves, and underneath it all, the chittering and calling of the creatures that have made this place home.

Between the buildings—those that still stand—the vegetation has claimed its territory with subtropical enthusiasm despite the cold. Tree ferns unfurl from storm drains. Vines heavy with trumpet flowers cascade from broken windows, their blooms opening to catch the warm updrafts. The plants grow in impossible profusion wherever the steam touches, creating hanging gardens from fire escapes and verdant caves within collapsed warehouses.

The fauna has adapted remarkably. Brass-winged beetles the size of dinner plates nest in the defunct boiler rooms, their larvae feeding on the specialized fungi that grow on warm metal. Colonies of steam mice—pale, nearly translucent creatures—dart through the pipe networks, their high-pitched communications adding to the industrial symphony. Herons with oil-slick feathers fish in the canals where warm runoff meets cold groundwater, creating a perpetual fog that never quite lifts.

The streets follow no modern logic, having warped and buckled over generations as the springs shifted beneath. What remains of the old rail system has become an elevated garden, the tracks now supporting terraces of ferns and industrial wildflowers. The few remaining residents have built catwalks and rope bridges between the stable structures, creating a secondary city above the steam-shrouded ground level.

At night, the quarter glows with bioluminescence—fungi and bacteria that have evolved in the warm, mineral-rich environment paint the walls with foxfire. The steam catches and diffuses this ghostly light, creating an atmosphere somewhere between a foundry and a fairy tale.

Time moves strangely here. The constant noise and motion, the eternal twilight beneath the steam, the way the moisture makes distances uncertain—visitors often emerge unsure whether they've spent hours or days in the Steaming Quarter. The city above has learned to leave it be, this pocket where the industrial age collapsed into something stranger, where the boundary between built and wild has dissolved in the mist.
