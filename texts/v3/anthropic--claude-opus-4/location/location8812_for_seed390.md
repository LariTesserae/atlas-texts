The Hanging Mills of Kaelora rise through perpetual mist, a vertical city carved into the face of a mountain so young its stone still weeps mineral tears. Here, at the edge of the world's newest range, the rock itself hasn't finished deciding what it wants to be—schist flakes away in sheets while fresh granite pushes up from below, creating a geology that rebuilds itself every few decades.

The city climbs through seven distinct atmospheric layers, each catching moisture differently. At the lowest tier, condensation from the valley's morning breath feeds elaborate gutter systems that channel precious water upward through a network of bamboo aqueducts and copper pipes. These wrap around buildings like vascular systems, ticking and gurgling in the quiet. Steam vents punctuate the streets where underground thermal activity meets the cool air, creating localized weather systems no larger than a courtyard.

Buildings sprout from the cliffsides like geometric shelf fungi, their foundations anchored deep into the living rock with industrial-grade bolts that must be adjusted monthly as the mountain grows. The architecture is dense and defensive against gravity—structures lean into each other for support, connected by enclosed bridges, catwalks, and cable cars that traverse the gaps. Every fifth building houses machinery: water pumps, ore processors, cable winches, and the great turbines that generate power from falling water. Their low mechanical hum provides the city's baseline sound, felt more than heard.

The lush vegetation seems impossible at first—this high, this cool, this industrial. But the constant mist and mineral-rich runoff from the infant stone create perfect conditions for specialized flora. Moss gardens cascade down factory walls. Ferns burst from every crack in the pavement. Terraced growing towers rise between apartments, their spiral exteriors wrapped in vines that follow the warmth of steam pipes. The plants here grow with unusual vigor, as if the embryonic geology beneath infuses them with its own urgent becoming.

The air tastes of copper and chlorophyll. Sometimes, when the wind shifts, you catch the scent of ozone from the fabrication quarter where they forge new anchor bolts from sky-iron. The city's industrial network operates in harmony with the mountain's growth—engineers monitor the stone's expansion with networks of strain gauges, adjusting building tensions like tuning an instrument.

Few animals make their homes here, though. The constant construction and reconstruction disturbs most wildlife. Occasionally, a family of cloud leopards pads across the highest bridges at dawn. Small birds nest in the machinery housings where the warmth keeps their eggs viable. But mostly the city belongs to its human inhabitants and their green companions.

In the market squares—carved platforms that jut out into open air—water sellers offer bottles of condensate from different elevations, each with its own mineral signature. Children play games involving the prediction of new stone formations, chalking their guesses on walls that might not exist next season. The sound of hammers on steel rings out at irregular intervals as work crews shore up buildings against the mountain's latest adjustment.

At night, bioluminescent mosses activate in response to the temperature drop, outlining the city's vertical geography in pale green constellations. The industrial lights—amber bulbs strung along the catwalks, blue-white floods illuminating work sites—create a second set of stars. Together they make the mountain face look like a circuit board slowly programming itself into existence.

The weather here operates in pockets. Rain might fall on the third tier while the fifth basks in filtered sunlight. Clouds form and dissipate within the city's vertical maze, sometimes trapped between building clusters for hours. The residents read these micro-climates like text, adjusting their daily routes based on where the moisture gathers.

This is a place of constant becoming. The city and mountain grow together, neither fully formed, both reaching toward a future shape that no one can quite predict. In the grinding of stone against steel, in the drip of condensation through industrial pipes, in the push of green life through every possible gap, Kaelora builds itself day by day—a vertical world balanced between the raw potential of new geology and the ingenious persistence of those who call it home.
