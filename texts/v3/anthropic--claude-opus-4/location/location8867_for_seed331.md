The city sleeps beneath ice.

In this pocket between frozen earth and glacial ceiling, water seeps eternal. It beads on stone that has never known sunlight, tracing mineral veins through walls carved by patient hands now centuries still. The drops gather, merge, fall with the softest percussion into channels worn smooth by their ancestors. These narrow streams wind between foundation stones, beneath archways that support nothing but their own weight, past doorways that open onto other doorways in an architecture of pure threshold.

The air tastes of iron and waiting. Cold enough that breath ghosts visible, wet enough that frost cannot quite form—everything hovers at the edge of transformation. Moisture condenses on every surface, making the dark stones gleam like sleeping eyes. The space is vast in its emptiness, yet intimate in scale: a single plaza, perhaps, or a covered market whose merchants never came. Columns rise into shadow, their capitals lost where ceiling becomes suggestion rather than certainty.

Through this hollow urban womb, life moves in careful abundance. Pale crustaceans no larger than fingernails navigate the shallow streams by touch, their antennae reading the water's subtle currents. Moths with wings like tissue paper rest on the underside of carved lintels, their bodies pulsing with barely perceptible breath. Somewhere in the walls, between fitted stones, colonies of something chitinous shift and resettle—not quite insects, not quite spiders, but some patient thing evolved for spaces between spaces.

The fauna have claimed every niche the builders left. Where water pools in worn depressions, translucent larvae curl and uncurl in slow motion. Stone corners host clusters of snails whose shells spiral in mathematical perfection, their soft bodies extended to graze on mineral deposits. Even the air has its citizens: gnats that move in loose constellations, never seeming to land, existing in a state of perpetual drift through the dim reaches.

This is architecture in embryo, a city caught in the moment before inhabitation, preserved in the amber of geological time. The buildings exist without purpose, infrastructure without function—streets that connect absence to absence, cisterns that collect water for no one, walls that divide identical emptiness. Yet the very purposelessness has become purpose: the space breathes with its own logic now, neither natural cave nor human construction but something between, around, beneath.

In the quietest moments, when even the water's drip seems to pause, you can hear the city's true sound: the infinitesimal scraping of snail on stone, the whisper of moth wing against carved surface, the almost-imagined settling of foundations that have found their final form. These sounds layer into silence so complete it becomes presence—not the absence of noise but the careful composition of small sounds into something approaching stillness.

The light, what little exists, comes from nowhere and everywhere. Perhaps phosphorescence in the water, perhaps some quality of the stone itself, perhaps the accumulated glow of all those pale bodies moving through darkness. It's enough to see shapes but not details, enough to navigate but not to read whatever inscriptions might mark these walls. The dimness makes the space larger and smaller simultaneously—passages recede into possibility, while what's visible seems pressed close, immediate, secret.

This is what cities dream in their first sleep: not the bustle of markets or the purpose of walls, but the pure potential of shaped space. Water finds its level between fitted stones. Small lives elaborate themselves in the margins. The cold preserves everything in a state of almost-but-not-yet, a municipal embryo wrapped in stone and silence, waiting without impatience for whatever comes next.
