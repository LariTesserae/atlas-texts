The Birth Peaks rise like frozen screams from the planet's mantle, their summits lost in perpetual shadow. This is a place still learning how to be solid.

The terrain convulses in slow motion. What appears to be mountain from one angle reveals itself as a wave of semi-cooled mineral foam, caught mid-surge and hardening by degrees. The ground temperature hovers at blood-warm, but there is no blood here, no water at all. The heat comes from below, from the planet's fevered core pushing up through cracks that web the landscape like golden veins.

These are not mountains in any traditional sense. They are extrusions—places where the planet's interior escapes upward in columnar fountains of metallic glass and crystallizing gases. Each peak grows visibly, adding perhaps a hand's width every local day, though "day" here is a technicality. The star that should warm this place is distant and dim, its light barely penetrating the particulate haze that rises from the cooling peaks. Most illumination comes from below: the faint amber glow of semi-molten substrate visible through fissures, and the occasional flare when a new vent opens.

The air—if it can be called that—is thick with metallic dust and complex long-chain molecules that failed to find their way into solid form. It tastes of copper and ozone, moves like syrup. Sound travels strangely here, muffled by the dense atmosphere but occasionally amplified by the hollow chambers forming within the growing peaks. The landscape groans and whispers: the crack of cooling mineral, the hiss of escaping gases, the deep bass note of tectonic adjustment far below.

Scattered across this embryonic mountainscape are structures that might be ruins or might be something else entirely. They appear to be made of the same material as the peaks themselves—a dark, glass-like substance shot through with threads of native copper. But their geometry is too regular for natural formation: perfect spheres half-buried in fresh flows, archways that frame nothing, spiral towers no taller than a person. They emerge from the ground already ancient-looking, as if the planet is remembering buildings that never were.

Are they artifacts of some prior civilization that found a way to encode its monuments into the planet's geological memory? Or are they simply the result of some exotic chemistry, crystal formations that coincidentally echo architectural forms? The distinction may not matter here, where the boundary between geology and artifact blurs.

The peaks continue to grow, their tops disappearing into the perpetual twilight above. Sometimes entire sections collapse without warning, sending avalanches of hot glass shards down slopes that reform behind them. New vents open with explosive force, fountaining incandescent material skyward before it cools and falls as obsidian rain. The scattered structures survive these upheavals unchanged, perhaps protected by their own strange nature, or perhaps continuously rebuilt by the same forces that first extruded them.

This is a landscape in violent birth, too young for erosion, too hot for water, too turbulent for life as we know it. Yet there is a terrible beauty in its becoming—in the way the amber light catches in the glass peaks, in the mathematical precision of the mysterious structures, in the constant transformation that makes each visit to this place a encounter with a new topology.

The peaks sing, sometimes. When the wind—that thick, metallic wind—blows through their hollow cores at just the right angle, they produce sounds like organ pipes or whalesong. The music is alien, atonal, but hauntingly complex. It seems to follow patterns, as if the landscape is trying to communicate something, or perhaps simply expressing the joy and agony of its own emergence into form.

This is not a place for human presence. We would suffocate in its atmosphere, be crushed by its pressure, burned by its heat. Yet standing at the edge of this turbulent genesis, one might feel a strange kinship with these nascent peaks—fellow travelers in the journey from formlessness to form, from potential to presence. The Birth Peaks are what they are: a world catching itself in the act of becoming, frozen in an eternal moment of crystalline emergence.
