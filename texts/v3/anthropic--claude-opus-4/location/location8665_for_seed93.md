The valley stretches impossibly wide, its boundaries flickering between near and distant like a breathing horizon. Steam rises from scattered puddles that appear and vanish with the logic of dreams, their surfaces reflecting not the sky above but the sky from three days ago, or perhaps three days hence.

This is new land, or land newly revealed—the black volcanic glass of recent flows catches normal daylight and holds it too long, releasing it in stuttering bursts that make shadows dance independently of their sources. The sharp edges haven't yet learned the compromise of erosion. Every surface cuts keenly between what is and what might be.

Vegetation erupts in concentrated patches where the rules permit growth. Massive ferns unfurl in fast-forward, their fronds reaching full extension in minutes before freezing mid-motion for hours. Vines snake through air as if climbing invisible lattices, their tendrils grasping at mathematical principles rather than physical supports. The green is too green, as if someone has turned up the saturation on reality itself. Between these islands of hypergrowth, the land lies bare and expectant.

The warmth here doesn't behave properly. It pools in corners where no corners exist, radiates from empty air, and sometimes runs in visible streams like honey-thick thermals that creatures could swim through if any dared approach. The heat makes the industrial ruins shimmer—a corrugated metal warehouse that phases between solid and suggestion, its roof panels ringing with the sound of rain that fell last week and continues falling in a parallel probability. 

Concrete foundations dot the landscape at irregular intervals, some supporting nothing but memory, others anchoring structures that exist only from certain angles. A smokestack rises from bare ground, its brick construction impeccable, belching steam that becomes rain that becomes the puddles that reflect wrong skies. The industrial machinery hums with purpose despite missing most of its parts—conveyor belts run through solid stone, pistons pump abstract concepts, generators produce electricity that flows upward into gathering clouds.

The sounds layer wrongly. The clank and hiss of phantom machinery provides a bass note that arrives before its own echo. Bird calls exist without birds, starting mid-song and ending in mechanical whirs. The landscape groans with the settling of new stone, punctuated by the sudden silence of spaces where sound has been deleted entirely.

In the middle distance, a cluster of storage tanks rust at accelerated speed, their decay racing their construction in a cycle that completes every few hours. Orange blooms spread across their surfaces like stop-motion flowers while fresh metal emerges from beneath. The ground around them is littered with tools that work on principles not yet invented—wrenches that turn bolts in parallel dimensions, measuring devices that quantify impossibility.

Time moves in eddies here. A single rain droplet might hang in the air for an hour while grass completes a full seasonal cycle beneath it. The few creatures that inhabit this space have adapted to the temporal inconsistencies—a deer grazes in three moments simultaneously, its body a blur of overlapping possibilities. Insects fly backward through their own flight paths, pollinating flowers that bloom inside-out.

The sky maintains its normalcy with suspicious determination, as if consciously resisting the reality breakdown below. But even it shows signs of strain—clouds that form perfect grid patterns, sun rays that bend at right angles, patches where the blue deepens into ultraviolet that the human eye shouldn't perceive but does.

This is a place caught in the act of becoming, where industrial ambition has collided with something far older and stranger. The sharp young geology provides a canvas for impossibilities, while the scattered remnants of human endeavor serve as anchor points for physics that can't quite decide which rules to follow. It's a landscape that hums with potential energy, where the sparse distribution of objects only emphasizes the weight of the space between them—space that might at any moment fold, invert, or reveal itself to be something else entirely.

The warmth persists, heavy and electric, like the air before a storm that exists in seventeen dimensions. And through it all, the slow patience of geological time tries to assert itself, even as minutes become years become seconds in the broken clockwork of this reality-shifted valley.
