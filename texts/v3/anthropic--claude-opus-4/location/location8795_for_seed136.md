The Rootworks sprawl beneath a pale green sky, where endless networks of living woody mass have woven themselves into a breathing city. There is no soil here — only the braided, twisted architecture of what might have once been trees but have long since forgotten any individual form. The vegetation has become infrastructure, growing in dense crystalline patterns that follow some alien mathematics, sprouting smooth corridors and vaulted chambers from its own substance.

Everything is plant. The ground is a springy mesh of interwoven roots thick as bridge cables, creating multiple levels that dip and rise in organic terraces. The walls are vertical gardens of bark that has learned to grow in sheets, punctured by oval breathing pores that wheeze softly in synchronized rhythm. What passes for buildings are bulbous growths, their surfaces covered in a fine fur of rootlets that taste the air. Inside, the chambers glow with a soft bioluminescence from fungal filaments embedded in the living walls.

The industrial machinery of this place has evolved from metal and steam into something stranger — great digestive factories where the plant-substrate processes itself, breaking down old growth and channeling nutrients through pipeline roots to areas of new construction. Fermentation chambers bubble and hiss, producing the gases that power the city's metabolism. Vast photosynthetic collectors unfurl during the day like mechanical flowers, their surfaces engineered to maximum efficiency, feeding energy into the throbbing network below.

The air is cool and thick with the smell of sap and chlorophyll. A fine mist of spores and pollen drifts constantly, visible in the diffused light that filters through the semi-translucent canopy overhead. The atmosphere has a greenhouse quality — humid despite the absence of water, as the plants recycle every molecule of moisture within their closed system.

Movement is everywhere, though subtle. Tendrils explore new spaces with patient determination. Branches extend and retract like muscular arms. Whole sections of the city reshape themselves hourly — walls thickening, passages narrowing or widening, new chambers budding from existing ones. The growth follows patterns that suggest intention, though whether guided by some distributed plant intelligence or the humans who must have once engineered this symbiosis is unclear.

The sound is a constant low susurrus — the whisper of air through breathing pores, the creak of expanding wood, the pop of seed pods opening in sequence. Occasionally, deeper groans echo through the structure as massive trunks adjust their positions, bearing the weight of this living architecture.

Transport arteries pulse with thick sap that carries both nutrients and passengers in bio-mechanical pods that bud from the walls when needed. These vessels are grown rather than built, their transparent skin showing the passengers suspended in thick amber fluid as they're digested through the city's circulation system and expelled at their destination, gasping and dripping.

The newest growths are sharp-edged and geometric, following the rigid patterns of their engineering, while older sections have begun to slouch and curve, developing their own organic logic. Where the two meet, you can see the tension — the fresh growth trying to maintain its prescribed form while the mature sections pull it toward something more chaotic and alive.

At the city's heart, vast processing cores hum with biological computation, their neural networks visible as glowing traceries beneath translucent bark. These living computers manage the resource flows, direct growth patterns, and maintain the delicate balance that keeps this impossible ecosystem from collapsing into chaos. Steam vents release excess heat from their calculations, creating pillars of warm air that spiral up through the canopy.

This is post-water civilization, where every drop has been incorporated into the permanent structure of the plant-city hybrid. The vegetation has evolved beyond needing free water, having bound every molecule into its tissues, creating a closed loop of perpetual growth and recycling. Even the inhabitants — if any remain — must negotiate their moisture needs directly with the city itself, trading labor or nutrients for the privilege of hydration.

The horizon curves with the weight of vegetation, creating a valley of living matter that extends for kilometers in every direction. Other growth-cities are visible in the distance, their spore-clouds mingling in the upper air, cross-pollinating ideas and genetic material in a slow exchange that keeps the system evolving, always reaching for some unimaginable final form.
