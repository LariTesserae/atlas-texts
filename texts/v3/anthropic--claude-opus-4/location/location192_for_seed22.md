The Singing Hollows stretch across the lowland basin like a fever dream of architecture and jungle, where the morning condensation drips from bioluminescent canopies onto streets that hum with frequencies just beyond human comprehension. This is a city—or was, or will be—that exists in vast pockets of emptiness between its inhabited spaces, as if someone built a metropolis and then removed nine-tenths of its mass, leaving behind only the essential nodes floating in humid air.

The vegetation doesn't simply grow here; it computes. Massive fern-analogues unfurl fractal fronds that process information through their chlorophyll-quantum matrices, their surfaces slick with dew that pools and runs in precise channels down into the root-cable networks. Vines thick as bridges span the empty chasms between building-clusters, their internal structures honeycomb-hollow yet strong enough to support the weight of the prowling synthi-cats and the great boom-moths whose wing beats generate the thunderous ambient roar that never ceases.

Each district of this sprawling lowland settlement occupies its own acoustic pocket, separated by kilometers of resonant void. The architecture—if it can be called that—consists of grown structures that blur the line between organism and edifice. Towers of calcified sound stand like tuning forks, their surfaces riddled with precisely calculated holes that catch the humid wind and add their voices to the overwhelming chorus. The inhabitants built these spaces to amplify certain frequencies while canceling others, creating zones of relative quiet within the storm of noise where thought and communication become possible.

The fauna here evolved to navigate by echolocation through the dim light that filters down through the canopy-computation-arrays. Creatures with overdeveloped acoustic organs populate every ecological niche: shriek-worms that burrow through the sound-soft soil, leaving tunnels that whistle in harmonics; colonies of drum-beetles whose synchronized movements on resonant surfaces create a perpetual rhythm section; and the crown predators, the silence-hunters, which move in bubbles of acoustic cancellation that allow them to approach prey undetected through the wall of sound.

Water appears wherever the sound waves converge in standing patterns, condensing from the humid air into perfect droplets that hang suspended until disturbed. These acoustic wells provide hydration for the teeming life, but also serve as nodes in the city's beyond-physics infrastructure. The original builders—long since evolved into something unrecognizable—designed the settlement to use sound as both structure and medium, creating spaces that exist only when certain frequencies align.

The streets, where they exist, are carpeted in thick moss that deadens footfalls while sprouting tiny speaker-flowers that relay messages through ultrasonic networks. Buildings phase in and out of full materiality as the sound-architecture shifts, their walls sometimes solid carbon-composite, sometimes merely holograms of compressed air vibrating at specific frequencies. The technology operates on principles that treat wave-particle duality as a construction material, building reality from interference patterns.

In the perpetual dimness, bioluminescent markers trace paths through the air itself, guiding travelers between the sparse islands of inhabitation. The emptiness between spaces isn't truly empty—it thrums with potential, with the substrate of reality exposed and singing. Occasionally, a new structure will precipitate from this acoustic medium like a crystal forming from supersaturated solution, adding another node to the sprawling, sparse network of the city.

The whole landscape pulses with life despite its emptiness, or perhaps because of it. The vast spaces between solid anchors allow the sound to build and flow in patterns impossible in denser environments. Weather systems form from acoustic pressure alone—sound-storms that sweep across the basin, temporarily materializing structures and creatures from pure vibration before letting them dissolve back into the flux.

This is a place where absence has as much presence as substance, where the city's true architecture lies not in its built forms but in the spaces between them, and where the deafening chorus of existence shapes reality with every beat, buzz, and bellowing cry. The Singing Hollows demonstrate that a landscape need not be solid to be substantial, and that emptiness, properly orchestrated, can teem with more life than any packed metropolis of stone and steel.
