The Incubation Plains stretch beneath a bruised twilight that never shifts, where the sky hangs low and thick like a membrane about to tear. This is new land—or perhaps pre-land—where the fundamental geology hasn't quite decided what it wants to become. The ground exists in a state of perpetual almost-ness: crystalline formations push up through surfaces that shimmer between states of matter, neither fully solid nor liquid, creating vast expanses of what might generously be called terrain.

Heat radiates not from any sun but from the substrate itself, as if the world-stuff generates fever in its effort to exist. The temperature hovers at the edge of biological tolerance—hot enough that any water would instantly sublime, yet the heat carries an organic quality, pulsing in slow waves across the nascent plains. The few structures that dot this emptiness appear to be grown rather than built: translucent pods and branching towers that could be enormous seed casings or abandoned chrysalides, their surfaces etched with patterns too regular for nature, too alive for architecture.

These structures pulse with a faint bioluminescence that provides the only light beyond the dim, constant glow of the sky-membrane. Inside their transparent walls, incomprehensible machinery operates on principles that merge the biological and quantum mechanical—probability engines that seem to be waiting for something, processing the raw potential of the unformed landscape into... something else.

The fauna here defies counting or classification. They exist in overwhelming numbers but in forms so alien that the eye struggles to separate individual creatures from the environment itself. Some appear as mobile heat-mirages, vast sheets of living distortion that ripple across the crystalline ground. Others manifest as geometric parasites on reality—angular beings that exist partially in this dimension, feeding on the incomplete physics of the place. The largest inhabitants might be mistaken for weather systems: living electromagnetic fields that graze on the quantum uncertainty of the pre-formed matter, occasionally condensing into brief, terrible physicality before dispersing again.

Sparse vegetation takes forms that challenge the definition of plant life. Metallic ferns unfurl fractal fronds that exist in eleven dimensions, only three of which are visible at any given moment. Crystalline growths that might be mineral or vegetable extend root networks through probability space rather than soil. These scattered life forms cluster around the structures, feeding on exotic radiations or perhaps serving as antennae for the machinery within.

The silence here is profound but not complete. Occasionally, the crystalline ground resonates with subsonic frequencies—the growing pains of a world still learning how to be solid. The fauna contribute no sound in any conventional sense, though sensitive equipment might detect alterations in local spacetime as they pass, a kind of gravitational whisper. The structures emit a barely audible hum that exists more in the bones than the ears, a vibration that suggests vast processes occurring just beyond perception.

In the gaps between structures, the emptiness is absolute. The scale creates a terrible loneliness—you could walk for days between oases of almost-life, with only the heat-drunk fauna for company. The horizon shimmers not with mirages but with the uncertain boundary between what is and what might be. Distance becomes negotiable here; the same journey might take hours or weeks depending on how firmly reality has set in that particular region.

This is a place outside of time, or perhaps before it. Nothing changes because change implies a progression from one state to another, and here everything exists in perpetual becoming. The structures neither decay nor grow, the fauna neither evolve nor die—they simply are, locked in an eternal present tense. Even decay is impossible in a place that hasn't fully decided on the rules of entropy.

The Incubation Plains wait with infinite patience, a vast nursery for physics and biology that haven't yet been born. The scattered structures serve as anchor points in this sea of potential, their impossible machinery gently guiding the raw stuff of creation toward some unimaginable future state. Here, in the hot dim emptiness where overwhelmingly strange life forms browse on uncertainty itself, a new kind of world is slowly, eternally, learning how to exist.
