The cathedral-grove pulses with slow, wet breath. Here, in this sunken hollow barely larger than a village square, the air itself has become a living membrane—thick with moisture that condenses and re-evaporates in languid cycles, never quite becoming rain, never quite clearing to mist.

The vegetation grows in impossible density, each plant seemingly occupying the same space as its neighbors through some trick of folded reality. Vines spiral through vines, their leaves tessellating in patterns that hurt to follow with the eye. The canopy overhead forms not one layer but dozens, each slightly out of phase with the others, creating a green ceiling that shifts and realigns as you watch. No single tree stands alone—they've grown together into cathedral pillars of fused bark and shared heartwood, their boundaries dissolved.

The ground squelches with every step, though it never quite floods. Water seeps up between the root-mat that serves as floor, beading on every surface, trickling down the grove-pillars in sheets so thin they're more like mobile mirrors. The roots themselves have grown into dense lattices, creating levels and half-levels, platforms that might hold a person's weight or might not, depending on the hour and the humidity.

Heat radiates from the vegetation itself—not the dry heat of sun-baked earth, but the fevered warmth of rapid growth and decay happening simultaneously. Leaves unfurl while still attached to withering stems. Flowers bloom inside seed pods. The newest growth pushes through the old without waiting for it to fall away, creating layers upon layers of living matter in various states of transformation.

The silence here is absolute, yet somehow full. No insects buzz, no birds call, no animals rustle through the understory. Even the water makes no sound as it seeps and flows. The plants themselves grow in perfect quiet, their expansion and contraction too slow for the ear to catch, though the eye can perceive the motion if one watches long enough. It's the silence of a held breath, of processes happening just below the threshold of perception.

In the dim green light that filters through the phase-shifted canopy, distances become uncertain. What seems an arm's length away might be unreachable; what appears distant might brush against your shoulder. The light itself seems to pool in certain spots, avoiding others, creating pockets of deeper shadow where the vegetation grows even more impossibly dense, knotting in on itself until the space seems to fold.

The grove's edges are sharp and definite—one step you're in the crushing density of green, the next you're out in whatever lies beyond. But finding those edges proves difficult. The space curves back on itself, paths lead in spirals, and what seemed like the way out brings you back to the center where the air is thickest and the silence is loudest.

Time moves strangely here. Water drops hang in the air longer than they should. New shoots appear between one glance and the next, yet the overall shape of the grove remains constant, as if growth and decay have achieved perfect equilibrium. The youngest leaves shine with the same ancient patience as the fused trunks they spring from. Everything is perpetually fresh, perpetually emerging, perpetually about to begin, yet nothing ever quite changes.

This is a place of waiting, though what it waits for remains unclear. Perhaps for the first sound to break its silence. Perhaps for its dense growth to finally achieve whatever impossible geometry it reaches toward. Perhaps simply for someone to witness its slow, fevered dreaming before stepping back out into the ordinary world, carrying the memory of its humid breath and silent transformations.
