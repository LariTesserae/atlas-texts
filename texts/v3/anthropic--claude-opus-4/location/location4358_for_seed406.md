The Crystal Throat

You must crawl to enter, pressing your belly against cold stone until the passage widens into a cavity no larger than a modest bedroom. Here, the mountain has swallowed a garden.

Every surface bristles with growth—not the green of water-fed plants, but something else entirely. Crystalline fronds unfurl from mineral substrates, their translucent leaves catching and splitting the brilliant light that somehow floods this buried chamber. The illumination has no obvious source; it simply suffuses the space as if the air itself were incandescent, casting no shadows, revealing every detail with painful clarity.

The vegetation grows in impossible density. Prismatic moss carpets floor and walls in layers so thick your fingers sink to the knuckles when you touch it. From these cushions rise forests of glass-like stems, none taller than your forearm, each crowned with geometric blooms that spiral inward infinitely. The plants grow through each other, into each other, their transparent tissues interpenetrating without conflict. Some have leaves like frozen smoke. Others fruit with perfect mineral spheres that chime softly when disturbed—the only sound in this muffled space.

The air tastes of copper and ozone, dry as ancient paper. These plants have never known water. Instead, they feed on something else—perhaps the light itself, perhaps the slow electrical currents that must flow through the mountain's veins. Their metabolism runs on geological time. A flower might take a human lifetime to open.

The walls show signs of great age. Smooth hollows indicate where the vegetation has slowly dissolved and reformed the rock over millennia, creating organic curves in what was once sharp stone. The plants and their substrate have grown together so long they've become a single system—stone feeding crystal feeding light feeding stone again.

In the center of the chamber, where the ceiling rises to its highest point, grows the crown jewel: a tree-like structure of pure white crystal whose branches fill the available space so completely that they've grown into precise mathematical curves, following the chamber's contours like a three-dimensional puzzle solved over centuries. Its leaves are thin as mica sheets, and where they overlap, they create moiré patterns that hurt to look at directly.

The temperature here stays forever cool, regulated by the mountain's mass and the plants' slow chemistry. No wind disturbs the stillness. No insects browse the crystal leaves. This is a complete world, packed into a space smaller than most closets, thriving on principles that diverge from everything the sunlit world knows.

You realize the entrance you crawled through is the only gap in this solid mass of growth. Every other inch of space is occupied, utilized, transformed into living crystal. The chamber breathes with a rhythm so slow you'd need to watch for days to see the walls expand and contract by millimeters.

This is what the mountain dreams: gardens of glass flowering in its dark hollows, fed by light that comes from nowhere, growing so slowly and steadily that erosion itself becomes a kind of cultivation.
