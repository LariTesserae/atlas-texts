The ground heaves and bubbles, not yet solid—a landscape caught in the act of becoming. Vast sheets of cooling proto-rock stretch across the lowlands, their surfaces still plastic and warm, forming slow-motion waves that freeze mid-crest. Steam rises from a thousand hairline fractures where the crust hasn't quite sealed, creating pillars of mist that dance in the crushing heat.

Water seeps up through these incomplete seams—not in torrents but as a constant, insidious presence. It beads on every surface, runs in temporary rivulets that change course by the hour as the ground beneath shifts and settles. The air itself sweats, so thick with moisture that breathing feels like drowning in reverse. Where the water pools, it doesn't rest—it percolates, bubbles, hisses against the infant stone.

Scattered across this primordial workshop are the ruins of industry, or perhaps industry-in-progress—it's impossible to tell if these structures are being built or destroyed. Corroded metal frameworks rise from the semi-solid ground like the ribs of mechanical whales. Some still function: a pump station draws water from nowhere and releases it to everywhere, its diesel engine coughing black smoke into the already oppressive air. A half-buried turbine spins erratically, powered by the ground's own contractions.

The vegetation that manages to exist here grows in defiance of logic—heat-loving pioneer species with waxy, almost metallic leaves that shed water like mercury. They sprout in loose colonies wherever the ground has been stable for more than a few days, their roots drilling desperately downward before the next upheaval. Some have adapted to be mobile, dragging themselves across the plastic stone on modified root-legs, following the ever-shifting zones of stability.

The fauna thrives in this chaos. Creatures pour from cracks in the earth—things with too many joints and heat-dissipating frills, their bodies designed for a world without constants. They swarm around the industrial remnants, building nests in exhaust pipes, feeding on the chemical runoff from machinery. Their calls create a cacophony: metallicscraping mixed with organic trills, the sound of evolution arguing with itself about which direction to take.

Everything moves here. The ground ripples and reforms. Geysers of mud erupt without warning, coating everything in a layer of primordial ooze that bakes to ceramic hardness within minutes. The industrial equipment shifts and settles, occasionally disappearing entirely only to be belched up elsewhere, transformed by its journey through the earth's bowels. Even the horizon won't hold still—heat mirages make the distance dance, showing glimpses of structures and landscapes that might exist, or might have existed, or might never exist at all.

The sun beats down relentlessly, but the real heat comes from below. This is a landscape powered by its own becoming, where the distinction between construction and destruction has lost all meaning. Chains of small explosions ripple across the flats as pockets of gas find ignition sources. New islands of stability form and dissolve. The mechanical and biological and geological blend into a single system of perpetual emergence.

At night—if night means anything in a place where the ground glows with its own fever—the activity only intensifies. Bioluminescent creatures emerge to feast on the heat-drunk insects that swarm the machinery. The metal structures groan and sing as they cool and heat in irregular cycles. New formations birth themselves from the earth with sounds like breaking bones and crying engines.

This is not a place of destruction but of endless, chaotic genesis—a workshop where the planet tries on new faces, discarding each before it fully forms. The industrial remnants aren't ruins but ingredients, slowly being digested and incorporated into whatever this landscape is becoming. Time moves differently here, measured not in days or years but in cycles of formation and reformation, each iteration stranger than the last.

The water never stops seeping. The heat never breaks. The ground never quite decides what it wants to be. And through it all, life—mechanical, biological, and categories yet unnamed—finds a way to flourish in the space between states, thriving on transformation itself.
