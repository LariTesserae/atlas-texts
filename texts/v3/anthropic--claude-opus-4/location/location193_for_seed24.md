The shallow basin stretches perhaps two hundred paces across, its floor a fine powder of oxidized minerals that settled here countless ages ago. The dust is rust-orange and bone-white, accumulated in gentle drifts that follow patterns set by winds that no longer blow. The air hangs utterly still, warm and dry as an oven's breath, carrying the faint metallic taste of iron and copper compounds.

Scattered across this mineral field rise the sentinels — sparse, twisted forms that might once have been vegetation, now transmuted into something else entirely. Each stands alone, separated by empty spans of dust. They are not quite stone, not quite wood, but something between: fibrous columns that branch and fork like ancient trees, their surfaces pocked with tiny chambers where moisture once flowed. The warmth has preserved them in this half-state for millennia, neither fully alive nor properly fossilized. Their pale grey-brown forms catch what little light filters down, showing delicate internal structures like frozen circulatory systems.

The darkness here is not absolute but perpetual twilight. Above, a thick atmospheric ceiling of suspended particulates blocks most radiation, creating a world of subtle gradations between shadow and deeper shadow. The mineral dust itself provides the faintest luminescence — a phosphorescent quality that emerges only when one's eyes adjust, painting the basin floor in ghostly half-tones.

No wind disturbs this place. No creatures move through the warm shadows. The silence is so complete it has weight, broken only by the occasional soft whisper of dust settling into new configurations — a process so slow it might take decades for a handful to find its resting place. The basin exists in perfect stasis, its every grain and twisted sentinel locked in arrangements that have persisted since the last waters evaporated into the sealed atmosphere above.

At the basin's edges, low ridges of harder rock show the ancient limits of whatever process created this pocket of stillness. The stone is worn smooth, pitted with tiny holes that suggest this was once a different kind of place entirely — perhaps a spring-fed pool, or a bog where strange plants filtered minerals from the water. Now only the memory remains, written in the placement of each transmuted trunk and the patient accumulation of metallic dust.

Time moves differently here, or perhaps not at all. In this warm darkness, in this cathedral of silence and sparse monuments, the distinction between a moment and an eon dissolves into the settling dust.
