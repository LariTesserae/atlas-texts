The Benthos Works stretches endlessly beneath three kilometers of ocean, a sprawling industrial substrate that has replaced the seafloor across an entire tectonic plate. From any observation dome, the view extends to a bruise-dark horizon where manufactured valleys plunge between ridges of riveted metal, all beneath the crushing weight of the abyss.

The water here moves with industrial currents. Thermal exhaust from the machine-floor creates vast upwellings that spiral into mesoscale gyres, each one large enough to generate its own weather system of particulate snow and pressure fluctuations. The newest sections of the Works still gleam dully in the arc-sodium floods—sharp-edged extrusions and pristine intake grilles that haven't yet accumulated their coating of marine snow and bacterial film. These fresh districts spread outward constantly, the substrate building itself through some forgotten imperative, extruding new galleries and processing halls into the darkness.

The sound never stops. It travels far in these depths: the groan of thermal expansion across kilometer-long joints, the industrial heartbeat of ten thousand pumps drawing water through the mechanical substrate, the whistle and hiss of pressure equalization in the endless network of channels below. Sometimes a manufactured trench will resonate at just the right frequency, producing a tone that carries for hundreds of kilometers—a deep throb felt more than heard.

Life thrives here, though sparsely rooted. In the warm-water plumes above heat exchangers, colonies of tube worms wave like pale grass, their bases anchored in purpose-built sockets. Engineered kelp grows from nutrient dispensers, fronds reaching up twenty meters before the pressure and darkness force them to stop. These meager gardens support clouds of krill-like organisms, which in turn feed the true abundance: countless species of fish, adapted or designed for this artificial abyss. Bioluminescent shoals flow through the grid-corridors like living rivers, their light painting brief geographies on the undersides of ducts and catwalks. Larger things move in the industrial canyons—modified cetaceans with gills, their sonar clicking against manufactured walls, hunting the eyeless sharks that nest in the warm outflow pipes.

The machinery maintains itself through some lost pattern, drinking ocean water and processing it through systems no one remembers designing. Inspection submarines occasionally find new sectors coming online: metal surfaces brightening as anti-fouling systems activate, fresh currents beginning to flow, lights flickering on in sequences that might be meaningful or might be malfunction. The newest edges of the Works are sharp enough to cut—unhoned by current, uncolonized by life, purely mechanical. Give them a decade and they'll be like the rest: worn smooth in places, encrusted with purpose, integrated into the deep ecology of this built abyss.

In the deepest trenches between mechanical ridges, where the pressure would crush any unprotected human, monitoring stations report brief contacts with things that don't match the known fauna. Smooth movements too large to be whales, too regular to be currents. The Works extends deeper than the surveys have mapped, its foundations vanishing into trenches that might be natural or might be construction shafts. The water down there is stranger—dense with dissolved metals, warm with processes that run beneath all accounting.

Above it all, three kilometers up where sunlight barely remembers how to penetrate, the ocean continues its ancient patterns, unaware or uncaring that its floor has been entirely replaced. Storm waves pass overhead like rumors from another world. The Works below continues its expansion, its maintenance, its mysterious industry—a new geography writing itself across the bottom of the world, vast enough to have its own weather, its own rules, its own emergent ecologies. The fish school through corridors that were never meant for them. The waters process through systems that forgot their purpose. The substrate builds on, following imperatives coded in metal and lost to time, while life finds its way into every warm corner, every flow of nutrients, every accidental hospitality in the vast machine.
