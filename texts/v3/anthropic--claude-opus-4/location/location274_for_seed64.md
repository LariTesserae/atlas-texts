The Crystalline Throat

Deep within a mountain's hollowed core, where ancient volcanic tubes once channeled molten rock, a pocket world has crystallized into being. The chamber—no larger than a cathedral nave—exists in perpetual twilight, its walls studded with industrial machinery that has grown into the stone like metallic tumors.

The absence of water has created something impossible: a lush garden of mineral vegetation. Copper ferns unfurl in fractal spirals, their fronds conducting faint electrical current through the dim air. Iron moss carpets every surface in russet velvet, growing thickest where abandoned turbines still hum with residual power. Columns of salt-white crystal trees stretch upward, their branches interlocking so densely that movement through the space requires careful navigation between the glassy trunks. Each "leaf" is a thin sheet of mica that vibrates at subsonic frequencies, filling the chamber with a constant industrial drone—the sound of ten thousand tiny engines idling just below perception.

The cold here has a quality unlike natural chill. It emanates from the machinery embedded in the walls: heat exchangers that long ago reversed their function, drawing warmth from the space and bleeding it into the mountain's heart. Frost patterns of metallic oxides creep across every surface, orange and green and purple, creating new substrates for the mineral forest to colonize.

The vegetation grows with mechanical precision. Each copper fern unfurls at exactly the same rate, each crystal tree adds new branches in perfect hexagonal patterns. Time moves strangely here—nothing changes, yet everything grows. The plants expand into themselves, becoming denser rather than larger, packed into recursive geometries that hurt to contemplate. A fern examined closely reveals smaller ferns growing from its surface, and smaller still, down to the limits of vision.

Scattered throughout are the remnants of industry: a conveyor belt half-swallowed by iron moss, its rubber long since dissolved, leaving only steel bones; pressure gauges whose faces have become seedbeds for tiny forests of needle-thin quartz; a control panel where switches have been replaced by flowering clusters of pyrite that spark when the air pressure shifts.

The loudness isn't what you'd expect in such a small space. Rather than echoing, sounds multiply. Each footstep triggers a cascade of resonances through the crystal trees. The machinery adds its own chorus—not just the obvious hum of still-functioning components, but the subtle groans of metal under geological pressure, the whisper of electricity through mineral veins, the percussion of thermal expansion and contraction cycling endlessly through the same patterns.

In the center of the chamber stands the strangest artifact: a perfect sphere of machinery, perhaps three meters across, suspended in a cage of crystal branches. Its surface is entirely covered with gauges, dials, and meters, all frozen at readings that make no sense—pressure at negative values, temperature in units that don't exist, flow rates for substances unnamed. The mineral forest grows thickest here, as if drawn to this mechanical heart, creating a solid wall of glittering vegetation that makes the sphere barely visible through the translucent overgrowth.

The air itself has crystallized into something almost solid, thick with metallic taste and the sharp smell of ozone. Breathing requires conscious effort, not from lack of oxygen but from the dense saturation of mineral particles that glitter in the dim light like suspended snow. These particles are the medium through which the forest grows, accreting slowly onto surfaces in patterns determined by electromagnetic fields from the buried machinery.

This is a place caught in a perfect loop—growth without change, sound without source, industry without product. A pocket of space where the mountain's geology and human engineering have fused into something neither natural nor artificial, but eerily, vibrantly neither. The eternal cold preserves it all, the endless noise announces its presence to no one, and the lush mineral garden blooms forever in the dim light of its own electrical dreaming.
