The salt city spreads across the ancient plateau like a coral reef lifted from primordial seas. At this elevation, where the air grows thin and clouds pass below rather than above, millennia of mineral deposits have accumulated into a sprawling metropolis of crystalline towers and sodium streets. No human hand built this place—the architecture arose from patient chemistry, from the endless cycle of moisture and evaporation that has shaped these heights since before life crawled from distant oceans.

In the cool morning, condensation beads on the translucent spires, trickling down through channels worn smooth by countless repetitions. The water follows the same paths it has traced for ages, depositing microscopic layers of dissolved minerals before evaporating in the afternoon sun. Some towers have grown hollow, their cores eaten away by this patient erosion, creating apartments and galleries where creatures nest.

The fauna here defies easy categorization. Generations of adaptation to this mineral world have rendered them nearly geological themselves—scale-backed browsers whose carapaces incorporate the very salts they consume, their shells becoming part of the city's substrate when they die. Smaller creatures move through the crystalline latticework like schools of fish through coral, their bodies translucent and shot through with mineral veins. They feed not on vegetation but on the biofilms that form wherever moisture lingers, and on each other in slow, deliberate hunts through the salt maze.

The streets—if they can be called that—formed where ancient drainage patterns carved valleys between the crystal growth. During the brief afternoon rains, these channels fill with brine that flows in temporary rivers toward the plateau's edge, carrying dissolved minerals that will precipitate out in new formations downstream. The sound of this flowing is the city's heartbeat, a quiet rushing that comes and goes with the weather.

At certain points where multiple drainage channels converge, vast halls have formed—cathedral spaces with walls of banded mineral deposits recording millions of years of accumulation. The creatures gather here during the rains, clustering on ledges above the flowing brine. Their quiet calls echo off the salt walls, a sound like wind chimes made of stone.

The light here plays tricks. The crystalline structures refract and reflect, creating a labyrinth of illumination even on overcast days. Rainbow patterns shift across surfaces as the sun tracks overhead. In some of the deeper galleries, bioluminescent bacteria native to the ancient salt deposits provide a steady blue-green glow, sustained by chemical reactions in the mineral matrix.

This is a place of profound slowness, where change happens on geological timescales. Yet it is vibrantly alive—the creatures moving through their mineral maze, the daily cycle of condensation and evaporation, the patient accumulation and erosion that builds and rebuilds the city continuously. Even now, in chambers too small for any creature to enter, new crystals are forming, adding their own minute contributions to this ancient, ever-growing metropolis of salt.
