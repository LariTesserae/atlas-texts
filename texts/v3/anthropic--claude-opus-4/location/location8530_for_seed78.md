You rise through the vertical sea.

This is a column of warm water suspended in air, a living tower that defies gravity through mechanisms your instruments cannot parse. The water holds its shape without walls—a perfect cylinder perhaps three hundred meters across, rising from mist-shrouded depths below to disappear into low-hanging cumulus above. You float within it, breathing normally despite full submersion, the liquid somehow both water and air.

The vegetation grows in three-dimensional freedom here. Kelp forests spiral upward without ocean floor to anchor them, their holdfasts gripping nothing but water itself. Massive pneumatocysts—gas bladders the size of houses—keep the plants suspended at different depths, creating a vertical ecosystem of terraces and clearings. The fronds are wrong though: too thick, too structured, their surfaces riddled with hexagonal chambers that pulse with bioluminescent signals. When you touch one, it responds with a subsonic thrum that makes your bones ache.

Between the kelp towers, smaller plants drift like aerial plankton. Some are clearly engineered—their leaves perfect fractals that seem to be computing something as they photosynthesize. Others might be natural, though in this place the distinction blurs. A carpet of phosphorescent moss has colonized one of the larger pneumatocysts, transforming it into a glowing green moon suspended in the aqueous atmosphere.

The sound here is overwhelming. Not the muffled quiet of normal underwater spaces, but a cacophony of biological machinery. The kelp towers creak and groan as they sway in thermal currents. Somewhere far above, something vast breathes—a rhythmic rushing that might be wind through leaves or might be something else entirely. The engineered plants add their own layer: high-frequency chittering, data-dense and urgent, a forest calculating its own growth in real-time.

You swim upward through a gap in the canopy and find one of the structures. It's more growth than building—a node where dozens of kelp strands have woven themselves into a dense knot. Inside the tangle, you glimpse architecture: passages lined with that same hexagonal pattern, spaces that might be rooms, surfaces too smooth and purposeful to be natural. But it's all made of plant matter, all alive, all part of the forest's body. Thin filaments extend from the structure into the surrounding water, sampling, processing, communicating with the greater organism.

A school of something darts past—not quite fish, not quite birds, their bodies elongated and translucent. They move in perfect synchronization, turning as one to avoid a drifting mass of vegetation. These are the only animals you've seen, though the forest's sounds suggest larger things moving in the depths between the kelp towers. Perhaps they're rare, or perhaps they're simply good at hiding in this vertical maze.

The warmth is constant, sourceless. The water itself seems to generate heat through some metabolic process, maintaining a temperature just above blood-warm. Where cooler currents intrude from outside the column, condensation forms, creating brief waterfalls that cascade through the water-air before dispersing. These thermal boundaries shimmer with refraction patterns that hurt to look at directly—another sign that physics works differently here.

You notice the youth of it all in the sharp edges of the kelp fronds, the unworn surfaces of the embedded structures, the clarity of the water itself. This ecosystem hasn't had time to accumulate detritus. No sediment settles in this gravity-defying sea. The plants grow with the desperate vigor of early succession, racing upward to claim their piece of the vertical territory. Some of the engineered elements still show tool marks—molecule-precise scarring where something built or modified them. Whatever created this place, it hasn't been gone long.

At the column's edge, the water maintains its impossible boundary through surface tension scaled up beyond reason. You can press your hand against it and feel the membrane flex without breaking. Beyond is normal air, normal physics, a mountainside dropping away into morning mist. But here inside, the warm water extends up and up, a tower of living ocean reaching toward clouds, its vegetation computing futures in frequencies you can't hear, its rare fauna darting between fronds that might be thinking, its scattered architectural nodes suggesting purposes you can only guess at.

The roar of growth fills your ears. The forest is building itself, even now, even as you watch. Each plant is both individual and component, each structure both shelter and organ. You float in the bloodstream of something vast and newly born, something teaching itself to be alive in defiance of natural law.
