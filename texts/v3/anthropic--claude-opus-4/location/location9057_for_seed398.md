The Canopy Engines stretch endlessly beneath a ceiling of interwoven bark and living wood, a vast industrial underdark where the concept of sky has been replaced by the breathing architecture of a single, continental organism. This is not a forest with trees, but rather one tree that has become a world—its trunk system a labyrinthine network of woody corridors kilometers in diameter, branching and merging in patterns that follow their own botanical logic across impossible distances.

The heat here is organic, metabolic. It radiates from the walls themselves, from the endless cellular respiration of cambium layers thick as city blocks. The air tastes of tannins and hot resin, moves in slow convection currents through hollow spaces large enough to generate their own weather systems—wood-scented thermals that rise through vertical galleries until they cool against upper reaches where the bark grows thick with condensed resins. No water exists here in any free form; what moisture the great tree requires moves in sealed vessels larger than subway tunnels, their contents forever locked away from the hollow spaces where other life might have dwelt.

The emptiness is profound. Between the curving walls of heartwood, spaces open into vaulted chambers that could hold entire cities, yet contain only air and the subdued orange glow of bioluminescent fungi that trace the wood grain in patterns visible only at tremendous scale. The tree's growth rings form terraced cliffs in these spaces, each ring a geological epoch of plant time, their surfaces worn smooth by the patient circulation of air across centuries. Sound carries strangely here—the constant deep groaning of wood adjusting to its own weight creates a bass note felt more than heard, punctuated by sharp cracks like thunder as stress fractures form and heal in the living architecture.

Scattered throughout this wooden cosmos, the remnants of industry molt like shed skins. Factories built into natural hollows in the wood continue their work unmanned, their brass and iron mechanisms powered by the tree's own electrochemical potential. Copper cables thick as ancient vines pierce the bark to draw current from the living wood, feeding production lines that stamp out components for unknown purposes. The machines run on biological time, their conveyor belts moving imperceptibly, completing perhaps one production cycle per year. Catwalks and maintenance ladders built for workers who no longer exist spiral around the machinery, their railings polished smooth by hands that haven't touched them for untold ages.

The industrial systems have achieved a perfect symbiosis with their host. Exhaust vents feed directly into the tree's respiratory system, their carbon dioxide absorbed and processed. The machines' waste heat contributes to the ambient temperature that keeps the great tree's metabolism running in its twilight realm. In return, the tree has grown around and through the industrial infrastructure, incorporating it into its body, sealing joints with resin, replacing broken parts with specially formed wood that mimics the original components' function.

At the grandest scale, air currents move through this place like trade winds, following paths carved by the tree's growth and the placement of the ancient machinery. Hot air rises from the factory sectors, cools as it travels through upper galleries where the wood grows denser, then sinks again in convection cells large enough to take days to complete their circuit. These winds carry the sound of the place—the industrial heartbeat of mechanisms that will run until the tree dies, the settling of wood that has held its form for millennia, the whisper of air through spaces too large to comprehend from any single vantage point.

This is a place locked in perfect stasis, where the concept of change has been reduced to the microscopic advance of growth rings and the wearing of metal against metal in machines that repeat their cycles with geological patience. The tree-world continues its metabolism, the factories continue their production, and the vast empty spaces between them remain undisturbed, holding their breath in the warm, resinous darkness.
