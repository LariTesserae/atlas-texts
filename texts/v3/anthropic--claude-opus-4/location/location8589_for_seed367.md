High above the cloud layer, where the air thins to whispers, the Terraces of Kylonn stretch across mountaintops that have been wholly transformed. Each surface gleams with engineered moisture—a million microscopic condensers woven into the substrate itself, harvesting water from the sparse atmosphere and weeping it constantly down crystalline channels that sing at frequencies just beyond hearing.

The cold here is absolute, held at precise temperatures by the thermal exchangers embedded in every surface. Ice forms and reforms in calculated patterns, creating a landscape of perpetual frost flowers that bloom and sublimate according to algorithms written into the mountain's bones. The original stone has long since been replaced, molecule by molecule, with materials that breathe and sweat and regulate themselves.

Vegetation erupts from every possible surface in dense, engineered abundance. These are no natural plants but hybrid constructs—part biological, part mechanical—that photosynthesize using the overwhelming light that floods this altitude. The light itself is wrong: too bright, too white, too many spectrums. It comes not just from the sun but from a thousand thousand micro-suns embedded in the cliff faces, in the overhangs, in structures that curve impossibly overhead, defying what should be possible at this elevation.

The terraces step upward and upward, each level packed with infrastructure so dense it appears almost organic. Moisture collection systems interweave with root networks. Thermal regulators nestle against hydroponic gardens. Everything touches everything else in an endless web of connection. Catwalks and platforms spiral between levels, their surfaces slick with engineered condensation that never quite freezes, never quite evaporates.

The sound is overwhelming—not from any single source but from the accumulation of a billion tiny processes. Water trickles through ten million channels. Air whistles through carefully calibrated openings. The growth-substrates hum with electrical current. The condensers whine at ultrasonic frequencies. Together they create a white noise so dense it becomes almost tangible, pressing against eardrums with physical weight.

Occasionally, something moves in the green density—not quite animal, not quite machine. These rare fauna-constructs tend the systems, their bodies perfectly adapted to the cold, the moisture, the blinding light. They move slowly, deliberately, their metabolisms tuned to the patient rhythms of the mountain. Days pass between their footsteps. Years between their maintenance cycles.

The whole system extends across valleys, over peaks, bridging impossible gaps with structures that seem to float but are actually held by forces that twist local physics into new shapes. In some places, water flows upward. In others, ice forms in spirals that reach toward the blazing artificial suns. The boundary between natural and constructed has not just blurred but been entirely erased—this is a landscape that never knew nature, built from the quantum level up according to principles that make their own rules.

And through it all, the water flows—condensing, channeling, pooling, evaporating, condensing again—in a cycle that feeds the lush engineered growth, powers the systems, and creates a perpetual mist that catches the too-bright light and throws it back in spectra human eyes were never meant to see.
