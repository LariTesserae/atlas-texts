The Brass Metropole stretches across the highland plateau like a fevered dream of industry, its copper-and-bronze towers rising from beds of crushed sulfur sand. No rain has fallen here in recorded memory—the atmosphere is too thin, too dry, too poisoned with metallic vapors that shimmer in waves above the foundry districts.

The city breathes through ten thousand vents. Steam erupts in sudden geysers from pressure-points where the underground forges connect to the surface, each blast carrying the tang of hot metal and rendered fat. The vents wheeze and sigh between eruptions, the only sound that punctuates the strange quiet of this place—for despite the constant motion, despite the endless industry, the Metropole makes almost no noise. The workers long ago evolved past vocal cords, communicating through pheromone clouds and the percussion of tools on metal. The machines run on principles that produce heat and motion but no sound, their gears meshing in perfect silence.

Everywhere, the creatures swarm. They are not quite insects, not quite crustaceans—chitinous beings with too many joints and oil-black eyes that navigate by infrared. They pour through the streets in rivers of clicking legs and twitching antennae, clustering at the feeding stations where synthetic proteins bubble up from underground vats. Some have been modified for specific tasks: welders with acetylene glands in their abdomens, carriers with cargo-spaces carved into their backs, scouts with elongated limbs for scaling the vast towers. They work without pause, dismantling and rebuilding sections of the city in an endless cycle of renewal and decay.

The architecture defies conventional understanding. Buildings grow from seed-forges, their metal bones extending upward through accretion and crystallization. When a structure reaches its breaking point—warped by heat-stress or weakened by the constant vibration of deep-earth industry—the swarm descends to consume it, breaking it down to base elements that will feed the next generation of construction. Nothing stays the same for long. A plaza that exists at dawn might be a pit by noon, a tower by evening. The only constants are the great anchor-pillars that descend unknown depths into the plateau, around which the city swirls like a living thing.

The light comes hard and yellow through the particulate haze, filtered by countless tons of metallic dust that never settles. It casts everything in shades of amber and rust, making the chrome fixtures gleam like trapped suns. At the city's edges, where the sprawl thins to scattered outposts and abandoned refineries, the plateau drops away into deeper badlands of slag and calcified industrial waste. Here the fauna grows stranger—feral machines that have evolved beyond their original programming, hybrid creatures that blur the line between biological and mechanical, colonies of living metal that spread across the cliff-faces like technological lichen.

The heat is crushing, radiating up from the forge-hearts and down from the too-close sun. The air shimmers with it, making the distant towers waver like mirages. In the deep production districts, where the real work happens, temperatures soar beyond biological tolerance. Only the adapted creatures can survive there, their organs replaced with heat-resistant alternatives, their blood running with coolant instead of water.

And through it all, the silence presses down—not peaceful, but pregnant with suppressed energy, like the moment before a boiler explodes. The Brass Metropole is never still, never stable, never complete. It exists in a state of perpetual becoming, each moment bringing new configurations, new purposes, new forms rising from the creative destruction of the old. The creatures swarm. The forges breathe. The city transforms itself, over and over, under the pitiless yellow sky.
