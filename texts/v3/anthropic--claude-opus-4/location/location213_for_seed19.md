The Ossuary Plateau spreads beneath a pale sky, its surface a vast expanse of compressed bone meal and calcified remains ground fine by geological ages. Here, at these extreme heights where the air grows thin and crystalline, the ancient grinding of the world has reduced everything to its final state—a powder-white tableland stretching to distant ridges that blur into the sky.

The fauna are everywhere and nowhere. They exist as calcified impressions pressed into the bone-dust surface, as shadows that move without bodies to cast them, as the faint rustling of chitin and scale in windless air. Creatures phase partially into existence—a wing materializing to beat once before dissolving, an eye opening in the pale ground to track movement before sinking away. The overwhelming presence of life exists in states between states, neither fully here nor fully gone.

What sparse vegetation grows takes strange forms. Thin filaments of something like fungus, but crystalline, push through the compacted surface in geometric patterns. These formations grow only along the stress fractures where reality has worn thin, feeding on some property of space itself rather than nutrients. They emit no sound, cast shadows at wrong angles, and occasionally flicker between existing and not.

Small cairns dot the plateau—primitive structures of stacked bone-stones that mark... something. Whether built by conscious hands or accumulated through stranger processes remains unclear. Some contain simple tools of knapped bone and beaten copper that feel older than they should be possible. The tools sometimes duplicate themselves when unobserved, or age backwards, or remember shapes they've never held.

The temperature hovers at that precise coolness where breath might fog but never does, where exposed skin prickles with cold that doesn't quite arrive. The light comes evenly from the entire dome of sky, casting no shadows except where the shadow-fauna move, creating a sourceless illumination that makes distances difficult to judge.

In the silence, only the faintest sounds emerge: the settling of dust that never settles, the distant keening of creatures that exist in adjacent possibilities, the subsonic thrum of the plateau itself slowly completing its eons-long process of becoming perfectly, eternally flat. Time moves but nothing changes—each moment identical to the last, each grain of bone dust locked in its final position while somehow still shifting when unobserved.

The density reveals itself in layers. Look closely at any square meter and find it packed with frozen history—fragments of a thousand different creatures compressed into the same space, existing simultaneously in the bone-meal matrix. The very air holds too much, occupied by the partial manifestations of the fauna that should not be able to coexist but do, creating a cramped vastness where everything is both too close and infinitely far.

At the edges where the plateau begins its descent, reality grows more stable, but here at the heights, the rules have worn through. Distance becomes negotiable. The same cairn can be reached by walking straight for an hour or by standing still until it arrives. The phantom fauna follow patterns that would require them to be in multiple places at once, and so they are.

This is a place in its final form, ground down to essential elements, yet somehow teeming with impossible life. An ending that refuses to end, cycling through its last moment forever, dense with absence, loud with silence, ancient beyond age yet somehow still becoming more itself with each unchanging instant.
