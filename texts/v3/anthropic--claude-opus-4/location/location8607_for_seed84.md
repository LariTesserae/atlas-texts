The Cistern of Perpetual Dripping

You descend through a narrow slot between worked stones, following the sound of water that never quite becomes a stream. The air grows thick and warm as you move deeper, carrying the mineral scent of wet limestone and something else—a sweetness like overripe fruit.

The chamber opens before you, no larger than a village shrine. Every surface has been carved, though the work is crude—chisel marks still visible where someone long ago hollowed this space from living rock. The walls weep continuously, beaded with condensation that forms and reforms in the humid air. These drops follow shallow grooves carved into the stone, channels that guide the water along spiraling paths toward the center.

The floor slopes gently inward to a shallow depression, perhaps three paces across, where the collected water forms a pool no deeper than your ankles. The water is perfectly still despite the constant dripping—each drop absorbed without ripple into the glassy surface. Stone lips ring the pool, worn smooth by countless hands or bodies.

Above, the ceiling vaults into darkness. Someone has carved crude spirals there too, and the moisture follows these paths in patient rotation. The only light filters down from somewhere high above—a gray, indirect glow that suggests distant daylight through many turns of stone. It's enough to see by, barely. Enough to make out the shapes that cluster in the shadows.

They hang from every carved protrusion, fill every niche and ledge—creatures like fist-sized sacks of wet leather, their skin translucent and pink as raw meat. Hundreds of them. Thousands. Their bodies pulse with slow, synchronized breathing. Occasionally one shifts position with a wet sound like lips parting, revealing the hint of soft teeth. They are warm-blooded; you can feel the heat they generate in this enclosed space, keeping the temperature constant, keeping the water cycling.

The creatures do not flee your presence. They barely acknowledge it. They have been here so long, generation upon generation in this unchanging sanctuary, that fear has been forgotten. They feed on the minerals in the water, on the microscopic life that blooms in the pool, on their own elderly and dead. Perfect recycling in a closed system.

Someone built this place. The tool marks suggest bronze chisels, perhaps copper. The geometry speaks of purpose—not shelter but something more specific. A battery, perhaps. A womb. A prayer. The creatures were placed here, or drew here, or were cultivated here. Now they simply are, as much a part of the architecture as the carved channels, the weeping walls, the eternal circulation of moisture in this warm, dim cavity.

Time moves differently here. The dripping has no rhythm—each drop falls when surface tension breaks, creating a pattern that never quite repeats. The creatures breathe. The water cycles. The stone channels guide their thin streams in patient spirals. Nothing changes because everything is always in motion, perfectly balanced.

You could stay here. Something about the place invites it—the warmth, the soft sounds of water and breathing, the sense of being held within something complete and self-sufficient. But you are not part of this system. Your presence introduces chaos, your breath adds moisture the equations don't account for. Already the creatures nearest you have begun to shift, their synchronized breathing disrupted by the new rhythm of your lungs.

The way out remains—that narrow slot between stones. But for now you stand ankle-deep in the ancient pool, watching drops follow their carved paths, listening to the whisper of a thousand small lives in perfect, purposeless equilibrium.
