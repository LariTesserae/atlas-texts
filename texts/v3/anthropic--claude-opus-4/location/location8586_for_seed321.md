The city breathes water through ten thousand broken mouths.

From this height, the settlement spreads across the valley like a vast coral formation, its buildings grown rather than built—crystalline structures that weep perpetually from their porous surfaces. The architecture follows no human logic. Towers spiral upward in helical curves, their surfaces riddled with channels that guide the endless condensation into cascading veils. Between them, narrower structures lean and merge, creating dense corridors where the sound of falling water builds into a physical pressure.

The roar is overwhelming. Water moves through this place in every possible way—trickling, gushing, seeping, misting—and each surface amplifies it. The buildings themselves hum with resonance, their hollow chambers and twisted passages creating organ pipes that sing with the water's passage. Stone walkways, worn smooth by centuries of flow, carry streams that split and merge according to rules that shift when no one watches. Sometimes the water flows upward, defying comprehension, climbing walls in spreading films before releasing in curtains that hang suspended too long in the humid air.

Vegetation erupts from every crevice with desperate vitality. Moss carpets spread up walls in patterns that suggest written language, while vines thick as arms weave through window-spaces that may never have held glass. Trees grow sideways from building faces, their roots threading through the porous stone to drink from internal reservoirs. Their leaves are broad and waxy, evolved to shed the constant moisture, and they smell of copper and ozone. In the dense spaces between structures, where the canopy blocks most light, phosphorescent fungi paint the walls in pale blue-green constellations.

The heat is oppressive, made worse by the saturation. Air and water merge into something almost solid. Breathing feels like drowning in reverse. The atmosphere shimmers with suspended droplets that refract light into brief, impossible spectra—colors that shouldn't exist, that make the eyes water trying to process them. Sometimes these droplets hang motionless, defying the constant motion everywhere else, like gaps in reality's fabric.

And there are gaps. Places where the water falls but never lands. Corridors that lead to rooms containing themselves. Buildings that appear solid from one angle dissolve into mist from another. The sound itself tears holes—moments where the deafening roar inverts into absolute silence so complete it feels like suffocation, before crashing back with renewed force. During these reality hiccups, observers report seeing the city's true form: something vast and breathing beneath the familiar stones, something for which the buildings are merely pores or gills.

Wildlife is scarce and strange. Occasionally, something moves in peripheral vision—too quick to identify, leaving only the sensation of wrongness. Bird calls echo from empty sky. Shadows of large creatures ripple across walls with no visible source. The few confirmed animals are altered: salamanders with too many legs that navigate the vertical streams, their skin transparent enough to watch their triple hearts beat; moths that drink sound instead of nectar, growing larger near the roar's epicenter; fish that swim through the air in the densest mist zones, their scales rattling like rain.

The city has no visible inhabitants, yet it maintains itself. Damaged sections heal like wounds. New growths appear overnight—a fresh tower budding from a rooftop, a bridge extending across a gap that wasn't there before. The water redistributes constantly, finding new patterns, as if the entire settlement is a single vast organism testing configurations. 

At the center, where the sound reaches volumes that crack stone, something pulses. A heartbeat in the cacophony. The buildings lean inward here, drawn like iron filings to a magnet, their highest reaches twisted into shapes that hurt to perceive. Water flows upward in great columns, dispersing into clouds that rain immediately back down. The cycle is visible here—the settlement drinking and weeping, drinking and weeping, in a rhythm that suggests purpose without revealing meaning.

The warmth never varies, day or night. The moisture never lessens. The sound never truly stops. This is a place held in perpetual climax, a fever dream of growth and flow and noise that has found its own twisted equilibrium. It exists at the boundary between states—liquid and solid, built and grown, real and impossible—thriving in the spaces between definitions.

Those who find this place rarely speak of leaving. The city incorporates all things eventually, they say, their voices joining the water's endless song.
