The Whisper Plateaus stretch endlessly beneath three suns that never set, their light so intense it has bleached color itself from the world. This is a place of contradictions that shouldn't exist—a vast urban sprawl built from carved bone and crystallized silence, where primitive tools have shaped a city the size of continents.

The ground is young volcanic glass, still sharp enough to sing when the rare winds touch it. Black obsidian flows frozen mid-cascade form the foundation, their edges keen as fresh-struck flint. No water has ever touched these heights—the air is so dry it pulls moisture from anything that dares contain it. The city-builders compensated by drilling deep into the glass, following veins of frozen sound down to where they could mine the echoes of ancient storms.

The settlement spreads in all directions, but sparsely—each structure separated by leagues of empty glass plain. Buildings rise as towers of fused bone, harvested from the overwhelming fauna that somehow thrives here without water, without plants, without any recognizable food chain. These creatures—if creatures they are—exist in defiance of biology. They are geometric impossibilities that graze on light itself, their bodies composed of nested angles that hurt to perceive directly. Vast herds of them drift between the bone towers like living architecture, silent despite their size.

The builders were primitive in tools but sophisticated in understanding. Using simple copper chisels and an intuitive grasp of resonance, they carved the bones into acoustic chambers that trap and slow time itself. Inside each tower, moments stretch like taffy. A dropped pebble takes hours to hit the floor. Conversations begun in the morning conclude by nightfall, though night never comes in this place of eternal noon.

The cold here is absolute zero's peculiar cousin—a cold that exists alongside blazing light, a cold that preserves without freezing. The fauna cluster around the towers not for warmth but for temporal shelter. In the slowed-time fields, they can rest from the exhausting business of existing in too many dimensions at once.

Sound barely exists. The creatures move in perfect silence. The city's inhabitants—long since evolved beyond their original forms—communicate through patterns scratched in bone, through the arrangement of mirror-shards that reflect thoughts instead of images. The only sounds are the faint crystalline notes when wind touches the glass plains, and even these are swallowed by the vast scale of the place.

The laws of physics have developed local variants here. Gravity pulls in several directions at once, allowing the bone towers to grow in spirals that would elsewhere collapse. Light pools in hollows like a liquid. The fauna leave tracks that exist in the past and future simultaneously but never in the present.

Change comes slowly to the Whisper Plateaus. The bone towers grow by microscopic increments as new creatures are harvested and their remains fused to the sprawl. The glass plains spread outward as fresh lava emerges and instantly vitrifies in the dry, cold brightness. The builders continue their patient work, using their primitive tools to shape grand designs that won't be complete for geological ages.

This is urban planning on a timeline that makes civilizations look like mayflies. This is a city being built by a people who discovered they had all the time in the world, and then discovered they had even more than that. The Whisper Plateaus are what happens when primitive technology meets infinite patience in a place where the rules have come undone, where evolution has given up and decided to try something entirely new.
