The Plateau of Perpetual Assembly rises thirty kilometers above the surrounding lowlands, a vast tabletop of raw mineral substrate where the world is still learning how to be. Here, in the thin air and brilliant light, a sprawling city grows itself from the ground up, molecule by molecule, in a process so gradual it seems almost still.

The foundation is not water but flowing mercury—rivers of quicksilver that move through channels carved by patient acids, carrying dissolved metals and rare earths to construction sites. Where these streams converge, crystalline towers precipitate out of solution, growing at a rate of perhaps a meter per decade. The city spreads across thousands of square kilometers, its buildings scattered like seeds waiting to germinate, separated by expanses of bare mineral ground that gleam copper and chrome under the harsh, unfiltered sun.

The vegetation here has never known water. Instead, forests of metallic ferns spread their fronds to collect static electricity from the dry wind. They grow in dense groves around the mercury tributaries, their silver leaves chiming softly when the breeze picks up. Between the groves, the land stretches empty—oxidized plains of rust-red and verdigris, broken only by the occasional emergence of a new building foundation, pushing up through the crust like a geometric mushroom.

The creatures that thrive here breathe methane and excrete ceramics. Herds of silicon-based grazers move slowly across the mineral fields, their crystalline shells refracting rainbows as they process raw ore into more complex compounds. Smaller creatures—things like jeweled beetles the size of housecats—tend the growing buildings, secreting catalysts that guide the precipitation process. Birds with wings of beaten gold ride the thermals that rise from the warm construction zones, their calls like struck bells in the thin atmosphere.

The industrial heart of this place is not built but grown. Factories assemble themselves according to templates encoded in the mercury flows, their walls rising as nested crystalline matrices that channel electricity and heat with perfect efficiency. Inside, the machinery builds itself too—gears precipitation from supersaturated solutions, circuits forming as minerals organize themselves into conductive pathways. The entire industrial process is nearly silent, just the faint hum of electricity and the occasional crack of expanding crystal.

The city has neighborhoods in various stages of emergence. In the oldest districts, towers of fused quartz and metallic glass rise hundreds of meters, their surfaces catching and throwing light across the plateau. These mature zones hum with activity—pneumatic transport tubes, crackling power grids, the soft whir of self-maintaining systems. But between them lie vast spaces where only the foundations exist, waiting centuries for their superstructures to precipitate. Some areas are merely stains on the ground where future city blocks will one day stand.

The atmosphere here is thin but charged, tasting of copper and ozone. The sun, unimpeded by water vapor, beats down with an intensity that would scorch water-based life, but here it powers the photovoltaic lichens that creep across every surface, feeding energy into the growing grid. At night, the aurora-like glow of industrial processes paints the sky in greens and purples, while bioluminescent creatures trace paths between the scattered settlements.

This is a patient place, where urban planning happens on geological timescales. The master blueprint exists not in any document but in the chemical composition of the mercury flows, in the electromagnetic fields that guide crystal growth, in the behavioral patterns of the creatures that tend this vast, slow garden of metal and stone. Walking through it, you might not notice any change from day to day, or even year to year. But come back in a century, and entire new districts will have emerged from the raw geology, while others have grown into gleaming spires that catch clouds around their peaks.

The silence here is not empty but expectant—the quiet of something vast taking shape, of a world building itself according to rules we're only beginning to understand. In the spaces between buildings, between thoughts, between heartbeats of this strange industrial ecosystem, you can almost hear it: the faint crystalline song of a city teaching itself how to exist.
