# Preparation Guide for the Living Mountain City

## Critical Mental Preparation

Before anything else, you must radically adjust your concept of what constitutes a "city." This settlement doesn't just *contain* life—it *is* life. Every surface, structure, and system is part of an interconnected biological web. You'll be walking through the organs of a vast organism, not streets. Prepare to feel like a foreign antibody until you learn to move with the city's rhythms rather than against them.

The sensory intensity will be overwhelming initially. Practice meditation or sensory processing techniques beforehand—you'll need them to avoid complete overstimulation from the constant motion, sound, and tactile experiences.

## Physical Conditioning

Start a rigorous balance and climbing regimen immediately. The raised walkways of lashed branches require exceptional balance, and they'll be slippery with condensation. Practice walking on wet, uneven surfaces. Strengthen your ankles—the moss alone is ankle-deep, and a twisted joint here could be catastrophic.

Build your tolerance to humidity by exercising in saunas or steam rooms. The city's microclimate is essentially a living greenhouse. If you're not accustomed to breathing saturated air while exerting yourself, you'll exhaust quickly.

## Essential Equipment

**Clothing**: Forget conventional fabrics. You need:
- Quick-dry, antimicrobial clothing that won't rot in constant moisture
- Multiple thin layers rather than single thick ones
- Clothing that can withstand snagging on thorns and rough bark
- Colors that won't show stains from plant saps and creature droppings

**Footwear**: This is critical. Bring:
- At least three pairs of shoes with aggressive tread
- Ankle support is mandatory
- Drainage holes or mesh panels
- Consider traditional climbing shoes for the more vertical sections
- Waterproof socks as backup

**Protection Gear**:
- A lightweight but sturdy hat or helmet for falling fruit pods and creature droppings
- Gloves with good grip that can handle wet surfaces
- Eye protection that won't fog in humidity
- A walking stick that can double as a probe for testing surfaces

## Biological Hazards and Preparations

Start taking broad-spectrum probiotics months in advance. The microbial environment will be unlike anything your body has encountered. 

Consider allergy testing for:
- Mold and fungal spores
- Unusual pollens
- Insect venom (the varieties here will be novel to your system)

Bring a comprehensive medical kit including:
- Antifungals (both topical and systemic)
- Antihistamines
- Epinephrine auto-injectors
- Antibiotics effective against unusual bacteria
- Anti-parasitic medications

## Cultural and Behavioral Adaptation

The inhabitants have a fundamentally different relationship with their environment. What you might see as "infestation" or "decay" they see as proper functioning. Never express disgust at the creatures sharing their living spaces—this would be like being revolted by someone's furniture.

Learn to recognize intentional cultivation versus unwanted growth. Accidentally damaging a carefully trained architectural plant could be equivalent to vandalism. When in doubt, don't touch, prune, or clear anything.

Practice eating while things move around you. Meals will involve creatures investigating your food, possibly sampling it. This isn't contamination—it's normal coexistence.

## Practical Skills to Develop

**Navigation**: Traditional methods won't work. The city constantly changes as it grows. Learn to navigate by:
- Water flow patterns
- Major trunk structures
- Sound landmarks (specific bird colonies, water features)
- Scent markers (different districts cultivate different aromatic plants)

**Communication**: With the constant biological cacophony, verbal communication often requires:
- Close proximity
- Hand signals
- Understanding of acoustic windows (times when certain creatures are quiet)

**Basic Bioengineering**: Learn to read:
- Growth patterns indicating structural weakness
- Signs of plant illness that might make structures unsafe
- Seasonal changes that affect pathway accessibility

## Daily Living Preparations

**Sleep**: You won't find silence. Bring:
- High-quality earplugs or noise machines with brown noise
- Eye masks (bioluminescent creatures are active at night)
- Hammocks or suspended sleeping systems (ground sleeping invites too many visitors)

**Hygiene**: Conventional approaches fail here. Prepare for:
- Constant dampness (bring antifungal powders)
- Limited traditional bathing (water is everywhere but not always clean)
- Grooming in public (privacy doesn't exist when walls are permeable)

**Food**: Your digestive system needs preparation:
- Gradually introduce fermented foods
- Practice eating insects (they're protein supplements here, not pests)
- Build tolerance to bitter flavors (many plants have defensive compounds)
- Learn to identify basic edible versus toxic growths

## Psychological Readiness

The city will test your boundaries between self and environment. You'll have creatures in your sleeping space, plants growing through your temporary dwelling, and constant biological processes occurring around and on you. 

Practice mindfulness techniques that help you see these intrusions as connections rather than violations. The city doesn't recognize the rigid boundaries between human space and nature that you're accustomed to.

Prepare for dreams to be especially vivid—something about the spore-rich air and constant biological activity affects sleep patterns. Many visitors report profound dreams of becoming part of the city's growth patterns.

## Social Integration

The inhabitants measure wealth differently—in successful cultivations, in harmony with growth patterns, in the ability to direct rather than dominate biological processes. Bring gifts that show understanding:
- Rare seeds (after checking they're not invasive)
- High-quality pruning tools
- Knowledge of cultivation techniques from your world
- Stories of other human-nature integrations

## Emergency Protocols

Always maintain:
- Multiple escape routes (growth can block paths overnight)
- Whistle or sound maker that cuts through biological noise
- Bright marker dyes to mark your path
- Emergency shelter that can withstand being overgrown
- Water purification (not all flowing water is safe, despite its abundance)

## Final Preparations

Spend time in the densest greenhouse or rainforest available to you. Sleep there if possible. Let your body begin adapting to constant humidity, restricted sightlines, and the feeling of being surrounded by active biological processes.

Most importantly, prepare to have every assumption about urban living challenged. This city doesn't just blur the line between natural and built environments—it eliminates it entirely. You're not visiting a place where humans live alongside nature; you're entering a superorganism where humans are just one of many participant species in a breathing, growing, dying, and regenerating collective entity.

The city will change you. Your microbiome will shift. Your sensory processing will adapt. Your concept of comfort will evolve. Plan for at least a month just to achieve basic functionality, and understand that leaving might be harder than arriving—not because of any physical barrier, but because part of you will have grown roots.
