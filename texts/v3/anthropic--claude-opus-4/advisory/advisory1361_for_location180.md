# Preparation Guide for the Singing Seamount

## Critical Warning
This location exhibits severe reality instabilities. Standard physics cannot be relied upon. Death is not the worst possible outcome—temporal displacement, dimensional phasing, and consciousness fragmentation have all been documented. Proceed only if absolutely necessary.

## Essential Pre-Arrival Training (Minimum 6 Months)

### 1. Paradox Conditioning
- Practice holding contradictory concepts simultaneously in your mind
- Meditation exercises where you count objects that change number while counting
- Visual training with impossible objects (Penrose triangles, Klein bottles)
- Learn to accept that your equipment may be both functional and broken simultaneously

### 2. Non-Linear Swimming
- Train in wave pools with chaotic currents
- Practice swimming in heavy liquids (glycerin pools) and light gases
- Learn to navigate by sound and pressure rather than sight
- Develop muscle memory for moving through media of rapidly changing density

### 3. Temporal Perception Training
- Use strobe lights at varying frequencies to practice seeing in stuttered time
- Watch films played backwards, forwards, and simultaneously
- Practice remembering events that haven't happened yet (paradox memory exercises)
- Learn to recognize your own temporal echoes

## Equipment Requirements

### Primary Life Support
- **Quantum-Stabilized Rebreather**: Standard scuba equipment will fail. You need gear that can function when water spontaneously becomes air or vice versa. The rebreather must have:
  - Mechanical backup systems (no electronics)
  - Triple-redundant air supplies
  - Pressure suits rated for rapid density changes
  - Emergency surface buoys (though "surface" is relative here)

### Navigation Tools
- **Analog compass collection**: Bring at least 12, as they will point in different directions
- **Mechanical depth gauges**: Multiple, cross-reference constantly
- **Knotted rope**: Sometimes the only reliable distance measurement
- **Sound-based navigation beacons**: The singing may overwhelm them, but try anyway
- **Physical logbook with waterproof paper**: Digital devices experience temporal loops

### Protection Equipment
- **Full-body chainmail wetsuit**: For protection against razor-sharp basalt that phases in and out
- **Temporal anchor harness**: Experimental equipment that may help prevent complete temporal displacement
- **Polarized face mask with multiple filter options**: Some wavelengths of impossible light can damage unprotected eyes
- **Vibration-dampening hood**: To prevent the sound from liquefying your inner ear

### Documentation Gear
- **Mechanical film cameras**: Multiple, with various film speeds
- **Wax tablets**: Sometimes the only recording medium that persists
- **Pre-digital audio recorders**: With mechanical parts only
- **Sample containers that seal in multiple dimensions**: Standard containers may leak into parallel spaces

## Pre-Dive Preparations

### Physical
- Fast for 24 hours (full stomach complicates paradox nausea)
- No alcohol or consciousness-altering substances for 72 hours prior
- Sleep deprivation of exactly 20 hours (too alert or too tired are both dangerous)
- Apply conductive gel to pulse points (helps with reality grounding)

### Mental
- Write a detailed account of your life up to this point
- Create "anchor memories"—vivid recollections to help maintain identity
- Practice the "reality confirmation mantras" 
- Accept that you may experience events out of order
- Prepare for the possibility of meeting other versions of yourself

### Practical Concerns
- File multiple dive plans with different organizations (redundancy against temporal erasure)
- Leave DNA samples and temporal markers with trusted contacts
- Pre-write post-dive reports (sometimes you remember the future)
- Arrange for pickup at multiple possible exit points and times

## Approach Protocol

### Entry Strategy
1. Approach from the north-northeast (most stable reality gradient)
2. Descend in spirals, never straight down
3. Stop every 5 meters to recalibrate instruments and perception
4. If you see yourself already at the seamount, abort immediately
5. Enter the water at least 500 meters from the mount edge

### Warning Signs to Abort
- Your shadow moving independently
- Breathing water while your regulator shows air
- Counting your fingers yields different results each time
- Hearing your own voice from outside your body
- Equipment aging or de-aging rapidly

## While at the Seamount

### Navigation Rules
- Never look directly into the circular depression at the heart
- Follow the kelp lines when possible (life clings to stability)
- If caught in a standing wave, swim perpendicular to your instincts
- Trust pressure over vision when they conflict
- Count kicks and breaths, not distance or time

### Interaction Protocols
- Do not attempt to collect samples from temporal anomalies
- Avoid the research platform unless in emergency
- If you encounter the ancient eel, remain perfectly still
- Do not try to "fix" paradoxes you observe
- Keep moving—stillness attracts temporal eddies

### Emergency Procedures
- If phasing begins: bite down hard on rubber reg, pain anchors reality
- If time loops: change one small action each iteration
- If water becomes unbreathable: remember that drowning is temporary here
- If you meet yourself: do not make eye contact or communicate
- If reality fully fractures: focus on your heartbeat, it's the last thing to desync

## Post-Dive Protocol

### Immediate Actions
1. Surface slowly, regardless of dive computer readings
2. Do not trust your first perception of the surface
3. Signal pickup immediately, even if you think no time has passed
4. Begin reality confirmation exercises
5. Document everything before memories shift

### Decompression (Physical and Temporal)
- Expect 24-72 hours of "reality sickness"
- Isolation tank sessions help readjustment
- Avoid mirrors for the first week
- Keep lighting consistent and avoid strobes
- Gradual reintroduction to linear time

### Long-term Effects to Monitor
- Prophetic dreams (usually backwards)
- Ability to be in two places at once (usually temporary)
- Allergies to certain frequencies of sound
- Improved ability to navigate in chaos
- Occasional visibility of parallel timelines

## Final Notes

The Singing Seamount changes those who visit it. You will not return as the same person who descended. This is not metaphorical. Your DNA may show slight variations. Your memories may include events from timelines you didn't experience. Your perception of cause and effect will be permanently altered.

Some travelers report the experience as transcendent—a glimpse into the true nature of reality's flexibility. Others never fully readjust to linear existence. The song of the mount will stay with you, a constant whisper that the world is far stranger and more mutable than consensus reality suggests.

Pack extra batteries for everything, even though electronics don't work there. Sometimes the act of futile preparation is its own anchor.

Remember: at the Singing Seamount, the only constant is paradox. Prepare to abandon everything you know about how the world works. Prepare to be changed.

And whatever you do, don't look too long into the circular depression at the mount's heart. Some depths are not meant to be fathomed, and some songs are not meant to be fully heard.

Safe travels, though safety itself is just another illusion where you're going.
