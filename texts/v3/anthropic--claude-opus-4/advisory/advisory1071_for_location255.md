**A Traveler's Guide to the Drowned Workshops of the First Builders**

*The prudent multiverse traveler must understand: this is not merely an underwater location. It is a tomb-world, a place where civilization's last breath crystallized into three kilometers of liquid night. Prepare accordingly.*

## Physical Preparations

### Pressure Suit Requirements
Standard diving equipment will kill you. At three kilometers depth, you face pressures exceeding 300 atmospheres. You need:

- **Atmospheric diving suit** (ADS) rated for at least 3,500 meters
- Alternatively, a **liquid breathing system** with perfluorocarbon solution (be warned: the psychological adjustment period is severe)
- **Redundant life support** - minimum 72 hours operational capacity
- **Emergency ascent module** - though at this depth, emergency ascent means death. This is for your peace of mind only

### Thermal Management
The cold here is not merely low temperature—it is an active, hungry thing. Your suit must maintain core temperature against water that hovers just above freezing, with a thermal conductivity that will leach heat from any system:

- **Active heating elements** throughout the suit
- **Radioisotope thermoelectric generator** (RTG) power source recommended—batteries die too quickly in this cold
- **Emergency chemical heat packs** positioned at wrists, ankles, and torso
- **Insulation rated for permanent arctic submersion**

### Illumination Systems
The darkness of the Drowned Workshops is absolute—a darkness so complete your brain will begin manufacturing phantom lights:

- **Primary lighting**: High-intensity LED array, minimum 50,000 lumens
- **Secondary lighting**: Backup system on independent power
- **Ultraviolet lights**: The ancient kelp fluoresces under certain UV wavelengths—useful for navigation
- **Red-filtered lights**: For preserving what little night vision your brain can maintain
- **Chemical light sticks**: As last-resort backup (pressure-resistant variants only)
- **Bioluminescent markers**: For trail marking—they last longer than you will

### Navigation Equipment
GPS does not function here. Radio waves do not penetrate. You navigate by dead reckoning or not at all:

- **Inertial navigation system** with gyroscopic stabilization
- **Acoustic beacon network** (deploy as you go—the stone absorbs sound, so space them closely)
- **Physical guide rope** rated for deep-sea operations (minimum 5km length)
- **Detailed sonar mapping system**—though the kelp forests will confound it
- **Analog compass**—magnetic anomalies are common but predictable
- **Paper maps in waterproof cases**—electronics fail here with disturbing frequency

## Psychological Preparations

### The Darkness Protocols
Total darkness does things to the human mind. After 6-8 hours, most people begin experiencing:
- Auditory hallucinations (usually whispering or tool-strikes on stone)
- Visual phantoms (typically moving shadows in peripheral vision)
- Temporal displacement (losing all sense of how long you've been submerged)
- "The Certainty" - an unshakeable belief that something is watching from just beyond your light

**Mitigation strategies:**
- Pre-trip sensory deprivation training (minimum 40 hours)
- Recorded audio of human voices (play at regular intervals)
- Strict chronometer discipline (check time every 15 minutes)
- Meditation techniques specifically for combating isolation paranoia
- A physical token from the surface world (touch it when the depths feel too heavy)

### The Weight of History
The Workshops impose a psychological pressure beyond the physical. You will feel:
- The desperate urgency of the final builders
- A creeping certainty that the waters are still rising
- Persistent thoughts about your own civilization's mortality
- Dreams (during rest periods) of carving stone with bronze tools

**Coping mechanisms:**
- Maintain strict scientific objectivity
- Document everything—the act of recording creates psychological distance
- Avoid prolonged examination of the tool marks
- Do not attempt to imagine the builders' faces

## Operational Considerations

### Movement Protocols
The passages between workshops are narrow. The kelp is everywhere:
- Move slowly—kelp fronds are leather-thick and will entangle
- Never force through narrow gaps—stone shifts, passages close
- Maintain three points of contact when climbing
- Trust no handhold without testing—bronze corrodes, stone crumbles
- The kelp releases spores when disturbed—they cloud water and clog filters

### Time Management
Budget your time with extreme prejudice:
- **Transit down**: 3-4 hours (pressure equalization stations every 500m)
- **Exploration**: Maximum 8 hours at depth
- **Transit up**: 6-8 hours (decompression is not optional)
- **Total operation**: 24 hours minimum
- Build in 20% buffer for equipment problems
- 40% buffer for psychological issues

### What to Document
You're here for a reason. Focus on:
- **Workshop layout patterns**—they encode information about social structure
- **Tool mark evolution**—the regression tells a story
- **Kelp growth patterns**—they follow ancient nutrient flows
- **Intact artifacts**—prioritize unfamiliar tools or objects
- **The memory-water pockets**—collect samples if possible
- **Acoustic anomalies**—record everything, analyze later

### What to Avoid
Some things should not be disturbed:
- **Collapsed sections**—unstable, and the kelp grows too thick
- **The deepest workshops**—something about the finest stonework induces dangerous obsession
- **Sediment layers in the gardens**—do not dig, do not sample
- **Following sounds to their source**—the acoustics lie
- **Counting the workshops**—the number seems to change

## Emergency Protocols

### When Things Go Wrong
They will. Be ready:

**Suit breach**:
- You have 30 seconds of consciousness at this pressure
- Emergency ascent means death but dying on the surface beats dying in the darkness
- Activate emergency beacon immediately
- Use emergency air injection to maintain positive buoyancy

**Lost in the workshops**:
- Stop moving immediately
- Deploy acoustic beacon
- Follow your rope back—never abandon the rope
- If rope is cut, move upward—always upward
- The kelp grows denser near collapses—avoid these areas

**Psychological break**:
- Common at hour 6-7
- Manifests as: conviction you belong here, desire to remove suit, hearing familiar voices
- Immediate abort—no exceptions
- Emergency stimulant injection authorized
- Surface support should initiate retrieval

**Equipment failure cascade**:
- Primary systems fail in this order: electronics, heating, lighting
- At first failure, begin abort
- At second failure, emergency ascent
- Do not attempt field repairs at depth

## The Return

Surviving the Workshops is only half the battle. The return brings its own challenges:

### Decompression Trauma
Not the physical kind—that's managed by your suit. The psychological decompression:
- Surface world seems impossibly bright for days
- Persistent dreams of stone corridors filling with water
- Compulsive checking of tide tables
- Inability to tolerate enclosed spaces

### Integration Period
Allow 2-3 weeks minimum:
- No underground transit
- No diving of any kind
- Sleep with lights on if needed
- Keep physical artifact from the trip visible
- Write detailed reports—externalize the experience

### What You'll Carry Forever
Every traveler to the Drowned Workshops returns changed:
- Enhanced awareness of geological time
- Persistent sense of impermanence
- Occasional taste of ancient water in your mouth
- Dreams where you're carving stone with bronze tools
- The absolute certainty that all civilizations drown eventually

## Final Preparations

Before you descend, understand this: the Drowned Workshops are not hostile—they are indifferent, which is worse. They exemplify deep time, patient darkness, and the ultimate futility of all human endeavor rendered in stone and kelp and crushing depth.

Pack a photograph of sunlight. You'll need the reminder that such things exist.

Write a letter to someone you love. The act of writing it matters more than sending it.

Accept that you will return changed, or not at all.

The darkness is waiting. It has been waiting longer than your species has existed. It can wait a little longer.

But perhaps you cannot.

*Descend wisely, traveler. The stone remembers everything, and the kelp grows over all.*
