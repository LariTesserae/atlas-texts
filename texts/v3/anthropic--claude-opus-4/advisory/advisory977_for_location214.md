# Preparation Guide for the Plateau City of Channels

## Essential Physical Preparations

### Water Management Gear
This city exists in a state of perpetual dampness. Everything you bring must either be waterproof or you must accept it will be constantly moist.

- **Waterproof pack liners** (multiple) - The fog and mist will penetrate any pack eventually
- **Dry bags of various sizes** - For electronics, documents, anything that absolutely must stay dry
- **Quick-dry clothing only** - Cotton will never dry here. Bring merino wool, synthetic materials
- **Waterproof/breathable shell jacket and pants** - Not for rain, but for the constant mist
- **Multiple pairs of wool or synthetic socks** - Your feet will always be damp; changing socks frequently prevents problems
- **Waterproof gloves** - The stone is slick and cold; you'll be using your hands for balance constantly
- **Gaiters** - To keep water from running down into your boots

### Footwear (Critical)
The wrong footwear here could be fatal. Every surface is water-polished stone with a film of moisture.

- **Primary boots**: High-ankle hiking boots with aggressive, soft-rubber soles (like approach shoes used in climbing)
- **Backup footwear**: A second pair, because nothing dries here
- **Microspikes or light crampons**: For the steeper carved stairways
- **Neoprene socks**: As a last resort if your feet are perpetually cold and wet

### Navigation and Safety
The scale and uniformity of the city makes disorientation likely.

- **Physical compass and altimeter**: Electronics may fail in the moisture
- **Waterproof maps in multiple scales**: Create your own grid reference system
- **Flagging tape or chalk**: To mark your path (remove when leaving)
- **Emergency whistle**: Sound carries strangely here with all the stone
- **Headlamp with red filter**: Plus backup lights and extra batteries in waterproof container
- **Personal locator beacon**: The city is vast; if injured, you might never be found
- **Rope (30-50 meters)**: Many areas may require rappelling or ascending
- **Basic climbing harness and hardware**: The city is vertical in many places

## Shelter Considerations

The empty buildings offer obvious shelter, but consider:

- **Bivvy sack rather than tent**: You'll likely sleep in the stone chambers
- **Inflatable sleeping pad**: Essential insulation from the cold stone
- **Synthetic sleeping bag**: Down will lose all loft in this humidity
- **Tarp**: To create dry spaces within the stone rooms
- **Small camp stove that works in high humidity**: Many stoves struggle in these conditions
- **Candles**: Both for light and to add a tiny bit of warmth/dryness to your shelter

## Food and Water

Paradoxically, while water is everywhere, potable water needs consideration.

- **Water purification**: The channel system is ancient; assume all water needs treatment
- **Collapsible water containers**: To collect clean rain when it falls
- **High-calorie, moisture-resistant food**: Energy bars in waterproof wrapping
- **No paper packaging**: It will disintegrate
- **Electrolyte supplements**: The constant dampness and exertion navigating the city

## Mental and Emotional Preparation

### Psychological Readiness
The city's scale and emptiness can be overwhelming. Prepare for:

- **Profound solitude**: You may go days without seeing another living thing larger than a mouse
- **Temporal vertigo**: The weight of centuries is palpable here
- **Decision fatigue**: Every intersection looks similar; the city offers no obvious paths
- **The urge to whisper**: The silence is so complete that normal speech feels like shouting

### Suggested Mental Exercises Before Arrival:
1. Spend time in abandoned buildings to acclimate to inhabited emptiness
2. Practice moving silently - you'll find yourself doing this instinctively there
3. Learn to be comfortable being damp for extended periods
4. Practice navigation in uniform environments

## Special Considerations

### Photography/Documentation
- **Weatherproof camera equipment**: The moisture will destroy electronics
- **Silica gel packs** (many): Changed daily
- **Backup storage in waterproof containers**
- **Physical notebook with waterproof paper**: Sometimes more reliable than electronics

### Time Planning
- **Move slowly**: The slick stone and complex three-dimensional navigation means a kilometer might take an hour
- **Start shelter preparation early**: Finding and preparing a dry sleeping space takes time
- **Allow for weather watching**: The city creates its own weather; learn its patterns

### Ethical Considerations
- **Take nothing**: This is a vast archaeological site
- **Leave nothing**: Pack out everything
- **Document, don't disturb**: The channels still function; don't block them
- **Respect the silence**: It's part of what this place is

## Warning Signs to Heed

**Turn back immediately if:**
- You begin to hear voices or music (auditory hallucinations from isolation)
- You lose track of which level you're on (vertical disorientation)
- Your hands become too cold to grip safely
- You notice yourself taking unnecessary risks to "make progress"
- The channels begin flowing heavily (flash flood risk)

## What to Expect Emotionally

Visitors report a peculiar emotional progression:
1. **First day**: Awe at the scale and engineering
2. **Day 2-3**: Creeping unease at the emptiness
3. **Day 4-5**: Either profound peace or unbearable anxiety (no middle ground)
4. **After a week**: A strange reluctance to leave, despite the discomfort

The city seems to select for certain temperaments. Those comfortable with solitude and patient observation find it mesmerizing. Those who need human connection or clear objectives find it unbearable.

## Final Notes

This is not a casual destination. The city demands respect—for its scale, its age, its engineered perfection, and its emptiness. Come prepared to move slowly, observe carefully, and accept discomfort. 

Most importantly, come prepared to leave. The city has a way of holding people, not through malice but through sheer presence. Set a firm departure date and keep to it. The plateau city has waited empty for centuries; it can wait longer. You, however, cannot.
