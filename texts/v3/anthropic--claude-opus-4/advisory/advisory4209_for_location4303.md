**A Multiverse Traveler's Guide to the Forgotten Cistern**

**CRITICAL WARNING:** This location exists in a temporal compression zone. Your perception of time will be severely distorted. What feels like minutes inside may be hours outside, or vice versa. Plan accordingly.

## Essential Preparations

### Respiratory Equipment
The air here isn't air as you know it. It's a suspension—part vapor, part liquid, part something else entirely. Bring:
- A specialized rebreather designed for high-humidity environments (standard diving equipment will clog within minutes)
- Backup mechanical filters (no electronics—they'll corrode)
- Consider coating your nasal passages with petroleum jelly or similar barrier
- Practice breathing shallowly; deep breaths here can literally drown you while standing

### Thermal Protection
The heat is *wrong* in ways that will disturb your body's natural regulation:
- Wear moisture-wicking base layers that can handle being perpetually soaked
- Avoid cotton entirely—it will become a suffocating second skin
- Bring cooling packs that activate chemically, not electronically
- Your core temperature will spike unpredictably; monitor for signs of fever that isn't fever

### The Creatures—Approach Protocol
Those pale, pulsing things covering every surface:
- **DO NOT TOUCH THEM DIRECTLY.** We don't know what they excrete or absorb
- They respond to vibrations—move slowly, deliberately
- Bring specimen containers if you must collect samples, but know that they rarely survive removal from their environment
- They may be photosensitive; avoid bright lights or sudden illumination
- Some travelers report psychosomatic effects from prolonged observation—itching that isn't there, sensation of movement under the skin

### Navigation and Orientation
The chamber's geometry is unstable—not physically, but perceptually:
- Bring analog compasses (multiple—they'll disagree with each other)
- Mark your entry point with biodegradable markers
- The phosphorescence can cause vertigo and spatial disorientation
- Count your steps; don't rely on visual landmarks
- The walls appear closer than they are, then suddenly farther

### Time Management
This cannot be overstated:
- Set multiple mechanical timers (no electronics)
- Have someone outside the chamber to signal you at predetermined intervals
- Your subjective experience of duration will be unreliable
- Some visitors report "lost time"—emerging to find hours have passed unnoticed
- Others experience the opposite—excruciating awareness of every second stretching

### Protective Clothing
- Full coverage but breathable—think tropical caving gear
- Sealed boots with excellent grip (the surfaces are treacherously slick)
- Gloves that can handle constant moisture
- Nothing with exposed metal—it will corrode rapidly
- Consider a full-face shield; the mist has unknown properties

### Psychological Preparation
This space will affect your mind:
- The abundance of creatures triggers deep revulsion in most humans
- The wrongness of the heat creates persistent anxiety
- Claustrophobia is common even in those who don't usually experience it
- Practice meditation techniques for managing disgust and unease
- Have a mental anchor—a song, a mantra, something from the outside world

### Recording Equipment
If you must document:
- Waterproof everything twice over
- Manual/mechanical devices only
- Sealed containers for written notes
- The phosphorescence interferes with most photography
- Audio recording captures sounds you won't hear with your ears

### Health Precautions
- Full spectrum antibiotics before and after
- Antifungal treatments (topical and systemic)
- The water must not be consumed under any circumstances
- Don't let it contact broken skin
- Some travelers develop persistent coughs lasting weeks after visits
- Monitor for unusual growths or skin changes post-visit

### Entry and Exit Strategy
- Never visit alone
- Establish clear communication signals with your surface team
- The way out may not appear the same as the way in
- The infrastructure penetrations (pipes, construction materials) are not stable climbing points
- Have multiple exit strategies
- Set a hard time limit and stick to it regardless of how you feel

### What to Expect Physically
- Your clothes will be soaked within seconds
- Persistent sensation of drowning while breathing
- Difficulty judging distances
- Muscle fatigue from the oppressive atmosphere
- Possible mild hallucinations from oxygen deprivation
- Nausea from the organic smell and heat

### Mental/Spiritual Preparation
This place exists outside normal reality—a pocket where city and nature have created something neither intended:
- You'll feel the weight of the city above as physical pressure
- The creatures' movements may seem coordinated, purposeful, watching
- The distinction between self and environment blurs in the heavy air
- Some report feeling "observed by the chamber itself"
- Prepare for existential unease about humanity's relationship with its built environment

### Emergency Protocols
- If you begin to feel feverish, exit immediately
- If the creatures begin moving rapidly or in unison, exit immediately
- If the water level changes noticeably, exit immediately
- If you lose track of time completely, your surface team should extract you
- Have a decontamination protocol ready topside

### Final Notes
This is not a place meant for human presence. It exists because of us but not for us. Visit with respect for its alien ecology. Take nothing but samples (if you must), leave nothing but footprints (and those will be erased within hours by the perpetual moisture). 

The cistern will remain long after the city above crumbles. You are visiting a space between spaces, where time moves like molasses and life has taken forms that challenge our definitions. Prepare accordingly, and remember—you are the alien here.
