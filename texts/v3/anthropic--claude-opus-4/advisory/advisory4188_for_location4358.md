# Preparation Guide for the Crystal Throat

## Critical Warnings

**This is not a casual destination.** The Crystal Throat exists at the intersection of geological and biological time scales. Your presence here will be an intrusion into a system that measures change in centuries. Prepare accordingly.

## Physical Preparations

### Body Conditioning
Begin flexibility training at least three months before your visit. The entrance crawl is not negotiable—you must compress your body against stone, moving on your belly through a passage that may scrape skin and compress lungs. Practice army crawls in progressively tighter spaces. Your shoulders, hips, and ribcage must learn to shift and flow.

### Protective Gear
- **Base layer**: Wear form-fitting synthetic materials that won't catch on crystal growths. Nothing loose, nothing that can snag.
- **Outer protection**: Thick leather or canvas over knees, elbows, chest, and stomach. The crystal moss may look soft, but its density suggests abrasive properties.
- **Gloves**: Double-layer—thin tactile inner gloves beneath puncture-resistant outers. You'll want to touch these impossible plants, but their glass-like nature demands caution.
- **Head protection**: A close-fitting cap to protect your scalp during the crawl. Hair should be tightly bound or braided flat.

### Breathing Apparatus
The copper-and-ozone atmosphere won't kill you immediately, but prolonged exposure to such dry, metallic air may damage mucous membranes. Bring:
- Petroleum jelly for nostrils and lips
- A respirator rated for metallic particles
- Backup: a wet cloth to breathe through if needed

## Sensory Preparations

### Vision Training
The shadowless, omnipresent light will be disorienting. Practice with:
- Ring lights that eliminate shadows
- White rooms or snow fields on bright days
- Meditation on geometric patterns—the moiré effects of overlapping crystal leaves can trigger vertigo or seizures in susceptible individuals

### Mental Conditioning
This space operates on non-human timescales. Prepare your mind:
- Practice stillness meditation, working up to 4-hour sessions
- Study time-lapse photography of geological processes
- Contemplate a single flower bud daily for months, imagining its imperceptibly slow bloom

## Equipment

### Essential Tools
- **Measurement devices**: Calipers, rulers, and thread for mapping the mathematical curves of growth patterns
- **Documentation**: Camera with macro lens and powerful flash (the sourceless light may not register properly on sensors)
- **Sample containers**: ONLY if you have permission from whatever consciousness governs this space. The ecosystem is too densely interconnected for casual collection.
- **Time-keeping**: Bring multiple methods. Electronic devices may malfunction in the presence of the mountain's electrical currents.

### Emergency Supplies
- **Backup light sources**: Though the chamber has its own illumination, you need light for the entrance crawl
- **First aid**: Focus on treatments for cuts from crystal edges and allergic reactions to unknown minerals
- **Emergency exit aids**: Rope, lubricant, anything that might help if you become stuck during exit

## Behavioral Protocols

### Movement
Every gesture must be deliberate. The crystalline fronds interpenetrate each other but may not extend the same courtesy to your flesh. Move as if through a museum made of razors and spun glass. The crystal moss can support weight, but test each step—older growths may be more fragile.

### Interaction
Do not attempt to pick the chiming fruit. Their sound may serve a function in the garden's slow metabolism. Instead:
- Observe their resonance patterns
- Note which disturbances cause chimes
- Listen for harmonics that might indicate communication

### The White Tree
The central tree-like structure demands special reverence. Its mathematical precision suggests intelligence or at least response to cosmic-level patterns. Approach it as you would a sleeping giant. Do not touch its mica-thin leaves—their moiré patterns indicate dimensional properties your brain isn't equipped to process directly.

## Temporal Considerations

### Duration
Plan for no more than 6 hours inside. The chamber's alien metabolism may begin to affect yours if you stay longer. Some travelers report:
- Slowing heartbeat
- Crystalline deposits in tear ducts
- Compulsion to root in place

### Timing
If possible, divine the chamber's breathing rhythm before entering. Entering during an expansion phase (even if only by millimeters) will ease your passage.

## Psychological Preparation

### Isolation Protocol
You will be alone in a space smaller than most bedrooms, surrounded by alien life. The silence is absolute except for crystal chimes. Practice:
- Solitary confinement exercises
- Sensory deprivation training
- Meditation in small, enclosed spaces

### Wonder Management
The impossibility of this garden may overwhelm. Some travelers report:
- Tears that won't stop
- Conviction that they've discovered the universe's secret blueprint
- Desire to never leave

Have a grounding protocol:
- A photograph of home
- A familiar scent in a sealed vial
- A memorized poem or song from your world

## Post-Visit Protocol

### Decontamination
- Check for crystal spores or fragments in clothing and hair
- Monitor your body for unusual growths over the following months
- Document any changes in your perception of time or light

### Integration
The Crystal Throat will change you. You've seen evolution running on electricity instead of water, geometry instead of competition. Allow time to process:
- Create art about your experience
- Speak to others who've visited similar impossible spaces
- Accept that some aspects will remain forever beyond language

## Final Preparations

### The Question
Before you crawl into that stone throat, know your intention. The Crystal Throat doesn't suffer casual visitors. Are you:
- A witness to impossible beauty?
- A student of alternative evolution?
- A seeker of the light that comes from nowhere?

Your answer will shape what the chamber reveals.

### The Offering
Consider bringing a gift for the garden—not physical, but conceptual. Perhaps:
- A new mathematical theorem
- A song hummed at the threshold
- A promise to carry its patterns back into the sunlit world

## Remember

You're entering a space where stone dreams and dreams crystallize. Where light feeds matter and matter becomes mathematics. Where a garden grows in perfect darkness, needing nothing from the world above.

Crawl carefully. Breathe slowly. And when you finally stand in that impossible cavity, surrounded by crystal forests that shouldn't exist, remember—you're the anomaly here, the brief flutter in a system that measures heartbeats in stone.

The mountain has swallowed a garden, and you're about to enter its throat. Make sure you're prepared to be digested and reformed by what you find there.
