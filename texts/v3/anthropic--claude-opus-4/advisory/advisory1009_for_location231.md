## Preparation Guide for the Breathing Gallery

### Critical Warnings

**This location is actively hostile to human physiology.** The Breathing Gallery operates as a macro-organism with its own digestive processes. You are not a visitor here—you are potential nutrients. Every surface, every organism, every breath of air is part of a system evolved to break down organic matter. Approach with extreme caution.

### Environmental Hazards

**Atmospheric Composition**: The air is saturated with spores, decomposition gases, and biological aerosols. Standard oxygen levels are present but compromised. You'll need a full-face respirator with P100 filters at minimum, preferably a closed-circuit rebreather. The spore load alone can colonize human lung tissue within hours.

**Temperature and Humidity**: Expect 35-40°C (95-104°F) with near 100% humidity. Your body cannot cool itself through perspiration in these conditions. Heat exhaustion occurs within 20-30 minutes without countermeasures.

**Bio-corrosive Environment**: The moisture coating all surfaces is enzymatically active. Exposed skin will begin breaking down within minutes. The sensation starts as mild itching and progresses to burning as your epidermis is digested.

### Essential Equipment

**Full Environmental Suit**: Not optional. Choose something rated for chemical/biological hazards with sealed seams. The material should be thick enough to resist enzymatic breakdown for at least 8 hours. Tyvek won't suffice—you need military-grade CBRN protection.

**Breathing Apparatus**: Closed-circuit rebreather strongly recommended. The Gallery's atmosphere will clog standard filters rapidly. Bring triple redundancy—two backup systems minimum. Calculate oxygen for twice your planned duration.

**Cooling System**: Active cooling vest with ice packs or battery-powered circulation. Without this, you'll succumb to hyperthermia before experiencing anything meaningful.

**Lighting**: The fungal bioluminescence is psychologically disturbing and insufficient for navigation. Bring sealed LED systems—the moisture will destroy anything with exposed circuits. Red filters recommended to preserve what night vision you can maintain.

**Foot Protection**: The floor combines slip hazards with digestive secretions. Over-boots with carbide studs for traction, chemically resistant material. The carved channels create trip hazards obscured by organic growth.

### Biological Threats

**The Moths**: Do not dismiss them as harmless. When they move en masse, they release psychoactive compounds. Witnesses report dissociative episodes, time distortion, and false memories. Their wing patterns exploit pareidolia—you'll see faces that aren't there, which degrades situational awareness.

**Transparent Salamanders**: Avoid contact. Their skin secretes compounds that pass through most glove materials. Effects include localized paralysis and disruption of proprioception. You won't feel your extremities properly for hours after exposure.

**Pool Inhabitants**: Do not approach the water. The organisms within respond to vibration and heat signatures. They're not hostile per se, but their investigation methods involve dissolution of foreign matter to understand its composition.

**The Pale Crickets**: Their leg-span allows them to drop onto visitors from root networks above. While not directly dangerous, their touch triggers an autonomous nervous system response—uncontrollable shuddering that can compromise your equipment seals.

### Psychological Preparation

The Gallery attacks perception as much as physiology. The combination of heat stress, obscured lighting, and biological pheromones creates a dissociative environment. You will experience:

- Time distortion (minutes feel like hours)
- False pattern recognition (seeing pathways that don't exist)
- Auditory hallucinations (voices in the cricket songs)
- Profound unease progressing to panic

**Countermeasures**: Pre-visit meditation focusing on objective anchors. Bring a mechanical watch—digital displays fail here. Set alarms every 15 minutes to reorient yourself temporally. Record audio notes constantly; your memory will be unreliable.

### Navigation Strategy

The Gallery's geometry shifts—not physically, but perceptually. The same path looks different on return. The carved channels in the platform offer the only reliable navigation aid. Map them before exploring further. They encode the space's actual layout versus its apparent one.

**Never lose sight of your entry point.** The root pillars create a maze effect compounded by psychological disorientation. Bring minimum 100m of chemlight rope. Anchor it outside and unspool as you go. This is your lifeline when spatial reasoning fails.

### Temporal Considerations

The Gallery operates on its own timescale. Biological processes that take days elsewhere happen in hours here. Your equipment degrades faster than expected. More disturbingly, your perception of duration becomes unreliable. Many visitors emerge believing they've been inside for days when only hours have passed—or vice versa.

Set hard limits: 2 hours maximum first visit. Use external timekeepers (someone outside tracking your duration). The temptation to stay longer intensifies the longer you remain—this is biochemical manipulation, not genuine curiosity.

### Cultural/Historical Respect

The worked stones suggest previous human presence, possibly ritualistic. Approach these areas with additional caution. They're focal points for the Gallery's more aggressive responses, as if the space remembers being disturbed. Don't attempt to clean or examine the carvings closely—this provokes environmental retaliation.

### Emergency Protocols

**Suit Breach**: Immediate evacuation. You have minutes before systemic breakdown begins. Don't attempt field repairs—the moisture infiltration is already beginning digestive processes.

**Disorientation**: Stop moving. Control breathing. Follow your rope back immediately. The urge to explore during dissociative episodes is the Gallery feeding on your curiosity.

**Equipment Failure**: Abort immediately. The Gallery seems to sense vulnerability and increases environmental pressure on compromised visitors.

**Biological Contact**: Don't attempt decontamination inside. The water accelerates enzymatic activity. Exit and undergo full chemical decontamination outside.

### Recovery Requirements

Post-visit medical examination essential. The spore load persists in respiratory systems for weeks. Antifungal treatment may be necessary. Psychological effects include:

- Recurring dreams of underground spaces
- Compulsion to return
- Distorted memories that become more vivid over time
- Synesthesia episodes (tasting colors, seeing sounds)

These fade but never fully resolve. The Gallery marks its visitors.

### Final Warnings

The Breathing Gallery is not a tourist destination. It's a functional organism that tolerates limited human presence while attempting to digest intruders. Every moment inside is a negotiation with an alien intelligence expressed through biology.

Do not go alone. Do not go unprepared. Do not go unless you accept that you'll return changed.

The builders who left those carved channels understood something we've forgotten: this place has its own agenda. They tried to read its language and abandoned the effort. Their stone platform remains as a warning—some thresholds shouldn't be crossed, some conversations shouldn't be attempted.

If you go, go quickly. Take what observations you need and leave. The Gallery is patient. It's been feeding on the curious for centuries, and it's very, very good at making visitors want to stay just a little longer, venture just a little deeper, until they become part of its permanent collection.

The moths will be waiting. They always are.
