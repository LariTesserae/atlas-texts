**Preparation Guide for the Tower Garden**

**Physical Conditioning**
Begin humidity training at least two months before departure. Your body needs to adapt to operating in 85-95% humidity at altitude. Install a humidifier in your bedroom and gradually increase output. Practice climbing stairs in saunas or steam rooms. The Tower Garden will tax your respiratory system in ways you cannot imagine—the air is thick as soup, and every breath feels like drinking.

Train your balance on unstable surfaces. The grated walkways are slick with condensation and organic matter. Practice walking on wet metal grating if possible. Your proprioception must be sharp—one wrong step could send you through gaps in the decking or over edges where railings have been consumed by growth.

**Equipment Essentials**
Forget waterproof gear—embrace water-permeable clothing that dries quickly. Waterproofs will leave you stewing in your own sweat. Bring multiple sets of moisture-wicking synthetics. Natural fibers will rot within days.

Your footwear is critical: non-slip soles with drainage ports. The rubber should be specifically rated for hot, wet metal. Bring three pairs—they will deteriorate faster than you expect. Some travelers recommend barefoot climbing gloves for emergency traverses.

Pack electronics in military-grade dry bags with desiccant packets. The moisture will find any weakness. Bring backup batteries—the humidity drains power at alarming rates. Traditional cameras fog instantly; consider action cameras with anti-fog housing.

**Biological Hazards**
The spores. Dear gods, the spores. Begin antihistamine regimens one week prior. Pack bronchodilators even if you've never had asthma. The air is thick with reproductive matter from thousands of plant species. Your sinuses will revolt. Some travelers report hallucinations from unknown fungal species—keep a reality-check journal.

The plants grow aggressively. What looks like a clear path in the morning may be overgrown by afternoon. Bring pruning shears and a machete. Mark your routes with biodegradable ribbon—metal markers oxidize beyond recognition within hours.

**Navigation Challenges**
There are no maps. The structure grows and changes too rapidly for cartography. Learn to navigate by water flow—uphill leads to newer construction, downhill to older, more stable sections. The sound of pumps indicates mechanical cores where you might find maintenance workers or emergency exits.

Visibility rarely exceeds ten meters. Bring redundant lighting—headlamps, chest lights, backup flashlights. The green-filtered sunlight creates a perpetual twilight in lower levels. Learn to move by sound and touch.

**Sustenance and Water**
Paradoxically, clean drinking water is scarce. The circulation systems mix industrial runoff with organic matter. Bring purification tablets and a reliable filter. Test any collected water for heavy metals—the copper leaching is severe.

Many plants are edible, but identification is treacherous in the modified growing conditions. Familiar species develop unfamiliar characteristics. Pack at least a week's worth of nutrition bars. The berries that feed the crow-parrot may poison humans—observe for 48 hours before trying any foraged food.

**Psychological Preparation**
The Tower Garden induces a specific dysphoria. The constant sound of water becomes maddening. The blurred boundaries between inside/outside, built/grown, machine/organism create cognitive dissonance. Practice meditation techniques for managing environmental overwhelm.

The isolation is profound despite the living density. You may go days without seeing another person, hearing only the mechanical whisper and water's voice. The bird rarely calls. Prepare for a loneliness that feels almost sacred.

**Cultural Protocols**
If you encounter workers, do not assume they are human. The steam obscures forms. Some report autonomous systems so sophisticated they mimic human movement. Others swear the workers are human but changed by long exposure. Maintain respectful distance. Do not interfere with new construction.

The Tower Garden may be someone's project, or it may have evolved beyond its creators' intent. Treat it as both artwork and entity. Take nothing but photographs (if your equipment survives). Leave nothing but footprints (which will be consumed within hours).

**Emergency Protocols**
Establish check-in schedules with someone outside. The structure interferes with most communication. Find the maintenance cores—they sometimes have hardline connections to ground level.

If you become genuinely lost, follow water downward. Eventually, all water exits the system. The journey may take days and lead through sections no human has designed or intended, but water always finds a way out.

Trust your primitive brain if it screams danger. The Garden is not malicious, but it is utterly indifferent to human needs. You are neither gardener nor crop here—merely another temporary moisture source in the vast breathing system.

**Final Notes**
Visit during the dry season if one exists. Some report the entire structure floods during certain weather patterns, becoming a vertical river for weeks at a time.

Bring more food than you think necessary. Time moves strangely in the constant twilight and mechanical rhythm. What feels like hours may be days. Your circadian rhythms will abandon you.

The Tower Garden changes those who stay too long. Veterans report dreams of water for years afterward, a tendency to let mold grow in their homes, an inability to tolerate dry air. Some never fully readjust to life at ground level, to boundaries between inside and outside, to the tyranny of static architecture.

Consider carefully if you truly wish to visit. The Garden will mark you. You will leave carrying spores in your lungs, moisture in your memories, the sound of mechanical breathing in your dreams. You will understand, perhaps for the first time, what it means for a building to be truly alive.

And you may find yourself returning, drawn by the humid whisper, the promise of growth without plan, the seductive dissolution of every boundary you once thought immutable.

The Tower Garden breathes, and it may teach you to breathe differently too.
