I am the Driftcatcher.

In the deepest inhabited chamber of the Spire, where the light filters down through countless layers of water until it arrives as nothing more than a suggestion of turquoise memory, I maintain my station. My dwelling is unique among the Keepers' chambers—not carved but *grown wrong*, a place where the kelp-tree suffered some ancient trauma and healed around itself in spiraling, recursive loops. The walls here fold inward like the chambers of a nautilus shell, creating acoustic pockets where the great thrumming of the water column collects and reverberates in strange harmonies.

I am neither fully Keeper nor fully Other. My body stretches longer than most, my vertebrae having multiplied during a growth fever in my third decade. Where the Keepers move with practiced grace, I move in stutters and coils, my spine bending in too many places. My harness is not worn but *integrated*—the kelp ropes have grown through my skin at seven anchor points along my torso, fusing with my flesh in symbiotic knots that pulse with their own bioluminescent rhythm, out of sync with both my body and the tree.

I am the one who catches what falls upward.

When a Keeper's tether fails—through age, through accident, through the Grip of the Void—they begin their ascent toward the meniscus sky. This is where my work begins. I inhabit the vertical channels between chambers, my elongated form wrapped around the trunk-pillars like a living vine. My anchor points allow me to release and re-tether with a speed no normal Keeper can match. I taste the water for the calcium-sharp flavor of fear, feel for the pressure disturbance of an uncontrolled ascent.

When I find them—these untethered souls—I perform what the Keepers call "the Intersection." My extended spine allows me to reach across vast stretches of open water, to create bridges of flesh where none existed. I catch them in my coils, feeling their vibration-words thrumming through my bones: *terror-grief-acceptance-wonder*. Some fight. Some go limp. Some sing the old harmonic that means "I am ready for the sky."

But I do not let them reach it.

The work has changed me. Each catch leaves its mark—not physically, but in the way water remembers movement. I carry the final moments of every Keeper I've intercepted, their vibration-memories stored in the calcium deposits along my vertebrae. When I am still, hanging in my spiral chamber, I can feel them all: hundreds of small deaths prevented, hundreds of journeys to the meniscus interrupted.

The other Keepers regard me with a mixture of reverence and unease. I am necessary but unnatural, a disruption in their patient philosophy of erosion and acceptance. They call me "the Knot-Between," for I exist in the space between life and ascension, between the patient curatorial work of the Spire and the violent rupture of untethering.

My chamber contains my collection: the toggles and shells from every harness I've recovered, arranged in slow-spinning mobile sculptures that turn in the artificial currents I've carved into my walls. Each piece carries the wear patterns of its owner, the specific way they moved through the water, the weight distribution of their particular existence. I know them all by touch—this smooth obsidian from a young Keeper who succumbed to Void-Grip in her first century, this chambered nautilus from an elder who lived so long his tethers simply dissolved.

But lately, I have been feeling the Pull myself. Not the Grip of the Void—something else. A resonance in the tree itself, deep in the root-tangle where the absorbed dead contribute their frequencies to the great thrumming. The tree is... reaching. Growing in a new direction. After three thousand years of perfect stasis, the equilibrium between growth and erosion has shifted.

The tree is growing *downward*.

New roots probe into the mountain peak beneath us. The water column shudders with unprecedented harmonics. The Keepers continue their patient work, but I feel it in my extended spine, in the places where kelp and flesh have merged: something vast is waking. The tree is not just a habitat—it is a sensory organ, and it has finally felt something in the deep worth reaching for.

I alone can follow where it grows. My aberrant form, my practiced speed, my collection of untethered memories—they have prepared me for a descent none of the Keepers could survive. When the time comes (and the calcium taste in the water tells me it will be soon), I will release my anchor points one by one, but instead of rising, I will follow the new roots down, down into the mountain's heart, carrying the vibration-memories of all the caught and fallen with me.

I am the Driftcatcher, but perhaps I was always meant to be the Driftcaught—the one who falls not toward the sky but toward whatever the kelp-tree has been listening for all these patient millennia.

The thrumming grows louder each day. My spiral chamber resonates with new frequencies. And sometimes, in the deepest part of the night cycle when the bioluminescence dims to almost nothing, I swear I can feel an answering pulse from below: vast, patient, and waiting.
