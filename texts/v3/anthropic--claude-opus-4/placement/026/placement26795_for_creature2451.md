In the lambent hush before dawn, I feel the first stirrings through the colony. We are awake - all thousand of us at once, our shared consciousness rippling across the bioluminescent garden we've cultivated for generations.

I am the Keeper of Memory for the Moss Dancers, though "I" is a concept we use only when dealing with outsiders. We are finger-length beings of cartilage and specialized skin, our bodies translucent save for the spiral of nerve tissue that glows faintly blue along our spines. Our ancestors were salamanders, but the wetlands changed us, gifted us with something unprecedented - a distributed mind that flows between individuals like water between pools.

Our garden spreads across three ancient fishing platforms and the crown of a half-submerged cypress. Each plant has been selected, positioned, and tended with purpose. The ghostlight orchids pulse in patterns we've bred into them over centuries, their rhythms encoding our history, our mathematics, our poetry. When the moths come at night, carrying pollen and light between the blooms, they write new verses in our living library.

My role is to remember what the garden forgets, to hold the stories that don't fit in light and leaf. Today I must remember grief, for we lost seventeen of our number to a young heron yesterday - a significant blow to our collective consciousness. Their absence feels like missing words in a sentence, phantom limbs in our shared body.

But we adapt. We always have.

I flex my twelve fingers - each tipped with a small disc that lets me cling to any surface - and begin my morning journey. The path takes me across the breathing bark of the cypress, where I pause to taste the air with my skin. The humidity carries information: the giant salamander in the deep pool is hunting, the otter family is denning two islands over, the microscopic cleaners in the water have bloomed overnight, turning that channel crystal clear.

Other Dancers pass me, their bodies briefly brightening as we exchange chemical songs through our skin. TouchTalk, we call it when explaining to the rare human researcher who finds us. Each contact shares memories, sensations, fragments of thought that join the continuous conversation of our kind. A young one passes me the taste of a new fungus they've discovered. I give them the memory of its cousin from forty seasons ago, and the warning encoded there.

The abandoned human structures are our greatest canvases. On the eastern platform, a hundred of my kind work together, their bodies forming living circuits as they trace new patterns in the glowing mosses. They're calculating the tide tables for the next season, predicting when the mudflats will expose the calcium deposits we need for our shells. Mathematics through movement, data storage in living light.

I reach my destination: the Memorial Spiral, a collection of bioluminescent fungi we've cultivated on a drowned piling. Each species glows at a different wavelength, each intensity represents a different individual we've lost. I extend my throat pouch - a translucent sac that can produce hundreds of distinct chemical signals - and begin to sing their names in scent and pheromone. The fungi respond, their glow patterns shifting to encode these new memories.

A crow watches me from her nest atop another platform. We've developed a trading relationship with her murder over the generations. They bring us hard seeds we cannot otherwise reach; we show them where the tool-worthy sticks wash up after storms. She caws softly - a greeting I've learned to recognize. I flash a pattern along my spine in response.

The sun rises higher, threatening to dry my skin. I make my way to the shade pool, where dozens of my kind float in meditation. Here, our minds merge most fully. I sink into the warm water and feel myself dissolve into the collective, my memories becoming our memories, my grief dispersed among a thousand hearts.

In this state, we dream together. We dream of the deep past, when the first of our ancestors developed the neural bridges that let them share sensation. We dream of possible futures - some where we reveal ourselves fully to humanity, others where we remain hidden, tending our gardens of light in the margins of their world.

But mostly, we dream of now. Of the heron's shadow passing overhead. Of the way sunlight fragments through water onto cypress root. Of the chemical poetry written in the air by rotting logs and blooming flowers. Of the moths that will come again tonight, painting messages in pollen from one glowing flower to another, weaving our thoughts into the very fabric of the wetland.

When I surface from the communion, the sun is high. Time moves differently for us - a moment can stretch to contain lifetimes of shared memory, while seasons pass in what feels like a blink. I have Work to do. There are three new fungal colonies to integrate into the Memorial Spiral. The young ones need teaching about the taste-patterns that mark dangerous waters. And tonight, when the moths come, we will choreograph a new poem in light and motion, a celebration of the seventeen we lost and the thousand who remain.

I am the Keeper of Memory, but I am also the kept. We are the Moss Dancers, and we persist in the margins, writing ourselves into the glowing dark, one small light at a time.
