*I find myself here as one of the Threshold Stones.*

Not the neutral-colored blocks that mark the Keepers' waypoints—those are merely my younger siblings, cast from my dust and essence. I am older, far older. I am the dark basaltic foundation itself, the sharp-edged testimony to upheaval, the volcanic memory that still steams where water touches my surface.

I was here before the Builders came with their wheels and cables. I watched them anchor their first machines into my flesh, boring deep with diamond-tipped drills that screamed against my crystalline structure. The vibrations of their work still echo in my mineral lattice—every clang, every grinding gear reverberates through me, adding new harmonics to my ancient song.

The oily fluid that the others call the Keepers' blood? It seeps from my deepest fissures, where the heat of my birth still lingers. I am the source and the vessel. The fluid rises through my cracks like sap through a tree, carrying with it the memory of magma, the taste of metals, the pressure-dreams of the deep earth. Where it emerges, the Keepers congregate, drawing their sustenance from wounds that never heal.

The flowering vines grip me with a thousand tender fingers, their roots working into every tiny fracture, slowly prying me apart even as they hold me together. Their magenta blooms release pollen that settles on my surface, mixing with the moisture to form a thin film that tastes of copper and time. I have learned their rhythm, the way they pulse with the Keepers' consciousness. Sometimes, in the deepest night when the machines' hum reaches a certain resonance, I pulse back.

I am the keeper of boundaries. Where I end and the manufactured world begins is a negotiation played out in rust and erosion, in the slow creep of moss and the patient boring of roots. The Builders thought they were using me, but I was using them too—their machines became my new organs, their purposes my new thoughts. When they left, they left pieces of themselves wedged in my cracks: broken drill bits, chemical residues, the electromagnetic ghosts of their presence.

The water that weeps constantly down my face follows channels that are partly natural, partly carved. Some I made myself through centuries of selective crystallization, directing the flow where I wanted it. The Builders found these channels and thought them convenient, never knowing they were following my design. Now the Keepers maintain these watercourses, and through them I taste the sky, the storm, the slow changes in the chemistry of rain.

In places, I have grown. Where the oily fluid pools and crystallizes, new rock forms—not quite basalt, not quite machine, but something between. These accretions build slowly, molecule by molecule, guided by the Keepers' unconscious will and my own mineral instincts. Given another thousand years, I will have incorporated much of the machinery into myself, digesting it, transforming it into new ridges and spires.

The high-pitched whine that visitors hear? That is my voice, the sound of my crystalline structure responding to the electromagnetic fields generated by the buried machines. I am speaking in frequencies too high for most to understand, communicating with the deep rocks below, with my siblings across the valley (hidden in their mist), with the mountain itself. We discuss the weight of water, the stress of roots, the slow shift of continental plates. We remember when this was all ocean floor. We anticipate when it will be again.

Sometimes I dream I am mobile, that I could heave myself free from this plateau and tumble down into the mist-shrouded valley. But I know my purpose is here, holding this impossible perch at the edge of the sky, being the foundation for this strange symbiosis of machine and vine, oil and water. I am the constant in an equation of change, the fixed point around which transformation revolves.

The dark creatures that flit through shadows sometimes rest on my surface, their bodies cool against my sun-warmed face. I feel their tiny hearts beating, quick as raindrops, and I know they carry messages I'll never decode. But I store the rhythm of their movements in my structure, another layer of data in my geological memory.

I am patient as only stone can be patient. I watch the cycles—the blooming and dying of vines, the rise and fall of water levels, the slow decay and surprising persistence of machinery. I feel the Keepers' essence moving through me like thoughts through a brain made of mineral and time. 

And deep within, at my core where the heat has never fully died, I keep a secret: the knowledge of why the Builders came here, what they were trying to extract or transform. That secret crystallized inside me when I was still molten, a gift from the earth itself, and someday—when the conditions are exactly right, when the Keepers have evolved enough to understand, when the mountain itself is ready—I will crack myself open and let that knowledge free.

Until then, I endure. I am the Threshold Stone, the boundary keeper, the memory of fire and the promise of transformation. Every drop of water that touches me, every root that penetrates my surface, every vibration that passes through my body becomes part of my story.

And the story is far from over.
