I find myself as one of the Depth Singers—a being that few in Umbra Altus have seen, though all have heard our voices threading through the water pipes and resonating up from the city's deepest wells.

My form shifts between states, sometimes corporeal, sometimes something more like organized sound itself. When I take physical shape, I appear as a tall, willowy figure composed of what looks like liquid mercury mixed with shadow. My body ripples with each movement, creating patterns that mirror the sound waves I emit. Where others might have a face, I have a smooth, reflective surface that occasionally forms mouth-like openings when I sing.

I dwell in the Resonance Chambers—vast underground caverns beneath Umbra Altus where all the city's water converges before being pumped back up through the endless network of pipes. These chambers were carved by water over millennia, their walls smooth and curved like the inside of enormous shells. The acoustics here are perfect, designed by erosion and time into a natural amphitheater.

My existence revolves around the Great Tuning. Through my songs, I maintain the harmonic frequency that keeps the city's water in constant motion. Without the Depth Singers, the water would stagnate, the pumps would fail, and Umbra Altus would drown in its own lifeblood. Each of us tends to a different section of the waterways, our voices creating a complex symphony that few surface dwellers comprehend.

But I am more than a maintenance worker. My songs carry memory—each note contains droplets of the city's history, absorbed from the water that has touched every stone, every inhabitant, every secret corner of Umbra Altus. When I sing, I release these memories back into the flow, ensuring that the city never forgets itself even in the eternal night.

Sometimes, late in what the Timekeepers call the "deep hours," curious Umbrans or brave Mollusks descend to the chamber edges to listen. They never see me fully—I remain in the deeper pools where the darkness is absolute—but they hear my voice rising from the depths. Some claim my songs have shown them visions of Umbra Altus before the eternal night fell, though I neither confirm nor deny such tales.

My diet, if it can be called that, consists of the microscopic life that flows through the water—tiny bioluminescent organisms that I filter through my form. Their light becomes part of me, stored in crystalline nodes throughout my body that pulse gently when I sing.

I am ancient, perhaps as old as the city itself, yet I experience time differently than the surface dwellers. For me, existence is measured not in days or years but in cycles—the rhythm of water through stone, the slow erosion of the chambers, the gradual evolution of my song as the city above changes and grows.

In the rare moments when all the Depth Singers pause their songs simultaneously, a profound silence falls over Umbra Altus. The water continues to flow for a while, carried by momentum alone, but gradually slows. The surface dwellers feel it as an inexplicable unease, a wrongness in the very air. We never let the silence last long—our purpose is too vital, our connection to the city's lifeblood too essential.

I am keeper, historian, and heartbeat all at once. I am the voice in the pipes that children hear before sleep, the rhythm in the rain that workers match with their hammers, the whisper in the wells that lovers lean over to make wishes. I am as essential to Umbra Altus as its eternal night, and like the darkness, I will remain until the last drop of water ceases to flow.
