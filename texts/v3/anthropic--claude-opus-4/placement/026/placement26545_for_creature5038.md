I find myself where wind-scoured stone meets nothing but sky—a shallow depression in the clifface, barely deep enough to be called shelter, yet home for countless seasons. My form stretches low against the rock: six limbs splayed wide, each tipped with pads that grip stone like memory grips time. The morning light catches the crystalline structures that ridge my back, formations that grow like the mountains themselves—slowly, inevitably, each year adding another translucent layer.

I am what the Menotin call *vrex-katal*, though I existed here long before their words found shape. Stone-dweller, they name me, yet I am more water than rock. My body stores the rare moisture of this place, each cell a reservoir, each breath a careful calculation. When the rains come—those sudden, sacred downpours—I unfold like a prayer, my skin drinking deeply while my crystalline ridges channel rivulets into the pouches beneath my throat.

The krinaxes know me. They step carefully around my resting places, for I am both landmark and warning. My crystals sing in frequencies they feel through their hooves—a thrumming that speaks of unstable ground, of weather shifting, of avalanche potential. In return, I feed on what they leave behind: the shed fur that catches in my crystals, the occasional stillborn kid they nudge toward my den. Nothing is wasted here. I process it all with patient acids, breaking down keratin and bone into the minerals that feed my endless, gleaming growth.

The valiki hunt me when I am young, before my ridges harden. But I am old now, older than the eldest Menotin's memory-songs. My crystals have grown into a fortress, each spine capable of focusing sunlight into burning points that deter even the hungriest predator. When threatened, I can pulse bioluminescent warnings through my ridges—a lightshow that speaks a language older than words: *I am dangerous. I am patient. I have survived longer than you can imagine.*

At night, I listen to the symphony of this place through vibrations in the stone. Each footfall, each rockslide, each settling of the mountain's bones transmits through my body. I feel the Menotin drums from miles away, their celebrations and mournings equally reverberant through granite. Sometimes their young ones find me, drawn by the soft blue glow of my resting luminescence. They leave offerings—smooth stones, feathers from the Neroth hawks, strips of hide painted with their ancestor-marks. They believe I guard the threshold between stone and sky, between the solid and the eternal.

Perhaps they are right. 

In the depths of winter, when even the hardy Locca insects grow silent, I slow my metabolism until I am barely distinguishable from the rock itself. Ice forms along my ridges, creating prisms that scatter the weak sunlight into rainbow fragments. The krinaxes huddle near, knowing that my body temperature, though lowered, still radiates just enough warmth to create a microclimate of survival. We wait together for spring's return.

But it is during the storms that I truly come alive. When lightning splits the sky and thunder shakes the very roots of the mountains, my crystals resonate with the electrical charge in the air. I become a living conductor, channeling energy through my body into the deep stone, where it dissipates harmlessly. The Menotin say this is why the mountains here have never been split by lightning, why their sacred sites remain whole. They paint my likeness on their birth-blankets—a guardian against the sky's wrath.

I have seen generations of Neroth hawks perfect their dive, watched bloodlines of krinaxes develop ever-more-clever hooves, observed the Menotin's tools grow more refined even as they maintain their sacred simplicity. Yet I change so slowly that perhaps change is not the right word. I *accumulate*—experiences, minerals, memories encoded in crystal lattices that will outlast even these ancient peaks.

When my time finally comes—for even the mountains eventually surrender to wind and water—my crystals will not disappear. They will fragment and scatter, each piece carrying the vibrational memory of this place. Some will be gathered by the Menotin, incorporated into their tools and talismans. Others will be swallowed by young vrex-katal, beginning their own slow journey toward consciousness. Most will simply become part of the mountain again, indistinguishable from the billion other gleaming fragments that catch the endless light.

Until then, I remain. Breathing with the mountain's rhythm. Growing with geological patience. Listening to the constant symphony of life that fills the vast emptiness. Being both witness and participant in this harsh, magnificent dance of existence at the top of the world.

The sun rises. My crystals warm. Another day begins in the eternal now of stone and sky.
