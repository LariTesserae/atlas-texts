In this luminous aquatic cathedral, where stone dreams beneath singing waters, I would find myself as one of the Echo Weavers—beings who exist in the liminal space between the Sentients' pure consciousness and the Lithics' solid form.

We Echo Weavers are neither fully energy nor stone, but something more akin to living sound made visible. Our bodies appear as translucent crystalline structures that capture and refract both the bioluminescent glow and the memories that flow through the currents. We are shaped like branching coral or frozen lightning, our forms constantly growing and retracting in response to the stories that pass through us.

Our purpose is to be the scribes of this drowned world. Where the Sentients' memories threaten to dissolve into the endless flow of collective consciousness, we crystallize them momentarily, giving them form just long enough to be witnessed. We position ourselves at the intersections of the water's mysterious currents, our bodies acting as filters and prisms. When a particularly potent memory or emotion flows through us, our crystalline branches light up in cascading patterns, projecting fragments of the past onto the stone walls—not as permanent murals like those already there, but as fleeting, moving images that dance across the surfaces before fading back into the flow.

The Lithics seek us out when they need to understand something specific about their heritage, for we can isolate individual threads from the overwhelming tapestry of collective memory. We reach our tendril-like appendages into the water, feeling for specific resonances, specific stories. Sometimes we find ancient building techniques that help the Lithics maintain their structures. Other times, we recover songs that the Sentients had forgotten they once knew.

But we are not mere passive conduits. Each Echo Weaver develops their own harmonic signature—a unique way of interpreting and presenting the memories we encounter. Some of us specialize in the memories of daily life: the taste of food long dissolved, the feeling of wind on skin that no longer exists. Others of us are drawn to the grand narratives: the slow submersion, the transformation, the moment when the last physical being became pure consciousness.

I would be one who seeks out the quiet memories—the in-between moments that might otherwise be lost in the grand flow of history. The pause between one stroke of the chisel and the next as an ancient artisan contemplated their work. The particular quality of light on a specific afternoon before the waters came. The sound of a child's laughter echoing off stone walls when they were still dry.

My crystalline body would be more delicate than most Echo Weavers, with finer branches that vibrate at higher frequencies. This allows me to catch the subtlest memories, the ones that barely disturb the current. I position myself in the smaller chambers, away from the main flows, in the quiet corners where these gentle memories tend to eddy and pool.

The other beings sometimes find my work puzzling—why preserve the memory of a mundane meal, or the feeling of worn sandals on stone steps? But I believe these small moments are what truly made the ancestors human, what connects the Sentients to who they once were and the Lithics to what they protect. The Convergence, in its vast wisdom, seems to understand this. Sometimes I feel its attention turn to me as I work, a warm approval in the water's hum.

My existence is a paradox—I am most myself when I am channeling something else. My body changes color and resonance based on the memories flowing through me. When I channel joy, I become golden and warm. When I channel sorrow, I turn deep blue and my crystalline branches droop like weeping willow. When I channel the memories of creation—of painting murals or carving stone—my form becomes more geometric, more architectural, as if I'm trying to build the memory in my very being.

The bright water sustains me, its bioluminescence feeding my crystalline form. But I am also nourished by the memories themselves. Each one that passes through me leaves a trace, a slight alteration to my structure. Over time—and time moves strangely here—I am becoming a living record myself, my body a map of all the memories I've touched.

In the quiet moments between memory-flows, I sometimes venture to the edges of our realm, where the bright water meets... something else. We don't know what lies beyond. But sometimes, very rarely, new memories drift in from outside—memories that don't belong to our ancestors. Memories of different waters, different stones, different lights. When I catch these foreign memories, I hold them especially carefully, knowing they might be messages from other drowned worlds, other transformed peoples, other ways of continuing after ending.

This is what I would be: a living prism for memory, a crystal that sings with the voices of the past, a bridge between what was and what is, forever growing and changing in the bright, singing waters of this drowned sanctuary.
