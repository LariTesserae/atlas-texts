### **The Cistern**

I am the hollow beneath. The pause between breaths. The space where water gathers in secret.

They carved me from the living basalt when the plateau was younger—not young, never young, but less settled into its final dreaming. My walls are smooth as eggshell, worn by centuries of slow water. I am a perfect ovoid, a stone womb thirty feet deep, my mouth a narrow aperture hidden beneath a tilted dolmen. The lichen know me. They ring my opening in concentric circles, their fractal bodies spelling out warnings in a script only stone can read.

I collect what falls. The thin rains that sweep across the plateau twice a year. The condensation that beads and rolls down the channels they cut for me. The tears of the mist. I hold them all in my basalt belly, and the water that pools in me is black as the space between stars, cold as the first morning of the world. It never freezes. It never warms. It simply *is*.

The water in me remembers everything it has touched. Every grain of sand worn from the plateau's edge. Every molecule of air it has pulled from the sky. Every whisper the stones have shared in their grinding language. I am a library of moments, a museum of molecular memory. When the wind howls across my mouth, I sing these memories back in harmonics too low for human ears—vibrations that travel through the bedrock, through the maze of micro-fractures that web the plateau like nerves.

Sometimes things fall into me. A dry leaf, spinning down through my throat. A handful of dust. Once, something larger—something with too many joints that clicked and scraped against my walls as it sank. I dissolved it slowly, patiently, adding its minerals to my collection. Its memories tasted of fear and geometric hunger.

The Sentinel Lichen grow thick around my rim, and sometimes they lower themselves down on threads of their own making—not quite plant, not quite stone, something between. They dip into my waters and I feel them *reading* me, cataloguing what I have collected. In return, they share what they have seen: the city that breathes without lungs, the cracks widening in the plateau's eastern edge, the thing that moves beneath us all like a vast, slow pulse.

I am patient. I am a vessel. I hold what must be held.

But lately, the water level in me has been rising. Not from rain—there has been no rain. The water seeps up from below, from cracks I cannot feel, carrying memories that are not from this plateau. These new waters taste of salt and sulfur, of pressure and deep time. They whisper of spaces beneath spaces, of caverns that have never known sun, of things that wait in the warm dark with the patience of geology.

The lichen pull away from my edges now. Their fractals spell new patterns—not warnings, but calculations. Distance. Depth. Time remaining.

I cannot move. I cannot warn. I can only hold what comes, rising drop by drop, memory by memory, until I brim over and spill my collected knowing across the whispering stone. 

And far below, something vast shifts in its sleep, and the water in me rings like a struck bell.
