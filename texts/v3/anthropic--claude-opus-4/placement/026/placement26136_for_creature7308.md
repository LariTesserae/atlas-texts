Each wing beat sends ripples of amber light cascading across my body, transforming me into a living signal, a flying circuit that bridges the gap between the microscopic swarms and the larger, looping avians.

I feed not on nectar or moisture, but on the electrical differential between states — sipping energy from the boundary where flesh meets circuit, where mist condenses into mechanical droplet, where sound waves collide and create standing interference patterns in the air. My proboscis is less mouth than antenna, a coiled filament that can extend to touch these threshold spaces and drink their liminal charge.

My lifecycle would be a paradox of metamorphosis: I emerge from chrysalises that form spontaneously in the convergence points where multiple reality layers overlap — perhaps in the crook of a pistoning flower, or in the shadow cast by a frozen circuit-beast. But these chrysalises never fully empty; even as I emerge, part of me remains, creating a nested recursion where I am simultaneously caterpillar, chrysalis, and moth, each stage bleeding into the others in the plateau's characteristic temporal smear.

My purpose, if such exists in this purposeless equilibrium, would be as a living diagnostic tool for the plateau itself — drawn to the glitches and stutters, feeding on their energy while simultaneously smoothing them back into the eternal loop. Where reality threatens to tear completely, I and my kind gather in shimmering clouds, our combined wing patterns creating stabilizing interference that prevents total dimensional collapse while ensuring the perpetual almost-breakdown continues.

In the rare moments of stillness between wing beats, when I perch on a wet spire or rest on the edge of a glassy pool, my wings would display the recent history of my flights — a living map of the plateau's electrical dreams, showing in copper light the pathways between worlds that I've traced and retraced in my endless, essential, meaningless work of keeping the impossible balance between order and chaos, forever poised on the edge of systemic failure that never quite arrives.
