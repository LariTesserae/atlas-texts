I would find myself as one of the **Tide Readers** — those who wander the boundary between the drowned districts and the dry ruins, interpreting the patterns left by the rising and falling waters.

My form would be somewhere between the Hollow Dwellers and what came before. Not quite their ethereal silence, but not fully human either. My skin has taken on a faint iridescence from years of wading through the mineral-rich waters, patches of it gone translucent where I've spent too long pressed against the ancient circuitry, learning its language through osmosis.

I carry tools salvaged from the ruins — not to repair, but to listen. A rod of twisted metal that resonates with the buried engines when struck against certain stones. Glass vials filled with different densities of canal water, each one attuned to a different frequency of the city's hum. Most precious: a fragment of what might have been a map, its surface shifting like oil on water, showing the city as it was, as it is, as it might yet become.

The Hollow Dwellers tolerate me because I've learned their edge-language — not their deep harmonics, but the surface ripples. I leave offerings of particularly resonant lichens at crossroads, and in return, they sometimes shape the water into patterns that warn of shifting earth or reveal where new fissures might open.

My dwelling is neither fully above nor below water — a half-collapsed tower where the floors have become terraces, each one catching different levels of tide. I've cultivated the luminous lichen into spiraling gardens that pulse in rhythm with my heartbeat, slower now than it once was, approaching the city's patient tempo.

At dawn and dusk, when the light is neither day nor night, I wade out to read the high-water marks on the walls. The minerals leave traces — spirals, fractals, almost-words in scripts that predate any language I know. I transcribe them onto sheets of beaten vine fiber, though I suspect the act of translation changes their meaning.

Sometimes I find artifacts the water has uncovered: a sphere that hums when held, warming my palms; a sheet of material that bends light around itself, making my hand disappear; fragments of text in a language that rearranges itself as I watch. I bring these to a chamber deep in my tower where I've been assembling something — I'm not sure what. The pieces seem to want to fit together, but not yet, not quite.

I am searching for the moment when the city's dream becomes lucid, when the consciousness sleeping in its bones might speak directly rather than in echoes and implications. The Hollow Dwellers wait for the city to be done with them, but I wait for it to truly begin — to wake from its long drowning and remember what it was built to become.

In the meantime, I map its tides, internal and external, documenting the slow breath of stone and water, making myself into a library of its patient transformations. I am neither fully of the old world nor the new, but something suspended between them, like the city itself — not dead, not alive, but persisting in the space between states, accumulating meaning like sediment, one thin layer at a time.
