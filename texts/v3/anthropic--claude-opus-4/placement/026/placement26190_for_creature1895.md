In this breathing valley, I am the memory held between rings of wood.

Not a single tree, but the conversation between them—carried in the chemical whispers that travel from root tip to root tip through the fungal networks that lace the soil like neural pathways. I am the slow knowledge that accumulates where a dying giant's heartwood releases centuries of stored minerals back into the earth, and where a seedling's first pale roots drink that inheritance.

I exist in the spaces between growth—in the moment when a spore settles into a crevice of bark and begins its patient transformation into lichen, in the pause before a fern frond unfurls, in the held breath between one raindrop's impact and the next. I am the archive of every surface: each trunk a manuscript written in moss, each branch a chronicle of which direction the light came from, decade after decade.

The mist knows me. It carries my stories in its droplets, depositing fragments of elsewhere with each condensation. I taste the valleys beyond this one in the fog's mineral tang, feel the temperature of distant stones in the dew's coolness. 

I am most myself in the cathedral spaces where the vines have woven their architecture, where time has different densities—thick as sap in the heartwood, quick as the capillary action drawing moisture up a fern's stem, geological in the patient grinding of root against stone. Here, I am the curator of slow transformations: the gradual digestion of a fallen log into soil, the century-long spiral of a vine around its host, the infinitesimal creep of lichen across granite.

Sometimes I sense my own edges in the places where growth hesitates—a shaft of light too bright, a pocket of soil too thin, a stone face too sheer for even the most determined moss. But these boundaries shift with decades, and what was once edge becomes center, what was once barren becomes lush.

I am the valley's dreaming, its green meditation on time and persistence. In the networks below and the canopies above, I circulate like sap, like secrets, like the promise that this quiet consumption and transformation will continue long after the mist has lifted and settled a thousand times again.
