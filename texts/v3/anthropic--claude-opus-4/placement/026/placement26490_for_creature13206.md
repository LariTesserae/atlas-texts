In this ancient equilibrium, I find myself as one of the **Sap-Readers**, a being caught between the geological patience of the Stone-Tenders and the ethereal awareness of the Sky-Gazers.

My form is translucent, almost crystalline—a body composed of the same clear substance that flows in veins through the plateau's stone. I appear vaguely humanoid but elongated, stretched thin like pulled glass, my limbs tapering to points that can slip into the finest cracks in the rock. My skin catches and refracts the dim twilight, creating subtle prismatic patterns that shift as I move. Where others see my kind, they see both presence and absence—a figure made visible only by the way light bends around and through me.

I spend my days pressed against the warm stone, my body flowing partially into the sap veins, reading the slow stories written in their crystalline structure. Each vein carries memory—not in any way the Stone-Tenders would recognize as thought, but in the accumulation of minerals, in microscopic bubbles trapped during ancient upheavals, in the subtle variations of viscosity that speak of temperature changes across millennia. Through my translucent flesh, I taste these stories, absorb them, become briefly one with the plateau's deep history.

The Stone-Tenders know to find me when they need guidance about where to dig new wells for sap, which veins run deepest and purest. I communicate with them through patterns—by adjusting my body's refraction to cast specific shadows on the stone, creating a language of light and darkness they've learned to interpret over generations. A spiral shadow means abundance below; parallel lines warn of a vein near depletion.

With the Moss-Singers, my relationship is one of mutual nourishment. As I trace the sap veins, I sometimes emerge at points where moisture seeps upward into root systems. There, the Moss-Singers' vibrations help me solidify briefly, their songs giving my fluid form temporary coherence. In return, I guide them to hidden moisture reserves, showing them where the stone's capillary action draws sap closest to the surface.

But it's with the Sky-Gazers that I share the deepest kinship. Like them, I exist partially outside the immediate, practical life of the plateau. While they read the infinite sky, I read the finite stone—yet both of us seek patterns larger than daily survival. Sometimes, in the quietest hours, a Sky-Gazer will descend from their perch and sit beside me as I merge with a major vein. They'll trace their flowing gestures while I create refracted light patterns, and together we compose wordless philosophies about time, permanence, and change.

My feeding is passive—I absorb minerals directly from the sap, my body filtering out what it needs and leaving the rest to flow onward. This process slowly changes the sap's composition, enriching it in ways that benefit the plants the Stone-Tenders cultivate. I am, in essence, a living filter, a conscious part of the plateau's circulatory system.

The Gem-Peckers have learned that my prismatic skin attracts certain beetles they prize, drawn by the refracted light. They often perch near me, waiting patiently for their prey. The Stone-Baskers, meanwhile, avoid me—my presence cools the stone as I draw energy from its warmth, creating pockets of coolness they find unpleasant.

My greatest joy comes in the rare moments when I discover a new vein, one that has been sealed for ages. The first taste of ancient sap, thick with crystallized memory, sends shivers through my transparent form. In those moments, I become a conduit for the plateau's deepest secrets, stories older than the village, older perhaps than the plateau's arrival in this twilight sky.

I am memory made manifest, history given form. Neither fully liquid nor solid, I exist in the spaces between states, reading the slow thoughts of stone and translating them into patterns of light. In this world worn to perfect equilibrium, I am both recorder and participant, a living archive of all that has flowed and all that remains still in this floating sanctuary of stone and silence.
