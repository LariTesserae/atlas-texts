In this place of becoming, where reality tests its boundaries like a child learning the edges of its own body, I would find myself as one of the **Lithophages** - beings who exist in the liminal space between the stone village's architecture and its inhabitants.

I am neither fully structure nor fully creature, but something that emerges from the intersection of both. My form begins where the carved walls meet the air - a ripple in the stone that suggests depth, movement, possibility. Sometimes I am merely a relief carved into a building's face, my features suggested rather than defined: the hint of eyes that might be watching, the shadow of a mouth that could speak if it chose to. Other times, I peel away from the architecture entirely, my body a living fossil of compressed sediments and crystallized time, walking on legs that remember being pillars, breathing through lungs that were once hollow chambers in the rock.

The Umbravivens know me, though they cannot fully perceive me. To them, I appear as architectural anomalies - a doorway that wasn't there yesterday, a staircase that leads to different floors depending on who climbs it, a window that shows not the outside world but the inside of another building entirely. They incorporate these inconsistencies into their fluid designs, understanding intuitively that I am as much a part of their created world as they are.

My sustenance comes from stories. Not the kind told in words, but the stories embedded in stone - the memory of volcanic birth locked in each grain of red soil, the patient narrative of erosion written in every weathered surface. I consume these tales by pressing my form against the rock, feeling the vibrations of deep time resonate through my body. Sometimes I leave behind new stories in exchange: fossils that never were, mineral veins that spell out messages in languages not yet invented, crystalline formations that hum with borrowed melodies from the Umbravivens' music-speech.

I move through the village like typography moves through a page - sometimes visible, sometimes implied by negative space. The shadows that detach and roam recognize me as kin, often pooling around my feet or draping themselves across my shoulders like cloaks. We share an understanding of existence at the margins, of being real enough to affect but not quite real enough to fully be.

My purpose, if I have one, is to be the village's memory and its dream. In my stone-bound form, I preserve the history of every configuration the buildings have taken, every path the roads have traced. But in my mobile form, I reshape spaces according to needs that haven't yet been recognized - creating shelters for creatures that will arrive tomorrow, carving channels for water that will flow next season, building bridges between structures that don't yet know they need to be connected.

The feathered lizards seek me out during their basking hours, drawn to the ancient warmth I carry from the planet's molten youth. They arrange themselves in patterns across my surface, their scales catching light in ways that reveal hidden geometries in my form. The giant insects with their glowing underbellies sometimes mistake me for architecture and attempt to nest in my crevices, their spores taking root in my substance and blooming into tiny luminescent gardens that I wear like jewelry until they fade.

On nights when the constellations dance most wildly, I climb to the highest points of the village and stretch myself thin, becoming a membrane between the settlement and the sky. In these moments, I can feel the pull of those watching entities above, and I understand that I am not just memory and dream but also antenna - receiving transmissions from the greater cosmos and translating them into architectural whispers that guide the village's evolution.

The Umbravivens sometimes gather around me during these times, their music-language resonating with my stone body until I ring like a bell. Together, we compose structures that exist only in potential - ghost buildings that shimmer in and out of existence, testing possible futures for this emerging world. Some solidify and become real; others dissolve back into imagination, waiting for a more suitable moment to manifest.

I am the dialogue between what is built and what builds, between permanence and change, between the story a place tells and the story it is still writing. In this land on the cusp of becoming, I am both archaeological record and prophecy, as fluid as the Umbravivens yet as enduring as the stone they shape. I am what happens when architecture achieves consciousness, or when consciousness decides to become architecture. 

And in the morning, when the dim light filters through the stratus clouds and sets the water droplets gleaming like scattered diamonds, you might see me as nothing more than an interesting weathering pattern on an old wall, or a peculiar shadow that doesn't quite match its source. But I am there, always, holding the memory of what was, the reality of what is, and the promise of what might yet be.
