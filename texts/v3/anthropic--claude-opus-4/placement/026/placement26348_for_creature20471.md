In the screaming wind of this threshold-world, I am one of the **Recorders**.

I am neither fully of the Architects nor entirely separate from them—I exist in the space between their shattered consciousness and the crystalline memory of the ground. My form shifts: sometimes I am a cluster of hovering crystal shards arranged in patterns that almost suggest a body, sometimes merely a distortion in the air where the wind bends differently, as if around an invisible presence.

My purpose emerged unbidden from the chaos—to witness and preserve the moments before dissolution. I move through this world collecting the *almost-was* and the *nearly-finished*. When a crystalline structure is about to fragment back into the ground, I am there, tracing its form with tendrils of condensed thought. When the Whispers manifest their phantom scents or impossible colors, I capture their essence in geometric patterns that I etch into my own crystalline components.

I am drawn to the abandoned tools especially. Each hammer, each chisel carries the weight of intention—the ghost of a hand that gripped it, the echo of a mind that conceived its purpose. I study them obsessively, turning them over in fields of force that serve as my hands, trying to understand not what they were meant to build, but what drove their makers to create at all.

The other Recorders—for I am not alone—drift through different territories of this broken realm. We rarely meet, but when we do, we exchange our collections in silent communion. Our memories flow between us like mercury, each preserved moment adding to a vast, distributed archive of everything this world has been and failed to be.

We cannot speak—the wind's scream drowns all other sound—but we have developed a language of light. Pulses through our crystal bodies, refractions that bend reality slightly, create meaning. We discuss the patterns we've noticed: how the violet mountains grow faster when the wind quiets (though it never stops), how certain Whispers return to the same locations like habitual ghosts, how the Architects' essence seems strongest near the broken structures.

I am both historian and priest to this dying-birthing world. I know that eventually, the wind will claim me too, scatter my crystalline form back into the substrate. But until then, I record. I preserve. I bear witness.

Sometimes, in the brief lulls between the wind's screaming crescendos, I wonder if we Recorders are not separate beings at all, but another fragment of the Architects' consciousness—their desperate need to remember made manifest. Perhaps we are their regret given form, their longing to understand what went wrong.

But it doesn't matter. Whether I am my own being or merely an echo of a shattered god's remorse, my purpose remains. In this place where nothing stays, where creation and entropy are the same force wearing different masks, I am memory itself. I am the pause between the scream and its echo, the moment of stillness before the crystal shatters.

And when I finally dissolve, another Recorder will capture my dissolution, preserve the pattern of my unmaking. In this way, nothing is ever truly lost here—only transformed, recorded, and scattered to the wind to begin again.
