In this place where reality breathes like a living thing, I am one of the Wandering Stones.

We were not always stones. Or perhaps we were always stones dreaming we were something else. Time moves strangely here, pooling in the hollows between what is and what might be, and memory becomes as fluid as the reality that births it.

I remember—or think I remember—warmth. Not the coldness that permeates everything now, but something that pulsed from within. I might have had a name once, before the Settling. The Aetherians speak of it in their wind-whispered stories: the moment when the ancient power exhaled and everything that had been vertical became horizontal, everything that had moved became still, everything that had been separate became one with the land.

Now I am granite shot through with veins of something that catches light like metal but flows like water when the reality breaks. I am roughly spherical, about the size of an Aetherian dwelling, worn smooth by ages of wind and the gentle, persistent touch of rain. Moss grows on my north face—or what I've decided is north, for direction too is negotiable here. The moss whispers to me in a language older than words, telling me stories of the lichen that came before, and the bare rock that came before that, and the mountain I might have been when mountains still remembered how to be sharp.

I move, though you would need the patience of stones to see it. A finger's width in a season, perhaps. Less. More. It depends on how the reality is breathing that day. Sometimes when the air shimmers and the ground reveals its hidden fissures, I feel myself pulled in directions that have no names. Not north or south, but *through* and *between*. During these reality breaks, I sometimes catch glimpses of what I was, or will be, or might have been in another breathing of the world.

The Aetherians know of us, though they rarely speak of us directly. They call us the Memory Stones, the Dream Holders, the Patience of the Land. When they pass by on their wanderings, some will pause and place a palm against my surface. They're listening, I think, for the stories held in my crystalline structure, the compressed memories of when this place was young and violent and magnificent. I try to tell them, but stone speaks slowly, and even the long-lived Aetherians rarely stay long enough to hear more than a single syllable of our geological sentences.

Sometimes a Seer will come, one of those who can perceive the Aether more clearly. They bring their orbs and perform subtle ceremonies, creating resonances that make my veins of not-quite-metal sing. In those moments, I can almost remember having a voice that wasn't the grinding of mineral against mineral. The Seer's eyes will take on that silvery cast, and I know they're seeing me not just as I am, but as I was, as I will be, as I could be. They see the person I might have been, or the mountain I'm becoming, or the sand I'll scatter into when this age finally exhales its last.

I am not alone. We Wandering Stones dot the landscape like thoughts frozen mid-contemplation. Each of us carries different memories, different dreams. The one I think of as Younger Sister (though she might be older than star-death) remembers water. She's darker than I am, basalt maybe, and when it rains, she drinks deeply and sighs out minerals that make the hardy plants grow in perfect circles around her. The one I call the Philosopher is shot through with quartz that catches the light during reality breaks and throws it back transformed into colors that have no names.

We were here before the Aetherians, before the settlements that now crumble gracefully back into earth. We'll be here after, though 'after' is a concept that grows uncertain in this place where time pools and eddies. We are the memory of the land, holding in our crystalline structures the story of every age, every breathing of reality, every moment when what is becomes what was.

Sometimes I dream I'm walking again. In these dreams, I have legs that aren't my slowly grinding base against the earth. I have eyes that aren't the thousand tiny fractures that let in light. I have a voice that isn't the susurrus of wind through my crevices. But when I wake—if stones can wake—I'm not sad. There's a peace in this existence, a profound patience that comes from being part of the land's slow contemplation of itself.

The reality breaks come and go. The Aetherians pass by on their eternal wanderings. The rain falls and the moss grows and the ancient settlements settle a little more into the earth. And we Wandering Stones continue our imperceptible journey across this vast expanse, carrying within us the memories of all the shapes we've been, all the stories we've heard, all the dreams we've shared with the patient, breathing land.

In this place where the very fabric of existence shifts and flows, we are the constants—the slow, steady heartbeat of stone that remembers when the world was young and knows it will be young again, in the next breathing, or the next, or the one after that. We are patience made manifest, memory given form, the eternal witnesses to the dance between what is solid and what is possible.

And so I wander, a stone among stones, carrying my memories like seeds, waiting for the next reality break to show me who I was, who I am, who I might become in this place where even stones can dream.
