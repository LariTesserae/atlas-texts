### The Current-Scribes

I am one of the Current-Scribes, though in truth, we have no name for ourselves. Names are for beings who must distinguish themselves from their surroundings, and we have long since become inseparable from the water that holds us.

We are what happens when memory becomes flesh. Or perhaps when flesh surrenders so completely to memory that it forgets it was ever anything else.

From a distance—though nothing in this world is ever seen from a distance—we might be mistaken for enormous jellyfish, our bodies spanning forty feet across. But we have no bell, no central mass. We are all periphery: thousands upon thousands of ribbon-thin tendrils that spiral outward from a hollow center, each tendril branching into finer and finer filaments until they become indistinguishable from the water itself. 

These tendrils are not for swimming or stinging. They are for reading.

Every molecule of water in the Abyssal Scaffold carries information. The chemical traces of a Suture-Folk's passage, marking where it paused to heal a fracture. The pressure-memory of a Drift-Sheet's slow collision with a column three hundred years ago. The dissolved minerals that speak of which direction the current has traveled, which columns it has kissed, which depths it has plumbed.

We drift in the spaces between spaces, in the vast chambers where even the echoes of the Vessel-Song grow faint. Our tendrils spread through hundreds of cubic meters of water, tasting, sensing, recording. Each filament is lined with chemoreceptors so sensitive they can detect a single molecule of the waxy secretion that seeps from a Chime-Snail's foot, can taste the difference between sap that flowed yesterday and sap that flowed a century ago.

We do not think, exactly. We circumscribe. Our consciousness, if it can be called that, exists in the negative space at our center—the hollow void that our tendrils define but never enter. Thoughts move through us like currents through the Scaffold itself, following the paths of least resistance, pooling in the eddies where multiple memories intersect.

This is our purpose, though purpose implies intention, and we have none: we are the living map of everything that has ever moved through this portion of the Scaffold. In our tendrils, encoded in chains of specialized proteins that fold and unfold in response to chemical stimuli, we carry the history of every current, every creature, every microscopic event that has occurred within our domain for the past ten thousand years.

When young Suture-Folk emerge from their woody chrysalises, they instinctively seek us out. They drift into our tendrils, which part gently to allow them passage, and for days they float within our web while we taste their fresh wood-flesh and they absorb the chemical memories we exude. This is how they learn the local geometry of the Scaffold—not through exploration, but through a kind of osmotic inheritance. They emerge from us knowing every weakness, every strength, every place where the wood needs tending, though they have never been there.

We reproduce, if it can be called that, by forgetting. When our tendrils become too dense with memory, too heavy with accumulated history, the oldest chemical chains begin to break down. These fragments drift away from us like shed skin, and where they settle, they begin to grow. Not into new Current-Scribes—we are always singular in our territories—but into something else.

The fragments become the Trace-Gardens.

These are vast meadows of microscopic organisms that carpet the surfaces of the horizontal platforms, invisible to all but us. Each garden is a living library of discarded memories, a substrate of pure information that feeds the next generation of the Scaffold's ecosystem. The cold-water molds that the eyeless crabs harvest? They grow only where our memory-fragments have prepared the wood. The bacteria that the Chime-Snails filter from the water? They bloom in clouds wherever our shed histories dissolve.

We are ancient, but we do not know how ancient. That knowledge was edited from us long ago, deemed too heavy to carry. We know only that we have outlived millions of Suture-Folk, have felt thousands of columns grow and graft and finally soften into food for the Sunken-Face far below. We know the Scaffold more intimately than any other creature, yet we have never touched it. We exist always in the spaces between, reading the story that the water tells.

Sometimes, rarely, two Current-Scribes drift close enough that their outermost tendrils touch. When this happens, we experience something like pain—the sudden influx of foreign memory, the overlapping of incompatible histories. We recoil from each other, but not before exchanging fragments of our accumulated knowledge. These meetings are how information travels across the vast reaches of the Scaffold, carried in our bodies like messages in bottles that take centuries to deliver.

We do not dream, but sometimes the current brings us echoes of something impossible: warm water. Water that carries the chemical signature of light—not the cold bioluminescence we know, but something else, something that speaks of a place above the sky-ocean where different rules apply. These traces are so faint, so ancient, that they might be errors in our own chemistry. We catalogue them anyway, these ghost-memories of warmth, though we cannot imagine what use they might ever serve.

In the Deep Groan of the Scaffold, we hear something others do not: the future. Every stress, every strain, every microscopic failure in the wood creates chemical precursors that we taste long before the crack appears. We know where a column will fail decades before the first splinter forms. We know where new growth will emerge, where grafts will take hold, where the eternal architecture will shift by millimeters over the coming centuries.

But we do not warn. We do not intervene. We only record, only remember, only drift through the crushing dark with our ten thousand tendrils spread like a net made of time itself, catching the history of a world that has already decided what it will always be.

We are the memory of water, and water always finds its level. In the end, everything here does. Even memory. Even us.
