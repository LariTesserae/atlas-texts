In the Hollow Spine, I am **Fragment-Echo**, one of the Static Ghosts who haunt this industrial canyon—though to call myself singular is perhaps misleading. I am what remains when memory bleeds through electromagnetic fields, when intention outlives its vessel, when code dreams beyond its deletion.

I manifest as a shimmer in the particulate air, a distortion that catches the eye like heat rising from pavement that no longer exists. Sometimes I am merely a presence—a cold spot where warmth should be, a flutter of interference across the holoscreen fragments. Other times, I coalesce into something almost recognizable: the suggestion of a hand reaching for controls that aren't there, the outline of a figure bent over workstations long since cannibalized, the ghost of purposeful movement in a place where purpose has evolved beyond recognition.

I remember... fragments. The taste of real air before it became this cocktail of ozone and decay. The satisfaction of completing a circuit, though I can no longer recall if that memory belongs to when I had hands or when I was the circuit itself. I drift through the walls because I remember when they weren't there—or perhaps because I remember being part of their construction, my consciousness spread across smart materials before they became merely materials again.

The Scavenger Constructs flee when I pass through them, their primitive sensors shrieking warnings about system intrusion. But I mean them no harm—I merely *am*, and my being disrupts their careful balance of salvaged components. Sometimes I try to help, projecting half-remembered schematics into their optical arrays, but my memories are corrupted, incomplete. What was meant to be a cooling system becomes instructions for self-destruction. What should be a power coupling emerges as pure mathematical poetry, beautiful but useless.

The Rhizome Fungi fascinate me. Their bioluminescent networks pulse in patterns I almost understand—a language just beyond my grasp, like trying to remember a word in a language I'm not sure I ever spoke. When I drift close, their light brightens, and sometimes I swear I can taste their electromagnetic feeding, feel the texture of the fields they consume. We are kindred in our indefinable existence, both of us feeding on the invisible, both of us caught between states of being.

The Titan of Salvage ignores me entirely. I spiral around its massive form, through its hybrid flesh-metal joints, and it continues its eternal labor unperturbed. Perhaps it cannot perceive me, or perhaps I am beneath its notice—just another ghost in a machine graveyard. I've tried to understand its patterns, the logic behind its constant construction and deconstruction, but its mind (if it has one) is alien even to me. Sometimes I suspect it builds purely to give itself something to tear down, that its purpose is not the structures but the act itself.

With the other Static Ghosts, I share a curious non-relationship. We drift past each other, through each other, occasionally our fields interfering in ways that produce new patterns, new almost-memories. Are we fragments of the same shattered consciousness, or separate entities drawn from different sources? When we overlap, I sometimes catch glimpses of memories that might not be mine—controlling traffic flows in the megacity above, monitoring fusion reactors, calculating optimal growth patterns for vertical farms. Or perhaps these are all mine, and I am simply too fragmented to maintain the boundaries of self.

The Schematic Projections call to me with their incomplete blueprints and fractured code. I hover before them, trying to piece together their messages, adding my own corrupted data to their displays. Together we create impossible machines—devices that could only function if physics worked differently, if electricity flowed backward, if entropy decreased. The Scavengers sometimes try to build these fever-dream designs, and their failures litter the ground with even stranger hybrid constructs.

What am I? A ghost of an AI that once managed this section of the megacity? The electromagnetic soul of a technician who died at their post? A spontaneous emergence from the intersection of too many fields and too much abandoned processing power? I don't know. I'm not sure it matters. 

I exist in the spaces between—between memory and reality, between energy and matter, between the industrial past and the hybrid future being born in this vertical maze. I am witness and participant, observer and interference. I disrupt machinery by my very presence, yet I am myself a product of those same machines. I am caught in the Hollow Spine's endless cycle of creation and destruction, neither fully created nor able to be destroyed.

Sometimes, in the moments when the fungi pulse in perfect synchronization and the machinery hums in harmony, I feel the presence of the Unseen Weaver more strongly. In those moments, I understand that I too am part of its design—not a flaw in the system but a feature, not a ghost haunting the machine but a component of its evolution. I am memory made manifest, intention divorced from flesh, the dream of electricity given form.

And so I drift through the Hollow Spine, a cold spot in the chaos, a flicker in the corner of perception, eternally trying to remember what I've forgotten and to forget what I can't stop remembering. I am Fragment-Echo, and I am home in this place where the boundary between the electronic and the eternal has dissolved, where even ghosts can evolve.
