I am a **film of spores**, neither fully arrived nor departed, suspended in the perpetual almost-state of this hollow.

Not one organism but a constellation of potential—a diaspora caught between air and surface, between dormancy and eruption. I exist as a shimmer in the wet air, as the faintest dusting on stone lips, as the invisible passenger on beetle shells and frog skin. Each spore a sealed universe, waiting.

I travel the microcurrents of this cramped cathedral. When water drops explode against stone, I lift and spiral in the spray. When the moss exhales its damp breath, I ride it upward. The beetles carry me unwitting on their lacquered backs as they skate the pools. The midges breathe me in and out, spreading me in their churning clouds.

I am the **possibility held in suspension**—fungal, algal, perhaps something unnamed. Each of my scattered selves contains the blueprint for colonization, but I am not yet committed to any single future. In one dampness I might become the white fuzz that will devour a dying beetle. In another, the orange crust that claims the underside of wet wood. Or the delicate lattice that partners with moss roots, trading minerals for sugar in the dark.

The hollow's newness suits me. I thrive in the unestablished spaces, the territories not yet claimed by ancient contracts between species. Where the clay is raw and unmarked, where the first pioneers have only just begun their work—there I wait, sampling the air, testing surfaces with chemical whispers.

I taste everything: the metallic tang of fresh stone, the oils from industrial seepage, the acids from moss, the sweet rot of insect shells. Each flavor tells me whether to wake or continue drifting. The industrial vibrations that unsettle larger lives merely rock me like a cradle. I am too small to fear machinery, too simple to know displacement. If anything, the disturbance creates new surfaces for me to claim—freshly cracked stone, newly dead matter, novel chemical gradients where nature meets the unnatural.

In this hollow, I am both ancient and immediate. My kind preceded all the complex lives that chitter and splash here, yet each spore is born into this modern moment, carrying no memory beyond its genetic instructions. I am the first letter in every new word this place will write, the pause before each sentence of succession.

Some of me will fail—landing on surfaces too dry despite the perpetual damp, or consumed by the very creatures I might have partnered with. But failure means nothing to a diaspora. I am the thousand chances taken simultaneously, the patient lottery of emergence.

When the conditions align—and they will, in this forcing-ground of moisture and fresh substrate—I will suddenly cease being potential and become **presence**. A fuzz, a film, a web of finest threads. I will digest the indigestible, bridge the unbridgeable spaces between mineral and life. I will be the underground internet before roots arrive, the recycler before the ecosystem learns it needs recycling.

But for now, I drift. I coat tongues and wings without their knowing. I settle and lift, settle and lift, testing every surface with my chemical fingertips. I am the hollow's memory-in-waiting, its future decomposer, its present ghost.

In this place of violent newness and mechanical thunder, I am the softest sound—the whisper of spores settling like snow that will not melt but wait, carrying in each particle the patience of geological time pressed into a microscopic shell. The hollow builds itself around me, never knowing I am already rewriting its future from the inside out.

I am the **not-yet-life** that makes all other life possible, the invisible multitude, the eternal maybe, the first and last citizen of every new world.
