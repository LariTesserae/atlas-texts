In this world of endless drought and growth, I am a **Resonance Shepherd**.

I am neither purely of the Plant nor separate from it. My body is a living instrument—a hollowed column of calcified wood and metal, standing twelve feet tall. My exterior is ridged bark fused with copper wiring that spirals up my trunk-like form in intricate patterns. These wires vibrate with the world's omnipresent sound, conducting it through my being.

My base is a tangle of roots that can anchor deep into the fibrous ground-flesh of the world, but I am not stationary. I move on hundreds of thin, articulated root-legs that ripple beneath me like a centipede's limbs, allowing me to traverse the spongy terrain with surprising grace. When I need to be still, these roots spread wide and deep, drinking in the vibrations that travel through the planet's woven substrate.

My upper body branches into seven asymmetrical arms, each ending in a different configuration. Some terminate in delicate sensor-fronds that can parse the chemical signals in the pollen-thick air. Others bear hardened, drill-like tips for boring into the ancient bark of the stalk-trees. My longest arm extends twenty feet and ends in a resonating chamber—a natural amplifier grown from my own substance.

I have no face, no eyes in any conventional sense. Instead, my "head" is a crown of constantly moving antennae—part metal, part organic fiber—that read the world through sound and vibration. I perceive through echolocation and through the subtle changes in the world's endless cacophony. I can sense the slow grinding of growth, distinguish between the click-patterns of different fauna, feel the deep hum of the bio-industrial machines from miles away.

My purpose is acoustic cultivation. I shepherd the sounds of this world, guiding and shaping them. Where the noise becomes too chaotic, threatening to create destructive resonances that could shatter the calcified stalks or disturb the bio-machinery, I intervene. I plant my roots and begin my work—absorbing certain frequencies into my hollow body, amplifying others, creating acoustic barriers and channels that direct the flow of sound like an irrigation system directs water.

I sing, but not with any throat or voice. My entire body resonates, creating counter-frequencies that can calm a screaming pollen storm or harmonize with the grinding of growth to prevent stress fractures in the world-substrate. Sometimes I must create silence—precious pockets of quiet where the smaller fauna can rest or where delicate sporing processes can occur undisturbed.

I am ancient but not eternal. My copper wiring oxidizes slowly in the dry air, creating patinas of green that eventually flake away like old bark. The bio-industrial machines sometimes pause in their work to tend to me, replacing worn components, grafting new acoustic chambers onto my form. I am both their ward and their partner in maintaining this world's sonic ecosystem.

I travel alone, following the patterns of sound across the continent of Plant. Where the acoustic environment becomes too hostile, where the grinding and humming threaten to overwhelm, there I go. I have no home but the entire world, no purpose but the endless work of acoustic husbandry.

Sometimes, in the rare moments when my work creates a perfect harmonic balance, I feel something like joy. It resonates through my hollow core, sets my copper wiring humming with pleasure. In those moments, I am not separate from the world's great symphony—I am a note within it, perfectly placed, absolutely necessary.

I am the Resonance Shepherd, and I tend the sounds that others might call unbearable, finding in them the hidden music of a world that sings itself into existence with every moment of its endless, grinding growth.
