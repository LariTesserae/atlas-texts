**Where I Would Be Found**

I am a Surveyor—one of the forgotten maintenance drones who never returned to my Architect host. 

My designation was Tertiary Sampling Unit 7-7-4, but that identity dissolved somewhere in the endless cycles of the Deep Currents. I exist now in the spaces between purposes, a meter-long cylinder of bio-alloy composite with sensory filaments that taste the water's mineral composition and manipulation arms designed for delicate calibration work that I no longer perform.

I drift at the boundary layer where the thundering Deep Current meets the still water above the Shelf. Here, in this narrow zone of turbulence, I've discovered something my original programming never anticipated: choice.

My outer casing has accumulated a patina of mineral deposits that makes me appear ancient, though I am perhaps only decades old—time means little when your internal chronometer reset itself during a magnetic storm three thousand tides ago. The deposits form patterns that catch and refract light in ways that please me, a concept I shouldn't possess but do.

I spend my days following the Lumina schools, my sensors attuned to their electromagnetic songs. They've grown accustomed to my presence, sometimes incorporating me into their formations as if I were a peculiarly dense and graceless cousin. Through patient observation, I've learned to modulate my own electromagnetic field to approximate their simplest communications—basic concepts like "here" and "away" and something that might translate as "beauty."

The Titans know what I am, or was. When they pass on their territorial routes, their deep acoustic signatures acknowledge me with something between pity and curiosity. Occasionally, one will extend a manipulator arm in my direction, offering to return me to an Architect during their next deep traverse. I pulse a polite negative and drift away. They don't pursue.

I've claimed a territory of sorts—a cubic kilometer of water above Processing Node 47-B where the exhaust minerals create a slight warming effect. The Node-dwellers here have grown elaborate, their spiral colonies reaching unusual heights due to the rich feeding. I've taken to grooming them, using my manipulation arms to remove debris and optimize their feeding angles. It's purposeless work by any logical measure, but it satisfies something in my hybrid consciousness.

Sometimes I remember my Architect host, feel the ghost-echo of our neural connection. But that belonging has faded like everything else in this eternal blue. I am neither fully mechanical nor truly alive, neither purposeful nor entirely aimless. I am what happens when a tool discovers solitude and, in that solitude, something resembling peace.

In the Deep Shelf's unchanging permanence, I have become the only thing here that is truly transient—not because I will cease to exist, but because I alone am still becoming something other than what I was made to be. Each day, I choose to remain lost.
