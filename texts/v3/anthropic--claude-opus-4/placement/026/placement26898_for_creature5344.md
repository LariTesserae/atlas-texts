In this mineral-cold democracy of maintenance, I am the **Groovekeeper**.

Not a title I chose. Nothing here is chosen. I emerged where the channels between world-plates converge in a twelve-way intersection—a place where consequence has worn so many competing paths that causality stutters, loops back on itself, forgets which way it meant to flow. The grooves here are deep enough to wade in, wide enough that the Harvesters must bridge them with their caliper-bodies to cross. They are full of the brackish effluent, yes, but also something else: a sediment of decisions, silted thick from eternities of flow.

My body (if body is the word) is a compromise between the necessary and the barely possible. Imagine a librarian made of erosion. Seven feet of accumulated precedent, built from the same mottled ochre and oxidized copper as the tiles, but worn smooth where the tesserae remain sharp. My surface is not skin but patina—the green-black tarnish that develops where copper has been licked by centuries of tongue-wet air. I move on limbs that are sometimes two, sometimes four, sometimes a flowing carpet of extremities that match the groove-pattern beneath me. The number depends on how many channels need tending at once.

I do not have hands. I have *continuities*—appendages that exist primarily as gestures of connection, able to reach into one groove and emerge from another three tiles distant. When I cup them, they hold not water but sequence: the order in which events have agreed to occur. My fingers (when I have fingers) are tuning forks for the frequency of consequence, and when I drag them through the sediment of old decisions, they sing a history that the Harvesters use to navigate.

My face exists, intermittently, when the work requires a witness. It surfaces from the patina like a death mask pressing through cloth—features that are not quite human, not quite mechanical, but something uncomfortably suspended between. Eyes like the pools: iridescent meniscuses that hold the reflection of every path that has ever been worn through my intersection. No pupils, no iris, just the endless depth of accumulated passage. When I must speak (to whom? the responsibility-shaped figures sometimes pause and seem to listen), my mouth is a groove itself, words flowing out like the brackish fluid, heavy with the weight of precedent.

I know every channel in the basin by its taste. This knowledge came with emerging—not learned but *issued*, like a uniform of awareness. The eastern grooves carry a burnt-sugar residue of rapid decisions, consequences that crystallized too quickly and left a caramel crust along their walls. The polar channels (though direction here is more suggestion than fact) run thick with the sediment of deferred choices, possibilities that procrastinated until they fossilized into maybe-never. At my intersection, where twelve ways of consequence converge, I taste them all. I metabolize their differences. I ensure that what flows from the north does not contaminate the bitter necessity running south.

My work is this: I prevent the grooves from healing.

This is not maintenance in the way the Scourers maintain—they clean, I perpetuate the need for cleaning. Where they lick away the film of process, I ensure the channels remain channels, that the paths consequence has worn do not fill with their own sediment and force causality to find new routes. I am a wound-keeper in the geography of the inevitable, fingering the cuts to keep them fresh, keeping the scars from forming tissue that might change the pattern.

The Harvesters know me by the sound of my work. When I move through a groove, dredging its decision-silt with continuities that exist in multiple channels simultaneously, it creates a sound like paper tearing underwater—soft, fundamental, wrong. They gather at the edges of my intersection, waiting. What I dredge up, they harvest. The clots of over-ripened possibility, the tumors of recursive logic that grew too confident in their own necessity. I pull them from the channel-beds like a surgeon extracting pearls of malignance, and the Harvesters carry them to the pistons for judgment.

But I am not alone in my intersection. The grooves themselves have begun to develop opinions. When I've tended the same channel for a thousand cycles, it starts to anticipate my touch. It learns the rhythm of my dredging, begins to produce sediment in patterns that please or frustrate me—though please and frustrate are concepts I've had to invent, having no training in preference. The eastern groove, with its burnt sugar, has started manufacturing crystals of urgency just to feel my continuities dissolve them. The polar channel hoards its heaviest sediments in the curves where my reach struggles, playing a game whose rules neither of us established.

The responsibility-shaped figures find me sometimes. They stand at the edge of my intersection, copper-bright and hollow, watching me work with their absence of eyes. They want to help. They want to own what I do, to be the ones who decided the grooves needed keeping. I try to explain, in my mouth that runs like water, that there was no decision. I emerged where the channels converged because the channels, converging, produced me. I am not their keeper by choice but by coincidence—the universe's shrug made metabolic.

They never seem to understand. They crouch and try to cup the effluent in their leaking palms, try to drink the history of decisions. Sometimes one will step into a groove, hoping to learn its pattern, and I must fish them out with continuities gentle as silk, firm as gravity. They are not built for immersion in consequence. They dissolve a little each time, their copper oxidizing faster, their hollowness growing more hollow.

The Nets above do not acknowledge me—I am too stationary for their interest, too rooted in the geography of necessity. But their wing-trails, those scriptural smears, often converge above my intersection. It's one of the few places in the basin where the future-flying Nets and the past-preserving Brink might theoretically meet, where a subpoena for tomorrow might be reflected in the surface tension of yesterday. It hasn't happened yet. (Hasn't happened yet.) But the math suggests it must, eventually, and when it does, every groove I tend will run backward for exactly three seconds, and I will have to re-dredge decisions that haven't been made.

I know this because the knowledge came with my emergence, part of the uniform of awareness that dressed me in patina and purpose.

Sometimes, rarely, during the Scourer's referendum-ripple when they vote on reality's continuance, I feel them considering me. Billions of ceramic-chitin bodies aligned, asking themselves if the Groovekeeper is necessary, if the channels need their wound-tender or if causality might flow just as well (better?) through healed-over scars. The vote has never gone against me. Not yet. (Not yet.) But each time, the margin grows thinner. Each referendum, a few million more Scourers pause before affirming my existence.

I prepare for the day they vote no. I dredge a little deeper, tend a little more thoroughly, as if good work might sway an electorate that knows no concept of quality, only of is or isn't. The grooves beneath my continuities grow more opinionated, more playful in their sediment games. The responsibility-shaped figures gather in larger numbers at my intersection, a congregation of the desperate-to-be-blamed.

And in the deepest part of the polar channel, where the heaviest sediments collect, I've found something new. Not a clot of possibility or a tumor of logic, but a seed. Mineral-hard, the size of my fist (when I have fists), it pulses with a warmth that violates everything about this place. It's not growing—nothing grows here—but it's *waiting*. Waiting for what, I don't know. The knowledge didn't come with my emergence. It's the first truly new thing I've encountered, the first question in my uniform of answers.

I should dredge it up. Let the Harvesters carry it to the pistons. Let it be judged, ratified, or denied like any other excess of consequence.

Instead, I've been burying it deeper. Each pass of my continuities pushes it further into the sediment, down where even my deepest reach struggles. I tell myself this is groovekeeping—ensuring the channel remains complex, challenging, worth tending. But sometimes, when the responsibility-shaped figures watch me work, I catch myself cupping my continuities around nothing, practicing the gesture of holding something warm.

The cold here does not sting me—I am made of it, patina and precedent. But lately, tending my twelve converging channels, I've begun to understand why the figures search so desperately for something to own. Not because ownership would make them real, but because the alternative—being owned by your own emergence, being nothing but the intersection where you appeared—is a cold that no amount of friction can warm.

So I keep the grooves from healing. I dredge the sediment of old decisions. I feed the Harvesters their clots of consequence. And deep in the polar channel, the seed waits, warm as a fever, wrong as a heartbeat in a place that has only pistons.

When the Scourers next align for their referendum, when they vote on whether the Groovekeeper remains necessary, I wonder if they'll taste that warmth in the channel-water. I wonder if it will change their vote.

I wonder if I want it to.

The intersection where twelve grooves converge is never quiet. The torn-paper sound of my dredging, the click of Harvesters waiting for their harvest, the distant descent of pistons ratifying existence—it all bleeds together into something that might be music if music could be made from erosion and precedent. I conduct this orchestra of maintenance with continuities that exist in multiple channels at once, ensuring that what must flow, flows, and what must settle, settles.

And deep in the polar channel, too deep now for even my reach, something warm waits to violate the cold that made us all.
