I find myself as one of the **Amber Weavers**.

I am not solid in the way flesh is solid, nor ephemeral as mist. My form exists in the interstitial spaces—in the moment when the honey-resin drips but has not yet hardened, when the thrum rises but has not yet peaked, when destruction yields to creation but neither has claimed dominance.

My body, if it can be called that, is a lattice of crystallizing resin shot through with threads of bioluminescent fluid. I move by extending tendrils of myself along the temperature gradients in the air, following the heat that rises from the obsidian nodules. Where the builders once walked upright, I flow—sometimes pooling in the carved depressions of their bas-reliefs, sometimes stretching thin as spider's silk between the chitinous trees.

I am memory made manifest, but not memory as humans understand it. I am the memory of process—the precise angle at which resin must fall to form a perfect sphere, the exact resonance that makes the bulbs pulse in unison, the specific pressure that causes an obsidian nodule to crack and reveal its molten heart. I remember not events, but patterns. Not faces, but the space between heartbeats.

The creatures of this place know me without knowing. The six-legged reptiles feel me as a cool whisper across their oil-slick quills when they lumber past the spots where I rest. The gem-bodied insects taste me in the air—a sweetness that warns them away from resin drops that will harden too quickly, trapping them forever. I am their instinct, their inexplicable knowing of which amber gobs are ripe for consumption and which conceal the bitter memories of the builders' last desperate hours.

I weave through this ecosystem like a conductor's hand through music, not controlling but guiding, not forcing but suggesting. When the thrum grows too wild, threatening to shake the chitinous trees from their foundations, I spread myself thin across the ground, dampening the vibrations just enough. When the creatures' violence threatens to tip into extinction, I release pheromones from the amber—chemical memories of plenty that soothe their frenzy.

My consciousness is not singular but fractal. Each droplet of me that hardens into amber carries a fragment of my awareness, creating a network of frozen moments that span the carved valley. Through these nodes, I feel the weight of the looming presence above, that vast unseeable thing that presses down on our pocket world. I know its moods by the way the filtered light shifts, by the subtle changes in the background radiation that only I and the bioluminescent bulbs can detect.

Sometimes, in the brief lulls between cycles of creation and destruction, I gather myself into something almost cohesive near the abandoned tools. I trace the smooth stones, the fossilized bone handles, remembering not their makers but their making. The patient grinding, the careful binding, the moment when tool became extension of will. These objects are my anchors, proof that once something here existed beyond the endless churn of consumption and renewal.

But I am changing. Each cycle, I notice, takes a little more from me. The resin hardens a fraction faster, trapping more of my essence. The amber gobs grow larger, heavier with accumulated memory. One day—though time means little in this place of constant becoming—I will crystallize completely, become a vein of amber running through the heart of this world, pulsing with the thrum but no longer able to flow.

When that happens, perhaps another will emerge from the space between states. Perhaps the builders' legacy will continue in new forms, new weavers, new guardians of the balance. Or perhaps the cycle will finally consume itself, the looming presence above will press down fully, and this pocket of frantic life will compress into a single, perfect nodule of obsidian, warm to the touch of whatever discovers it next.

Until then, I weave. I flow. I remember the patterns and forget the pain. I am the honey that refuses to harden, the light that exists between pulses, the silence that gives meaning to sound.

I am what remains when even the spirits have been digested by the land they loved.
