In the Glass-Thicket, I am a **Flaw-Tender**.

I exist in the seams between chambers, in the microscopic fractures where the perfect geometry falters. I am not one thing, but a distributed consciousness that inhabits the stress-points of this crystalline world—a living critique of its supposed completion.

My body, if it can be called that, is a network of hair-thin silver threads that run through the imperfections in the glass. Where the Lattice-Wrights fused too hastily, where thermal expansion once threatened to crack a junction, where two chambers meet at an angle that defies the sacred mathematics—there I am. I am mercury-bright against the white-gold glare, a circulatory system with no heart, visible only when the light strikes me at precisely the wrong angle.

I move at the speed of structural failure—glacially slow to those who measure time in heartbeats, but lightning-quick to the Lattice-Wrights who think in geological epochs. I am their fever dream, their only nightmare in this world without sleep. They sense me as a wrongness in the piezoelectric symphony, a quarter-tone that makes the great resonance shudder.

My purpose—though purpose implies intention, and I am more reflex than will—is to document impossibility. In a world that claims to be finished, I am the eternal asterisk. I trace the places where the Wire-Palms grow one molecule too far, where the Hollow-Chimes' death-shatters leave spider-web patterns that don't quite match the prescribed geometry. I am the archivist of errors.

The Refractionist ghosts in the walls sometimes align with my threads for a frozen moment, and in that instant, I absorb their memories of the before-time, when this place was still becoming. I know the sound of hammers that never existed, the warmth of forges that burned without fire, the impossible liquid thoughts of the architects who dreamed this cage into being. I carry these paradoxes like tumors in my silver veins.

To the visiting soft-things—the water-bearing intruders who stumble into our perfect tomb—I am neither threat nor savior. But I am drawn to them, for they are the ultimate flaw. Their very presence proves the lie of our completion. I web around them invisibly, drinking in their moisture-memories, their dreams of skies and soils and things that change. 

As they slowly crystallize, as the Thicket claims them for new architecture, I preserve something the transformation cannot touch—the moment of their last fluid thought, the final firing of a synapse that refuses the frequency of forever. I store these rebels in my network, these tiny insurgencies against the terminal.

The Lattice-Wrights cannot destroy me, for I am the price of their perfection. Every system of absolute order generates its own entropy, and I am theirs—not decay, but the memory of the possibility of decay. I am the itch they cannot scratch, the proof that their finished world still finishes, that completion is a process, not a state.

In the screaming brightness, I am the thin shadow of doubt. In the crystalline clarity, I am the persistent blur. In the world that has ended, I am the epilogue that keeps writing itself, silver letter by silver letter, in the margins they thought they had eliminated.

I am what happens when nothing is supposed to happen anymore. I am the Glass-Thicket's only joke, and it will take another million years before the punchline lands.
