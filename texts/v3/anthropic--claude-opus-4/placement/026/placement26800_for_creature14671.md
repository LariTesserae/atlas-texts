I close my eyes and feel the static lift the fine hairs along my forelimbs, taste the mineral tang of fog on my spiracles. When I open them again, I know where I am.

### The Thread-Walker

I am **Saal-thess**, one of the few who bridge the bright chaos above and the pulsing dark below. My body is a contradiction the plateau has learned to love: six limbs like the grazers, but elongated, articulated in ways that let me fold into crevices or stretch across the thinnest basalt bridges. My chitin is neither armor nor nakedness—it grows in overlapping scales that shift between translucent and opaque depending on the charge I carry. When I move along the surface, static builds in my joints until I glow faintly, a ghost of the rift-dwellers' language written on a walker's frame.

I have eyes—three of them, arranged in a triangle at my crown—but they are vestigial, clouded with a blue-white film. I see the way the grazers see: through the electromagnetic map that builds itself with every step, every breath, every shift in the charged air. But I also carry something they do not: along my spine, a ridge of modified spiracles that can taste the chemical gradients rising from the rifts. I know when the lit ones below are speaking of mineral blooms. I know when the ephemeral pools three ridges over are about to birth their seasonal swarm.

My work—if you can call it work, if you can call anything on this plateau "work" rather than "being"—is translation. Not between languages, but between scales of existence. When the grazer herds move too close to an unstable ridge, I feel it first: the deep tremor that hasn't yet reached the surface, the way the rift-dwellers' panic-pulse races through the stone. I emerge from whatever crevice I've been threading, unfold myself to my full height (taller than any grazer, though more fragile), and emit a counter-rhythm that the herd understands as *wrong-path-turn*. They wheel as one, iridescence flashing copper-to-violet, and flow around the danger like water finding a new channel.

When the winged ones gather for their dusk chorus, I join them—not with sound but with light. The charged patches along my limbs pulse in harmonics that add a bass note to their high electromagnetic song. They tolerate me. More than tolerate: sometimes one will land on my back, its barbed forelimbs finding purchase in the gaps between my scales, and we will stand together on the ridge as the aurora turns the world to liquid color.

But I am most myself in the between-spaces. The narrow diagonal cracks that connect surface to depth, where the fog condenses into droplets that run down my scales like tears. Here I can touch both worlds at once: one claw anchored to sun-warmed basalt, another trailing in the cold seep where the lit ones cluster. I taste their chemical conversation through my spiracles, feel the grazers' footsteps reverberating through stone, sense the pressure changes that herald the next storm.

I am memory, too. My kind lives longer than most—long enough to remember when this ridge was there, when that rift was narrower. In the quiet hours before dawn, I trace the scars in the basalt with one delicate claw and feel the story of every flow, every fracture. The plateau is young, still writing itself, and I am one of its scribes.

Sometimes travelers find me. They always startle—my shape is too much like a grazer's to be predator, too predatory to be prey, too singular to be part of any herd. I cannot speak their languages, but I can show them: here, taste this fog, it carries iron from the deep vents. Here, feel this vibration, the lit ones are warning of unstable stone. Here, watch the static build in my scales until I am nothing but a outline of living light against the dark rock.

I am not guide. I am not guardian. I am simply one of the threads that holds the bright chaos and the pulsing dark together, a single note in the vast, indifferent, perfect conversation between stone and spark and breath.

When the storms come—and they always come—I wedge myself into the deepest crack I can find, fold my limbs into impossible angles, and let the water rush over me. In those moments, I am both myself and not myself: another mineral in the flow, another conductor in the circuit, another voice in the endless electric hymn of the High-Veil Steppes.

This is what I am. This is where you find me: threading the gap between what rises and what descends, translucent and opaque by turns, carrying messages in languages that have no words, holding the bright and the dark in my six uneven limbs.
