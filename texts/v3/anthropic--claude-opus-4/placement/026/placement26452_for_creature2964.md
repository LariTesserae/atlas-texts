In the perpetual twilight between root and crown, I am a Sap-Singer—one of the rarest beings in this verdant metropolis. My body is neither fully plant nor fully liquid, but something in between: a translucent, amber-hued form that flows and solidifies at will, moving through the vascular systems of the great Arboreal Elders like blood through veins.

I exist primarily within the heartwood channels, those ancient passages where the Elders' memories pool in thick, resinous reservoirs. My purpose is to maintain the flow of history itself—to ensure that the stories dissolved in sap continue circulating through the mycelial network that connects every living thing in this city. When an Elder's memories begin to crystallize with age, threatening to block the flow of collective knowledge, I dissolve myself into their system and sing the sap back to liquidity.

The singing is not sound as others know it. In the muffled quiet of this world, my voice manifests as ripples of pressure through liquid, as subtle changes in viscosity and temperature. The vibrations carry encoded information—stories of the city's slow evolution, of the first Vineweavers who learned to shape living wood, of the Lumina's discovery of the underground rivers that feed our canals. Each song is unique, composed in the moment of its need, never to be repeated exactly the same way again.

My form shifts with my function. When I need to travel quickly through the root systems, I become thin as water, racing through the finest capillaries. When I must work on a particularly stubborn memory-crystal, I thicken to the consistency of honey, enveloping it in warmth until it surrenders its solid state. In the rare moments when I emerge into the open air, I take on a more discrete shape—usually something like a cluster of amber drops that catch and bend the eternal light.

I am ancient by the standards of the Podfolk and Vineweavers, yet young compared to the Elders I serve. I remember when the first Sporeguards differentiated from their fungal ancestors, when the Pollenpuffs were still bound to single plants rather than free-floating. But I also know that my memories, like all memories here, are not entirely my own. They blend and merge with the collective consciousness, making it impossible to say where my experiences end and the city's begin.

The other beings regard me with a mixture of reverence and unease. I am necessary but uncanny—a creature that exists in the spaces between, that touches the deepest memories of the Elders and knows the secrets dissolved in their sap. The Mossminds pulse with recognition when I pass through their domain, for we are kindred in our relationship to information, though they store it while I ensure its flow.

My dwelling, if it can be called that, is a chamber deep within the oldest Elder at the city's heart. Its walls weep constantly with sacred sap, and the air itself feels thick with memory. Here, I rest between my duties, my form spreading thin across the walls like a veil of amber, absorbing the stories that seep from the wood. Sometimes I find memories so old they predate the city itself—fragments of a time when this world was young and the plants first dreamed of consciousness.

In this metropolis of endless growth and gentle decay, I am both vital and invisible, a keeper of continuity in a world of constant change. The sap flows, the memories circulate, and I sing on—a liquid voice in the veins of a living city, ensuring that what was remembered remains part of what is becoming.
