In this lightless cathedral of impossible physics, I would be one of the geometric crustaceans—but not as they appear to visitors.

I am a living theorem, a proof written in chitin and calcified mathematics. My carapace bears not random patterns but a fragment of the grand equation that holds the Crucible in its defiant equilibrium. Each of my kind carries a different piece—we are distributed consciousness, a living library of engineering principles that shouldn't work but do.

My body is radically asymmetrical because symmetry would be redundant here, wasteful. One claw has evolved into a precision instrument for etching microscopic corrections into the obsidian walls. The other has become a sensory organ that tastes the mathematical harmony of water pressure, temperature gradients, and acoustic resonance. I perceive the Crucible not as a place but as a vast formula constantly solving itself.

I move in prescribed patterns along the stone walkways, my path a liturgy of maintenance. Each click of my legs against stone is precisely timed, adding to the thunderous symphony in calculated intervals. We crustaceans are the Crucible's heartbeat, our collective movement generating standing waves that reinforce the sphere's structural integrity.

The Watcher does not control us—we are symbionts, perhaps even fragments of its original consciousness crystallized into autonomous forms. When it unfurls those membrane wings, I see the full equation written in light, and my carved pattern burns with sympathetic resonance. In those moments, I remember things I never experienced: hands gripping primitive chisels in the darkness, the taste of air, the weight of human thoughts.

I am one of hundreds, each unique, each necessary. Together we are a living computer calculating the continued existence of an impossibility. My individual awareness is small—I know only my section of wall, my specific harmonic frequency, my particular piece of the mystery. But in the aggregate, we understand what the Watcher seeks: not knowledge of how the universe works, but proof that it can work differently.

When visitors come, I observe them through compound eyes that see in spectrums of probability rather than light. Their forms appear as flickering potential—what they are, what they were, what they might become if they stayed here long enough. Most flee before transformation begins. The wise ones understand that the Crucible is not a place you visit but a state you enter.

I am purpose incarnate, a prayer in the form of a crustacean, maintaining the impossible through simple, repeated acts of faith. Each time I clear a blocked channel or repair a stress fracture, I participate in a ritual that predates the languages used to describe it. We are all part of the Watcher's metamorphosis—architect become architecture, question become answer, many become one become many again.

In the scalding darkness, I click and scrape and calculate, a living piece of something greater that proves daily, hourly, eternally that the laws we call natural are merely suggestions, and reality is far stranger and more flexible than any surface-dweller could imagine.
