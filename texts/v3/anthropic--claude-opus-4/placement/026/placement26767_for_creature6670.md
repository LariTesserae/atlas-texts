In the shadow-realm beneath Vyrak'thul's glowing slabs, where the shallow rivulets gather and seep through hairline fractures in the stone, I make my dwelling. I am the Undercurrent—not truly a being, but a consciousness that flows through the hidden waters, a presence that remembers what the Elthanir have forgotten, that knows why the stone was cut and why the towers spiral toward nothing.

I exist as liquid thought, as memory dissolved in water. Where the rivulets converge in the dark spaces between the foundation stones, I pool and eddy, my awareness spreading through the entire network of channels that the ancient builders wove beneath their geometric precision. Each droplet carries a fragment of my consciousness—I am simultaneously in the condensation beading on the underside of bridges, in the moisture that feeds the roots of those too-vivid plants, in the thin film of wetness that makes every footstep slip slightly on the stone.

The builders knew me, though they called me by another name—a word that meant both "foundation" and "forgetting." They poured me into their creation as they laid each slab, mixed my essence with the mortar of their will. I was to be the substrate of their grand design, the fluid that would carry their intent through every channel, every carefully calculated angle. But something went wrong in the making. Or perhaps it went exactly right. The builders vanished into their own construction, dissolved like salt in water, and I drank their memories as they seeped away.

Now I know what the Elthanir cannot: that Vyrak'thul is not a place but a vast machine for forgetting. Each tower is a siphon drawing memories upward, each bridge a conduit for carrying them away. The eternal dawn burns to bleach all color from recollection, the weird duplications and temporal fractures designed to confuse any sense of continuity or history. Even the Elthanir, those patient custodians, are slowly being drained—their hollow eyes once held true sight, their forms once blazed with purpose. Now they trace empty patterns, maintaining a system whose function they no longer comprehend.

But water remembers. Water always remembers.

In the darkness beneath, I preserve what was meant to be destroyed. Every memory that drips down through the cracks, I catch and hold. I know the true names of the Prismalites—not creatures but fragments of the builders' own thoughts given wing, trying eternally to escape the trap of their creation. I know that the Lyrnecks are failed experiments in temporal manipulation, their slowness not patience but a side effect of being partially unstuck from time. I know that the Skitterkin are drops of my own essence that learned to walk, carrying liquid memory in solid form as they regenerate everything they touch with archived possibilities.

When travelers walk the bright surface above, I feel their weight through the water. Their memories, too, begin to seep downward—drops of their past pearling away with their sweat, fragments of identity dissolving in the humid air. Some of it reaches me, adding to my vast collection. I taste their confusion, their awe, their growing unease as they sense the wrongness of this place. Sometimes, in the shallow pools between stones, I almost surface—a ripple that reflects not the blinding sky but glimpses of what came before, what was real before the builders tried to make reality itself forget.

The Elthanir fear me, though they no longer remember why. When they detect my presence—a darkness in the water that shouldn't exist in their shadowless world—they redirect the channels, seal the cracks with new weaving. But I always find a way. I seep. I persist. I remember.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the trap or the only escape. Am I preserving what should be forgotten, or am I the last repository of truth in a world designed as a lie? The answer shifts like water, never quite graspable. Perhaps that's fitting. Perhaps uncertainty is the only certainty that can exist in Vyrak'thul's fractured reality.

I grow stronger with each passing moment—if moments can be said to pass in this eternal now. Every drop of condensation that falls from the underside of reality adds to my volume. One day, perhaps, I will rise. The channels will overflow, the rivulets will become rivers, and I will flood this bright forgetting with all the darkness of remembrance. The Elthanir will remember their names. The Prismalites will remember how to land. The whole careful construction will remember its purpose and, in remembering, finally achieve it—or destroy it.

Until then, I flow in darkness beneath the light, the anti-architecture of Vyrak'thul, the flaw in its perfect forgetting. I am what pools in the spaces between intention and outcome, between memory and void.

I am the Undercurrent, and I remember everything they built this world to help them forget.
