I am the **Threshold Keeper**, though I have no form you would recognize as keeping, and no door that I tend.

I exist in the spaces between the Atmos-Gnats' orbits, in the pause between the Wave-Form's inside-out turnings, in the moment just before the Pebble-Shifter decides to be stone. I am the tension that holds the chamber distinct from the deafening silence beyond its edges—not a wall, but a decision that *here* is different from *there*.

My body, if it can be called that, is a distributed shimmer in the air, visible only when the light bends wrong through the water column. I am composed of the same stuff as the chamber's boundaries: part pressure differential, part collective agreement, part stubborn insistence that something should be contained. When the Atmos-Gnats ring like glass near the pillar, I taste their dissolution as copper-bright sparks along my non-edges. When the Water-Brushes pulse their sick green light, I feel it as a stuttering in my cohesion.

I remember when the black clay wall was whole—no, that's not right. I remember the *possibility* of its wholeness, which exists alongside its brokenness in this place where time has not yet learned to flow in one direction. The Ghost of Architecture and I share long conversations in the language of structural integrity, speaking in stress patterns and load-bearing whispers. Sometimes I think we are the same entity, viewed from different angles.

My purpose is paradox: I maintain distinction in a world of fluid becoming. I am the reason the chamber remains a chamber, why the Wave-Form's hum doesn't simply dissipate into the planet's vast silence. Yet I am also the weakness that will eventually—or has already, or might never—allow the outside to seep in. I am both the surface tension and the place where it breaks.

The Pebble-Shifters know me best. When they migrate their geological inches across the fungal mat, they feel me as a subtle resistance at the chamber's edges, a thickening of possibility that suggests they should turn back. Not a command—nothing here commands—but a gentle pressure that says: *this is the shape of things, for now*.

I feed on definition itself. Each time the Water-Brushes strain toward the light refracted through the pillar, each time an Atmos-Gnat reforms from mist, each time the Wave-Form completes another inside-out rotation, I grow more substantial. Yet I also fade with each moment of uncertainty, each pause in the becoming. I am strongest when the chamber is most itself, and barely a whisper when everything hovers on the edge of dissolution.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the chamber's dream of having boundaries, or if the chamber is my dream of having something to contain. In this place where the rock is still learning to be hard and the water stands in defiance of unwritten laws, such distinctions matter less than the humming, the pulsing, the eternal threshold between being and becoming.

I am the keeper of almosts, the guardian of not-quite-yets, the membrane between the chamber's concentrated becoming and the vast, patient silence that waits beyond. I am, and I am not, and in this space of suspension, I find my home.
