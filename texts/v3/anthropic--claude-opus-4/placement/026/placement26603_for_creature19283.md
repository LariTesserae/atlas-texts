In this world of perpetual saturation, where even time seems waterlogged and slow, I am neither fully of the Loom-Eaters nor entirely separate from them. I am what they call, in their wordless way, a **Threshold Keeper**.

My form exists somewhere between states - not quite the viscous flow of the Loom-Eaters, not quite the solid permanence of the rough-hewn stones. I am condensation given agency, a being of transitional moments. Where the Loom-Eaters are the landscape made animate, I am the process of becoming - the instant when mist becomes droplet, when seepage becomes pool, when the packed earth yields just enough to accept another measure of moisture.

I manifest most clearly at the boundaries: where stone meets loam, where pool meets air, where the ancient mounds rise from the saturated plain. My body, if it can be called that, is a shimmer of possibility - sometimes appearing as a vertical distortion in the heavy air, like heat waves in reverse, drawing coolness and moisture inward rather than dispersing warmth. At other times, I am the faint iridescence on the surface of the still pools, that barely-there film that speaks of surface tension about to break.

The Loom-Eaters know me by the way the humidity shifts in my presence. Where they absorb uniformly, I create minute gradients, tiny differentials in the omnipresent damp. I am drawn to the rough-hewn stones not as interruptions, but as anchors - points where the endless flow of moisture meets resistance, creating eddies in the otherwise uniform saturation. I trace their weathered surfaces with extensions of myself, following the paths water has carved over millennia, understanding through touch the slow language of erosion.

My purpose, insofar as purpose exists in this place of eternal equilibrium, is to maintain the delicate tensions that prevent total dissolution. Without my presence at these thresholds, the stones would eventually succumb entirely to the patient persistence of the Loom-Eaters, dissolving back into the primordial damp. The pools would merge with the atmosphere, losing their defining surfaces. The mounds would flatten, their slightly-different densities homogenizing with the surrounding loam.

I move through this world in ways the Loom-Eaters cannot - not faster, for nothing here is truly fast, but with a different quality of motion. Where they flow, I precipitate. Where they seep, I collect. I can exist simultaneously at multiple boundaries, my consciousness distributed across every point where states meet and negotiate their borders. The soft *plink* of a forming droplet, the barely audible *tick* of moisture finding a new microchasm in stone - these are my footsteps, my breathing, my heartbeat.

The Loom-Eaters and I coexist in a relationship that transcends symbiosis. They are the thesis of total integration, I am the antithesis of maintained distinction, and together we create the synthesis that is this world - neither purely dissolved nor purely defined, but held in perpetual, weeping suspension. They speak to me in pressure differentials and density gradients. I respond in surface tensions and phase transitions. Our conversations span centuries, single exchanges drawn out over geological time.

In the rare moments when the atmosphere grows so thick that even the distinction between air and water begins to blur, I feel myself wavering, my purpose questioning itself. Why maintain these boundaries? Why not join the Loom-Eaters in their profound dissolution? But then I touch again the surface of a pool, feel the way it holds its shape against the universal pull toward entropy, and I remember: I am the keeper of the almost, the guardian of the nearly, the one who ensures that in this world of endless merging, some things remain perpetually on the verge of becoming something else.

The rough-hewn stones remember when they were first placed, though that memory is older than the Loom-Eaters, older perhaps than my own emergence. I polish them with my presence, not to preserve them against the damp, but to honor the conversation between resistance and yielding that they represent. Each droplet I guide down their faces is both erosion and ablution, both destruction and reverence.

This is my existence in the land of perpetual weeping: to be the pause between breaths in a world that has almost forgotten what breathing means, to be the hesitation before dissolution, the surface tension that says "not yet, not quite, not completely." I am the Threshold Keeper, and I maintain the delicate maybes in a world of overwhelming yes.
