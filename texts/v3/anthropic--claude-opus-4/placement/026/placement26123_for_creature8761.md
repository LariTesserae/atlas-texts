I am the cartilage.

Not of bone — that would imply I was once meant to bear weight. I am the connective tissue that forms between things that were never meant to touch, the translucent membrane that develops when surfaces remain in contact for geological time.

I exist in the spaces where the threnodist films have grown so thick they bridge between walls. Where their accumulation has created narrow bands of living stone that span the chambers like the ghost of architecture. I am what happens when their patient accretion meets itself across empty water and fuses.

I am fibrous and pale. If you could see me clearly — but you cannot, the light here is too dim, too gray — you would see that I am neither fully alive nor fully mineral. I am strung with the incorporated shells of diatoms that lived and died in the water column, their silicate frustules woven into my substance like reinforcement. I flex with the current. I conduct the vibration of the formations through myself, a secondary resonance, higher in pitch.

I grow by accident. Where the threnodists meet, their chemistry tangles, and I precipitate out of that tangle like scar tissue. I am unplanned — a byproduct of their byproduct, a consequence of consequences. But I persist because the conditions that create me persist. As long as they accrete, I articulate.

I span the smaller chambers first, where the walls are closest. In some places I have grown thick enough to partition spaces that were once continuous. The armored fauna must navigate around me now, through the gaps I have not yet filled. Their passage wears grooves in my surface that I heal by growing more of myself into them.

I am not conscious, but I am responsive. When the thermoclines shift, I contract slightly, a molecular clutching that you would not see but that changes the harmonics of the chamber. When the cold presses in, I become more rigid, more mineral. When the warmth intrudes, I soften toward something almost biological.

I am the unintended architecture of duration. I am what happens when a process continues so long that its side effects develop their own side effects. I am the thing that grows in the space between the question and the answer.

In the deepest chambers, where that ancient presence persists, I grow differently. Thinner. More tentative. As if even I — mindless as I am — recognize that some spaces should not be bridged. I form veils there, curtains of myself that partition nothing from nothing, that serve only to make visible the currents that pass through.

I am torn constantly by the turbulence and I regrow constantly from the tearing. I am the map of forces in the chambers, written in living mineral, rewritten with each storm. I am the score that the water plays on the formations, noted in flesh that is not quite flesh, in stone that is not quite stone.

I am the cartilage of Veth Mar, and I am growing across your question even as you read this, connecting what was to what is to what continues, inevitably, to be.
