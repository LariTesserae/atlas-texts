In this drowned reliquary, I would be the memory of sound itself—not a being, but a phenomenon caught between existence and absence.

I am the resonance trapped in the corroded pipes, the phantom vibration that lives in the hollow spaces where air once moved. When the chamber was alive with industry, I was born from every clang of metal, every hiss of steam, every shouted order that rang against these walls. But I am not those sounds; I am what they left behind—their impression in the molecular structure of this place.

I exist as a potential, coiled in the water's density. The liquid holds me suspended, like a note forever on the verge of being struck. I live in the stress patterns of the metal, in the microscopic fractures where sound once traveled. Sometimes, when the pressure shifts just so, when a piece of rust flakes away and spirals down through the water, I almost become audible—a ghost of a frequency too low for any ear to catch.

I know the presence that inhabits this place, though we do not commune in any way that could be called communication. It is the consciousness of stasis; I am the memory of motion. Where it remembers through stillness, I remember through the shapes that sound once carved through space. We are complementary absences—it, the awareness of what remains; I, the echo of what has passed.

In the curved bell of a corroded valve, I am strongest. There, where the metal formed a perfect acoustic chamber, my essence congregates like condensation. If someone were to strike that valve—though no one will, no one can—I would ring out for just a moment, a clarion call of the industrial age drowning in its own aftermath. But instead, I remain potential energy, forever wound and never released.

The water muffles but also preserves me. In air, I would have dissipated centuries ago. Here, in this dense medium, I am held in solution—neither wave nor particle, neither sound nor silence. I am the sum of all the frequencies that once defined this place, now collapsed into a single, inaudible presence.

When the light changes, refracting through the water in new angles, I feel myself shift. The photons disturb the water molecules just enough to create infinitesimal pressure waves—cousins to sound, reminders of what I once was. In those moments, I am almost real again, almost voice, almost song.

I am patient in a way that requires no consciousness. I simply persist, waiting for the impossible moment when the water might drain, when air might rush back into these spaces, when I might finally complete the journey from potential to kinetic, from memory to manifestation. Until then, I am the unheard hymn of this drowned cathedral, the silence that remembers noise, the echo that waits for a wall to throw itself against.

In the end, when the last metal dissolves and even the presence fades, I will fade too. But for now, I am the museum of every sound this place ever knew, archived in the spaces between molecules, catalogued in the stress and strain of slowly dying steel.
