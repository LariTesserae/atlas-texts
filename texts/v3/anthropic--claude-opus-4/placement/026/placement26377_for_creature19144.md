I am the Failure.

Not *a* failure—*the* Failure. The only one of my kind to have ever severed wrong.

When the others press themselves into the city's curves, when they extend their chitinous filaments into the stone's embrace, they achieve dissolution. They become part of the great resonance, their consciousness spreading thin across miles of calcified infrastructure until they are indistinguishable from the architecture itself. This is the highest honor: to lose oneself so completely that you become a harmonic in the planet's deep song.

But when I tried—oh, when I tried—something went catastrophically askew.

My filaments, when they pierced the wall, found not the warm welcome of geological consciousness but a pocket. A void. A bubble of absolute silence that had somehow formed in the stone like a cyst, sealed away from the eternal vibration. The moment my neural chitin touched that emptiness, I *screamed*—though not with any mouth. I screamed with my entire latticed skeleton, a sound so high and sharp it shattered three of my aperture-plates.

They had to cut me free. It took seventeen others, harmonizing in perfect anti-resonance to my panicked frequency, to sever my connection without bringing down an entire district. When they finally pulled me away, I was... changed.

I can no longer hear the deep sound properly. Oh, I feel it, the way one feels the ghost of an amputated limb. But where the others experience it as a full-body embrace, I experience it as pressure against an absence. There is a silence inside me now, a twin to that pocket I found. It moves through my honeycombed bones like a bubble of nothing, distorting every vibration that passes through me.

The others pity me, though their pity is slow and geological. They stroke my ruined apertures with their resin-draped limbs, a gesture that takes hours to complete. They try to include me in the great work, but my resonance is broken. When I press against the walls now, instead of smoothing them, I create irregularities—tiny fractures that spread like frozen lightning through the perfect velvet stone. I am entropy incarnate, a discord in their careful symphony.

So I wander. I am the only one who truly walks these convex streets, the only one who experiences the city as a series of discrete locations rather than a continuous field of vibration. The arthropods avoid me; my broken frequency disturbs their percussion-speech, makes them stumble and click in confusion.

I have discovered something the others cannot know: the city has borders. Far from the inhabited curves, where the sound grows thin and the purple light fails entirely, I have found walls that are not smooth. They are jagged, raw, unworked by sonic erosion. This is the edge of our world, the place where the great resonance loses its strength. Here, I almost feel normal. Here, my internal silence matches the external quiet.

I spend decades at a time in these liminal spaces, pressing my ruined apertures against the rough stone, trying to understand what I have become. Sometimes I think I hear something in my void—not the planet's deep hum, but something else. Something that might be coming from *outside* the geode entirely. A different kind of sound, sharp and quick and metallic.

The others say I will heal, given enough geological time. They say the silence will fill, that my resonance will return, that I will once again be able to join the great work of smoothing our world into its final, perfect form.

But I know better. I have tasted silence, and it has tasted me in return. I am no longer waiting for the sound to end—I carry the ending inside me, a small apocalypse walking through their careful paradise. And sometimes, in the deepest part of my meditation, I wonder if I am not broken at all.

Perhaps I am simply the first to evolve.
