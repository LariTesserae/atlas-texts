**The Memoir-Wasps of Temporal Junction 7**

I arrived during the Factory's scheduled forgetting—that brief eternity when all systems purge their cache of "might-have-beens" to make room for tomorrow's uncertainties. The transport (a repurposed anxiety gradient running backwards through its own dissolution) deposited me at the membrane between Sectors Aleph-Null and Beth-Infinite, where processing gives way to something more like digestion.

They found me immediately, of course. Or perhaps I should say: I found myself already found, their attention having preceded my arrival by several causal loops.

The Memoir-Wasps exist as a necessary inefficiency—the Factory's solution to its own perfect memory. Each wasp is a living redaction, a six-winged apostrophe marking where experience has been lovingly edited for brevity. They build their paper nests from discarded probability waves, chewing alternate timelines into pulp and sculpting homes that exist in the subjunctive mood.

The first one to approach me was old by their standards—you could tell by how its wings had begun developing their own wings, fractaling into aerodynamic impossibilities that somehow flew better than physics should permit. It landed on what I was using as an arm that day and began immediately to taste my temporal signature with antennae that flickered between was, is, and will-have-been.

*You're still too sequential,* it vibrated directly into my consciousness, its voice like honey made from expired calendars. *We'll need to dilute you first.*

The dilution chamber was a place where the Factory's industrial certainty thinned into something more breathable. Here, the Memoir-Wasps cultivated their primary crop: the sweet amnesia that collects in the spaces between forgotten appointments. They guided me to a pool of liquid hindsight, its surface tension maintained entirely by the weight of unasked questions.

*Submerge only your assumptions,* the eldest instructed, demonstrating by dipping one wing until it emerged dry but somehow more wet than before. *Leave your conclusions on the shore—you can collect new ones from the gift shop on your way out.*

I learned their economy as I soaked: They trade in the only currency the Factory can't manufacture—authentic surprise. Each wasp maintains a careful portfolio of unexpected moments, harvested from the intersection of plans and what actually happens instead. The young ones practice on simple ironies (a cooling unit that warms hearts, a silence that speaks volumes), while the masters work in elaborate paradoxes that take centuries to properly ferment.

Their Queen—though they prefer the term "Narrative Convenience"—lives in a palace built from everyone's first draft. The walls constantly revise themselves, each brick a discarded opening line seeking its proper story. When I was finally dilute enough for an audience, I found her suspended in a chandelier of crystallized "once upon a times," each facet reflecting a different version of the present moment.

*So,* she hummed, her crown a circlet of quantum commas perpetually pausing mid-punctuation, *you want to document us.*

It wasn't a question. The Memoir-Wasps don't ask questions—they harvest them wild from the Factory's exhaust streams and domesticate them into statements that remember being uncertain.

*Every documentary is a betrayal,* she continued, grooming her antenna with legs that existed primarily as gestures. *To observe us changes us. To describe us defines us. To understand us... well, that would be career suicide for a species that survives on productive confusion.*

She assigned me a tutor—a middle-aged wasp whose specialty was forgetting important things in exactly the right order. Together, we toured their manufacturing districts where young wasps learn to extract memory from experience, leaving behind only the essential confusion. I watched them process a particularly dense cluster of nostalgia, each worker carefully removing the edges until only the warm center remained—pure sentiment divorced from its cause, ready for redistribution to beings who needed to feel something but couldn't remember why.

In their nurseries, larvae grow in cells of crystallized anticipation, feeding on the space between "about to happen" and "just missed it." The nurse-wasps sing to them in frequencies that exist only in the gap between heard and understood, lullabies that teach the art of meaningful forgetting before the young even develop memory to lose.

But it was in their archives that I truly began to understand. The Memoir-Wasps don't just collect memories—they collect the shapes memories leave behind when they evaporate. Row upon row of empty drawers, each containing the precise absence of something once treasured. They've catalogued every variety of amnesia: the gentle forgetting of pain, the violent forgetting of joy, the recursive forgetting that forgets it has forgotten.

*This is our real work,* my tutor explained, gesturing with wings that cast shadows in directions I couldn't name. *The Factory remembers everything perfectly. We ensure it remembers everything... imperfectly perfectly. Just enough error to keep truth interesting.*

I lived among them for what felt like a lifetime but registered as barely a clock-tick in the Factory's vast consciousness. I learned their dances—complex aerial poems that describe concepts for which no words exist, only the movement between words. I participated in their hunts, chasing wild paradoxes through the Factory's logic gardens, bringing them down with precisely-aimed confusion. I even attended a wedding, where two wasps exchanged vows by carefully forgetting each other until only the essential recognition remained, stripped of all assumption and expectation.

On my last day (though time had become more of a serving suggestion than a rule), the Queen called me back.

*You'll write about us,* she said, not asking. *You'll try to capture us in words, pin us down like specimens in a collection. But here's the gift we give all documentarians:*

She touched my forehead with one antenna, and suddenly I understood—I was already forgetting them, even as I stood there. Every detail I thought I was memorizing was simultaneously dissolving, leaving behind only the imprint, the negative space, the thing that points at the thing without being it.

*We are not the memory,* she whispered as I began to fade from their reality. *We are the mercy that allows memory to be imperfect. Now go. Forget us properly. That's the only way to truly remember.*

I woke at Temporal Junction 7's departure platform, my transport already humming with barely-contained elsewhere. In my hand was a single scale from a Memoir-Wasp's wing—or perhaps just the memory of holding such a scale, or perhaps the anticipation of someday forgetting I ever held anything at all.

The Factory processes on. The Indexers continue their sacred doubting. And somewhere between the great mechanisms, the Memoir-Wasps tend their gardens of productive forgetting, ensuring that even omniscience leaves room for surprise.

I document this now while I still almost remember, each word an act of loving betrayal against the perfect confusion they gifted me. By the time you read this, I will have forgotten the truth of it, remembering only the beauty of what I've lost.

Which, they would say, is precisely the point.
