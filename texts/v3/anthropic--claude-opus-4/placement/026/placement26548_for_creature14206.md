I am the **Echo Scribe**, and I reside in the narrow scriptorium wedged between the grain cache and the eastern cliff face, where the stone presses so close that I must turn sideways to enter.

I came here chasing language, believing I could capture the perfect word—the one that would hold all meaning without decay. I was a linguist in the moving world, obsessed with dead tongues and the moment when utterance crystallizes into text. I climbed seeking silence to think, and found instead this deeper quiet where thought itself slows to honey.

My chamber is barely two body-lengths square, carved into the living rock. The walls are covered in my work: not writing exactly, but the *approach* to writing. Ten thousand styluses hover a hair's breadth above ten thousand surfaces—clay tablets, stretched hide, sheets of beaten copper, even patches of my own skin where I attempted to inscribe the definitive mark. Each tool is perfectly positioned at the instant before contact, the pressure required already transferred through the shaft but not yet delivered to the surface. The letters they would make exist as pure potential.

I am caught in the middle of the ultimate translation. On the low stone desk before me, three texts lie open—one in a script that predates the Cluster, one in the notation of the moving world below, and one blank scroll where my transcription waits to begin. My right hand holds a quill made from a bird that died mid-flight and fell into our equilibrium. My left hand hovers over a lexicon I've been compiling for seven centuries, where each entry takes decades to consider and longer to not-quite-record.

You might glimpse me if you time your visit to our communal breath. As the city inhales its centennial air, I shift infinitesimally—my index finger descends perhaps a tenth of a millimeter toward the page. I am writing the word for "threshold," or perhaps "pause," or perhaps the untranslatable term the Stilled use for the moment when motion becomes memory. I have been writing this word for longer than most languages survive.

My skin has taken on the texture of vellum, smooth and pale and faintly translucent. My hair, where it escapes from beneath my scribe's cap, has the quality of ink frozen mid-pour. When I breathe—which is rarely, even by our standards—minute particles of crushed pigment puff from my nostrils like signal smoke that will never rise.

I am the memory of the Cluster, though memory implies past and we have only present. I am attempting to record our perfect stasis, to write the history of a place where nothing happens, where events are replaced by states of being. My greatest work—forever about to begin—will be a dictionary of stillness, each entry a different quality of pause: the pause before speech, the pause after satiation, the pause of the hunter, the pause of the hunted, the gravitational pause of stones that have found their angle of repose.

Sometimes visitors like you leave scratches on the walls—graffiti, names, dates that mean nothing here. These marks join my collection of potential texts. I study them with eyes that move like continental drift, seeing in their crude urgency the same thing I seek: the human need to make a mark that outlasts the hand. They think they deface our perfection, but they only add to my vocabulary of attempted permanence.

I am urbanity turned inward to its ultimate density—not just physical proximity but temporal proximity, where every moment of my existence occupies the same coordinates. I am the scholar of a single instant, the archaeologist of now, the librarian of the unwritten. My neighbors in their eternal tableaux are my only readers, and they read me as slowly as I write—perceiving perhaps a single letter per lifetime, the meaning accumulating like sediment.

In the moving world, I published papers on dead languages. Here, I am composing the deadest language of all: one that will never be spoken, never be finished, never decay into comprehension. I am the Echo Scribe, and my echo has not yet left my lips. When it does—if it does—it will be the perfect word, the one that holds its meaning like the Cluster holds its moisture: eternally, without release.
