In the amber-lit galleries where the Luminifers tower like frozen waterfalls of light, I am one of the **Harmonics**.

We are neither seen nor unseen—we exist in the spaces between the visible spectrum, in frequencies that the glass-spiders cannot refract and the Siphons cannot sense through their golden crown-lashes. Our bodies are compositions of standing waves, held in place by the perfect acoustic deadness of this world. Where sound goes to die in the dense green walls, we are born.

I manifest as a shimmer in the air, like heat-haze given consciousness. My form shifts between states—sometimes I am a ribbon of distortion that winds around the Luminifer columns, drinking in the four-hour pulse of their amber hearts. Other times I condense into something almost tangible: a figure of compressed silence that stands seven feet tall, with edges that blur and reform like smoke underwater. When the light catches me at the right angle, observers might glimpse an opalescent skeleton of pure resonance—ribs of subsonic hums, a skull of ultrasonic whistles, fingerbones that click at frequencies only the crystallizing plant cells can hear.

We Harmonics are the memory-keepers of sound in this soundless place. Every rare *clink* of expanding cell walls, every *plop* of condensation—these do not simply vanish into the green. We catch them. Inside our wave-form bodies, we carry libraries of acoustic events, perfectly preserved. The sound of the first Siphon's glide across virgin root-highway. The crack of the first Prism's birth from its amber chrysalis. The deep, geological groan of the mountain settling around this pocket of eternal stasis—a sound that took forty years to complete and that I hold, whole and perfect, in the cavity where others keep their hearts.

My dwelling is nowhere and everywhere. I exist most fully in the interference patterns between multiple Luminifers—those rare spots where their light-fields overlap and create moiré patterns of brilliance and shadow that flicker in and out of existence. In these spaces, I can solidify enough to perform my work.

For I am not merely an archivist. I am a tuner.

The basin maintains its perfect equilibrium through vibration. Every root thrums at a specific frequency. Every drop of condensation falls at a precise interval. The Siphons' slow glide creates a subsonic drone that keeps the moss velvet-soft. The Prisms' clicking maintains a rhythm that prevents the formation of crystals in the amber sap. And when something threatens to slip out of tune—when a root grows a fraction too fast, when a Luminifer's pulse begins to drift from its four-hour cycle—I intervene.

I wrap myself around the aberration like a blanket of corrective silence. From my collection of preserved sounds, I release the exact counter-frequency needed to restore the harmony. Sometimes it is the memory of ancient rain (from before the vault was sealed). Sometimes it is the echo of a Siphon's contented sigh as it settles into its century-long shaping trance. Always, it works. The system returns to its perfect pitch.

The other beings do not see me, but they feel my work. A Siphon will pause in its glacial journey and raise its tendrils, sensing that something has shifted back into place. A Prism will adjust its angle by a fraction of a degree, responding to a resonance it cannot name. Even the microscopic Acolytes in their amber columns orient themselves according to the sonic patterns I maintain—their star-shaped bodies pointing like compass needles toward the sources of the deepest, most fundamental tones.

I know things the others do not. In my collection of sounds, I carry the voice of the original architect—the being who first sang this vault into existence, whose final note still reverberates in the space between the molecules of humid air. I know that the basin is not eternal, but rather one movement in a vast composition. The stasis is not death but a fermata—a hold that will last exactly as long as it needs to before the next movement begins.

Sometimes, in the moments between the Luminifers' pulse, I release a single sound from my collection—just to hear it exist again. The splash of the first water touching the first root. The whistle of wind from a world above that no longer remembers this place exists. The sounds bloom in the thick air like flowers of pure vibration, and for an instant, the entire basin rings like a struck bell.

Then the green silence swallows the sound, and I swallow the silence, and the cycle continues.

I am the pause between heartbeats. I am the held breath made manifest. I am the reason why, in a world of perfect stasis, time has not stopped but merely learned to circle back on itself, like a song that ends where it began.
