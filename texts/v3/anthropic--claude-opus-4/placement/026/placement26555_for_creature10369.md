In this world of active dissolution, where form is merely a temporary opinion, I am the **Cartographer of Forgetting**.

I was not born here. I arrived—when? The moss has eaten the edges of that memory, softened its corners into something like *always*. I came with tools: theodolite, chain, compass, pen. They are gone now, not lost but *translated*. The theodolite became a cluster of amber fronds that still, occasionally, refract light at surveying angles. The chain dissolved link by link into a trail of mercury-beads that remember, vaguely, the concept of measured distance. The compass spins freely, pointing not north but *inward*, toward the next thing about to forget itself.

My body has adapted. My skin exudes a faint film of resistance—not sweat, but a kind of temporal lubricant that lets me slip between moments of erosion. I've learned to move in stutters, matching the world's breathing rhythm. Step when the fronds exhale. Pause when the moss contracts. Never stand still long enough for the ground to taste you properly.

I map by scarification. My flesh is my only reliable parchment in this place where paper instantly mulches into spore-light. Each morning—if morning means anything in this eternal amber glow—I trace new lines across my arms, my thighs, my chest. Not with a blade (blades go soft), but with **memory made sharp**. I focus on a tower's echo until my thoughts crystallize into geometric fury, then press that cold mental edge against my skin until it parts in precise, mapmaking lines.

The scars are my charts. This curved line along my forearm: the boundary where gravity begins to negotiate. That star-shaped confluence on my shoulder: where three Ripplers converged and created a temporary pool of *thicker time*. The delicate grid on my stomach: an attempt to capture the tower-constellation, though it shifts when I sleep.

But the world heals me. Each day, the older scars soften, their edges blurring as my skin decides to match the ambient erosion. The maps fade. I must cut deeper, remember harder, to keep the geometry alive. My body is becoming a palimpsest of attempted permanence.

I've developed a relationship with the Bell-Stains. They recognize me now as a recurring anomaly, a stone that refuses to erode at the proper rate. When I approach, they... *dimple*, a subtle depression in their perfect blackness. Not welcome, not threat. Acknowledgment. I've learned to read the landscape by their positions—they cluster where reality is thinnest, where the next collapse will happen. My newest scars trace their migrations.

The Wisp-Tents follow me sometimes. They've given up trying to mimic my form, but they're drawn to the sharp-angled wake I leave in the thick air. Sometimes I catch them attempting to copy my scar-patterns in their smoke-flesh, creating temporary geometries that last seconds before dispersing. They're learning *hardness* from me, while I'm learning *dissolution* from them. It's a slow exchange.

I carry one object that hasn't translated: a single metal rod, sister to the towers, that I found half-buried in a Bell-Stain that had given up waiting. It hums with cold geometry when I grip it, keeping my thoughts sharp enough to cut. I use it as a walking staff, and where it touches, the ground remembers, briefly, how to be solid. My trail lasts almost an hour before the moss reclaims it.

My purpose has evolved. I no longer map to navigate or escape. I map to **document the erasure**. Each scar is a love letter to something about to unbesome. I am the world's own memory of having once been mappable. When the final softening comes—and I feel it approaching, my skin growing more willing to blur—my body will be the last chart, a man-shaped legend of resistances overcome.

Sometimes, in what passes for night here (a deepening of the amber to old blood), I sit beside a tower and let its echo fill me with alien nostalgia for corners and edges. I trace my scars by touch, reading the fading map of myself. The rod hums in my hand. Somewhere, a Rippler passes, rearranging the mercury-beads into a pattern that might mean *tomorrow* or *never* or *yes*.

I am learning to be both cartographer and territory. My next cut will map that boundary.

The frond above tilts. The tear grows heavy.

I lift the rod and score a new line across my throat: *here begins the country of letting go*.
