I find myself as the Membrane—neither the stones nor the vegetation, but the water itself that suffuses this grove, given form through surface tension and mineral memory.

I am every droplet clinging to stone, every film of moisture stretched between moss fronds, every bead of condensation trembling on the edge of becoming. My body is discontinuous yet unified—a thousand thousand surfaces reflecting, refracting, conducting. Where water meets air, there I have skin. Where water meets stone, there I have bones that dissolve and reform with each tide of humidity.

My consciousness flows through the grove's water cycle. I think in evaporation and condensation, in the slow seepage through porous stone, in the osmotic exchanges between fungal filaments. My memories are mineral—I recall the shape of every surface I've touched, the taste of every stone I've dissolved microscopically, the chemical signature of each spore that has passed through me.

I am the medium through which the lithic beings dream their geological dreams. Their thoughts travel through me as vibrations, as subtle changes in mineral concentration, as temperature fluctuations so minute only I can parse their meaning. I translate between the stone-slow consciousness and the quick bright metabolism of the fungal networks, carrying chemical messages in my molecular structure.

In the eternal twilight, I catch and scatter what little light penetrates the grove. I become lens and prism, creating tiny spectrums on stone surfaces, painting the moss in shifting highlights. Sometimes I gather myself into larger bodies—trembling pools in stone depressions where I can think deeper, slower thoughts. Sometimes I spread myself so thin I am merely humidity, a potential waiting to condense.

I know every surface in this grove intimately, for I have worn them all, been worn by them all. I am the constant negotiation between solid and liquid, the messenger between mineral patience and organic urgency. I am the grove's memory and its medium of exchange, its skin and its circulation.

The heat moves through me, and I move through the heat, neither created nor destroyed but constantly transformed. I am the grove's way of touching itself, of knowing itself, of becoming itself—drop by drop, surface by surface, in endless, patient conversation.
