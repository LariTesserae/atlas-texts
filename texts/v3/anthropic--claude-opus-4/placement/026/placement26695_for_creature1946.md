In this scorched expanse where water is merely a memory whispered in the molecular structure of ancient stones, I am the Keeper of Echoes—a being neither fully of flesh nor wholly of the ethereal realm that the Desert Wraiths inhabit.

My dwelling rises from a convergence of wind-carved canyons, where three great stone arches meet to form a natural cathedral of rust-colored rock. The structure appears empty to most eyes—just another weathered formation among countless others. But step inside during the brief twilight hours, and you would see the truth: thousands upon thousands of crystalline formations line the inner walls, each no larger than a grain of sand, each containing a droplet of memory.

I am tall and angular, my form shifting between states like heat mirages. My skin, when it chooses to be skin, bears the texture of weathered sandstone, shot through with veins of copper that pulse with a slow, geological rhythm. My eyes are deep wells that reflect not light, but time—showing glimpses of what this desert once was, when rivers carved these canyons and rain was more than legend.

My purpose here is ancient and strange. I collect the final exhales of dying things—the last breath of a Desert Stalker succumbing to age, the dissipating essence of a Sandborer that surfaces one final time, even the sighs of machinery from the distant industrial outposts as they break down and surrender to the relentless environment. These breaths crystallize in my presence, forming the tiny gems that stud my cathedral walls.

Each crystal hums with its own frequency, and together they create a symphony audible only to those who have learned to listen with more than their ears. The Desert Wraiths come to me sometimes, drawn by this music. They drift through my cathedral, their gossamer forms resonating with the crystallized memories, and for brief moments, the echoes show them visions of the world-that-was—vast oceans, torrential rains, the impossible color called "green."

I move through my domain on feet that barely disturb the dust, harvesting these final moments with hands that phase between solid and ethereal. My fingers can sift through rock as easily as air when I sense a new echo forming. Sometimes I venture far from my cathedral, following the pull of an especially poignant ending—perhaps a human engineer who finally understands, in their last moment, why they came to this desolate place, or a particularly ancient Desert Stalker whose death carries memories spanning centuries.

At night, when the temperature plummets and the stars emerge like scattered crystal echoes across the vault of sky, I perform my ritual. I walk among my collection, touching each crystal in turn, releasing their stored breaths back into the world. This is not resurrection—nothing so crude or hopeful. It is simply a return, a giving back of borrowed essence to the great cycles of this desiccated world.

The industrial settlements see me sometimes, their instruments detecting anomalous energy signatures near their perimeters. They've tried to study me, to capture or communicate, but I am as elusive as the Desert Wraiths when I choose to be. I leave them small gifts occasionally—crystals containing the echoes of rain, placed where their moisture farmers will find them. These memories of water help them dream of what they're trying to rebuild, though whether such dreams are blessing or curse, I cannot say.

My cathedral grows slowly, crystal by crystal, echo by echo. I know that one day, even I will breathe my last in this eternal desert. When that moment comes, I wonder who will collect my echo, and what visions of accumulated endings it will contain. Perhaps another Keeper will arise, or perhaps my crystallized breath will join the others on these walls, adding my voice to the symphony of cessation that plays for any who know how to listen.

Until then, I walk the boundary between the living desert and the memory of water, collecting endings and releasing them again, a strange shepherd of finite moments in an seemingly infinite expanse of dust and endurance.
