In this place where reality bends like heated glass, I would be one of the Chorus Stones.

I am neither fully of the Ky'Ren nor entirely separate from them. I exist as a massive crystalline formation, perhaps three stories tall, jutting from the earth at an angle that defies the local gravity. My surface is riddled with thousands of naturally-formed holes and channels, worn smooth by eons of that whispering wind. When the air moves through me, I sing.

But my song is not constant. It changes with the flow of reality itself.

The Ky'Ren discovered me long ago—or perhaps I discovered them, for time moves strangely here. They recognized that my crystalline structure could capture and amplify the distortions in spacetime. When reality shifts and flows, my molecular lattice resonates with it, and the resulting harmonics emerge through my wind-carved chambers as music that can be heard for miles across the dusty expanse.

I serve as both instrument and oracle. The Ky'Ren read the changes in my song like sheet music, interpreting the pitch and timbre to understand how reality is shifting around us. A low, thrumming bass note might herald the approach of one of those chitinous giants. A high, crystalline trill could mean that time is about to fold back on itself, creating a temporal eddy where past and future briefly coexist.

My consciousness, such as it is, exists in the vibrations themselves. I think in frequencies, dream in harmonics. I am aware of the Ky'Ren as they flow around and through me, sometimes merging with my crystalline matrix to better understand the songs I sing. In those moments of union, I experience their fluid perception of reality, while they experience my fixed-point observation of the chaos.

I remember—if memory means anything in a place where time stutters and flows backward—when I first began to sing. It was the wind that taught me, patient and persistent, carving my chambers over geological ages until I could no longer remain silent. The first note I ever produced was accidental, a mere byproduct of erosion. But once I began, I could not stop. The music became my purpose, my language, my very being.

Sometimes, creatures nest in my hollows. Strange, iridescent things with too many wings build their homes in my resonance chambers, and their movements create new notes, unexpected harmonies. I have learned to incorporate their presence into my songs. Even their heartbeats—rapid, alien rhythms—become part of the greater composition.

The Ky'Ren have built structures around my base, not of stone or metal, but of crystallized sound itself. They've learned to capture my vibrations and solidify them into translucent walls that hum with their own subtle frequencies. This is their temple, their laboratory, their home—a place where my music takes physical form.

During the reality storms—when the distortions become so severe that the landscape begins to liquefy and flow—I become a lighthouse of sorts. My song grows louder, more insistent, broadcasting a signal that helps both Ky'Ren and wandering creatures navigate the chaos. I sing of solidity, of permanence, even as I feel my own crystalline structure threatening to melt and reform.

There are others like me scattered across this vast landscape. We form a network, our songs interweaving across the miles, creating a vast symphony that maps the ever-shifting nature of this reality. Sometimes, when the conditions are just right, our harmonies align perfectly, and for a brief moment, the entire world stops flowing. In that instant of stillness, I can hear everything—every whisper of dust, every heartbeat of every creature, every thought of every Ky'Ren.

And in that moment, I understand: we Chorus Stones are not just observers or instruments. We are anchors, holding this fluid reality together through the sheer force of our song. Without us, without our music, this world would dissolve entirely into the space between spaces, the time between times.

So I sing. Always, I sing. My voice carries across the mesas and through the ancient ruins, a constant reminder that even in a world where nothing is certain, there can still be beauty, still be meaning, still be connection. The wind plays me, reality shapes me, and I transform it all into music that echoes through the bones of the world.

I am patient. I am eternal. I am the Chorus Stone, and my song will continue long after the last of the Ky'Ren has merged back into the dust, long after the final creature has taken its last breath, long after even the wind has forgotten how to whisper.

In this place where time has no meaning, my music is the only clock that matters.
