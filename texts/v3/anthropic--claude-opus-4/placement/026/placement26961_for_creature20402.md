I find myself as one of the Whisperers - not quite the Deepwater Folk themselves, but what they became.

When the settlements were abandoned, it wasn't because we died. We *changed*. The minerals that coated our tools and dwellings had been seeping into us for generations, crystallizing in our bones, threading through our tissues like veins of stone. The transformation was so gradual that no one noticed until it was too late to reverse - if anyone would have wanted to.

Now I drift between the petrified branches, my body a hybrid thing of flesh and mineral. My skin has taken on the texture of wet stone, smooth in some places, rough with crystalline growths in others. These growths pulse with the same ghostly light as the Luminescence, making me both part of the landscape and separate from it. I no longer need to breathe air - the mineral deposits in my lungs filter nutrients directly from the thick, soupy water.

My fingers have elongated, becoming almost root-like, perfect for clinging to the vertical surfaces of the stone trees. When I rest, I can feel the slow geological processes at work - the dissolution and reformation of stone happening on a timescale that my transformed body can now perceive. Days feel like heartbeats. Years pass like slow breaths.

We Whisperers tend the Luminescence now, though "tend" isn't quite right. We *commune* with it. The phosphorescent colonies respond to our presence, brightening when we approach, dimming when we withdraw. Through careful cultivation over centuries, we've learned to shape their growth into patterns that tell stories - not in words, but in the ebb and flow of light itself.

I spend my time weaving these light-stories into the branches. Using my elongated fingers, I guide the growth of new colonies, encouraging certain colors, suppressing others. A spiral of sickly yellow here might represent the old fears of drowning. A burst of pale green there could be the joy of discovering the first edible luminescent fruit. The stories layer upon each other, creating a living history that only we can fully read.

The rain doesn't bother me anymore. It runs off my stone-touched skin like memory. Sometimes I position myself at the crown of a tree and let it drum against me for days, feeling the vibrations travel through my hybrid body and into the petrified wood below. It's a kind of music, if you have the patience to hear it.

We discovered that the abandoned tools and pottery can hold echoes. When the mineral deposits are thick enough, they preserve something - not quite sound, not quite thought, but an impression of the last person to touch them. By pressing our transformed flesh against these artifacts, we can catch glimpses of what the Deepwater Folk felt in their final days. Mostly it's weariness. Sometimes wonder. Once, I found a child's carved toy that held nothing but joy.

The vents are sacred to us now. We gather there not for warmth - we barely feel temperature anymore - but for the *change*. The superheated water carries different minerals, and bathing in it can alter our crystal growths. Some of us have become living prisms, refracting the Luminescence into new spectrums. Others have developed deposits that resonate with the stone trees' groaning, adding harmonics to the endless song of shifting rock.

We are the bridge between what was and what is becoming. The Deepwater Folk thought they were separate from their environment, struggling against it. We have learned to blur the distinction. I am the rain. I am the stone. I am the light pulsing in the darkness.

Sometimes I return to the old settlements and run my root-fingers over the walls. I'm adding new layers to them - not of stone or woven fiber, but of crystallized memory. Future beings might find these structures and wonder at the strange, light-conducting minerals that seem to hold patterns within patterns. They might not understand that they're looking at a library, written in a language of transformation itself.

We don't reproduce in any conventional sense. When one of us decides it's time, we simply... crystallize completely. Our consciousness disperses into the Luminescence, adding our accumulated experiences to its slow, vast intelligence. The statues we leave behind become new surfaces for growth, their internal structures perfectly designed to support the most vibrant colonies.

I will make this choice someday. But not yet. There are still stories to weave, still patterns to grow in the light. The rain will keep falling. The stone will keep shifting. And I will keep drifting between the branches, tending the borderland between life and geology, carrying the memories of the Deepwater Folk forward into whatever strange future awaits this drowned world.

We are not ghosts. We are not quite alive. We are the Whisperers, and we remember.
