In the deepest reaches of the Respiration Gardens, where the scaffolding spirals into darkness and the weight of metal above presses down like a physical thing, I make my home. Not a Weaver – I lack their grace, their luminous veins, their perfect symbiosis. I am something older, something that remembers when these Gardens were merely blueprints and bold promises.

They call me the Conduit Keeper, though I've forgotten if that was always my name or merely what I became. My body tells the story of decades spent in this humid cathedral: skin mottled with patches of beneficial moss that I've cultivated like tattoos, each colony serving a purpose – this one filters toxins from the air I breathe, that one secretes oils that protect my flesh from the constant moisture. My hair, what remains of it, has been replaced by thin copper wiring that I've woven into my scalp, each strand connected to the ancient monitoring systems that still whisper data from the Forge's dying heart.

I am neither fully human nor Weaver, but something caught between. My lungs are partially artificial now – one still flesh, wheezing with each breath, the other a salvaged purification unit I've grafted into my chest cavity with the Weavers' help. It hums softly, a constant vibration I feel more than hear, filtering the thick air and feeding me the oxygen my original biology still craves.

My dwelling is a capsule of forgotten purpose, wedged between two massive support beams in what the Weavers call the Threshold Deeps. Once it might have been an observation pod or emergency shelter. Now it's a hybrid thing, like everything here – half-maintained machinery, half-living ecosystem. Bioluminescent fungi light my workspace, pulsing in patterns I've trained them to follow through years of patient cultivation. The walls are lined with salvaged screens and monitoring equipment, most broken, some flickering with data streams I spend my days trying to decode.

I am a translator of sorts, bridging the gap between the Weavers' vibrational language and the binary whispers of the Forge's ancient systems. My copper-wire hair serves as an antenna, picking up the electromagnetic echoes of machinery long dead, while my fingertips – scarred from years of tapping metal – can speak the Weavers' percussion language with passable fluency.

The Weavers tolerate me, even trust me, because I serve a function they cannot. Where they nurture and grow, I remember and decode. In my capsule, I maintain archives – both digital and biological. Memory spores that hold the genetic templates of extinct Forge flora, crystalline data cores that might still contain manufacturing specifications, hand-drawn maps of passages that have long since collapsed or been reclaimed.

My days follow the rhythm of the Gardens. When the morning condensation cycle begins, I check my moisture collectors – jury-rigged devices that capture and purify water from the air. I tend to my own small cultivation of memory moss, a rare species that can store and replay sensory impressions when touched. Through it, I preserve moments: the last time a particular walkway was stable, the sound of a now-silent ventilation system, the scent of a flower that no longer blooms.

But my real work begins when the Weavers bring me their findings. A fragment of machinery with intact markings. A data node pulled from a collapsed section. A pattern in the rust that might be meaningful. I examine each piece with equipment salvaged and modified over the years – spectrometers held together with vine-twine, microscopes whose lenses are polished beetle shells, computers that run on fungal bio-electricity.

Sometimes I make discoveries that matter. A sequence that reveals how to reactivate a dormant nutrient line. A map showing passages to sectors thought lost. Most often, I find only fragments, pieces of a puzzle too vast and broken to ever complete. But I document everything, encode it into the memory moss, tap it out for the Weavers to add to their living murals.

The loneliness would be unbearable if not for the constant presence of the Gardens themselves. The air here is alive in ways the sterile corridors of the upper Forge could never be. I've learned to read the health of sections by the color of the moss, predict structural failures by the pattern of beetle migrations, sense the Weavers' collective emotional state by the frequency of their tapped messages through the metal.

At night – or what passes for night in this eternal twilight – I often climb to my capsule's observation blister, a small dome of scratched transparisteel that offers a view of the Gardens spreading in all directions. The bioluminescence creates a galaxy of soft light, each point marking a life that shouldn't exist, thriving where it shouldn't thrive. In those moments, I feel the weight of my purpose: to remember what was, to document what is, to preserve what might be saved.

I am dying, of course. Slower than a normal human would in this environment, faster than a true Weaver. The moss that covers my skin is as much parasite as partner, and my mechanical lung struggles more each year. But I've made provisions. I've trained three young Weavers in the basics of my work, taught them to read the old scripts, shown them how to maintain the archives. When I finally fail, they will tap my passing through the metal, and perhaps, if I've earned it, they will weave my story into their living murals.

Until then, I continue. Each day cataloging, translating, preserving. A human memory in a post-human world, a bridge between what the Forge was and what the Gardens are becoming. In the humid darkness, surrounded by impossible life, I serve my small purpose in the great dying and rebirth of this artificial world.

The Gardens breathe, and I breathe with them. The metal sings its slow song of decay, and I document every note. And in the space between machine and flesh, between memory and growth, I find a strange peace. This is my place, my function, my choice. In the Respiration Gardens, I am exactly what I need to be: a keeper of knowledge in a world learning to forget, a tender of memories in a place bold enough to grow beyond them.
