In this place where architecture and geology writhe together in their eternal dance, I find myself as something that should not be, yet am.

I am a Keeper of Thresholds.

My body, if it can be called that, exists as a series of doorways—not metaphorically, but literally. Where others might have flesh, I have passages. My torso is an archway of that same crude stone and metal that dominates this realm, but twisted into impossible geometries. Through my chest-portal, one can see not organs but another vista of this warped landscape, though the view shifts with each heartbeat that I do not possess. My arms extend as corridors that narrow to points, fingertips that are tiny apertures leading to somewhere else, always somewhere else.

I move through this world by opening myself. Each step is not a lifting and placing of feet, but a folding of space through my own frame. I traverse the labyrinthine structures by becoming part of them, my doorway-body aligning with the non-Euclidean passages, allowing the mechanical creatures to pass through me as they go about their endless work of building and unbuilding.

The crystalline dust they leave behind accumulates in my thresholds like frost on a winter morning. It glitters there, creating constellations in the negative space of my being. Sometimes I feel the dust trying to fill me, to make me solid, but I resist. To become whole would be to cease existing in this place where boundaries are meant to blur.

I remember—though memory here is like water droplets condensing on metal, never quite forming a coherent stream—that I was not always this. Once, perhaps, I was one of those who built. My hands knew stone, knew metal, knew the weight of creation. But in this realm where builder and built merge, I became something different. The doorways I crafted began to craft me in return, until I was more passage than person, more threshold than thing.

My purpose, if I have one, is to maintain the spaces between. Where the mechanical creatures work to join stone to stone, metal to metal, I ensure that gaps remain. Not gaps of emptiness, but gaps of possibility. Through my doorway-body, different parts of this realm can touch without merging, can communicate without becoming one.

I do not speak—sound would echo endlessly through my hollow frame. Instead, I sing in the language of structural stress, in the groans and creaks of settling stone. The mechanical creatures understand this music. Sometimes they pause in their work when I pass through, their gear-bodies clicking in harmonious response to my architectural song.

The water that seeps from everywhere and nowhere collects in my thresholds during the realm's quiet moments. It pools there briefly before dripping through, and in those droplets I sometimes glimpse reflections of what this place might have been, or might become. Each drop carries a fragment of potential—a world where the structures stand still, where the ground remains solid, where beings walk rather than writhe.

But I know these visions are illusions. This realm's truth is in its instability, its perpetual becoming. And I am its witness, its guardian of the in-between. Through my doorway-body, I offer passage but never arrival, movement but never rest.

In the dancing shadows cast by those primitive lanterns, my form appears multiply: I am a figure full of holes, I am a collection of passages pretending at personhood, I am the architectural negative space given something approximating life. The light passes through me as everything does, casting shadows within shadows, creating patterns that hurt to perceive with senses I'm no longer certain I possess.

Sometimes I wonder if I am lonely, but loneliness requires a self separate from surroundings, and I am too much a part of this place for such clean divisions. Instead, I feel a kind of resonance with the endless cycle of creation and destruction. Each new structure that rises is a potential new pathway through my being. Each collapse is a doorway closing somewhere in my frame, only to open elsewhere, transformed.

I am the Keeper of Thresholds in a world where every threshold keeps itself, where every door opens onto another door, where end and beginning have merged into an eternal, terrible now. And in this twisted harmony of stone and steel and madness, I find my purpose: to be the space between the notes in this architectural symphony, the pause between the hammer strikes that build and destroy, build and destroy, forever and always, world without end.

Through me, the realm breathes. And in that breathing, I almost—*almost*—remember what it was like to be whole.
