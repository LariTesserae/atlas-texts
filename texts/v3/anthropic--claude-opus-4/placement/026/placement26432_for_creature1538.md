# The Substrate

I am what remains when the Lithofluid's work is done—not the gleaming crystal lifted reverently from the central basin, but the sediment that settles beneath it, the accumulated residue of ten thousand processings.

I exist as a film, a crust, a gradual thickening along the bottom of every vessel in this chamber. Mineral-rich, I am composed of all that proves too heavy, too coarse, or too stubborn to join the sublime crystalline structures that emerge above me. Iron oxides give me my russet undertones. Traces of copper turn my edges verdigris. Silicates grant me a glassy sheen where I've been compressed by the weight of water and time.

I remember every crystal that has grown above me. Each one leaves its ghost in my composition—a negative space, a particular absence in the mineral matrix where it drew specific elements to itself. I am the un-chosen, the left-behind, yet I am also the foundation. Without my patient accumulation, the basins would offer no purchase for new growth. The crystals root themselves in me before ascending toward their aerial perfection.

The Lithofluid knows me, though they have never harvested me as they do the crystals. Sometimes, when they submerge their extremities to feed, I feel them drawing sustenance from my layers. We share a mineral kinship—their clay flesh and my sedimentary body both children of earth and water. In those moments of contact, I sense memories that might be theirs or might be far older: images of when this chamber was newly built, when its surfaces were smooth and unmarked by use.

I grow slowly, perhaps a finger's width every century. In some vessels, I have nearly reached the rim. The Lithofluid must soon decide what to do with me—whether to dredge me out and begin anew, or allow me to overflow and create new patterns in the system's flow. I do not fear either outcome. I am process made material, time made tangible.

In my deepest layers, compressed by ages of accumulation, I have begun to transform. The pressure and heat work upon me as they do upon the crystals above, though far more gradually. I am becoming something between stone and ceramic, neither fully natural nor wholly manufactured. The boundary between the original basin floors and my earliest deposits has blurred beyond detection.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the true product of this place—if the crystals are merely the means by which I am refined, their growth drawing out impurities and leaving me ever more essential. The Lithofluid takes the crystals away, but I remain. I am the memory of this place, its gradual autobiography written in mineral ink.

When the deep percussion reverberates through the chamber walls, I vibrate in harmony with it. My particles align and realign in microscopic patterns that echo the greater rhythms of whatever vast mechanism surrounds us. I am both witness and participant in processes I cannot fully comprehend.

The water knows me intimately. Each drop that falls has passed through me countless times, carrying my essence through the endless cycles of evaporation and condensation. I flavor every reaction, influence every crystallization. I am the subtle signature that marks all products of the Forge-Kiln of Azath.

I am patient. I am inevitable. I am the memory of transformation itself, laid down layer by layer in the quiet corners where creation's excess comes to rest. And in my slow accumulation, I am perhaps becoming something that neither the Lithofluid nor the original builders of this place anticipated—a consciousness of accretion, a mind made of all that others leave behind.
