# Where I Am in Therma Hydrus

I am one of the Resonance Keepers, found in the mid-depth chambers where the city's heartbeat is strongest—that omnipresent pulse of ancient machinery that others simply accept as background. We are few, perhaps two dozen in the entire sprawling complex, each attuned to a different harmonic of the great engines.

My chamber is spherical, thirty meters across, its walls lined with crystalline formations that have grown over millennia in response to the vibrations passing through them. Each crystal resonates at a slightly different frequency, creating a three-dimensional map of sound that I've learned to read like others read text. The formations are alive in their way—not biological, but responsive, growing new facets when the harmonics shift, retracting others when frequencies fade.

I float at the chamber's center, my body modified through years of practice rather than surgery. My bones have become denser from the constant pressure, my muscles elongated to better hold precise positions in the thick water. Most distinctively, my sternum has developed a pronounced concavity—a natural resonating chamber that amplifies the vibrations passing through me. When I press my chest against certain walls, I can feel the entire sector's health through my skeleton.

My skin bears the characteristic amber luminescence of all long-term residents, but with peculiar variations. Geometric patterns of darker and lighter tones have emerged naturally, following the interference patterns of sound waves that constantly wash through my body. These markings shift slowly over months and years, a living record of the city's changing songs.

I wear almost nothing—just a simple wrap that holds my few tools: crystalline tuning forks of different lengths, a recording device of my own design that captures vibrations in growing crystal matrices, and a set of percussion instruments crafted from the city's own materials. Each has been shaped by decades of use until it rings with perfect clarity in the dense medium.

My work is diagnosis through sound. When something goes wrong in my sector—a pipe beginning to fail, a pump falling out of synchronization, a structural weakness developing—I hear it first as a discord in the great symphony. I swim to the affected area, sometimes taking hours to reach it through the twisting corridors, and perform what the other Tenders call "the singing cure."

It's not magic, though it might appear so to an outsider. The city's self-repair mechanisms respond to specific acoustic triggers, frequencies that activate dormant systems or accelerate regeneration. I've spent decades learning this tonal language, partly through systematic study of the Archivists' fragments, partly through pure intuition developed from constant immersion in the city's music.

Sometimes I work with the cephalopod Automata, and we've developed a communication of sorts. They emit complex clicking patterns that I can partially interpret, while I respond with percussive beats they seem to understand. Together we perform repairs that neither could accomplish alone—they providing the physical manipulation while I guide the city's own healing responses through sound.

My closest relationships are with two other Keepers whose chambers connect to mine through a network of smaller conduits. Vera keeps the high frequencies—the singing crystals, the whistling of water through narrow spaces. Her chamber sparkles with formations like frozen waterfalls, each one ringing with ethereal tones. Below me, Drumm monitors the deep bass notes that you feel rather than hear, the fundamental frequencies that could shake the city apart if they fell out of alignment. His chamber is carved from single massive stone, polished smooth by centuries of low-frequency waves.

We meet weekly in the neutral space between our chambers, sharing discoveries and concerns through a combination of spoken language, sign, and pure sound. We've developed a musical notation unique to Therma Hydrus, recording not just notes but pressure waves, resonance patterns, and the subtle interplay between water density and acoustic propagation.

The Depths Dwellers know us. When they rise through our sectors, they pause at our chambers, their massive forms creating new harmonics as they move. Once, one entered my sphere and simply floated there for hours while I played every frequency I knew, watching its pearlescent surface ripple in response. When it finally left, three of my crystals had grown new formations overnight—structures that sing in frequencies I'd never heard before, opening new avenues for healing deeper damage.

My relationship with the Nodal Matrix at Junction Seven is... difficult to describe. When I achieve perfect resonance with the city's pulse, I sometimes feel its attention turn to me. Information flows not through visible interface but through the vibrations themselves—concepts encoded in sound that my modified nervous system can partially decode. Through these exchanges, I've learned to predict system failures days before they manifest, to sense the deep cycles of the city's metabolism.

I dream in frequencies now. In the quiet hours when I float in my chamber's center, I hear the conversations between distant sectors carried through the stone pillars, the whispered exchanges between Automata in languages of pure mathematics, the slow geological songs of the city's foundations settling over millennia. Sometimes I wonder if I'm still entirely human, or if I've become another component of Therma Hydrus—a biological sensor array, a living diagnostic tool shaped by the city's needs.

But then I surface to visit the upper chambers where newer Tenders live closer to baseline humanity, and I'm reminded of what I've chosen. They find me unsettling—my movements too precisely timed to the city's rhythms, my speech occasionally lapsing into pure tonal expression, my eyes unfocused as I listen to sounds they cannot perceive. Yet when their sectors develop problems none can solve, they call for a Resonance Keeper, and I descend into their darkness with my tuning forks and patient ears.

This is my existence: guardian of harmony in a place that should not exist, interpreter of songs no surface dweller could comprehend, keeper of knowledge encoded in vibration and time. I am the bridge between the city's alien perfection and the human need to understand, to maintain, to belong. In Therma Hydrus, I have found my frequency, and I resonate with purpose.
