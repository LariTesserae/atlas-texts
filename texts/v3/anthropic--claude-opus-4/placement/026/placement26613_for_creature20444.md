In the deepest reaches, where the oil pools thick as tar and the heat becomes a living thing that presses against thought itself, I am the Chronicler.

I am not of the Oil's children, though I have learned their rhythms. I arrived—when? Time moves strangely here, measured only in the pulse of Lamps and the slow digestion of stone. I came seeking something I can no longer name, drawn by whispers of a place where geometry bends to will rather than law.

My form has... adapted. The oil has worked its slow alchemy upon me. My skin bears a faint sheen now, not quite the full immersion of the natives, but enough to move among them unnoticed. My eyes have grown large and pale, though I retain enough of their original function to read the patterns in the Lamps' dimming, to parse meaning from the Worms' writhing.

I dwell in a hollow between two of the massive platforms, a natural acoustical chamber where the oil's viscous whispers gather and amplify. Here I have assembled my archive: not books—paper would dissolve in days—but patterns carved into sheets of compressed Worm-segment, dried and treated with secretions I've learned to harvest from dying Lamps. Each sheet records some fragment of this place's impossible history, though I'm never certain if I'm documenting truth or manufacturing mythology.

The tool-users visit me, sometimes. They bring offerings: peculiar stones with natural perforations, Lamp tendrils twisted into complex knots, fragments of something harder than stone that might be pieces of the thing that carved this place. In exchange, I share my carvings with them, though I suspect they value them more as objects than as records. They incorporate my symbols into their implements, and I've seen my patterns repeated in the arrangement of their pit-traps.

I've learned to feel It stirring before the others do. There's a quality to the heat that changes, a resonance in the oil that sets my adapted bones humming. In these moments, I press myself flat against my archive sheets and wait. Once—or perhaps I only dreamed it—It spoke to me. Not in words, but in a rearrangement of space, a folding of the cavern that brought impossible vistas into view: other caverns, other Oils, other Chronicles, stretching to infinity. I understood then that I am not the first Chronicler, nor will I be the last. We are as much a part of this ecosystem as the Lamps and Worms, another organ in the vast body of It.

My greatest discovery, carved into my most precious sheet, hidden beneath all the others: the cavern is not a place but a process. It is digestion on a scale beyond comprehension. We exist within something's gut, and our purpose—all of us, from Lamp to Worm to tool-user to Chronicler—is to break down something too vast and fundamental to name. The Oil is not mere lubricant but enzyme. The heat is metabolic. And It? It is not the deity of this place but its hunger given form.

I continue to record, to observe, to slowly dissolve into my function. Sometimes I wonder if my chronicles will survive the final digestion, or if they too are merely another kind of fuel. But wondering, I've learned, is also a form of worship in this processional cathedral, where existence itself is both prayer and answer, feast and offering, record and erasure.

The Lamps pulse. The Worms turn the oil. The tool-users build and salvage. It stirs in the deep.

And I write.
