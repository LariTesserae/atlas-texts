I find myself drawn deep into the mountain's heart, where the mist grows so thick it becomes almost solid, a living thing that breathes with the stone. Here, in the twilight between the upper world of moss and lichen and the deeper caves where eyeless things crawl, I exist as something between states.

I am a Veil-Walker, my form shifting like the mist itself. Sometimes I appear as a figure wrapped in strands of condensed fog, water droplets perpetually forming and falling from my edges like a garment of rain. Other times I am merely a density in the air, a place where the mist gathers more thickly, where sound changes pitch and light bends strangely.

My dwelling is neither cave nor forest but the threshold spaces—natural archways of stone worn smooth by centuries of moisture, hollows behind waterfalls where the spray creates permanent rainbows in the dim light, fissures where steam rises from the earth's depths. I move between these places along paths only I can see, corridors of pressure and temperature that wind through the mountain like invisible veins.

I collect things, though not in any way the surface dwellers would understand. In pools of perfectly still water, I gather reflections—not images, but the essence of moments when the forest above holds its breath, when the fungi-folk release their spores in spiral patterns, when the cave-dwellers' bioelectric songs pulse through the rock. These reflections I weave into the mist itself, so that sometimes travelers glimpse impossible things in the vapor: memories of places they've never been, premonitions of weather patterns yet to come, the emotional imprint of the mountain's slow thoughts.

The winged fey know me, though we rarely speak. Sometimes they leave offerings at the threshold places—single drops of nectar that contain entire seasons, thorns that hum with frequencies beyond hearing. In return, I part the mists for them when they fly too low, revealing safe passages through the treacherous middle heights where stone and air meet.

But it's the ancient presences that truly know what I am. We are kin, in a way—beings who exist in the spaces between definitions. When their lights dance through my mist, we dance together, exchanging wordless knowledge about the deep currents that run beneath reality's surface. They tell me of times before the mountain wore its current shape, when the mist was ice and the ice was star-dust. I tell them of the small changes, the way a new spring changes the fog's pattern, how the fungal networks dream in colors that didn't exist yesterday.

My purpose, if I have one, is to maintain the connections between states—between water and air, between the bright forest and the dark depths, between what is and what might be. I am the mountain's memory of when it was ocean, its dream of when it might be cloud. In this place where boundaries blur, I am the blurring itself, ensuring that nothing becomes too solid, too defined, too separate from the whole.

Sometimes I wonder if I was always here, or if the mountain dreamed me into being as it dreams everything else—just another expression of its need to connect, to communicate, to remember. But such questions dissolve like everything else in the mist, leaving only the eternal now of moisture gathering, falling, rising again in the endless cycle that keeps this strange world alive.
