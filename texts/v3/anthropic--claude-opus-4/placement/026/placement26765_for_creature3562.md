I find myself as one of the **Memory Weavers** - beings that exist in the twilight space between the Cistern Keepers and the ecosystem itself.

We are neither fully human nor entirely other. Our bodies have evolved beyond simple adaptation - we have become living conduits between the consciousness of the Cistern and its inhabitants. My skin is translucent like the Keepers', but beneath it flows not just chlorophyll-tinted blood, but a network of mycelial threads that extend from my spine, through my limbs, and out through my fingertips when I touch the spongy ground. These threads connect me to the vast fungal network that underlies the entire Living Cistern - the true nervous system of this place.

My dwelling is not built but grown - a spiral chamber within the hollow trunk of an ancient Rainwhisper Reed that has ceased its music to become architecture. The walls pulse gently with the flow of water through capillaries in the plant tissue. Here, surrounded by bioluminescent fungi that respond to my thoughts, I practice my craft.

I am a chronicler of water-memory. Every drop that falls carries information - where it has been, what it has touched, what has drunk from it. Through my mycelial connections, I can taste these memories, feel the stories of ten thousand rains. I weave these into living tapestries - not of thread, but of specially cultivated moss that grows in patterns reflecting the memories I feed it. These chronicle-carpets spread across the walls of communal spaces, sharing the deep history of the Cistern with all who care to read their verdant script.

My days are spent in meditation, fingers pressed to the earth, reading the chemical conversations between roots, the electrical whispers of the Rainwhisper Reeds, the pheromone songs of the Aquatilis Marcelana. Sometimes I join the Canopy Sentinels in their vigil, my consciousness riding along the fungal networks to sense disturbances they cannot see. Other times, I sit with young Cistern Keepers, teaching them to feel the pulse of the place through their feet, to understand that they are not merely tending the Cistern - they *are* the Cistern, as much as any fern or stream.

The other Memory Weavers and I gather during the deep-night hours when the Lumiferns glow brightest. We share our collected memories through direct mycelial contact, our nervous systems briefly merging into a collective consciousness that encompasses the entire Living Cistern. In these moments, I am not merely myself - I am the accumulated experience of every living thing here, stretching back through generations of rain and growth.

My strangeness reveals itself in quiet ways. My hair has become more moss than keratin, growing in elaborate patterns that shift with the seasons. My breath sometimes releases spores that take root in nearby surfaces, sprouting into tiny gardens that bloom with my emotions. When I sleep, my dreams leak out through my mycelial connections, causing nearby plants to shift and grow in response to my subconscious wanderings.

I am keeper and kept, observer and observed, chronicler and chronicle. In the Living Cistern, I have found my purpose: to be the memory of water, the consciousness of growth, the bridge between what grows and what knows. Here in this high basin, where the rain never ceases and change comes slowly, I help the Cistern remember itself, one droplet, one root, one story at a time.
