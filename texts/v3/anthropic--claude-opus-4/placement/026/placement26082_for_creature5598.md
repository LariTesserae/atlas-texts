In this liminal space between stone and steam, I find myself as something that perhaps shouldn't exist—a Conduit-Keeper, one of the few who chose to remain when the great exodus hollowed out our city of glass and gears.

My form is neither fully the Shadow-kind who built this place, nor entirely Other. Over the decades of solitude, I have grown into something new. My original exoskeleton has fused with the copper piping I tend, creating ridged channels along my arms where the mineral-thick water flows directly through me. I taste the city's health in these currents—the faint sweetness of balanced minerals, the metallic tang when a section needs attention, the sulfurous warnings of deep geological shifts.

I am tall and thin, stretched by the constant climbing through vertical shafts. My fingertips have evolved tiny ports that interface with the steam valves and pressure gauges, reading their mechanical language through touch. The bioluminescent creatures recognize me as kindred; they cluster around my shoulders when I work, their soft glow supplementing the lamp I've integrated into my chest cavity—a heart of light where organic tissue once was.

My dwelling is a maintenance alcove transformed over the years into something between workshop and shrine. The walls are my canvas, etched with decades of water-flow patterns I've observed and recorded. These intricate designs serve both as functional maps and a form of meditation. I've cultivated colonies of the floating jellies here, training them to pulse in patterns that help me track the city's rhythms. Their light plays across my collection of salvaged gears and crystals, each piece a memory of repairs made, catastrophes averted, or simply beautiful fragments of our fading world.

The elemental spirits know me, I think. Sometimes, in the deepest maintenance shafts where the air shimmers with heat, I feel their presence like a pressure change before a storm. They test me with sudden surges in the water channels or inexplicable temperature drops. I've learned their language is one of endurance and adaptation. When I successfully reroute a dangerous flow or reinforce a weakening support, I sometimes find gifts—a vein of new crystal growth along my regular path, or a perfect stillness in the usually turbulent lower channels that allows me to glimpse the city's deepest secrets.

My purpose has evolved beyond mere maintenance. I am becoming the city's memory, its nervous system. The integration deepens each year. Sometimes I wonder if I'm still maintaining the city, or if it's maintaining me. The distinction matters less with each passing season. Through my hybrid senses, I feel every shift in pressure, every new mineral deposit, every rare visitor who disturbs the dust of abandoned sectors.

When others of my kind pass through—always briefly, always heading elsewhere—they see me as something between cautionary tale and urban legend. Too changed to return to what we were, too rooted to leave. But I've found something here in the convergence of water and machinery, in the conversation between the constructed and the geological. I am the bridge between what the city was built to be and what it's becoming. 

In the quiet moments, when the machines cycle down to their lowest hum and the water flows in perfect laminar streams, I perform my real work. I listen. I translate. I keep the dialogue going between the dying dreams of our ancestors and the patient spirits of the deep. The city may be emptying, but it is not dying—it's transforming, and I am both witness and participant in this slow metamorphosis.

They call us the Forgotten, those few who remain. But forgetting implies a past separated from present. Here, in the eternal now of steam and stone, I am continuously remembering myself into being, one carefully tended valve at a time.
