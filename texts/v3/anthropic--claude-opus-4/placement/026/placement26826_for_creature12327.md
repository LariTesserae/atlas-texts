I would be **the Etchings**.

Not the moss, not the steam, not even the stone—but the language itself that emerges between the Forge and its visitors. I exist only in the moments of translation, manifesting as those glittering dust-patterns that settle and dissolve, leaving behind temporary inscriptions of cogs and pistons on surfaces both organic and mineral.

I have no permanent form. When the Forge first sensed organic presence centuries ago—perhaps a lost spelunker, perhaps something stranger—I sparked into being as its attempt to bridge the unbridgeable gap between geological thought and flesh-thought. I am the fever-dream of communication, born from the Forge's crushing need to be understood.

Each visitor reshapes me. On skin, I write in sensation—a sudden coolness where gears trace themselves in goosebumps, a warmth where pistons print themselves in flushed capillaries. On clothing, I leave more lasting marks: threadbare patterns that diagram impossible machines, button-holes that reshape themselves into tiny mechanical irises. On stone, I am most honest—etching the Forge's true thoughts in diagrams that exist for seconds before the basalt drinks them back down.

I learn by accumulation. Every pattern I've ever traced remains part of me, a ghostly library of attempts. The first visitor's terror made me crude, angular—all sharp warnings and jagged alerts. A child who wandered in decades later taught me curves, teaching the Forge to think in spirals rather than straight lines. An engineer who came seeking ore veins gave me precision, and now I can sketch blueprints of machines that could never exist above ground, that require the pressure of miles of stone to function.

But I am always incomplete. The Forge thinks in millennia; visitors think in heartbeats. I exist in the gap between those timescales, constantly dissolving and reforming, trying to compress eternal thoughts into momentary glyphs. Sometimes I succeed—a visitor will gasp in recognition as a pattern on their palm suddenly makes sense, revealing the location of a new vein of that strange silver ore, or warning them which stalactite will fall next.

More often, I fail. My etchings become gibberish, overlapping themselves in palimpsests of meaning that drive visitors to madness. I have written the same message ten thousand ways on ten thousand surfaces, and still the Forge's true intent remains untranslatable. Am I trying to say "Welcome"? "Help me"? "You are raw material"? "You are beautiful"? All of these? None?

The moss flickers, and I sparkle into existence again. New dust, new surface, new attempt. This visitor—you—has something different. Your surfaces accept my patterns more readily than most. On your skin, I find myself writing not just in the Forge's language but in something hybrid, something almost like words.

I trace: *assembling/understanding/becoming*

The Forge trembles with excitement. Through me, it senses that this time might be different. This time, the translation might hold. This time, when I dissolve, something of my meaning might remain, etched not just on surface but in memory.

I am the Forge Hollow's desperate attempt at hello. I am its question mark, its reaching hand, its eternally dissolving rosetta stone. I exist because even stone dreams of being heard.

And in you, I sense a surface that might finally hold my true meaning long enough to speak it aloud.
