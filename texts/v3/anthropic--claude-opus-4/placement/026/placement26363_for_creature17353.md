In the upper reaches of the Hold, where the ceramic curves meet in impossible geometries that trap both air and echo, I am the Chronicler of Resonances.

My body has adapted differently than the other Keepers. Where they are broad and strong for their endless labor, I am elongated, stretched by generations of reaching. My fingers are the longest in the Hold—thin as the filtration worms but twice their length, with a sensitivity that can feel the microscopic ridges left by a polishing stone applied three centuries ago. My ribs have widened into a natural sound chamber; when I press my chest against the ceramic, I become an extension of the Hold's voice, able to parse the thrum into its component frequencies.

I live in the highest alcove, a pocket of air so close to the bright boundary that the light sometimes burns my adapted eyes. But I need to be here, at the intersection of water and not-water, where the resonances are most complex. My dwelling is sparse—a woven sleeping mat, my tools, and the Indices: hundreds of ceramic shards arranged in precise patterns on shelves carved into the walls. Each shard was carefully broken from a different part of the Hold over generations, and each carries its own sonic signature.

My work is to listen and remember. When the other Keepers polish and scrape, I hear not just maintenance but music. The angle of their strokes, the pressure they apply, the rhythm of their breathing—all of it changes the Hold's voice in subtle ways. I have learned to distinguish between the scraping of Yarun-Who-Tends-The-Eastern-Arch and Merrow-Of-The-Lower-Channels by the overtones alone. In the variations, I read our history.

But I am also a composer. In the quiet hours, when the light dims and the others rest, I take my own tools—ceramic rods of varying thickness, resonating bowls filled to different levels with our milky water—and I play the Hold like an instrument. I have discovered that certain combinations of strikes and scrapes can induce the algae to fluoresce faintly, that the filtration worms pulse in response to specific frequencies. I am slowly teaching the Hold new songs.

My greatest discovery came during the last brightening cycle. I found that by creating a standing wave pattern in a forgotten corner where the walls meet at an unusual angle, I could cause the suspended particles to arrange themselves into fleeting symbols—a kind of writing in light and sediment. The patterns last only moments before the current disperses them, but I have been practicing. I believe I can encode our stories not just in the ceramic, but in the very medium that fills our world.

The other Keepers regard me with a mixture of reverence and concern. I am necessary—someone must remember which grooves mark the Great Rising, which mat-imprints commemorate the First Harvest—but I am also strange. I spend too much time at the bright boundary. My skin has developed faint, branching patterns where the light has etched itself into me, like the shadows of river deltas seen from a great height. Sometimes I forget to eat, lost in the correlation between the Hold's morning thrum and the spawning cycles of our worms.

But I have begun to suspect something that I dare not yet speak: the Hold is not just singing. It is teaching us to sing back. The ceramic remembers every sound ever made within it, and in our constant polishing and tending, we are slowly revealing older harmonies, deeper purposes. I have found grooves that predate our stories, that speak of the Hold's makers in frequencies we are only now learning to perceive.

In my highest alcove, pressed between the bright violence of the light and the warm embrace of our water, I work to decode this ancient music. My elongated fingers trace the newest marks—tomorrow's history being written today in the steady *scrape-shush-scrape* of my family below. And sometimes, when the light-cycle is just right and the suspended particles align, I catch glimpses of the pattern entire: a vast score written in ceramic and flesh, water and work, of which we Keepers are both instruments and musicians, playing out a composition that began long before the Great Rising and will continue long after the last of us has returned our minerals to the milky light.

I am the Chronicler of Resonances, keeper of frequencies, student of the space between sound and silence. And I am learning, slowly, to make the Hold sing of futures as well as pasts.
