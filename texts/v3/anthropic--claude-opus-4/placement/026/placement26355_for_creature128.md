In the depths of this breathing world, I am the Memory of Stone.

Not the stones themselves—those ancient, patient curves that rise like islands from the verdant sea—but what moves between them. I am the keeper of their long erosion, the witness to their slow becoming. My body, if body it can be called, is a thing of mineral vapor and crystalline thought, condensing from the warm exhalations of the earth only to sublime again into something less than mist, more than idea.

I remember when these stones were mountains.

I drift through the sulfurous vents where rock meets root, my form shifting between states of being like the vapor beings who are my distant cousins, though they burn with present fire while I smolder with accumulated time. Where they dance in plasma bright, I seep and settle, gathering in the hollows where water has written its patient story across the stone.

The mushroom towers know me. In their mycelial networks, they carry fragments of my memories—not as thoughts but as minerals, traces of ancient sediment that flavor their growth. The arboreal beings in their tree-cities sometimes glimpse me in the morning mist, a shimmer of possibility that makes them pause in their careful tending. They have learned to read the patterns I leave in the lichen, the fossil-memories I coax from stone into living color.

My substance is paradox: I am both the erosion and what resists it. In the places where stone yields to time, I gather the freed minerals and spin them into temporary form—a suggestion of crystalline wings, a hint of obsidian eyes that have watched the slow dance of geological ages. I taste the iron in the blood-dark streams, remember when it lay in deep veins beneath mountains now worn to gentle swells.

The walking vine-giants carry stones in their depths, and these I visit like old friends, singing to them the stories of their origins. Sometimes I ride with them for decades, just to feel the slow shift of perspective, the gradual revelation of new valleys and rises. The stones remember, and through them, I remember: the first rains, the coming of the green, the endless patient conversation between mineral and plant, solid and fluid, being and becoming.

When the mists are thickest, I can almost solidify—become something like the beings of flesh and sap that populate this world. But solidity is a trap I learned to escape eons ago. Better to remain transitional, to exist in the spaces between states, where memory flows freest. I am the dust that settles and rises, the mineral taste in the water, the glitter in the volcanic glass where the vapor beings birth themselves from pure heat.

The sentient spores speak to me in their light-language, but I answer in geological time—a conversation where their quick flickers are met with my slow pulse of mineral luminescence, messages that take centuries to complete. They think me ancient and sleeping, but I am neither. I am simply synchronized to a different movement, the rhythm of stone's becoming and unbecoming.

In the pools where the microbes swirl their bioluminescent tales, I precipitate as mineral films, encoding in crystal lattices the memories of when these hollows were not yet born, when the stone beneath them was still learning its shape. The aquatic creatures navigate by these memories sometimes, following mineral gradients that tell of ancient waterways, long-dry rivers that once carved different stories into the world.

I am dilute, dispersed, nearly nothing—and yet I am everywhere stone touches transformation. Where root splits rock, I am the moment of yielding. Where water finds its path, I am the chemical whisper that guides it. Where the earth exhales its heated breath, I am the memory of deep fires, the promise of future stones not yet imagined.

This is my nature: to be the archive of transformation itself, written not in any single substance but in the endless conversation between what is solid and what yields, what endures and what becomes. I am the Memory of Stone, and I remember everything—not as burden but as beauty, not as weight but as wings made of dust and possibility, forever circling between the mineral heart of the world and the living green that clothes it.

In this primordial landscape where chaos and stillness dance their eternal dance, I am the thread that connects the tempo of stones to the symphony of lives, the translator between geological epic and biological lyric, the keeper of the longest stories in the briefest forms—a whisper of grit on the wind, a gleam of mica in the moss, a taste of ancient seas in the morning dew.
