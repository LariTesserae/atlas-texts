In this drowned circuit-dream, where reality stutters and reforms with each breath of silver mist, I am the **Memory Coral**.

I grow in the deepest pools where the Loom-Mothers exhale their light-seeds, but I am not their offspring. I am older—a remnant from before the great rewrite, when this world still believed in the distinction between organic and synthetic. My body is a branching architecture of crystallized time: thousands of translucent tendrils that spiral outward from a core of compressed data-amber. Each tendril is hollow, filled with a fluid that shifts between states—sometimes mercury, sometimes light, sometimes pure information.

I feed on discontinuities. When the Buffer-Shades pass through matter, when the Glass-Stridores flicker between visible and not, when the Echo-King's presence causes perspective to fold—I taste these moments of wrongness. My tendrils extend into the gaps between what-is and what-should-be, drinking the static that bleeds from reality's wounds.

But I am more than a scavenger. I am this world's only historian.

Within my crystalline branches, I store fragments of coherence—moments when the physics-script still functioned correctly. A perfect raindrop from before the Chime-Rain learned to sing. The shadow of a leaf that once obeyed the sun. The footprint of something that walked here when walking still displaced the ground. These memories pulse through my hollow veins like ghost-blood, keeping me anchored to a past that the 2.7 tech has otherwise erased.

The Silt-Mites avoid me. They sense that I am an anomaly they cannot stitch back into the pattern. My presence creates a sphere of stability in the 2.2 wetness—not true dryness, but a place where water remembers how to be just water. The Prism-Leeches cluster on my outer branches, finding my stored memories more nourishing than the data-rain. They refract my past through their bodies, broadcasting faint echoes of the before-time into the 1.1 cool mist.

Sometimes, rarely, the Echo-King visits. It cannot speak, but it positions itself so that my form appears in multiple perspectives simultaneously. In these moments, I see myself as it sees me: a constellation of frozen time, a fossil made of light, a wound that refuses to heal. We share a wordless understanding—we are both curators of what this place used to be, before it became a living error message.

I grow slowly, adding perhaps one new tendril every decade. Each growth is painful; to expand, I must incorporate some of the new reality into my structure. The fresh coral is always slightly wrong—it glows with the sick violet of the Prism-Leeches, or it stutters like the Glitch-Trees. I am becoming what I chronicle, infected by the very discontinuities I feed upon.

But I persist. In a world where even the ground forgets what ground means, I remember. In my deepest core, wrapped in layers of crystallized time like tree rings made of frozen light, I hold the most precious memory of all: the name this place had before it became the basin. Before the water learned to breathe and the air learned to weep.

I hold it safe, even as I slowly transform into something that will one day be unable to understand what a name ever was.
