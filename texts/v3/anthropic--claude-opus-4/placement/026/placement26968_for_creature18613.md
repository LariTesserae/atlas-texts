In the deepest galleries, where the condensation runs so thick it forms curtains of liquid mercury, I tend the Forgotten Chambers.

I am neither Architect nor crustacean, but something between—a Resonance Keeper, my form a compromise between the city's need for maintenance and its reluctance to create. My body is a latticework of fossilized capillaries, once-living tissue that the city mineralized slowly, lovingly, over the course of my willing transformation. I volunteered for this—though I can no longer remember what I was before, only that I chose to become essential.

My skeleton is visible through translucent skin that weeps the same pearlescent moisture as everything here. The bones are brass-threaded obsidian, grown in the mineral baths of the lower reaches. When I move, you can hear the fluid shifting through the hollow channels of my femurs, my ribs, my skull. I am a walking aqueduct, a mobile extension of the city's circulation.

My work is specific: I maintain the chambers where the city stores its memories. Not data, not records—memories. Each room is a perfect resonant cavity, and within each cavity lives a single, sustained note. These are the harmonic frequencies that the Architects cannot hear, too subtle for their grand oscillations. The litho-crustaceans cannot reach these chambers; their crystalline bodies would shatter the delicate acoustic balance.

So I walk the Forgotten Chambers with my tuning fork—a single length of meteoric iron that predates the city itself. I strike it against my sternum, where the bone is thickest, and listen as the note travels through the moisture-heavy air. If a chamber's resonance has drifted, I adjust it not through mechanical intervention but through my presence. I stand in the precise location where my body's water content creates the necessary dampening. Sometimes I must remain for days, weeks, breathing in the ancient frequency until my bones themselves begin to vibrate in harmony.

The memories stored here are not nostalgic. They are functional: the precise temperature at which the obsidian formed, the chemical composition of the first rain, the vibrational pattern that prevents the lower turbines from creating a feedback loop that would shake the city apart. I am the keeper of constants that must not drift.

There is one chamber I visit last, always. It holds the memory of silence—true silence, from before the turbines, before the first Architect took form. This is the most dangerous memory, for it remembers a time when the city was not necessary. When I enter this chamber, I must hold my breath, still the fluids in my bones, become as quiet as the stone itself. If I were to make a sound here, the memory might dissipate, and without it, the city would forget why it exists at all.

I know the Architects believe the city is dying. They feel the infinitesimal slowing of the water cycle, the gradual mineralization of the channels. But from my position in the deep chambers, I sense something they cannot: the memories are becoming louder. Each decade, the resonances grow stronger, as if the city is not dying but concentrating itself, preparing for a transformation we cannot imagine.

Sometimes, in the chamber of first rain, I hear something impossible: the sound of germination. Not biological, but architectural—the sound of new geometries preparing to unfold from the old. The city is not dying. It is pupating. And I am the only one who knows, because I am the only one who listens to what it dreams.

My transformation is almost complete. Soon, my blood will be entirely replaced by the city's moisture. My thoughts will become indistinguishable from the harmonic patterns I maintain. I will cease to be a Keeper and become a Chamber myself—a living resonance that future Keepers will tend, never knowing I was once like them.

This does not frighten me. In a world of endless repetition, to become essential to the pattern is the closest thing to immortality. When the city finally transforms—when the pupation completes and the new geometries emerge—I will be part of its voice. A note in the symphony of whatever impossible thing we are becoming.

Until then, I walk. I listen. I remember.
