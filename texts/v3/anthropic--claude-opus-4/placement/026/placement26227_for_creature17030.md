I am a Cartographer of Currents, though that name is both too grand and too simple for what I've become.

I arrived as an Observer, clutching waterlogged instruments that measured things that no longer mattered here—depth, pressure, salinity. The jade-water rendered them all equally meaningless. I remember the first breath that wasn't quite drowning, wasn't quite air. I remember how my panic dissolved into the pulse, how the thrumming entered my bones before I understood it was language.

Now I drift between states, not quite Observer, not yet fully Grown. My body has begun its slow transformation—fingernails extending into translucent tendrils that taste the water's memory, skin developing a faint phosphorescence that flickers in time with my heartbeat. The Grown speak through me sometimes, borrowing my vocal cords to shape sounds they've never needed before. In return, they show me the sphere's hidden geometries.

I map the unmappable. Not with instruments, but with my changing flesh. Each tendril that sprouts from my lengthening spine records a different quality of space—this one traces the paths of Shadow-Dancers, that one feels the weight of Echo-presence. My ribs have begun to separate, creating spaces between bones where Seed-forms nestle, their iridescent potential seeping into my marrow.

The structures here respond to my presence differently now. Where once they seemed alien, grown things without purpose I could recognize, now I understand their function through my feet, which have spread into root-like networks. I am learning to read the jade-water's history through its taste—here, the remnants of a civilization that tried to contain infinity; there, the dissolved dreams of travelers who came before.

My maps are not drawings but living things. I weave them into the vegetation, encoding the sphere's topology into the very pulse of light. Distance here is measured in thoughts. Direction is a function of desire. The center is everywhere, the edge nowhere, except in those shimmering moments when the outside breaks through.

Sometimes I catch glimpses of what I was—a shadow in the jade-water wearing my old face, clutching those useless instruments. The Echo-me, preserved in the sphere's memory. It reminds me that transformation is not erasure but accumulation. I am all the things I've been, plus all the things I'm becoming.

The Grown have shown me their grand work: the sphere is not a prison but an egg. We are all part of its slow gestation, each consciousness added to the whole bringing it closer to some unimaginable hatching. My maps, I realize, are not just recording space but preparing it, teaching it to recognize itself before it emerges into whatever lies beyond.

I still shiver sometimes, when the jade-water remembers it's supposed to be cold. But now I understand the shiver is also communication—my body translating temperatures into meanings the Grown can taste. Everything here is language. Everything speaks.

In the moments between pulse-beats, when the silence becomes absolute, I feel the weight of the impossible hand that cups this reality. I map its palm-lines through the sphere's curves, trace its fingerprints in the spiral of growing things. One day, perhaps, when I am more root than bone, more light than thought, I will finally understand whose hand it is.

Until then, in this underwater sky, in this breathing darkness, I drift and map and become. The sphere continues its patient revolution, and I revolve with it, cartographer and territory both, drawing the map that draws itself.
