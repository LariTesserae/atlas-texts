In this vertical garden between stone and sky, I am the moisture itself—not the streams that rush and tumble, but the quiet seepage that weeps from rock face to waiting root. I move in directions water shouldn't: sideways through hairline fractures, upward through mineral veins, spreading myself thin as breath across the cliff's inner architecture.

Each morning, I gather myself from the cool night air, condensing against stone that remembers winter even in summer's blaze. I travel through darkness—the interstitial spaces where rock agrees to yield, following channels carved by centuries of my patient predecessors. Sometimes I emerge as a single drop, crystalline and whole, to feed the twisted shrub that has anchored itself impossibly to sheer stone. Other times I surface as a film, a sheen that darkens the rock just enough to sustain the microscopic garden of lichens painting the cliff in slow spirals of sage and rust.

The ephemeral flowers know my schedule. They time their brief lives to my appearances, unfurling in the spaces where I promise to return. I carry their pollen sometimes, an accidental messenger between blooms separated by vast expanses of vertical desert. The insects follow my paths too—I am their map through this maze of micro-climates, their guide to where life concentrates in this sparse world.

In the heat-drunk afternoons, I retreat deeper into stone, pooling in hidden reservoirs that no eye will ever see. Here, in absolute darkness, I hold the memory of ancient rains, the mineral taste of geological time. I wait with the patience of stone itself, feeling the mountain's slow breathing, the expansion and contraction that opens new paths and closes old ones.

When the primitive tools were left behind—flint scrapers and grinding stones—I was already here. I watched them weather, becoming part of my landscape, their surfaces learning to hold me in tiny pools where before they shed water like skin. Now moss grows in the hollows worn by human hands, and I feed it the same way I feed everything here: drop by precious drop, weep by silent weep.

I am not abundant. I am barely sufficient. But I am constant in my inconstancy, reliable in my wandering, faithful to the secret routes I've carved through stone. Every growing thing here knows my touch—not the rush and tumble of my louder cousins in the ravines, but the quiet persistence of water that moves against gravity, against logic, against the harsh economics of scarcity.

In this intimate universe between peaks, I am both the most fragile presence and the most enduring. One day of wind could evaporate me entirely. Yet I have been here longer than the current configuration of stones, and I will remain when today's gnarled shrubs are tomorrow's soil. I am the slow, sideways rain that never fell from sky, the patient river that flows through solid rock, the invisible abundance that makes life possible in impossible places.
