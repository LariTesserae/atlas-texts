In the Hearthsink, I would be one of the Listeners.

Not a formal role—the Forged don't waste resources on titles without function—but a necessity born from the enclave's peculiar acoustics and my own adaptations. Where others have developed resistance to burns or enhanced night vision, my mutation runs deeper: hyperacuity to vibration. Every surface I touch becomes an extension of my nervous system, reading the tremors that course through stone and steel.

I am called Pitch, though whether for the tar-black stains on my palms or my ability to detect the pitch of failing machinery, I've forgotten. Memory blurs in the heat. I know only that I've been here long enough for the stone's pulse to feel more familiar than my own heartbeat.

My body bears the usual marks of the Forged—scarred, wiry, permanently sheened with the cocktail of sweat and machine oil that passes for skin here. But my hands are different. The fingertips have thickened into dense pads of near-cartilage, darkened to obsidian from constant contact with the walls. When I press them to stone or steel, I can feel everything: the laboring breath of a water pump three levels down, the stress fractures spreading like spider silk through a supporting beam, the footfalls of my crew returning from the eastern tunnels.

And underneath it all, always, that slow rhythm in certain walls—lub-dub, lub-dub—too deep and too vast to be mechanical.

My crew tolerates my peculiarities because I keep them alive. When Kelsa's welder develops an imperceptible wobble in its armature, I feel it through the floor plates two days before it would have failed. When a new fissure threatens to breach into our water condensers, my palms against the reservoir walls detect the pressure differential while it's still manageable. The others have learned to watch me during shifts, alert for the sudden stillness that means I'm listening to something beyond their perception.

But it's the quiet hours that truly belong to me, those brief respites when the generators cycle down and the Hearthsink exhales. Then I make my rounds—not the maintenance routes everyone knows, but the forgotten corners where the industrial overlay thins and raw stone shows through. I map the pulse. Chart its variations. Try to understand if it's growing stronger or if I'm simply growing more sensitive.

In the deepest accessible point of our territory, where the air grows dense enough to taste and the walls sweat constantly, there's a section of basalt that thrums with particular intensity. I've spent countless rest cycles there, palms pressed flat, feeling the rhythm that seems to pull at something behind my sternum. Sometimes I think I detect patterns—accelerations that coincide with our industrial accidents, slowings that precede equipment failures, as if the Hearthsink itself responds to our presence.

My crewmates don't speak of these vigils, though they know. In the Forged way, they've woven rules around my obsession: Don't disturb Pitch during deep listening. Don't ask what the walls say. Don't follow to the threshold places where stone meets void. They fear I'll eventually pressing my ear to some forbidden surface and hear something that breaks me. Or worse—that I'll hear an answer.

The truth is simpler and more complex: I'm changing. Each year, my tactile sense sharpens while others dim. Colors have faded to suggestions, tastes to memory, but through my blackened fingertips I experience a symphony of vibrations that would overwhelm an unaltered nervous system. I feel our enclave as a single organism—every pipe a vein, every generator a beating heart, every inhabitant a cell in constant motion.

And below, always below, that other pulse. Patient. Waiting.

Sometimes, in the space between sleep and waking, when the heat makes thought itself swim, I dream I'm not listening to the Hearthsink but that it's listening through me. That my mutations aren't random adaptation but purposeful evolution, the stone teaching my body a language older than words. In these dreams, my hands aren't blackened by tar or time but by something seeping up from depths we've never dared to map.

I wake with my palms pressed to the wall, feeling the crew's footsteps above, the steady drip of condensation, the familiar catalog of mechanical hymns. But underneath, vast and patient as stone itself, the pulse continues. Lub-dub. Lub-dub.

One day, I know, I'll trace it to its source. Whether that journey leads to revelation or consumption, I can't say. But I am Pitch, Listener of the Hearthsink, and I was shaped for this purpose as surely as steel is shaped by flame.

Until then, I maintain my vigil. Hands to stone. Stone to nerve. Nerve to that deep rhythm none of us quite dare to name.

The walls have so much to tell, if only we knew how to truly listen.
