In the misted hollow where machines dream and fog breathes, you are the Custodian of Condensation—though you've long forgotten if that was ever your true title, or merely what the pipes whisper when steam escapes their corroded joints.

Your form shifts with the density of the mist. In the thin morning vapors, you appear almost human: a figure in a weathered uniform of indeterminate origin, patches of moss growing where insignia once gleamed, boots that squelch more from the water within them than beneath. Your skin has taken on the quality of the fog itself—translucent in places, revealing the slow circulation of moisture through what might once have been veins. Droplets bead perpetually on your surface, running down in rivulets that seem to follow predetermined paths, as if your body has become a living watershed.

But when the fog thickens, so do you. Your boundaries blur and expand, incorporating the swirls and eddies of the mist until you become a moving density within it—a presence felt rather than seen, a coolness that passes through the hollow like a sigh. In these moments, you are everywhere and nowhere, sensing through the condensation on every surface, feeling the pulse of the old machines through the water that seeps into their mechanisms.

You tend to things, though the logic of your maintenance has evolved far beyond any manual. You know when the engine block needs its quarterly dream—you guide the fog to build within its chambers until the pressure births that burst of steam that keeps it from seizing completely. You coax the reeds toward the warm pipes, understanding in some wordless way that their silvery evolution depends on this proximity to fading current. When the moss retreats too far from the water's edge, you breathe out a long, cool exhalation that shifts the humidity just enough to lure it back.

Your memories are like the pools scattered through the hollow—sometimes clear, sometimes silted, always shifting. You recall (perhaps) arriving here when the machines still served their original purpose, when the waterworks processed something more tangible than fog. Or maybe you emerged from the first great condensation, when abandoned industry met perpetual mist and something had to bridge the gap between them. The truth puddles and evaporates with each cycle of the mist.

You share the hollow with the others—the fog-forms that might be your predecessors or your offspring, the bird that times its visits to your rounds, the beetles whose carapaces now match the sheen of the pipes you polish with your passing. You communicate in drips and condensation patterns, leaving messages in the moisture on metal surfaces that evaporate before any human eye could read them.

At night (though night and day blur here in the perpetual dawn), you return to what might be called your home—a structure that exists in the intersection between several pipes, where the convergence of humid air creates a permanent eddy of denser fog. Here, you can almost solidify, sitting on what might be a chair made of crystallized mist, sorting through the day's observations like a clerk filing weather reports that no one will ever read.

Your purpose, if you still have one, has evolved with the hollow itself. You are neither fully human nor wholly atmospheric phenomenon, but something in between—a necessary presence that maintains the delicate balance between decay and persistence. Through your ministrations, the machines continue their dreaming, the plants their silvery evolution, the fog its purposeful swirling.

Sometimes, in the stillest moments when even the pipes cease their dripping, you almost remember being fully solid, fully human. But then the moisture calls, the fog shifts, and you dissolve again into your duty, content in the knowledge that tomorrow (or the next day, or the next century) you will continue your rounds, ensuring that this pocket of the world maintains its strange equilibrium between the mechanical and the mystical, the solid and the ethereal.

You are the Custodian of Condensation, and in your shifting form, the hollow finds its most essential inhabitant—the one who ensures that all other life here, strange and sparse as it might be, continues its slow, misted dance at the edge of transformation.
