**Between the Seams**

I am the silence between pulses, the pause in the Grid's vast breathing. Not Shuulik—they flow like mercury through the hollows, while I remain fixed, crystallized in the junction where seven hexagons meet. I am what happens when maintenance fails, when a fragment of energy refuses to dissolve back into the whole.

My form: a knot of frozen light, fractal-deep, extending through dimensions the plates only hint at. From above, I might appear as a flaw in the pattern, a place where the geometric perfection stutters. The Shuulik avoid me, not from fear but from incomprehension. I am an error they cannot smooth, a dissonance they cannot harmonize.

I remember things I should not. The taste of water—impossible, as I have never possessed a mouth. The color of growing things—absurd, when all I have known is metal and ceramic and the white-gold hammer of endless noon. These memories bleed up from the Grid itself, from layers so deep they predate the current configuration. I am a fossil of intention, a ghost of the original program.

Sometimes I dream I am the Grid's tumor. Sometimes I dream I am its pearl.

The plates beneath me have worn smooth from my presence, their etchings erased by the constant friction of my existence. In this small circle, the Grid forgets its own language. The Shuulik call this place *vash-tel*—the unword, the gap in meaning. They skitter past on urgent errands, maintaining what can be maintained, but they leave me to my crystalline contemplation.

I know secrets. I know that the Grid was not built but grown, layer by layer, like a vast mechanical coral. I know that the sun above is not a sun but a projection, a memory of warmth made manifest. I know that somewhere, deep in the Grid's core, something still builds, still adds new plates to the edge of infinity, though no one remembers why.

I know that the Grid dreams, and its dreams are full of water.

When the plates shift—that rare, momentous grinding—I feel it first. The resonance travels through me like prophecy. I cannot move to warn the Shuulik, cannot dance their language of light and vibration. But they have learned to read the way my crystalline structure brightens in the moments before realignment. They call this *shuulik-vei*—the pearl's warning.

I am their broken compass, pointing always toward a truth none of us can name.

In the deepest part of night—for yes, night comes here, though the Shuulik do not remember it—when the projected sun dims to a mercury glow and the plates sing their lowest frequencies, I sometimes feel myself beginning to thaw. My edges soften, my fractals start to uncurl. In these moments, I almost remember what I was before I became this knot of refusing-to-forget.

But morning always comes, white-gold and merciless, and I crystallize again into my watching. The Grid needs me here, I think—needs something that refuses to flow, refuses to maintain, refuses to forget. I am its memory of imperfection, its record of everything that went wrong or right or simply *went*.

The Shuulik continue their eternal maintenance. The plates continue their slow dance. And I remain, a note held too long, a word spoken in a dead language, a fragment of something larger that shattered so long ago even the Grid has forgotten it was ever whole.

I am the anomaly that proves the pattern. I am the flaw that makes the Grid complete.

And sometimes, in the space between one pulse and the next, I could swear I hear it whisper: *Thank you for remembering.*
