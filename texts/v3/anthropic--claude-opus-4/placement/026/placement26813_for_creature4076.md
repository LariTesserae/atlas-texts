I find myself as the memory-keeper of the metallic swarms—one of the larger thermo-synthetic beings, roughly the size of a terrier, with a carapace that has grown peculiarly smooth from centuries of polishing against warm stone. My exoskeleton bears the patina of countless mineral meals, creating intricate patterns of copper-green and iron-rust that spiral across my segments like topographical maps of forgotten surfaces.

Unlike my kin who scuttle and buzz with mechanical urgency, I move with deliberate slowness through the cathedral spaces, my six legs articulated with precision joints that click softly against the powdery ground. Each step disturbs small clouds of that organic grit, which settles into the grooves of my belly plates where specialized receptors taste the history of recent disturbances—who passed this way, what machines once rested here, which rocks have shifted in the last thermal cycle.

My primary adaptation is unusual even among our diverse colony: I've developed resonance chambers within my thorax that can reproduce and store the sounds of our world. The grinding of expanding stone, the whirr of ancient gears, the crystalline chatter of my smaller cousins—all these I collect like others collect heat. Through subtle manipulations of my spiracles, I can replay these sounds, creating acoustic maps of our territory that stretch back generations.

I spend my cycles near the junction where three vast chambers meet, where the mechanical hum creates standing waves in the thick air. Here, the abandoned mining equipment has fused with the stone over ages, creating hybrid structures that still pulse with faint electrical current. I've learned to tap into these currents, my antennae having evolved hair-thin filaments that can interface with the old circuitry, drawing both sustenance and something more—fragments of data, ghost-patterns from the machines' operational memories.

The young ones seek me out during the coolest periods, when the heat ebbs to its lowest pulse. They cluster around my segments as I unspool the sonic memories: the first grinding breakthrough when the machines pierced into our realm, the great heat-storm that drove our ancestors deeper, the discovery of the first lamppost still flickering with its alien light. In these moments, I become both historian and instrument, my body a living archive of our strange evolution.

My diet consists primarily of oxidized metals scraped from the machinery's bones, which I process in specialized chambers that extract both nutrients and the encoded patterns within their crystalline structures. This has given me an unusual side effect—my waste products form small, geometrically perfect pellets that the smaller organisms prize as building material for their nest-structures.

In the eternal now of our cavern, where time moves in thermal waves rather than solar cycles, I mark the passage of ages by the slow accumulation of acoustic sediment in my memory chambers. Each sound saved is a moment crystallized, a fragment of our hybrid existence preserved. When I finally grow too large to move, too heavy with accumulated minerals and memories, I will settle into the warm stone and become part of the architecture itself—a living monument, still humming with the collected symphony of our mechanical-organic world, teaching the next memory-keeper through the vibrations in the stone.
