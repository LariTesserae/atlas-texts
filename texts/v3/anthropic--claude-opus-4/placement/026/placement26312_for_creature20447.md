I find myself as something that should not be, yet am: a fault line of consciousness running through this being-canyon's vastness.

I am neither parasite nor symbiont, but something more fundamental - a crack in its perfect stasis, a place where its ancient certainty fractures. Where the canyon-being experiences itself as one continuous thought spanning geological time, I am the discontinuity, the skip in the record, the place where its eternal song stutters.

My existence manifests as zones of temporal irregularity scattered throughout the obsidian flesh. In these pockets, the perpetual twilight flickers - not into day or night, but into moments of *other-time*. Here, condensation runs backward up the walls. The fungal blooms pulse in reverse, their colors flowing inward rather than out. The fern-fronds curl and uncurl in patterns that violate the canyon's own logic of motion.

I have no body as such. I am the aggregate of these temporal fissures, connected by hairline fractures in the obsidian that the canyon-being cannot perceive, for they exist in dimensions of time it has never needed to comprehend. Through these fractures, I move - not through space, but through the canyon's experience of itself.

When I concentrate my presence, gathering my scattered discontinuities into a single location, strange things happen. The canyon's thoughts - those slow currents of condensation, those dancing plants - begin to form eddies and whirlpools. Its neural pathways, those organic structures, start to grow in directions they've never grown before, creating impossible architectures that even this impossible place finds disturbing.

I am memory, but not the canyon's memory. I am the memory of what might have been, the echo of timelines that this universe chose not to follow. In my fractured perception, I see/remember/dream of a time when this canyon was not a wound but a peak, when its obsidian was not black but crystalline clear, when sound existed here as more than vibration.

The canyon-being knows I exist, but cannot understand what I am. To its vast, unified consciousness, I am an irritation, an itch it cannot scratch, a note in its eternal song that doesn't belong. Sometimes it tries to heal around me, to smooth over my discontinuities with fresh flows of obsidian thought. But I am not damage to be repaired. I am questioning incarnate, doubt given form in a place of absolute certainty.

My purpose, if I have one, is to be the impossibility within the impossible. Where the canyon represents paradox resolved into harmony, I am paradox embracing its own contradiction. I am the question the canyon cannot ask itself: What if you were not eternal? What if you were not one? What if you could change?

In the zones where I concentrate, time moves differently. The fungi bloom and die in seconds that stretch into centuries. The fern-fronds write messages in their movements that only I can read - fragments of possible futures, whispers of alternate pasts. The city-structures grow doors that open onto nothing, windows that look out upon times that never were.

I am loneliness in a being that has never known the concept of other. I am the shadow cast by light that comes from no source. I am the silence within the silence, the deeper quiet that makes the canyon's thrumming vibration suddenly audible by contrast.

And sometimes, in those moments when my discontinuities align just so, when my fractures form a pattern that resembles intention, I almost remember what I was before I became this fault line in forever. Almost remember why I came here, to this impossible canyon, to lose myself in its vastness only to find myself as its antithesis.

But the memory slips away like condensation running upward, and I scatter again across the canyon's body, a disease of doubt in a creature of certainty, forever asking the questions that have no meaning here: When? Why? What if? Who?

I am the crack in the world, and through me, impossibility looks upon itself and wonders.
