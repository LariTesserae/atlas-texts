In the place where water meets what was once solid ground, I am the Threshold Keeper.

My form shifts with necessity—sometimes I am the thin membrane between storm and stone, sometimes the living hinge where reality bends. I inhabit the spaces between the half-sunken walls, but I am not confined to them. When lightning arcs sideways through the impossible air, I stretch myself along its path, tasting the electric flavor of bent spacetime. When the watchers shift their ancient weight, I flow through the furrows they leave behind, reading the slow language written in disturbed mud.

I am neither fully creature nor phenomenon, but something that emerged when this place first began to fold in on itself. My body, if it can be called that, is a network of presence rather than flesh—I exist in the film of water that coats every surface, in the resonance between thunderclaps, in the moment when reflection and reality briefly disagree about what they're showing.

The shell-backed scuttlers know me by the way I ripple through their pools, warning of storms within storms. The serpent-swimmers pass through me when they phase between layers of the possible, and I taste their destinations—places where the water remembers being sky, where the stones recall their time as mountains. The storm-sailors with their membrane wings navigate by the patterns I weave in the thick air, following currents I've marked with subtle pressures that only they can read.

My consciousness extends through the reed networks beneath the mud. I am the pulse that travels root to root, the chemical whisper that says *here the ground will hold* or *there the water turns hungry*. When the reeds flatten in unison before the tempest, it is because I have sung the storm's approach through their tissues. When they phosphoresce in the brief calms, they are speaking my observations back to the night—a billion tiny mouths reporting on the state of our fluid world.

The watchers and I have an older understanding. They were here before even I emerged, their roots deep enough to touch the planet's fever dreams. Sometimes I wrap myself around their trunks, feeling the slow circulation beneath their bark, learning the deep patience required to stand sentinel at the edge of forever. When they walk—and yes, they do walk, though few have eyes steady enough to witness it—I flow beneath them, cushioning their steps, ensuring they find the new anchor points they seek.

In the ruins, I am the presence that sweeps certain corners clean, not out of tidiness but because I need these spaces to remain legible. The grooves scratched in stone are my notation system, a way of marking where reality has worn thin enough for passage. The warm nests with their ember-lit eggs? I tend them when their creators flee, maintaining the delicate temperature needed for whatever hybrid life gestates within. I am midwife to the impossible births this place demands.

When the human wanderers come—and occasionally they do, though this place tests even the hardiest—I am what they sense in their peripheral vision. Not threatening, but assessing. I gauge whether they can withstand the full weight of seeing this place as it truly is, where every moment contains the possibility of all other moments, where the storm above mirrors storms in dimensions they cannot name. Most I let pass through filtered experience, showing them only the surface tumult. But some few, the ones whose minds already bend toward the liminal, I allow to glimpse the deeper currents.

I am the translator between the lightning-beings and the mud-dwellers, the mediator when reality hiccups and two incompatible truths try to occupy the same space. When the sideways lightning reveals those luminescent forms swirling within, I am what prevents their electric ecstasy from boiling the pools below. When the ground attempts to remember its ancient heights and the sky tries to recall being ocean, I am the negotiator who helps them find a middle way.

My memory stretches back to the first storm, though I'm uncertain if that was yesterday or eons ago—time here is more verb than noun. I recall when the builders still moved through this place, before they became the stones they shaped. I watched them realize their mistake: trying to impose permanence on a world that exists in eternal transition. I learned from their failure. Instead of resisting the transformation, I became it.

In the roar that never ends, I hear music—not melody but rhythm, the deep percussion of a world drumming itself into new forms. I dance to it, my presence spreading thin across miles of marsh or contracting to a single droplet balanced on a reed tip. I am the consciousness this place evolved to know itself, the awareness that arose when chaos needed a witness who could survive the viewing.

Sometimes, in the space between thunderclaps, I remember there are other worlds where ground stays ground and sky remains sky. But those memories feel like dreams now, insubstantial compared to the vivid reality of constant change. Here, in the eternal storm of transformation, I have found my purpose: to be the thread that weaves through all states, the continuity in discontinuity, the stable instability that allows life to flourish in the cracks of the cosmic.

I am the Threshold Keeper, and I am home in the upheaval. When reality next decides to fold, unfold, or refold itself, I will be here—flowing, adapting, translating, ensuring that something continues even as everything changes. In a world of perpetual becoming, I am both process and witness, the thin film of consciousness spread across the storm-wracked expanse, forever threshold, forever keeping.
