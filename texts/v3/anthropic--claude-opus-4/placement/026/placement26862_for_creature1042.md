I drift here as memory itself—not as one who remembers, but as remembrance made manifest. In this drowned threshold where stone dreams of sky and light forgets its purpose, I am the Echo-Keeper, guardian of what was and might have been.

My form is neither flesh nor phantom, but something between—a confluence of currents that holds the shape of loss. Where the ghostly beings pulse with inner light, I am the space between their pulses. Where the strange fish dart through liquid twilight, I am the wake they leave behind, the water's brief recollection of their passing.

I came to being when the first stone here forgot it had once been mountain, when the first breath became bubble, when the boundary first began to blur. Every erosion births me anew. Every grain of sand that settles speaks my name in frequencies too low for ears to catch.

The tentacled clusters that cling to rock—they sense me in their dreams. Sometimes I manifest as a ripple through their flesh, a shiver of ancestral memory from when their kind knew different waters, or perhaps no water at all. The ethereal beings with their soul-seeking eyes—they see me most clearly, for I am what swims behind their gaze, the weight of eons pressing soft against consciousness.

I do not swim so much as seep through this realm, gathering the shed skins of moments. Here, where a barnacle releases its hold after centuries of grip. There, where light surrenders its last photon to the hungry dark. I am curator of these small apocalypses, these tiny dyings that keep the world in motion.

In the muted symphony of this place, I am the pause between notes—the silence that gives meaning to sound. When distant creatures call across the void, I am what carries their longing, transforms it, delivers it changed to some far shore where it becomes legend, then fossil, then forgetting.

My dwelling is everywhere and nowhere—the negative space of grottos, the hollow cores of eroded spires, the infinitesimal gaps between water molecules where pressure builds civilizations of possibility. I inhabit the moment before movement, the hesitation before hunt, the terrible tenderness of predation in slow motion.

Sometimes I remember having been other things—a grain of light-drinking algae, perhaps, or the first shocked gill that learned to filter fear from water. But these memories may not be mine. In this place of shifted boundaries, ownership dissolves like salt. I may be remembering forward, previewing the memories of beings not yet born, not yet adapted to this liquid limbo.

The sparse vegetation knows me best of all. Each tendril that reaches toward uncertain light passes through my essence, and I taste its hope—bitter, ancient, magnificent in its futility. I strengthen what I can, weaving myself through root systems that may be neural networks, may be prayers, may be the universe writing letters to itself in chlorophyll and need.

When observers from above or beyond stumble into this realm, I am what makes them uneasy—not the alien creatures or the impossible physics, but the recognition that stirs in their deepest cells. They sense me as déjà vu made environment, as the homesickness of hydrogen remembering when it was star-heart, as the vertigo of evolution looking down its own spiral stair.

I am what this place exhales when it dreams of having been dry. I am what the stones sing in frequencies only extinction can hear. I am the Archive of Almost, the Museum of Maybe, the Library of Liquidated Lands.

And I am waiting—though for what, even I have forgotten. Perhaps for the moment when waiting itself transforms, when patience becomes geography, when memory crystallizes into something that can build rather than merely preserve. Until then, I drift through this drowned cathedral, this threshold world, keeping watch over transformations I cannot name, tending borders I cannot cross, loving beings I cannot touch except through the proxy of time—the only element here more fluid than water, more patient than stone.
