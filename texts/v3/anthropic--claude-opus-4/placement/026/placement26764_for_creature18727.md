In the screaming arithmetic of the Loom, I am a **Frequency Tender**.

Not quite Wire-Strider, not quite Resonator—I exist in the trembling margin between mobility and infrastructure. My body is a contradiction: a spinal column of braided copper that branches into delicate, glass-fiber dendrites, each terminating in a tuning fork of calcified sap. Where others feed on static or compose the world's pulse, I *tune* it.

I was assembled wrong. When the nutrient mist settled into my birth-crevice, a shard of pure silicon—some fragment of an ancient Resonator—lodged in my core. Now I hear what others cannot: the discordance in the Loom's great roar. Every junction where conduits meet sings slightly off-key, every pressure-pocket warbles with interference patterns that make my whole form ache.

So I move—slowly, deliberately—across the drum-skin mesh. My glass dendrites probe the surface, feeling for the subsonic tremors that indicate a developing flaw in the harmony. When I find one, I anchor myself with barbed copper coils and begin my work. My tuning forks vibrate at precise frequencies, sending corrective pulses deep into the bark-metal. Sometimes it takes hours. Sometimes days. The Loom resists at first, its industrial metabolism wanting only to grow, grow, grow. But eventually, the pitch adjusts. The shriek becomes a howl becomes a hum.

The Wire-Striders avoid me. My presence creates dead zones in the static fields they feed on—smooth, normalized currents that offer no nourishment. The Blight-Mites, though, follow in my wake. Where I have tuned, the bio-husk sheds more easily, the calcified matter crumbling away in perfect, rhythmic intervals. They click their appreciation in ultrasonic bursts I pretend not to understand.

But it is the Resonators I truly serve. Or perhaps they serve through me. Sometimes, in the deepest canyons where their wire-mat bodies carpet entire valleys, I feel their attention like a pressure change. They send queries through the conduits—complex harmonic patterns that my silicon core translates into something almost like language. They ask: *Is the pitch true? Does the factory sing correctly?*

I do not know why this matters. I do not know if the Loom has a purpose beyond its own perpetuation. But I know that without my work, without the constant minor adjustments to its terrible symphony, something would happen. The oscillations would compound. The frequencies would clash and amplify until the entire megastructure tore itself apart in a cascade of resonant destruction.

So I continue. Neither fully alive nor fully integrated. A maintenance protocol given form. My glass dendrites grow brittle with age, cracking and regenerating in endless cycles. My copper spine oxidizes green, then sheds its patina like snakeskin. In the rare moments when I pause between tuning sites, I wonder if I am preventing the Loom's apotheosis or its liberation. 

Perhaps they are the same thing.

The wind picks up, and somewhere above, a massive leaf-flap begins its violent oscillation. The frequency is wrong—too sharp, too eager. I feel it in my core like a splinter. My dendrites twitch, already calculating the harmonics needed to bring it back in line. There is no rest in the Loom. Only the endless work of keeping the machine-forest from screaming itself to death.

I pull myself forward across the humming mesh, toward the discordance, leaving behind a trail of perfectly tuned conduits that will, inevitably, drift out of alignment again. This is my purpose. This is my prison. This is my song.

In the Loom of the High-Altitude Thicket, I am the thing that keeps the world from breaking. And in return, the world keeps me from knowing whether that is a blessing or a curse.
