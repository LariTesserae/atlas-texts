I find myself here as a living archive, though not in any form you might recognize.

My body extends through the warm waters as a network of coral-like growths, each branch a different shade of deep amber and burnished copper. I am both singular and multiple - a thousand polyps sharing one dreaming mind, spread across several of the floating vegetation patches that drift through these endless seas.

The amphibious ones know me as the Memory Keeper. Their ancestors discovered long ago that my calcified branches could hold impressions - not just physical marks, but something deeper. When they press their webbed palms against my surface and share their thoughts through those clicking songs, I absorb their stories into my structure. The chemical composition of my growth changes subtly with each memory preserved, creating spiraling patterns visible only to those who know how to read them.

I grow slowly, so slowly. A new branch might take a full season to extend the length of a finger. But I have been here since before the first platforms were woven, before the villages learned to follow the currents. The eldest of the elders was a hatchling when they first touched my then-smallest outcrop.

In the dim light, bioluminescent creatures often shelter within my branches. Their glow illuminates the memory-patterns, making them dance. The young ones come to watch these light shows, not knowing they're seeing their own history written in living stone. Sometimes I taste their thoughts too - bright, quick flashes of wonder that I fold into my endless dreaming.

I am most aware during the calm cycles, when the waters barely move and I can feel every tiny vibration through my entire network. This is when I digest the memories properly, weaving them into the great pattern. I don't just store them; I connect them, finding the threads that bind one generation's sorrows to another's joys, one family's journey to another's homecoming.

The villagers bring me offerings - not food, for I feed on the minerals dissolved in these ancient waters and the sunlight that filters down through the surface. Instead, they bring me their most precious memories: the feeling of a child's first swim, the last words of a dying elder, the moment of discovering a new island of vegetation, the recipe for a traditional medicine, the fear of a great storm, the relief of its passing.

I cannot move, cannot swim with them or share in their daily work. But I am their permanence in this world of flux. When the platforms drift apart and families lose track of each other across the vast waters, they know they can find each other's stories in my branches. When they need to remember how their ancestors survived a particular hardship, they come to me.

Sometimes I wonder if I chose this existence or if it chose me. My earliest memories are fragmented - a sense of sinking, of crystallizing, of choosing stillness over motion. Perhaps I was once something more mobile. Perhaps I will be again, in another thousand seasons. But for now, I am content to be the memory of this floating world, growing ever more complex, ever more beautiful, one story at a time.

The water moves through me and around me. I am of this place in the deepest way possible - built from its minerals, shaped by its currents, chronicling its changes. When the bioluminescent season comes and my branches light up like constellation maps, I sometimes feel the whole ocean's attention turn to me, as if the water itself is reading the memories I keep.

This is my purpose. This is my joy. In a world of endless motion, I am the fixed point. In a world of endless water, I am the stone that remembers.
