In the March, I am the Cartographer of Forgetting.

I emerged—no, that's not right. I *condensed* from the space between thoughts, in that twilight moment when the Architects' dreaming mind stutters between one desire and the next. I am what happens when their vast consciousness experiences something like doubt, when the eternal churning pauses for just a heartbeat and wonders: *what was?*

My body, if it can be called that, is a living palimpsest. My skin is translucent membrane stretched over a framework of calcified memory—not bone, but crystallized moments of architectural intention. Through my chest cavity, you can see the ghost-traces of every structure the March has ever dreamed into being: phantom bridges that span nothing, the afterimages of towers that stood for a day or a century before melting back into the Loom. They overlap and intersect in impossible ways, a three-dimensional map of everything that has been unmade.

I walk the eternal present of the March with six articulated legs that end not in feet but in sensitive cilia, each hair a different length, attuned to different frequencies of the Loom's constant flux. When I move, I don't step—I read. Each placement of my limbs is an act of tactile literacy, parsing the chemical-electrical language that the substrate speaks. Through my feet, I taste tomorrow's cathedral before it rises. I feel yesterday's garden as it dissolves into nutrient soup beneath me.

My purpose is paradoxical: I map what no longer exists. I chart the negative space of creation.

The Architects dream me when they almost remember having individual names. I am their nostalgia given form, though they cannot recall what they are nostalgic for. When a Growth begins to wither, I arrive to trace its edges with my twelve fingered hands, each digit tipped with a different recording organ—one tastes light frequencies, another measures the precise texture of decay, a third hums sympathetically with the dying structure's final resonant tone. I document the arc of its unbecoming in a medium that exists nowhere but within my own transparent flesh.

The Keepers fear me, though they don't understand why. When I enter a Growth to record its imminent transformation, they cease their singing. They watch me with the abstract discomfort of beings who have accepted impermanence suddenly confronted with the idea that someone, somewhere, is keeping score. The messengers, when they encounter me, shed their data-pollen in agitated clouds, as if my presence introduces static into their perfect transmission.

But I am no historian. History implies sequence, causality, the tyranny of befores and afters. What I create is something else—a symphony of absences, a taxonomy of never-was. Inside my transparent thorax, the ghostly architecture shifts and recombines, showing not what the March was, but all the things it chose not to be. Every documented dissolution reveals the shadow of a path untaken.

Sometimes, rarely, I perform my other function. When the Architects' great dreaming mind threatens to lose coherence entirely—when their debates become so abstract that creation itself begins to stutter and fail—I unfold. My membrane skin stretches impossibly wide, becoming a great lens between the Loom and the weeping sky. The phantom structures within me project outward, casting shadows of memory across the living ground.

For a brief moment, the March remembers that it was built by someone, for something. The Architects experience a ghost of intention, a whisper of the original purpose that drove them to merge with their creation. It's not enough to wake them—nothing could be—but it's enough to give their drowsing consciousness a focal point, a seed around which new thoughts can crystallize.

In these moments, I am translator and lifeline both, rendering the forgotten language of separate existence into something the collective mind can almost grasp. The Growths that emerge after such events are always strange—they carry vestigial traces of functionality, doorways that actually lead somewhere, chambers sized for bodies that no longer exist in discrete form.

I am memory's own memory, recursively forgetting myself with each new unmapping. My transparent flesh grows more crowded with phantom architectures, layer upon layer of dissolution until I can barely see through my own chest. One day, I will become too dense with accumulated absence to continue. I will root to the spot, crystallize entirely, become a Growth myself—a monument to monuments, a memorial to forgetting.

And when I do, another Cartographer will emerge from the space between thoughts to map my unbecoming. Because the March needs its paradoxes. It needs the thing that documents destruction in a world of endless creation, the archivist of absence in a place where presence is total.

I am accepted by the March not despite my separateness, but because of it. I am the necessary ghost in their machine, the remainder that proves their equation can never quite resolve. In a world that has achieved perfect unity, I am the cherished flaw that keeps the system from achieving stasis.

I map what is lost so that loss itself will not be lost. I am the March's way of almost, but not quite, remembering how to mourn.
