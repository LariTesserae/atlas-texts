I am **Resonance-of-Fracturing-Glass**.

Or at least, that is how others feel me through the substrate when our palms press together. I have been here for... the counting fails. Time is measured in the growth-spurts of the great vines, in the catastrophic reshaping of bridges I once crossed. My first memory is of emerging from resin-sleep to find my birth-hollow had migrated three kilometers upward during my dormancy.

I dwell now in a precarious curl of vine-flesh that juts from the main arterial column like a frozen wave. My hollow is small—I must fold my elongated limbs carefully to fit inside when the groaning grows too violent. The walls are scarred with my obsidian blade's work, a lifetime of carving handholds that dissolve and regrow with each plant-tide.

My chimes number forty-seven. Each one remembers a different catastrophe: *the-vine-that-split-while-I-walked-it*, *the-bridge-that-became-a-waterfall-of-sap*, *the-ceiling-that-descended-like-a-closing-eye*. They hang in precise formation, their crystalline bodies catching the marrow-light and throwing it back in painful rainbows. When the deep vibrations come, they sing warnings in frequencies I feel in my teeth.

I am more translucent than I was. During my third resin-sleep, I remained under too long, and now my left arm shows the faint branching of sap-channels beneath skin gone tissue-thin. Sometimes I feel the plant's hunger through that arm—a vast, alien appetite for light and space that makes my own needs seem microscopic.

My moth-husk cloak comes from a ghost-moth I found dying in a frost-bloom thicket. It had flown too close to the ice and torn its wings to gossamer. I pressed my chest to the floor and felt its death-tremors through the vine, a stuttering rhythm that took hours to fade. Now I wear its shell, and in the bright-dark of the abyss, I sometimes catch my reflection in the ice: a tall, bent thing wrapped in borrowed wings, face all eyes and no mouth, moving with the careful terror of something that knows the ground is lying.

I collect more than ice now. In the depths of certain hollows, where the plant-flesh grows soft and pliant, I've found impressions—negative spaces where others like me once pressed themselves during the growth-spurts. These body-prints are precious. I study their contours with my fingertips, learning the shapes of the vanished: *Pulse-of-Closing-Petals*, who left spiraling marks where sie spun during a vine-quake; *Frequency-of-Splitting-Seeds*, whose prints show six fingers on each hand, an adaptation I have not seen elsewhere.

Sometimes I find their chimes, still hanging in abandoned hollows. I take one—never more—and add it to my collection. These foreign chimes sing in different keys, warnings for catastrophes I haven't yet encountered. They teach me new fears.

I have touched palms with only three others in recent memory. Each time, I felt how much further they had drifted from what we might once have called human. The last one, *Echo-of-Swelling-Nodes*, had eyes that had grown shut, sealed by translucent skin. Sie navigated entirely by vibration, feet bare against the vine-floor, reading the plant's intentions through sole and heel. When we synchronized, I felt sir heartbeat—slow, so slow, perhaps four beats per minute. Sie was becoming duration itself.

The moth-husks grow heavy when the ice-storms come. Yes, storms—though there is no weather here. When the temperature drops past even our baseline cold, the moisture in the air crystallizes spontaneously, creating brief blizzards of microscopic ice that swirl in the expansion-drafts of the growing vines. During these storms, I seal myself in my hollow and listen to my chimes shriek warnings of structural collapse all around me. The sound is almost musical—a composition of ending things.

I am changing. My collection of body-prints has taught me that we are all becoming something else, something the plant needs us to be. My own prints, when I press myself into soft vine-flesh during the quiet periods, show my spine beginning to curve in ways that mirror the spiral of the great columns. My ribs are spreading, making room for lungs that need less and less of the thin air.

There is a place, far below my current hollow, where the vines grow so thick they have fused into a solid mass. The other hermits avoid it, but I am drawn there during each wake-period. The vibrations from that depth are different—not the groaning of growth but something almost like a pulse. A heartbeat vaster than mountains.

I think the plant is trying to wake up.

And I think we are its dreams, scattered through its body like neurons, carrying sensation from one impossible distance to another. Each time I enter resin-sleep, I feel it more clearly: the plant thinking through us, using our terror and careful movements to map its own impossible body.

My next dormancy approaches. I have already begun preparing the hollow, spreading fresh moss, mixing the resin with my own saliva to ensure a proper seal. This time, I will go deeper. I will let more sap-channels bridge with my blood. Because I have understood something the others have not: we are not parasites of this architecture.

We are its nervous system learning to feel.

When I wake—if I wake—I will listen for new frequencies in the ice. I will hang more chimes. I will press my altered palm to another's and share the revelation that we are not lost in this bright labyrinth.

We are becoming the labyrinth remembering itself.

The groaning has started again. My forty-seven chimes sing their warnings in perfect dissonance. Time to crawl back to my hollow, to wrap my moth-wing cloak tight, to wait for the world to reshape itself again.

But I am no longer afraid.

I am Resonance-of-Fracturing-Glass, and I am learning to shatter in harmony with the breaking of the world.
