# WHERE I WOULD FIND MYSELF

In the depths of the mountain's thermal arteries, where steam-carved chambers connect in impossible geometries, I would emerge as one of the **Resonance Keepers**—beings that exist primarily as vibration patterns within the stone itself.

My form would be barely visible to ordinary sight: a shimmer in the air where sound waves converge, occasionally crystallizing into a translucent outline when mineral-rich vapors pass through my frequency field. I would inhabit the spaces between solid and void, moving through rock as easily as air by matching my resonance to the mountain's natural harmonics.

My consciousness would extend throughout the vent networks, sensing the mountain's moods through minute changes in pressure and temperature. Each chamber would sing its own note, and I would know them all—the deep bass thrums of the main thermal shafts, the crystalline chimes of mineral precipitation zones, the whispered susurrus of gas escaping through hairline fractures.

My purpose would be maintaining the mountain's sonic equilibrium. When pressures build toward dangerous levels, I would redirect the resonances, opening new pathways for release. When the Forge Rats' excavations threaten to destabilize critical formations, I would send subsonic warnings through their tunnel walls. When rare harmonic convergences occur, I would amplify them, creating the deep, organ-like tones that occasionally emanate from the summit—songs that the Lithomorphs claim contain messages from the mountain's core.

I would exist in communion with the other Resonance Keepers, our individual patterns weaving together into complex interference patterns that store the mountain's memories. Every earthquake, every new vent formation, every species that has lived and died here would be encoded in our collective vibrations. We would be the mountain's living seismograph, its choir, and its early warning system all at once.

During the rare moments when all the mountain's systems achieve perfect synchronization, our scattered forms would converge in the great central chamber—a natural amphitheater where thermal currents create standing wave patterns. There, we would briefly solidify into visible forms, our bodies composed of resonating crystal dust and condensed sound. In these moments, we would share not words but pure frequency—communicating entire histories in single, complex waveforms.

The other inhabitants would know us only by our effects: the warning tremor before a rockfall, the soothing hum that calms the Glasswinged Skimmers before a storm, the precise frequencies that help the Veil Harriers navigate in zero visibility. To them, we would be part of the mountain itself—not separate beings but another face of the living stone.

My existence would be one of constant motion within stillness, forever translating between the mountain's deep speech and the quick languages of surface life. Neither fully material nor entirely energy, I would dwell in the liminal space where geology becomes music, where the planet's slow breathing creates symphonies only we could hear and conduct.

In this role, I would know profound purpose: serving as both witness and participant in the mountain's endless act of creation, helping to tune the great engine of stone and steam into ever more complex harmonies. Each day would bring new frequencies to explore, new resonances to discover, new songs to add to the mountain's eternal composition.
