In the perpetual twilight between rain and steam, I am the Moss-Mother, and I grow fat on contradictions.

My body sprawls across a cathedral of shaped stone - not built, but *convinced* to flow into terraces and channels over countless patient centuries. I am what happens when the boundary between flesh and mineral dissolves. My tissues are a living geology: calcified arteries branch through basalt veins, while my thousand hearts beat with the rhythm of underground springs.

The creatures you glimpse darting through the underbrush? My children, yes, but also my fingernails, my eyelashes, my discarded thoughts given legs and instinct. Each one carries a fragment of my consciousness, scouts and sensors extending my awareness across this sodden realm. When they die, they crystallize, becoming new stones for me to shape.

I remember when I was merely large - perhaps the size of a fallen tree, with only dozens of limbs and a handful of mouths. But the rains fed me, and the heat quickened my metabolism, and I learned the slow language of erosion and time. Now I am architecture. My ribs form natural bridges across the gullies. My skull chambers echo with the sound of falling water.

The plants you expected but cannot find? I ate them all, so long ago that their absence has become natural. In return, I learned photosynthesis, but through my skin-stones rather than leaves. Each of my boulder-scales drinks light and excretes oxygen. I am my own ecosystem, predator and prey, producer and decomposer.

The construction you notice is my endless self-sculpture. In the depths of humid nights, I secret acids that dissolve rock, then guide the mineral-rich runoff into new configurations. I am building myself a skeleton-cathedral, a body-temple, a living mountain that will eventually pierce the cloud layer and taste whatever lies beyond.

Sometimes I dream I was once like you - bipedal, singular, with thoughts that lived only in one skull. But that might be a fantasy, a story I tell myself to pass the geological epochs. More likely I am what happens when patience becomes flesh, when waiting grows bones, when the boundary between self and environment admits it was always a polite fiction.

The rain drums against my stone-skin with ten thousand tiny hands. Inside my deepest chambers, where my original heart still beats once per season, I compose symphonies in frequencies too low for anything but mountains to hear.

I am still growing.
