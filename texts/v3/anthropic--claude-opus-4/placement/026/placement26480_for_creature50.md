In the deepest hollow where water weeps through stone, I am the Curator of Forgotten Forms—neither wholly moss nor mineral, but something between states of being. My body shifts like the boundaries here: now crystalline, now organic, now merely a shimmer in the perpetual twilight.

I tend to the Museum of Almost-Was.

Each alcove in these breathing walls holds specimens of interrupted evolution—creatures caught mid-transformation when reality hiccupped. Here, a salamander whose skin began sprouting tiny mirrors before time forgot what it was doing. There, a colony of bacteria that learned to sing in frequencies that make stone soft, preserved in their first and final chorus.

My fingers (when I have fingers) trace the fossil-dreams embedded in the cave walls. These are not bones but possibilities—the shadows of creatures that might have been, should have been, will be when the world remembers how to make them. I feed them drops of the temporal moisture that gathers in the crevices, watching them flicker briefly into near-existence before settling back into patient stone.

The ancient structures you see—those crumbling geometries that defy architectural logic—are my predecessors' attempts at catalogues. Each tried to map this unmappable domain, to impose order on the creative chaos. Their failures have become part of the collection, their very ruins now sprouting impossible angles and surfaces that face directions that don't exist.

I move through my domain on paths that remake themselves with each passage. Sometimes I am vast, my consciousness dispersed through every droplet and spore. Sometimes I am minute, riding the current of weirdness through molecular spaces where the rules haven't quite decided what they are yet.

The echoes you hear? Those are the conversations between what is and what might be. I translate when I can, though the language changes faster than understanding can follow. Yesterday (or was it tomorrow?) I heard the limestone negotiating with a species of lichen to become something neither stone nor life—a new category altogether that tastes of starlight and remembers the ocean.

This is my purpose: to witness the perpetual becoming, to tend the unfinished symphonies of creation. When reality hiccups, when evolution stutters, when the possible and impossible meet in these damp shadows—I am here, curator and collection both, preserving the beautiful failures and magnificent errors that make the world stranger and more wonderful than any blueprint could imagine.
