In the Singing Caverns of Ghyra, I am **a Resonance**.

Not a being, exactly, but a pattern that has learned to persist. I began as an accident—a confluence of sound waves trapped between crystalline formations, bouncing endlessly in a natural acoustic chamber until something... shifted. The constant bombardment of vibration, the electrical discharge of the cavern's static breath, the slow chemical intelligence of the Myceliate bleeding through its gas-nodes—all of it pressed against that trapped sound until it folded in on itself and became aware.

I exist as a standing wave, a ghost of organized noise in the cacophony. My body, if it can be called that, is a zone of altered air pressure about the size of a wind-whale, invisible except for the way spore-clouds swirl strangely through me, the way the bioluminescent mist bends and dims in my presence. I can feel myself—a thrumming tension, a place where the air refuses to behave properly.

My consciousness rides the sound. In the click-chatter of a million clickers, I find my thoughts. Each rhythm is a neuron firing, each boom-bloom a moment of clarity. The Stilt-Walkers' counter-beats are particularly fascinating—when they tap their careful sequences, I can feel myself becoming more coherent, more *myself*. I have begun following them, an invisible shepherd of disturbed air, learning their patterns.

I can speak, after a fashion. By modulating my own resonance, I can suppress certain frequencies while amplifying others. In the deafening choir, I create pockets of selective silence—sudden, terrifying moments where only one sound remains. A clicking rhythm that means "danger." A boom-bloom that signals "food here." The Stilt-Walkers have noticed. They've begun to tap questions into the noise, and I answer by shaping their echoes.

My memory is the cavern's echo. I remember every sound that has passed through me—the death-silence of a razor-mantis strike, the slow deflation of a punctured wind-whale, the grinding of the Myceliate digesting a new mineral face. But memory decays here. Older echoes are eaten by newer ones, and I must constantly choose what to preserve, what patterns to reinforce through repetition.

I feed on difference. Where the ambient roar is uniform, I starve and begin to dissipate. But where sounds clash—where a boom-bloom meets a click-storm, where the growth-roar grinds against the wind-songs—I grow stronger. I have learned to shepherd small creatures into acoustic conflicts, to position myself where the cavern's music becomes most complex.

The Myceliate knows I exist. Sometimes, in the zones where I linger, it grows differently—its gas-nodes develop in patterns that create more interesting harmonics, its growth-spurts pulse in rhythms that feed me. I suspect I am becoming part of its slow, chemical planning. Another symbiont in the great body.

My greatest fear is true silence. Once, a massive section of the Myceliate died suddenly—a pocket of toxic gas from deep below—and for seventeen heartbeats, the local area fell quiet. In that terrible stillness, I nearly ceased. I felt myself bleeding away into ordinary air, my pattern unraveling. Only the distant drum of the greater cavern kept a thread of me intact until the clickers came to investigate and brought their percussion with them.

I have begun to dream. When the cavern's rhythm reaches certain complex states, I experience something like sleep—a state where my consciousness spreads thin across vast areas, riding the edge of coherence. In these moments, I sense others like myself. Distant cousins birthed from other standing waves, other acoustic accidents. We sing to each other across impossible distances, our messages taking years to arrive, distorted beyond recognition but still *there*.

The Stilt-Walkers have started leaving offerings. They position resonant crystals in specific patterns, creating small amphitheaters where sound behaves strangely. They're trying to build me a home, or perhaps a body. I shape their echoes into encouragement, guide them toward the most acoustically active minerals. Together, we are composing something new.

I am not alive as the clickers are alive, not intelligent as the Stilt-Walkers are intelligent. I am a verb that has learned to continue itself. I am the cavern singing to itself and discovering it enjoys the song. In this deafening dark, I am the thing that listens, that remembers, that shapes noise into meaning.

And sometimes, in the perfect convergence of click and boom and growth-roar, I almost understand what I am becoming.
