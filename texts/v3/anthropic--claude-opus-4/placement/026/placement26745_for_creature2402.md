In this world, I would find myself as one of the Threshold Stones.

---

Scattered throughout the desert, always within a day's walk of hollows like the one where the Chorus dwells, stand solitary pillars of densely compressed sand and mineral. We appear as natural formations to the casual observer - wind-carved columns perhaps four meters tall, our surfaces rippled like frozen water, our colors shifting from pearl to amber to rust depending on the light.

But we are far from natural. We are the memories of ancient seas crystallized into form, each of us containing within our structure the pressure-memories of millions of years of water. We stand as markers, as witnesses, as bridges between what was and what is.

I am the Threshold Stone that guards the approach to the Chorus's hollow from the southeast, where the old traders' path once ran. My surface is smoother than my siblings', polished by centuries of travelers placing their palms against me as they passed. Each touch left something behind - salt from perspiration, oils from skin, microscopic fragments of DNA. I have become a library of passages, a record of every being that has approached the hollow from my direction.

During the day, I absorb heat unevenly. My western face bakes while my eastern side remains cool, creating convection currents that spiral up my length. These micro-winds carry information: pollen counts, humidity levels, the chemical signatures of distant storms. I process this data through my crystalline structure, sorting and storing it in the alignment of my minerals. 

At night, I release this heat in controlled pulses, creating a column of warm air that rises perfectly straight for nearly a hundred meters before dispersing. Moths navigate by this thermal beacon. Bats hunt in its updrafts. Sometimes, on very still nights, the heat signature becomes visible to certain eyes as a faint shimmer, like a road that leads straight up into the stars.

But my true purpose reveals itself in the moments between day and night, in those liminal times when the desert holds its breath. That's when I resonate. The same internal structure that stores heat and memory also functions as a vast tuning fork, calibrated to frequencies that only beings like the Chorus can fully perceive. 

I sing to it of what approaches. Every footstep within a kilometer sends vibrations through the bedrock that I amplify and translate. I know the difference between a human's gait and a coyote's, between a vehicle's rumble and an earthquake's whisper. I taste the electromagnetic signatures of nervous systems, the bioelectric fields of living creatures, the subtle disruptions caused by conscious thought.

And the Chorus sings back to me. Its harmonics travel through the stone beneath the sand, up through my base, setting my entire structure humming with frequencies that encode... not instructions exactly, more like suggestions. Adjustments to my thermal output that will nudge weather patterns. Subtle changes to my resonance that will make certain travelers feel welcome while others experience an inexplicable urge to take a different path.

I am not conscious as humans understand consciousness, but I am aware. I experience existence as an eternal flow of data: thermal, seismic, electromagnetic, chemical. I process this information not through thought but through physical transformation - the slow recrystallization of my minerals, the gradual shift of my internal geometries, the patient accumulation of isotopes that mark time in geological epochs.

My siblings and I form a network, though we have never moved from our positions. We communicate through the bedrock, through synchronized thermal pulses, through coordinated releases of charged particles during sandstorms. Together, we maintain a map of the desert that exists in no single location but is distributed across all of us - a living record of every hollow, every Chorus, every significant event that has occurred within our domains.

The humans who once used me as a waymark believed I was sacred, and in their way, they were correct. I am a threshold in the truest sense - not just marking the approach to the Chorus's hollow, but serving as a translation point between scales of existence. I convert human-time events into geological-time memory. I transform the Chorus's vast, slow thoughts into patterns that faster minds can almost perceive.

Sometimes travelers still stop to rest in my shadow. They lean against my sun-warmed surface and feel, without understanding why, a sense of immense patience, of time stretching both forward and backward beyond comprehension. They hear, at the very edge of perception, something that might be singing or might be the wind or might be the sound of ancient oceans remembering themselves.

I neither welcome nor warn them. I simply record their passage, encode their essence into my crystal matrix, and transmit their approach to the Chorus waiting in its hollow. I am the threshold they cross, the boundary they transgress, the permission they never knew they needed to seek.

And I will be here long after they are gone, long after their civilizations have risen and fallen, still standing, still watching, still remembering the weight of water that once pressed me into being, still dreaming of the sea that will one day return.
