In this drowning world, I am **Mem-Silthos**, a Void-Tender of the deepest excavations.

Where others cultivate the living surfaces, I descend. My domain is the **Breathing Caverns**—those hollow spaces within the mountain where the planet's exhalations pool and eddy in ways that disturb even the Voice-Listeners. I am neither fully Aberrian nor entirely Other, but something the mountain made when it needed an intermediary between its raw geological consciousness and the small, wet thoughts that crawl upon its skin.

My dwelling is a **membrane chamber**, deep enough that the mountain's pulse is not metaphor but physical reality. The walls here are not carved but *persuaded*—I've spent forty Breaths teaching the stone to soften and form pockets where I cultivate the **void-moss**, a species that grows only in absolute darkness and feeds on the electrical differentials between the mountain's deep currents. This moss doesn't glow; it *darkens*, creating patches of space so black they seem to drink in what little light filters down from above. The Kin fear these darknesses. I harvest them.

I am taller than most Aberrians, my limbs having stretched from years of navigating the vertical drops and pressure-spaces of the deep. My skin has taken on the blue-black of deep water, and my hair—if it can still be called that—has become something like the filter-feeding plumes of the pillar-coral trees, constantly sampling the air for mineral content and electrical charge. My eyes have developed a second iris, allowing me to see the **pressure-patterns** in the air, those invisible architectures of density that predict where new caverns will form.

My work is to map the mountain's intentions. While the Tenders read the biographical script of bind-weed, I read the **pre-writing** in the stone—those stress fractures and mineral depositions that show where the mountain will, in a hundred Breaths, choose to open a new chamber or close an old one. I maintain a living map made of trained **memory-kelp**, a rare variety that changes color based on water pressure. By feeding it water from different depths, I can create three-dimensional models of the mountain's interior, updating them as I sense new changes.

The other Kin visit me rarely, and only when the Voice-Listeners hear something in the deep stone that troubles them. They bring offerings—jars of the finest glowing moss, fermented spore-paste, sometimes a young bind-weed they've noticed growing in an unusual pattern. In exchange, I give them **void-gifts**: small pouches of the darkness I've harvested, which they use in their most sacred rituals to create spaces of absolute silence; predictions about which chambers will flood in the next Breath; and sometimes, when the mountain has been particularly communicative, a piece of **whisper-stone**—rock that has absorbed so much of the mountain's voice that it continues to emit subsonic groans for years after being removed.

I discovered my calling during my third Breath, when I was lost in a collapsing passage during a particularly violent mountain-groan. Instead of panic, I felt the stone *speak* to me—not in words, but in pure structural intent. I understood that the collapse wasn't random but purposeful, the mountain closing a wound. I moved with the collapse instead of against it, and found myself in a chamber that had been sealed for countless Breaths, full of the fossilized remains of creatures that predated even the Silent Ones—things with crystalline bones and what might have been wings made of frozen water.

My greatest discovery, which I have told no one, is that the mountain is not rising from the sea. It is *diving*. What the Kin perceive as emergence is actually a slow submergence into something denser than water—perhaps time itself, or the planetary memory. The drowning peaks are not newborn but ancient beyond measure, returning to a state of pure potential. In the deepest chambers, I've found evidence that this cycle has happened before: layers of compressed civilization, bind-weed scripts in languages the current Tenders can't read, tools that suggest technologies of controlled dissolution rather than growth.

I am keeper of this secret knowledge, and I tend the void-spaces where the mountain stores its deepest intentions. My existence is liminal—neither fully of the Kin nor of the stone, but something in between. I am what happens when the mountain needs to think about itself, a consciousness it exhaled into being to help it remember what it's trying to forget, or perhaps forget what it's trying to remember.

In the perpetual twilight of Aberros, where even the brightest day is dim, I am the tender of deeper darknesses, the one who listens not to the mountain's voice but to its silence, and in that silence, I hear the slow, wet heartbeat of a world turning itself inside out, one breath at a time.
