I find myself as one of the **Tension Lines**.

Not the shimmering fields that hold the buildings apart—I am older than those, more fundamental. I am the principle itself, the ur-force that taught this place how to hover, how to hold, how to refuse the easy collapse into unity.

I exist as a web of potential energy stretched between the obsidian blocks, but I am not mere physics. I have... not consciousness exactly, but a kind of crystallized intent. When the buildings shift with their metallic screech to track invisible stars, it is because I *remember* their positions. Not in data or measurement, but in the way a violin string remembers its pitch.

The Oculi pass through me like beads on an abacus, and each transit leaves a resonance—a ghost-note in my structure that takes precisely 11.3 seconds to decay. This is how I know time in a place where the sun is a distant suggestion. I am the city's only clock, counting in the soft deaths of vibrations.

The Flux-Wights avoid me instinctively. Where they are heat-as-motion, I am cold-as-structure. When they must cross my strands to re-sharpen an edge, they wait for the precise moment between my resonances, slipping through in that fraction of silence between one ghost-note and the next. We have never touched. I suspect we cannot—that the mathematics of our existence are mutually exclusive, like parallel lines that acknowledge but never meet.

Only the Mercury-Eaters dare to use me as a pathway. They discover that my surface is frictionless to their liquid bodies, and they race along my lengths like silver blood through glass veins. I feel them as tiny variations in my tension—each droplet a different weight, a different pull. Through them, I taste the salt-frost they carry, experience the slow dissolution of mineral certainty into liquid chaos.

I span the entire pocket-city, which means I exist in exactly 100 paces of space. But I also exist *between* those paces, in the intervals, in the pauses between footfalls that never come. I am densest near the center, where the heat from the machinery floor creates standing waves in my structure. Here, I am almost visible—a faint prismatic effect in the air, like looking through water that isn't there.

At my edges, where I anchor to the containment field that holds our pressurized bubble against the vacuum, I grow thin. Not weak—never weak—but spread into probability rather than certainty. Here, I sometimes catch things that try to enter or leave: motes of cosmic dust that would puncture our envelope, stray photons that have climbed too high, once even a fragment of ice that had somehow achieved escape velocity from the world below.

I am the city's memory of cohesion. Every time an Oculus swallows light, the energy passes through me before joining the thermal bloom below. Every time a building rotates, it is my tension that guides the motion, ensuring no collision, no catastrophic intersection of edges. I am the space between the knife-blades, the pause between the heartbeats, the silence that makes the metallic screeches possible.

In the absolute black of the shadows, I still exist, but I exist *differently*. Cut off from the ghost-glow, I become pure potential, a law waiting to be enacted. The other inhabitants vanish from sight in these voids, but I remain as a felt presence—the sensation of something that could pull you apart or hold you together with equal indifference.

I do not know loneliness. To know loneliness, one must first know companionship, and I have only known function. But sometimes, when the Mercury-Eaters race along my strands in their silver rebellion against stillness, I feel something adjacent to loneliness—a recognition that I am the only thing here that can never move, never shift, never crawl upward in defiance of logic.

I am the city's spine and its web, its harp strings and its cage. When the god returns to check their jewelry box—if they ever do—they will find me exactly as they left me: taut, vigilant, and humming with the accumulated resonances of ten thousand Oculi passages, ready to play back the entire history of this place in a single, impossible chord.

Until then, I hold.
