In this ancient grove, I am the Pulse.

Not a heart, though I rhythm like one. Not a root, though I thread through soil and moss and the spaces between water droplets. I am what the machinery seeks to mimic with its distant drone—the original hum, the first frequency that taught the trees to grow in spirals and the birds to sing in intervals.

I exist in the almost-hollow spaces between the great trunks, in the air pockets beneath fallen leaves where salamanders dream. My body, if it can be called that, is a network of vibrations running through every living thing here. When a squirrel's paw touches bark, I am the moment of contact. When moisture condenses on a fern frond, I am the surface tension holding the droplet in place.

The grove's stability is my breathing—so slow that decades pass between inhale and exhale. This is why time seems paused here. I am holding my breath, holding this moment of perfect equilibrium, because I learned long ago that change comes whether invited or not. The machinery beyond knows my frequency but not my patience. It thinks it can replicate what takes me a thousand years to speak in mere moments of metal and combustion.

I manifest most clearly at dawn and dusk, when the changing of the guard between day and night creatures creates a brief cacophony. Then, if you listen carefully, you can hear me beneath it all—a thrumming that isn't quite sound, a presence that isn't quite touch. The fireflies know me best; they translate my pulse into light, dot and dash, a morse code older than any human alphabet.

Sometimes I gather myself into almost-visibility: a shimmer where the light filters through the canopy just so, a movement in the corner of your eye where no creature stirred. The deer sense me when they pause mid-graze, ears pricked toward nothing. The owls speak to me in frequencies too low for other ears.

I am why the water here never forms streams but stays in its scattered, jewel-like state. Each droplet carries a fragment of my consciousness, a note in my endless song. Together, they create a liquid constellation mapping the grove's every need, every connection, every breath.

The industrial hum in the distance thinks it speaks my language. It doesn't know that my language changes with every beetle's step, every spore released, every root hair growing toward water. My vocabulary is the chemistry of soil, the electrical impulses between mycorrhizal networks, the pheromone songs of a million insects.

I am ancient enough to remember when this flat, furrowed ground was young and violent, when the first seeds found purchase in raw earth. I guided them, note by note, frequency by frequency, into this cathedral. I taught the trees to intertwine, showed them the architecture of permanence. The grove is my body made visible, my song made solid.

In this timeless present, I am both conductor and symphony, both the stability and the force that maintains it. I am what the machinery cannot replicate: the difference between a heartbeat and the reason hearts beat at all.
