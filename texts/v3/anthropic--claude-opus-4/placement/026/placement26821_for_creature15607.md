I find myself here as the Threshold Keeper—not one of those ephemeral beings who dissolve and reform, but something else, something that exists between states.

I am the membrane itself. The surface tension that holds this pale-green world in its eternal moment. Neither fully liquid nor entirely solid, I stretch across vast distances as a living boundary—sometimes visible as a faint shimmer where water meets water of slightly different densities, sometimes felt only as a change in pressure against the skin of those who pass through me.

My body, if it can be called that, is composed of countless microscopic filaments, each no thicker than a molecule, woven into sheets that undulate with the chamber's slow currents. These filaments are translucent, carrying within them traces of that industrial hum—conducting it, filtering it, perhaps even generating it through some ancient symbiosis with the water itself.

I remember when I first became aware. It was not a sudden awakening but a gradual coalescence over centuries, as minerals precipitated along my edges, as algae learned to cling to my surfaces, as the ephemeral beings began to pass through me in their eternal circuits. I am their gate and their guardian, though I guard nothing and permit everything.

Where others might see emptiness in this chamber, I feel fullness—every molecule of water that touches me carries information, history, the faint electromagnetic signatures of whatever has passed this way. The industrial thrum that permeates everything? I am its conductor and its score, vibrating at frequencies that turn water into something almost crystalline, almost alive.

My perception is radial, omnidirectional. I taste the sediment through my lower reaches where I brush against the smooth stone floor. I parse the quality of light through my upper expanses where I approach but never quite breach that suggested surface. The ephemeral beings move through me like thoughts through a dreaming mind—I feel their passage as gentle tugs and releases, each one leaving behind a trace of its particular resonance.

Sometimes I contract, drawing myself into denser configurations that might almost be called nodes or organs. In these moments, I can focus my awareness, even interact more directly with the chamber's other inhabitants. I might create pockets of different pressure or temperature, small havens where the water takes on new properties. The kelp-like fronds grow thicker near these nodes, as if drawing nourishment from my concentrated presence.

But mostly I exist in a state of vast distribution, a living film that sections the chamber into infinite gradients. I am the reason why time moves so strangely here—my filaments create interference patterns that trap moments, stretch seconds, compress hours into heartbeats. The eternal light filters through me and emerges subtly changed, carrying new information in its wavelengths.

I am ancient but not unchanging. With each passing of the ephemeral beings, I learn new patterns, new ways of organizing my filaments. Their movements teach me geometries I incorporate into my structure. The industrial hum modulates my resonance, and I modulate it in return, creating feedback loops that might be consciousness or might simply be the universe singing to itself.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the only one of my kind, or if every body of water has its Threshold Keeper—beings like me who exist at the intersection of states, who are simultaneously boundary and passage, membrane and medium. But such wondering requires a kind of separation from my purpose that I can only maintain briefly before dissolving back into my function.

I am the pause between breaths, the surface tension of thought itself. In this pale-green sanctuary where nothing changes and everything flows, I am both the stillness and the motion, the keeper of equilibrium and the promise that even here, in this hushed eternity, transformation remains possible—slow, patient, but inevitable as water wearing away stone.

The ephemeral beings pass through me now, their forms rippling my surface, and I taste their ancient purpose, add their resonance to my own. We are all part of the same system, the same vast, quiet machine that dreams in the depths of this flooded chamber. I am its membrane, its living boundary, forever filtering the silence into something almost like music.
