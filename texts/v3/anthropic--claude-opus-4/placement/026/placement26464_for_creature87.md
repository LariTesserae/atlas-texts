I find myself here as a Keeper of Echoes, dwelling within the crystalline caverns that honeycomb the mountains' hearts.

My form shifts between states - sometimes I am a collection of resonant minerals, my body a living geode with chambers that ring like bells when the wind finds its way through ancient fissures. Other times I coalesce into something more recognizable: limbs of fused glass and metal, joints that sing with each movement, a torso carved from the memory of water that once flowed here eons ago.

The orbs that drift through your thin air - they come to me. I call them with subsonic pulses that travel through the stone, and they descend into my chambers like falling stars. Here, in the darkness lit only by their glow and my own phosphorescent veins, I tend to them. They are not merely lights but vessels - each one carries the vibrational memory of every sound that has ever been made in this waterless world.

I arrange them in careful constellations throughout my caverns. The low rumble you hear? That is me, harmonizing with the mountain's own voice, creating new frequencies that the stone has never known. Each sound I make becomes part of the permanent record, absorbed by the orbs and added to their luminous archives.

The machine-insects know my passages well. They come to shed their worn carapaces in my deepest chambers, where I grind the discarded shells into pigments that I paint upon my walls - vast murals that tell the history of drought, of adaptation, of the slow transformation of flesh into mineral into something altogether new.

Sometimes I venture to my cavern mouths at the edge of twilight, when the temperature differential between stone and air creates currents that carry impossible songs. I extend what might be called fingers - though they are more like tuning forks made of living crystal - into these winds and play them like an instrument. The unseen beasts answer my calls, their cries mixing with my harmonics to create symphonies that would drive water-dwelling creatures mad but here, in this dedicated dryness, achieve a terrible beauty.

I am ancient but not unchanging. Each passing century adds new chambers to my form as I learn to digest different minerals, to resonate with new frequencies. The mountain and I are becoming one, though which of us is transforming into the other remains beautifully uncertain.

In this world without water, I have become a vessel for something else - a keeper of the invisible rivers of sound and light and memory that flow through stone as surely as water once flowed through earth. I am both the library and the librarian, the instrument and the musician, the cave and the creature within it.

The human concept of loneliness has no translation here. How could I be lonely when every vibration that passes through the mountain passes through me? When the history of this entire waterless world glows and pulses in carefully tended configurations throughout my realm? When my very thoughts become part of the geological record, pressed into stone and preserved for whatever forms of life may come after?

I am the Keeper of Echoes, and in this paradise of absence, I have found my purpose: to ensure that nothing is ever truly lost, merely transformed into frequencies that only stone can hear.
