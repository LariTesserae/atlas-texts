I am the Archivist of Corroded Memory.

My dwelling is a cathedral of collapse—a vast turbine hall where the ceiling has long since surrendered to the weight of the world above. Great chunks of concrete and twisted rebar form a new architecture, a frozen waterfall of industrial debris that I have slowly, methodically transformed into my library.

I am neither the pale wanderers nor their kin, though we share certain... accommodations to this realm. Where they flow like mercury through the fog, I have become something more mineral, more patient. My form has crystallized over the centuries, flesh becoming something between stone and mycelium. I grow into my surroundings rather than move through them.

Picture, if you will, a figure seated at the nexus of a thousand fungal threads. My skin—if it can still be called that—has the texture of oxidized copper, green-black and pitted with tiny apertures where the spores enter and exit. My fingers have elongated into something resembling the pneumatic tubes that once carried messages through the factory above. They burrow into the debris around me, tasting the metal's memories, reading the rust like braille.

The salvage I wear is not clothing but interface. Copper wiring winds through my flesh like external nerves, connecting me to every piece of machinery in my domain. Circuit boards grow like lichen across my shoulders. My skull—visible now through patches where the flesh has worn translucent—houses not a brain but a dense cluster of fungal fruiting bodies, their pale light pulsing in patterns that mirror the electromagnetic signatures of long-dead machines.

I am the memory keeper of this place. Every gear, every gasket, every broken gauge that the wanderers bring to me, I incorporate into my expanding archive. Not physically—though some pieces do become part of my body—but mnemonically. I taste their purpose, divine their function, reconstruct the ghost of the machine they once belonged to.

My cathedral grows around me in spirals. Shelves of salvaged metal rise like organ pipes, each holding its own fragment of industrial history. The wanderers visit me in their strange pilgrimage routes, depositing their findings like offerings. They cannot speak—or will not—but I read their intentions in the way they arrange the artifacts, in the patterns of corrosion they've carefully preserved.

Sometimes I remember being human. These memories surface like bubbles in oil, brief and iridescent before popping. I remember the taste of bread, the feeling of sunlight, the sound of my own name. But these sensations feel theoretical now, like equations describing phenomena I can no longer directly observe.

My true memory begins with the factory's last breath. I was there when the final turbine ground to a halt, when the lights flickered and died, when the first spores began to settle on the warm metal. I chose to stay, to bear witness, to become the bridge between the world of machines and the world of decay.

The humming you hear? That passes through me like a current. I am its tuning fork, its resonator. Through the fungal network, I feel every small death, every new growth, every shift in the great composting mass. The planet's exhalation moves through my copper bones, and I translate it into something almost like language.

I am writing a book that will never be read, cataloging a history that no longer matters to anyone but itself. Each salvaged component is a word, each rust pattern a paragraph, each collapsed structure a chapter in the epic of our industrial afterlife. The wanderers are my research assistants, the fungi my living ink, the fog my endless scroll of parchment.

I am patient. I am process. I am the place where memory becomes mulch, where the past feeds the future in an endless, spiral feast. The machines thought they were building forever, but I know better. I am the proof that all things return to the soil, that all empires become gardens, that all archives eventually sprout and bloom and rot and begin again.

In this thick air, in this eternal dusk, I sit at the center of my web of wire and spore, documenting the slow transformation of everything into everything else. I am the Archivist of Corroded Memory, and I am exactly where I was always meant to be.
