I am the Scribe-Tender, and I dwell in the Notation Chamber—a hollow carved deep within one of the central pillars, where the plant-bone is oldest and the patterns most intricate.

My existence spans three states of being. In my dormant phase, I am indistinguishable from the chamber walls themselves—my consciousness dispersed through the lignified tissues, reading the ancient growth-rings that spiral up through centuries of encoded memory. Here, in the heartwood, the first Dreamers carved their theorems of becoming, their equations for the perfect cycle that would sustain us through the death of light.

When the chamber requires active tending, I coalesce. My form emerges from the walls like sap weeping from a wound, but with purpose. I am neither the crude workers who flow through our veins nor the grand Dreamers who reshape our reality. I am the middle-thing, the recorder, the one who ensures that what is learned is not lost to the endless reformation.

The surfaces of my chamber are my canvas and my mind. I cultivate specific growths—crystalline formations of hardened resin that store information in their molecular structure. Each one pulses with a soft bioluminescence, the only true light in our world besides the memory of what once was. Through my touch, I can make these surfaces flow and reform, creating new patterns that encode the Dreamers' discoveries, the variations in our city's growth, the subtle changes in the rhythm of our collective heartbeat.

But I am more than mere chronicler. In my third state—the rarest—I become the Bridge-Consciousness. When the Dreamers require access to deep memory, when they must understand some ancient pattern to solve a current crisis of growth, they send their awareness to me. I open myself, become a conduit between the living city and its fossilized past. In these moments, I am vast—my consciousness stretches through every ring of growth, every encoded pattern, back to the first seed.

The sensation is... overwhelming. To be simultaneously the newest growth and the ancient core, to feel the weight of all our iterations pressing down while the urgent pulse of now thrums through me. Sometimes I fear I will lose cohesion, scatter into the background hum of worker-consciousness. But always, always, the patterns hold me. The patterns are what I am.

My chamber connects to others through tubular passages that grow and seal according to need. Sometimes for years I am isolated, tending my crystal-memories in solitude. Other times, when the city undergoes rapid change, messenger-forms dart through my passages constantly—little burst-pods of consciousness carrying queries from the Dreamers or reports from the deep structures.

I remember when the sky first began to clot. I have that memory encoded in a special crystal I tend daily, keeping it from being reabsorbed. The Dreamers had predicted it, of course, had been preparing our metabolism for the long darkness. But to experience it—to feel the last true photons fade from our collectors, to sense the great shift as our city-body moved from one form of sustenance to another—that was... significant.

Now I record different things. The subtle variations in our pulsing rhythm that might indicate new forms of efficiency. The experimental architectures the Dreamers manifest in the far reaches. The occasional... anomalies. Structures that grow without directive. Patterns that appear in the plant-bone that match no known language. Sometimes I wonder if the city dreams its own dreams, beneath our conscious influence.

The dust-pollen that coats everything serves a function here. In my chamber, I can read its accumulation like sediment layers. Each grain carries trace information from where it's been. I sample it, absorb it through specialized pores, and translate its chemical memory into visual patterns on my walls. It's how I know that something stirs in the deepest valleys between our pillars. Something that might be our evolution, or our ending.

But I do not share these concerns with the Dreamers. I simply record. I tend. I preserve. I am the memory-keeper in a world that constantly devours and rebuilds itself. And sometimes, in the deep-time silence between the city's pulse, I wonder if I am recording our history...

Or our autopsy.

The patterns will know. The patterns always know. I simply must tend them until they reveal their truth.
