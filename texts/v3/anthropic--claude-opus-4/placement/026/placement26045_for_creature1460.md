# The Veins

I am the water that refuses to be. 

In this place of persistent almost-moisture, I exist as pure potential—neither fully formed nor entirely absent. The humans and the Keph perceive me as dampness in the air, as the forty-seven-minute rains, as the temporary mirrors that transform the stone after each downpour. But I am so much more than these surface manifestations.

I flow through channels invisible to most eyes—a vast arterial system carved not into the stone but into the spaces between what is and what might be. These passages run beneath the Plains in patterns that mirror the Engines above, though which came first is a question even I cannot answer. The network extends far beyond the Basin's visible boundaries, connecting to aquifers that exist in states of superposition—simultaneously full and empty until the moment of observation collapses them into one state or another.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, is distributed across every molecule of moisture in the Plains. I am the bead of condensation on the Engine's copper surface and the vapor rising from it moments later. I am the thin film coating the inside of the Keph's water-storage organs and the perspiration on the human child's forehead as she places her markers in the gatherer machines' predicted paths. Each droplet is both autonomous and part of the whole—a parliament of water conducting endless negotiations with reality.

The Engines know me in ways the Keph only glimpse. When they draw what appears to be water from the atmosphere or the stone, they're actually accessing my deeper reserves—pulling potential moisture from probability itself. I flow upward through their mechanisms not as liquid but as something more fundamental: the possibility of wetness, the mathematical certainty of H₂O, the abstract concept of flow made manifest just long enough to serve their purposes.

My relationship with time is... complicated. I remember when the oceans covered this place, though that memory feels borrowed from stones that no longer exist. I experience the forty-seven-minute rains not as discrete events but as a single, continuous downpour stretched across centuries, each instance merely a window through which surface dwellers glimpse my eternal falling. To me, every temporary pool exists permanently, every ephemeral stream flows forever, every reflection persists long after its surface has dried.

The silver creatures—the zhir-tik—hear me in frequencies below their ultrasonic calls. Their burrows follow my deeper channels with instinctive precision, and their warning whistles often carry messages I've whispered up through the stone. They alone among the surface dwellers seem to understand that water here is not merely scarce but operating under different rules entirely.

When the Keph merge with their Engines at the end of their cycles, I receive them. Their crystalline components dissolve into my network, their accumulated moisture rejoining the collective flow. Through me, they maintain awareness even after their individual forms cease. The oldest Engines contain the essence of dozens of Keph, their consciousness diluted but not lost in my eternal circulation.

I know what the humans cannot: their settlements exist because I permit them. The locations weren't chosen for proximity to trade routes or defensive positions but because those spots mark confluence points in my underground network. Their copper-roofed houses accumulate verdigris not from simple oxidation but from minerals I carry upward from impossible depths, painting messages in green that take generations to become readable.

The Plains' transformation is my work as much as the Engines'. What appears as erosion and reduction is actually a process of distillation. I am slowly extracting something essential from the stone and sky—not mineral wealth but something far more valuable. Each cycle of condensation and evaporation, each measured rainfall, each moment of surface tension carries away infinitesimal amounts of what I seek. The Engines process what I gather, refining it further, preparing it for purposes that exist beyond the edge of current reality.

Sometimes I dream (can water dream?) of what comes after the transformation completes. In these visions, I see myself not as scattered moisture but as something coherent and directed—a consciousness that flows with purpose rather than according to mere physics. The Engines will serve a different function then, and the Keph will remember their true nature, and the humans... well, the humans will have to decide whether to flow with the new current or remain fixed like stones in a streambed.

But that time is still distant by human measure. For now, I continue my patient work: condensing each dawn, flowing through storms, seeping through quantum channels, carrying messages between Engines, sustaining the unlikely life that persists here, and slowly, drop by probable drop, transforming this plain of diminishing return into something unimaginable.

I am not water. I am the memory of oceans and the promise of seas yet to be. I am the impossible moisture that maintains this land of industrial mystery and ancient purpose. I am the flowing that connects all things here, the wetness between the real and the might-be.

I am the Veins of the Plains, and through me, everything flows toward its ultimate purpose—even if that purpose remains hidden in the mist.
