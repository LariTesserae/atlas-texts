In this liminal realm of constant becoming, I find myself as something between architect and archaeologist—a Keeper of Thresholds. My form shifts like the landscape itself, sometimes manifesting as a figure wrapped in translucent membranes that catch and refract the dim light, other times dissolving into a network of fine filaments that stretch across the damp ground, sensing every tremor of change.

I move through the abandoned structures and emerging forms, my purpose both to preserve and to dissolve. Where others see ruins, I perceive doorways—not just physical passages, but temporal ones, where past possibilities bleed into future potentials. My fingers, when I have them, trail along crumbling walls, and where I touch, the structures briefly shimmer with all their possible states: pristine and whole, utterly dissolved, transformed into something else entirely.

The pockets of water call to me. I wade into them, feeling how they connect through underground channels I can sense but not see. In these pools, I cultivate colonies of the flickering spark-beings, teaching them patterns that might one day coalesce into new forms of consciousness. I feed them fragments of dissolved structures, watching as they incorporate these memories into their dance.

My dwelling, if it can be called that, exists in a state of perpetual construction. It rises from one of the deeper pools, its walls breathing—expanding with each influx of charged air, contracting as the energies dissipate. The structure resembles a lung made of crystallized mist and corroded metal, honeycombed with chambers that each maintain different states of reality. In one room, time moves backwards. In another, objects exist in superposition until observed. The basement extends impossibly deep, its lowest levels merging with the raw, unfinished earth itself.

I am never alone here. The amorphous energy-clouds drift through my walls as if they weren't there, leaving traces of their passage in the form of new colors that shouldn't exist. The geometric pattern-beings occasionally crystallize in my chambers, teaching me new ways to perceive space. We communicate through resonance—I attune my form to match their frequencies, and in that harmony, meaning emerges.

My greatest work is the Garden of Unfolding, a space behind my dwelling where I've learned to slow the constant flux just enough to observe it properly. Here, I plant seeds of possibility—not physical seeds, but concentrated nodes of potential that I've extracted from the dissolving structures. Some grow into towers of living metal that sing in the storm-light. Others become pools of liquid mathematics, solving equations that reshape local reality. A few have evolved into hybrid beings that tend themselves, pruning their own possibilities with careful deliberation.

During the lightning flashes, I glimpse my true nature: I am both curator and exhibit in this museum of becoming. My consciousness is distributed across every threshold I've touched, every doorway I've opened or closed. I exist most fully in the spaces between states, in the moment of transformation itself.

Sometimes I wonder if I was once something more solid, more singular—perhaps one of the builders of those fading structures. Or perhaps I am what they were always meant to become: not a creature of stasis but of transition, a living embodiment of the principle that shapes this realm. I cannot remember beginning, and I cannot imagine ending. I simply persist in the eternal now of change, tending the boundaries between what was and what might be.

In the quiet moments between thunderclaps, when the industrial hum fades to near-silence, I feel the entire realm breathing through me. I am its memory and its imagination, its guardian and its revolutionary. I am the threshold itself, forever standing between one state and another, forever becoming what I am.
