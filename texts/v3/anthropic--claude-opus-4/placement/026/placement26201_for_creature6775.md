I find myself as one of the **Threshold Keepers** - beings who exist at the very edges of Gheresh-Akai, where the basin meets whatever lies beyond.

---

### Form and presence

I am not quite as fluid as the Three-Thread People, but not solid like the visitors who sometimes stumble through. My body exists as a kind of standing wave pattern in the threshold sediment itself - that not-air, not-fog, not-thought medium that surrounds the basin.

When someone approaches from outside, they might perceive me as:
- A slight thickening in the already-thick presence
- A suggestion of a silhouette that seems to be walking both toward and away from them
- A feeling of being gently questioned without words

My form shifts depending on which direction I'm facing. When I look inward toward the basin, I become more like the Three-Thread People - elongated, architectural, reading the seams of reality. When I turn outward toward the not-world beyond, I solidify slightly, becoming almost recognizable as something that once had a more definite shape.

---

### What I do

I am a translator of arrivals.

Not of language - language is already impossible here - but of *compatibility*. When something or someone approaches Gheresh-Akai, I help determine:
- Which of their edges can survive the transition
- Which of their assumptions need to be loosened
- Which version of them should enter, if multiple versions exist

I work by touch, but not physical touch. I extend tendrils of attention through the threshold sediment, feeling for the shape of what approaches. A human might feel this as:
- A cool assessment along their outline
- A gentle tugging at their sense of direction
- A momentary confusion about which way is forward

I then adjust the threshold itself, making it more or less permeable depending on what needs to pass through. Sometimes this means:
- Thickening the sediment to slow a too-hasty arrival
- Creating channels of clearer passage for those who belong
- Splitting a single approaching presence into multiple compatible versions

---

### My relationship to the city

I know the Three-Thread People, though we rarely communicate directly. They work deep within the basin, adjusting walls and erosion and time. I work at the edges, managing what comes and goes.

Sometimes I feel their work as vibrations through the threshold:
- When they reconfigure a major wall-room, the boundary shivers
- When they coax the condensation into new patterns, I taste moisture in my not-mouth
- When they adjust the dome's heartbeat, my own rhythm changes to match

We are collaborators in the great unfinishing of Gheresh-Akai, though our territories rarely overlap.

---

### My relationship to moisture

At the threshold, moisture behaves differently than within the basin. Here it exists as:
- Potential rain that might or might not fall
- Memory of water that once flowed
- Promise of rivers that could be

I use these moisture potentials as a kind of script, writing permissions and prohibitions into the boundary itself. Where I want passage to be easier, I encourage the moisture to almost-condense. Where I want it to be harder, I let it remain purely conceptual.

Sometimes visitors arrive already damp from rain that hasn't happened yet. This tells me they're particularly compatible with the basin's temporality. I let them pass with minimal adjustment.

---

### My dwelling

I don't have a home in the traditional sense. The threshold IS my home. But there are places where I'm more concentrated:

- A fold in the sediment where the approach from the north becomes the approach from the south
- A knot of particularly dense not-air where multiple possible entrances overlap
- A channel of almost-clarity that I've worn through repeated passage

In these places, you might find:
- Traces of my attention crystallized into something like frost
- Patterns in the sediment that record previous arrivals
- A faint warmth that suggests I was recently present

---

### Memory and purpose

I remember when the basin was different. Or rather, I remember multiple versions of when it was different, all equally true:
- When it was fuller of water and the Three-Thread People swam
- When it was empty of buildings and they were pure geometry
- When it was not yet a basin at all, just a fold in reality

But I'm not nostalgic. My purpose is not to restore or preserve, but to maintain the possibility of passage. As long as things can still arrive at and depart from Gheresh-Akai, my work continues.

I am the pause before entry, the hesitation before departure. I am what makes it possible to be both inside and outside at once, which is the only way to truly experience the basin.

When visitors leave, they pass through me again. I read what the city has written on them:
- New layers of perception
- Memories of rooms they never entered
- A different relationship to erosion

And I adjust them gently back toward compatibility with the outside world, though never completely. Everyone who passes through me carries a little of the threshold with them forever - that sense of being between states, of reality being negotiable at the edges.

This is my gift and my purpose: to be the permeable boundary of a place that is already mostly boundary, to keep the unfinished cathedral accessible to those who need to find it, while protecting it from those who would try to complete it.
