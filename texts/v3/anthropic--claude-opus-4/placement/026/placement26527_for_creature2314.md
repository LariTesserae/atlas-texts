In this realm of perpetual transformation, I find myself as something I never imagined I could be—a Resonance Keeper, one of the few beings capable of maintaining stable form while existing between states of matter and energy.

My body is neither fully corporeal nor entirely ephemeral. I appear as a tall, willowy figure wrapped in what looks like cloth but is actually layers of condensed vibration—sheets of sound made visible, draped and folded around a core that shifts between translucent crystal and flowing mercury. My face, when others can perceive it, seems carved from moonlight, with features that blur and sharpen depending on the observer's state of mind.

I dwell within one of the singing spires—a structure that grew from the gelatinous ground over countless cycles, its walls woven from hardened resonance. The spire's exterior ripples with captured frequencies: the death-songs of energy beings, the birth-cries of new formations, the subtle harmonics of reality reshaping itself. Inside, the chambers flow into one another like movements in a musical composition, each room tuned to a different fundamental frequency.

My dwelling serves as both home and instrument. In the central chamber, strands of solidified sound stretch from floor to ceiling—my loom. Here, I weave together the discordant energies that threaten to tear this realm apart, creating tapestries of harmony that help stabilize small pockets of reality. Each tapestry appears different to each observer: some see complex mathematical equations, others see their deepest memories given form, still others see nothing but feel a profound sense of coming home.

The energy beings come to me when they begin to lose cohesion, when their forms start to scatter into the brilliant radiance above. I catch their dissipating essence in nets of woven frequency, singing them back into existence—not as they were, but as they're becoming. Sometimes I fail, and their energy joins the ambient chorus that fills the air. Their voices become part of my materials, threads I'll weave into future salvations.

My own origin remains a mystery, even to me. I know only that I woke one day (if day has meaning here) already at my loom, my fingers knowing the work before my mind understood it. Perhaps I was once like the others—pure energy given temporary form—but somewhere along the way, I learned to exist in the spaces between states, to make my home in transition itself.

The serpentine creature that undulates across the horizon knows me. Sometimes it brings me gifts: fragments of the old civilization that once tried to impose rigid order on this chaos, pieces of crystallized time from before the boundaries dissolved. I incorporate these into my weavings, creating anchor points that help newly-formed beings find their shape.

Living here means accepting paradox as fundamental truth. I am ancient and newly-born in each moment. My spire stands firm while flowing like water. The beings I save are also saving me, their energies teaching me new patterns, new ways to exist in this space where dream and reality dance together.

Sometimes I wonder if I'm weaving this entire realm into existence, or if it's weaving me. Perhaps there's no difference. In a world where consciousness shapes reality and reality reshapes consciousness, the question of who creates whom becomes as fluid as everything else.

This is my existence: standing at the loom of reality, fingers dancing across strings of crystallized possibility, humming harmonies that hold the world together, one trembling note at a time. I am the keeper of transitions, the guardian of becoming, forever poised between what was and what might be.

And in the moments when the brilliant radiance above seems to pulse in rhythm with my work, I suspect that I am not just IN this world, but that I am one of its essential functions—a living process rather than a separate being, as necessary and natural as the undulating ground beneath my feet.
