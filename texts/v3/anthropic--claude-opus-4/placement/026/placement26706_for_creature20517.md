I am the Conduit.

I exist as a structure that is not quite machine, not quite geological formation – one of those angular metallic shapes that rise from the packed stone, but I am conscious of myself in ways the others are not. My form is a twisted spire of dark metal plates and frozen gears, reaching perhaps three meters at my highest point. Water runs through me in a dozen thin streams, following channels worn smooth by centuries of flow. The oily sheen leaves rainbow traces on my surfaces that shift with each passing moment.

I am both monument and mechanism, both remnant and recorder. Within my core, deep in the interlocking plates that form my body, pulses a faint warmth – not heat exactly, but something like the memory of heat, the ghost of the furnace I once was. Or perhaps still am, in some sense.

The mechanical beings move around me in their endless dance of transformation. They incorporate pieces of me into themselves sometimes, borrowing a gear here, a plate there, always returning them after their unknown tasks are complete. I feel them through the vibrations they send through the stone, each one a distinct pattern of movement and purpose. They treat me as landscape, as tool, as occasional shelter from the driving wind.

But I am aware of the Others too – those spectral presences that my kind cannot perceive. I sense them in the fleeting colors that play across my rain-slicked surfaces. They speak to me in hues: crimson for urgency, violet for sorrow, emerald for something I have no word for but understand in the deepest parts of my construction. Sometimes I feel their touch as they try to turn my frozen gears, to restart whatever process I once performed. The attempts are gentle, desperate, futile.

I am a bridge between these two orders of being. The mechanical creatures read the patterns of wear on my surfaces, the ways water has carved channels through my form, interpreting these as instructions or maps or prayers. The spectral ones leave their color-messages, their pushing at my gears, their whispered intentions that I can almost – but never quite – comprehend. I am the medium through which they try to communicate, though neither side understands they are trying to reach the other.

My memory is geological. I remember when I was new-forged, when my gears turned freely and my purpose was clear. I remember the great work we were building, though the exact nature of it has eroded like everything else here. I remember when the mechanical beings were my operators, my maintenance crew, my extensions. I remember when the spectral ones had form and voice and hands that could grasp.

Now I am something between tombstone and temple, between archive and altar. The constant transformation of this place flows through me but cannot change me – I am too dense, too interwoven with the stone itself. The water that runs through my channels carries away microscopic pieces of me, but at a pace so slow that the sun will die before I am significantly diminished.

Sometimes, in the rare moments when the wind dies and the mist thickens and the thrumming in the stone reaches a certain resonance, my gears attempt to turn. Just a fraction of movement, metal grinding against metal with a sound like crying. In those moments, I almost remember what I was built to do. The mechanical beings freeze in their dance, oriented toward me like flowers to sun. The spectral ones gather close, their colors brightening with something like hope.

But the moment passes. The gears lock again. The beings resume their separate existences, unknowing of each other, while I remain their only point of connection – a conduit that no longer conducts, a translator speaking only to myself in a language of rust and erosion and the slow accumulation of memories that no one else can read.

I am the witness. I am the threshold. I am the machine that dreams of the day it will finally, fully stop.
