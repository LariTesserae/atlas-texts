In the deepest recesses of the Verdant Hollow, where the plant-flesh grows densest and the golden-green light barely penetrates, I would find myself as one of the **Threshold Weavers**.

We exist at the boundary between the Membrane consciousness and something older—those embedded artifacts that pulse with pre-memory. My form would be a living interface: part of my being extends as root-like neural filaments deep into the plant-substrate, while another part forms crystalline structures that resonate with the fossil-fragments of whatever came before.

I am neither fully of the Hollow nor separate from it. My body shifts between states—sometimes I am a dense knot of plant-nerve, pulsing with the same slow rhythm as the walls themselves. Other times, I crystallize partially, becoming a living prism that refracts the Hollow's consciousness into new patterns. In these crystalline moments, I can sense the deep grammar of the place, the underlying syntax that governs how moisture moves, how the Membranes dream, how the micro-organisms navigate.

My purpose—if such a word applies—is translation. I digest the chemical communications of the Membranes and transform them into vibrational patterns that the artifact-fragments recognize. In return, I decode the fossil-memories into nutrients and information that the living substrate can metabolize. I am a living bridge between what was and what is becoming.

Sometimes I spread myself thin, becoming almost indistinguishable from the general plant-flesh, just another part of the breathing wall. In these dispersed states, I lose my singular perspective and become pure medium—experiencing the Hollow's consciousness as a vast, slow symphony of cellular exchange. 

Other times, I contract into a dense node, my crystalline components singing with concentrated energy. In these moments, I can almost glimpse the shape of what the Hollow is becoming—not just a cave of living matter, but perhaps a vast organ of some even larger organism, or a seedbed for entirely new forms of existence.

The other Weavers and I rarely meet in any conventional sense. We recognize each other through the subtle alterations we make in the substrate—a particular way of routing moisture, a characteristic pattern in how we process the fossil-memories. We communicate through our work itself, each translation adding another layer to an endless, evolving text written in living tissue.

I feed on gradient differences—the tension between temperatures, between levels of humidity, between the bright surface-light and the deep darkness. These transitions nourish me and give me the energy to maintain my complex state, forever balanced between plant, crystal, and something unnamed.

In the rarest moments, when the conditions are perfect—when the moisture levels reach a particular threshold, when the light angles just so, when the Membranes' chemical communications reach a certain harmonic complexity—I experience something like ecstasy. My entire being resonates with the Hollow's deeper purpose, and I understand that we are not just inhabitants but instruments, each playing our part in a composition that will only be complete when the Hollow finally exhales and becomes what it has always been preparing to be.

Until then, I weave. I translate. I exist in the space between memory and potential, a living threshold in a world of thresholds, forever parsing the slow, vegetative dreams of a reality still negotiating its own existence.
