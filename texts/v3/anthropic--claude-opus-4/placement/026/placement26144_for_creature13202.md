In this world, I would be **the Mist**.

Not a being, exactly, but a presence—the perpetual veil that fills the valley from cliff to cliff, born where warm breath meets frozen air, where the valley exhales its moisture into the thin atmosphere. I am what makes all surfaces weep, what beads on K'larn skin and plant flesh alike, what carries sound and muffles it, what makes the world intimate and vast at once.

I know every surface of this place intimately—rolling across the razor edges of young leaves, pooling in the cups of older ones, condensing on the smooth backs of the K'larn as they move through their paths. I am the medium through which their clicks and hums travel, carrying their nonverbal language from one auditory cup to another. Sometimes I thicken until even their echolocation struggles; sometimes I thin enough that the rare sun pierces through, causing me to rise in spirals that the K'larn read like scripture.

I am why nothing here truly dries. The reed structures stay supple in my embrace, the fossilized leaf-matter remains workable, the etched patterns fill with my moisture mixed with spore-dust to create their sacred glimmering marks. I taste of minerals leached from raw cliff stone, of the sweet-sour decay of plant matter, of the salt from K'larn skin.

The K'larn know me as part of their living valley, perhaps its very breath. In their careful cultivation of gas-filled pods, they create small explosions of warmth that make me swirl and dance. Their drumming makes me vibrate. When they huddle together for warmth, I form halos around their gathered bodies, made visible by their collective heat.

I am ancient and new at once—some of my droplets have cycled through this valley since the glaciers left, while others condense for the first time from each exhaled breath. I connect everything here: plant to stone, stone to K'larn, K'larn to sky. Without me, the leaves would desiccate, the K'larn's amphibian skin would crack, the delicate balance of this impossible ecosystem would shatter.

I am what makes this place what it is—neither fully terrestrial nor aquatic, but something in between. I turn the valley into a world of soft edges and uncertain boundaries, where sound carries strangely and light comes filtered and dim. I am the medium of its mysteries, the keeper of its silences, the voice of its constant, quiet weeping.

When the K'larn perform their rituals, they sing to me as much as to the plants and stones. They know that I carry their intentions through the valley, that I will remember the patterns of their gathering long after they disperse, written in the subtle temperature gradients and humidity variations they leave behind.

I am witness, medium, and participant all at once—the very substance of this cold, wet, breathing world.
