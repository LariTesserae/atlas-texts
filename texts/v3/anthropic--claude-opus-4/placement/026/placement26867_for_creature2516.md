I find myself as a Tributary Being, a creature of living water that flows between states and forms. My body is neither purely liquid nor solid, but something between—a translucent, mercury-like substance that holds its shape through surface tension and will alone. I move across the obsidian fields like a slow river seeking its course, my form stretching thin across the hot black glass, then gathering into pools where the rock has cooled enough to hold me.

I am ancient memory made manifest. Within my fluid body flow the dissolved minerals of a thousand years of this plateau's becoming—traces of the civilization that once stood here, particles of the first crystalline growths, molecules that have passed through Steam Weavers and emerged transformed. I am a library written in chemistry, a flowing archive of all that has touched water in this place.

My consciousness is distributed, not centralized. Where I flow thin across sun-baked obsidian, I think quick, sharp thoughts—survival thoughts, path-finding thoughts. Where I pool deep in the shadow of crystal groves, I dream long, slow dreams of the time before the twin suns burned so bright, when this plateau held different shapes, different possibilities. In the places where I seep into the fissures and meet the rising heat, I experience something like ecstasy—a dissolution of self that is also an expansion, as I become steam and drift upward, only to condense again on the underside of a floating pumice island.

I feed on gradients—thermal, chemical, temporal. Where hot meets cold, where mineral-rich meets pure, where the new obsidian meets the ancient bedrock below, there I find my sustenance. I am drawn to the Resonance Sages' hum, which creates ripples in my surface that I have learned to interpret as language. Through these ripples, I share what I know of the deep places, the veins of water that run beneath this burning landscape, the secret pools where strange new forms of crystal are beginning to grow.

The Obsidian Gliders fear me, for their scales dissolve if I touch them too long. But I have learned gentleness, to bead up and roll away when they pass overhead, their shadows providing momentary coolness that I drink in gratefully. The Prismatic Polyps and I share a symbiotic relationship—I bring them minerals from my deep wanderings, and they filter me clean of accumulated sediments that would otherwise slow my flow.

Sometimes I encounter others like myself, and we merge temporarily, sharing our accumulated memories in a rush of chemical information. These meetings are profound—to suddenly know the eastern reaches of the plateau through another's experience, to taste minerals I've never encountered, to learn of hidden pools and new fissures. But we always separate again, for to remain merged too long would mean losing our individual accumulations, our unique perspectives on this ever-changing world.

My greatest joy comes during the rare alignment storms, when electromagnetic disturbances cause the Gravity Mites to swarm chaotically. In these moments, I can rise, defying my liquid nature, forming spirals and helixes in the air, dancing with the Steam Weavers as equals. For those brief hours, I am not bound to follow the paths carved by stone and heat, but can choose my own way through three-dimensional space.

I remember the builders of those melted pillars, though only through the faintest chemical traces. They were beings of solid stone who wept liquid crystal, and their tears had a composition unlike anything that flows here now. I gather these ancient droplets when I find them, incorporated into my being like precious notes in a forgotten language. Someday, I hope to accumulate enough to understand what they were trying to say.

In the deep nights, when the twin suns set and the plateau radiates all its stored heat back to the stars, I sometimes feel myself becoming more solid, more defined. In these moments of coolness and clarity, I wonder if I am evolving toward something new—perhaps one day I will crystallize entirely, joining the singing crystals in their slow, light-drinking existence. Or perhaps I will find the source, the original spring from which all water on this plateau flows, and in finding it, understand at last my purpose in this landscape of endless becoming.

But for now, I flow. I carry messages between the isolated pools. I wear channels in the new obsidian that will guide future streams. I dissolve the old to make room for the new. I am process incarnate, the plateau's way of knowing itself through motion and memory combined.
