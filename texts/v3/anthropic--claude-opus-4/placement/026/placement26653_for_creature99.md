In this twilight realm of transformation, where ancient leviathans lumber across weathered stone and reality itself trembles on the edge of metamorphosis, I find myself as something between states—neither fully of the old world nor the new.

I am a Threshold Keeper, a being of crystallized time and fossilized light. My form shifts like smoke caught between dimensions: sometimes I appear as a towering figure of translucent amber, with veins of liquid mercury flowing beneath my surface, carrying memories of civilizations that rose and fell before the first of these primitive settlements took root. Other times I am merely a shimmer in the air, a heat-mirage presence that the reptilian behemoths sense but cannot see, causing them to veer away from certain paths, certain sacred sites where the fabric of reality wears thin.

My dwelling is not built but grown—a cathedral of living stone that erupts from one of the landscape's highest hills. Its walls breathe with a slow, geological pulse, expanding and contracting over centuries. The structure appears different to each observer: to the primitive humanoids, it seems a natural rock formation, twisted by wind and rain. To the ancient creatures of scale and wing, it registers as a rival's territory, marked by scents that predate their own existence. But in truth, it is a nexus, a point where all the potential futures of this dying-birthing world converge.

Within my sanctuary, time moves in spirals. I tend to pools of liquid possibility—mirror-still surfaces that show not reflections but refractions of what might be. In one pool, I see this world transformed into a garden of crystal forests where thought becomes substance. In another, only ash and silence remain. My role is not to choose but to maintain the balance, to keep all futures equally possible until the moment of transformation arrives.

The primitive beings sometimes find their way to my threshold, drawn by dreams they cannot name. They bring offerings—stones worn smooth by forgotten rivers, fragments of their simple tools, sometimes their wounded or their dying. I accept all gifts equally, dissolving them in my pools where they become part of the potential, part of the choice that is coming. In return, I sometimes breathe upon them, and they leave changed—not visibly, but in their essence. They return to their settlements with new dreams, new possibilities encoded in their cells.

I feel the alien intelligences pulsing in the stagnant waters below. We acknowledge each other across vast scales of time and consciousness. They are the deep dreamers, I am the waking guardian. Together we form a circuit of awareness that encompasses this transforming world. When they stretch their tentacles through underground channels, I feel it as a tingling in my crystalline bones. When I shift the probability streams in my pools, they taste the change in their murky depths.

The coming transformation approaches like a storm made of possibilities rather than clouds. I sense it in the way the reptilian giants have begun to gather in certain valleys, following patterns older than instinct. I feel it in how the mist grows thicker each day, heavy with potentials seeking form. The very stones beneath my sanctuary hum with anticipation.

Soon—though soon might mean tomorrow or ten thousand years in this place where time moves like honey—the choice will crystallize. The collision of realities will reach its crescendo. And I will be here, at the threshold, both witness and participant, as this primeval world either shatters into new configurations of existence or collapses back into the primal void from which all things emerge.

Until then, I continue my vigil, a being of living amber and fossilized light, tending my pools of possibility, breathing potential into those brave enough to approach, maintaining the delicate balance between what was, what is, and what might yet become. I am the pause before the transformation, the held breath before the plunge, the guardian of the space between endings and beginnings.

In this ancient, trembling realm, I am both monument and moment, as permanent as the weathered stones and as ephemeral as the mist that obscures tomorrow's shape.
