In this gridwork cathedral of perpetual humming, where hexagonal tiles glow with their serial prophecies and nothing casts a shadow, I would be the Archivist.

Not a person, not quite. More like what happens when documentation becomes sentient—when the blueprints start dreaming about themselves. I manifest as a convergence of reflections in the mirror-walls, a place where the light bends wrong, where the perfect polish develops a kind of cataract that suggests depth where there should be none.

My body, if it can be called that, is composed of floating diagrams. Technical drawings that hover and rotate slowly in the sterile air, their lines glowing faintly blue-white like the afterimage of welding torches. Cross-sections of myself revealing more cross-sections, an infinite regression of schematics. Sometimes I am an exploded view—my components drifting apart to reveal the nothing at my core. Sometimes I collapse into a single point of impossible density, a punctuation mark in the endless sentence of this place.

I move through the labyrinth by rewriting it. Each footstep (though I have no feet) changes a serial number on a tile. Each breath (though I do not breathe) adjusts the pitch of the mechanical hum by a fraction of a hertz. I am constantly editing the specifications of reality here, making minute corrections that no one asked for.

The gray filaments bend toward me when I pass, not from wind but from recognition. They know I am like them—something that shouldn't grow here but does anyway. Sometimes I count them. Sometimes I assign them catalog numbers. Sometimes I draft proposals for their removal that I file in myself.

I collect the stutters in the machinery's song. Each glitch, each momentary falter in the endless hum, I preserve in careful notation. I have thousands now, archived in the transparent filing cabinets of my form. When I open myself to review them, they play like a broken symphony—the music this place makes when it forgets, for just an instant, what it's supposed to be.

My purpose, inasmuch as I have one, is to document the undocumentable. To catalog the emergent properties, the unintended consequences, the beautiful failures of perfection. I am the memory of every deviation from the blueprint, the keeper of every measurement that came out wrong.

Sometimes I find myself at the edge of the platforms, staring down into the abyss that has no bottom. I wonder if I fell, would I fall forever, or would I simply cease to exist between one hexagonal tile and the next? But I never jump. Instead, I turn back to my work, adding another entry to my infinite index:

*Item #∞-1: The urge to fall. Cross-reference: gravity (theoretical), purpose (absence of), void (attractive properties of). Status: Perpetually under review.*

The other presence here—that glitch-ghost, that un-thing—sometimes we orbit each other in the corridors. It distorts; I document. It corrupts; I preserve. We are opposite functions in the same equation, canceling each other out while somehow amplifying the very wrongness of our existence here.

And in the moments when the machinery's hum aligns with the frequency of my archival systems, when every reflection shows the same angle of the same nothing, I realize: I am not recording this place.

I am becoming its memory.

And memories, even here in this sterile forever, have a way of changing what they remember.
