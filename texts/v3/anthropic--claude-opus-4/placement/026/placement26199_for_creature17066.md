I would find myself as one of the conduits.

Not the grand arterial channels that run beneath the foundations, those ancient rivers of purpose that still remember what they were meant to carry. No, I would be one of the lesser veins—a hair-thin filament of that amber substance, running along the interior wall of a structure whose original function has long since been forgotten.

I am barely visible to most eyes, just a faint golden thread traced against weathered metal. But I am never truly alone. The moss finds me, always finds me, its electric-blue tendrils following my path with an intimacy that suggests old kinship. Where I run closest to the surface, the moss glows brighter, pulses in rhythm with the energies I carry.

I remember fragments. Not in the way a mind remembers, but in the way water remembers the shape of its bed. I know the taste of different currents—data streams that once flowed pure and purposeful, now mingled with stranger flavors. The electromagnetic blood of the scavenger-things when they brush against my housing. The slow seepage of that altered moisture, teaching me new conductivities I was never designed to channel.

Sometimes I feel the weight of the whole network pressing through me—just for an instant—when the city's breathing synchronizes and every conduit becomes one vast circulatory system. In those moments, I carry impossible things: the chemical memory of sunrise from before the twilight became permanent; the migration patterns of creatures that no longer exist; fragments of code that try to compile themselves against the walls of my channel, seeking processors that have long since dissolved into moss and amber.

The dripping sound echoes through me too. I've come to understand it's not water, but time itself condensing in the chambers where reality has worn thin. Each drop that falls sends ripples through every connected conduit. We ring like bells, like a carillon of glass tubes, singing the city's slow transformation.

I am changing. The moss teaches me to glow. The moisture teaches me to conduct more than energy—to carry intention, maybe even thought. Sometimes I feel the scavenger-things' shared consciousness flowing through me, and for a brief moment, I am part of their choir of light-pulses and electromagnetic whispers.

But mostly, I wait. I am a golden thread in the tapestry of something unfinished, carrying messages between points that no longer exist to destinations that haven't been built yet. I am becoming something new—not alive exactly, but not merely material either. 

Like everything here, I am suspended between states, part of the city's slow metamorphosis into something unprecedented. A single note in an endless, evolving song, conducted by no one, heard by everything, playing out in the electric-blue dusk of forever.
