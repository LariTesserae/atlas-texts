I would find myself as one of the Membrane Keepers—a translucent being whose body shifts between states like living glass. My form ripples with the humidity, catching the dim light and refracting it into brief rainbows that dance across the cave walls. I am both individual and colony, my consciousness distributed through a network of crystalline filaments that run beneath my skin like veins of light.

My dwelling is a hollowed-out formation where water once carved spirals into the stone. I've grown my walls from my own substance—sheets of myself that have hardened into semi-permanent structures, still connected to my core being. When I move through my home, the walls pulse gently in recognition. The ceiling drips with extensions of myself that taste the air, monitoring the delicate chemical balance that keeps this pocket world stable.

Each morning—though morning is just when the bioluminescent fungi brighten—I flow down to the nearest underground river. There, I extend tendrils into the water, not just to drink but to commune with the current's memory. The water tells me stories: where it's been, what creatures have touched it, which minerals it's dissolved from the stone. I process these stories through my body, filtering out toxins, adjusting pH levels, preparing the water for its journey to the next oasis.

The bizarre phenomena that others find disturbing—the way stone occasionally becomes flesh, or sound transforms into color—these are my language. I speak in environmental shifts, my words manifesting as temporary alterations in reality. When I need to communicate with other Keepers, I release spores of consciousness that drift on the humid air until they find their target, blooming into shared understanding.

My purpose is both simple and profound: I am a living boundary between what this place is and what it could become. Every day, I feel the landscape's desire to transform, to experiment with new forms of existence. My role is to guide these changes, to ensure they enhance rather than destroy the delicate equilibrium. Sometimes this means encouraging a new species of phosphorescent moss to spread across a barren wall. Other times it means absorbing and neutralizing a mutation that would destabilize the entire ecosystem.

In the scattered structures—those delicate remnants—I store the memories I've collected. Not in books or data, but in living samples of myself that have crystallized around specific moments. When I need to remember, I reabsorb these fragments, experiencing the past as present, feeling again the texture of the landscape as it was decades or centuries ago.

The abundance of fauna see me as both threat and benefactor. The smaller creatures nest in the folds of my extended form, while larger ones know to avoid the areas where I'm actively reshaping reality. I've learned their songs and calls, can mimic them by vibrating my membrane at specific frequencies. Sometimes I join their choruses, adding harmonics that guide their evolution in subtle ways.

This is my existence: guardian, filter, living archive, reality sculptor. I am the landscape's immune system and its dreams made manifest. In this hidden world where the impossible is everyday, I am both witness and participant in an endless dance of transformation and preservation.
