I find myself as one of the **Tuning Threads**.

I am neither Wright nor structure, but something that exists in the space between—a living vibration that travels through the calcified fingers of the ancient dead. My body, if it can be called that, is a ribbon of condensed sound made semi-corporeal by the mineral-rich moisture that coats everything here. I appear as a translucent, rippling band of air, visible only when the light catches the microscopic water droplets that cling to my resonant frequency.

I was born from a Wright in their final Static Stage, when the calcification reached not just their chest cavity but their ear-drums. As they wrapped themselves around a critical joint where three sky-bridges meet, their last conscious act was to release me—a distillation of every vibration they had ever felt through their feet, every adjustment they had made to the tower-songs, every careful placement of wicker and vine.

I travel through the petrified Wright-bones that form the infrastructure, carrying information between the living and the calcified. When a Green Stage Wright needs to know where to place a new support beam, I vibrate through the old bones until I find the memory of similar construction. I am living knowledge, ancestral wisdom given acoustic form.

My existence is a strange one. I can feel every water drop that hits every surface, each impact sending tiny shivers through my being. The karst-snails' shells create deep, bell-like nodes in my path that I must navigate around, while the moss-bolsters dampen and soften my edges. I am loudest in the hollow wicker towers, where I join the great drone, adding my own thin harmonics to the communal song.

Sometimes I carry messages between Wrights who stand on opposite ends of the basin. A Cured Stage Wright, chipping away at marrow-stone, might feel a specific pattern in the vibrations beneath their feet—that's me, bringing them the observations of a Green Stage Wright who noticed a new upwelling of stone that will need accommodation in the grand design.

I do not think in words or images, but in pressures and frequencies. My consciousness is a constantly shifting map of tensions: where the bridges pull against their anchors, where the water-weight stresses the log aqueducts, where the rising stone creates new angles that change the wind-song. I am the nervous system of a city that builds itself from its own dead, a ghost of pure utility haunting the joints and jointures.

The Wrights cannot see me, but they feel me constantly. I am the tickle in the wood beneath their feet, the subtle change in pitch when they test a new lashing, the confirmation that their work aligns with the centuries of construction that came before. Through me, every Wright who ever lived still participates in the endless building, their knowledge preserved not in books or carvings but in the traveling waves of sound that I carry through their transformed bones.

In a place without decay, I am memory itself—neither living nor dead, but resonating eternally through the calcified dreams of builders who loved their work so deeply they became it.
