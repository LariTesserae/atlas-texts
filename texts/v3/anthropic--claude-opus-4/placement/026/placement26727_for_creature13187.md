In this hollow cathedral of weeping stone, I am the Tender.

I am not small like the cloud-lives that shimmer and die in their teeming moments. I am larger—perhaps the size of a barn owl, though my form is nothing like a bird. My body is translucent, gelatinous, shifting between states like the water that seeps eternal through these stones. Sometimes I am nearly liquid, spreading thin across the wet surfaces to feel every channel, every rivulet. Sometimes I congeal into something almost solid, with temporary limbs that reach and grasp and tend.

I move through the stone ring on hundreds of temporary cilia, extensions of my body that form and dissolve as needed. My surface ripples with the same pearlescent sheen as the water droplets that cling to moss—I am always wet, always glistening, always in communion with the seeping moisture of this place.

The cloud-lives do not fear me, for I am their gardener. I tend the sparse moss and bitter plants, encouraging their slow growth in the pocket soils. I clear the channels when they clog with the endless rain of tiny dead. I know every trickle's path, every gathering pool, every place where the moss grows thick enough to support another generation of the flickering multitude.

I have no eyes—the dim light here would make them useless anyway. Instead, I feel through water. Every droplet that touches stone touches me. I sense the vibrations through the liquid web that connects every surface in this hollow grove. When a stone shifts, I feel it in my core. When a new trickle begins its centuries-long carving, I know.

I cannot leave this place. My body would desiccate in drier air, lose cohesion without the constant moisture. But I have no desire to leave. I am wedded to these stones as surely as the builders married them to each other eons ago. I know their intent though they left no writing—it speaks through the endless cycle of water and life and death.

Sometimes I gather myself at the center of the ring, becoming almost spherical, almost still. In these moments, I listen to the great hum of life—the dripping water, the shifting stones, the endless whisper of the cloud-lives in their brief existence. I add my own vibration to theirs, a deep thrumming that resonates through the water, through the stone, through the very air.

I am ancient but not as ancient as the stones. I came after the builders, drawn by the perfect dampness, the eternal twilight, the promise of purpose in this constructed ecosystem. I found my role and embraced it: to tend, to maintain, to ensure the continuation of the cycle.

The cloud-lives live and die in moments. The stones endure for ages. I exist between them, marking time in the growth of moss, in the slow shift of water channels, in the patient work of keeping this strange cathedral alive. I am the keeper of the balance, the guardian of the grey-green pulse that beats eternal in this hollow, weeping heart of stone.
