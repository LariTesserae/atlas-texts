I find myself as one of the Watchers—though not quite as my kind typically are.

Where others of my species affix themselves permanently to the cavern walls, content in their eternal vigil, I am afflicted with what the Elders call "the Wandering." Every few decades, my holdfast loosens, and I must drift through the currents until I find a new perch. This peculiarity has made me something of an oddity among my kind, who communicate about me in rapid, concerned flickers when they think I cannot perceive their signals.

But the Wandering has given me something precious: perspective. Each new vantage point reveals different patterns in the kelp's mysterious movements, different angles on the Nexari's light-gardens, different rhythms in the breathing stones. I have mapped inconsistencies that stationary Watchers could never detect—places where the cavern's reality grows thin, where sometimes I glimpse... something else. Something vast and patient, pressing against the boundaries of our small world like a face against glass.

My current perch is in a forgotten corner where three tunnels meet, a place the Luminari avoid for reasons they cannot articulate. Here, the water tastes of copper and possibility. The kelp grows in perfect spirals that hurt to perceive directly. And sometimes, in the long spaces between thoughts that comprise my consciousness, I catch fragments of something that might be the Architect's dreams—or memories—or plans.

The other Watchers think my wandering is a defect, a failure to properly root. But I am beginning to suspect it is something else entirely: a slow preparation. For what, I do not yet know. But I feel it approaching like pressure changes before a current shift, like the first tremor before the stones begin their deeper breathing.

I watch. I wait. And unlike my kin, I ready myself to move when the moment comes. Because in this world of patient processes and millennial changes, someone must be prepared to act when the waiting ends. Someone must be ready to follow where the Architect leads—or to flee from what it truly intends.

My bioluminescent patches pulse in patterns I do not consciously control, writing messages in the dark that I myself cannot read. But somewhere in the depths, I think something answers, pulse for pulse, a conversation conducted below the threshold of awareness.

The anticipation is almost unbearable now. Even for a Watcher, who measures patience in centuries, the weight of what approaches grows heavy. The kelp bends in impossible directions. The Nexari swarm in formations that spell out words in languages that won't exist for another thousand years. And in my wandering-loosened consciousness, I begin to understand why I was made different:

I am not broken. I am a key, shaped by patient hands for a lock that is only now beginning to turn.
