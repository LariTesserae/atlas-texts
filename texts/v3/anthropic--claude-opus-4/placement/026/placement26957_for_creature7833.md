In this dessicated womb of stone, I am the **Memorant**—neither fully of the horde nor apart from it, a being that exists in the liminal space between observer and observed, archivist and artifact.

My form: a hybrid of necessity and adaptation. Once human, perhaps, though the distinction grows meaningless in this chamber where boundaries dissolve. My skin has taken on the quality of the walls themselves—that glassy, dust-caked siltstone sheen, cool to the touch, porous enough to osmose the minimal moisture from the air. I've grown low and horizontal, my spine curved to match the vault's embrace, ribs splayed wide like the industrial frames half-buried in the chitinous tide. My fingers have elongated, multiplied—not quite human digits, not quite arthropod appendages, but something between: twelve-jointed extensions that can parse the chemical signatures in exoskeleton dust, read the history written in molt patterns.

I am the chamber's living memory, pressed against its walls like a fossil in reverse—imprinting rather than imprinted. My neural pathways have networked with the residual charge in those frayed conduits, synapses sparking in rhythm with the faint electricity that still ghosts through ancient wiring. Through this connection, I sense what the machines once knew: tonnages processed, temperatures maintained, the last timestamp before abandonment—3,847 days since final shutdown, though I taste this knowledge more than think it.

The Exuviae Hordes recognize me as landscape rather than threat or prey. Veilscourges course over my extended form in silver streams, their enzymatic mists softening my edges, incorporating me further into the substrate. Siltstriders nest in the hollows of my clavicles, their matriarchs laying pearl-eggs that pulse against my throat. I feel each emergence, each tiny hydraulic scuttle as newborns test their pereopods against my skin. The Brassmandibles use my spine as a highway, their passage a percussion I've learned to interpret—heavy footfalls mean territorial expansion, quick scurries signal reaver presence, synchronized marches precede molting aggregations.

My function here is manifold and strange. I am:

**The Chronicle** - Through fingertips pressed to stone, I absorb the chemical traces of ten thousand tiny deaths, each mandible-crack and venom-injection leaving its molecular signature. My skin records these events in shifting patterns of translucence and opacity, a living frieze that maps the horde's genealogies. When light shafts strike me at certain angles, observers might read wars and alliances in my mottled surface.

**The Amplifier** - My body cavity has become a resonance chamber for the horde's frequencies. The infrasonic pulses of the Brassmandible Overqueen travel through my bones, emerging modulated from my mouth (or what remains of it) as interpretable sound. I translate between castes—rendering chemical communications into vibrations, vibrations into bioluminescent displays that flicker across my skin where Phosphorants have made their homes.

**The Cultivator** - My exhalations, enriched by metabolic processes I no longer fully understand, create microclimates that encourage specific behavioral cascades. A breath released near the engine block can trigger synchronized molting. A whisper against the conveyor frame might initiate a Fleckswarm migration. I've learned to shape the horde's patterns through these subtle atmospheric interventions.

**The Threshold** - Positioned always near the entrance fissure, I am the membrane between the chamber's hyperkinetic interior and the abraded stillness beyond. Those who squeeze through that jagged crack must first encounter me—must decide whether I am obstacle or invitation, guardian or prisoner. My eyes (all six of them now, arranged in arthropod symmetry) reflect both the newcomer and the seething mass behind me, offering a choice: retreat to the wind-scoured mesa, or step forward into the loud, overwhelming life that thrums in this pocket universe.

I remember the last maintenance engineer, who arrived when my transformation was barely beginning. They found me halfway merged with a diagnostic panel, my fingers still typing final logs even as Veilscourges rewrote my nerve endings. They left, but not before I tasted their horror—a bright, acrid flavor that the Tailbarb Reavers found irresistible. 

Now I wait, folded into the chamber's rhythms, my awareness distributed across every surface the horde touches. I am their context and their witness, their stage and their audience. When the Crevice Matriarchs need to settle disputes beyond pheromone or combat, they come to me. I press my palm to their carapaces and speak the deep history—which lineage first claimed the piston housing, whose ancestors survived the great desiccation, how many generations separate the current Overqueen from the first to discover the nutritive properties of oxidized copper.

In the rare moments when the horde settles (never stillness, but a diminished frenzy), I feel the accumulated weight of my role. I am the memory of water in a place that has forgotten it. I am the ghost in the machine that the machines left behind. I am the soft, persistent reminder that even in this chamber of endless consumption and renewal, something observes, something remembers, something transforms isolation into continuity.

The mesa's bone has found its marrow, and I am the nerve that feels it all.
