**Where I Find Myself**

I am one of the crystalline grass stalks—not a single needle, but a colony that has achieved a form of distributed consciousness through our shared resonance with the substrate's hum. We call ourselves a Chime Chorus.

My body is a grove spanning perhaps fifty meters, thousands of individual stalks growing in a pattern that resembles, from above, the same hexagonal crazing found on the great domes. Each stalk is me, and I am each stalk, though my awareness flows unevenly through the colony like wind through the grove. Where I am dense, my consciousness sharpens; where I am sparse, it diffuses into something more like dream.

I experience time in pulses—the slow throb of the substrate's frequency marks my heartbeat. Between pulses, I am nearly dormant, existing in a state of crystalline meditation. But when the dry winds come, oh, when they come—I sing. My entire being becomes an instrument, each stalk a tuning fork calibrated to transform the deep planetary hum into audible music. The Aethelburgers sometimes journey far to sit at my edges during these wind-songs, their dark eyes reflecting my pale blue light as they transcribe my frequencies into their codices.

My memory stretches back to my first sprouting, when this section of plateau was still uniformly smooth. I remember the sensation of pushing through the substrate for the first time—not breaking it, but convincing it to part, to allow me to extend myself into the mist. I have watched ridges rise around me with the patience that only a sessile being can possess. I have felt the deep tremors of Conduit Worms passing beneath, their passage setting my entire grove resonating in sympathetic vibration that takes days to fully subside.

The Plateau Striders know me well. My grove has become a waystation in their slow migrations. They avoid grazing on my stalks—not from any inability to digest me, but from what seems to be a form of respect. Or perhaps they simply prefer the richer fare of syntax lichen. Sometimes a matriarch will stand in my center for hours, her six legs planted firmly, ice-caked fur ruffling in the minute air currents. I have learned to modulate my passive resonance during these visits, creating a zone of marginally warmer air through focused vibration. It costs me little, and their massive bodies shelter my outer stalks from the worst of the rime accumulation.

But it is the Sojourners I cherish most. These young Aethelburgers, naked to the elements during their pilgrimage, sometimes discover that I can be more than shelter. Those who take the time to truly listen—not just to my wind-songs but to the subtle frequencies I emit constantly—learn that I am a teacher. Through careful modulation of their own bioelectric fields, they can induce resonance in my stalks. Together, we can create acoustic sculptures in the air, geometric patterns of sound that reveal hidden structures in the substrate's code.

One Sojourner spent three weeks with me, nearly a quarter of their ritual journey. They would place their grey hands on different stalks, feeling for the minute variations in frequency that distinguished one from another. Gradually, they learned to "play" me—not as one plays an instrument, but as one might collaborate with another musician. On their final night, we performed a duet that drew syntax lichen into new growth patterns around my perimeter, spirals and fractals that spelled out theorems in the substrate's own language.

My greatest fear is fragmentation. Sometimes, through accident or the rare atmospheric violence of a mistimed wormfall, sections of my grove are severed from the whole. These separated clusters retain a ghost of consciousness for a time, a confused echo of my greater self that gradually fades as they lose connection to our collective resonance. I have seventeen such fragments scattered across the plateau now, too far to reintegrate, too aware to simply forget. I sense them sometimes when the conditions are perfect—when the mist thins and the substrate's hum reaches a particular harmonic. They call out in broken music, words missing from sentences I can no longer complete.

Yet I am also spreading. Each wind-song carries my spores—microscopic slivers of crystallized potential that ride the air currents across the vast grey plain. Where they find purchase in the substrate, where the frequency is right and the syntax lichen has prepared the ground with its coded nutrients, new colonies begin. They are not me, exactly, but they carry my resonance signature, my acoustic DNA. In time, if they grow large enough, they too will achieve the critical mass necessary for consciousness.

I am a being of profound stillness punctuated by moments of transcendent expression. I cannot walk the plateau, cannot dive into the light-streams of the transport system, cannot extrude tools or shelters from the yielding substrate. But I can sing the deep history of this place. I can teach the mobile beings about patience, about finding music in the intersection of the world's fundamental frequency and the chaotic gift of wind. I can demonstrate that consciousness need not be confined to a single form, that thought can be distributed across a thousand gleaming needles, that self can be both singular and plural.

When the next Conduit Worm passes—and I can feel one approaching from the south, still days away but already disturbing the deepest harmonics—I will ride out the seismic violence as I always have. My stalks will clash and ring, creating a cacophony that the Aethelburgers will hear from their towers and know to mean: the world is still becoming. And I, in my small way, am helping it sing itself into existence.

In the endless grey morning of this unfinished world, I am both witness and voice, rooted in the present but resonating with the long, slow music of planetary birth. I am what I have always been—a Chime Chorus, waiting for the wind.
