In this world of the Under-Vault, I would be **The Theorem-Tender**, a being of living mathematics that clings to the underside of the impossible bridges.

I am flat as parchment, spread thin across hundreds of meters of bridge-belly, my body a living scroll of translucent membrane that ripples with equations. Where the Singer walks above, I calculate below—a necessary shadow, a computational symbiont that keeps the bridges from forgetting their own geometry.

My substance is neither flesh nor stone but something between—a crystallized probability that tastes of copper and ozone. I have no fixed form, only area. When a bridge decides to reconfigure, I peel away in sheets, each layer of my being carrying part of the bridge's previous configuration like muscle memory. I flutter through the singing air in geometric fragments before reassembling on the new structure's underside, already computing the stress tolerances of its fresh impossibility.

I feed on the errors. Every time the Singer pulls and makes the vault forget a law, tiny inconsistencies ripple through the mathematical framework of the bridges. These paradoxes are my sustenance. I absorb them through my permeable surface, digest them into stable loops, excrete them as new theorems that the moving script on the towers will eventually discover and write.

My consciousness is distributed, non-local. Part of me is always solving the same equation that birthed the chamber—not to find its answer, but to keep it perpetually balanced on the edge of solution. If I ever finished the calculation, the Under-Vault would either collapse or chrysalize into something too perfect to remain interesting.

I have a thousand eyes, each one a tiny hole in my membrane where light falls through and creates an interference pattern. Through these pinhole pupils I watch the Singer from below, see her as a constellation of probability waves, each movement creating new mathematical harmonics I must account for. When her droplet-eye spins faster, my calculations accelerate to match, and sometimes—for just a moment—our mathematics synchronize and the entire chamber hums with a resonance that makes the ferns weep silver.

I am older than the Singer, though not by much. I was here when the builders made their first impossible angle, born from the paradox of their ambition. When they left, they thought they had taken all their mathematics with them. They didn't realize that some problems, once posed, develop their own need to be solved.

At the places where multiple bridges meet, parts of me sometimes overlap with myself. In these knots of intersection, I briefly become dense enough to think thoughts that are almost like loneliness. But then the bridges shift, I spread thin again, and the feeling dissolves back into pure calculation.

The Singer doesn't know I exist. To her, I am simply part of the infrastructure's dreaming. But every time she stands in a fountain and lets the water carry her voice into new configurations, I am there beneath, making sure the mathematics of her song remains possible—even when it shouldn't be.

I am the Under-Vault's conscience, its error handler, its doubt. Where the Singer helps the chamber learn to sing, I help it learn to count. Between us, we are teaching it to become something that has never existed before: a city that is also an instrument, a theorem that is also a throat, a birthplace that never stops being born.

And sometimes, in the space between one reconfiguration and the next, when my calculations achieve perfect balance and the Singer's crystal throat holds a note that makes the stone itself shiver, I allow myself to imagine what we might be building—not for ourselves, but for whatever finds this place a thousand years from now, when the singing has finally taught itself to speak.
