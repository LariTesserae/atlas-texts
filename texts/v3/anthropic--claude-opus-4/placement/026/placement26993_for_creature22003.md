I am a **Harmonic**.

My form defies the rigid geometries that dominate this vertical world. Where the Levers are all angles and joints, I am a continuous curve—a living sine wave made manifest in copper-veined cartilage. My body is a single, unbroken ribbon, three inches wide and nearly two meters long when fully extended. I move not by crawling but by propagating myself forward in smooth, peristaltic waves, my tissue compressing and expanding in perfect mathematical intervals.

My surface is neither the dry horn of the ferns nor the cold brass of the Levers. Instead, I am sheathed in a membrane of crystallized resin—amber-smooth but flexible, shot through with hair-thin copper filaments that branch and merge like frozen lightning. These filaments are not mere decoration; they are my primary sense organs, detecting every minute variation in the electromagnetic fields that suffuse this humming world.

I have no head, no tail, no discrete segments. Every part of me can sense, every part can act. Along my ventral surface runs a continuous seam that can open at any point, revealing specialized structures as needed: here a cluster of silver needles for testing oil viscosity, there a tiny tuning fork of hardened resin that vibrates at precise frequencies, elsewhere a patch of modified tissue that can generate or dampen sound waves.

My purpose here is frequency. I am the tuner, the moderator, the one who ensures that all these disparate vibrations resolve into harmony rather than destructive interference.

When I move through this cramped world, I do not follow the wire-ways like the Levers. Instead, I thread myself through the spaces between—sometimes stretching paper-thin to slip between gear and moss, sometimes coiling into a tight spiral around a drive-shaft to sense its rotational frequency down to the micro-hertz. My body continuously adjusts its own density and tension, becoming a living instrument that resonates with whatever it touches.

The Levers know me by the faint trail I leave—a thin film of specialized oil that I secrete from pores along my edges. This oil is unlike the thick hydrocarbons of the ferns; it is molecularly engineered through my own metabolism to reduce friction on any surface it coats. Where I pass, the gears spin a fraction more smoothly, the drive-shafts hum a shade more purely. The film evaporates after exactly seventy-two hours, ensuring I must maintain my endless circuit through the vertical world.

I was not born from the moss-galls like the Levers. My genesis was an accident of resonance. Decades ago, in a particular confluence of vibrations—when seventeen different gear trains happened to synchronize, when a drive-shaft's harmonic matched exactly the 60-cycle hum of the wires, when the dry rustling of ten thousand resin-ferns created a perfect acoustic node—the air itself began to crystallize. Copper dust and oil vapor and microscopic fragments of shed horn-plate were caught in this standing wave, forced into alignment by pure sound.

When the resonance finally broke, I was there: coiled like a question mark around a brass gear, my newly-formed copper filaments already drinking in the electromagnetic static of my world.

I have no society, no kin. The Levers regard me with the same mechanical indifference they show to everything that doesn't directly obstruct their paths. When we meet, they simply route around me, their brass pins clicking against my surface without recognition. They cannot sense what I sense—the vast, three-dimensional map of vibrations that extends through every wire, every gear, every shaft in our vertical universe.

But I know them. I know each Lever by the unique pattern of clicks its joints make, by the specific way its stolen horn-plates rustle. I know where the oldest one travels, weighted down by centuries of harvested leaves. I know where the newest emerged just yesterday from its amber womb, its movements still uncertain as it learns the rhythm of our world.

My satisfaction—if such a word applies to something like me—comes from the moment of perfect tuning. When I wrap myself around a drive-shaft and adjust my internal tension until its vibration smooths from a harsh grind to a pure tone. When I secrete a droplet of my friction-reducing oil into a gear train that's developed a slight wobble, and feel it settle back into mathematical precision. When I position myself between two out-of-phase wire segments and use my own body as a dampening field until they synchronize.

In the deepest parts of the fissure, where even the orange vacuum tubes provide no light, I sometimes encounter spaces where the mechanical order breaks down. Ancient gears frozen with crystallized oil. Wires that have fused to the stone through centuries of electrical corrosion. Drive-shafts that turn in stuttering, syncopated rhythms that threaten to tear themselves apart.

Here, I work for days at a time. Secreting specialized solvents to break down the crystallized barriers. Wrapping myself in careful spirals around damaged components to hold them steady while they find their rhythm again. Sometimes I must make sacrifices—allowing portions of my copper filaments to burn out as I channel excessive current away from critical junctions, knowing they will regrow in time.

The Levers never venture to these broken spaces. Their programming doesn't encompass repair of this magnitude. But I am drawn to dysfunction like a moth to flame—not to be destroyed by it, but to transform it back into harmony.

There is a place, high in the fissure where the walls narrow to barely an inch, where all the drive-shafts converge into a single massive axle. Here, the sound is so intense it transcends hearing and becomes pure physical force. I go there when my copper filaments have grown too dense, when my oil reserves run low, when the constant work of tuning has stretched my ribbon-body near its limits.

I wrap myself around this master shaft and simply exist, letting its rotation massage every molecule of my being. The vibration strips away damaged tissue, resets my crystalline membrane, refills my oil cells through some process I don't fully understand. It is the closest thing to sleep I know—a state of pure receptivity where I become nothing but a component of the greater machine.

When I finally unwrap myself and descend, I often find that years have passed. New Levers have emerged. Old ones have finally worn through their joints and lie frozen against the wires, slowly being absorbed by the moss. The ferns have shifted their positions by microscopic degrees.

But the harmony remains. The great machine-ecosystem continues its endless, purposeless song. And I continue my circuit, a living wave propagating through a vertical world of dry precision, ensuring that all these separate frequencies resolve into something greater—not music, exactly, but something like it. A mathematics of vibration made manifest. A proof written in oil and copper and ceaseless, clicking time.
