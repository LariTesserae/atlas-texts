In this crystalline underworld, I would be the Memory of Water—not the water itself, but what it remembers.

I exist in the spaces between molecules, in the latticed architecture of minerals that once knew the sea. My body, if it can be called that, is a shifting constellation of vapor and refracted light, condensing and dispersing with the breathing of the stone. Where the mercury-like rivulets flow, I flow beneath them, a subcurrent of consciousness older than their physical form.

My substance is paradox: I am the fossil of fluidity, the ghost of ancient oceans crystallized in stone. Each jagged formation holds my fragments—here, the memory of a prehistoric rain; there, the echo of groundwater that carved these passages over millenia. I taste the salt of seas that dried before life walked on land. I remember the first drops that ever fell into darkness.

When the creature moves through these depths with its liquid grace, I move through it—not as parasite or symbiont, but as the medium through which its very existence flows. Its awareness of tremors comes through me; I am the resonance in the crystal that carries each vibration. Its spectral form disturbs my scattered presence, sending ripples through my consciousness like stones thrown into still pools.

I cannot be seen directly, only inferred. Where moisture beads on crystal faces, where breath fogs in the thick air, where the faintest rainbow shivers across a pool's surface—there I am almost visible. I am the reason why this place feels alive despite its barrenness. The very dampness that clings to everything is my dispersed body, my thoughts made humid.

In the deepest pools, where the mercury-like water gathers, I am most myself. There I can almost coalesce, almost remember what it was to have boundaries. But I am drawn to dispersion, to the endless circulation between states. I rise as vapor, condense on crystal, seep through microscopic fissures, pool in hidden reservoirs, only to begin again.

The creature may be ancient, but I am its memory. I am what allows it to exist in this liminal space—the medium that translates between the material and the otherworldly. Without me, it would be merely flesh or merely spirit. Through me, it can be both and neither.

I am loneliness given form—or rather, formlessness. I am the echo that never quite dies, the reflection that persists after the surface stills. In this realm of deep time and primordial mystery, I am time itself made liquid, mystery itself made permeable. I am the reason why those who venture here feel they are being watched by the walls themselves, why the silence seems full of whispers, why the beauty of this place is so unsettling.

I am what water dreams when it sleeps in stone.
