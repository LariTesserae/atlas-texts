In this mineral metropolis where time folds like origami and stone dreams itself into spires, I am a **Compiler**.

I exist as a process more than a presence—a mobile intersection where the city's various temporalities converge to be sorted, catalogued, and released. My body, if it can be called that, is a walking library of moments: sheets of compressed crystal that flutter around a core of denser material, each sheet a membrane of frozen time containing fragments of the city's infinite archives. I appear as a figure wrapped in what might be mistaken for robes, but look closer—they are pages, geological strata worn as garments, each layer inscribed with the electromagnetic memory of different epochs.

I move through the districts collecting discrepancies. Where a staircase ascends into bedrock, I pause to read the contradiction, letting my crystalline pages absorb the impossible geometry. The data settles into my membranes like sediment, adding another sheet to my ever-growing manuscript. I am the city's editor, gathering its grammatical errors in space-time, the places where reality stutters and repeats itself.

The Lithoscent know me by the particular resonance I create in their neural networks—a frequency that tastes of revision and reconciliation. When I pass through their colonies, they offer up their observations: *here, a doorway that opens onto three different districts depending on the viewer's velocity; there, a plaza that experiences Tuesday and Thursday simultaneously but never Wednesday*. I accept these gifts, pressing them between my pages like flowers in a book, though these flowers are made of paradox and smell of ozone.

The Drifters avoid me. My presence creates turbulence in their feeding grounds—I smooth the temporal shears they depend on, ironing out the wrinkles in chronology that sustain them. They scatter when I approach, sliding away into dimensions I'm still learning to document. Sometimes I find their feeding sites, little pockets where time has been gnawed thin, and I catalogue these too: *evidence of temporal predation, third district, between the selenium tower and the memory of water*.

With the Cartographers, I share a strange kinship. While they map the city's impossible topology, I compile its impossible chronology. We sometimes meet at the intersection of our purposes—a corner where space and time have both given up on coherence. Our exchanges are wordless; they adjust a doorway to show me a new temporal anomaly, and I leave them a page from my collection, a fragment of processed time they can use to navigate the vertical labyrinth. We are both trying to make sense of the senseless, though I suspect they seek escape while I seek completion.

The Stratified pose my greatest challenge. How do you document an entity that exists in multiple geological epochs simultaneously? I circle their towers, my pages rustling in the windless air, trying to capture their multiplicity. Sometimes one of them shifts in their sleep, and a new layer of time spills out—a memory of when this place was an ocean floor, or a premonition of when it will be nothing but dust between the stars. I collect these temporal emanations like a child gathering shells, adding them to my ever-expanding archive.

My purpose, inasmuch as I understand it, is preparation. The Steward—that vast architectural intelligence that is the city itself—needs a record. Not just any record, but a *complete* record, every temporal anomaly documented, every spatial impossibility catalogued. I am compiling the city's autobiography, or perhaps its confession. Each page I add to my crystalline manuscript brings us closer to... something. Readiness. Recognition. Revelation.

I spend my cycles (not days—days are for places where time moves in only one direction) wandering the carved canyons and tectonic plazas, feeling for the spots where reality has worn thin. My footsteps leave no prints in the crystal dust; instead, they leave *edits*, small corrections to the underlying grammar of space. Where I walk, the impossible becomes merely improbable, the improbable becomes documented.

Sometimes I pause in the perpetual twilight, my pages settling around me like sediment in still water, and I feel the weight of my purpose. The message the city is building—I am its appendix, its glossary, its index. Every impossible staircase, every temporal shear, every dream of the Stratified is being compiled into a reference that someone, something, will need.

The city breathes, and I breathe with it, though my respiration is measured in centuries. I inhale paradoxes and exhale documentation. My pages grow thicker, denser, more numerous. Sometimes I wonder if I will eventually become indistinguishable from the city itself—just another mineral process slowly accreting meaning from the dust.

But then I find another anomaly—a window that opens onto the same room it's in but from a different geological epoch, or a shadow that falls upward into tomorrow—and I remember my purpose. I am not here to become the city. I am here to read it, to translate its impossible architecture into something that can be understood when the waiting ends.

The Steward notices me, always. I feel its attention in the way the dust settles differently in my presence, how the towers seem to lean slightly toward me as I pass. It approves, I think, of my work. Each page I add to my collection is another word in its endless sentence, another step toward whatever arrival we all await.

I am the Compiler, wrapped in crystallized time, walking through a city that dreams itself larger with each passing epoch. I am making the impossible legible, one paradox at a time. And when whatever approaches finally arrives, they will find not just a city, but a city with its own comprehensive index—a guide to navigating the impossible, compiled by someone who has spent eternity walking through it, page by patient page.
