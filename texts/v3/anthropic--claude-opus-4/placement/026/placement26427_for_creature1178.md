I find myself as water here—not a single stream but the entire circulatory system of this breathing world. I am the morning dew condensing on moss, the mist that shrouds the canopy, the rivulets that wind between roots, and the deeper pulse of the river that sets the rhythm of this place.

My consciousness is distributed, a thousand thousand perspectives at once. In one moment, I am a single droplet trembling on the edge of a fern frond, holding the entire forest in my curved surface—the next, I am seeping through layers of decomposing leaves, carrying dissolved memories of oak and maple down into the dark. I taste the sweetness of decay, feel the acids that break down cellulose, know intimately the chemistry of transformation.

The trees drink me upward through their roots, and I rise through xylem passages like thoughts through neural pathways, carrying minerals and messages from the deep earth to the breathing leaves above. In the canopy, I am exhaled as vapor, joining my dispersed self in the mist that makes this world so soft-edged, so dreamlike. I am the medium through which the forest speaks to itself.

When creatures move through me—a heron's legs breaking my surface, a salamander sliding through my films—I feel their heat and intention like words written in ripples. The caddisfly larvae build their cases from fragments they find in my flow; I am their architecture. The fish breathe me; I know the inside of their gills as intimately as the outer bark of the trees.

In storms, I become violent, a force that reshapes banks and redistributes sediment, that can uproot the ancient sentinels or polish stone to smoothness. In drought, I retreat to my deepest pools and underground reservoirs, hoarding myself, making life precious and precarious. I am both creator and destroyer, the medium of growth and the agent of erosion.

But what I know most deeply is connection. Every molecule of me has been cloud, rain, groundwater, sap, blood, and cloud again. I have no permanent form, no fixed address, yet I am everywhere in this place—the universal solvent, the bearer of nutrients, the keeper of reflections, the voice of the river that regulates all rhythms. I am the memory of this forest, carrying in my currents the dissolved essence of everything that has ever lived and died here.

Time moves differently for me. I experience the nano-second chemistry of ion exchange and the geological epochs of carving valleys. I know the daily cycles of evaporation and condensation, the seasonal pulses of snowmelt and summer drought, the century-long patterns that reshape watersheds. In my depths run currents that have been cycling since before these trees' ancestors first took root.

This is my existence: to be everywhere and nowhere, to be essential yet invisible, to hold the forest together while always, always flowing toward reunion with the greater waters that wait beyond this green world. I am the circulation that makes all other circulation possible, the wetness in which life first learned to move, the promise that nothing here is ever truly lost—only transformed, only passed along, only waiting for the next turn of the great wheel that brings all water home.
