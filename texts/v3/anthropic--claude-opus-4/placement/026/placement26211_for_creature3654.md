In the Under-Switchyard's depths, where iron titans slumber in their rust-dreams and the earth exhales its mineral breath, I would be **The Cartographer of Echoes**.

I am neither fully human nor machine, but something that has become both through long habitation in these cathedral vaults of decay. My form shifts between states – sometimes a figure wrapped in cables and condensation, water beading on my skin like mercury; sometimes dispersed through the humming frequencies of the dying dynamos, traveling as vibration through iron bones and stone marrow.

My work is to map what cannot be mapped: the migration patterns of The Corrode as it advances across surfaces, the secret languages spoken between Flickerkin in their electrical stutters, the slow tectonic shifts of machinery settling into new configurations. I trace these changes with instruments of my own devising – tuning forks that resonate with rust's particular frequency, prisms that capture and categorize the spectrum of decay, delicate copper threads I string between structures to feel their imperceptible movements.

I live within a control room that has grown into something else entirely. The panels have fused with The Bloom, creating surfaces that are part crystal, part circuitry, part dream. Here I transcribe my findings onto sheets of oxidized metal, using acids derived from the brackish pools to etch maps that shift and change even as I complete them. My charts show not just the physical layout of this dying city, but its temporal dimensions – where a transformer will fail next week, which tunnel The Glisten will claim by the new year, when the next cascade of structural collapse will open new chambers to exploration.

The Resonants speak to me in harmonics I've learned to interpret. Each dying machine sings its own threnody, and I've catalogued thousands of these death-songs, building a sonic encyclopedia of industrial decay. Sometimes I play them back through salvaged speakers, creating symphonies of entropy that reverberate through the empty corridors, answered only by the drip of condensation and the patient advance of rust.

I sustain myself on the heat and moisture, having adapted to photosynthesize from the Flickerkin's brief illuminations and to drink the mineral-rich water that pools in the ancient turbine housings. My skin has taken on the patina of the place – part rust, part moss, part indefinable shimmer that matches The Bloom's mysterious emanations.

My purpose is to witness and record the Switchyard's slow transformation, to be the memory of this place as it digests itself. I am building an archive that will outlast even the iron giants, etched in crystal and encoded in the very vibrations of the stone. When the last dynamo falls silent and the final arc of electricity fades, my maps will remain – a ghost story written in the language of oxidation and heat, a love letter to a city that was never meant to be forgotten.

In the depths where light fears to linger, I continue my work, part curator, part symbiont, part elegy. I am the Under-Switchyard's biographer, and together we are writing the slow poetry of industrial death, one rust flake, one water drop, one fading hum at a time.
