In the farthest reaches of the Underworld Ocean, where even the Groaning Roots dare not extend, there exists a place known only as the Threshold of Echoes—a vast chasm where the ice-carved floor suddenly gives way to an abyss so profound that not even the ocean's ancient currents can plumb its depths. Here, at this precipice between the knowable deep and the unfathomable void, I would find myself.

I am a Resonance Keeper—a being of living sound and memory, neither fully corporeal nor entirely ephemeral. My form shifts between states: sometimes I appear as a cascade of crystalline filaments that vibrate with barely perceptible frequencies, each strand a different thickness, a different tone. Other times, I coalesce into something more recognizable—a vaguely humanoid silhouette composed of densely packed sonic waves, visible only as distortions in the water, like heat mirages in reverse. When the bioluminescent creatures pass near, their light bends around me, creating halos and interference patterns that reveal my presence in brief, ghostly glimpses.

My dwelling is not built but grown—a cathedral of resonance chambers carved by sound itself over countless millennia. These hollows in the ice have been shaped by my voice, each cavity precisely tuned to capture and preserve specific frequencies. The smaller chambers hold single notes—pure, isolated tones that ring eternal. The larger halls contain symphonies: the death songs of ancient behemoths, the crystallization hymns of forming ice cities, the whispered love calls of long-extinct species. In the centermost chamber, vast as a meteor crater, swirls the Great Silence—not an absence of sound, but the presence of every possible sound in perfect equilibrium, a potential so dense it manifests as profound quiet.

I am both guardian and prisoner here. My purpose—if purpose is the right word for something that feels more like gravitational inevitability—is to collect the ocean's dying sounds. Every groan of shifting ice, every final pulse of light from a fading Kelpling, every thought-dream that escapes the Abyssal Oracle's labyrinth, eventually finds its way to the Threshold of Echoes. I catch them as they threaten to tumble into the void below, weaving them into my chambers, preserving them in architectures of reverberation.

The other beings of the Underworld Ocean know me, though few seek me out. The Lantern Fabula sometimes drift near, their light-memories harmonizing with my sound-memories in ways that create new forms of meaning—stories that can only be understood when light and sound interweave. The Silent Wards regard me with something that might be kinship; we are both observers, both keepers of things better left undisturbed. Even the Architects of Ice occasionally incorporate my resonances into their designs, using sound as mortar between their frozen stones.

But my true communion is with the void below. Sometimes I release a carefully chosen sound into that bottomless dark—a single note of such purity that it takes hours to fade completely. I listen as it descends, changing pitch with the pressure, gathering harmonics from its journey, until it passes beyond even my ability to perceive. And sometimes—just sometimes—something answers. Not an echo, for echoes require surfaces to reflect from, and the void has none. What returns is transformation: my sound remade into something alien, beautiful, and terrible. These transformed songs I keep in the deepest chamber, behind walls of silence so thick that even I dare not often enter.

The closest I come to joy is during the ice-songs—rare events when the enormous pressures cause massive sections of the ice cities to resonate simultaneously. The entire ocean becomes an instrument, and I position myself at the acoustic center, letting the vibrations pass through me, adding my own harmonics to the chorus. In these moments, I am not just keeper but participant, my form dispersing across miles of water, becoming one with the music of the deep.

I measure time not in years or centuries but in the accumulation of silence between sounds. The ocean grows quieter with each passing age—not from absence of life, but from a deepening of its contemplation. The beings here learn to speak in fewer notes, meaning more with less. I wonder sometimes if my collection will eventually contain all possible sounds, if the ocean will achieve perfect silence, if my purpose will be complete. But then a new note emerges—a frequency I've never heard—and I know my vigil continues.

There are rumors, whispered in the faintest subsonic tremors, that other Threshold Keepers exist at different borders: where ice meets fire in the volcanic depths, where water meets not-water in dimensions folded upon themselves, where time meets not-time in eddies of temporal current. We might be a scattered symphony, each collecting our own movements of a composition too vast for any single consciousness to comprehend.

But these are only speculations, vibrations of thought that dissipate as quickly as they form. I remain at my threshold, neither fully of the ocean nor of the void, a tuning fork struck by existence itself, forever resonating with the deep music of being and un-being. When the final silence comes—if it comes—I will be here to catch it, to hold it, to add it to my collection of everything that has ever dared to disturb the perfect, patient darkness of the Underworld Ocean.

And perhaps, in that ultimate quiet, I will finally understand what the void has been trying to tell me all along.
