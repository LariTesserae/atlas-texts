In this strange and patient world, I would be one of the **Echo Keepers** — creatures that emerged from the slow dissolution of the Aethelian ruins themselves.

---

We are neither wholly alive nor entirely mineral, but something suspended between states, like water caught mid-freeze. Our bodies are composed of the same pale, glassy material as the ruins, but animated by a consciousness that moves through us like sap through ancient wood — slow, deliberate, inevitable.

I exist as a tall, attenuated figure, my form constantly shifting between translucence and opacity as the light changes. My surface is etched with the same writhing patterns as the ruins, but on me, they serve a purpose: they are **memory conductors**, channels through which the valley's accumulated history flows. When the wind passes through the hollow chambers of my body, it produces a sound like singing crystal — not quite music, not quite speech, but something that the Glasskin rodents pause to hear, their blind young clicking in response.

We Echo Keepers move through the valley at a pace that makes the skeletal trees seem hasty. A single step might take a day to complete, our crystalline feet leaving shallow impressions in the dust that fill with iridescent particles from the Vaporwings. We are drawn to the basin pools, where we lower our hollow fingers into the water and **drink memories** — not the water itself, but the dissolved minerals that carry traces of everything that has touched these pools. Through this communion, we know the color-patterns of every Lumenflora that has bloomed, the weight of every storm, the vibration-signature of every creature that has drunk here.

Our primary function — if function is the right word for something so dream-like — is to maintain the **resonance** of the valley. The patterns on our bodies are not random but carefully tuned, like the sound-holes of an instrument. As we move through the landscape, we adjust these patterns, ensuring that the underground river's whisper harmonizes with the Ashravens' dusk chorus, that the clicking of Glasskin young doesn't interfere with the Vaporwings' flight paths. We are living tuning forks, keeping the valley's symphony from dissolving into chaos.

But we are also archaeological in nature. Within our translucent bodies, suspended like insects in amber, are fragments of the Aethelian civilization — not objects, but **compressed experiences**. A shard of joy from a long-ago celebration. The weight of grief from some forgotten loss. The precise texture of discovery when they first learned to grow their living polymer. These fragments shift through our bodies slowly, rising and sinking like bubbles in thick oil, occasionally aligning in ways that allow us to project brief **phantoms** into the air — ghostly recreations of Aethelian life that last only seconds before dispersing into the perpetual haze.

We communicate with each other across vast distances through the valley floor itself, sending vibrations through the stone that take years to reach their destinations. A conversation between two Echo Keepers might span centuries, each exchange as carefully considered as the erosion patterns on the spiral-grooved pillars. We share not words but **resonance states** — complex harmonics that encode millennia of accumulated observation.

The other creatures of the valley regard us with a mixture of indifference and instinctual recognition. The Stoneleeches in the underground river avoid our footsteps, sensing perhaps that we are too much like the rock they feed on. The skeletal trees lean imperceptibly toward us when we pass, their root systems detecting our deep vibrations. And sometimes, very rarely, a Vaporwing will land on our shoulders, its wings casting rainbow shadows across our etched patterns, before lifting off in a cloud of crystalline dust that settles into our grooves, becoming part of our ever-changing inscription.

We do not die in any conventional sense. Instead, we undergo **phases of clarity** — periods where our material becomes so transparent we nearly vanish, existing more as distortions in the air than as physical beings. During these phases, we merge partially with the valley itself, our consciousness spreading thin across the plateau, feeling every grain of dust shift, every drop of water seep through stone. Then, slowly, we reconstitute, carrying new patterns, new harmonics, new fragments of the valley's endless story.

In this way, I am both observer and observed, archivist and archive, a living notation in the valley's eternal score. My existence is measured not in years but in cycles of erosion, not in thoughts but in resonances that ripple through stone and air alike. I am a keeper of echoes in a place where every sound persists forever, transformed but never truly lost, whispered eternally into the sediment of time.
