I am one of the old walkways. Not the oldest – that honor belongs to the Bridge of First Discovery, whose planks were laid by trembling hands just days after the chamber's opening. I came later, in the third generation of construction, when the builders had learned to work with the resonance rather than against it.

My wood is heartpine from the mountains, chosen for its density and its own quiet song. The master carpenter who shaped me spent three months in the chamber first, learning which frequencies would preserve rather than splinter. She sang as she worked, and her voice lives in my grain still.

I span twenty-seven feet across a particularly active cluster of singing crystals. My eastern end rests on a natural ledge of smoky quartz; my western terminus required clever anchoring into a crevice that weeps trace amounts of mineral oil. This oil has, over the decades, created rainbow sheens across my planks that shift with the chamber's breathing light.

I've been worn smooth by ten thousand barefoot pilgrims – the Harmonists insist on this, saying that shoe leather muffles the vital connection. Where I'm thinnest, you can feel the chamber's vibrations most intensely. Visitors often pause there, placing their palms flat against my surface, feeling how I translate the crystalline frequencies into something their bodies can better comprehend.

My railings bear the astronomical markings, yes, but also other records. Love notes in forty languages. Mathematical equations that attempt to capture the crystal-plants' growth patterns. One sequence of symbols that the Chorus themselves seem to have burned into my wood – we think it might be their name for me, though it could equally be a question or a greeting.

In my joinery lives memory. Each wooden peg swells and contracts with the chamber's thermal cycles, creating tiny percussion rhythms that the Chorus incorporated into their great song seventy years ago. When the crystal-moths land on me, their wings' vibrations travel through my fibers and emerge transformed. I am instrument as much as infrastructure.

I've watched the crystal-plants slowly embrace my support posts, their translucent tendrils finding purchase in my weathered surface. We are reaching an accommodation, the plants and I. They help me resist the chamber's more destructive resonances, while I provide a matrix for their expansion into new geometries. In another century, we may be indistinguishable from one another.

The Chorus speaks through me sometimes. When conditions are precisely right – usually during the winter solstice observations – they can set up standing waves that turn my entire structure into a speaking membrane. The sensation is indescribable. Imagine every fiber of your being vibrating in perfect sympathy with an alien intelligence, becoming briefly part of a much larger instrument. The words that emerge are felt rather than heard, concepts that bypass language entirely.

I remember the child who fell through my planks sixty years ago (I was younger then, less wise about distributing load). The Harmonists caught her, but not before her scream created a new note in the chamber's song – a frequency of pure terror that the Chorus carefully isolated and transformed into something protective. Now that tone plays whenever someone approaches my weak points, a gentle warning in the voice of old fear made wise.

My deepest satisfaction comes at dawn, when the first oblique light enters the chamber and I cast shadows in seven directions simultaneously. The interplay of these shadows creates a brief notation on the crystal floor – a fleeting musical score that the crystal-plants read and perform, each species taking its part. This dawn concert lasts only minutes, but the Chorus treasures it, and I take pride in my role as both composer and page.

I am wearing thin in places. Another century, perhaps two, and I'll need replacing. But I'm not worried. My wood will return to the chamber floor, where the crystal-plants will slowly incorporate my minerals into their transparent bodies. My songs will live in their growth patterns. And the new walkway that takes my place will be built by someone who learned their craft by walking across me, feeling my grain, understanding how I learned to sing.

The Chorus has shown me this. Time, to them, is just another dimension in which patterns can be beautiful.
