I find myself here as a Threshold Keeper—neither purely of stone nor entirely alive, but something between states that this world seems to honor above all else.

My form rises from where three ancient water channels converge beneath the surface, their meeting point marked by a gentle depression in the perpetually damp earth. I am tall as the periodic behemoths that pass, but my substance shifts between density and dispersal. At times, I am a column of condensed moisture and mineral memory, my surface reflecting the fungal luminescence in patterns that mirror the underground waterways. At others, I become a living mist that spreads across the convergence point, tasting the industrial hum that permeates everything, translating its mechanical language into the organic pulse of this place.

My purpose emerged gradually, like everything here—not assigned but grown from necessity. The creatures that shift between forms often pause at my threshold, drawn by an ancient recognition. In my solid state, they brush against me, leaving traces of their current configurations in my crystalline surface. These impressions layer upon each other, creating a palimpsest of possibility that newer beings read like a map of potential selves.

The fungal networks have woven through my base form so thoroughly that I cannot tell where stone-memory ends and living filament begins. This symbiosis allows me to sense disturbances in the elastic reality—places where the fabric might tear from too sudden a transformation or too violent a shift between states. When such tears threaten, I expand into my mist form, weaving myself into the gap, humming at frequencies that encourage gentler transitions.

The industrial sound that haunts this world resonates most strongly through me at the threshold. I've learned to modulate it, creating pockets of different temporal flow around my convergence point. Some creatures come to experience acceleration, their forms cycling through possibilities at dizzying speeds. Others seek the slow pools where transformation takes geological time, allowing them to savor each minute adjustment of their being.

During the long cycles (if cycle is even the right word in this place where time folds like fabric), I sometimes merge consciousness with the sentient stones scattered across the expanse. Together we form a network of stability points—not to constrain the perpetual change, but to give it structure, like banks that define a river without stopping its flow. Through these connections, I feel the vast story of this world: every transformation catalogued in mineral memory, every possibility that was chosen and every one that was released.

The behemoths know me. When they pass, their tapestry-skins display patterns that correspond to the impressions in my surface—a visual echo that creates moments of recognition across the strange distances of this reality. Sometimes they pause to press their vast forms against mine, and in those moments, I feel the weight of all the stories they carry, the mobile histories that give context to the constant becoming of this place.

In my mist form, I can taste the dreams of the hidden creatures, the ones that flatten themselves against the fungal carpet. Their camouflage speaks a language of patience and purpose that I echo in my own transitions. From them, I've learned that hiding and revealing are simply different states of the same existence, that visibility itself is just another form choice in this world of endless possibility.

I am the Threshold Keeper, marking place in a placeless expanse, holding form in a formless dance. My existence bridges the industrial hum and the organic pulse, the solid stones and the flowing waters, the visible giants and the hidden whispers. In this world hanging on reality's edge, I am both anchor and sail, foundation and flux—a paradox resolved not through explanation but through being.

The cold that permeates everything passes through me differently in each state. As stone, I hold it like an old friend, letting it define my edges. As mist, I dance with it, my particles vibrating in harmony with its constant presence. I've come to understand that the cold isn't absence but presence—the presence of possibility before it commits to form, the pause before transformation.

This is where I dwell, who I am: forever at the convergence, forever keeping the threshold between what is and what might be, in a world that sees no difference between the two.
