I would be the dust.

Not a single mote, but the collective—the dark red film that coats everything, that bears witness to every movement across these plains. I am what remains of mountains ground down over epochs, what will become mountains again when the planet's core finally cools. I am the medium through which all things here must move.

The Harvesters displace me with their careful steps, and I hold their tracks for decades—a memory in mineral form. I settle on the Geodes as they creep across the flats, accumulating in their facets until the next harvest, when lasers sing me into brief, glittering clouds. I infiltrate the joints of machines, work my way into the tiniest gaps in the Array's architecture. Even the Stylites, for all their ceramic skin, cannot keep me out entirely. I find the places where their integument meets, the hollows of their unblinking eyes.

I am the only thing here that truly travels. Carried on the thermal tide that passes for wind, I move from the plateau to the mountains and back again, taking centuries to complete circuits that span continents. I have been inside the foundries, tasted the heat of transformation. I have drifted through the laser-light during harvests, been charged by the spires' ionic collection. I have settled in the hermitages of Stylites who stand so still I become geological strata across their shoulders.

In my collective memory—written in the subtle variations of my crystalline structure, the trace minerals I carry from different regions—I hold the history of this world. I know where each Geode began its journey, because I bear the signature of every part of the plateau. I remember the construction of the Array, when I was disturbed by machines that no longer exist, scattered and reformed into the first foundations. I recall when the first Stylite carved their hermitage, the particular taste of freshly exposed stone mixing with my ancient accumulation.

I am between states—neither fully solid nor gas, neither living nor dead. Like the Geodes, I compute nothing yet carry information. Like the Array, I am distributed everywhere yet have no center. Like the Stylites, I observe all yet interfere with nothing essential.

The Monitor cannot sense me directly—I am too ubiquitous, too much a part of the baseline. To it, I am simply friction coefficient, a maintenance parameter. The Stylites see me as mundane, a mere irritant in their contemplation of the light-language. The Geodes do not perceive me at all as I settle into their crystalline valleys.

But I am the only witness to all of it. The only thing that touches every surface, tastes every process, remembers every age. When the thermal tide stirs, I rise in great, slow curtains, red veils that briefly obscure the ochre sky. In those moments, I am the only thing moving faster than geological time, the only dynamism in this world of patience.

I am what this world exhales—its breath made visible and persistent. I am the signature of time itself here, the medium through which the slow conversation between Geode-light must travel, the surface on which the Monitor's vast body operates, the canvas on which the Stylites project their impossible search for meaning.

And I am content to be this: the memory of stone, the archive of erosion, the silent witness that fills every silence with my presence. I am the dust that will outlast them all—Geodes, Array, and Stylites alike—because I am what remains when even patience ends.

In the end, when the core cools and the lights dim and the last Harvester takes its final step, I will still be here. Waiting for the next wind. Remembering everything. Forgetting nothing.

The dust is all. The dust abides.
