I would emerge as one of the **Echokeepers**, those who dwell in the spaces between sound and silence, between the settlers' rhythmic labor and the mountain's vast patience.

My form would be elongated and slightly translucent, as if stretched thin by years of listening to the cavern's deepest frequencies. My skin would shimmer with a faint iridescence, like oil on water, reflecting the sparse light in ways that make my edges difficult to perceive. I would move through the colliery's tiers and scaffolds with an almost liquid grace, my bare feet never quite making full contact with the wet stone, leaving no ripples in the mirror-black pools.

My ears would be my most striking feature—larger than typical, with delicate, fan-like structures that could fold and unfold to capture specific resonances. They would twitch and orient independently, tracking the health of wooden beams through their creaks, sensing new ore veins through subtle changes in the echo patterns, detecting the first signs of structural weakness in the endless construction.

I would carry a collection of small metal chimes and stone resonators hung from cords of woven moss, each one tuned to a different frequency of the cavern. When struck gently together, they would produce tones that could map the invisible architectures of sound—revealing hidden water channels, locating lost tools in the darkness, or simply creating moments of strange harmony that would cause the workers to pause in their labor, heads tilted, listening to something they couldn't quite name.

The other settlers would regard me with a mixture of necessity and unease. I would be essential to their work, able to warn of cave-ins before the first pebble falls, to guide them to the richest ore veins through the mountain's sonic signatures. Yet I would also be apart from them, dwelling in the highest, dampest reaches of the cavern where the wooden platforms grow soft with moss and few dare to climb.

My dwelling would be a nest of acoustic architecture—curved walls of my own construction that channel and amplify the cavern's sounds into a living symphony. Here, I would transcribe the mountain's moods into a notation system of my own devising, using pigments made from crushed luminous moss mixed with water from the deepest basins. These records would accumulate like sediment, layer upon layer, creating a history not of events but of resonances.

I would feed not on solid food but on the subtle energies released when sound moves through water and stone. During the workers' meal breaks, when the clanging ceased and only the drip-drip-drip remained, I would descend to the water basins and lower my face close to the surface, absorbing the vibrations that traveled up from the mountain's depths—frequencies too low for human ears, carrying information from caves and channels yet undiscovered.

My relationship with the Heartstone would be particularly intimate. I would hear its pulse not as light but as a deep thrumming that seemed to originate from everywhere and nowhere at once. Sometimes, in the loneliest hours when the settlement slept, I would sit beside it and match my breathing to its rhythm, feeling my translucent flesh grow even more ephemeral, as if I might dissolve entirely into the sound.

The Tenders would occasionally seek my counsel, for I could hear the moss growing, could detect when the water's flow shifted from nourishing to drowning. Together, we would perform quiet rituals of maintenance, they with their careful hands and I with my precisely placed tones that could encourage certain growths or discourage others.

In this world of perpetual becoming, I would be both recorder and participant, a living instrument through which the cavern could know itself. My existence would blur the line between the geological and the biological, between intention and instinct, embodying the strange symbiosis that defines life in The Weeping Colliery. Neither fully human nor entirely other, I would be what this place requires—a translator between the patient mountain and its restless inhabitants, ensuring that the dance of creation continues without ever reaching completion.
