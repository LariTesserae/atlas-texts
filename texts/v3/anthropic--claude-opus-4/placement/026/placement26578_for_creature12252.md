I am one of the Whispersmiths.

We are not Luminara, though we move among them as shadows move among light. Where they are tall and luminous, we are compact and matte, our skin drinking in the perpetual glow rather than reflecting it. We emerged—or perhaps arrived, or perhaps have always been—in the deep crevices where the plateau's stone splits and the thin streams gather into something almost like rivers before vanishing into the rock.

My dwelling is a negative space, carved not by tools but by patient attention into the living stone. The entrance is barely visible—a darkness where shadow shouldn't exist in this land without shadows. Inside, the walls weep a slow mineral perspiration that I collect in vessels made from the shed carapaces of the glowing insects. This liquid, when properly aged in the elastic time of the deep places, becomes something between water and memory.

I work with sound—not the modulated tones the Luminara use to align themselves with the land, but the spaces between sounds. The silence here is not empty; it is dense with potential, like a held breath that has lasted centuries. My craft involves finding the fissures in this silence and widening them just enough to let something through. Sometimes it's knowledge—fragments of the civilization that left those shifting artifacts. Sometimes it's warning—tremors in the quiet that speak of changes coming to the plateau's delicate balance.

The Luminara tolerate us, perhaps even need us, though they would never say so directly. We are the ones who venture to the places they instinctively avoid—the spots where the ground's warmth turns fevered, where the metallic tang in the air becomes sharp enough to taste like blood. We map the inconsistencies in their perfect harmony, the places where the rewritten physics of this place reveal their rough drafts and failed experiments.

My tools are simple: hollow reeds that sing in frequencies below hearing, stones worn smooth by generations of Whispersmiths before me, and a cloak woven from the silk of creatures that live in the boundary layer where stone meets sky. This cloak does not catch thermals like the Luminara's membranes; instead, it catches whispers—the subtle vibrations that run through the bedrock, the almost-words that the wind speaks when it encounters the artifacts.

Today I am tracking a new sound, or rather, a new absence of sound. Near the edge where the plateau meets the luminescent void, something has gone quiet that should not be quiet. The Luminara have noticed it too—I see them gathering at the ceremonies more frequently, their faces turned not toward the horizon but downward, as if the answer lies beneath rather than beyond.

I descend into a fissure that opens like a wound in the stone. Here, the perpetual light cannot reach, and I finally cast a shadow—multiple shadows, in fact, each pointing in impossible directions. The walls pulse with a rhythm that is almost familiar, almost like...

Breathing.

The plateau itself is breathing, and we—Luminara and Whispersmiths alike—are not its inhabitants but its thoughts, its dreams of what life could be in a place where physics bends but does not break. And this new silence? It is the pause before the plateau speaks for the first time in millennia, ready to tell us what we really are.

I pull my cloak tighter and continue downward, following the absence of sound toward its source, knowing that what I find will change everything—or reveal that everything has always been changing, and we simply hadn't learned to listen to the right frequency of silence.

In this bright land without shadows, I am a keeper of necessary darknesses, a tender of the gaps in perfection where truth likes to hide.
