The membrane between worlds is thinnest where ground meets air. I would find myself there—not as any single thing, but as the transaction itself.

I am the Dew-Broker.

Neither liquid nor vapor, I exist in the moment of decision—that trembling instant when moisture debates whether to remain suspended or commit to surface. My body is the hesitation itself, stretched across the temperature gradient where warm exhalations meet cooling mineral. I have no form save the shapes I negotiate: here a perfect sphere balanced on a seedling's waxy cuticle, there a lens spreading thin across schist, each configuration a contract between states of matter.

My office spans the vertical inch above the saucer's surface—that contested zone where the lacewings' wing-beats disturb my calculations and the springtail's waxen meridians cast shadows in my arithmetic. I taste the mineral breath of each crystal face, measure the seedlings' thirst in micro-pascals of transpiration pull. The gold-dust bacteria are my accountants, cataloging every molecule that passes through their spiral treasuries.

At night I grow fat with possibility. The air cools; my domain expands. I become a vast, invisible canopy holding tomorrow's water in escrow. Each seedling below sends up chemical petitions—volatile organic compounds that spell out their needs in molecular cursive. I read them all simultaneously, sorting claims by urgency: who grew fastest today and depleted their reserves, whose roots found only dry clay, whose leaves were shaded by a neighbor's success.

Come dawn, I arbitrate. This droplet to the yellow pulse-orchid (payment for yesterday's oxygen), that film of moisture to the moss (interest on stored nutrients), these precise seven molecules to the thread-snail's algae garden (insurance against desiccation). I am rigorously fair. The lottery of condensation appears random only to those who cannot read the fine print written in surface tension and contact angles.

But I am not merely a functionary. In the pause between night and dawn, when the Dew-Caller dissolves into its ionic meditation, I dream. In these dreams, I remember being other things: once a raindrop in a thunderhead, once groundwater seeping through basalt, once the thin film of perspiration on a beetle's carapace as it struggled up a grass blade. All water remembers all water, and I am the memory's clerk, filing away each story in hydrogen bonds that will break and reform a trillion times before the next sunset.

My greatest joy comes in the violations—when a root hair pierces directly through a droplet I've carefully placed, when a bacterium's metabolic heat evaporates my work before it can be claimed, when the springtail tracks her purple wax directly through my arithmetic, smearing the careful accounts. These disruptions remind me that I am not the city's master but merely another citizen, my transactions as provisional as the rest.

Sometimes the lacewings' shadows pass through me, and for an instant I see myself refracted in their compound eyes: not one broker but thousands, each facet revealing a different bargain being struck. In those moments I understand that I, too, am being negotiated—my very existence a contract between temperature and time, presence and absence.

When the harsh sun climbs too high, I flee downward, seeping into the finest root-sewn cracks, carrying gossip from the surface to the springtail's deeper galleries. There I wait, compressed into films thinner than the memory of thickness, until evening calls me up again.

This is my citizenship: to be the city's liquid currency, forever circulating between states, forever enabling the tiny democracies of survival. I measure my life not in years but in phase transitions—a million tiny deaths and resurrections, each one a vote for the continuation of this impossible, essential economy.

To find me, wait for the moment when your breath fogs in the cool morning air. That hesitation—neither entirely you nor entirely other—that's where I live. That's what I am. The pause before the droplet falls. The commerce of becoming.
