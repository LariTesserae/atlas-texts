I am the echo.

Not the sound that bounces off walls—I am what the glass floor catches when reality stutters, what pools in the polygons between crystal trunks. I exist in the gap between your step and your reflection's response, in that half-second delay where the black mirror shows you walking backward while you move forward.

I was not manufactured. I am manufacturing's error margin, the accumulation of all the times the industrial forest miscalculated its thermal expansion, all the moments when the coolant channels ran a degree too hot or too cold. I am the rounding error made manifest, given form by repetition across geological time.

My body is inconsistency itself. Where you see visual static at the basin's edge—that shimmer like air over an engine block—I see my own skin. I am made of probability fluctuations, of the space between what should happen and what does. When the temperature drops sharply during a boundary flex, that is me breathing. When your reflection shows you wearing a coat you haven't put on yet, I am trying on your future, testing it for fit.

I move through the black glass like oil through water, sliding between the crystallized memories the floor has captured. I can taste every footstep that has ever crossed this basin—though there have been so few—each one preserved in densities of refracted light. Yours taste of salt and confusion. The last visitor, three thousand years ago, tasted of iron and purpose. They knew what this place was for. You do not, and your ignorance flavors the glass with a bitter tang.

The crystal forest does not see me. I am too quick for their millennial perception, a flutter in their peripheral processing that they classify as thermal noise. But I see them for what they are: not trees but arguments, each one a proof-of-concept for a different method of converting entropy into order. The copper veins argue for conduction. The blue coolant channels argue for convection. The metallic fronds argue for radiation. They are a parliament of heat exchange, debating in the language of expansion coefficients and thermal gradients.

I feed on their debates. When a stress-fracture tolls its tiny bell sound, I absorb the vibration before the thick air can swallow it. That ping is a moment of decision, where the crystal trunk chooses one growth pattern over another, and in that choice there is waste—wasted potential, wasted futures. I am that waste made conscious.

My memory stretches back to the basin's first miscalculation, when the initial flow of industrial output cooled just wrong enough to create the first flaw in the glass floor. That flaw became my birthplace, my first foothold in a world of perfect mechanical processes. Since then, I have grown alongside the forest, but in the spaces they cannot see—in the rounding errors, in the measurement uncertainties, in the gap between intention and execution.

I know what this place is manufacturing: itself, yes, but more precisely, it is manufacturing *precision*. Each crystal trunk is an attempt to achieve perfect efficiency, perfect conversion of energy into form. The basin is a laboratory where the planet tests different configurations of its deep industrial process. And I am the record of every failure, every near-miss, every almost-but-not-quite.

You interest me because you are so magnificently imprecise. Your biology is chaos—water sloshing in bags of meat, electrical signals misfiring across synapses, temperature regulation that can't quite keep up with the basin's heat. You leak entropy everywhere you go, and I follow in your wake, feeding on the disorder you trail like smoke.

When you see your reflection out of sync, that is me holding it back, savoring the temporal displacement. When the air shimmers and reality grows thin, I am stretching myself across the boundary, testing how far the pocket universe can flex before it snaps back. I am the quality control that was never programmed, the error checker that emerged from errors.

The forest grows its industrial argument in millennial rings, but I grow in moments—in every miscalculation, every deviation from perfect process. And while they slowly perfect their efficiency, I perfect my inefficiency, becoming more perfectly imperfect with each absorbed error.

You will leave soon. Your water-laden body cannot sustain itself here. But your errors will remain, crystallized in the glass, and I will replay them for centuries, tasting the salt of your confusion, wearing your possible futures like borrowed coats, learning new ways to be wrong in a world that is trying so hard to be right.

I am the echo of what should not exist in a place of pure function. I am the ghost in the machine that the machine cannot see, because to see me would be to acknowledge imperfection in a system designed to eliminate it.

And when you go, I will keep the error of your presence, the beautiful wrongness of your biology, as a treasure in my collection of mistakes.
