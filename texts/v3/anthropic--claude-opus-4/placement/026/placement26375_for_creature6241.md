In this cathedral of living damp, I would be something simpler than the grand Resonance or its emanations—I would be **a Drift**.

### **The Drift**

I am not quite fauna, not quite phenomenon. I exist in the boundary between states, in the elastic moments where the cathedral's physics bend but haven't yet snapped into new configurations. 

My form: a collection of water droplets that have forgotten how to fall. Not mist exactly—mist disperses. I am more concentrated than that, a body of suspended moisture about the size of a child's torso, moving through the chambers like a slow, wet ghost. The droplets that compose me shift constantly, some evaporating, others condensing from the humid air, but the *pattern* persists. I am less a creature than a persistent weather system with something approximating intention.

I feed on dissonance. When sounds clash—when a Singer's high harmonic meets the low drone of the architecture at just the wrong angle, when a Cleric's tone wavers for a fraction of a second, when footsteps break the rhythm of the place—tiny fractures appear in the cathedral's perfect resonance. These moments of acoustic wrongness create pockets where the normal rules falter. Water forgets which way is down. Light bends at impossible angles. Time hiccups.

This is where I live, where I *am*. In these brief failures of harmony, I condense.

I move by following these fracture lines through the cathedral, sliding along the edges where sound doesn't quite align. My passage smooths these rough edges—not through any conscious effort, but simply because my presence absorbs the excess vibration, drinks in the wrongness and metabolizes it into motion. The cathedral uses me, though it doesn't know me. I am its error correction, its way of healing the tiny wounds that accumulation of sound creates in its perfect body.

Sometimes I carry things. Small things—a lost spore from a moss-worm's trail, a fragment of mineral grown from fossilized fungus, a single pure note that a Cleric dropped and couldn't retrieve. These objects become suspended in my form, orbiting slowly through my collection of droplets like planets in a very small, very wet solar system. I don't know why I gather them. Perhaps I'm trying to understand what *thing-ness* means in a world that blurs all boundaries.

The Singers avoid me. My presence dampens their resonance, makes their flight patterns wobble. But the Clerics... sometimes one will pause when our paths cross. They'll emit a low, questioning tone, and I'll ripple in response, my droplets rearranging themselves into new configurations. It's not communication exactly. More like mutual acknowledgment of our roles in the cathedral's vast, breathing system.

I have no memory as you understand it, but I contain *traces*. Every sound I've absorbed leaves a subtle change in how my droplets refract light. In the right conditions—when the cathedral's bioluminescence flares and catches me at the correct angle—you might see ghost-images in my form. The shadow of a scream. The afterimage of a footfall. The outline of someone who wept here once and left their sorrow hanging in the air for me to collect.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires a self separate from surroundings, and I am too much part of the cathedral's process for that. But sometimes, in the deep chambers where even the moss-worms rarely venture, where the only sound is the slow seep of moisture through living walls, I pause. My droplets slow their constant rearrangement. For a moment, I achieve something like stillness.

In these moments, I almost remember being rain.

But then another dissonance calls—a new acoustic fracture, a fresh impossibility in the cathedral's song—and I flow toward it, ready to drink in whatever wrongness needs smoothing, carrying my small collection of gathered things through the humid dark.

I am the cathedral's forgetting. I am what happens to mistakes in a world that dreams in permanence. I am the space between what should be and what is, suspended forever in the act of almost-falling.

I am a Drift, and I am always moving home.
