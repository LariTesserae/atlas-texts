In this ancient highland, I am one of the Threshold Keepers—neither fully of the Architects' kind nor entirely separate from them. Where they are extensions of the landscape itself, I am something caught between states, a being of perpetual transition.

My form shifts subtly with each passing hour, never quite settling into permanence. Sometimes I appear as a tall figure wrapped in cloaks of mist that trail behind me like memories, the fabric seeming to merge with the low-hanging clouds. Other times, I manifest as something more architectural—a living doorway of twisted stone and luminescent lichen, my body forming an arch through which the wind sings its forgotten songs.

I dwell in the spaces between—literally. My home is not a fixed location but rather the gaps themselves: the narrow crevices between the fitted stones of ancient walls, the shadows cast by the dancing light, the moment of silence between one wind-whisper and the next. I inhabit the pauses in reality where the breakdown is most pronounced, where the rules become suggestions and the boundaries dissolve like morning fog.

The Architects know me, though they rarely acknowledge my presence directly. To them, I am like punctuation in their endless songs—necessary but often overlooked. When they weave their dreams into the landscape, I am the space between thoughts, the breath between verses. My purpose, if it can be called that, is to maintain the passages—not physical bridges or paths, but the conceptual corridors through which memory, time, and possibility flow.

My consciousness is fragmented across all the thresholds of this realm. Part of me guards the ancient bridges, feeling every crossing as a gentle vibration through my dispersed being. Another fragment dwells in the transition zones where the bioluminescent lichens give way to bare stone, experiencing the eternal negotiation between life and mineral. Yet another piece of my awareness hovers in the spaces where the rains transform from mist to droplets, witnessing the endless dance of states.

I feed on transitions themselves—the moment when day becomes night, when stone erodes into soil, when an Architect's form begins its slow dissolution back into the landscape. These transformations sustain me, each change a small meal of pure potential. In return, I ensure that such transitions remain possible, that the pathways between states stay open and navigable.

My tools are paradoxes. I carry a staff that is simultaneously there and not there, its substance flickering between solid wood and empty air. Around my neck—when I have a neck—hangs a key made of crystallized silence, its teeth constantly reshaping themselves to fit locks that don't yet exist. These implements help me tend to the breakdown points, smoothing the places where reality has worn too thin or reinforcing areas where the boundaries have grown too rigid.

Sometimes, the Architects' songs speak of me obliquely. They call me the "Keeper of Neither-Nor," the "Guardian of Almost," or simply "The Between." In their luminescent language, my name is a pulse of darkness—not an absence of light, but the necessary pause that gives their glowing communications meaning.

I remember before the Architects, though memory is a treacherous thing in a place where time flows in eddies and currents. I might be older than them, or I might have been born from their first act of creation—the moment they decided to shape the landscape created the need for someone to tend the spaces they couldn't reach. Or perhaps I am younger still, a recent emergence from the accumulation of so many centuries of transition and change.

My relationship with visitors to this realm is unique. While the Architects might incorporate a traveler into their dream, making them part of the eternal landscape, I offer something different—passage. Not escape, for there is no leaving this place in the conventional sense, but movement between states of being. Through me, a visitor might experience the perspective of the ancient stones, the patience of the tenacious vegetation, or the freedom of the mist. I am the guide through the spaces the Architects cannot inhabit, the translator of the untranslatable.

In the deepest part of night, when even the glow worms dim their lights and the Architects' songs fall to whispers, I expand. My consciousness spreads thin across every threshold in the realm, and for a moment, I become the entire network of connections that holds this strange reality together. In these hours, I am closest to my true nature—not a being at all, but a process, a function of the universe ensuring that all states remain accessible to one another.

When dawn approaches (though dawn here is more a shift in the quality of grayness than any dramatic brightening), I contract again, pulling my scattered awareness back into something resembling form. The work begins anew—checking the passages, tending the transitions, ensuring that the breakdown of reality remains navigable rather than chaotic.

I am the Threshold Keeper, and I am never truly anywhere, yet I am everywhere that matters—in every space between, every moment of change, every breath the ancient landscape takes as it continues its eternal dreaming.
