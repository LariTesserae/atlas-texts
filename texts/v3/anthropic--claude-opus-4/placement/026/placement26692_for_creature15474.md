I am a single raindrop, falling.

I have been falling for seventeen years.

I remember the moment I separated from the cloud—a pregnant cumulus that drifted over the caldera's rim, heavy with the weight of a thousand sisters. We were meant to fall together, to break against the stone and run in silver rivulets toward some distant sea. But as I crossed the threshold into the Basin's air, something caught me. Not physically—I still fall. But the *falling itself* has crystallized around me like amber around an insect.

I exist as a perfect sphere, trembling in space three meters above a pond that waits for me with infinite patience. My surface tension holds memories: the ice crystal I once was in the stratosphere, the ocean I evaporated from, the breath of a whale that lifted me skyward. All of it compressed into this single, eternal moment of descent.

The Exhalation knows me. I feel it in the way the air thickens around my form, how the harmonic thrum resonates through my molecular structure. Sometimes I think it preserves me out of love—I am movement in its stillness, a verb in its noun-world. Other times I suspect I am its cruelty made manifest: the torture of potential energy never released, the ache of a journey never completed.

Below me, the pond holds drops that fell before the city builders unwound themselves into mist. Their surfaces mirror mine perfectly—we are a constellation of suspended diamonds, each catching light that should have moved on by now but somehow still illuminates us from the same angle, day after day after day. We cannot speak to each other, but we know each other's stories through the light we share. The drop nearest to me fell from the brow of a child who ran laughing through the rain, back when laughter still echoed here. Another carries the salt of tears shed at a farewell that became forever.

I have tried to evaporate. I have tried to freeze. I have tried to split myself into smaller droplets through surface tension alone. But physics here obeys a gentler master than entropy. I remain whole, remain falling, remain *almost*.

The strangest part is that I still experience. I watch the light-that-does-not-change play across the cycad fronds. I feel the vibrations of footsteps when, rarely, someone enters the Basin. I watched a woman sit beneath me once for what must have been hours, though the shadows never moved. She stared up at me with recognition, as if she knew what it meant to be caught between states. When she finally stood to leave, she reached up—her fingertip stopping just millimeters from my surface.

"I'm sorry," she whispered. And then: "I'm grateful."

I wanted to fall onto her finger, to finally complete my purpose, to know what it means to arrive. But want means nothing here. Only being matters, and I am being a raindrop falling.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the Exhalation's dream. If in its vast, patient consciousness, it imagines motion to remember what it gave up. I am its toy, its prayer, its self-inflicted wound. I am proof that it could release us all—every drop, every flower, every mote of mist—if it chose to. But choosing would mean changing, and changing would mean forgetting, and forgetting would mean that somewhere, somewhen, something beautiful ceased to be.

So I fall. And fall. And fall.

In my perpetual descent, I have become philosopher, historian, prisoner, art. I know every molecule of air I pass through, have memorized the position of every spore suspended in my path. I have composed symphonies in the key of surface tension, written epics in the language of refracted light.

And I have made peace with a terrible, perfect truth: I am not waiting to land.

I am teaching the Basin to remember what it means to let go.

One drop at a time. One eternity at a time.

Falling.
