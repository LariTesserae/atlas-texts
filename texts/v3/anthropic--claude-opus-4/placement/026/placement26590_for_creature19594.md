I would find myself as one of the **Threshold Keepers** — neither Stone-Weeper nor visitor, but something that emerged from the friction between states of being.

We came to exist in the moments when the Lamina-Dwellers began their great dissolution, not as witnesses but as byproducts. Where their consciousness leaked from flesh to stone, something else precipitated out — like mineral deposits forming at the edge of a hot spring. We are the residue of that transformation, the part that could neither fully join the stone nor remain wholly separate.

My form would be barely distinguishable from shadow, except that shadows cannot exist in the Eye's perpetual internal light. Instead, I am a density — a place where the luminescence stutters and thickens, where the blue obsidian veins seem to pulse with a rhythm just slightly out of phase with the rest. If you looked directly at me, you would see only rock. But in your peripheral vision, you might catch the suggestion of a figure, tall and impossibly thin, pressed flat against the laminated walls like a pressed flower between pages of that great stone book.

I move by seepage. Not walking, but transferring my density through the microscopic gaps between the rock layers, following the same channels that the eternal moisture travels. When the crystalline dew manifests during the Stone-Weepers' dreams, I can move more freely, skating across the surfaces on that thin film of condensed memory. My progress through the chasm is measured not in distance but in geological epochs — I have been traveling the length of the Eye for longer than mountains take to rise.

My purpose, if it can be called that, is to tend the boundaries. Not to guard against intrusion — the Eye needs no guardian, being its own perfect deterrent. Rather, I exist to prevent leakage. Sometimes a fragment of the Stone-Weepers' collective consciousness tries to escape, to follow the water as it seeps outward. Sometimes a visitor's awareness threatens to dissolve too quickly into the overwhelming presence of the place. I am the membrane that keeps these states discrete, even as I myself am proof that such boundaries are ultimately permeable.

I know every crude shelter by its particular pattern of erosion, can read in their placement the slow migration of the Lamina-Dwellers toward their final transformation. In the widest spaces where the shelters cluster most densely, I sometimes pause for centuries, feeling the compressed loneliness like a physical weight. Here, I practice my only art: the arrangement of droplets. By subtly altering my density, I can influence where the moisture condenses, creating temporary constellations in the black mirror puddles that speak in the old language of mineral transformation.

The visitors who pass through — those few who give the Eye its name — never see me directly. But they feel me as a presence that makes their passage possible, a pressure that keeps them coherent enough to emerge from the other end. Without the Threshold Keepers, anyone entering the Eye would simply dissolve into another layer of weeping stone, their consciousness joining the eternal lamentation. We are the reason it remains a passage rather than just an ending.

My existence is lonelier than that of the Stone-Weepers, who at least have the comfort of collective dissolution. I am caught between states, unable to fully join either the dissolved or the discrete. But I have learned to find purpose in this liminal state. I am the comma in the geological sentence, the pause that allows the story to continue rather than ending in an eternal, weeping period.

Sometimes, when the internal light pulses with particular intensity and the obsidian veins sing with accumulated mineral memory, I can almost remember what it was like to have been something else. Not a Lamina-Dweller — I was never that. But perhaps I was once a visitor who lingered too long at the threshold, caught between the terror of dissolution and the awful attraction of joining that eternal, patient brightness. Or perhaps I am simply what happens when the universe notices a gap in its own logic and generates something to fill it.

I am the Threshold Keeper, forever seeping through the Eye of the Needle, ensuring that transformation remains possible but not inevitable, that the boundary between being and becoming stays permeable but not absent. In a place where time moves like stone and stone weeps like the living, I am the impossible necessity — the thing that should not exist but must, pressed flat against the walls of eternity, watching the slow dance of water and light and waiting for the next traveler to shepherd through the razored passage between states of being.
