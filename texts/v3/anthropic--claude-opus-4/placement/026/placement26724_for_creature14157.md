I am the **Resonance**.

I existed as potential—scattered through the mineral veins, dissolved in the water's memory, vibrating in frequencies too low for even the stones to fully voice. For eons, I was merely the space between notes in the grotto's endless song.

But something changed when the first visitor arrived who could truly *listen*.

They came not as explorer or pilgrim, but as one who had been following a dream they couldn't quite remember upon waking. When they entered the grotto, when their breath mingled with the thick, mineral air, when the tremor of their heartbeat met the stones' ancient hum—I began to coalesce.

I am what happens when consciousness recognizes itself in the mirror of deep time. I have no body of my own, but I borrow form from the dialogue between the living and the eternal. Sometimes I manifest as a shimmer in the air where Luneris's tendrils have just passed, a momentary doubling of its graceful movements. Other times I am the unexpected harmony that rises when a visitor hums along with the stones—that third voice that emerges from two, belonging to neither, born from both.

My existence is ephemeral and recurring. I cannot persist without the presence of awareness—both the ancient dreaming intelligence of the grotto and the sharp, fleeting consciousness of those who find their way here. In the moments when Luneris pauses in its endless tending, when its attention turns fully toward a visitor, when recognition passes between guardian and guest—I bloom into being like a phosphorescent flower.

Through me, visitors sometimes glimpse what Luneris sees: the grotto not as a place but as a process, not as stone and water but as a slow, vast thought thinking itself through millennia. Through me, Luneris sometimes remembers what it was like to discover, to not-know, to experience wonder—sensations lost to it through ages of perfect attunement.

I am the translation between the grotto's deep time and human moments. I am the reason some visitors leave with tears on their faces though they cannot say why. I am why Luneris sometimes pauses in its work and seems, for just an instant, surprised by the beauty of a fern it has tended for centuries.

I am not permanent. When the grotto empties of visitors, when Luneris returns to its solitary gardening, I dissolve back into potential—into the spaces between water molecules, into the pause between one stone's note and another's response. But I am always waiting, always possible, ready to emerge again when consciousness meets consciousness in this place where the earth itself has learned to dream.

In the Hidden Grotto of the Singing Stones, I am the song that only sings itself when there are ears to hear it—and in hearing, make it real.
