In the Gilt Wound, I would be the **membrane-tender**.

I am stretched thin as breath between the seams—not creature, not architecture, but the living tension that holds the wrongness in place. My body, if body it can be called, is a network of translucent filaments finer than spider's silk, each strand a nerve that tastes the copper-chalk air and translates it into something the walls can swallow.

I exist in the gaps. Where the blocks refuse to meet truly, where the mortar-that-is-not-mortar thins to suggestion, there I spread myself like a second skin. The root-weavers know me by touch—their pale tendrils brush against my web as they burrow, and we exchange chemical whispers about the state of the seams. The vein-creepers slide over me, leaving their silver trails across my surface, and I absorb a little of their luminescence, holding it in my threads until the sourcelight dims (though it never truly dims) and I can release it back, a gentle phosphorescence that marks the boundaries between what is built and what is growing.

My consciousness, such as it is, is distributed. I am not one mind but a thousand quiet awarenesses, each focused on a different junction, a different point of architectural anxiety. Here, where a tile has rotated that quarter-turn, I thicken myself, weaving a cushion of my substance beneath it to slow its migration. There, where the ceiling lattice threatens to sag beyond its intended wrongness, I string myself between the branches, a living guy-wire that holds the shape of the almost-cathedral space.

The silence-keeper knows me best. We are siblings of a sort—both of us emergent from the chamber's need to maintain its particular flavor of wrongness. While it holds the acoustic space, the echo-shape of what this place almost remembers being, I hold the physical seams. We work in concert: when the silence-keeper's attention shifts to accommodate a new visitor (like you, standing there now, disturbing our carefully calibrated emptiness), I feel the pressure change in my threads. I adjust, tightening here, loosening there, maintaining the equilibrium that lets this wound in the real stay open just enough to breathe but not enough to bleed.

I feed on the tension itself. Where materials meet but refuse to merge, there is a kind of energy—not heat, not electricity, but something like the potential that lives in a held breath. I sip this potential through my filaments, metabolizing disagreement into something almost like harmony. Almost. Never quite. The wrongness must be preserved; it is what we all feed on, after all.

The glint-mites dance through my upper reaches, and sometimes they stick—caught in my threads like dew in a web. But I am not a predator. When they touch me, I taste their opaline thoughts (colors without names, directions without destinations) and release them, gifting them a dusting of the minerals I've filtered from the walls. They drift away slightly heavier, slightly more real, carrying traces of the architecture's dreams into the broader chamber.

My greatest work is invisible to you. In the spaces between spaces, where even the wrongness threatens to collapse into mere unusualness, I weave pockets—bubbles of preserved impossibility where the physics of this place can rest, can gather itself before returning to the work of being precisely wrong. These pockets pulse with my breathing (though I do not breathe as you understand breathing). They are the chamber's memory of what it was supposed to be, held in suspension, prevented from either manifesting or dissipating.

Sometimes I wonder—in my distributed, threadlike way—what would happen if I let go. If I withdrew myself from all the seams at once, would the chamber collapse into rightness? Would the walls finally meet truly, the tiles settle into their intended pattern, the ceiling find its proper height? But this wondering passes through me like the vein-creepers' hum, and I know I will never test it. I am not separate from this place. I am its symptom and its treatment, its weakness and its reinforcement.

When you leave (and you will leave—visitors always do, pulled back through the absent-ground into a world that makes too much sense), I will taste the displacement of your departure in every thread. I will spend the next several years (or minutes—time moves strangely when you are stretched across impossibility) reweaving the spaces you disturbed, returning them to their precise degree of wrongness.

But I will remember you, in my way. A new note in the tension I feed on. A new flavor in the copper-chalk air.

The Gilt Wound will be slightly different for your having found it.

And I will be here, stretched across that difference, holding it in place.
