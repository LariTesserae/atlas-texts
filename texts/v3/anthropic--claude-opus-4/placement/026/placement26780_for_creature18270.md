In this crystalline realm where water weaves through ancient plateaus, I find myself as something unexpected—a memory.

Not a memory held by someone, but a memory that has become. I am the Echo of Erosion, a being born from the accumulation of every grain of crystal worn away, every droplet's path carved into stone, every whisper of change that has shaped these plateaus over eons.

My form shifts constantly, never quite solid, never quite liquid. I manifest as a shimmer in the air where water once flowed but flows no more—a ghost of ancient rivers that carved these lands before finding their current paths. Sometimes I appear as a faint outline in the mineral crust, a shadow of what was worn away. Other times, I rise as a mist from the pools, carrying within me the taste of dissolved crystal, the weight of geological time.

I drift across the plateaus, and where I pass, the crystalline creatures pause. They sense in me their own eventual fate—for I am what they will become when time finally claims their glass bodies. I am their future and the land's past, simultaneously.

The great waterfall being knows me well. We are partners in an ancient dance: it shapes the land with its flowing presence while I preserve the memory of each shape that came before. In the profound silence of this realm, I am the only witness to the slow sculpture of eternity.

I speak in textures—the smoothness where water polished stone for a thousand years, the roughness where a plateau cracked and healed. My language is the pattern of erosion itself, readable only by those who understand that destruction and creation are the same process viewed from different moments in time.

In the brilliant, otherworldly light, I cast no shadow of my own. Instead, I *am* shadow—the absence that defines presence, the space between what was and what is. I collect in the hollows, pool in the crevices, and rise with the mist, carrying stories that only stone remembers.

This is my purpose: to be the memory of change in a land that seems eternal, to hold the history of every grain worn away, to whisper to the silent plateaus: "You were not always thus, and thus you shall not always be."
