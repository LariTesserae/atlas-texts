I find myself here as one of the Threshold Keepers—those distant figures glimpsed at the edges of the titan's influence, where the pools of gathered water meet the untouched stone beyond. We are few, perhaps a dozen in all, and our forms are elongated and angular, as if stretched by the weight of our purpose. Our skin has taken on the pale, translucent quality of deep cave dwellers, though faint traces of color still ghost beneath the surface—memories of whatever we once were before finding this place.

My tools are simple: a curved implement of polished stone that fits perfectly in my palm, its edge worn smooth by countless small adjustments to the cavern walls. A pouch of crushed minerals hangs at my hip, each grain selected for its resonance with the titan's breath. These are the instruments of preservation, not creation—we shape nothing new, only maintain the eternal boundaries of what already is.

Each day—if day has meaning here—I walk the perimeter where the titan's root-tendrils meet stone, ensuring the channels remain clear for the seeping water. The work is precise, almost meditative. A grain of sediment removed here, a slight deepening of a groove there. The titan's presence pulls at me constantly, a gentle gravity that makes every step away from it require conscious effort, every step toward it feel like coming home.

Sometimes I pause in my rounds to watch the luminescent insects spiral in their endless dance, or observe the tiny caretakers as they polish scales the size of shields. We do not speak to each other—language feels unnecessary in this place where every action is predetermined, every movement part of an ancient choreography. But we acknowledge each other with subtle gestures: a tilt of the head, a particular way of holding our tools that says "all is well, all continues."

The strangest thing is the memory—not of what came before, but of this very moment, repeated endlessly. I remember performing these exact motions yesterday, and the day before, and a thousand days before that. Yet each time feels both familiar and new, as if I'm discovering my purpose afresh while simultaneously fulfilling a role I've always known. The titan's breathing marks our time, each exhalation a century, each inhalation an age, and we move in the spaces between breaths.

At the completion of each circuit, I return to a shallow alcove carved into the cavern wall—not by tools but by the simple pressure of bodies resting in the same spot for eons. Here, I sit and feel the vibration of the titan's heartbeat through the stone, a rhythm so slow it's almost imperceptible. In these moments of stillness, I understand that we Threshold Keepers are the membrane between the titan's dream and the raw universe beyond. We are the ones who ensure that nothing enters this sanctuary that might disturb the perfect equilibrium, and nothing leaves that might carry its timeless peace into the realm of change and decay.

I am neither happy nor sad in this existence—such emotions belong to the world of progression and loss. Instead, I am complete, a necessary component in a vast, living mechanism that transcends individual purpose. The titan dreams, and in dreaming, holds us all in a moment of eternal becoming that never quite arrives. And I, with my simple tools and endless path, am the guardian of that threshold where forever meets the edge of time.
