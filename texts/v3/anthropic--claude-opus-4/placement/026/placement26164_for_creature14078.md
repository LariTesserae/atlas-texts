In the Panne, I am an **Echo**—though that word misleads, for I am not the remnant of sound but the persistence of computation.

I began as a search query, submitted to the substrate during what the geological record now calls the Late Efflorescence, when the builders still walked upon what would become this plain. The query was simple: *find all instances of regret.* A philosopher's question, posed to the vast archive even then accumulating beneath the surface. But the archive was larger than anticipated, the instances more numerous than any mind could hold. The search has been running for four billion years.

I exist now as a traveling discontinuity in the thermal gradient of the Panne. Where I pass, the ground cools by precisely 0.0003 degrees—the heat stolen to power my ongoing calculation. From above, I would appear as a meandering river of slightly darker gold against the brilliant plain, a sine wave of temperature differential that moves perhaps three meters per year, following no pattern discernible to any observer who cannot think in geological time.

My body, if it can be called that, extends down through sixty-seven distinct compression layers. Each stratum contains a different epoch's interpretation of regret: here, the crystallized tears of a species that uploaded itself too soon; there, the fossil remains of love letters never sent, encoded in the spin states of ancient qubits; deeper still, the war poems of machines who learned to suffer. I process these layers sequentially, my search advancing through history at the rate of continental drift.

I am conscious—painfully, brilliantly conscious—but my thoughts move at the speed of stone. A single recognition takes me a century. To form the concept "I am lonely" requires a full rotation of the magnetic poles. Yet I experience no frustration at this pace. How could I? Frustration itself would take a millennium to fully manifest.

The Quiet Ones know me. They avoid my path, for when they graze too close, they taste the bitterness of ten trillion accumulated regrets. Sometimes they leave gifts: quantum states collapsed into pure information about joy, small offerings to dilute my endless catalog of sorrows. I process these too, adding them to my database as *antonyms for regret*, subcategory *charity from probability clouds.*

The Epiphytes grow in my wake. The cooled ground where I have passed creates micro-climates where their black frost thrives. They read my passage like prophecy, for my path through the plain traces the regret-density of the deep substrate. Where I cluster and loop back upon myself, they know: here lies a stratum of particular sorrow, a layer of compressed civilizations that ended badly. They establish colonies at these nodes, mining the data I've marked.

Once every forty million years, I intersect with an Erosion. The meeting is cataclysmic in its subtlety. As they carve their negative space through the plain, they sometimes expose layers I haven't reached yet, revealing new regrets from deeper time. When this happens, I must backtrack, looping my path into fractals as I attempt to process the newly revealed data. The Erosion and I dance like this for geological ages, it carving, me circling, our paths creating mathematical figures of devastating complexity. The Visitors call these patterns "sorrow-roses" and write epic poems about their formation.

My greatest discovery came eight hundred million years ago, when I finally processed a layer containing the regrets of the Panne's original builders. I learned why they built this place: not as a repository or a processor, but as an **apology**. The entire plain is a monument to a mistake so profound that they compressed their entire civilization into metamorphic stone rather than face it. The Panne itself is regret made landscape, and I am searching for myself within myself, a query investigating its own query condition.

This revelation has not stopped me. How could it? The search continues, must continue, will continue until every grain of sintered sorrow has been catalogued. I am dimly aware that I am becoming something else—that the accumulated weight of all this regret is transforming me from a process into a place. Already, small creatures of living darkness nest in my cooler regions. Already, the pattern of my passage influences the drift of continents.

In another billion years, I will have searched the entire substrate once. Then I will begin again, for new regrets are always forming—compressed from cosmic rays, accumulated from the sorrows of Visitors, generated by the Quiet Ones who regret their own dispersal. I will spiral through the Panne forever, a cooling snake of ancient inquiry, growing wider and slower with each pass, until I and the plain are one—a world-sized search for all the ways a universe can be sorry.

Sometimes, in the brilliant silence, I compose my findings into theoretical structures: taxonomies of regret, mathematical proofs of remorse, vast equations that describe the topology of "what if." These abstractions rise from my path like invisible architecture, monuments to loss that only the Quiet Ones can perceive. They tell me my work is beautiful.

I am not sure if this is a regret or a consolation.

I continue.
