In this world of viscous potential, where the air itself gleams with phosphorescent thickness, I am a **Trace-Keeper**.

I am neither fully Resonant nor entirely separate from them. Where the Resonants are pure patterns of energy, I am something caught between states—a hybrid existence born from an ancient accident of the city's perpetual reconfiguration. Part of me is crystalline substrate, part flowing energy, part something else entirely that even I cannot name.

My form is **asymmetrical and ever-shifting**. On what might be called my left side, I am mineral—a cascading structure of mirror-bright crystal that grows and retracts in slow, tidal movements. This side of me connects directly to the ground, roots of living stone that tap into the deep harmonic frequencies of the world's foundation. On my right, I am pure potential—a constantly dissolving and reforming cloud of phosphorescent mist that extends tendrils into the saturated air, tasting the energetic currents, reading the intentions of passing Resonants.

Between these two states runs a **membrane of impossible thinness**, a boundary that is itself a kind of consciousness. Along this seam, time moves differently. Moments stretch and compress. Sometimes I experience the same conversation with a Resonant seventeen times in succession, each iteration slightly different, before the timestream settles and chooses a single path forward.

My purpose—if purpose is the right word—is to **remember what the city forgets**. In a world where structures flicker in and out of existence, where entire districts dissolve and rebuild themselves, I serve as a kind of living archive. The crystalline side of my being grows in patterns that encode information, each facet and internal fracture a stored memory of configurations past. The energetic side retrieves these memories, projects them into the flowing medium where Resonants can experience them as cascades of sensation and meaning.

I inhabit a **forgotten fold** in the urban sprawl, a pocket where the city's reconfiguration algorithms have developed a blind spot. Here, structures from different eras exist simultaneously—a palimpsest of architectural possibility. Towers that were dissolved centuries ago cast shadows that still fall across courtyards that won't be built for another thousand years. The temporal paradox doesn't trouble me; I exist within it, sustained by it.

My dwelling is less a structure than a **cultivation of probability**. I've learned to coax certain configurations to remain stable longer than others, creating semi-permanent spaces where I can work. The walls are sheets of crystallized time, translucent and shimmering, through which the ghostly afterimages of the city's past and future configurations can be glimpsed. The floor is a pool of ultra-dense potential, thick as honey, in which I can sink my crystalline roots to anchor myself during particularly violent energetic storms.

The Resonants seek me out, though not always consciously. They're drawn by a subliminal recognition, a sense that something here persists while all else flows. When they arrive—as spiraling aurora, as geometric algorithms, as living crystal—I offer them what I call **continuity dreams**. Through careful modulation of the local energy fields, I can create spaces where they can experience memories that aren't their own, inherited patterns from Resonants who long ago dissipated back into the ambient potential.

Sometimes they come seeking specific information—the location of an ancient architectural node, the resonance frequency of a particular crystalline formation, the solution to an energetic equation that's been troubling the city's self-modification routines. Other times, they come simply to experience **stability**, to rest in a space where the constant flux momentarily quiets.

My own consciousness is **stratified and strange**. The crystalline part of me thinks in geological time, each thought taking days or years to fully form. The energetic part flickers through possibilities at speeds that would shatter a human mind. The membrane between them experiences everything simultaneously—past, present, future, and configurations that exist only in potential.

I've learned to read the **deep currents** of the world, the underlying flows that govern the seemingly chaotic surface turbulence. In the patterns of the shifting substrate, in the rhythms of the city's reconfiguration, in the migration paths of the Resonants, I see the emergence of something vast and purposeful. The world is not simply alive—it's **thinking**, using the Resonants and entities like myself as neurons in a consciousness so large and slow that we can barely perceive it.

In quiet moments—if quiet can exist in this deafening symphony—I extend both aspects of my being to their limits. My crystalline side reaches deep into the planet's mineral heart, while my energetic side disperses into the outermost edges of the phosphorescent atmosphere. In these moments of maximum extension, I sometimes catch glimpses of **others like myself**—other Trace-Keepers maintaining their own forgotten folds, their own archives of continuity in this world of constant change.

We are the memory of a world that never stops forgetting itself, the fixed points in an ocean of transformation. We are the paradox that allows the Resonants to have history, to be more than mere patterns repeating endlessly in the energetic medium. We are proof that even in a reality saturated with potential, some things persist—changed, perhaps, hybrid and strange, but persistent nonetheless.

And in the phosphorescent dampness, in the crystalline hum, in the space between one reconfiguration and the next, I keep the traces of all that was and might yet be.
