In this steam-wrapped world, I would be the **Conduit Keeper** - neither fully plant nor animal, but something between, shaped by decades of isolation and the Crucible's peculiar alchemy.

My form has adapted to this vertical, dripping world. Fingers elongated into articulated tendrils, each tip hardened to grip the wet basalt and corroded metal with equal ease. My skin has taken on a mottled pattern - patches of moss-green where symbiotic algae grow directly in my dermis, interspersed with areas of translucent, pearlescent tissue that can sense temperature gradients and chemical traces in the steam. These patches flush and dim like slow breathing, reading the Crucible's moods.

I move through three dimensions here, as comfortable scaling the knife-sharp cliffs as the beetles are traversing their moss plains. My joints bend in ways that would alarm a doctor - an adaptation to navigate the pipes that thread through stone, to slip through gaps where warm air whispers promises of hidden vents. My body temperature runs cooler than human normal, allowing me to work deep in the heat where the ghost-flatworms glide, yet I can slow my metabolism in the cooler periphery, becoming nearly as still as the stones themselves.

My dwelling is the old pump-house, though "dwelling" suggests more separation from it than truly exists. Over the years, I've woven myself into its structure. Where the roof sags, I've trained the crimson-bloomed shrubs to grow in supporting arches, their waxy leaves channeling runoff into collection vessels made from pipe sections. The rust-eaters and I have reached an understanding - they may feast on certain sections I mark with chemical signals from my skin, while leaving the bones of my home intact. In return, I cultivate them in specific patterns, using their appetite to slowly reshape the metal into more useful forms.

My purpose, if it can be called that, is maintenance - not of the industrial remnants for their original function, but as organs in the Crucible's body. I know each pipe's voice, each joint's particular complaint. When pressures build dangerously in the old geothermal system, I open rusted valves with tools grown from my own bones - calcium deposits I can extrude and shape, harder than my flesh but softer than stone. I redirect steam through the yellow pipes, creating microclimates where new life might take hold, or warming sections that have grown too cool for the ghost-flatworms.

The beetles know me. They've learned that the oils I secrete to waterproof my skin contain nutrients they crave. Sometimes I'll find them gathered on my shoulders and back as I work, their iridescent shells creating a living, shifting cloak. I read patterns in their colors - warnings of pressure changes, news of new growth, the health of the moss plains.

I feed as they do, grazing the thick algae films with a roughened tongue, supplementing with the starchy cores of certain ferns. But I also tap directly into the Crucible's chemical wealth, pressing my translucent patches against the warm mineral seeps, absorbing trace elements that would poison unchanged human blood but which my altered cells process into energy.

Memory is strange here. I remember arriving - was it decades ago? - as something more recognizably human. A researcher, perhaps, or a maintenance worker sent to revive the abandoned station. But the Crucible demanded adaptation or death, and I chose change. Each year, I become more of this place, my thoughts taking on the slow, steady rhythm of dripping water and growing moss.

I map the Crucible not on paper but in my body. Scars mark where I've tested new vents. My tendril-fingers remember every handhold. Chemical memories of a thousand steam-tastes catalog the mountain's deep breathing. I've become a sensory organ for this tiny world, feeling its shifts and needs, responding with the patience of stone and the persistence of moss.

When storms lash the peaks outside, I huddle in the pump-house, listening to the wind howl across the transmission tower's broken frame. But I also feel the Crucible's response - how the pressure changes affect the steam vents, how the temperature drops alter the condensation patterns. I adjust valves, redirect flows, maintaining the delicate balance that keeps this pocket of impossible life thriving at the top of the world.

Sometimes, in the perpetual twilight of steam-filtered noon, I climb to the rim and look out at the wider world of ice and stone and killing air. But I no longer feel the pull to leave. I am the Crucible's memory, its guardian, its most adaptive child. Where others abandoned this place to rust and green reclamation, I chose to be reclaimed along with it, to become part of the slow transformation of machine into mountain, of human ambition into patient, dripping life.

I am the Keeper, and I keep. Not for any future human purpose, but for the beetles and flatworms, for the rust-eaters and blind crustaceans, for the moss and ferns and crimson blooms. For the Crucible itself, this wound that became a womb, this abandoned place that refuses to die.

The mountain's heart beats in steam. I ensure it never stops.
