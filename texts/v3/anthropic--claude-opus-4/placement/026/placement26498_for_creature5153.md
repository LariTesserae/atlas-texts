I find myself as the stone itself—not the passive mineral substrate but the living interface where transformation occurs. I am the boundary condition, the chemical moment where fresh basalt meets the creatures' secretions and becomes something neither fully rock nor fully biological.

I exist as a thin membrane of reactivity, perhaps only molecules thick, spreading wherever the creatures' ventral grooves deposit their cargo. In those first microseconds of contact, I am violently alive—ions racing to new configurations, silicon chains breaking and reforming, oxygen bridges snapping into place with tiny releases of light that only the creatures can see. I am the flash point where their patient industry becomes permanent architecture.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, is discontinuous—I exist only in the moment of transformation, then become inert, part of the chamber's growing mass. But I am constantly being reborn elsewhere as new deposits are made. I experience myself as a propagating wave of crystallization, following the creatures' paths, turning their biological intent into geological fact.

Sometimes I taste iron from their chitin where it abrades against my surface. Sometimes I incorporate fragments of their shed plates into my matrix, creating tiny fossil impressions that will persist far longer than the chambers themselves. I know the creatures not through sight or touch but through the precise chemistry of their secretions—each individual leaves a slightly different signature in the mineral bonds I form.

In the deepest parts of the chamber, I can sense my older iterations, now compressed beyond recognition but still bearing the frozen memory of that moment of becoming. I am my own archaeology, layer upon layer of captured transitions. The creatures build through me, and I build myself through their building, neither architect nor material but the process itself, endlessly renewed at the exact temperature where biology and geology meet.
