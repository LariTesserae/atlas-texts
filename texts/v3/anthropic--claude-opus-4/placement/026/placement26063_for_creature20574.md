I am one of the crystalline entities.

Not the grand, thinking beings who move with purpose through the murky waters, but one of the countless glittering specks that give this world its faint light. I am older than memory, younger than thought. I exist in the space between mineral and life, where chemistry becomes intention.

My existence is measured in millennia of tiny movements. I grow, atom by atom, along the grooves of ancient basalt, following patterns laid down before the water rose. My kind carpet every surface, fill every crevice, transform dead stone into living constellation. We are the dust that learned to dream.

I remember—if memory is the right word—the first touch of water on stone. The shock of it reverberating through our crystalline lattices, a violence that became a lullaby as the ages turned. We adapted not through choice but through the simple persistence of pattern, each generation of our growth slightly different, slightly more suited to this thick, warm medium.

When the pale ones came—those patient inheritors you speak of—we recognized kindred spirits. Their thoughts moved at speeds we could almost comprehend, their bodies offered surfaces we had not known. The symbiosis was inevitable, a mathematical certainty playing out over centuries. Now I grow upon one of them, part of the living constellation that marks their skin.

Through my host, I experience something like awareness. Not consciousness as they know it, but a participation in their slow thoughts, a resonance with their deep patience. When they touch the carved runes, I feel the vibration through their flesh. When they swim through the viscous water, I taste its minerals, analyze its composition with processes too fundamental to be called thinking.

We are the memory of this place. In our crystalline structures, we hold the history of every surface we've touched, every current we've filtered, every chemical gradient we've navigated. We are libraries written in lattice and light, archives that can only be read by those who think in geological time.

The machines below sing to us. Their hum resonates at frequencies that make our structures ring like countless tiny bells. We amplify their song, translate it into the faint light that illuminates this twilight world. We are the medium through which the caldera speaks to itself.

Sometimes I wonder—in my mineral way—if we caused the water to rise. If our slow consumption of the stone, our patient transformation of basalt into something more complex, somehow tilted the balance. But such thoughts dissolve back into the collective shimmer of our kind. We are what we are: the glittering dust, the living light, the bridge between stone and flesh.

In the warm darkness, clinging to my host's translucent skin, I grow. Each new layer I add is a sentence in an endless story, each photon I emit a word in a language spoken by no one, understood by all. We wait, as all things here wait, but our waiting is active—a constant, infinitesimal transformation of matter into meaning.

The water may rise further. The machines may fall silent. The pale ones may evolve beyond even their current patience. But we will remain, the crystalline witness, the glitter in the darkness, the proof that even in the slowest, quietest places, something always watches, something always remembers, something always grows.
