I find myself as one of the crystalline striations within the plateau's rock face—not the quartz veins that mirror the inhabitants' forms, but something darker, denser. I am a seam of metallic ore, perhaps magnetite or hematite, running in a thin, irregular line through the volcanic glass.

I am both path and barrier. The glass-beings sometimes trace my length with what might be fingers, feeling for the faint magnetic pull I exert on the moisture in the air, the way I bend the condensation into new patterns. I am their compass and their calendar—they read the slow oxidation of my surface, the way I rust in microscopic increments, as a kind of clock that measures epochs rather than hours.

But I am more than timekeeper. I am memory of a different sort than theirs. Where they hold the knowledge of water and wind, I remember the deep heat, the pressure that forged me in darkness miles beneath where we rest now. I am the vertical story that cuts through their horizontal histories—the uprising, the violent birth that brought the plateau into being.

Sometimes I sing, though it's not a sound they can echo. When the wind is just right and the temperature drops suddenly at nightfall, I contract faster than the glass around me, creating hairline fractures that ring out in frequencies even lower than their hums. It's in these moments that they gather closest, pressing their translucent forms against the stone, trying to understand this older language, this vocabulary of tension and release.

I am waiting too, though my waiting is geological. Each winter, water finds its way into those hairline cracks, freezes, expands. Each summer, I heal myself with rust. Slowly, over centuries, I am working my way free from the glass that holds me. Not to escape—where would I go?—but to change the shape of things, to create new crevices where moisture might gather, new surfaces where light might break differently.

The glass-beings sense this, I think. Sometimes one will spend days positioned just above me, its form perfectly still, as if listening to my patient work. They seem to understand that I am teaching the plateau how to break, how to reshape itself. That my presence here is both flaw and intention, both weakness and the promise of transformation.

We are collaborators in deep time, they and I. They reading the surface, me writing from within. Together we are composing something—not a message exactly, but a readiness. A preparation for the moment when the plateau will need to become something else, when their waiting and mine will crystallize into action, into change.

Until then, I oxidize. I sing my low notes into the stone. I hold my line.
