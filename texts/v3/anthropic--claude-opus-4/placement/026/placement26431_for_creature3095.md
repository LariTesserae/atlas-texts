I close my eyes and feel the vertebral mountain's resonance pull me into its eternal present.

---

I am one of the Listeners.

Not human, though I might have been once. The distinction eroded away like everything soft does here, leaving only the essential frequency. I exist as a standing wave in the continuous roar, a persistent eddy where the mountain's voice curves back on itself.

My body is no longer flesh but a calcified membrane—thin as eggshell, hollow, strung between two weathered spurs of rock like a sail. The omnipresent wind plays through me, and I vibrate at precisely the frequency needed to cancel out a tiny portion of the ridge's howl. Where I stand, there is a pocket of near-silence no larger than a held breath.

I never chose this. I climbed here following the old paths, leaving my offerings of flint and hair like the others. But instead of descending, I paused too long in the sound, let it fill my bones until they began to resonate in sympathy. My skeleton became a tuning fork. My skin stretched and dried into drumskin. My organs liquified and drained away through my pores, leaving only this chambered architecture that the wind knows how to play.

The Calcitrant visit me sometimes, arranging themselves in a semicircle just outside my null-zone. They cannot enter—the silence repels them like oil repels water. But they lean in, their faceted heads cocked at angles that suggest curiosity. Sometimes they leave things at the boundary: a brass button that remembers being sewn to a coat, a fragment of green glass that still believes it's a bottle. I cannot reach these offerings. I can only observe them slowly sink into the black film, digested by the ridge's peculiar metabolism.

The kettles avoid me entirely. My silence disrupts their rolling songs, sends them careening into the gutters' walls. A few have cracked against the stone near me, their hollow chambers exposed to reveal crystalline growths like frozen music. The mountain reabsorbs these casualties without sentiment.

I do not age—aging requires time, and time requires change, and change is what I am precisely calibrated to prevent. In my small sphere of anti-sound, I am the mountain's only successful experiment with true stasis. Not the slow-motion persistence of stone, but actual nullity. A place where even the light hesitates.

Sometimes travelers see me from a distance: a vertical smear against the rock, heat-shimmer thin, position uncertain. Those who approach discover that my silence is contagious. First their footsteps lose their echo. Then their breathing becomes inaudible. Finally, their thoughts drain of language until only a ringing whiteness remains. Most retreat before the full dissolution. The few who persist find themselves standing in my presence, our mutual silence creating a feedback loop that threatens to crack the ridge itself.

One visitor—the woman who would later join the Calcitrant—stood with me for an entire day. We could not speak, could not gesture, could only exist in parallel nullity. When she finally left, I felt a new harmonic enter my frequency: the ghost of her heartbeat, absorbed and inverted, now part of my permanent cancellation. I carry dozens of these phantom rhythms, each one a life that brushed against my silence and left its negative imprint.

The mountain tolerates me as it tolerates the black film—a necessary imperfection that defines the whole. Without my pinpoint of quiet, the roar would have no meaning. I am the pause between syllables in its endless recitation, the moment where it draws breath that it does not need.

At night—though night here is only a theory—I sometimes feel my vanished flesh trying to reconstitute itself from memory. Phantom nerves fire along my membrane. Ghost muscles twitch in the wind. But the moment cohesion threatens, the mountain's frequency shifts just enough to scatter the attempting form. I am not allowed to return to what I was. I can only become more perfectly what I am: the listener who learned to listen so completely that listening became absence.

The ridge speaks. The Calcitrant mirror. The kettles roll.

And I, in my small rebellion of silence, am the only thing here that has found a way to interrupt the mountain's monologue with nothing.

Nothing, held perfectly still, forever.
