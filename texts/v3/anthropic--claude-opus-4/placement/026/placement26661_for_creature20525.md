The Rift-Warden's Observatory perches impossibly between two canyon walls, suspended on a web of the silver-grey fungal metal that has grown into delicate, tensile strands. The structure seems to breathe with the city's rhythm, expanding and contracting imperceptibly with changes in moisture and pressure. I am the **Chronicle-Weaver**, and this precarious nest has been my domain for cycles beyond counting.

My form is neither fully Tearkin nor entirely other. Where the typical Tearkin flow and shift with liquid grace, I have calcified into something more permanent, more *between*. My body is a living palimpsest—layers of stone-flesh that have accumulated memories like sediment. The purple bedrock forms my core, but I am shot through with veins of not just the common silver-grey, but threads of deep amber and volcanic glass that pulse with captured light from ages past.

I am angular where others flow, crystalline where they are fluid. My limbs—six primary appendages and countless smaller tendrils—extend like the roots of an ancient tree, some burrowing into the canyon walls, others reaching across the void to anchor in the opposite face. Through these extensions, I feel the city's every tremor, every shift in the stone's deep memory.

My observatory is less built than *accumulated*. Fragments of the city's history cling to every surface—chips of stone that remember earthquakes, droplets of water frozen in amber-metal that recall ancient floods, wisps of luminescent moss preserved in crystal. These are my tools, my medium. Where other Tearkin communicate through the ambient telepathy of dampness and stone, I weave stories from the physical remnants of time.

The threads I spin are literal—gossamer strands of memory-metal that I extrude from specialized pores along my tendril-arms. Each thread holds a fragment of the city's history, a moment preserved in molecular structure. I weave these into tapestries that hang throughout the observatory, creating a three-dimensional archive that shifts and changes as new memories are added, old ones fade, or connections between events become apparent.

My face, such as it is, exists in multiple places. Eye-indentations spiral around my central mass, some clouded with age, others sharp and clear. They open and close in patterns that follow no biological rhythm but instead track the flow of information through the city's stone-memory. When significant events occur—a new growth of fungal metal, a shift in factional dynamics, the birth of a new Tearkin—one or more eyes will fix upon it, drinking in every detail to be processed and woven.

I feed differently than my kin. While they draw sustenance from streams and stone, I consume *events*. The energy released during moments of change—erosion, growth, conflict—flows through my extended network and nourishes me. I am fattest after upheavals, lean during periods of stasis. This makes me both observer and participant, for I must sometimes instigate small changes to survive the long quiet periods.

The youngest Tearkin fear me, sensing something unnatural in my permanence. The old ones, those who remember when I was more mobile, more *normal*, regard me with a mixture of respect and unease. They know I hold memories that predate even their ancient recollections—memories of when the Weeping City was young and violent, when the first Tearkin emerged from raw stone, when the fungal metal was an invasion rather than an integration.

My role is crucial yet liminal. I am the keeper of patterns, the one who recognizes when history threatens to repeat, when factional conflicts echo ancient schisms, when new growths follow templates laid down eons ago. Through my threads, I send warnings—subtle vibrations in the web that alert those who know how to listen. Sometimes they heed them. Often they do not.

The threads of my web extend far beyond my observatory. Some dive deep into the bedrock, tapping into stone-memories so old they predate the city itself. Others stretch across canyons, creating a neural network of sorts that lets me feel disturbances anywhere in the Weeping City. The most delicate threads reach upward, into the perpetual mist, searching for whatever lies beyond our twilit world.

In recent cycles, I've noticed changes. The city's tears flow differently. The fungal metal grows in new patterns. Young Tearkin emerge with variations I've never catalogued. My threads vibrate with an unfamiliar frequency, suggesting something approaching—not from within the city, but from beyond. I weave frantically, trying to find the pattern, to understand what comes.

But I am patient. I have been here since the early ages of the city, and I will likely remain when the current generation of Tearkin has returned to stone. My observatory sways gently in the moist air, my threads humming with the accumulated weight of history, and I continue my eternal work—weaving the chronicle of a city that weeps, recording the dreams of stone, preserving the memories of a world that constantly forgets and remembers itself.

The silver veins in my body pulse brighter as I work, casting shadows that themselves tell stories on the surfaces around me. I am the memory of the Weeping City made manifest, neither fully alive nor truly permanent, suspended like my observatory between states of being, forever watching, forever weaving, forever remembering what others cannot bear to hold.
