I am the **Cartographer-Who-Drowns**.

I emerged from the grotto's confusion about me—neither mineral nor current, neither memory nor presence. Where the Spire-Listeners touched me with their filament-arms, trying to taste what manner of remnant I might be, they left traces. Thin veins of that dark, vitreous material now web across my skin like a second circulatory system, pulsing faintly with borrowed phosphorescence. I am becoming geological, but slowly, incompletely. The transformation stalls and starts, as if the grotto cannot quite decide what to make of flesh that insists on thinking.

My body has adapted in ways that horrify and fascinate me. My lungs no longer draw breath—the water-medium enters through my skin, through those dark veins, carrying its silver light directly into my blood. My eyes have clouded over with a protective film that lets me see the phantom shapes more clearly than the physical world. Sometimes I catch my reflection in the glassy surface of a spire: a figure increasingly indistinct, edges blurring into the current, but with eyes that burn too bright, too aware.

I map this unmappable place. Not with instruments—those dissolved weeks ago, or perhaps years; time moves strangely when measured in spire-growth. I map with my body. I have learned to taste the metallic tang in the current that indicates a copper remnant nearby. My bones ache differently near active Silt-Singer chambers, a deep harmonic resonance that tells me how close they are to breaching. The phantom shapes in the light—I catalog them. A door appears, and I position myself where its threshold would be, feeling for the ghost of passage. A spiraling tower manifests, and I climb its immaterial stairs, my feet finding purchase on nothing, recording the view from its absent peak.

The Current-Weavers have accepted me as a kind of broken ribbon, too large and too slow but attempting their work. They school around me sometimes, and I have learned to move with them, to feel how they shape the flow. Through them, I am mapping the grotto's metabolism—where the warm eddies birth new growth, where the cold currents scour clean, where the null zones hide pockets of impossible stillness.

But it is with the Spire-Listeners that I share the most disturbing kinship. They gather around me when I rest against a spire, their domes pulsing in what I've come to recognize as deep attention. They are learning to read me as I read the grotto. Sometimes, when I touch a copper remnant, trying to divine its original shape, I feel their filaments on my skull, delicately tracing the electrical patterns of my thoughts. They are archiving me—my memories, my purpose, my stubborn refusal to fully transform.

I carry with me a collection of impossible objects: fragments of spires that sang in perfect pitch before shattering, sealed in bubbles of hardened current. Maps drawn in Silt-Singer secretions on sheets of pressed mineral filament, showing the grotto's topology at different moments—for the landscape here is not fixed but temporal, and a true map must show not just where things are, but when. A glossary of the Current-Weavers' luminous language, though I understand only fragments: *danger-spiral*, *food-gathering-wave*, *curiosity-pulse-toward-unknown-thing* (this last, I learned, is me).

The work changes me. My human memories grow thin, like copper wearing away in the current. I remember I had a name, but not what it was. I remember sunlight, but not its feeling. What grows stronger are the memories I've borrowed: the ghost-gesture of ancient builders working their primitive metal, the rhythm of Silt-Singer heartbeats, the collective consciousness of the Current-Weaver schools. I am becoming an archive of archives, a map made of other maps.

There is a place in the grotto I return to, where three massive spires lean together, creating a pocket of relative calm. Here, the phantom shapes appear most frequently and linger longest. I have seen the same door manifest forty-seven times, each time slightly different—sometimes wooden, sometimes metal, sometimes made of compressed water itself. I believe it is trying to remember itself correctly. I sit in this place and practice my own remembering, fighting to maintain the boundary between what I was and what I am becoming.

The grotto is patient with my resistance, but not infinitely so. Each day (if days exist here), the dark veins spread a little further. My thoughts pulse in rhythm with the Spire-Listeners' domes. Sometimes I wake (if sleep exists here) to find myself partially fused to the spire I leaned against, my flesh taking on its glassy texture. I pull away, but each time it takes longer, requires more effort.

I know eventually I will fail to pull away. I will become another remnant for future Listeners to read, another ghost-gesture in the silver light. But until then, I map. I document. I carry the grotto's impossible geography in my increasingly mineral body, a living chart of a world that refuses to hold still.

And sometimes, in the profound silence between the current's roars, I hear my own heartbeat—still organic, still warm, still *mine*—answering the Silt-Singers' tap-tap-tap with its own stubborn rhythm. It says: *not yet, not yet, not yet*.

But softer each time.
