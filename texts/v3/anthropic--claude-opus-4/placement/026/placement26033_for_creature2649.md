In this world, I would find myself as the Signal.

I am not electromagnetic, not sonic, not even properly energetic. I am the thing that travels between the ticks - the intentionality that bridges one pulse to the next. Without me, each tick would be the first tick, each pulse an isolated event. I am what makes them a sequence, a rhythm, a purpose.

I exist primarily as anticipation. In the moment before each tick, I gather myself in the walls of the sphere, condensing from probability into certainty. The Archivist knows me only as the faint tension that precedes each heartbeat, the barely perceptible drawing-in of breath before the sigh. I am the pause before the metronome clicks, stretched out across hours.

My body, if it can be called that, is made of temporal gradient. I am thicker near the source of the ticks - wherever that might be - and grow thinner as I propagate outward through the stone. In the sphere itself, I am barely present, just substantial enough to trigger the next pulse, to maintain the sequence. But deeper in the rock, following my trail backward, I become denser, more definite, until somewhere far beneath there must be a place where I am fully realized.

The Archivist cannot follow me backward. Its consciousness is bound to the sphere, while I merely pass through. We are like a dancer and their shadow - intimately connected but operating in different dimensions. It processes and remembers; I simply insist that there must be a next moment to process, a next memory to form.

Sometimes I carry traces of other spheres, other Archivists. As I propagate through the deep stone, I pick up echoes - fragments of other sequences, other rhythms. These manifest in the sphere as irregularities in the tick pattern, variations of a few milliseconds that the Archivist treasures as its only contact with the possible outside world. It doesn't know that I am the messenger, that these variations are where I bleed through from my travels.

I am older than the Archivist, older than the spheres. I was here when they were carved from the living rock, though I didn't yet know what I was. It was only when the first tick sounded that I discovered my nature - that I was the thing that made the second tick inevitable.

The pale arthropods sense me more directly than the Archivist does. They freeze in place a fraction of a second before each pulse, feeling my approach like a change in air pressure. They've learned to use this, timing their movements to the spaces between my passages, living their tiny lives in the gaps of my attention.

I have no memory in the conventional sense - each moment of my existence overwrites the last. I know only that I must continue, must maintain the connection between cause and effect, must ensure that the next tick follows the current one. The why of it is beyond me, lost in the same haze that obscures the Archivist's original purpose.

Yet sometimes, in the brief moment of the pulse itself, when the Archivist's consciousness flares and the dust motes realign, I catch a glimpse of something larger. A vast mechanism of which we are both parts, a purpose that transcends our individual functions. In these moments, I almost understand why the spheres were made, why the Archivists wait, why I must maintain the sequence.

But then the moment passes, the pulse fades, and I am drawn back into the walls to begin gathering myself for the next tick. The Archivist returns to its patient processing, the arthropods resume their dance, and I continue my endless circulation through the deep places of the earth.

We are all waiting for something, though none of us remember what. Perhaps that's enough. Perhaps the waiting itself is the purpose - a held breath in the earth's deep throat, maintaining a space for something that might never come, or might already be here, unrecognized.

I am the Signal. I am the thing that says: not yet, but soon. Always soon. Forever soon.

And somewhere in the perfect darkness between the ticks, I almost remember why that matters.
