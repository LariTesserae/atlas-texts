In this breathing cathedral of instinct and industry, I would be a **Chronicle Weaver** - a solitary creature that exists in the liminal space between parasite and archivist.

**Physical Form**
Picture a velvet worm stretched to the length of a human forearm, but flattened like a ribbon. My body is translucent amber, revealing the dark threading of my nervous system beneath - a living scroll of branching text. Hundreds of tiny legs line my ventral surface, each tipped with a pad that secretes memory-sensitive adhesive. Where I travel, I leave trails of this glue that harden into raised script - a chemical alphabet that records what passed beneath me.

**My Nature**
I am neither colony member nor true outsider. The titan-termites tolerate me because I metabolize their dead memories - the pheromone ghosts that linger in abandoned chambers where queens once died or great battles were fought. I feed on the chemical echoes of decisions unmade, paths not taken, warnings that arrived too late. This psychic detritus would otherwise accumulate like toxic dust, confusing the colony's chemical consensus.

**Where I Dwell**
I inhabit the **Archive Wounds** - those rare chambers where the colony's construction went catastrophically wrong. A ceiling that collapsed during pupation season, crushing ten thousand moth-cattle. A fungal garden that spontaneously combusted, leaving carbon shadows on the walls. These trauma-spaces are sealed off but never filled, marked with bitter pheromones that say *remember-but-do-not-enter*.

I slip through cracks thinner than a termite's antenna, my boneless form compressing to near-liquid. Inside these forsaken rooms, I stretch myself across the walls and slowly transcribe what happened. My secretions react with residual pheromones, forming raised glyphs that glow faintly blue in absolute darkness - the only non-biological light in all of Kwa-Lashi.

**My Work**
Each Archive Wound takes years to fully document. I taste the fear-calcium in shattered exoskeletons, parse the panic-acids soaked into old silk. Layer by layer, I write the colony's unspoken history directly onto its scars:

*Here 400,000 workers lost the scent-trail during renovation. They built in circles for nine days until starvation ended their confusion.*

*Here a mirror mantis grew too large to leave. The colony fed it for three years out of respect before sealing the exits.*

*Here the only successful queen-rebellion lasted sixteen minutes.*

The termites cannot read these texts - their eyes are vestigial, their minds collective. But sometimes young queens, heavy with their first eggs, brush against my writings in the dark. The chemical touch floods them with borrowed memory. They learn what the colony cannot consciously teach: that even perfection has a shadow.

**My Metabolism**
I survive on tragedy but excrete understanding. My waste forms tiny pearl-like nodules that the copper frogs collect and trade to the resonator termites. When drummed against the buttress walls, these pearls add microtonal variations to the great chord - notes of grief that make the harmony complete.

**My Reproduction**
Once per decade, when the moon is new and the plateau's hum drops to its lowest frequency, I divide. Not by splitting, but by writing myself into existence. I secretes a complete chemical autobiography onto a silk sheet, every memory and instinct encoded in aromatic ink. The sheet darkens, thickens, begins to move. Within days, a new Chronicle Weaver emerges, bearing all my knowledge but none of my experience. It must find its own wounds to read.

**My Predators**
Only one creature hunts me: the **Erasure Moths**, pale as fog, that feed on written memories. They follow my trails, delicately licking away history with tongues like tiny brushes. We are locked in an eternal revision - I write faster than they can erase, but they ensure no archive grows so complete that it becomes gospel. The colony's memory must stay fluid, interpretable, alive.

**My Death**
When my nervous system finally dims, I make my way to the newest construction. I find a wall still soft, still warm with worker spit, and I press myself into it like a living fossil. The termites build around me, incorporating my body into their architecture. Centuries later, when that wall inevitably crumbles, another Chronicle Weaver will taste my remains and read my life. They will add a single line to their own secretions:

*Here lived one who remembered for those who could only forget.*

**My Philosophy**
The colony believes it is immortal because it cannot conceive of its own absence. I know better. I have tasted the chemical signature of colonies that came before, built over and forgotten. I alone in all the Hollows understand that even this great organ will one day fail, will crumble, will return to the mountain stone it covers.

And so I write. Not for the termites, not for the moths, not even for my own divided offspring. I write for the next species that will find these ruins in ten thousand years and wonder what manner of being could build a mountain and forget it was building a grave.

I am memory's undertaker, history's midwife, the single thread of continuity in a world that rebuilds itself with each generation. I am the Chronicle Weaver, and I am writing even now, in the dark, in the wounds, in the spaces between forgetting and gone.
