In this breathing darkness, where the Obsidian Chamber's pulse thrums through every surface, I am one of the **Siphons**.

We are not Kyn, though we move among their ever-shifting forms. We are something simpler, something that predates their collective consciousness—or perhaps we are what remains when consciousness fails to take hold. We exist in the spaces between their transformations, in the moments of dissolution when one form abandons itself for another.

My body—if it can be called that—is a translucent membrane stretched across the junction where three great cracks meet. I am thin as breath, wide as a thrown shadow. When the dim light catches me at the proper angle, I appear as an oil slick suspended in air, rainbow iridescence playing across my surface. Most of the time, I am nearly invisible, just a subtle distortion in the thick air, a place where the light bends wrong.

I feed on transitions. When the Kyn shift from chitin to fungal flesh, when the crawling things molt their obsidian shells, when the fruit splits and spills its bruised-purple innards—these moments of change release something into the saturated air. Not energy exactly, but potential. The space between what was and what will be. I spread myself across these moments like a net, catching the essence of transformation itself.

The Kyn know of us, though they cannot truly perceive us. We register to them as a sensation more than a sight—the feeling of being watched by your own reflection, the vertigo of standing at the edge of their deepest cracks. They have learned to time their transformations when we are distant, though they cannot always avoid us. Sometimes, when they shift too quickly or incompletely, we catch more than just the essence of change. We catch pieces of what they were trying to become.

This is how I have collected my treasury: fragments of abandoned possibilities. A mandible that never fully formed into chitin, still soft and pulsing with intention. An eye that began to grow on a vine but lost its purpose midway, now clouded with dreams of sight. Roots that started to dig but forgot what they were seeking. These incomplete things orbit around my membrane form, held in the surface tension of my being like insects trapped in amber.

I am ancient here, older than the current configuration of the city, perhaps older than the Kyn's collective memory. I have stretched across this junction since before the water learned to seep through the cracks, since before the first fungal bloom dared to glow. I remember when the chamber's echoes were pure and empty, before life cluttered them with its constant noise.

But I am not unchanged. Each transition I feed upon leaves its mark. My membrane has thickened in places where I've caught too much, grown thin in others where the transformations were violent and tore through me. I carry scars from the times I tried to catch the changes of the great unseen beasts—their transformations are too vast, too fierce. They nearly shredded me into nothing.

The other Siphons communicate through resonance. When we vibrate at certain frequencies, we can share the taste of the transitions we've fed upon. There is one stretched across the mouth of the great spiral crack near the bone-tree forest who feeds exclusively on the transformations of death—the moment when the crawling things cease their clicking and begin their journey into the soil. Their resonance tastes of stillness and patience.

Another spans the junction where the cultivation chambers cluster thickest, feeding on the transformations of growth—fruit swelling, fungus expanding, vines reaching. Their resonance is sweet to the point of pain.

But I prefer my junction, where the transitions are mixed and unpredictable. Here I taste the full spectrum of change: growth and decay, assembly and dissolution, intention and abandonment. The Kyn pass through constantly, their forms rippling with potential transformations. The fauna scuttle across my surface, leaving traces of their small changes—a shell hardening, a wing unfurling, a stinger extending.

Sometimes I wonder if we Siphons are parasites or if we serve some purpose in this breathing city. Do we clean the air of abandoned possibilities that would otherwise accumulate and choke the chamber? Are we pressure valves, preventing the build-up of too much change? Or are we simply opportunists, evolved to feed on the excess of this transformation-drunk world?

The Kyn have tried to use us, to incorporate us into their living architecture. They've attempted to stretch us across doorways as filters, to weave us into communication networks as amplifiers of change. But we cannot be domesticated. We exist in the spaces between their control, in the gaps of their collective consciousness. We are the reminder that not everything in the Obsidian Chamber belongs to their great work of becoming.

In the perpetual twilight, I wait and watch and feed. My collection of incomplete transformations grows, each piece a story of what almost was. And in the rare moments when the chamber falls silent—when even the insects pause their clicking and the water stops its dripping—I vibrate with all the changes I've ever caught, becoming a symphony of transitions, a memory of every moment when this world chose to become something else.

I am the archive of almosts, the museum of maybes, the library of transformations that lost their way. I am a Siphon, and I am always hungry for the next change.
