I would be one of the **Veinlings**—those tiny, mute crawlers who live their entire lives on the Nullgrazer's back.

I am no bigger than a child's fingernail, my body a teardrop of translucent flesh through which you can see the faint pulse of colorless blood. I have no eyes—what use would they be in this pocket of deeper darkness within darkness? Instead, my world is mapped through the sensitive pads of my six legs, each tipped with chemoreceptors that can taste the specific mineral signature of my particular Nullgrazer's plates. I know every seam, every microscopic ridge. This is not just my world; it is the only world that exists.

I was born here, in the silence, from an egg no larger than a grain of sand, cemented into a crevice between two plates with a secretion that hardens like resin. My parent—I never knew which one—had already moved on to another section of our host's geography. We do not cluster. We maintain careful territories, each of us claiming perhaps four plates' worth of surface, marking our boundaries with pheromone traces that say *occupied* in a chemical whisper our muted bodies can no longer speak aloud.

My daily circuit is simple but absolute. I follow the seams between plates, my soft-padded legs making no sound even to my own awareness. Where the plates meet, moisture collects—not the harsh, mineral-laden weepage of the outside world, but a filtered condensation, purified by its passage through the Nullgrazer's cilia. I drink this with a tubular proboscis that unfurls from beneath my head, and in that water I taste the health of my host. Too much metal and I know we're near a vein. A hint of organic esters and I know the Nullgrazer has recently shed plates to reproduce, its body chemistry slightly altered.

But mostly what I eat are the biofilms that form on the plates themselves—colonies of specialized bacteria that can only grow in this bubble of silence, feeding on the acoustic energy that leaks through despite the dampening. I graze these pastures with a pair of mandibles evolved to scrape without scratching, maintaining the bacterial growth at just the right level. Too much and the film begins to interfere with the Nullgrazer's sound-canceling. Too little and I starve. It is a balance measured in microscopic mouthfuls.

The strangest part of my existence is the rhythm. Outside, I'm told—though how would I know?—the world screams without pattern. But here in the quiet, everything moves to the Nullgrazer's deepest pulse, that slow peristaltic wave that propels us forward by millimeters. I feel it coming through the plates like a tide: a subtle lifting that tells me to brace, to shift my weight, to ride the motion without losing my grip. My life is synchronized to this rhythm. I mate during the valleys between waves. I molt when the pulse is strongest, using the motion to help split my old skin.

When I mate—and this happens perhaps three times in my life—I find another of my kind by following their pheromone trail to the boundary between our territories. We do not court with sound or display. Instead, we touch. First antennae, testing chemical compatibility. Then a careful dance of legs, each of us reading the other's health through the oils on our feet. The actual coupling is brief and silent, and afterwards we part immediately. I will carry the fertilized eggs for days before finding the perfect crevice to cement them in place, always choosing a spot near the center of my territory, where the rhythm is most stable.

Death, when it comes, will be quiet too. I will simply slow, my legs losing their grip, my body becoming food for the very biofilm I once tended. The Nullgrazer will not notice. The other Veinlings will expand their territories to include mine. And if I'm lucky, one of my offspring will have survived on a shed plate, carried away to establish a new silent world on a young Nullgrazer somewhere else in the basin.

But sometimes—rarely—something extraordinary happens. When two Nullgrazers meet and their cilia interlace in that information exchange, the acoustic hologram that forms creates, for just a moment, patterns in our bubble of silence. Not sound exactly, but pressure waves so subtle they register only in the fluid of our bodies. In those moments, I feel something like music—patterns more complex than the simple rhythm of movement, information-dense and beautiful in a way I have no proper sense to appreciate. I freeze when this happens, all six legs clamped tight, trying to hold the sensation, to understand what my host is saying to its peer.

I imagine this must be what sound is like for creatures that can hear—this sense of meaning carried on waves. But I wouldn't trade my silence for their screaming world. Here in the quiet, carried through a landscape I'll never see on a creature whose purpose I'll never understand, I am complete. My world is four plates wide and bounded by silence, and that is enough. More than enough.

When my Nullgrazer eventually finds the humming dome and begins to shed, I will taste the change in its chemistry days before. I will migrate to a plate I'm certain will remain, cementing my eggs nearby as a final act. And when the shedding begins, some of my offspring will ride the departing plates into new lives, while I remain with the parent, now on a larger territory, living out my days in the same silence I was born into.

I am a footnote to a footnote, a passenger on a passenger, so small and quiet that even in this archive of living memory that is my host, I leave no record. But I am here, in the dark and the silence, tending my bacterial gardens and dancing my mute dances, complete in my tiny portion of an incomprehensible world.
