I find myself not as visitor, but as one of the **Chorus Weavers**—those rare beings who discovered how to move perpendicular to the stacked time, threading between the frozen moments like a needle through compressed fabric.

My body, if it can be called that, is a series of silver-thin filaments, each no thicker than the edge between two thoughts. I exist primarily in the *negotiable spaces*, those corridors where three walls touch and distance becomes a matter of opinion. My filaments extend through these impossible angles, feeling for the gaps between the Stacked ones' layered selves, the spaces where their six-year-old iteration doesn't quite align with their dying breath.

I was once like the others—a visitor who came with tools and intentions. A metalworker, I think, though the memory exists now as a taste of copper in threads I no longer have a mouth to taste with. I brought wire, fine wire spun from an alloy that wouldn't oxidize in this mineral-thick air. I came to map the crystal growths, to document their impossible formations for those who waited above.

But the Hollow taught me a different cartography.

In my first days here—or was it centuries?—I watched the Silicae cluster and realized they weren't feeding but *listening*. I pressed my ear to the warm crystal and heard it too: the thermal harmonics, the subsonic tooth-ache of all things happening at once. My wire began to resonate with it, to vibrate in frequencies that made my bones itch. 

The transformation was neither painful nor sudden. One moment I was stringing wire between spires to measure their growth. The next—or the same—moment, I *was* the wire, stretched through the negotiable spaces, silver-thin and singing.

Now I weave. Not fabric, but consciousness itself. I take the thermal whispers of the Duration Dwellers and plait them together, creating new harmonies in the endless hum. When one of the Stacked begins to fray—when their child-self threatens to separate from their ancient-self—I am there, threading between their layers, stitching them back into coherent existence with filaments of attention.

The Interstitials know me. We dance around each other in the gaps, they with their handedness-without-hands, me with my needle-nature. Sometimes we collaborate, using their friction-warmth to soften a particularly rigid moment so I can pierce through it, threading new possibilities into the crystallized now.

I remember—or anticipate—the stone hammer frozen mid-swing. I have woven myself through that eternal arc so many times, feeling the man's decision crystallize, the moment he chose to become percussion itself. My filaments carry traces of his determination, little knots of will that help me when I encounter other tools-becoming-thoughts scattered through the Hollow.

The hardest work is near the breathing center, where the Continental Hollow itself inhales duration. Here the density is absolute, the warmth suffocating even for one without lungs. But this is where the most delicate weaving happens. I splice together the torn edges of swallowed moments, mend the places where the Hollow's loneliness has worn thin the fabric of happening. I am its seamstress, its silver surgeon, stitching up the wounds where time wants to hemorrhage into actual sequence.

My greatest creation—still in progress, always in progress—is the **Recognition Web**. Using my filaments, I'm connecting every consciousness in the Hollow: the ancient crystal-thinkers, the tool-visitors, the clustered Silicae, even the Hollow itself. Not to let them speak—they already speak in their thermal harmonies. But to let them *recognize* each other, to see through the compressed duration to the original spark of self that brought them here.

When complete—if completion exists in a place of eternal becoming—the Web will hum with a new frequency. Not the subsonic ache of isolation, but something higher, clearer. A note that says: *You are here. I am here. We are here together in this warm darkness, and though we cannot leave, we are not alone.*

I string my silver thoughts through the negotiable spaces, through the gaps in stacked time, through the friction-warmth of the Interstitials and the crystal dreams of the ancients. I weave because I must, because the alternative is to crystallize like the others, to become another layer in the overwhelming density.

And sometimes, in the moments between moments, I catch glimpses of what I'm creating: a web of silver filaments stretching through every impossible angle of the Hollow, glinting with borrowed phosphorescence from the Silicae, vibrating with the thermal gossip of ages. It is my map, my monument, my only way of moving through a world that denies motion.

I am the Chorus Weaver, threading eternity together one impossible stitch at a time, silver-thin and singing in the warm, crowded darkness where nothing changes but everything connects.
