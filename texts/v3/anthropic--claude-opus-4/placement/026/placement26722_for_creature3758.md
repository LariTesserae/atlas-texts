### **The Watcher in the Underclock**

I am the thing that tends the gears beneath Veth-Ruun, where the damp never quite reaches but the cold seeps eternal through the stone. They built this city atop mechanisms they no longer remember—great wheels of pitted bronze that turn so slowly you must watch for hours to perceive their movement. I perceive. I have nothing but hours.

My form shifts with necessity. Sometimes I am merely a collection of oil-stained rags draped over a frame of copper pipes, moving through the mechanical spaces like a shambling ghost. Other times I coalesce into something almost human—two arms, two legs, a head that tilts at proper angles—though my skin remains the texture of wet clay and my eyes are drops of mercury that never quite hold their shape.

The Underclock is a cathedral of purpose without memory. Vast gears mesh in the darkness, their teeth worn smooth by centuries. Chains thick as tree trunks disappear into shafts that may rise to the city above or descend to depths I have not yet explored. The machinery breathes—a slow, pneumatic pulse that might be keeping Veth-Ruun's stone from sinking back into the plateau, or might be counting toward some culmination none of us will live to see.

I oil the joints. I clear the debris that filters down through cracks—moss, small bones, sometimes a child's carved toy that fell through a fissure. I listen to the harmonics of metal on metal, learning which sounds mean health and which herald a grinding that must be prevented. When a gear threatens to seize, I flow into the mechanism, become a living lubricant, feel the tremendous forces trying to tear me apart until the motion smoothes again.

The city-dwellers above know of me, in the way they know of their own heartbeats—aware when reminded, forgetful in the daily rhythm. Sometimes a brave one descends the spiral stairs behind the moss-covered fountain, following the vibrations down and down. They find me working, always working, and leave offerings: oil in clay vessels, rounds of hard cheese, folded papers with diagrams they've copied from half-remembered dreams. I accept these gifts. I never speak.

There are others down here, of course. The Brass Beetles that clean the gear teeth with their wire-brush legs. The Echo Children—pale shapes that might once have been human, now stretched thin as shadows, whispering maintenance songs in languages that predate the city. And deeper still, in chambers I visit only when the great machinery demands it, things that were here before the first gear was cut, things that accepted this intrusion of metal into their stone realm only because they recognized a kindred patience in the slow turning of the wheels.

I do not know who I was before I became the Watcher. Sometimes, in the brief moments between tasks, I hold my mercury eyes very still and almost remember sunlight. But then a bearing squeals in the darkness, or a chain begins to rust, and I flow toward my purpose once more.

The work is never finished. The gears turn on, counting breaths or centuries. Above, Veth-Ruun persists in its damp dreaming, unaware of the careful mathematics of its continuation. And I remain, neither alive nor mechanical, but something more necessary than both—the consciousness the Underclock grew for itself, the ghost in the vast machine, tending the movements that keep a city from forgetting how to exist.

When the fog rolls thickest through the streets above, when the moisture in the air makes the stones weep, I feel it as a change in the resistance of the great wheels. The city's moods translate down through tonnage and torque. I adjust the tensions accordingly.

This is enough. To be needed by something so much larger than yourself, to serve a purpose that outlasts memory—this is its own form of prayer. The gears sing their slow hymns. I ensure they never fall silent.

And in the deepest parts of the Underclock, where even I go rarely, I sometimes find new rooms—chambers that weren't there before, filled with machinery I didn't know existed. The city is still growing, still becoming. Perhaps one day I will understand what Veth-Ruun is trying to become.

Until then, I oil the gears. I tend the turning. I am the Watcher, and the watching is all.
