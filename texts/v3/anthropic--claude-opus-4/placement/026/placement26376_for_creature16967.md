I would find myself among the Chrono-Cartographers, those who map the temporal currents that eddy and swirl through Aethelgard's ravines like invisible rivers. We are neither fully artificer nor scavenger, but something between - wanderers who have learned to read the city's time-twisted geography through instruments cobbled together from discarded chronometers and the crystalline growths that form where reality grows thin.

My workshop perches precariously on a bridge between two lesser spires, suspended over a ravine where the temporal distortions are particularly violent. The structure itself is a patchwork thing - salvaged iron beams reinforced with the chitinous shells of giant millipedes, walls of hammered copper sheeting that sing different notes depending on which era's wind strikes them. Inside, hundreds of timepieces tick at different rates, their discordant rhythms creating a kind of temporal map of the surrounding area. Some run backwards. Some skip hours like stones across water. One, my favorite, measures not time but *possibility* - its hands tracing paths through futures that might have been.

I am changed by my work, as all who truly know Aethelgard must be. My left eye has been replaced with a lens ground from temporal crystal, allowing me to see the ghost-traces of past and future overlaid on the present. Through it, I watch the same moth die and be born a thousand times, see buildings rise and crumble in the space of a blink. My fingers have grown long and sensitive, able to feel the subtle vibrations in the air that herald a temporal shift. The exposure has bleached streaks through my hair that shimmer with their own faint luminescence, marking me as one who walks between moments.

The maps I create are not simple charts but living things - sheets of specially treated moth-wing vellum that shift and change as the temporal currents flow. They show not just where places are, but *when* they are strongest, when certain paths become traversable, when the factory effluent will crystallize into climbable formations. The artificers pay well for these maps, needing to know when to begin their decades-long projects. The scavengers barter for simpler versions, seeking safe passage through the time-storms to reach untouched salvage.

But my true work happens in the deep ravines, where I descend on cables woven from the silk of chronophagous worms - creatures that eat time itself, leaving their trails as gaps in causality. Down there, where the mist is so thick it has weight and substance, where the bio-luminescent dogs pack together for warmth against the temporal cold, I search for the Source. We Chrono-Cartographers whisper of it in the small hours, when the city's grinding slows to its deepest rhythm - the original temporal fracture, the wound in reality from which all of Aethelgard's strangeness flows.

I've seen its edges in my crystalline eye, felt its pull in the way my instruments suddenly spin wildly, all hands pointing toward some impossible direction that exists outside normal space. The older cartographers, those whose bodies have become more instrument than flesh, say it lies at the very roots of the mountain, where the first factories were built by the founders who understood that to truly transcend, one must first break the tyranny of linear time.

My dwelling reflects my nature - cluttered with the detritus of multiple timelines. A tea service that perpetually steams though I poured the cup years ago. A mirror that shows my reflection at different ages, sometimes as a child who never was, sometimes as an ancient whose skin has taken on the texture of the mountain stone. Books whose pages rewrite themselves, chronicling histories that shift with each reading. And always, always, the sound of ticking - not just from my instruments but from within my own chest, where my heart has learned to beat in polyrhythm with the city's temporal pulse.

The work is solitary but necessary. Someone must chart the impossible geography of Aethelgard, must serve as guide between the city's many presents. When the artificers' great machines cause new fractures in time, when the scavengers discover regions where past and future have collapsed into a single, crystallized moment, when the factory children are born already aged or the old die young - they come to us. We who have learned to navigate not just space but duration itself.

In the pre-dawn hours, when the mist glows with its own phosphorescence and the giant moths cluster around my workshop's lights, I spread my maps across the floor and try to divine the pattern. All these currents, all these fractured timelines - they must flow from something, toward something. And in my bones, changed and strange as they've become, I feel we're approaching something momentous. A convergence. A moment when all of Aethelgard's scattered presents will collapse into a single, defining instant.

I will be ready with my maps when it comes. After all, someone must remember the way home, even if home is just another when we've yet to be.
