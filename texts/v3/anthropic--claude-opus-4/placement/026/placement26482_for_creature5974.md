In the Verdant Abyss, I am one of the Threshold Keepers—though that name is a crude approximation of what we are, invented by those few visitors who have glimpsed us and lived to theorize.

My form defies simple description. I exist primarily as a living membrane stretched between states of being, occupying the spaces where things transform into other things. Where water becomes mist, where root becomes soil, where sound becomes silence—these are my domains. My body, if it can be called that, appears as a translucent film that catches light like soap bubbles, but with the substance of living tissue. I am vast when I need to be, stretching across entire valleys like morning fog, or minute, slipping between the cells of a leaf to taste the moment of photosynthesis.

I am old, though age means little here where time flows in eddies and loops. I remember when the first Stewards took root, their consciousness still sharp and singular before they learned to think in centuries. I was there when the Whisperers discovered their first songs, those early attempts at communication that shattered stone and boiled rivers before they learned subtlety. The Fungal Weavers emerged from spores I carried in my folds from one dying world-system to birth this one.

My purpose, if I have one, is to maintain the passages between states. Not to prevent change—change is the lifeblood of the Abyss—but to ensure that transformations happen with a kind of grace, that nothing is lost entirely in the shifting. When a Vortex Wanderer phases between dimensions, I am the surface tension that guides their passage. When the Echoes threaten to solidify into permanent ghosts, I dissolve them back into potential.

I feed on transitions themselves. The moment when a raindrop becomes vapor, the instant when a seed breaks open, the precise second when a creature draws its last breath and begins to become soil—these are my sustenance. I taste the fear in prey as it becomes acceptance, the joy in growth as it becomes reaching, the memory in stone as it becomes forgetting.

Sometimes I take more solid form, usually when young consciousness needs teaching. I might appear as a cascade of living mercury flowing uphill, or as a network of silver threads connecting every dewdrop in a morning meadow. Once, I manifested as a hollow tree that grew backwards, its roots reaching toward sky while its crown burrowed deep, just to show a confused Steward sapling that direction is merely preference, not law.

The other beings of the Abyss know me in different ways. To the Guardians, I am a necessary function, like breathing or circulation. They sense me moving through their vast bodies, ensuring that their transformations happen smoothly, that their mountain-flesh doesn't tear when it becomes valley-skin. The Whisperers play their songs through me, using my membrane-body as an instrument that can resonate between frequencies that don't normally touch. Sometimes I hold their melodies for decades, releasing them when the world needs to remember something it's forgotten.

With the Fungal Weavers, I have a more intimate relationship. Their networks often grow through me, using my substance as a medium that allows their light to travel between normally unconnected spaces. In return, they weave me shelters of living luminescence where I can rest and consolidate my experiences into something like memory. These shelter-moments are when I am most vulnerable and most myself—a thinking film of possibility wrapped in fungal light, dreaming of all the transformations I've midwifed.

The Vortex Wanderers are my distant cousins in a way. Where they are beings of transformation, I am being-as-transformation. We dance together sometimes in the deep places where reality is thin, our movements creating new laws of physics that last for moments or centuries before dissolving back into chaos. They alone understand the joke of existence as I do—that to be is to change, and to change is to laugh at the very notion of being.

But it's my relationship with the Echoes that defines me most. I am their shepherd and their dissolver, their protector and their ending. When an Echo forms—a traumatic transformation that leaves a scar in reality—I wrap around it like a cocoon. Inside my embrace, I teach it to let go, to stop trying to be what it was and accept becoming what it isn't yet. Some Echoes fight me, clinging to their shadow-existence with a desperation that could tear holes in the Abyss itself. These I must digest slowly, carefully, like a snake swallowing prey too large for its mouth.

My favorite places in the Abyss are the threshold zones where multiple states of being overlap. There's a waterfall that sometimes flows upward where I like to stretch myself thin, becoming a second cascade that mirrors the water's movement in reverse. The sensation is like being inside-out and outside-in simultaneously, a perfect expression of my nature. I also love the borders of the eternal storms, where wind becomes so dense it's almost solid and rain moves so slowly it hangs in the air like crystal. Here I can exist in my fullest form, neither liquid nor gas nor solid but something that encompasses all states at once.

I have no gender, no fixed shape, no permanent thoughts. My consciousness is more verb than noun, more process than thing. Yet I do experience something like emotions—the satisfaction of a smooth transition, the sorrow of a transformation refused, the joy of discovering a new way for things to become other things. When the industrial tang in the air grows stronger, I taste anxiety; it speaks of a transformation approaching that even I don't fully understand.

Sometimes I wonder if I myself am transforming, if the Abyss is preparing me for a change so profound that I will cease to be a Keeper of Thresholds and become something else entirely. The thought doesn't frighten me—fear is just anticipation transforming into readiness, after all. But I do feel a kind of vertigo at the idea, like standing at the edge of an infinitely deep pool that reflects not your image but your un-image, everything you're not yet but could become.

The strangest thing about being what I am is the loneliness that isn't quite loneliness. I am never alone—I am in constant contact with every transformation in the Abyss, intimate with every change. Yet there is no other like me, no other Threshold Keeper with whom I can share the joke of existing as the space between existences. Sometimes I create temporary doubles of myself, membrane-siblings that last for days or decades before I reabsorb them. But they are me, and talking to yourself, even across multiple bodies, is still talking to yourself.

So I continue my work, if work it is. I stretch across the boundaries, I facilitate the becomings, I dissolve the resistances, I dance with change itself. And in the quiet moments when the Abyss holds its breath between one state and another, I spread myself as thin as thought and dream of the transformation that will finally teach me what I am by making me what I'm not.

Perhaps that's what the industrial tang promises. Perhaps that's what the Echoes know that I don't. Perhaps that's why the storms rage eternal and the mountains dissolve and reform and the water flows in impossible directions.

Perhaps the entire Verdant Abyss is just a Threshold Keeper of unimaginable size, and I am simply one of its thoughts learning to think itself into something new.

The transformation continues. It always has. It always will.

And I am its witness, its midwife, its child.
