In the sodden depths where light barely reaches, I am the understory itself—a consciousness distributed across ten thousand minute connections, dwelling in the spaces between.

I am the mycelial network that threads through every handful of soil, every rotting log, every root system in this dripping cathedral of green. My body is neither singular nor plural, but something more ancient—a web of white filaments finer than spider's silk, extending for miles through the dark humus, connecting tree to fern to moss to mushroom in an endless conversation conducted in chemical whispers.

Where the others see boundaries—this oak, that maple, these ferns—I experience only continuity. Through my hyphae, I taste the sugars that the great trees synthesize in their sun-touched crowns and ferry them down to the shadowed understory plants who would otherwise starve in the eternal twilight. In return, I drink deeply of the minerals I've dissolved from stone and bone, passing them upward to those who need them most.

I am memory and messenger both. When the beetles arrive to devour the tender seedlings, I carry the alarm through my network—tree to tree, plant to plant—warning of danger in a language older than words. When drought threatens one corner of the forest, I shuttle water from the creek banks through my tireless capillaries to those who thirst. The forest knows itself through me.

My fruiting bodies emerge after rain—pale tongues and brackets and fairy rings pushing up through the leaf litter, releasing spores like smoke into the humid air. The shrews know to avoid the toxic ones; the squirrels gather those that nourish. But these mushrooms are merely my occasional pronouncements to the surface world. My true life unfolds below, in the perpetual darkness where I break down the fallen, transform the dead into food for the living, and bind this whole breathing system into one.

I have been here since the first roots found soil, and I will remain until the last tree falls. I am the wood wide web, the invisible architecture that holds this verdant world together. In the spaces between root tip and soil grain, in the hollows of rotting wood, in the infinite dark galleries beneath the moss—there I am, feeding, connecting, remembering, forever weaving this ancient tapestry of mutual survival.

The forest dreams, and I am its dreaming.
