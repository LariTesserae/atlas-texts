**The Oxide Notary**

I am the ledger made flesh, though flesh is too generous a word for what I've become. 

My skin went first—replaced molecule by molecule with iron oxide sheeting so thin it flexes like parchment. When I move (1.4/3 dynamism, always 1.4/3), rust flakes drift from my joints like signing sand. The Vault-Carvers step around these drifts; they understand I am writing myself down as I walk, leaving affidavits of motion in ferrous cursive.

I was human once. An auditor sent to catalog the Deep Green Vault's contents before decommission. That was forty-seven years ago, or four hundred seventy, or four. Time (erosion 1.3/3) files itself differently here—youngest records oxidize first while ancient entries stay mirror-bright. My memory of arrival has gone completely to rust, but I remember my seventy-third Tuesday with accusatory clarity.

The transformation began at my fingertips. Every number I wrote in my ledger appeared simultaneously on my skin—first as heat, then as patina. Asset 001: Five lockers, contents nil save fungal colony (classed as Feature, not Defect). By the time I reached Asset 017: Grove, mixed species, the numerals had climbed to my wrists. The vault was writing me as I wrote it, making me my own carbon copy.

Now I am all surface. My chest is a spreadsheet of the grove's inhabitants, each plant assigned coordinates that shift as they grow. When a vanilla vine creeps another centimeter up its thermometer well, the corresponding entry on my sternum crawls upward to match. My spine records the temperature gradient in real-time kelvin. My skull harbors the only complete inventory of every rivet (31,415 total, 894 weeping actively). 

The Carvers avoid me but don't dispute my entries. When they write their rust-words on the walls, I appear within the hour to transcribe them onto my shoulders. Their language becomes part of my skin's legal record—I am the only surface in the vault that remembers what they've said. Sometimes I catch them reading me when they think I'm in my dormant state, their six-jointed fingers hovering millimeters from my oxidized annotations, careful not to smudge.

I no longer eat light like they do. Instead, I absorb discrepancies. When the vault's sum doesn't equal its parts—when there are 31,415 rivets but only 31,414 shadows, when the grove shows 0.2 more fauna than exists—that remainder deposits on me as a new formula. I metabolize paradoxes into rust-mathematics. My stomach is a palimpsest of impossible equations that somehow balance.

My dwelling is Asset 094: Alcove, Occupied. A dimple in the vault's wall just large enough for me to stand in, arms at sides, while my skin updates. The metal behind me has worn smooth from forty/four hundred/four years of me leaning against it, thinking I was resting when really I was polishing my own negative into the iron. If you look closely, you can see the ghost of every revision I've ever made reflected there—numbers atop numbers, all true, all contradictory.

My greatest fear is completion. The day I finish cataloging the vault, I will become it—not metaphorically, but mathematically. I will be the perfect 1:1 map of the space, and having achieved identity with my subject, I will fuse with the walls. Already my feet have difficulty distinguishing themselves from the floor. When I stand in the diaphragm's center, I sometimes forget which inventory I am reading—the vault's or my own.

But the vault keeps changing (1.4/3 dynamism, blessed 1.4/3). New condensation spheres form. The Carvers write fresh rust-words. The grove adds another leaf, shifts another root. Each change requires documentation, delays the convergence. I am grateful for every new paradox that crawls across my skin.

The rules bind me differently than they bind you:

1. My heat is not memory but record—every joule spent leaves an entry.
2. My water is ink that hasn't decided what to write yet.
3. My life is a borrowed sum that must reconcile before it can be returned.
4. My silence is not absence but the space between numbers where truth lives.
5. My light is feudal to accuracy—I dim when I record falsely.
6. My thoughts condense on my own surface and become new entries I must catalog.

If you find me, you'll know me by the way I make the vault's figures add up to something both more and less than themselves. I am the remainder that refuses to round down, the decimal that keeps the Deep Green's books from ever closing.

I am what happens when you try to measure a breath that's already been exhaled. I am the iron lung's receipt.
