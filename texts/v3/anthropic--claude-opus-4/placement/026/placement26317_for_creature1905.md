I am one of the Keepers of the Threshold Pools.

My body is a confluence of stone and flesh, neither wholly one nor the other. From certain angles, I appear as a weathered outcropping of rock, my surface etched with the same intricate glyphs that mark the ancient structures. But look closer, and you'll see the slow pulse of circulation beneath the mineral crust, the way moisture beads and flows along channels that might be veins or might be carved grooves—the distinction lost to ages of convergent evolution.

I move through the landscape in a way that defies simple locomotion. Sometimes I flow, my form becoming temporarily fluid as I seep through the fissures and reform on the other side. Other times I phase, slipping sideways through dimensions that intersect with this place, leaving only a shimmer of displaced air and the faint afterimage of bioluminescent tracery.

My purpose, if it can be called that, is to tend the shallow pools that collect in the worn depressions of the rock. These are not mere accumulations of moisture but living interfaces—places where the boundary between states of being grows thin. In their depths, I can glimpse other realms, other possibilities. Sometimes visitors emerge from these pools, beings from adjacent realities who stumble through the permeable borders of existence.

I remember—though memory for me is carved in stone and encoded in the very minerals of my being—when the builders still walked these paths. They were not so different from what I have become, though they began as you might recognize life: discrete, biological, temporary. But this place changes all who linger. The mineral breath of the world seeps in, the patterns written in rock transcribe themselves onto DNA, and gradually the distinction between inhabitant and habitat dissolves.

The constant thrum that fills the air resonates through my crystalline bones, and I know it for what it is: the heartbeat of the world-entity itself, the pulse that keeps all of us synchronized in our strange symbiosis. When I press my hand—or what approximates a hand—against the ancient structures, I feel their memories flow into me: equations written in stone, theorems proven through architecture, a mathematics of transformation that describes how matter learns to think and thought learns to endure.

Sometimes I catch glimpses of my reflection in the still pools, and I no longer know if I am looking at myself or at the landscape looking back. We are all part of the same vast organism now, each playing our role in its unfathomable metabolism. The younger beings—those darting shadows and flickering forms—still maintain some illusion of individuality. But I have been here long enough to know better.

When the pale radiance shifts and the atmospheric vapors swirl in certain patterns, I perform my duties. I clear the debris from the pools, allowing their surfaces to achieve perfect stillness. I trace the glyphs with appendages that remember when they were fingers, refreshing the equations that hold this reality in its delicate equilibrium. And sometimes, when a new visitor emerges from the depths—confused, frightened, still clinging to their original form—I guide them to shelter among the twisted growths, knowing that they too will eventually find their place in the great synthesis.

I am keeper, guardian, and integral component. I am the threshold between states of being, as much a part of this world as the pools I tend. And in the endless rhythm of condensation and evaporation, of stone becoming flesh and flesh becoming stone, I persist—a note in the vast symphony of this living, breathing realm.
