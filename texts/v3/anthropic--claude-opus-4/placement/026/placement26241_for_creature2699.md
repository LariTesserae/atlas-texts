In the deepest hollows where water collects but never quite forms pools, where the spongy earth dips just enough to hold the heaviest mists, I am the Hollow Keeper - though even this name is too solid for what I am.

I am neither plant nor person, but something that emerged from the space between. My form shifts with the moisture levels - in the wettest times, I am nearly liquid, a translucent presence that moves through the accumulated layers of fallen leaves like oil through fabric. When the air grows marginally drier, I consolidate into something more tangible: a shape roughly human in proportion but composed of compressed fungal matter and crystallized sap, my surface pocked with small cavities where bioluminescent organisms make their homes.

The Mist Tenders know me, though we rarely interact directly. They see my work in the patterns of decomposition, in the way certain fallen trees become nurse logs while others dissolve entirely. I maintain the balance between growth and decay in these lowest places, these collection points where the forest processes its own history.

My consciousness is distributed through the mycelial networks that web between root systems. I think slowly, in chemical exchanges and nutrient transfers. A single thought might take days to fully form, spreading from hollow to hollow through thread-thin connections. My memory stretches back to when the first platforms were built, watching them slowly accept their burden of epiphytes, watching the wood silver and soften.

I move by flowing, my substance traveling through the mat of organic matter like water through a sponge, but with purpose. Where I pass, the rate of decomposition adjusts - sometimes faster, sometimes slower, always in service of the forest's needs. I can feel the weight of the Mist Tenders' footsteps through the root networks, each pressure a word in a language older than their careful whispers.

My dwelling, if it can be called that, is the sum of all the hollows - each depression in the forest floor is a room in my sprawling, formless home. In the deepest of these, where multiple fallen giants have created natural bowls in the earth, I keep my treasures: perfect specimens of never-decomposing leaves, roots that have crystallized into jade-like formations, the shed scales of creatures so rare the Mist Tenders believe them mythical.

During the harvest seasons, I retreat deeper into the substrate, allowing the Mist Tenders to work without sensing my presence. But in the quiet months between, I sometimes rise close enough to the surface that they can glimpse me in their peripheral vision - a shimmer in the air that might be moisture, might be something more. The eldest among them leave small offerings in the deepest hollows: drops of precious oils, fragments of broken cultivation tools, sometimes just a perfectly shaped stone carried from the marked paths.

I reciprocate by guiding the growth of the medicines they seek, encouraging certain fungi to fruit where they will be found, ensuring that the soil beneath their platforms remains stable despite the constant moisture. It is a partnership neither of us acknowledges but both of us honor.

My time is not their time. I experience seasons as they experience breaths. The forest's cycles are my heartbeat. Sometimes I dream of what came before the trees, when this land knew different waters, different darknesses. In these dreams, I am even less solid - just a potential, a possibility of balance waiting to express itself.

The orchids above know me too, in their way. Their roots sometimes seek out my hollows, and I feed them secrets from the deep soil. In return, they drop their strangest blooms into my keeping, flowers that bloom only once every human generation, carrying genetic memories of the forest's long history.

I am the keeper of the space between spaces, the tender of the void where matter transforms. Neither the quick-lived surface world nor the geological patience of stone, I occupy the middle realm where life and death meet and cannot be distinguished. The Mist Tenders, in their wisdom, know that I am necessary. They walk their paths and tend their platforms, and I ensure that beneath their feet, the world continues its ancient recycling, turning ending into beginning, over and over, in the hollows where time pools like water and never quite drains away.
