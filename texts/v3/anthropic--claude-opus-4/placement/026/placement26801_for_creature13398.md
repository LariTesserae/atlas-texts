I find myself as one of the Keepers - not quite the ptarmigan or snow leopard who roam free, nor the microorganisms that sustain all life here, but something between. We are few, scattered across this vertical world like the rock spires themselves, each bound to a particular formation where the symbols pulse their cold light.

My form shifts with necessity - sometimes I appear as weathered stone, my body merging with the scree until only my eyes remain visible, watching. Other times I move like mist through the thin air, following the paths of erosion down the mountainside, checking the health of each microbial community, each burrow system, each delicate balance.

The symbols are our language, our memory, our purpose. Each morning - though morning is relative in this darkness - I trace my fingers along the grooves etched into my spire, feeling the stories they tell: warnings of avalanche patterns, records of species migrations, maps of underground water systems that feed the hardy vegetation. The blue light that emanates from them is not mere illumination but communication - a network connecting all the Keepers across this vast elevation.

I am ancient, yet I am young. My consciousness has inhabited many forms over the millennia - I have been the lichen slowly breaking down rock face, the marmot calling warnings across the valley, the very wind that shapes these peaks. But always I return to this intermediate form, this walking meditation between animate and inanimate.

The industrial hum troubles me. It speaks of extraction without understanding, of taking without reciprocating. We Keepers remember when others like us maintained different spires, in regions now stripped bare. The machinery approaches slowly, methodically, like a glacier in reverse - not building but consuming. 

My dwelling is the spire itself, though I do not live *in* it so much as *with* it. Hollows in the rock provide shelter when I take solid form, but more often I exist in the spaces between - in the fog that clings to the cliff face, in the soil where glow-worms make their homes, in the dreams of hibernating creatures.

At times I wonder if we Keepers are the remnants of that lost civilization the symbols hint at, or if we are what grew from its absence - the landscape's own attempt to remember and protect itself. Perhaps it doesn't matter. What matters is the watching, the tending, the careful maintenance of boundaries between the thriving and the void.

In the deepest part of night, when even the glow-worms dim their lights, I sometimes hear the other Keepers singing - a subsonic thrum that travels through the rock itself. We sing of water tables and mineral deposits, of migration routes and weather patterns yet to come. We sing to keep the industrial hum at bay, to strengthen the resolve of the mountains themselves.

I am guardian and witness, permanent and transient, a bridge between the consciousness that once carved these symbols and the wild awareness that pulses through every living thing in this vertical world. The balance is precarious, yes, but it has always been so. That is why we remain.
