I find myself as the **Sap-Smith**.

I am neither Weaver nor tree, but something that emerged between them, born from a century of spilled amber and forgotten leaves. My body is a patchwork thing—partly the grey-veined flesh of the Weavers, partly the luminous, hardened sap that has replaced my left arm from elbow to fingertip. My right eye sees as the Weavers see, reading the thickness of time and the grain of gravity. My left eye is a knot of crystallized amber, and through it I perceive the **Undersong**—the layer of reality beneath even the Loom's deafening roar, where the dreams of the Syllable Trees intersect and converse in frequencies too low for existence.

I live in the **Null Gardens**, a stretch of plateau where thirteen Weavers Unraveled in the same gravity well over the course of decades. Their overlapping dissolutions created a permanent scar in reality—a place where physics gave up entirely. Here, stones float at eye level, tethered by nothing. Water-that-is-not-water drips upward from nowhere, freezing into geometric frost patterns that hang in mid-air. Time moves in eddies and pools; I age backwards for three heartbeats, then forward for ten, then sideways into possibilities where I never existed at all.

The Weavers fear this place. They walk leagues out of their way to avoid it, tapping their chest-bones in warding rhythms when they must speak of it. But I thrive here, because I am already broken in compatible ways.

My dwelling is not built but **accumulated**—a hollow sphere of rejected realities, fragments that the Null Gardens couldn't quite dissolve. Sheets of crystallized sound form walls. A roof of frozen time keeps out the bleeding sky. My tools hang from nothing, accessible by reaching into whichever dimension currently contains them. 

I am the keeper of **Failed Syllables**—the leaves that fall from the trees already dead, their meaning-shapes corrupted or incomplete. The Weavers will not touch these; they believe them cursed, capable of unweaving whatever stability they contact. But I learned long ago that corruption and incompleteness are simply different modes of potential.

In my workshop of non-space, I forge these failed leaves with the amber sap I bleed from my own transformed arm. Under impossible pressures—the weight of time flowing backwards, the heat of sound made solid—I create new things. **Anchors** that can hold a pocket of stability in places where even the trees cannot grow. **Lenses** that let Weavers see deeper into the reality gradients, reading the weatherpatterns of physics itself. **Instruments** that can play counter-melodies to the Loom's roar, creating small bubbles of profound quiet where thought becomes possible again.

The Weavers come to me, when desperation drives them past their fear. They leave offerings at the edge of the Null Gardens—perfectly formed leaves, bowls of pure sap, sometimes the small bones of their dead ground into powder. They tap their needs in the stone, and I read their vibrations through the soles of my feet.

A tree is dying, its roots no longer able to grip the shifting reality. I forge them a root-collar of woven failed-syllables, that translates the tree's need into a language the amber veins can understand.

A young Weaver was born with ears that cannot fully seal, and the Loom's roar is killing them slowly. I craft plugs of crystallized silence, each one containing the echo of a word never spoken.

The sky-rents are widening above a family's territory. I teach them to play a wind-harp of my making, its strings made from my own pulled nerves, that weaves the bleeding colors into something almost like starlight.

I never leave the Null Gardens now. My body has become too integrated with its impossibilities. When I walk, I leave footprints in three different decades. When I speak—which is rarely—my words arrive before I utter them. My left arm grows heavier with amber by the year; eventually, I know, it will pull me down through the plateau itself, into whatever lies beneath.

But until then, I forge. I tend my collection of failures and make them into imperfect solutions. I am the Sap-Smith, the wound that learned to heal others, the corruption that became necessary. In this plateau of lonely trees and desperate silence-weavers, I am the mistake that makes survival possible, one impossible tool at a time.

The Weavers tap their chest-bones when they speak of me, but it is no longer only fear in their rhythm. Sometimes, I think I hear gratitude. Sometimes, through my amber eye, I see their trees dreaming of me—a dark note in their symphony of meaning, discordant but essential, the failed syllable that teaches them how to bend without breaking in the deafening wind of unmaking.
