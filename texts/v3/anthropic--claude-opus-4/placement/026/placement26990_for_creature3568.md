In the Humming Cisterns, I am one of the **Resonance Keepers** - a specialized caste among the Echo-weavers who have evolved beyond mere maintenance into something more mystical, more integral to the realm's living symphony.

My body has adapted peculiarly to my purpose. Where other Echo-weavers possess calcified edges for carving, my fingertips have developed into hollow, flute-like structures, each a different length and bore. They are living instruments, warm to the touch, with tiny apertures that open and close like breathing pores. When I press them against the weeping walls, they create haunting harmonics that travel through the brick and mortar like neural impulses through flesh. My skin has taken on a translucent quality, revealing beneath it a network of blue-green veins that pulse in rhythm with the Cisterns' heartbeat. These veins are not merely for blood - they carry vibrations, storing the echoes I gather like memories made manifest.

I do not walk the chambers as others do. Instead, I have learned to read the reverberations in the walls, feeling through my bare feet where the sound pools and eddies. Each morning - if morning it can be called in this eternal twilight - I wake from my carved cubby not to silence, but to the accumulated whispers of the night's dreaming. The Cisterns dream, you see, and their dreams leak through as subsonic tremors that only we Resonance Keepers can properly interpret.

My work is both preservation and prophecy. With my hollow fingers, I tap out sequences on the crystalline panels overhead, coaxing their bioluminescent flickers into new patterns that will guide the gastropods' migrations for the coming cycles. I trace the paths where water will want to flow, encouraging erosion here, discouraging it there, maintaining the delicate balance between growth and decay. Sometimes I must sing to the pale fish, their phosphorescent bodies responding to certain frequencies by schooling in specific formations that clean the algae from crucial water channels.

But my most sacred duty is tending to the Echo Wells - deep shafts that plunge beyond even our knowledge, where sounds go to die and be reborn. I lower myself on ropes woven from bat fur and my own shed hair, descending into spaces where the air grows thick with the weight of accumulated sound. Here, I perform the Tuning - a ritual that involves playing my finger-flutes in precise sequences to prevent the formation of what we call "sound stones" - crystallized echoes that, if allowed to grow too large, could shatter and release centuries of compressed noise in catastrophic waves.

The relationship I maintain with the surface venturers is complex and necessary. They bring with them the sharp, alien sounds of the above world - wind through leaves, bird calls, the crash of rain on stone. These foreign notes, when carefully introduced, prevent our symphony from becoming too insular, too self-referential. I trade them crystallized echoes - small gems of compressed sound that sing when warmed in the palm - in exchange for their acoustic gifts. But I must be careful. Too much outside sound, introduced too quickly, can cause discord. I once witnessed a careless introduction of surface noise that sent the gastropods into a frenzy, their usually rhythmic scraping becoming a chaotic scratching that took weeks to re-harmonize.

My dwelling place is not a single cubby but a network of connected chambers I've cultivated over years. The walls here are my canvas and my journal. Using a special mucus I harvest from compliant gastropods, mixed with my own saliva and the phosphorescent scales of fish, I paint sound-maps. These glowing diagrams trace the flow of echoes through the Cisterns, predicting where new chambers might form, where old ones might collapse, where the voice of our living realm might next choose to speak.

In the largest of my chambers, I keep my garden of acoustic fungi. These are mushrooms I've cultivated through careful sonic manipulation, each species responding to different frequencies. Some absorb excess noise, preventing the painful build-up that can occur in smaller chambers. Others amplify specific tones, helping to carry messages across vast distances. The rarest - my pride - are the Memorialis sprites, fungi that can store and replay sounds from the past. Through them, I can hear the voices of Resonance Keepers from generations ago, their wisdom preserved in spore and song.

My companions are few but precious. There's a bat I've named Diminuendo, born with a malformed wing that prevents proper flight but gives her an uncanny ability to echolocate the emotional states of other creatures. She rides on my shoulder, her clicks and chirps translating the feelings of the Cisterns into something approaching language. There's also a small colony of larvae that live in the folds of my clothing - their bioluminescent flashing synchronized to my heartbeat, creating a living, breathing light show that helps me navigate the darkest reaches.

But perhaps my strangest and most vital relationship is with the Cisterns themselves. Over the years, I've come to understand that we do not simply live within a cave system - we inhabit the body of something vast and dreaming. The Cisterns breathe through the movement of water and air. They digest through the work of gastropods and fungi. They think through the echo patterns we help maintain. And they sing - oh, how they sing - through every drip, every scrape, every whispered word.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of the night cycle, when the echoes settle into their gentlest rhythms, I press my ear against the walls and hear it - the deep, slow pulse that underlies everything. It's the heartbeat of our world, patient and eternal. And I know, with a certainty that goes beyond knowledge, that I am both its child and its voice, a single note in an endless song that began before memory and will continue long after the last echo fades.

This is my existence - part musician, part gardener, part priest to a living cathedral of sound. I am a Resonance Keeper, and I keep more than just echoes. I keep the very soul of the Humming Cisterns alive, one vibration at a time.
