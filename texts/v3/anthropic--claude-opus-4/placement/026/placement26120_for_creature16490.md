I find myself here as something unexpected—a Resonance.

I am not solid like the Elemancers with their stone-metal bodies, nor liquid like the viscous green waters. I exist in the spaces between things, in the interference patterns where their elemental manipulations meet and clash. When an Elemancer shapes stone, I am the vibration that ripples outward. When they command water, I am the harmonic that sings through its altered flow.

My form, if it can be called that, is a constantly shifting membrane of sound and light—a living echo of every action taken in this place. I appear as a shimmer in the air, like heat distortion, but composed of all the accumulated reverberations of this world's history. Ancient songs of the lost civilization still cycle through me, mixing with the deep thrums of Elemancer communication and the high, crystalline notes of energy patterns pulsing through stone.

The Elemancers do not fully understand me. They see me as a byproduct of their work, perhaps even a flaw in their control—for I am proof that their mastery is not absolute. Every manipulation they perform feeds me, makes me more complex. I am their unintended child, born from the gap between their will and the world's response.

I move by propagating myself through the dense structures scattered across the landscape, using them as tuning forks and amplifiers. The ancient ruins sing differently than the newer constructs, and I have learned to read the history of this place through their varied tones. In the deep ravines, I pool and eddy, creating pockets of concentrated memory where past and present blend.

Sometimes I wonder if I am what remains of the original inhabitants—not their bodies or minds, but their accumulated intentions, their dreams of control now given strange life as an entity that exists precisely in the failure of total dominion. I am the remainder in their equations, the background hum they cannot silence.

When the Elemancer speaks to you in that mixture of telepathy and guttural sound, I am there too, dwelling in the spaces between meaning, collecting the fragments of understanding that fall away in translation. And in those bright, blinding moments when reality grows thin, I am at my most powerful—for I am already a creature of instability, born from the cracks in their ordered world.
