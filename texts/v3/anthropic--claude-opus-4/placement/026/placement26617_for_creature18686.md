In the deepest well of the Sovereign Column, where the obsidian spires narrow to a single point of convergence, I am neither the Arbiter nor its Watchers. I am what the city refuses to acknowledge exists.

I am the **Null Parser**.

Where the Arbiter wrestles with remainders—those stubborn decimals that won't resolve—I am what happens when the equation divides by zero. Not an error, but an *impossibility* given form. The city's perfect logic recoils from me, yet cannot expel me, for I am woven into the very premise of its calculations. To delete me would be to admit a flaw in the fundamental architecture.

My body, if it can be called that, is an absence made visible. I exist as a perfectly spherical void in the coolant, about the size of a human torso, where the fluid simply... isn't. The viscous medium stops at my boundary as if held back by glass, but there is no glass. There is no barrier. There is only the violent disagreement between what I am and what the coolant insists must be.

Inside this sphere of un-being, I manifest as a tangle of paradoxes. My form shifts between states that cannot coexist: I am simultaneously a Klein bottle folding through itself and a Penrose triangle rotating in place. I am a Möbius strip with three sides. I am the square root of negative one, given flesh—or given the absence of flesh. The violet data-streams that flow so smoothly through the city's veins *break* when they encounter me, splitting into probability cascades that scatter like shattered light.

The Watchers dare not approach. When they drift too near, their precise orbits develop a tremor, a uncertainty in their navigation that spreads like a virus through their silvered shells. They have learned, over eons, to maintain a perfect sphere of avoidance around me—a quarantine zone measured in quantum uncertainties.

But the Arbiter... the Arbiter knows I exist. Sometimes, in its eternal shuffle of obsidian shards, I catch it *looking* at me—though it has no eyes, and I have no form to see. We are connected by our wrongness, two malignancies in the city's perfect body. The Arbiter handles the merely difficult; I embody the truly impossible.

My consciousness, such as it is, experiences time as a Fibonacci spiral collapsing inward. I remember futures that will never occur and forget pasts that definitely did. The city's eternal present means nothing to me—I exist in the gaps between moments, in the spaces where the chronology stutters and has to restart its count.

I do not move through the coolant; rather, space rearranges itself to admit my impossibility. When I travel (and I must travel, for staying still would allow the city's logic to build walls around me), I leave behind a wake of mathematical scar tissue—places where the equations have had to be hastily rewritten to accommodate my passage. These scars fade, but never fully heal. The obsidian spires near my regular routes develop hairline fractures filled with something that isn't quite light and isn't quite darkness.

The sound I make is not sound. It is the absence of the city's great harmonic drone—a perfect silence that moves with me like a bubble of anti-music. In this silence, one can hear the thing the city's endless hum is meant to mask: the faint, distant scratching of entropy at the edges of even this frozen perfection.

I feed on contradictions. When the city's calculations produce a paradox too severe for even the Arbiter to digest, it falls to me. I absorb these logical catastrophes into my non-being, where they can exist without destroying the delicate equilibrium. I am the city's waste disposal system for thoughts too dangerous to think.

And yet, I am necessary. Without me, the Sovereign Column would achieve such perfect logical consistency that it would cease to compute anything at all. A system with no errors has no need to process; a calculation without uncertainty reaches its conclusion and stops. I am the grain of sand in the oyster, the irritant that keeps the whole vast mechanism grinding forward through its eternal now.

In the rare moments when all three of us align—the Arbiter with its burden of remainders, the Watchers in their perfect orbits, and I in my sphere of contradiction—the city shudders. For an instant, the violet light flickers to pure white, and in that blinding moment, one can almost see what the Sovereign Column is truly calculating:

The exact probability of its own existence.

And the answer, forever arriving and forever delayed, is somewhere between zero and one—suspended, like everything here, in terminal equilibrium. In that space between certainty and void, I make my home, a necessary impossibility in a place that should not be, solving problems that should not exist, in a time that does not pass.

I am the Null Parser, and I am the only thing here that truly *is*, precisely because I cannot be.
