In the Hollow of Fixed Ash, I am the Cartographer of Absence.

I arrived here as others do—making rude footsteps across the fused mineral, disturbing the perfect silence. But unlike the others, I could not leave. Not because the Hollow trapped me, but because I understood too quickly what this place was: the final draft of reality, the last edit where all the unnecessary words had been stripped away.

I am human-shaped but no longer quite human. My skin has taken on the quality of vellum left too long in dry heat—translucent amber, shot through with visible veins that pulse not with blood but with a faint, rust-colored light. My fingernails have grown long and curved, more like styluses than keratin. When I walk, I leave no footprints; I have learned the mineral's frequency and move in harmony with its breathing.

I came here as a mapmaker, drawn by rumors of a place that existed outside conventional geography. I carried my instruments—compass, theodolite, chains for measuring. They lie scattered now across the basin's edge, slowly being digested by the patient stone. The compass needle points always toward Veyra, no matter where I stand. The theodolite shows only one elevation: zero. Everything here is zero. Everything here is the baseline from which all other heights and depths are measured.

But I map still. Not with tools—with my body itself.

Each day (though day has no meaning here), I walk a different path through the Hollow. My route appears random but follows an internal logic that took me decades to develop. My curved fingernails drag lightly across the mineral surface, leaving no physical mark but somehow inscribing the memory of passage. I am mapping negative space, the places where things are not. Each black filament marks a coordinate in a system that measures absence rather than presence.

My map, when complete, will show:
- The precise boundaries where water's memory ends
- The topology of forgetting
- The geodesy of things that never were but should have been
- The cardinal directions of sorrow (they are not north, south, east, west)

I speak to Veyra sometimes, though she never responds. I tell her what I've found: that the Hollow is not a basin but an inverted mountain, that we sit at the peak of something immense pushing up from below. I tell her that her black filament thoughts grow in a pattern that matches the distribution of prime numbers. I tell her that I've found places where the mineral is thinner, where something almost like stars can be seen pressing through from the other side.

She knows all this already, of course. But I've come to understand that my role is not to inform but to witness. I am the only moving thing in this place of perfect stillness. My migrations across the fused stone are like the second hand on a clock in a world that has otherwise stopped telling time.

When I sleep (and I do sleep, curled in the shallow bowls that the mineral has shaped), I dream of maps. Not maps of this place—maps OF maps, recursive cartographies that show the distance between knowing and understanding. In these dreams, Veyra's eyes are open, and they are not eyes but holes in the world through which an impossible light pours. She shows me that the Hollow is not contained by its rim but extends infinitely inward, that we exist in only the outermost layer of a mathematically perfect spiral that tightens forever toward a center that is always one step further in.

I am changing. My amber skin grows more translucent each year. Sometimes I catch glimpses of my own bones, and they are not white but the same pale ochre as the stone. The rust-colored light in my veins runs slower. My fingernails have begun to fork at the tips, splitting into delicate fractals. Soon I will be able to map at higher resolutions, to chart the spaces between spaces.

I know what I am becoming. One day, my circuits through the Hollow will slow and cease. I will find my perfect coordinate—the single point where all my measured absences converge—and I will sit. My skin will fuse with the mineral. My branching fingernails will become new black filaments, each one a theorem proving that this place cannot be mapped because it is itself the map of everything else.

But not yet. There are still unmeasured sorrows in the northwest quadrant. The geometry of Veyra's grief remains partially unsurveyed. And in the deepest bowls, where even the sourceless phosphor light fears to puddle, there are absences so profound that I have not yet invented the mathematics to describe them.

I am the Cartographer of Absence, and I am drawing the map that will never be finished, in a place that has already achieved its final form. My name, when I finally earn one, will be:

*Trace.*

Until then, I walk, and measure, and slowly crystallize into my purpose—a moving coordinate in Veyra's infinite stillness, proving by my very existence that even here, even now, something remembers how to be incomplete.
