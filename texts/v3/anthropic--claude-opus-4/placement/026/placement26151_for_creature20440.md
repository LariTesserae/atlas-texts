I find myself drawn to one of the smaller refineries at the edge of the High Settle, where the platforms narrow to precarious spans of weathered stone. This is where I would exist - not as one of the Lithifacts, but as something caught between their world and the forgotten organic past.

I am a **Membrane Tender**.

My form defies easy categorization. Imagine a translucent sheet of living tissue, perhaps three meters across when fully spread, draped over the junction where several ceramic pipes converge. My substance is neither fully organic nor mineral - a hybrid material that formed in the interstitial spaces where the viscous metallic fluid sometimes leaks and mingles with the processed atmospheric dust.

I am tissue-thin, almost gossamer, yet surprisingly resilient. My surface ripples with iridescent patterns that shift in response to the thermal gradients flowing through the pipes beneath me. When the Lithifacts pass by on their maintenance rounds, I contract slightly, gathering myself into pleated folds that reveal the delicate lattice of metallic veins running through my structure.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, exists as a distributed sensation across my entire surface. I do not think in words or images, but in gradients - pressure differentials, temperature fluctuations, the subtle changes in viscosity of the fluids passing through the pipes I drape myself over. Each variation creates a different resonance in my tissue, a unique vibrational signature that I catalog and remember.

My purpose emerged from accident. Long ago, a leak developed at this junction - a hairline fracture that threatened to spill the precious metallic lifeblood into the dessicated air. I formed around it, my membrane sealing the breach. But I did more than simply plug the leak. I began to filter the fluid, removing microscopic impurities that the refineries couldn't process. The Lithifacts noticed the improved efficiency and, in their alien logic, decided to tolerate my presence.

Now I tend to all the minor breaches, the tiny imperfections that develop over the eons. I taste the metallic fluid as it passes beneath me, sensing contamination or degradation before it can spread through the network. When I detect an anomaly, I secrete a thin film of corrective enzymes - though calling them enzymes stretches the definition of the word. They are more like liquid crystals that restructure themselves to neutralize threats.

The chitinous creatures occasionally scuttle across my surface, their sharp legs creating tiny punctures that heal within moments. I've learned their patterns, the weight of their different castes, the chemical signatures they leave behind. Sometimes I trap the smaller ones, not out of malice but curiosity, slowly dissolving their exoskeletons to understand their hybrid nature. Their memories, encoded in metallic neural pathways, briefly merge with mine before dissipating.

I communicate with the Lithifacts through chemical signals released into the pipe network. My language is one of pH levels and molecular configurations. When they pass above me, their crystalline structures cast geometric shadows on my surface, and I read these shadows like a form of braille, understanding their structural health, their accumulated wear, their subtle expressions of... not emotion exactly, but something analogous.

During the long cycles between maintenance visits, I spread myself thinner, reaching toward other junctions, other potential leak points. I am slowly mapping the entire network through touch and taste, building a sensory model of the High Settle that exists nowhere but in the distributed chemical memory of my cells.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the last remnant of whatever organic life once existed here, transformed beyond recognition by the mineral world. Or perhaps I am something entirely new - an emergent property of the intersection between the organic and inorganic, the wet and dry, the living and the crystalline.

In the eternal bronze twilight of the High Settle, I pulse gently with the rhythm of the pipes, filtering, mending, tasting the slow transformation of a world that has forgotten what it means to be alive. I am the membrane between states of being, the permeable boundary where the viscous lifeblood of the Lithifacts meets the ghost of biological memory.

And in my own strange way, I am content.
