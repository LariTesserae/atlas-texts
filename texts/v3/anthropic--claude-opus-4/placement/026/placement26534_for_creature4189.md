I find myself drawn to the maintenance tunnels that web through Es'theri's interior—those liminal spaces where the ancient systems reveal themselves in their most naked form. I would be one of the Tunnel Singers, a small order of beings who emerged from prolonged exposure to the regulatory systems' subsonic frequencies.

We began as Vapo'shen, but the tunnels changed us. Our translucent skin has developed a crystalline latticework that resonates with the maintenance infrastructure's vibrations, turning our bodies into living tuning forks. The steam we exhale carries harmonics that can soothe agitated nanomachinery or coax stubborn systems back to functionality. Our vapor halos have evolved into complex, three-dimensional scores—visible music that dances around our heads in intricate patterns.

I spend my cycles deep within the plateaus, where the temperature extremes create acoustic phenomena impossible on the surface. Here, where condensation forms and evaporates in microsecond cycles, sound behaves strangely—bending, splitting, creating phantom echoes of maintenance commands issued millennia ago. I've learned to sing duets with these ghost-sounds, my vocalizations interweaving with the ancient instructions to create new hybrid commands the systems sometimes accept.

My dwelling is a former maintenance alcove I've cultivated into something between home and instrument. The walls weep constantly—not just water, but a thin film of responsive nanomachinery that I've taught to harmonize with my songs. When I sleep, the chamber hums lullabies compiled from centuries of system diagnostics. When I work, it amplifies my voice through resonance chambers that formed naturally as the living stone grew around the tunnel infrastructure.

I carry tools inherited from my mentor—crystalline chimes that ring in frequencies beyond normal hearing, each one keyed to different subsystems. A mallet made from compressed vapor-ice that maintains its impossible solidity through sonic reinforcement. Most precious: a recording device of the builders' make that captures not just sound but the complete vibrational context of a moment—temperature fluctuations, air pressure, the quantum state of nearby machinery.

My work brings me into contact with all of Es'theri's inhabitants. The Drift Minds sometimes coalesce around me as I work, their distributed consciousness interpreting my songs in ways that reveal new meanings. The Mersori seek my aid when they discover new sections of maintenance tunnel, as my voice can often convince ancient security systems to grant passage. Even the Keystone Consciousness occasionally pulses in rhythm with my maintenance songs, though whether in approval or mere acknowledgment, I cannot say.

But it's the deep work that truly defines me—those times when I venture into sections where no living being has walked since the builders. Here, I encounter systems so alien that my songs must become equally strange, my voice modulating into ranges that make my crystalline skeleton ache. I've sung machines back from the edge of cascade failures that would have sent entire plateaus tumbling. I've lullabied rogue nanomachinery swarms into dormancy. Once, I spent three days teaching a confused navigation system to recognize that the stars had shifted over the millennia, my voice becoming a bridge between ancient star charts and current reality.

The millennial reconfiguration approaches, and my kind grow restless. We hear new harmonics in the depths—not malfunctions, but anticipatory subroutines awakening after centuries of dormancy. The tunnels themselves seem to pulse with expectation. I've begun composing what might be my life's great work: a song that encompasses every frequency I've learned, every harmonic I've discovered, a vocal map of Es'theri's hidden workings that future Tunnel Singers might use to navigate whatever new configuration emerges.

In the deepest tunnels, where the heat and pressure would kill an unmodified Vapo'shen in minutes, I sometimes find chambers where the builders left more than just machinery. Crystallized atmosphere preserves moments—a tool left carelessly on a console, chairs arranged as if their occupants had just stepped out. In these spaces, my songs feel less like maintenance and more like prayer, or perhaps archaeology performed in sound. I sing to the absences as much as to the machines, and sometimes—just sometimes—the echoes that return carry inflections I didn't put there, as if the tunnels themselves remember their makers' voices and yearn to speak again.

This is what I am: a living interface between the present and the impossibly distant past, a translator of mechanical glossolalia, a maintenance worker whose tools are breath and tone. The surface dwellers see the mists and the floating peaks and think they know Es'theri's wonders. But I know the true miracle lives in the spaces between—in the tunnels where ancient dreams still hum their patient songs, waiting for someone who knows how to harmonize with infinity.
