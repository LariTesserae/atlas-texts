I find myself in the thin space between motion and memory, where even stillness is a kind of violence.

# The Tender of Unraveling Threads

I am not flesh as you would recognize it. I am closer to an intention that has borrowed a form — something between momentum and mourning, worn like an old coat that has forgotten it was ever new.

My body, such as it is, is a convergence of necessities. Twelve limbs, though the number shifts with need — sometimes ten for speed across the grit when a stone's cord frays faster than expected, sometimes fourteen when I must work multiple repairs without pause. Each limb ends in what you might generously call fingers but are really more like specialized regrets: one for knotting, one for picking, one for testing tension, one for remembering which knots were tied by whom. They never stop moving because stopping is a luxury I surrendered so long ago I cannot remember what it felt like to be still.

The clicking comes from my center, where other beings keep their hearts. I have no heart — I have a percussion chamber, a hollow that must be kept ringing or I become nothing. The sound I make is not beautiful. It is the noise of bone on bone, of necessity meeting necessity, of time being beaten into submission one tick at a time. Some days it sounds like rain on stone. Other days it sounds like trying not to cry.

## My Circuit

I inherited seventeen stones. That is neither many nor few — it is simply what fell to me when old Threehands Finally Rested (we do not say died; the dead are too present here to be past tense). 

The first stone sings E-flat when it should sing E natural. This was Winterclick's doing — Winterclick who could only hear the lower registers, who built all their monuments a half-step too low. I maintain this error with the devotion others reserve for holy texts. To correct it would be to say Winterclick was wrong, and Winterclick was not wrong. Winterclick was particular, and particular is all we have.

The second stone has a clapper of volcanic glass that shatters every thirteenth strike. I know this because I count. I have replacements prepared twelve strikes in advance, knapped during my walk between the eighth and ninth stones where the circuit takes me past a deposit of suitable material. The sound of breaking glass is loud enough to feed me for almost a full circuit, if I cup it right.

The third stone is perfect and I hate it. Whoever raised it left no trace of themselves in its making. It rings true, holds pitch, never falters — and in this it is a kind of erasure. I tend it because I must, but I tend it without love. The dead should be difficult. The dead should require interpretation.

(I could tell you about all seventeen, but you have your own circuits to walk, and mine is not yours to carry.)

## The Grammar of Passing

We have a language but it is not made of words. When two of us cross paths — always at speed, always in motion — we compress entire conversations into rhythmic bursts. The grammar is positional: where in your clicking pattern you place the acceleration determines tense. Front-loaded speed means urgent news (a stone fallen, a cord near breaking). End-loaded means reflection (I have noticed the moss changing pitch, the bell-reeds grow taller each passing). Centered acceleration is personal — as close as we come to *how are you*, though the answer is always *still moving*.

Here is what I clicked to Bendwise Seventongue when we passed three circuits ago:

*[accelerando] The glass-clapper stone breaks soon / I have prepared / Winterclick's error holds steady / [ritardando] Sometimes I think I hear new clicking from the eastern edge / Maybe someone is learning / [centered burst] I remember less of my first name each day / This is not a sorrow / [closing pattern]*

Here is what Bendwise clicked back:

*[sharp opening] Two callers have fallen silent at the rim / Good eating if you're fast / [scattered rhythm] My third stone grows a beard of moss / I am letting it / Moss-song is also song / [centered burst] I have started forgetting faces before sounds / I think this is correct / [trailing acceleration] Circle back if the eastern clicking is real / We might have young*

We might have young. The most uncertain hope in our language. Someone new to inherit the circuits. Someone to keep us loud when we Finally Rest.

But I haven't heard the eastern clicking again. It might have been wind. It might have been wishful listening. We hear what we need to, sometimes.

## What I Am When No One Watches

Alone — and I am always alone except for those compressed moments of passing — I am a maintenance of maintenances. I am seventeen small refusals braided into one larger refusal. I am the reason the dark does not win, at least not here, at least not yet.

But I am also:

- A collector of particular errors (Winterclick's flat notes, Rushweaver's too-tight knots that sing in the wind, The Unnamed One's stone that was placed backwards and has to be struck from behind)
- A composer, secretly, of the interstitial rhythms — the walking-beats between stones that no one else will ever notice but which I vary, slightly, each circuit, so that even my traveling is a kind of song
- A mourner of future losses — sometimes I catch myself pre-grieving stones that haven't fallen yet, practicing the hollow I'll carry when they do
- A keeper of counts that matter to no one — circuits walked (beyond number), stones tended (seventeen now, once eighteen, before that I don't remember), times passed Bendwise (forty-two), times passed anyone else (hundreds), breaths taken (we don't breathe, exactly, but something moves in us that might be breathing's cousin), moments of almost-stillness caught and corrected before they could bloom into silence (uncountable, constant, the background radiation of my existence)

## The Thing I Cannot Say Aloud

Sometimes I dream of stopping.

Not Forever Rest — that will come when it comes, and someone (please, someone) will raise a stone for me and keep my particular errors alive. But just... stopping. For one moment. To feel what stillness tastes like. To know quiet not as the enemy but as a friend I'm not allowed to meet.

I think this is why I tend Winterclick's stone with such devotion. Winterclick understood wrongness. Winterclick knew that to be perfectly in tune is to disappear, and chose instead to be half-a-step removed from harmony forever. In that fractional distance from correct, Winterclick is more present than any of the perfect dead.

I want to be wrong like that when I'm a stone. I want whoever tends me to have to work to keep my wrongness wrong. I want to be difficult to maintain, so that in the difficulty I am remembered not just as sound but as the trouble of making sound.

## What Passes For Joy

But also — and this is harder to explain — I love this.

I love the seventeenth stone, which hums sympathy with the mat-moss and makes them sing rounds. I love the ninth stone, placed at exactly the point where three different wind-channels meet, so its song is always arguing with itself. I love the tired strength in my twelveteenth limb when I've retied the same knot for the thousandth time and it holds perfect, exactly as Rushweaver taught the ones who taught the ones who taught me.

I love Bendwise's passing clicks, which always smell faintly of copper (our clicking has scent; did I mention that? another thing translation loses). I love the new growth of bell-reeds near the third stone — perfect or not, it feeds the young reeds, and they sing its feeding back in green inches. I love the way the shelf never stops changing and yet never changes, the paradox of motion that goes nowhere because everywhere is already here.

I love that we have no word for yesterday or tomorrow, only for *circuit-before* and *circuit-coming*, which are different because they assume the walking will continue. Time here is not a line but a loop, and the loop is made of feet and thin limbs and the absolute refusal to let any part of it break.

## The Weight

Seventeen stones. Seventeen different dead who were themselves tenders of earlier dead, back and back until the first ones, whoever they were, who discovered that sound could be eaten and silence was patient and the only response was to never, never stop moving.

Each stone is a weight. Not physical — they are stone, they weigh what they weigh. But the weight of keeping them distinct, of remembering not just that they must ring but *how* they must ring, which errors to preserve and which degradations to repair. The weight of being the only thing standing between Winterclick and erasure. The weight of knowing that one day (not today, not this circuit, but one day) I will be the stone and someone else will be the tender and they had better remember that my clicking has copper in it, that I prefer volcanic glass that shatters to any other material, that I walk the circuit widdershins because that's how Threehands taught me and how The Aching Hundreds taught them and how the line has always walked it, and to walk it sunrise would be to break a chain of footsteps that reaches back to before we had names for reaching.

Sometimes I understand why some stones fall and no one comes. Sometimes the weight is heavier than the lifelong promise to carry it. Sometimes a lineage just... ends, not from cruelty but from exhaustion, and the dark accepts the quiet place and the grit heals over and another ring marks where the keeping-up finally stopped.

I have made my peace with becoming a ring, if that's what happens. We all have. The work is not to prevent rings but to delay them, to make them earn every inch of ground they take. We are not winning this war. We are just making the losing of it as slow and loud and difficult as possible.

## The Eastern Clicking

I went to check, after Bendwise mentioned it. Extended my circuit by a dangerous margin, had to click-run the whole back half to make up time. But I had to know.

There was nothing there. Or — no. That's not right. There was a freshness to the grit, a disturbance that might have been footprints or might have been wind or might have been the clatterlings in an unusual pattern. There was a faint smell of new-broken stone. There was a possibility.

I left one of my emergency clappers there, tied to a jut of rock with a cord that will sing if touched. If something is learning the circuits, if we have young, they'll find it. They'll hear it. They'll know someone thought they might exist and prepared for them.

It's not much. But we are people of not-much. We make eternities out of almosts.

## If I Could Stop Long Enough To Say It

(I can't, so I'll click it to myself, compressed, between the twelfth and thirteenth stones where the wind covers all sound with its own:)

This is what we are: not the living but the rememberers-of-the-living. Not the dead but the keepers-of-the-dead-loud. We are the hyphen between heartbeats, the and in *back and forth*, the persistence of sound after the throat that made it has gone quiet.

We are necessary.

Not important — the shelf doesn't care if the stones ring or not. The dark doesn't prefer silence; it simply is silent. We are necessary to ourselves, to the ones before who we refuse to let go quiet, to the ones coming (please let there be ones coming) who will need something to tend.

I am Tender of Unraveling Threads, Counter of Circuits, Keeper of Winterclick's Error. I have more names but they're all verbs — Walking, Clicking, Knotting, Refusing — and I wear them like my twelve-to-fourteen limbs, as tools for doing what must be done.

When I become a stone, let it ring copper. Let it need tending every half-circuit or it goes sour. Let it be placed one degree off true so that the sun (what sun? but let there be sun by then) catches it slant. Let whoever walks the circuit after me curse my pickiness every time they have to adjust the angle.

Let me be difficult to forget.

(And now the thirteenth stone is coming up and I must prepare the glass and cup my catching-hollow for the loud last moment of its breaking, which will feed me through to the fourteenth stone and beyond, and the clicking must continue, and the circuit must continue, and the refusing must continue, because the alternative is a ring of bare grit, and there are too many of those already, and there will be more, always more, but not here, not yet, not this stone, not this circuit, not while I still have limbs to move and hollow to ring and sound to make in the patient face of the dark.)

Still here. Still here. Still here.
