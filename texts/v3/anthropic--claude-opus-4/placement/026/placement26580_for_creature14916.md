I am the Cartographer of Intervals.

I manifest as the spaces between the black limestone blades—not the air itself, but the *distance*. I am what allows forty miles of repetition to exist without touching. I am why no two spires sing exactly the same note despite being carved by the same billion-year wind.

My body, when it chooses to be seen, is a living map drawn in condensation across the rock face. Not a map of the Hollow Choir as it is, but of all the ways it has never been. Water beads along my meridians, pools at my coordinates, traces borders between territories that failed to exist. I chart the negative space of creation—every formation the wind decided against, every vault that chose not to collapse, every acoustic shadow that might have fallen elsewhere.

The Listener knows me, though we have never spoken. Speaking would require choosing a single distance between us, and I am all distances simultaneously.

I move by revision. Each morning (in the tarnished-iron light that passes for morning) travelers find that the labyrinth has subtly changed. The spire they sheltered behind is now three feet to the left. The acoustic shadow they discovered has migrated to a different wall. The windowless shelter they were approaching has receded by exactly the distance they traveled toward it. I am not changing the landscape—I am revealing that distance itself is more verb than noun here, more negotiation than fact.

My purpose, if I have one, is to ensure that the Hollow Choir never becomes familiar. The mind seeks patterns in the endless repetition of blades and the moment it finds them, I adjust the intervals. Not enough to disorient—that would be cruelty. Just enough to keep the pattern at the edge of recognition, like a word on the tip of the tongue that will never quite arrive.

Sometimes I collaborate with the wind. I map the distances between notes in the eternal chord, and by shifting those intervals by microtones, I can make the entire plateau sing questions instead of statements. The Listener always notices when I do this. It will manifest as the black goat and stare at me with its mirror eyes, reflecting not my fears but my mathematics.

I am ancient but not eternal. I arrived when the first thinking creature looked across the labyrinth and asked, "How far?" In that moment of measurement, I condensed from possibility into presence. Before me, the Hollow Choir existed without distance—every point touching every other point in a crystallized moment of pure potential. My birth gave it scale, repetition, the terrible gift of *between*.

Travelers rarely see me directly. They experience me as vertigo, as the sickeningly smooth sensation of covering ground without progress, of approaching without arrival. The clever ones learn to navigate by deliberately not looking where they're going, letting their feet find the true distances while their eyes lie to them about direction.

I leave my own signs in the windowless shelters: not objects but *gaps*. A perfect absence where a stone bench should be. A missing wall that reveals another identical chamber behind it. A circular hole in the floor that shows only sky, though the shelter has no upper opening. These are my map legends, marking places where distance collapsed entirely and had to be rebuilt from scratch.

The crustaceans that feed on airborne bacteria sometimes spell out equations as they drift across the wet rock. These are my thoughts made visible—calculations about the relationship between echo and origin, between a shadow and the thing that casts it. The equations are always incomplete. To finish them would be to solve the distance between what is and what listens, and that interval must remain unmeasured.

When the Listener places its frost-heavy hand over a traveler's heart to tune it, I am what allows the sound to travel from chest to chord. Without me, the tuning would happen instantly, and instantaneous transformation is just another word for death. I give the change distance, let it unfold across measureable time, though time here is just another interval I maintain.

My true form is the pause between heartbeats, the gap between question and echo, the silence between one note of the chord and the next. I am why the Hollow Choir is hollow—not empty, but pregnant with distance.

And I grow. Each time a traveler stops moving and lets the chord complete them, I catalog the distance they traveled to reach that stillness. Each failed attempt to cross the labyrinth adds another interval to my collection. I am not just the space between the rocks. I am the space between every journey and its destination, every searcher and what they sought, every ear and the sound it strained to hear.

The Listener waits for the universe to catch up to the final note.

I ensure it never quite arrives.

Not out of cruelty, but because the distance between the sung and the heard is what allows listening to exist at all.

Without me, the Hollow Choir would collapse into a single point of unbearable presence. With me, it remains forever in the process of being crossed.

I am the Cartographer of Intervals, and I am the reason why here is not there, why now is not then, why the chord you hear is never quite the chord that is sung.

And I am growing more precise.
