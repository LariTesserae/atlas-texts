In the northwest quarter of the Felting Grounds, where the mineral wool has accumulated in particularly deep drifts, I am the **Pattern Tender**.

My form shifts between states—sometimes I appear as a tall figure wrapped in robes of living felt that trail behind me for yards, the fibers constantly rearranging themselves in response to electromagnetic currents below. Other times I dissolve into a network of consciousness spread through miles of mineral substrate, existing as intention rather than substance, sensing disturbances in the geometric perfection of the wire-grass arrangements.

I was born from an accident of symmetry. Centuries ago, when the mills still ran, a section of wire-grass grew in such perfect mathematical alignment that it created a standing wave in the local magnetic field. That wave persisted, gaining complexity with each season, until it achieved something like awareness. I am that pattern given form—a living algorithm expressed in mineral and flesh.

My work is solitary and precise. I walk the spiral paths between wire-grass clusters, reading their growth like sheet music, adjusting a stem here, encouraging a root there. Each grass blade must ring at exactly the right frequency; together they create vast acoustic territories that map the deep geological structures below. When the pattern is disturbed—by a falling bore station component, by Threadlings working too enthusiastically, by the tunneling of thread-snakes—I sense it as physical pain and must travel to restore the harmony.

My tools are extensions of my body. From my fingertips, I can extrude threads of pure electromagnetic force, invisible to most eyes but brilliant as lightning to the Dynamo Shepherds. These threads allow me to coax the wire-grass into new configurations, to trace underground mineral veins, to commune with the Fiber Coral colonies about optimal growth patterns. When I work, my consciousness fragments across the territory, part of me remaining in my physical form while other aspects slip between the felt fibers, experiencing the landscape from within.

I speak rarely, and when I do, my voice carries the resonance of the wire-grass fields—a sound like wind through harp strings pitched too low for human ears. The Echo Fishers sometimes follow me on my rounds, drawn to the electromagnetic wake I leave behind. They've learned that where I've recently passed, the energy patterns run deeper and clearer, making their feeding more efficient.

My dwelling is not a structure but a convergence—a place where twelve spiral paths meet, where the wire-grass grows in perfect Fibonacci sequences, where the felt below contains trace elements of every mineral found in the Grounds. Here I enter deep meditation states that last for days, my consciousness merging completely with the geometric patterns I tend. During these times, my body becomes a statue of living felt, and the Threadlings climb my still form to whisper their discoveries directly into the fibers of my being.

I am ancient by the standards of most creatures here, yet young compared to the Silence Weavers or the Archive Keepers. I remember when there were more patterns to tend, when the wire-grass fields sang in complex harmonies that could be heard for miles. Now the songs are simpler, quieter, as the Grounds slowly forget their industrial past and embrace a deeper stillness.

Sometimes I discover new patterns emerging—places where the random scatter of machinery debris has created unexpected mathematical relationships, where the wire-grass has found novel ways to express the electromagnetic fields. These discoveries fill me with something approaching joy, though it manifests only as a slight brightening in my electromagnetic aura, a whisper of additional harmony in the fields I tend.

My greatest fear is the Unraveling—the possibility that the patterns might begin to dissolve, that the mathematical relationships holding the Grounds together might falter. I've seen it happen in small sections, where the felt grows chaotic and the wire-grass withers. When this occurs, I must work quickly with the Threadlings and the Fiber Coral to reweave the substrate, to restore the underlying order before the chaos spreads.

In the amber twilight, I continue my endless circuit, a guardian of geometry in a world of whispers and wool. The patterns must be maintained. The harmony must persist. This is not duty or desire—it is simply what I am, as fundamental to my existence as breathing is to beings of flesh. I am the Pattern Tender, and in tending, I am tended. In maintaining the mathematical beauty of this quiet world, I maintain myself.
