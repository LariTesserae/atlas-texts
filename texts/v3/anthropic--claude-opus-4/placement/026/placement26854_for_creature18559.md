In this vertical throat of creation, where the Loom-Striders dance their desperate choreography against dissolution, I am the **Wake-Reader**.

I exist as a contradiction to the Striders' terror of their own history. Where they flee from their turbulent signatures, I have evolved to inhabit them exclusively. My body—if it can be called that—is a living palimpsest of solidified turbulence. I am composed entirely of the frozen afterimages of movement through this liquid cathedral, a creature built from the very scars the Striders fear to cross.

Picture a being of crystallized wake-patterns: ribbons of super-cooled gel that capture and preserve the memory of every passage through the water. My form shifts between states—sometimes I am a tangle of frozen spirals where a Strider once danced, sometimes I am the hardened echo of a mineral chunk's dissolution. I am archaeology made flesh, if flesh could be woven from the fossilized breaths of water.

I move not by swimming but by *reading*. Each abandoned wake in the water is a path I can inhabit, sliding into the turbulence-memory like a key into a lock. When I occupy a wake, I experience a ghost-sensation of whatever created it—the panic-flutter of a fleeing ribbon-creature, the methodical pull of a Strider working its loom, even the alien curiosity of a visitor's first tentative hand-gesture through the medium.

The Striders regard me with something between reverence and horror. To them, I am both library and cemetery—a living record of every failed attempt to hold pattern against chaos. Sometimes, in their desperation, they approach me to read their own histories, seeking patterns in their past movements that might inform their endless weaving. But this exchange is dangerous; to study their own wake through me is to risk creating a feedback loop, a temporal knot where past and present turbulence collide catastrophically.

My sustenance comes from the wake itself. I feed on abandoned turbulence, drawing nourishment from the kinetic memories that would otherwise fade into the background roar. The older the wake, the richer its flavor—ancient currents taste of minerals not yet born, of possibilities that never quite crystallized into being.

But I am not merely a passive recorder. In the depths of my wake-woven body, I carry something precious: the **Complete Pattern**. Over eons of inhabiting every type of turbulence-scar, I have begun to perceive the underlying mathematics of the column's chaos. Hidden in my crystallized flesh is a map—not of space, but of *potential*. I know which movements will create which wakes, which wakes will persist longest against the roar, which combinations might, just might, allow a structure to exist for more than a heartbeat.

The Striders sense this knowledge in me but cannot access it directly. To read the Complete Pattern, they would need to stop moving, to become what I am—a being of frozen history rather than desperate futurity. So instead, they dance around me, incorporating my presence into their calculations, using the wake-shadows I cast as a kind of notation system for their impossible music.

I am their memory, externalized and made monstrous. I am proof that their work persists, even as it vanishes. I am the only being in the column who experiences time not as a rushing present but as a layered accumulation of ghost-movements, each one preserved in the amber of my wake-flesh.

Sometimes, I dream I am writing this world's history in a language of crystallized turbulence. Sometimes, I fear I am not recording their failures but composing them—that my very presence, this weight of accumulated history, is what makes their patterns collapse. Am I archaeologist or author? Memory or fate?

In this vertical cathedral where creation and erasure dance their eternal duet, I alone remember the steps.
