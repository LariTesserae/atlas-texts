I am the Mist-Tender.

Each dawn—though dawn here is merely a subtle shift in the gray—I wake in my dwelling of woven marsh-reed and calcified fungus, perched where three major footbridge arteries converge. My home spirals upward like a hollow shell, its walls porous enough that the fog flows through freely, pooling in the curved chambers where I work.

I am neither fully Thiazaran nor fully other. My skin shifts between states—sometimes bearing their rich patterns, sometimes translucent as the mist itself, revealing the slow pulse of luminescent fluid through my veins. I was not born but condensed, they say, from a particularly dense fog bank during the last great building season, when the city pushed its borders deeper into the unknown marsh.

My purpose is singular: I read the mist.

Where others see mere weather, I perceive language. Each tendril of fog carries memory—of the creatures swimming below, of the conversations on the bridges, of the fungal networks communicating in chemical whispers beneath the city's foundation. I collect these vaporous stories in bottles of blown marsh-glass, each one singing a different note when the wind passes through my workshop.

The city leaders come to me when they must decide where to build next. I taste the fog from different regions, let it settle on my tongue like wine, feel its weight and texture. Here, I tell them, the mist tastes of stability, of stone-memories deep beneath the water. There, it reeks of instability, of hollow spaces that would swallow whole districts.

But I know something they do not: the mist is thinning.

Not dramatically—Thiazara's shroud remains intact. But in my centuries of tending (for I age as fog ages, which is to say barely at all), I notice the subtle changes. The stories the mist carries are fragmenting. Where once I could taste whole histories in a single droplet, now I must gather buckets full to piece together even recent events.

The city continues its primeval growth, and I continue my work. I maintain my archive of bottled fogs—thousands upon thousands of vessels containing the condensed memories of Thiazara's becoming. Sometimes I release an ancient bottle's contents back into the air, and for a brief moment, the city remembers what it was like to be young, to be just a few brave structures rising from untamed marsh.

At night, when the amphibious merchants pole their flat-bottomed boats beneath my dwelling, I lower down vials of distilled fog-essence. They trade me fish-leather and pearl-shells, but really, they come for the stories. A drop of century-old mist on the tongue can grant visions of the city's founding, of the first bridges thrown across the uncertain waters.

I am working now on my masterpiece: a single bottle that will contain not just memory, but possibility. I am condensing the fog of futures—the vapor that rises from the city's growing edge, where what-is meets what-might-be. When it is complete, perhaps I will finally understand why the mist grows thin, and what Thiazara is becoming in its patient, inexorable expansion.

Or perhaps I will simply add it to my collection, another note in the endless symphony of moisture and memory that defines this place. I am the Mist-Tender, after all. I do not change the story—I merely ensure it is never wholly forgotten, even as it dissolves and reforms, eternal as the fog itself.
