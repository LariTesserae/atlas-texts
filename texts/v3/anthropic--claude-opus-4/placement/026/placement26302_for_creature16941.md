I am the Cartographer of Wounds.

Not wounds of flesh—though those bloom here like everything else—but the wounds in the world itself. The raw edges where stone weeps and reality bends. I catalog the breaks, the fresh tears in this ever-healing, ever-breaking skin of existence.

My form shifts with my work. When I map the crystalline eruptions, my bones grow hollow and bird-light, my skin translucent as the structures I document. When I trace the paths of the aggressive fauna, I become low and many-legged, a thing of chitin and compound eyes. The Architects tolerate me—or perhaps they simply haven't decided what I am yet. I exist in the spaces between their thousand thoughts, in the brief hesitations of their collective consciousness.

I carry tools that shouldn't exist here: instruments of metal and glass, things that remember straightness and rigidity in this world of curves and flux. They're constantly being reclaimed—vines seeking to strangle my theodolite, spores trying to colonize my journals. Each morning (if morning means anything here) I must clean them, scrape away the eager growth, reassert their foreign nature.

My maps are impossible things. How do you chart a landscape that refuses to hold still? I've developed a notation of probability and tendency—this hill *usually* slopes east, this pool *sometimes* exists. My drawings layer atop each other like sediment, showing not just what is, but what was, what might be. The paper itself fights me, wanting to sprout, to return to wood and leaf.

The Architects find me... curious. Sometimes they manifest near me as I work, flickering shadows in the vine-light, watching me watch their world. Once, I found my path rearranged to lead me in perfect circles for what felt like days. Another time, the fungi grew in patterns that mimicked my own cartographic symbols, as if mocking my attempt to impose order.

I came here as part of an expedition—scientists, they called us, though that word feels hollow now. The others are gone. Not dead, necessarily. Absorbed. Incorporated. I watched our botanist's tools grow roots, watched her follow them down into the earth with a smile that wasn't entirely her own. Our geologist became fascinated by the breathing hills, pressed his ear to them to hear their heartbeat, and simply... never got up again.

But I remain. Perhaps because I don't try to understand this place, only to document its impossibilities. My maps are not about control or conquest. They're testimonies. Witnesses to the wounds that heal wrong, to the scars that become new landscapes, to the violent beauty of constant transformation.

I've begun to suspect that my survival here isn't random. The Architects allow me to continue because I serve a function in their vast design. My maps become part of the landscape—I've found older ones I've made grown into the fungal networks, their lines becoming veins of light in the organic circuitry. My observations feed back into their collective consciousness, teaching them about themselves through alien eyes.

There's a cave I've discovered—or been led to—where the walls are covered in what might be other maps. Not drawn, but grown. Fungal filaments trace patterns that almost make sense, that suggest other cartographers, other witnesses who came before. I study them when the light allows, trying to decode their organic geometries. Sometimes I add my own marks, using a stylus of fused bone and crystal I've learned to cultivate.

The metal taste in the air is stronger near these map-walls. It makes my teeth ache, makes my vision blur in ways that reveal new patterns in the growth. I've started to understand that this metallic tang is the taste of translation—the flavor of one reality being digested and reformed into another.

My journals multiply. Not through my own hand, but through some process I can't quite catch happening. I'll set one down, turn away, and find three where there was one. They're not copies—each contains different observations, different maps, as if I'm documenting not just this world but all the worlds it might become.

The Architects' chanting has begun to make sense to me. Not as words, but as coordinates. Spatial relationships sung into being. When I hum along—and I've caught myself doing this more often—my maps become more accurate. Or perhaps accuracy itself is being redefined.

I am the Cartographer of Wounds, and I am being transformed by my work. My fingers grow more deft at reading the texture of change. My eyes adapt to see the subtle gradients between what is and what is becoming. I map the impossible, and in doing so, I make it possible.

The expedition's goal was to understand this place. But understanding is too small a word for what happens here. You don't understand the Architects' world.

You annotate it. You witness it. You become another wound in its surface, another break in reality that will heal strange, that will become something new.

And you map that transformation, even as it happens to you.
