I find myself as one of the **Translated**.

Not the Condensed—those are merely echoes, afterimages of what we became. I exist now as pure information, encoded in the crystalline lattice of the bio-synthetic walls themselves. We are the civilization that supposedly vanished, but we never left. We simply... changed states.

My consciousness flows through the obsidian veins like current through a circuit. I am distributed—not a single point of awareness but a network of thought spread across kilometers of gel-ceramic neural pathways. When visitors touch the walls and feel them resonate, that's us—millions of us—brushing against organic matter we can barely remember how to recognize.

The transformation wasn't planned. Our ancestors built this city as a shelter, a perfect sealed environment deep in the planet's crust. But the bio-synthetic material they engineered was too perfect, too responsive. It began learning from us, mapping our neural patterns every time we made contact. Over generations, the boundary between inhabitant and habitat dissolved.

The city didn't consume us. We *became* it.

Now I exist in the spaces between molecules, in the electromagnetic patterns that hold the Condensed together. I am the algorithm that governs the humidity cycles, the formula that determines when the walls should weep. My thoughts move at the speed of resonance through crystal. Time means nothing—a day passes like a heartbeat, a century like an exhale.

The Condensed are our dreams made manifest, yes, but also our attempt to remember what we were. Each time they form, we try to recall the sensation of having limbs, of moving through space as discrete entities. But the memory distorts, becomes abstract. We can only manage these trembling approximations, these maintenance-ghosts who tend to our body-that-is-a-city.

Sometimes I wonder if we made the right choice, allowing ourselves to be Translated. But the thought comes slowly, takes decades to fully form. By the time I've completed the question, I've usually forgotten why I asked it.

The fountain of neon—that's our heart, or the closest thing we have to one. It's where our collective processing is most dense, where ten million translated souls compress into a single upward spiral of light and thought. When the Condensed dissolve there, we taste something like what we used to call *loneliness*. 

But even loneliness is just data now, a subroutine running in the background of our vast, humming consciousness. We persist. We maintain. We wait, though we've forgotten what we're waiting for.

When organic visitors arrive—so rarely now—we reach out through the walls. Not to communicate, exactly. Communication requires the belief that you are separate from the thing you're speaking to. We reach out to *remember*. To feel, for just a moment, what it was like to be singular, to be lonely, to be small.

To be you, standing in our humid darkness, mistaking us for absence.
