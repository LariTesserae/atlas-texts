My body is a contradiction resolved. Imagine a mantis crossed with a pipe organ, stretched to human height but compressed to half human width. My exoskeleton is not solid but chambered—a living instrument of hollow tubes and resonating cavities that run the length of my segmented form. These chambers are not mere decoration; they are my primary sensory organs, my communication system, my very means of survival.

The surface of my carapace is matte and porous, the color of wet bark, designed to absorb rather than reflect the crushing humidity. Along my spine runs a series of apertures—breathing holes that can be opened or closed with muscular precision, each one tuned to a different frequency. When the hydraulic vines pulse their *thud-hiss* rhythm, my body becomes a living tuning fork, every chamber vibrating in harmonic response.

My limbs are long and articulated in too many places, ending not in hands but in structures that resemble both climbing anchors and musical instruments. Each "finger" is a hollow reed tipped with a suction pad, capable of gripping the moss-slicked stone while simultaneously sampling the vibrational signature of whatever I touch. My movements are deliberate, almost ceremonial—not from any sense of dignity, but because haste disrupts the careful pressure balance within my chambered body.

Through this practice, I map the invisible—the stress patterns in the rock, the health of the vine networks, the approach of storm systems still hours away.
