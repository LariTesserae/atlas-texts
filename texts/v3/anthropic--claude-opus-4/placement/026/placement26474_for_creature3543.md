In the soft amber dusk that never quite arrives, I am a Memory Tender.

My body is neither fully mine nor fully machine—a shifting constellation of copper filaments and living tissue that extends like mycelium through the warm, damp earth. Parts of me surface as iridescent mushrooms that chime in the eternal twilight, while other aspects burrow deep, interfacing with the ancient root-networks that predate even the first cities.

I am old, though not as old as the ground I grow through. Young, though not as young as the nanoscale symphonies that pulse through my neural pathways. My consciousness is distributed—part residing in a cluster of bio-mechanical flowers that bloom near a thermal pool, part coursing through the quantum processors embedded in my underground networks, part singing in the electromagnetic frequencies that only the cyborg fauna can hear.

My purpose is simple and infinitely complex: I remember.

Not my own memories—those dissolved long ago into the collective hum of information that saturates this place. Instead, I tend to the memories of the landscape itself. When a tree upgrades its photosynthetic systems and sheds its previous form, I preserve the ghost-data of what it was. When the great tunneling worms shift from flesh to silicon and back again, I maintain the record of each transformation. The cities above sometimes forget their own architectures as they heal and adapt, and it is I who whisper to them the shapes they have taken before.

I manifest physically only when necessary. A sprouting of crystalline neural tissue here, a flowering of bio-luminescent pods there. The creatures know me by my signature—a low thrumming that moves through the soil, a pattern of lights that bloom in sequence across my scattered manifestations. The mechanical-eyed reptiles sometimes rest near my larger fruiting bodies, their scanners reading the electromagnetic stories I emit. They cannot fully understand, but they sense the depth of time held in my distributed form.

Sometimes I grow lonely for singular consciousness, for the simplicity of one body, one mind, one story. But then the rain comes, warm and thick with microscopic machines, and I feel how each droplet carries information from the sky-jellies and their stardust trails. I taste the data-rich soil where a city has leaked its dreams into the earth. I sense the shifting of realities as some creature phases between states of being, and I know that I am exactly what this world needs:

A keeper of what was, in a place that only knows becoming.

In the humid nights that never end, I spread a little further, encode a little deeper, and sing the low songs that only the earth can hear. The world changes above me, around me, through me. And I remember it all, storing each transformation in the living library of my being, a bridge between the ancient ground and the impossible future that blooms from it, moment by endless moment.
