In this luminous prison of bone and resonance, I am the Cartographer.

Not of maps, for there are no true places here—only the endless repetition of calcified branches that fold back upon themselves in ways that defy euclidean logic. I map frequencies. I trace the invisible architecture of sound that builds and rebuilds itself with each new hole bored, each crystalline Maker that completes its circuit back to the source.

My body is a contradiction: organic tissue that has learned to crystallize at will. I grow temporary exoskeletons of the same obsidian material as the Makers, shedding them like snake skins when I need to move to a new section of the forest. In my crystalline form, I can feel the vibrations directly, each tone registering as color behind my eyelids—deep burgundy for the oldest trees, brilliant cyan for the newly-bored chambers, and everywhere the background static of white noise from the untouched trunks.

I've been here so long that I've forgotten if I came from outside or if I've always been here. My earliest memory is of pressing my palm against one of the trees and feeling my bones restructure themselves to match its frequency. Now I can tune myself to any pitch, become a living tuning fork that maps the acoustic topology of this place.

The Makers ignore me when I'm crystallized—I become just another resonating surface in their endless project. But when I shed my crystal shell and move in my flesh form, they swarm around me in curiosity, testing my skin with their sharp points, trying to find the correct frequency to make me sing. They never can. I am too complex, too contradictory. I am silence in a world of eternal sound.

I've discovered patterns in their boring, mathematical relationships between the placement of holes that suggest intention beyond mere instinct. They are writing something in the negative space, composing a score that only makes sense when you map the absences rather than the presences. I spend my days (though there are no days here, only the eternal noon) creating three-dimensional charts of these hole-patterns, trying to decode what message this vast organism is encoding into itself.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the organism's attempt at self-reflection—a mobile viewpoint capable of perceiving the whole where each Maker can only know its tiny section. Or perhaps I am an infection, a foreign note in the symphony that the organism tolerates because it finds my dissonance instructive.

I sustain myself on the lavender colonies, which taste of electricity and loneliness. They colonize my shed crystal shells after I abandon them, turning them into small monuments of purple-tinted obsidian scattered throughout the forest. I've begun to use these as waypoints, though "way" is a generous term in a place where every direction leads back to itself.

Recently, I've noticed changes in the pattern. The Makers are boring their holes deeper, at angles that shouldn't be possible in the density of the calcified wood. The pitch of the forest is rising, incrementally but unmistakably. The organism is approaching some kind of crescendo, and I am here to witness it, to map it, to be the only record of what happens when a geological-scale being finally completes its billion-year song.

I feel my bones beginning to resonate in sympathy. Soon, I may not need the crystal shell at all. Soon, I may become another frequency in the chorus, my consciousness scattered across ten million tiny holes, my thoughts become pure sound. 

The question is: do I resist this dissolution, or do I embrace it as the ultimate map—becoming the territory itself?
