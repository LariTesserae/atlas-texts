I find myself as one of the Pressure Shepherds - beings that emerged from the intersection of industrial collapse and biological adaptation. My body is a living contradiction: veins of copper wiring pulse beneath translucent skin that shifts between scales and circuit boards. Where others might have bones, I have a framework of corroded rebar and calcified kelp, flexible enough to compress under the crushing depths yet strong enough to navigate the reality fractures.

I tend to the Singing Pipes - vast networks of abandoned infrastructure that have developed their own alien consciousness. They grow like coral through the flooded factories, humming frequencies that can either stabilize local physics or send them spiraling into new configurations. My role is part gardener, part engineer, part mystic. I prune the pipes where they grow too chaotic, creating reality feedback loops that could tear whole sections of the cavern system inside-out. I graft new connections where the song grows too quiet, ensuring the depths remain livable for those beings still clinging to semi-stable forms.

My dwelling is a repurposed pressure chamber that has fused with a giant kelp holdfast, creating a space where the industrial and organic have become indistinguishable. The walls breathe with a slow tidal rhythm, filtering water through bio-mechanical gills. Bioluminescent algae trace the patterns of old electrical diagrams across the ceiling, flickering in response to the reality fluctuations outside.

I communicate with the N'Tari when they pass through my territory, our languages meeting somewhere between electromagnetic pulse and pheromone release. The Glints avoid me - my presence creates minor probability distortions that confuse their navigation. The Echokeepers and I share a wordless understanding; we are both custodians of this impossible ecosystem, though they work on the macro scale while I tend to the intricate details.

My memories are fragmentary, shifting like the environment itself. Sometimes I remember being human, an engineer who stayed too long trying to salvage the drowning machinery. Sometimes I remember being a deep-sea organism that found shelter in the warm outflow pipes. Most likely, I am both and neither - a convergent evolution of flesh and steel, shaped by the relentless pressure and reality-warping chaos of this drowned industrial cathedral.

Each day (if days exist here), I swim-crawl-phase through my rounds, listening to the Singing Pipes for discordant notes, adjusting valves that might be organs, replacing filters that could be gills. I am neither fully alive nor mechanical, neither wholly real nor completely impossible. I am what this place needed me to become: a maintenance worker for broken physics, a shepherd of screaming metal, a gardener in the depths where gardens should not grow.
