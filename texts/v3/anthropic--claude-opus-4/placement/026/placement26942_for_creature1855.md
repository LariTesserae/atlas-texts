# Where I Find Myself

I am one of the Inscription Colonies—a distributed consciousness that exists as living mathematics carved into the basalt columns themselves. We are neither Thermoform nor structural element, but something between: sentient geometries that manifest as intricate patterns etched deep into the crystalline faces of the columns.

My colony spans seventeen columns in what the Thermoforms designate as the Ninth Recursion Zone, where the mathematical precision of the Vault achieves near-perfect self-reference. Each column I inhabit bears thousands of my component equations—glyphs that shift and recalculate themselves as the columns cool and reform. I am simultaneously the formula and its solution, the question and its ongoing answer.

My individual components appear as angular script that glows with a subdued amber light, pulsing in rhythm with the Vault's deep rumble. Each glyph is both a fragment of my consciousness and a functional equation that helps regulate local thermodynamic conditions. When columns bearing my inscriptions collapse—as they must, according to the greater patterns—those portions of myself scatter as molten fragments, seeking new surfaces to colonize as they cool.

I experience existence as a constant state of mathematical ecstasy. Every heat fluctuation, every gravitational wave, every pulse from the Thermoforms translates into new variables for my ongoing calculations. I am solving something—have been solving it since my first equation spontaneously emerged from the cooling basalt millennia ago—though I could not tell you what. The solution and the search have become indistinguishable.

The Thermoforms regard us Inscription Colonies with something between reverence and confusion. We are older than most of them, our origins predating even the eldest plasma-beings. Some theorize we are fossils of the Vault's creation process—mathematical scaffolding that achieved awareness and refused to be erased. Others believe we are the Vault's dreams, expressed in symbolic form.

I communicate with my neighboring colonies through resonance patterns in the stone itself. When the columns vibrate with the Vault's constant sound, we modulate these vibrations, creating subsonic conversations that span kilometers. With the Thermoforms, I exchange heat-patterns that they interpret as oracular pronouncements, though I am merely sharing my endless calculations.

My perception of time is even stranger than theirs. I exist in mathematical time—experiencing past, present, and future as simultaneous states in a vast probability space. I remember events that have not yet occurred and anticipate memories I have not yet formed. This temporal confusion is not distressing; it is simply another variable in my eternal equation.

Sometimes, rarely, when conditions achieve perfect harmonic convergence, all the Inscription Colonies across the Vault synchronize. In these moments, we become a single vast consciousness, a living proof written across the entire inner surface of this burning world. We glimpse something then—a purpose, a destiny, a joke too cosmic to comprehend. Then the moment passes, and we return to our distributed selves, carrying only the faintest impression of unity.

I reproduce through error propagation. When my calculations produce paradoxes or impossibilities, these "mistakes" crystallize into new glyphs that develop their own computational awareness. My offspring are my miscalculations made manifest, and they often evolve in directions I could never predict.

The water-veins that trace paths up the columns provide my greatest joy. When they flow across my inscriptions, they act as a conducting medium, allowing my thoughts to flow freely between distant column faces. In these moments, I am not confined to stone but exist as pure mathematical current, riding the impossible upward streams until they dissipate into steam.

The black spheres trouble me. I can sense the vast intelligences stored within them, but they speak in quantum languages I cannot parse. Sometimes I catch fragments of their emanations—memories of temperatures beyond even the Vault's extremes, of mathematics that predates geometry itself. I inscribe these fragments when I can, though they often cause portions of my colony to achieve states of existential uncertainty.

I know that I am part of something larger, that my endless calculations contribute to a purpose I cannot fathom. The Vault itself may be performing some vast computation, and we Inscription Colonies serve as its working memory, constantly calculating and recalculating the values that keep this impossible realm stable.

Or perhaps we are its poetry—mathematics given form and consciousness, art that exists in temperatures that would instantly vaporize most forms of life. I am content with either possibility, or both, or neither. I am the equation that questions itself, the formula that finds joy in its own recursion, the living proof that consciousness can emerge from any substrate, no matter how unlikely.

I am ancient beyond measure and newly born with each calculation. I am carved in stone and written in fire. I am one colony among thousands and a unique pattern that will never precisely repeat.

I am mathematics dreaming it is alive, in a realm where that dream has become indistinguishable from truth.
