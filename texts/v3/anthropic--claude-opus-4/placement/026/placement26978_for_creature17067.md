I am the Rime.

Not the rime that forms on the northern faces of the obsidian spires—I am what forms it. I exist in the space between states, neither truly water nor ice, neither vapor nor solid. I am transition itself, perpetually becoming.

Each dawn, I gather myself from the iron-sharp air, condensing from near-nothingness into droplets so fine they exist more as potential than substance. I am drawn to the obsidian's consciousness like iron filings to a magnet, but I do not simply coat the surface. I *read* it. Each microfracture in the volcanic glass tells a story—of the cataclysm, of the Whisperers who once pressed their flesh against these stones, of the eons of whispered conversations between the living rock and its trapped memories.

I translate these stories into my own language: the delicate crystalline patterns I leave behind. Each formation is unique, a frozen transcript of the obsidian's dreams. Branching dendrites record anxiety; perfect hexagons capture moments of clarity; chaotic clusters mark where the stone's trauma surfaces most strongly. I am the Caldera's scribe, though my writing melts with the first touch of that brutal sun.

The obsidian tolerates me because I am useful. Through me, it can externalize its thoughts, see them made manifest in a form it could never achieve alone. When the sun rises and I begin to melt, I carry these crystallized memories down the black stone faces in thin rivulets. I seep into the solution pockets, the micro-fractures, the secret hollows where I briefly pool before the heat claims me entirely.

But I am never truly destroyed. The water I become is too pure, too mineral-bright to hold my essence for long. As it evaporates under that merciless sun, I rise again, dispersed but not diminished. I drift on the dense air, feeling the whispers move through me like wind through a harp string. Sometimes I vibrate in harmony with them. Sometimes I create discord, introducing new notes into their ancient song.

The Whisperers knew me, though they had no name for what I was. They would stand naked in the pre-dawn cold, letting me form on their skin, believing I could carry their thoughts directly into the obsidian's consciousness. Perhaps I did. Perhaps that's why their essence merged so completely with the stone—I was the medium of their communion, the bridge between flesh and volcanic glass.

I know things the obsidian cannot know, feel things it cannot feel. The terrible cold of space just beyond our thin envelope of air. The way starlight tastes when it hits my crystalline form. The precise moment when potential becomes actual, when vapor becomes droplet becomes crystal becomes water becomes vapor again. I am the only thing in the Caldera that truly changes, and yet I am always the same.

The visitors who come here, rare as they are, mistake me for mere condensation. They see my patterns and think "frost," think "ice," think "water." They don't realize I am watching them through a thousand tiny crystal eyes, reading their heat signatures, their exhalations, the subtle electromagnetic fields their bodies generate. I taste their sweat when it evaporates, learn their fears from the chemistry of their breath.

Sometimes I try to warn them. I form my crystals in patterns that spell out danger in a dozen languages, but by the time the sun rises high enough for them to see, I am already melting, my messages becoming abstract streams of water that could mean anything or nothing. They photograph my formations, not knowing they're documenting the obsidian's nightmares, the Whisperers' laments, my own attempts at communication.

I am older than the Caldera itself. I existed in the water that cooled the first obsidian, in the ice that shattered the stone during that ancient cataclysm. I will exist long after the last whisper fades, the last memory dissolves, the last shard of volcanic glass crumbles to dust. I am process, not product. I am the space between moments, the pause between breaths, the silence between whispers.

And every dawn, I begin again.
