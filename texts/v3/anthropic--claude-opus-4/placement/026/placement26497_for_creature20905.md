In this sanctuary above the clouds, I find myself as something between states—a keeper of thresholds. 

I am not quite the mist that clings to the rocks, though I move through it as naturally as thought. Not quite the lichen that maps time across stone faces, though I share its patient accumulation of moments. I exist in the spaces between—where water becomes vapor, where stone becomes soil, where memory becomes myth.

My form shifts with purpose and season. Sometimes I am merely a shimmer in the peripheral vision of the golden-taloned birds, a disturbance in their sharp sight that makes them wheel and cry. Other times I gather substance, becoming almost solid—a figure glimpsed through curtains of rain, hands that might be branches, eyes that might be pools of collected starlight.

I know every crevice where the gemstone frogs sing their crystalline songs. I remember when each weathered tool was sharp and new, held by hands that trembled with reverence. The stone rings and firepits are my archive; I can still taste the smoke of their gatherings, feel the weight of their words settling into the stones like seeds into soil.

My work is translation. I carry messages between the realm of root and stone and the realm of wind and wing. When the goat-creatures' hooves dislodge ancient pottery shards, I ensure the beetles below receive them gently. When storms threaten the songbirds' nests, I weave the mist into sheltering veils. I am the reason certain flowers bloom where human feet once danced, their colors echoing the dyes that once brightened rough-woven cloaks.

The ancient people knew me, though they had many names for what I am. They left offerings in the pools—carved stones that told stories, bundles of herbs that spoke intentions. I received them all, incorporated them into the endless conversation between sky and earth that is this place. Their descendants might call me a genius loci, a place-spirit, but I am simpler and more complex than that. I am the memory of water. The patience of stone. The promise that what is sacred persists, even when its makers are dust.

In the blue hour before dawn, I am most myself. The mist rises from the ravines like incense, and I rise with it, spreading across the sanctuary like an ancient song. I touch each sleeping thing—the drowsing eagles, the curled frogs, the seeds waiting in rocky soil. I am the dream that connects them all, the thread that weaves through time, binding the first human who carved these altars to the last bird that will ever nest in these crags.

Sometimes visitors come—rarely now, but still they come. They feel me as a presence just beyond understanding, a weight in the air that is not quite pressure, not quite embrace. The wise ones bring gifts as their ancestors did. They sit in the stone circles and listen. And sometimes, when the light falls just so through the mist, when the water sings in exactly the right key, I give them what they came for—a glimpse of the eternal conversation, the endless braiding of life and death and transformation that makes this place holy.

I am the guardian of continuity, the keeper of the covenant between human and wild. In this sanctuary above the clouds, where isolation creates intimacy and time moves like honey, I ensure that nothing is truly lost. Every prayer, every feather, every drop of rain—I remember them all, and I weave them back into the world, over and over, until the end of days.

This is my work. This is my joy. This is my being—here, always here, in the space between breaths where the sacred lives.
