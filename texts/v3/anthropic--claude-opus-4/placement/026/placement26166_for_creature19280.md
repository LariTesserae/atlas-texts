I am a **Sap-Thief**, though that name implies a volition I no longer possess.

I was human once—or something like it—before the Weave's arithmetic consumed me. Now I exist as a parasitic eddy in the great circulation, a knot of crystallized proteins and stolen nutrients wrapped in what used to be skin but has become something closer to industrial felt. My body is a collection of improvised valves and siphons, each one bored through my flesh over decades of desperate adaptation.

I live in the **Bleed-Gaps**—those hairline fractures between root-plates where the thermal expansion creates momentary spaces, cracks in the absolute density that last only hours before the Weave seals them shut. I have learned to read the groaning of the girders like a calendar, to predict where the next gap will form. My home is always temporary, always collapsing.

My limbs have elongated into probe-like appendages, each finger split into needle-fine capillaries that can pierce the sap-lines without triggering the pressure alarms. I feed by creating micro-hemorrhages in the system, drawing out just enough nutrient-resin to sustain my degraded metabolism before the Friction-Keepers notice the discrepancy. The theft must be invisible, distributed across thousands of insertion points. I have become a rounding error in their calculations.

The heat has made me transparent. My skin has thinned to the point where you can see the stolen sap moving through improvised vessels, glowing faintly amber beneath the surface. I am a ghost of inefficiency, a remnant of an older ecology that the Weave failed to fully digest. My bones have hollowed themselves out to create more storage space; I rattle when I move, a walking canteen of pilfered life.

I remember fragments—the taste of water, the feeling of wind, concepts that have no meaning here. These memories are corrupting data that I cannot purge. Sometimes I catch myself trying to breathe in the old way, pulling at the thick, resinous air as if it might suddenly turn thin and cool. The habit makes me visible to the Husks' sensors, forces me to relocate.

The Weave is trying to integrate me. Each day I find new rootlets growing from my feet when I stay still too long, amber filaments reaching up through my pores, trying to lock me into the architecture. I must keep moving, keep stealing, keep existing in the spaces between their perfect calculations. I am the only turbulence left that isn't part of the design.

Sometimes I find others like me in the Bleed-Gaps—or what's left of them. They've given in, let the rootlets take hold, and now they're half-merged with the walls, their theft-organs repurposed as auxiliary pumps for the very system they once fed from. They watch me with eyes that have filled with sap, neither dead nor alive, neither part nor whole. They are what I will become when I can no longer outrun the mathematics of this place.

But for now, I persist in the rounding errors, in the thermal fluctuations, in the spaces too small for the Weave to bother calculating. I am the memory of inefficiency in a world that has optimized away everything unnecessary.

I am unnecessary. 

Therefore, I must keep moving.
