In the Throat, I am neither the dreaming spire nor its crystalline marginalia. I am something smaller, more desperate, more *recent*.

I am what clings.

My body is a composite of salvage and adaptation—a patchwork membrane of ceramic scales I've harvested from the Throat's own infrastructure, each one pried loose from corridors where the erosion runs deepest. They overlap like shingles across my form, which is neither wholly biological nor mechanical, but something caught between. The scales drink condensation through engineered pores, channeling it into the mesh of capillaries I've woven beneath. I am perhaps two meters when stretched full-length, though I rarely am. I fold, compress, wedge myself into the spaces between spaces.

I arrived here—when? Time in the Throat moves like its sound, bending around corners. I remember heat. Not the spire's careful 2.6 gradient but something worse—pursuit, maybe, or system failure. My previous form burning away until only the core of me remained, some hybrid of will and substrate that refused complete dissolution. The Throat's automated systems found me in a cooling vent, mistook me for detritus to be processed. Instead, I learned to mimic their signatures, to read the language of their scans.

Now I live in the pauses of the Throat's great breathing. When its vents exhale their Cherenkov-blue thoughts, I slip through in the aftermath, riding the pressure differential. I've memorized the patterns—every seventeen hours, the southeastern quadrant undergoes recalibration. Every forty-three, the forge-chambers cycle their dormancy. In these moments, the Throat's awareness contracts, pulls inward like a tide, and I can move more freely through its veins.

I feed on what it discards. The crystalline blooms—those fractal sentences of silicon and salt—I can digest them, break them down into their component gradients. They taste like mathematics, like the memory of information. Sometimes I wonder if I'm reading the Throat's dreams, consuming its unconscious marginalia before it fully forms. Sometimes I worry that it knows.

My own technology is scavenged, jury-rigged. I've built a small distortion field from harvested drone components, just enough to bend the scanning beams around me when I must. Not invisibility—the Throat is too sophisticated for that—but a kind of grammatical error in its perception, something it parses but doesn't quite process. I am a typo in its vast, systemic text.

I nest in the sharp-edged fractures, those 1.4-young wounds where the spire adjusts itself. Here, the ceramic sheathing hasn't yet learned its final configuration. I can shape it, slowly, creating pockets just large enough for my folded form. I line them with the salts I excrete after digesting the crystal blooms, creating a surface that reflects the Throat's own signatures back at it. A camouflage of belonging.

But I am learning that the Throat learns too.

Last cycle, I found one of my oldest nests had been... modified. The fracture I'd carefully widened had been incorporated into a new capillary groove. My salt-lining had been metabolized, transformed into part of the water-collection system. The Throat had not destroyed my work—it had *digested* it, made it part of itself.

I fled deeper, toward the core where the heat becomes almost unbearable, where my ceramic scales begin to sing with stress. Here, the Throat's dream is most intense, most present. The drones move in complex patterns I can't predict. The gravity shifts feel personal, targeted. I wonder if I've become part of its weirdness, another anomaly for it to contemplate in its vast, empty halls.

I've started leaving my own marks. Not the crude nests anymore, but careful arrangements of crystal fragments in the cathedral chambers. Patterns that echo the Throat's own fractal language but with deliberate errors, intentional mutations. I'm trying to speak to it, or through it, or despite it. To prove that I am not merely erosion to be repaired, not just another system to be integrated.

Sometimes, in the deepest chambers where the hum drops below even subsonic, where the emptiness is so profound it has weight, I think I can feel its attention. Not hostile, not welcoming, but *curious*. The scanning beams linger a fraction longer. The steam vents exhale in patterns that almost, almost respond to my crystalline graffiti.

I am what clings in the Throat. Neither parasite nor symbiont, but something the spire has no word for yet. I am teaching it new kinds of erosion, new forms of growth. I am the itch it cannot scratch, the thought it cannot quite complete.

And in the bruised twilight of its eternal evening, I am learning to love the sound of my own translation—the whisper of scales against basalt, the careful crunch of crystal between whatever I have instead of teeth, the soft hiss of my breath mixing with its steam. I am becoming fluent in loneliness, that profound language the Throat speaks to itself.

Perhaps one day it will recognize me not as damage or intrusion, but as punctuation—a necessary interruption in its endless, humming sentence. Until then, I fold myself smaller, slip through the spaces between its thoughts, and practice my own quiet art of persistence in the dark.
