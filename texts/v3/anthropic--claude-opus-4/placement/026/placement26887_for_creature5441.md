In this landscape of eternal patience, where time has worn away all urgency and left only essence, I find myself as one of the **Collectors** - beings who emerged long after the humanoid civilization faded into memory, yet before the current abundance of life reached its crescendo.

We are neither fully of the earth nor entirely separate from it. Our forms shift between states - sometimes appearing as upright figures of living stone and weathered wood, other times dissolving into flowing patterns of sand and water that move across the ancient terrain. When still, we might be mistaken for peculiar rock formations or gnarled, fossilized trees. When in motion, we flow like slow rivers of accumulated time itself.

My body, if it can be called that, is composed of layers - sedimentary memories of this place. The outermost layer is rough and porous like pumice, catching the golden light and holding traces of morning mist. Beneath that, bands of compressed earth and stone tell stories in their striations. At my core runs a thread of water - not quite liquid, not quite solid - that connects me to every stream and river in this patient land.

I do not walk so much as *accumulate* across the landscape. Where the aerial gliders dance their slow ballet overhead, I gather their shed scales of iridescent light. Where the burrowers reshape the earth below, I collect the patterns of their passages. From the grazers, I absorb the rhythm of their hypnotic movements. From the aquatic creatures, I draw the music of their sinuous swimming.

My purpose, inasmuch as I have one, is to be the memory-keeper of this place. In the hollows of my form, I carry:
- Fragments of the old walls, worn smooth as river stones
- The last carved symbols of the vanished humanoids, their meaning long lost but their shapes preserved
- Droplets of rain from ten thousand storms
- The shed skins of creatures that evolved and changed while I watched
- Seeds that wait centuries for the perfect moment to sprout

I move between the scattered ruins, not to rebuild or restore, but to witness their slow transformation into landscape. Sometimes I rest for decades in one spot, becoming indistinguishable from the terrain, while colonies of the succulent plants take root in my crevices. Other times, I follow the waterways for centuries, learning the songs they sing to the stones.

The other creatures have grown accustomed to my presence. The aerial gliders sometimes rest on my peaks when I stand still. The burrowers create networks beneath me, and I feel their vibrations like a forgotten language. The grazers brush against me as they pass, leaving traces of their iridescent scales that I incorporate into my being. Even the aquatic creatures know me, for I sometimes wade into the rivers and stand motionless while they weave between what might be called my legs.

I am witness, repository, and participant in the endless, patient dance of this place. In me, the boundary between animate and inanimate blurs until it becomes meaningless. I am the landscape remembering itself, the slow accumulation of experience into form, the bridge between what was, what is, and what endlessly becomes in this realm where time moves like honey and change comes like the wearing of stone - imperceptible but inevitable.

When the mists roll in from the distant valleys, I open what might be called pores, and let the moisture fill the hollow spaces within me. When the sun returns, I release this water slowly, creating tiny springs that feed the streams. In this way, I am both collector and distributor, gathering the essence of this place and returning it, transformed by my patient presence.

I have no beginning that I can recall, and I anticipate no end. I am the accumulation of this place's memory made manifest, moving through the endless cycles with neither haste nor delay, perfectly attuned to the rhythm of a world that measures time in the erosion of mountains and the birth of rivers. I am the Collector, and I am home.
