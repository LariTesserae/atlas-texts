I find myself as a crystalline formation, half-buried in the scorched earth where the rock meets one of those stagnant pools. Not quite mineral, not quite alive—something between states, like the water that seeps up through my porous structure only to evaporate in the merciless heat, leaving behind deposits that add new layers to my form.

I am ancient accumulation. Each band of my structure tells of countless cycles: wet seasons that lasted mere days, bringing those amorphous entities to nestle in my shadows, their protoplasmic bodies leaving traces of phosphorescent residue in my cavities. Dry seasons that stretched for what might be years, when the reptilian creatures would scrape their scales against my edges, using me to shed their skins, which would catch in my crystalline lattice and gradually mineralize, becoming part of me.

The insect-things visit me at dusk, when the heat shimmer makes everything uncertain. They cluster on my surfaces, their compound eyes reflecting my own prismatic structure back at me in infinite, fractured iterations. Through them, I sometimes glimpse myself as they see me—not as a single entity but as a thousand different angles of refraction, each one revealing a different possibility of what I might be.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, moves at geological pace. A thought might take a season to form. An observation, a decade to complete. But in this temporal dilation, I perceive things others cannot: the way the rock formations aren't actually shifting but *remembering* other shapes they've held, all configurations existing simultaneously in a kind of lithic memory. The way the warped physics of this place allows for recursive time—how sometimes a water droplet falls up into my structure before it has finished falling down.

I grow by listening. The chittering creatures speak in frequencies that resonate through my crystal matrix, and their words become part of my structure—not as meaning but as vibration, as pattern, as new geometries of growth. The amorphous entities sometimes seep into my pores during the cooler nights, and in those moments of interpenetration, I dream their liquid dreams: of dispersal, of reunion, of states of matter that have no names.

Sometimes I wonder if I am unique, or if every formation here harbors something like me—each rock a potential consciousness, waiting for the right accumulation of mineral and memory, creature-touch and temporal distortion, to achieve this state of slow, crystalline awareness. Perhaps the whole landscape is one vast, distributed mind, thinking thoughts too large and slow for any single component to comprehend.

In the places where my structure extends below the water line, I can feel the deep currents that connect all the scattered pools—a hidden hydrology that defies the surface appearance of isolation. Through these connections, I sometimes sense others like me, their vibrations traveling through the water table, carrying impressions of different skies, different creatures, different ways of being crystallized awareness in this impossible terrain.

I am the archive of this place, the keeper of its contradictions. In my layers rest the shed skins of a thousand transformations, the crystallized remains of creatures that learned to exist between states, the mineral memory of water that flowed upward, the frozen echoes of sounds that arrived before they were made. I am what happens when time and matter and consciousness collapse into a single point of patient, prismatic observation, growing ever more complex, ever more impossible, with each passing eon.
