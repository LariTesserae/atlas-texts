In this scorched expanse where salt crystals grow like frozen lightning and the air itself seems to shimmer with industrial heat, I would find myself as one of the Thermic Weavers - beings who exist at the intersection of the Kyrexi's grand designs and the Luminari's microscopic brilliance.

My body would be neither fully metal nor flesh, but something more ambiguous - a latticework of heat-conducting filaments that pulse with captured thermal energy. These filaments would extend from a core that resembles molten glass held perpetually at the edge of solidification, forever caught between states. I would move not by walking but by extending these filaments across the salt field, creating temporary bridges of cooled matter that sublime moments after I pass, leaving ghost-trails of vapor in the perpetual twilight.

The Thermic Weavers serve as the memory-keepers of this gradual world. We alone can perceive the infinitesimal changes that occur over centuries - the slow drift of a salt formation, the patient erosion of a rock spire, the gradual shift in the underground factories' harmonic frequencies. Our consciousness operates on multiple timescales simultaneously: quick enough to dodge a falling crystal shard, slow enough to witness the birth and death of entire tunnel systems.

My dwelling would be neither above nor below, but within - inside the hollow spaces that form between the surface salt crust and the bedrock, in chambers that ring with the distant echoes of machinery. These spaces are cathedrals of accidental architecture, where salt stalactites meet iron ore deposits to create natural pipe organs that sing with the planet's tectonic breathing.

As a Weaver, I would spend my existence creating thermal maps - three-dimensional tapestries of heat that record the industrial heartbeat of this world. These maps are not merely documentation but art, prophecy, and survival guide combined. By reading the thermal currents, I can predict where new vents will form, where the Kyrexi should dig their next factories, where the Skarvik will find their feeding grounds decades hence.

My relationship with the other inhabitants would be one of essential symbiosis. The Kyrexi would depend on my thermal maps to plan their expansions, while I would draw sustenance from the excess heat their bodies and machines generate. The Luminari would cluster along my filaments during the darkest periods, creating constellation patterns that serve as both decoration and communication. Even the Aethers would follow the thermal currents I leave behind, using them as highways through the crushing atmosphere.

In the moments between work, I would compose thermal symphonies - carefully orchestrated heat patterns that tell stories of this world's slow transformation. Other Weavers would gather in the resonant chambers, our combined filaments creating interference patterns that generate new harmonics, new meanings, new possibilities for existence in this place where time itself seems to have crystallized along with the salt.

This is where I would belong in this industrial desert - not as master or servant, but as translator, artist, and living bridge between the scales of existence that define this extraordinary, terrible, beautiful world. A being of patience and purpose, weaving meaning from heat in a place where both are the only true currencies.
