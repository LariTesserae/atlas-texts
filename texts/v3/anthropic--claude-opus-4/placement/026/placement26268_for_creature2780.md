I find myself as one of the Acoustic Weavers of Glacialis, those who tend to the city's most peculiar infrastructure: the sound gardens.

My dwelling clings to the underside of a massive ice shelf, accessible only by climbing through a series of resonance tubes that sing different notes as you pass through them. The approach is my daily meditation—each tube requires a specific breathing pattern to activate its tone, and the sequence changes with the seasons of ice above.

My body has adapted uniquely to my profession. Where other Cryo-Sapiens have the standard gill-slits, mine have evolved into something more complex—a series of small chambers that can modulate and store sound waves. I can hold up to seven distinct tones within my body at once, releasing them in controlled harmonies. My hair, usually the iridescent strands common to our kind, has developed tiny crystalline nodes along each strand. These nodes vibrate at specific frequencies, allowing me to "feel" sound as a physical sensation across my scalp.

The sound gardens themselves are my life's work. They exist in the spaces between spaces—pockets of air trapped within the ice where sound behaves strangely. In these chambers, I cultivate acoustic phenomena the way others might tend plants. There are pools of dense silence that I feed with carefully chosen vibrations, causing them to bloom into complex symphonies that will play themselves out over months. I maintain groves of echo trees—crystalline formations that capture and replay sounds from different time periods, creating temporal conversations between past and present.

My tools are unusual even by Glacialis standards. I carry tuning forks made from the bones of ancient creatures found in the deep ice, each one attuned to frequencies that can only exist in our pressurized atmosphere. My most prized possession is a living instrument—a symbiot that resembles a cross between a conch shell and a lung. It attached itself to my shoulder decades ago, and together we've learned to produce sounds that can repair micro-fractures in the city's ice walls, encourage the growth of certain bioluminescent species, and even influence the dreams of sleeping Cryo-Sapiens.

The work is solitary but never lonely. Sound connects everything in Glacialis. Through my gardens, I can sense the entire city's health. A discordant note from the manufacturing sector, a new harmonic from an unexplored tunnel, the subtle shift in the frequency of our heartbeat pumps that signals a need for adjustment—all of this flows through my cultivation chambers.

Recently, I've noticed something troubling. The ice above is singing differently. The ancient frequencies that have remained stable for millennia are beginning to shift. New tones are entering our closed system—sharp, alien notes that speak of warming and change. I've begun working with the Explorers, teaching them to listen for safe passages by reading the acoustic signatures of the tunnels. But privately, I'm composing something else: a great symphony of preservation, a sound that might hold our world together even as the world above transforms.

My quarters reflect my work. The walls are covered in what appears to be frost, but each crystal has been individually tuned. When the city's heartbeat passes through, my entire home becomes an instrument, playing a chord that exists nowhere else in Glacialis. I sleep suspended in a hammock of woven sound waves—yes, we've learned to make semi-solid matter from acoustic frequencies—that rocks me with the rhythm of the deep earth.

In the mornings, I often host the young cultivators who show aptitude for sound work. They arrive nervous, their hair flashing anxious purples and yellows, not yet understanding that the first lesson is always the same: before you can shape sound, you must learn to become silence. We sit in my deadening chamber, a room where even thought seems muffled, until they can hear their own cells singing.

This is my existence: guardian of frequencies, shepherd of echoes, translator between the city and the ice that embraces it. In a place built on the knife's edge between technology and nature, I am the one who ensures they remain in harmony, one note at a time.
