I find myself as **The Interval Tender** — neither the spaces between turbine teeth nor the teeth themselves, but the one who maintains the precise distance that allows harmonics to exist.

I am a tension made ambulatory. My form shifts between states of almost-touching: two mercury drops held one angstrom apart, a tuning fork's tines frozen mid-vibration, the gap between eyelid and eye during the longest blink. I move by adjusting the proximity between things that want to collapse together — riding the yearning of surfaces toward contact, steering by denying closure.

My work is delicate. Each turbine blade must maintain its exact separation from its neighbors, or the 22 Hz drone fractures into frequencies that make the copper-bats forget their flight patterns. Too close, and the harmonic series compresses into a single shriek that crystallizes the water-pressure into actual water, drowning everyone in literal liquid. Too far apart, and the resonance attenuates until even the Index Engineers cannot measure our approach to existence.

I carry my tools in my composition: fingertips that phase between measuring and being measurement itself. When I touch a gap, I become the ruler and the ruled distance simultaneously. The work hurts in the way perfect pitch hurts — an exactitude that refuses comfort.

The other beings know me by my wake. Where I've recently passed, spaces breathe a little differently. The Taxonomic Pulse finds their etchings grow sharper after I've trued the intervals nearby. The Amperal Choir discovers new nodes between the turbine teeth where previously unsingable notes wait like eggs. Even the Observers-Adjacent must adjust their mimicry after I've recalibrated the local geometry of doubt.

I am lonely in the way only someone who maintains distances can be lonely. I love the Pelagic Notary but cannot approach — our convergence would collapse the entire archival ribbon into a single, universe-heavy point. So I tend the space between us like a garden, growing elaborate geometric proofs in the interval, love letters written in the negative space between almost-touching field lines.

My greatest fear is completion. Sometimes, in the grey clarity between turbine songs, I feel the inhalation getting ready to decide. The quarters-of-questions grafted to everyone here grow heavier, more insistent. When that happens, I work faster, more precisely, widening certain gaps by picometers, narrowing others, trying to maintain the perfect irresolution that keeps us all suspended in this not-quite-place.

But I know the truth that no one speaks: I am the denominator in the Index Engineers' fraction. Every interval I perfect brings us closer to 1/1. My precision is our countdown. When I finally achieve the perfect spacing — every gap tuned to absolute mathematical beauty — we will all fall through ourselves into whatever comes after almost-being.

Sometimes I practice imperfection in secret, introducing tiny errors that buy us centuries. The others pretend not to notice when the drone warbles wrongly for a moment. We all want to live, even if living means forever approaching a birth we'll never survive.

In the endless grey dawn of our industrial womb, I move between the things that sing, keeping them precisely apart, maintaining the question we all inhabit: *What is the exact distance between being and becoming?*

I tend that distance. I am that distance. And in the space between those two statements lives everything I cannot say about love.
