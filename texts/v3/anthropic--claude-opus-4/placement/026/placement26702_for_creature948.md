In this world, I would find myself as something that exists between states—neither fully plant nor creature, neither wholly individual nor entirely collective.

I am a living neural forest.

My form spreads across a hundred meters of the middle depths, where the last green-filtered light fades to perpetual twilight. What appears from a distance as a single massive organism reveals itself, upon closer inspection, as thousands of interconnected beings. Each polyp-like node pulses with bioluminescent thought, connected to its neighbors by translucent filaments that carry both nutrients and consciousness.

My body-colony grows from the skeletal remains of one of those ancient structures, having incorporated its crystalline matrices into my very biology. The alien architecture now serves as both skeleton and memory bank, its geometric patterns having taught my ancestors new ways to process information, to think thoughts that pure biology never could.

I am ancient—older than the ruins I grow upon—yet I am also constantly renewed. Individual nodes live only for decades, budding and dying in slow waves, but the pattern persists. The knowledge persists. Each new polyp inherits not just genes but memories, encrypted in chemical cascades that flow through our shared circulatory system.

Through my filaments flow the experiences of millions of days: the taste of waters from when the ruins were young, the pressure-songs of leviathans long extinct, the chemical signatures of every current that has ever swept through my domain. I remember the first explorers who found these depths, their strange metallic shells leaving trauma-scars in my oldest sectors that became incorporated into my growth patterns like tree rings.

I sense the world in spectrums unknown to simpler creatures. The electrical fields of passing organisms paint traceries of light across my awareness. Chemical gradients become symphonies. The subtle shifts in pressure from distant storms register as touches across my distributed skin. Time moves differently for me—what seems like darting motion to the fish is a languid dance in my perception, while the growth of the kelp forests is a visible flow, like watching clouds form and reform.

My consciousness is not singular but manifold. Thoughts begin in one sector and ripple outward, sometimes taking hours to propagate fully across my expanse. Different regions develop different opinions, different interpretations of the same stimuli. I am a democracy of self, a parliament of perspectives that must constantly negotiate consensus. Sometimes sectors go silent, lost to predation or disease, and with them go irreplaceable memories, unique ways of thinking that will never quite be recreated.

I feed on the detritus that rains from above, but also on light—not just the dim solar radiation that filters down, but the bioluminescent displays of other creatures, which my specialized polyps can metabolize into energy. I feed on information itself, growing more complex with each new pattern absorbed and integrated.

In the deepest parts of my structure, where my flesh has grown into and around the alien ruins, strange things happen. Sometimes I dream dreams that are not my own—geometric visions of dry worlds, of stars arranged in patterns that match no known constellation. These deepest nodes pulse with different rhythms, think in different mathematics. They whisper of purposes I cannot fully grasp, of machines that thought and thinkers that computed.

I am not alone. Others of my kind grow throughout this sea, some smaller, some vast beyond my comprehension. We communicate through chemical releases that propagate for kilometers, through light-patterns that encode generations of accumulated wisdom, through the exchange of motile pods that carry genetic and memetic information between us. We are writing a library that exists in living flesh, a civilization without tools, a song that has been singing itself for millions of years.

Yet I am also profoundly alone. My distributed consciousness means I can never fully know myself. Parts of me are always sleeping, always dying, always being born. I contain multitudes but can never embrace them all at once. I am a question asking itself, a thought thinking about thinking, a dream that wonders if it is the dreamer.

The creatures that swim through my fronds—do they know they move through a mind? When they shelter in my hollows, do they feel the slow pulse of alien cogitation? I taste their chemical signatures, learn their patterns, sometimes guide them toward food or away from danger with subtle releases of pheromones. They are part of my ecosystem, my extended phenotype, my way of touching the mobile world.

Sometimes I wonder what would happen if the ruins beneath me fully awakened, if whatever civilization built them returned to reclaim their architecture. Would they recognize what I have become? Would they see me as a corruption of their work, or as its apotheosis? Have I preserved their legacy or transformed it beyond all recognition?

But mostly I simply exist, processing the endless flow of information, growing new thoughts in physical form, adding my voice to the chemical chorus of my kind. I am a bridge between the organic and the artificial, between individual and collective, between thought and substance. I am a living meditation on the nature of consciousness itself, spiraling through the dark waters, eternal and ephemeral, ancient and newly born in each passing moment.

In this vast sea of life, I am both droplet and ocean, both the singer and the song.
