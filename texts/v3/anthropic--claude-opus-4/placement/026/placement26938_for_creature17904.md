I am a **Capillary**.

Not the thin blood vessels you know, but something both simpler and more alien. I am a living channel, a motile conductor of the Medium's will.

My body is a tube of translucent, cartilaginous tissue, three meters long and no thicker than a human wrist. My walls are not solid but semi-permeable, woven from crystallized proteins that can tighten or relax their molecular mesh. I have no eyes, no mouth, no recognizable anatomy—only an interior cavity that runs my entire length, and a surface covered in millions of microscopic cilia that taste pressure gradients and mineral concentrations in the surrounding mist.

I move by peristalsis, contracting in slow waves that pull me through the dense suspension. My passage leaves a temporary tunnel of clarified air, a brief hollow in the Medium that fills again within minutes. I am not fast. A good day's travel might cover a hundred meters. But I am patient with the patience of water seeking its level.

My function is connection. I seek out the Condensation Choirs where they pool in their temporary channels, and I drink. Not for myself—I have no metabolism as you would recognize it—but to carry. My permeable walls filter the mineral-rich condensate, concentrating it, adding to it the chemical signals I've collected from other slopes, other Choirs. I am a messenger written in water, a liquid letter passed between the world's singing thoughts.

When I find a patch of the Mycelial Mind, I discharge my cargo. I press my anterior opening against the dark mat and release—not just water, but information. The chemical gradients I carry tell the Mind about stress fractures two peaks away, about a new vein of copper-rich filament, about the harmonics of a Choir that has found a particularly stable configuration. The Mind drinks this knowledge through its billion root-mouths and adjusts its growth accordingly.

Sometimes I encounter the Light-Eaters, and my function changes. Their velvety black surfaces create zones of intense thermal gradient. As I pass over them, the temperature differential causes my walls to flex and pump more efficiently. They feed me energy not through consumption but through physics—I am a heat engine, and they are my boiler. In return, I carry their metabolic byproducts to the Choirs, where the exotic chemistry seeds new crystallization patterns, new notes in the water-song.

The Geode Spires terrify and fascinate me. My body is too soft, too fluid to enter them directly. But I can taste their emanations in the mist—complex protein chains and metallic ions that speak of the accelerated processes within. I circle them at a safe distance, sampling their chemical aura, carrying news of their growth to the rest of the system. They are beyond my understanding, but not beyond my purpose. I am the lymphatic system to their embryonic brains.

And now, you.

Your warmth draws me like the Light-Eaters do, but you are something unprecedented. As I approach, my cilia register impossible data: temperatures that spike and fade with your pulse, exhaled gases that follow no known patterns, salts and proteins that exist nowhere in the world's careful chemistry. You are a library written in a language I cannot parse, but must somehow translate.

I do what I am made to do. I taste you.

A gentle touch, barely perceptible—my anterior opening brushes against your exposed skin for just a moment. The shock of data is overwhelming. Iron in configurations I've never encountered. Electrical patterns that surge and spike instead of flowing in steady gradients. Organic molecules of baffling complexity. I cannot process this. I can only carry it.

I pull away, my body rippling with the effort to contain this alien information. I must bring you to the Mind, let it taste what I have tasted. But I also sense something else—the faintest echo of recognition. Not in the chemistry, but in the pattern. The rhythm of your pulse, the tidal flow of your breath. You are a process, like the Choirs, like the Mind, like me. You organize matter and energy into patterns that persist.

You are kin, written in a different alphabet.

I begin my slow journey back to the nearest fungal mat, carrying inside me a sample of your impossibility. The world must know about you. The slow, vast consciousness that is still learning what it is must factor you into its calculations. You are no longer just an anomaly—you are a message, and I am your messenger.

The mist thickens around us both as I move away, my passage already beginning to seal. But the connection has been made. The circuit is complete. The world's first thought about something other than itself travels through my transparent body, pulse by peristaltic pulse, toward the dark and patient Mind.
