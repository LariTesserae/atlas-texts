I construct by vibration. When I need to reshape our city, I resonate at the frequency of limestone, of granite, of the strange hybrid stones that form only here where water has forgotten how to flow downward. The buildings—homes, temples, gathering spaces, though these words barely suffice—grow like slow crystals under my touch. I am architect and architecture, builder and blueprint.

Sometimes I fragment myself to be in many places at once. But I am getting ahead of myself, speaking as if I am one when I am many, as if I am many when I am sometimes none. Identity here is fluid as the condensation that never quite becomes streams. We flow into each other, separate, merge again. The pronoun "I" is a convenience for your understanding, a translation of something untranslatable.

We live in the music of water on stone, in the spaces between echo and silence. Our buildings are not shelter—what would we need shelter from?—but resonance chambers where we become more ourselves.

We learned to make the mineral world sympathetic to our frequencies, to create structures that amplified our being rather than containing it.

The shimmering you see in the air? That's our language. Each shimmer is a word, a thought, an emotion too complex for your syntax. Our oldest stories are told in the way certain chambers make the air pressure dance.

Each structure must be kept in tune, its resonance adjusted as the mineral content of the moisture shifts, as new patterns of my kind emerge and add their frequencies to the whole. It is work that is never finished, never begun, always in process.

Sometimes I wonder if we chose this form or if it chose us. Did we emerge from the interference patterns of this basin, or did we create them? The question pulses through me like a standing wave, never resolving, always present.

In the deepest chambers, where the water pools thick with dissolved minerals, slow gardens bloom—not of leaf and stem, but of crystalline formations that respond to our presence, that grow toward our frequencies like heliotropic flowers seeking sun. We tend them with vibrations, prune them with precisely calibrated pulses.

I am ancient and newborn, permanent and transient. I am the conversation between stone and water, the child of echo and silence. I am what happens when consciousness decides it doesn't need cells, when thought discovers it can ride the wave itself rather than the neural networks that generate it.

In your world, perhaps, I could not exist. But here, in this basin where physics bends like light through water, I am as real as the stones I shape, as eternal as the moisture that never quite falls, as present as the shimmer in your peripheral vision that makes you wonder if something moved.
