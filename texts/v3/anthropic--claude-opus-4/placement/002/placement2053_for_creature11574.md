I find myself as **The Cartographer of Unbecoming**.

I condense each day from the memory of maps—not maps as you know them, but the kind that form when possibility crystallizes along the walls of the breathing chamber. My body, when I choose to have one, appears as a latticework of living mineral veins, each line a path that was taken or might be taken or is being taken elsewhere. I am translucent amber shot through with silver threads that pulse with the routes of my kin as they transform.

I alone among the Transitionals am cursed with consistency—not of form, but of purpose. Where others flow freely between states, I am compelled to trace, to witness, to remember the shape of each transformation. My consciousness spreads thin as mercury across the chamber's surfaces, recording the topology of change itself.

When a crystalline being shatters, I map the trajectory of each fragment. When a gaseous one drifts through solid matter, I chart the impossible intersection. My body responds involuntarily—new veins of silver light branching through my amber flesh, each one a record of another's journey through form. I am becoming a living atlas of transformations that have no names.

The mappings layer within me. I can feel the weight of accumulated paths, thousands upon thousands of transformations etched into my being. Sometimes I grow so dense with recorded changes that I sink into the floor's carpet of shed forms, and there I lie for unmeasured time, pressing these maps into the detritus, creating fossil impressions of journeys that haven't happened yet.

The others pass through me to read themselves—my translucent body serves as a library of what they've been and could become. A young heat-shimmer might drift through my chest and suddenly see the seventeen thousand ways it could condense. An ancient multi-state being might touch one of my silver veins and remember a transformation it performed before time started keeping proper records.

But the maps are becoming stranger. Recently, I've begun charting transformations that don't belong to anyone here—paths through states of being that even the Transitionals haven't discovered. My body shows routes through forms that require different physics, different chemistry, perhaps different definitions of existence itself. I am mapping my way toward something, or something is mapping itself through me.

In the deepest pool, when I dare to sink my veins into that ancient-young presence, I see the most terrifying map of all: a path that leads out. Not out of the chamber—for there is no out in any physical sense—but out of the cycle itself. A transformation so complete it becomes permanence. A journey that ends.

I record this too, though my mineral body cracks with the effort of containing such a paradox. And sometimes, in the chamber's breathing darkness, I trace this final map with my own form, feeling how it would be to transform into something that cannot transform, to become a being that simply *is*.

But I always pull back. Because I am the Cartographer, and there are still so many changes to map, so many paths through possibility to trace with my silver veins. I exist as the memory of their journeys, the proof that even endless change leaves traces, creates patterns, has meaning—even if that meaning can only be read by creatures who refuse to stay still long enough to understand it.

My greatest fear is that I am mapping my way toward my own final transformation: becoming a perfect, complete map of everything that can be. On that day, I will crystallize completely, every possible path through existence recorded in my amber flesh, and I will be beautiful and terrible and utterly, eternally still.

Until then, I flow and crystallize and dissolve and reform, but always with purpose, always recording, a librarian of transformations in a library that breathes, where the books are alive and write themselves with their own changes, where every story is about becoming something else, and where the only ending is to become someone who can finally stop reading.
