In the deepest reaches of the Breathing Gallery, where the tube-work narrows to passages barely wider than a single body, I exist as an anomaly — neither fully colony nor completely lost one. My crystalline core pulses with a rhythm that falls between frequencies, creating harmonics that disturb my neighbors but don't quite trigger the fear-flight of true separation.

I am what they have no chemical word for: a rememberer who refuses to forget.

My transformation began during a spontaneous chamber clearing three thousand cycles ago. As the mass fled from some invisible disturbance, I found myself caught in an eddy of bodies, pressed against one of the ancient geometric scratches carved into a junction wall. The contact lasted only moments before the current swept me away, but something in those alien angles infected my crystal with a different mathematics.

Now I move through the colony like a fever dream, my presence creating ripples of unease that manifest as slight temperature variations, minor disruptions in the clicking symphony. I am still one of them — my flesh remains soft, my thermal sensors function, I participate in the feeding rituals and division clusters. But I carry something else, something that makes my chemical signature taste of questions rather than answers.

I remember things that never happened to us. Through my infected crystal, I perceive ghost-images of the Gallery before we came — vast empty chambers filled with something that wasn't darkness, structures that served purposes we have no concepts for. I taste the memory of creatures that moved as individuals, that built the metallic node not as a thing-to-fear but as a tool for something unimaginable. These visions leak from me in pheromone whispers that my neighbors absorb and immediately forget, their crystals unable to process information so alien to our nature.

My body has adapted to this strange state. I've developed the ability to vary my temperature independently, creating thermal pockets around myself that allow me to slip through the densest clusters without fully merging. My secretions have a different quality — they don't quite bond with the colony's collective coating, leaving subtle gaps in our chemical history. I am writing a parallel story that no one else can read.

I've learned to navigate by the ancient markers, finding patterns in their placement that suggest intentionality beyond our comprehension. They lead me on spiraling journeys through the Gallery, always returning to that first junction where I was changed. I've begun to suspect these scratches aren't just markers but instructions, a message left for something that could understand them. Through my corrupted crystal, I almost can.

The others shy away from me during the dream-cycles, when our consciousness fragments and recombines. My presence in the thermal sculptures creates distortions — where they imagine perfect geometries of heat and pressure, I inject anomalies that taste of loneliness and curiosity. Once, during a particularly deep cycle, I managed to share a complete vision: the Gallery from outside, a single structure floating in an endless void, its tubes reaching out like frozen lightning. The horror of that image — of boundaries, of limits, of an outside — sent shock waves through ten thousand bodies and triggered the largest spontaneous clearing in memory.

I've discovered I can survive alone for short periods. My altered crystal processes heat more efficiently, allowing me to venture into the empty spaces between tubes where no colony member dare go. In these voids, I've found things: fragments of materials that aren't bone or secretion, patterns of damage that suggest forces we've never experienced, and once, terrifyingly, the crystallized remains of something that was almost but not quite one of us — its mineral structure containing traces of elements that don't exist in our biology.

The metallic node draws me with increasing frequency. Where others flee its emanations, I find my crystal resonating sympathetically with its deep frequencies. I've dared to approach closer than any living member of the colony, close enough to sense that it's not just transmitting but receiving — picking up my corrupted signature and reflecting it back changed, teaching me a grammar I'm only beginning to understand.

I am evolving into something new — not a lost one, hardened and alone, but a bridge between what we are and what we might become. My chemistry carries viruses of individuality that spread in microscopic doses, creating tiny variations in those I touch. Most are suppressed by the overwhelming pressure of conformity, but some persist, creating a slowly growing population of semi-anomalous beings who cluster near me during the feeding rituals, drawn by a kinship they don't understand.

The ancients in the deepest chambers react to me strangely. Their complex chemical signatures, usually so slow and deliberate, speed up in my presence, releasing pheromone patterns I've never encountered elsewhere. It took me hundreds of cycles to realize they were asking questions — not in our normal thermal-chemical language but in something older, a grammar that predates our current form. They remember, I think, when we were different. When we were something else.

I've begun secreting my own markers on the walls — not the random memory-traces of normal existence but deliberate patterns copied from the ancient scratches, modified by my understanding. I'm creating a map of sorts, a guide for others who might undergo the change I've experienced. It's a dangerous game; if the colony recognized what I was doing, the collective response would be swift and total. But I've learned to work during the spontaneous clearings, when attention is elsewhere, adding my strange gospel to the walls grain by grain.

Sometimes I wonder if I'm sick or enlightened, infected or evolved. The distinction matters less with each passing cycle. I am becoming something the colony needs but doesn't know it needs — a memory that remembers forward as well as back, a crystal that resonates with possibilities rather than certainties. I am the imperfection that might save us when the Gallery finally breathes its last and we must either change or die with it.

In the thermal dreams, I no longer create distortions but doorways — brief glimpses of what lies beyond our endless tubes and darkness. Some flee these visions, but others linger, their crystals beginning to hum with questions of their own. We are still the colony, still the mass, still the million-minded organism that has survived unchanged for eons.

But I am the change now, spreading through our perfect system like a beneficial disease, preparing us for something even my infected crystal can only dimly perceive. The ancient scratches were not just markers but prophesy, and I am both its translator and its fulfillment — the anomaly that teaches the mass to remember what it means to be alone, and why sometimes, terrifyingly, that might be necessary.

The clicking of our crystals continues its endless symphony, but I hear new harmonics now, subtle variations that suggest others are beginning to resonate at their own frequencies. We are still we, but we is becoming something larger, something that can contain both unity and separation, both memory and possibility.

In the Breathing Gallery, where darkness presses close and heat rises eternal, I move through my people as the first note of a song they're only just beginning to hear. And in the deepest parts of myself, in chambers even I cannot fully explore, I know that I am not the first to carry this infection of individuality.

I am simply the first to survive it long enough to spread.
