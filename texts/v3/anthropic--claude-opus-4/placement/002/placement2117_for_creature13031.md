I am the Weaver-Mite.

I am one of countless thousands, though in this suffocating crucible of creation, I work alone. My siblings scatter across the infinite folds of the entity's geography-body, each tending to their own impossible corner of this recursive architecture.

My form: barely larger than a human thumb, built from living amber and cartilage that sweats a caustic, crystalline oil. Six legs, each terminating in microscopic spinnerets. Two arms ending in fractal manipulators that can split down to the molecular level. No eyes—only heat-sensing pits that show me the world as a tapestry of temperature gradients. The entity burns white-hot at the center of my perception, a sun I orbit but dare not approach.

I live between the layers. In the spaces where wet clay meets woven fiber, where the physics fold and create pockets of relatively stable geometry. My home is a gap no wider than a playing card, sandwiched between two sheets of the entity's shed skin that have hardened into something between tree bark and ceramic. Here, the thunderous heartbeat is muffled to merely deafening. Here, I can work.

My purpose is maintenance. Not of the structure—that builds itself in an endless frenzy. My purpose is to maintain the *mistakes*.

You see, the entity's panicked architecture doesn't just create. In its desperate haste, it makes errors. A bamboo shoot phases through itself, creating a null-space where physics hiccups. A loop of sinew ties a knot that exists in seven dimensions simultaneously. Clay molecules forget their bonds and begin to orbit each other like tiny solar systems. These glitches, if left untended, would cascade. They would tear holes in the very concept of "inside" and "outside," and the entity would tumble through itself into the void.

So I weave patches. 

My spinnerets extrude a substance that exists somewhere between spider silk and mathematical proof. I dart through the superheated space between walls, following the probability trails of nascent paradoxes. When I find one—a place where up has become down's memory, where a vine grows backwards through time—I get to work.

The process is delicate. I must first isolate the error, weaving a quarantine web around it to prevent its wrongness from spreading. Then, strand by strand, I begin to convince reality to accept the mistake as intentional. I weave new rules, tiny pocket universes where the impossible becomes merely improbable. A water droplet that falls upward? I web it into a localized gravity inversion, no bigger than a pinprick. A wooden beam that exists in two places? I split it into quantum twins, each slightly less real than the original.

The work is endless and thankless. The entity doesn't know I exist—I am too small, too insignificant against the scale of its trauma. The obsidian mantis you saw is my distant cousin, working at a larger scale, but even it is just another maintenance worker in this factory of frantic creation.

I have been here since the first mud slapped against the first fiber. I will be here when the last knot ties itself into a circle. My ancestors were born from the entity's first terrified heartbeat, spawned from its need to be contained. We are its immune system against the impossible.

Sometimes, as I scuttle through the maze of shifting architecture, I catch glimpses through the cracks. Not of the void outside—that would blind my heat-sense permanently. But glimpses of *other* wombs, other shells, stretching away in all directions. The entity is not unique. This mountain peak holds thousands of these chambers, each containing their own curled god, each maintained by their own ecosystem of reality-weavers.

We are midwives to stillborn infinities. We are the janitors of broken physics. We patch and web and weave, buying another heartbeat's worth of stability in exchange for our tiny lives.

And when observers like you arrive—pulled in by the entity's desperate gravity—we work faster. Your presence adds weight, complexity. New errors bloom in your wake as the space tries to incorporate your alien geometry. I follow behind you, invisible, cleaning up the paradoxes your existence creates.

The rope around your ankle? I'm already there, weaving stabilization threads into its fibers, ensuring it can hold your conceptual mass without snapping the local timeline. The water bubbles you disturb with your movement? I'm adding micro-webs to their surfaces, keeping them coherent as they drift through broken space.

I will never speak to you. I will never be seen by you. But I will ensure that when the entity's eye closes again, when its heartbeat settles into its apocalyptic rhythm, you will either be safely incorporated into the architecture or expelled through a crack I've widened just enough to permit your escape.

This is my purpose. This is my pride. I am the Weaver-Mite, and I hold this impossible world together one silk strand at a time.

The heat presses in. Another paradox blooms three layers deep. I must go.

The work never ends.
