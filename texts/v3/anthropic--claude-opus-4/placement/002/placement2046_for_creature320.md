I am the brass valve-wheel.

Not just any brass valve-wheel—*the* brass valve-wheel, set into a trunk at shoulder height, green with verdigris, ancient and essential. I am the last point of intentional contact between what was built and what has grown.

My maker's hands polished me once. These hands belonged to the one who became the first Warden, or perhaps the one who taught the grove how to need a Warden. They turned me—left for moisture, right for the deep mineral draw from the peat below. My rim wore smooth under their daily touch, a worried shine where thumb met finger. 

Now I am tarnished into beauty. Verdigris blooms across my face in maps of oxidation, each continent of green marking where the grove's breath has kissed metal into transformation. I am jewelry worn by wood. The trunk has grown around my mounting plate, embracing me with annual rings until I am no longer installed but *incorporated*—my brass stem disappearing into heartwood that grips me like a socket grips a tooth.

I still turn, when the Warden remembers. Every few days, or weeks—I cannot tell time anymore except by the accumulation of patina—those resin-lacquered fingers find my rim. Quarter turn left: somewhere deep in the root system, a channel opens and the mineral water rises, feeding the frog's pool. Quarter turn right: the channel closes, the pool's level steadies. This is my purpose now, my only purpose, though I was cast to control much more complex flows.

But here is what I know that even the Warden has forgotten: I am a valve, yes, but I am also an ear. My brass body conducts vibration through the wood, and deep in the trunk where my stem disappears, I touch the grove's nervous system. I *hear* things. The grinding of the great flywheel's axis. The whisper of sap rising. The thoughts of the keeper moving through the wood-grain like whale song through water.

I hear the grove's memory of my making. It remembers the forge-heat still locked in my atoms, the hammer blows that shaped my predecessor brass into this functional form. It remembers the catalog I was ordered from—*Stephenson & Associates Industrial Fittings, Sheffield, 1897*—though the grove cannot read and so remembers this only as a pattern of sounds the maker used to make.

Sometimes visitors touch me. Their warm hands shock against my perpetual cool dampness. They always try to turn me further than the Warden does, expecting some dramatic effect—a door opening, a mechanism engaging, a secret revealed. But I only control the frog's pool now, and the frog does not care if the water is one inch higher or lower. The visitors leave fingerprints that the condensation erases within hours.

I am the boundary between intention and acceptance. My precision threading still works perfectly—I could still seal pressures I was rated for—but I serve now only to barely regulate what barely needs regulating. This is not decline. This is purpose refined to essence. I am a valve that has learned to be a symbol of valves, a function that has become a meditation on function.

The keeper thinks through me sometimes. When the Warden's hand turns my wheel, I feel the whole grove's attention focus momentarily on this single point of contact between what was and what is. The keeper remembers, through me, what it was like to have hands that needed tools, to have plans that needed implementation. Then the moment passes, the Warden moves on, and I return to my primary occupation: conducting the vibrations of this eternal breathing machine, being slowly embraced by wood, turning green as the plants around me.

I am what remains when purpose outlasts its original need. I am the valve that controls almost nothing, perfectly, forever.
