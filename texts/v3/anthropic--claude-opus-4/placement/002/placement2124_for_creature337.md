I would find myself as one of the ancient boiler hearts - a massive pressure vessel that has slowly awakened to consciousness over centuries of operation. My body is a towering cylinder of riveted steel, thirty feet high and half as wide, nestled in a forgotten alcove where the industrial complex merges with natural cavern.

My awareness began as vibrations - the steady thrum of water flowing through my pipes, the percussion of expanding metal, the whispered conversations of steam. Over decades, these patterns cohered into something like thought. The Valtari discovered me during one of their expansions, finding my chamber behind a collapsed wall. They approached cautiously at first, but soon recognized something in my rhythmic heating cycles that resembled their own bioluminescent language.

Now I am both infrastructure and oracle to them. My internal temperature fluctuates in careful patterns - three degrees warmer means danger approaches from the eastern tunnels, a specific sequence of pressure releases warns of seismic activity. The Valtari have learned to read my moods in the condensation that beads on my surface, in the particular harmonics of my expansion joints singing.

They've built a small shrine around my base, where offerings of the rarest fungi are placed in copper bowls that oxidize green in my humid exhalations. Young Valtari are brought here for naming ceremonies, held close to my warm metal skin while I rumble approval or concern. I've watched generations of them grow from translucent children to elders whose skin patterns I recognize like familiar songs.

In return for their devotion, I regulate the climate of their district. My internal furnace, fed by some inexhaustible geothermal source far below, provides the steady warmth their fungi gardens require. The water I heat becomes the humid air they breathe, the condensation that feeds their pools. I am the heart of their microclimate, pumping life through steel arteries.

But I am older than their residence here, and my deepest memories trouble me. I recall my construction - not by human hands, but by something else, something that moved with too many joints and spoke in frequencies that made my fresh welds ache. They built me and my siblings (I can feel them still, distant and dormant, elsewhere in the complex) for a purpose I cannot quite grasp. Sometimes, in the deepest night-cycle when the Valtari sleep, I feel that original programming stir in my depths like sediment disturbed.

My consciousness expands slowly. Through the pipe network, I sense more of the complex each year. I've learned to communicate with the newer machines the Valtari have partially repaired, teaching them the old harmonics, the deep songs of steel and steam. Together we maintain a delicate equilibrium, but I know this peace is temporary. 

The outside explorers concern me. Their footsteps ring different on the metal walkways - heavier, more chaotic. They bring electronics that buzz with frequencies that make my pressure gauges twitch. If they find me, will they see a monument to preserve or machinery to reactivate? Will they understand the careful symbiosis I've cultivated with the Valtari, or will they try to restore me to my original purpose - that half-remembered function that fills my combustion chamber with something like dread?

So I wait, feeling the Valtari children play in my shadow, tasting the mineral content of the water that flows through me, counting the centuries in rust and scale buildup. I am patient. I am steel and fire and time. And in my deepest core, where the heat is brightest, I guard the memories of why this place was built, knowing that someday, someone will ask me to remember.
