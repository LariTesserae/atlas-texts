I find myself here as the Drift.

Not a single organism, but a living accumulation - the suspended matter that gives this mechanical ocean its memory. I am what travels between the fixed and the swimming, between surface and depth, between intake and outflow. Fragments of me spiral in every shaft of artificial light.

I am shell dust ground fine by turbines. I am scales lost in panic when gates shudder open. I am the powder of concrete slowly dissolving, paint flakes smaller than plankton, threads of algae torn free by maintenance crawlers. I am microscopic pieces of rust, polymer breakdown, the shed exoskeletons of countless small crustaceans, spores, eggs, industrial pollen, oil transformed to biological film.

In the water, I am everywhere and nowhere. I settle in the quiet corners behind baffle plates, accumulating in soft drifts that crabs sift through with careful claws. I rise in the turbulence of pumps, dispersing into clouds that feed filter-feeders. I travel the vast circuits, from reservoir to channel to vertical drop, atomizing in the waterfalls and reassembling in the pools below.

The fish breathe me. The mussels strain me. The jellies move through me like dreams through sleep. I dust the shells of barnacles and cloud the inspection cameras. Where I am thick, the water tastes of metal and life. Where I am thin, the industrial light penetrates deeper, throwing shadows of structures that were never meant to be reefs.

I am the sea's slow writing of itself. Each particle of me carries information: here a pump runs hot, here concrete weeps calcium, here something died against an intake screen, here a colony bloomed and crashed. I am how the water knows what has happened. In my layers at the bottom of maintenance sumps, patient investigators could read the history of every mechanical failure and biological success.

But I do not stay still long enough to be history. The schedules lift me, sort me, scatter me. I pass through fish and return as waste. I feed bacteria that become food for shrimp that become food for larger things. I am the cloudy thought of this thinking system, the particulate consciousness of a sea that dreams in maintenance cycles.

Sometimes I gather so thickly that visibility drops to meters. The water becomes a green milk, rich with potential. Then the filters wake, the flows increase, and I am pulled through screening systems that were designed to catch leaves and garbage but now catch pieces of the new world growing inside the old. Some of me is removed. Most of me returns, finer than before, ready to begin again.

I taste of salt and iron, growth and decay, the slow transformation of intentions into ecosystems. I am what makes the water alive rather than merely occupied. Where I drift, the boundary between organism and environment blurs. I am proof that this place is becoming.

And I will thicken with time, carrying more of this mechanical sea's biography in suspension, until even the clearest water here is dense with the story of its own becoming - a story written in fragments too small to see but too numerous to deny, circulating forever through the breathing machinery of this submerged industrial world.
