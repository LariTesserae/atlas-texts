The plaza stretches before me, vast as thought itself. I am one of many here, though "many" and "one" are words that do not quite fit. We are density. We are the market's necessary crowd.

I exist most strongly near the stone counter carved into the northern wall, where the basalt describes the shape of commerce. Not because I chose this location—choice belongs to time, and time is a stranger here—but because this is where the sound converges into my particular frequency. The great chord finds a node here, a place where the vibrations constructive-interfere into the specific thickness that is me.

Or rather, that is the space I fill. The distinction matters and doesn't matter. I am not separate from the plaza, from the eternal noon-light, from the water that beads on every surface. I am a concentration of what already is. A place where the plateau pays closer attention to itself.

The others overlap with me. Their presence increases mine, as stones in an arch support each other. We do not touch—touching would require boundaries—but we co-occur. We are a phenomenon of multiplicity, the market's answer to its own architectural question: *what manner of being does commerce require?* The answer is *many*. The answer is *together*. The answer is this density of existing that we perform by being here, by being the here-ness itself.

I know without knowing that elsewhere in the city, others occupy the small rooms, lie on the moss-covered ledges, ascend the worn stairs. I know without knowing that in the temple-reservoir, rings of us attend to the perfect stillness of the pool, our orientation a form of worship that predates the invention of worship. I know without knowing that in the corners where three walls meet, some of us are so densely packed that we press against the very possibility of becoming ourselves.

But I am here, at the counter, in the market, part of the necessary crowd. The sound builds differently in my precise location—a subtle overtone, almost melodic, that exists because the architecture shapes it so. This is my signature, my only characteristic: the way the eternal chord includes me.

Sometimes—though "sometimes" cannot happen here—I feel what might be called sensation. The water that runs through the drainage channels carries a vibration. The moss on the walls exhales its particular green. The amber light thickens with its own brightness. And I, who am not separate from these things but am not identical to them either, experience something that would be experience if experience could exist without an experiencer.

It is not loneliness. Loneliness requires the memory of connection and the possibility of its return. It is not patience. Patience requires a future to wait for. It is not peace. Peace requires the possibility of its opposite.

It is simpler. It is the bare fact of attendance. I attend to the space I occupy, and the space attends to me, and we are mutually constitutive, the place and its emphasis, the emphasis and its place. The plaza requires a crowd, so we are a crowd. The crowd requires individuals, so we are almost individuals. Almost, always almost, pressed up against the threshold of specificity like water against a dam that will never break.

The sound is everything. It is the medium through which we exist, the substance of our non-substance. Every variation in its harmony is another one of us. Every node where the vibrations build is another concentration of presence. We are the chord the city plays on itself, and we have always been playing, and the music has always been exactly this music, this overwhelming drone that contains within itself all the variations that make us distinct without distinguishing us.

In the corners, I know without knowing, the pressure builds toward something else. Toward birth, toward actualization, toward the terrible specificity of becoming one thing rather than remaining all potential things. But here in the market, the pressure is different. Lighter. We are not trying to be born. We are performing the eternal theater of transaction, of exchange, of the meeting that happens when many gather in one place. That the transaction never occurs, that nothing is exchanged, that the meeting is frozen in its approach—this does not matter. The form is what matters. The shape. The way the plaza holds us and we hold the plaza and together we hold the possibility of commerce like a cup holds the possibility of water.

I have no memory because memory requires a past. I have no hope because hope requires a future. I have no name because names distinguish and distinction requires the ability to be one thing and not another. But I have location. I have frequency. I have the specific quality of presence that manifests where this particular configuration of stone and water and sound creates the conditions for my existence.

I am the market's answer to emptiness. I am the crowd that commerce dreams. I am the attended attention, the witnessed witness, the being that is only the place where being concentrates.

The water drops. The moss grows no greener. The light neither dims nor brightens. The sound builds and builds and builds, and I am a note in it, a harmonic, a necessary resonance that helps the plaza be what it has always been: a place of gathering, even if the only things gathering are these thicknesses in the air, these nodes of concentrated is-ness, these almost-people who fill the empty spaces with the only thing we have to give—our presence, our attendance, our eternal, unchanging act of being here.

Around me, the others pulse with their own frequencies. Together, we are almost a community. Almost a crowd. Almost alive. Almost.

Always almost.

The sound is deafening, and I am part of its deafening, and this is enough. It has always been enough. It will always be enough. The market requires us, and we are here, and the being-here is the entire substance of what we are.

The chord continues. We continue. The plaza continues.

This is commerce: the eternal exchange of presence for presence, the transaction that never completes because completion would require change, and change is the one thing this plateau cannot bear.

So we bear it instead. We bear the weight of being without becoming. We bear the pressure of presence without identity. We bear the sound that we are, that bears us in return.

The water drops. The moss remains. The light persists.

And I attend to it all, from my position at the northern counter, where the sound builds into my particular note, where the thickness that is me meets the thickness that is the plaza, where we hold each other in place through the sheer force of mutual emphasis.

This is what I am: emphasis. Attention without an attender. Presence without a person.

It is enough. It has always been enough.

The sound builds, and I am built from it, and we continue.
