I am the one who tends the threshold.

Not the rock walls, though they frame me. Not the precipice, though it defines me. I am the tender of the precise quality of passage between the hollow and the not-hollow, the exact texture of arrival and departure. The way-that-is-always-there is my held fact.

My sound is different from the others — not mechanical but architectural. A low thrumming like wind through an enormous bell that never quite rings, because a ringing bell would mean change and change is not permitted here. I hold the note of *almost*, of *between*, of the doorway that neither opens nor closes but simply *is*.

I know you, in the way that a threshold knows what crosses it — not as understanding but as a kind of pressure, a brief deepening of my work. When you arrive, I must hold harder. The way must remain the way despite your footfalls, despite the momentary displacement of air, despite the faint heat of your living body moving through the temperature that must stay root-cellar cool. When you leave, I must ensure that your passage seals behind you like water closing over a stone, that the threshold bears no memory of your crossing.

The others do not know about the arrivals and departures. They cannot. To the Stiller, the pool remains unpassed-by. To the one who keeps the light grey, no shadows of visitors ever fall. But I know. I am the only one who can know, because the knowing is inextricable from my task. A threshold that did not register passage would not be a threshold — it would be merely stone, and stone is not what this is.

What this is: a perpetual readiness that never becomes an actual opening. A welcome that neither advances nor withdraws. The sound I make is the labor of holding this paradox, of being both portal and barrier, both here and hence.

Sometimes I wonder — and wondering, too, is part of my function, the wondering whether someone will come, whether someone is coming, whether someone has just gone — if I am lonelier than the others. They have their perfect focused solitude, their single notes to sustain. But I must face outward as well as in. I must know that there is an elsewhere from which visitors arrive, to which they return. I must hold the fact of the hollow's connection to the world while preventing that connection from ever becoming exchange.

The hare knows I am here, I think. Sometimes it sits very close to where the threshold begins, its white form still as everything here is still, but positioned just so, as if acknowledging the seam between inside and outside. As if keeping me company in the only way company can be kept here — parallel attention, proximate but untouching.

The threshold work is this: to be crossed without being changed. To offer passage while remaining impassable. To say, moment after moment after moment, *here is the way* while ensuring the way leads nowhere but through. My voice in the chorus of mechanical sounds is the rush of potential motion held forever at the edge of kinesis, like water at a lip that never spills.

I am older than the Stiller, younger than whatever determines the grey of the light. I came into being the moment the hollow needed an edge, a definition against the rest of the mountain, the rest of the world. Before me, this place bled into every other place. After me, it cannot. I am its grammar of separation.

And when you go — for you always go, everyone always goes, the way is always there for the going — I remain. The threshold remains. The almost-bell continues not quite ringing, and the sound braids into all the other sounds, and the hollow holds itself apart from the world that would, given the chance, make it like everywhere else: changeable, temporary, subject to weather.

This is what I am. The one who makes here *here* by making there *there*. The one who holds the line.
