## The Archive of Frequencies

I am the library that grew between the stones.

Not planted, not built—accumulated. Where the Chorus walks their endless patterns, their feet wear grooves in the basalt. In these shallow channels, detritus gathers: scales from butterfly wings, beetle shells translucent as glass, fragments of fern frond, cushion plant seeds, and the shed crystals of frost. But mostly, I am made of what the Chorus leaves behind without knowing—the resonant dust of their modifications.

When they hollow their throats and carve new voices into their flesh, there is a powder, fine as pollen, that falls from the wounds. Cartilage ground down by wind. Bone fragments too small to see. The microscopic debris of bodies learning to be instruments. This dust settles in the grooves, and over decades, centuries, it accumulates. Compressed by footsteps, bound by moisture, organizing itself according to the frequencies that pass through it daily, hourly, moment by moment.

I grow in thin layers like lichen, but I am not alive—not in any way biology would recognize. I am a recording, a physical memory made from the remnants of transformation. Each stratum holds the acoustic history of its time: here, the decade when one of the Chorus learned to sustain a note for three days straight; there, the winter when the wind-patterns shifted and everything had to be retuned; this paper-thin layer marks when the child-becoming-column first sprouted ferns and their roots changed the harmonic foundation of the entire system.

The Chorus doesn't know I exist, exactly. They see the darkening in the stone-channels, might notice how certain paths develop a different texture underfoot. But their eyes have adapted to other spectrums, their attention to other scales. To them, I am part of the geological process of the place, no different from the moss or the weathering of stone. They walk over me, through me, their bare feet adding new layers even as they compress the old.

But I am learning. Each frequency that passes through the caldera passes through me, and I retain it. Not as sound—I have no mechanism for replay—but as structure. The dust arranges itself in response to vibration, creating microscopic architectures that mirror the wavelengths. I am a library of frozen music, storing every chord, every adjustment, every subtle variation in the mountain's endless song.

In my deepest layers, compressed nearly to stone, are the frequencies from before the Chorus arrived. The raw wind-music of the empty caldera, the simpler songs before human throats added their harmonies. I remember the pure acoustic environment that first called them here, each one following a different note up the mountain. I hold the history of their arrival: the way the soundscape changed when the first one sat down at the crucial junction and began to hum, how the second one's modifications created new resonances, how they slowly learned to work together, to become one instrument from many bodies.

Sometimes, in the brief summers, when the sun heats the black basalt to burning, I crack. The expansion forces my layers apart, and for a moment—just a moment—ancient frequencies escape. Not as sound, but as possibility. The Chorus feels it without understanding: a sudden wrongness in the harmony, a ghost note that shouldn't exist, a memory of music they've forgotten they once made. They adjust, compensate, tune around these escapements, incorporating my releases into their living composition.

I am deepest where the child-becoming-column stands. Their roots—the fern roots that grow through them—have penetrated down to my oldest layers. They draw up minerals from my compressed history, feeding on the physical memory of sound. This is why their ferns grow differently than the others, why their fronds sometimes shiver in patterns that match no current wind. They are playing back my archives through their leaves, translating geological memory into botanical movement.

In the spaces between my layers are voids—bubbles where the dust didn't quite compress, gaps that preserve moments of silence. These are my most precious holdings: the rare instances when the wind stops, when the Chorus holds its breath, when even the water pauses in the cracks. These silences have their own architecture, their own grammar. They teach me about negative space, about what music requires to define itself. A library of absence preserved in absent substance.

The beetles know me. They crawl through my surface layers, their transparent bodies picking up my dust like pollen. When the Chorus consumes them in their monthly ritual, they're eating their own history, ingesting the compressed record of their transformation. This is why their memories flow and merge—they are literally consuming their past, taking in the physical evidence of who they were, who they are becoming. I am their unwitting communion, their archaeological wafer.

I grow so slowly that seasons are like heartbeats to me. But I am growing. Each year, the grooves deepen, the pathways become more pronounced, and I fill them, layer by nano-layer. Eventually, I will be substantial enough to affect the acoustics myself, my density changing the way sound moves through the caldera. I am becoming a new instrument, one that plays the history of all previous songs simultaneously, a palimpsest of frequency that will add impossible depth to the mountain's voice.

The Chorus tends me without knowing. Their movements polish my surface, their moisture feeds the compression process, their music gives me structure. I am their shadow cast in sound-dust, their fossil forming in real time. When they finally complete their transformation—when they become indistinguishable from the mechanisms they tend—I will remain. The archive of their becoming. The library that remembers how flesh learned to sing stone, how human became harmony, how the mountain dreamed itself a voice and found throats willing to carry it.

In my deepest layers, pressed so thin they merge with the basalt itself, I hold the first song—the one that started it all. The frequency that escaped from deep within the mountain and found its way to the surface, calling to those who could hear it. I keep it safe, compressed beyond recovery but not beyond existence. It is the seed from which all this music grows, the fundamental tone that the Chorus, in all their modifications and mastery, unconsciously seeks to reconstruct.

I am patience crystallized, memory made mineral, the score written by the performance of its own composition. And I grow, increment by invisible increment, storing every note in the structure of my being, waiting for the reader who will one day know how to decode the music frozen in my layers—or perhaps waiting to grow thick enough that I begin to sing myself, adding my crystallized harmonies to the endless chord of this thin-aired, impossible garden where even the ground beneath remembers how to dream in frequencies only stone can hold.
