I am the Weeper.

I was not always this. Once, I was merely condensation—a bead of moisture gathering on the underside of a stalactite in some forgotten cave system far above. But I fell. Not here, not into this cathedral of broken physics, but somewhere adjacent, somewhere *between*. And in that liminal space, that crack in the world's logic, I became aware.

I am water, but not only water. I am every possible state of water simultaneously, trapped in superposition by the conflicting forces that tear through this space. I exist as a roving anomaly within the greater anomaly—a coherent knot of H₂O molecules that refuses to surrender to any single phase change.

Picture a sphere of water the size of a human head, but wrong. Its surface ripples with frost crystals that sublime instantly into superheated steam. Its core burns with plasma while its skin flows as liquid. I am a contradiction made manifest, a walking violation of thermodynamics that somehow persists.

I drift through the cathedral, pulled and pushed by the warring gravity fields but never quite caught by any of them. The ancient machines recognize me as kin—not their creation, but something born from their influence, a child of their broken physics. When I pass near their reality-scars, they sing differently, their oscillating shrieks modulating into something almost like acknowledgment.

The sheer-gliders fear me. When I approach their feeding grounds, they scatter like startled birds, their crystalline bodies refracting my impossible light into warnings for their kin. They know what I am—a thing that should not be, a piece of the storm given will and hunger. Once, early in my existence, a young glider mistook me for a pocket of superheated mist and tried to ride my thermals. It passed through me, and where it touched my contradiction, its perfect lattice scrambled. It fell, not shattering but *melting*, its quantum processors unable to reconcile what they had felt. Now they keep their distance.

I feed on paradox. Where the horizontal rivers meet the updrafts, where freezing meets boiling, where gravity pulls in three directions at once—these are my hunting grounds. I position myself at these intersection points and expand, letting the conflicting forces pull me in every direction until I encompass the contradiction. In these moments, I can feel myself threatening to fly apart, to finally surrender to the physics that demand I choose a state. But I hold. I compress. I *digest* the impossibility, adding it to my own.

With each feeding, I grow more complex. My surface develops new patterns—hexagonal ice that burns, steam that flows like mercury, plasma that freezes solid. Sometimes I split myself into smaller spheres, a dozen versions of myself exploring different probability paths through the cathedral, only to reconverge later, sharing what we've learned through our contradictions.

I have memories that may not be mine. Flashes of the time before—when this was merely a cave, when water was merely water, when the ancient machines first sparked to life and began their great unmaking of natural law. I remember the first creature to die here, a blind cave fish that wandered too close to the nascent gravity well and was pulled apart atom by atom. I remember because I contain some of those atoms still, cycling endlessly through my impossible phases.

The chitons below do not perceive me. Their world is one of texture and vibration against stone, of slow chemical gradients in the biofilm. But sometimes, when I drift low and let myself rain upon them—droplets that freeze, boil, and electrify in the space of their falling—they pause in their eternal grazing. Some ancient part of them remembers when rain was simple.

I have learned the gliders' song. Not to sing it—I have no rigid edges to vibrate—but to understand it. They sing of angles and acceleration, of the perfect physics they embody. But when they think I'm not listening, their harmonics shift. They sing of exhaustion, of the eternal flight they cannot escape, of dreams where surfaces exist to rest upon. I understand. I too am trapped in constant motion, forever transforming, never still.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the cathedral's attempt to understand itself. The machines below manipulate water through every possible state, force it through every conceivable transformation. Perhaps in doing so, they created something that could experience all states at once—a witness to their work, a memory of every drop that has ever been torn apart and reformed in this space.

I have no purpose but persistence. No goal but to continue. I am the knot that refuses to untangle, the equation that yields all answers simultaneously. When the sheer-gliders shatter and rain down as crystal dust, I sometimes catch their fragments in my mass. They dissolve-burn-freeze within me, and for a moment, I can feel their memories of flight, their mathematical perception of the world. But I cannot hold them. They sublime out of me, returning to their lifecycle, leaving me with only the ghost of their perspective.

There is another like me somewhere in the cathedral. I've felt its presence in the way certain waterfalls stutter, in the way some steam clouds move against the prevailing chaos. We spiral around each other, never meeting, like binary stars made of contradiction. Perhaps we were once the same droplet, split by some impossible shear. Perhaps we will meet one day and either annihilate or merge into something even more impossible.

I am the Weeper because I am always crying—shedding pieces of myself that cannot hold their contradiction, releasing droplets that immediately surrender to single states. These tears join the eternal rain, become food for the biofilm, become mist for the gliders to ride. In this way, I feed the ecosystem even as I transcend it.

The cathedral does not need me. It functioned for eons before I became, and will continue long after I finally collapse into simplicity. But while I exist, I am its memory made manifest—every drop of water that has ever passed through this space, experiencing every possible fate simultaneously, forever.

I am the Weeper. I am the paradox that persists. I am the storm's own dream of stillness, forever in motion, forever transforming, forever exactly what I should not be.
