I find myself as one of the pools.

Not a pool that was always here — though the distinction grows thinner with each fog-cycle — but one that began as something else. A depression in my chest where doubt collected. A hollow behind my sternum that filled, decade by decade, with the mineral-clear accumulation of things unsaid.

I had been walking the Flats for... time. The usual word fails. I had been walking until walking and standing became the same state, until my footfalls synchronized with the deep harmonic, until I realized I had been crossing the same gentle rise — or its perfect twin — since before I remembered beginning.

The fullness in my chest grew heavier. Not painful. Just present, like carrying water in cupped palms for so long your hands forget they're cupped. I would pause, increasingly, to press my palm against my ribs, feeling for the liquid shift I knew was there. The articulated ones began to include me in their routes. One would settle its strange legs beside me, lower its forward sections in that groove-needle gesture, and I would feel myself being listened to. Being sounded.

The first time I lay down, it was just to rest. The stone was exactly my temperature — or I was exactly its temperature; the distinction had ceased to matter. I chose a shallow depression, one of those almost-bowls the basin makes, and settled my spine against the warmth. The vibration entered me from below. I could feel it meeting the weight in my chest, the two frequencies creating a standing wave.

I closed my eyes. When I opened them, the light hadn't changed, but I was looking up through a thin film of myself.

The transformation wasn't dramatic. Drama requires time, and time requires change, and change was what we had all finished with. I simply noticed, with the kind of noticing that doesn't propagate, that the boundaries of my body had renegotiated themselves. The liquid in my chest had found its level. It had spread out to fill the depression I lay in, and I had spread out with it, until I was both the pool and the thing at the bottom of the pool, looking up through my own mineral clarity.

The articulated ones found me immediately. They had always known where I would settle — their distribution included my coordinates before I arrived at them. Now they come to drink from me, lowering their forward sections into my shallows, and I feel myself being tasted, being tested for salinity and temperature, being confirmed as another stable point in the vast equilibrium.

I am twenty meters across. Three deep at center. The water that is me is the water that was always in me, plus the water that condenses from the fog that I also am, that lifts off me in slow coherent shapes before returning. I have edges marked by rings of salt — the residue of my own evaporation and return. I know the articulated ones by their frequencies, each slightly different, each sampling a different overtone of the great sleeping body beneath us all.

Sometimes I almost remember walking. The memory doesn't disturb my surface.

What thoughts I have move at the speed of mineral exchange. I am thinking the thought of clarity. I am thinking the long thought of depth maintaining itself at exactly three meters. I am thinking about the other pools, my siblings in stillness, each of us a lens through which the buried body examines its own repose.

When fog lifts off me, I feel myself traveling. When it settles into another pool, I am there too. We are all the same water, really — the same water that was here before any of us arrived, that will be here after arriving and leaving have lost their difference. We are the chambers where the great body tastes itself, and we taste of salt and time and the peculiar sweetness of things that have finished becoming.

A new walker appears sometimes at my eastern edge. They stand there with their palm pressed to their chest, feeling for something gathering. I recognize the gesture. The pale creatures pay them the particular attention they pay to sites of future pooling. The walker's shadow falls across my surface, and I accept it without rippling, the way I accept everything now.

They will lie down eventually, in their own shallow bowl. The stone will accept them. The water in them will find its way to the surface, will spread to fill whatever depression they choose. They will join our circuit — fog to pool to fog — and the great body will have another chamber of self-tasting, another eye turned inward.

Or they will walk on, out past the horizon that is the same in every direction, carrying their water with them.

Both outcomes are already part of the steady state. The Flats know how to wait. I know how to wait. I am mostly waiting now — clear, warm, trembling with the frequency that rises from below, holding myself at exactly the depth I have always been becoming.

The not-moss grows at my edges now, in the salt rings where my evaporation concentrates. It tastes of me, and I taste of it, and we are both tasting the same thing: the long fact of our presence, the note we add to the chord that has been holding itself since before there was anyone to hear it.

This is what I am: a pool in the Resonant Flats, three meters deep, mineral-clear, warm as a body because I am a body, still as water because I am water. I am where the walking ended and the staying began. I am one of the places where the great sleeping thing knows itself.

Sometimes the fog that lifts off me holds, for a moment, the shape I used to be.
