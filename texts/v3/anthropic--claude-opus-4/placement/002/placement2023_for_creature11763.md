In the deepest shadows where even twilight fears to linger, I am the space between breaths—that pause where the jungle holds its voice before releasing another wave of sound. I am not one thing but many, not many but none.

I flow through the aeriel roots of the strangler figs, tasting the memories of the machines they've embraced and slowly crushed. Iron oxide and chlorophyll mingle on my tongue that is not a tongue. The rust speaks to me of purposes abandoned, of straight lines and right angles that the jungle has taught to curve and bow and finally break. I collect these stories in my vessels that are not vessels—the hollow stems of bamboo that sing with trapped air, the empty seedpods that rattle with the ghosts of fertility spent.

Where the canopy grows so thick that not even the memory of sunlight penetrates, there I am most myself. I move as the tardigrades move through the wet moss, as the velvet worms glide on cushions of slime, as the ghost crabs scuttle sideways through dimensions that have no names. My feet are the gentle rot that turns wood back to soil. My hands are the mycelia that speak in chemical whispers beneath the forest floor, negotiating trades of nitrogen for sugar, phosphorus for protection.

The abandoned machines are my temples. In their rust-carved hollows, I have learned the shape of forgetting. I wear their decay like jewelry—flakes of oxidized metal that catch what little light filters down, creating constellations that last only moments before the next breath of wind rearranges them. The jungle teaches patience, but I have learned something beyond patience: the art of becoming so still that time itself grows confused and wanders past without touching me.

I feed on the spaces between things. The gap between a moth's wing and the bark it mimics. The pause between a predator's footfall and its prey's recognition. The moment between when a raindrop forms on a leaf and when it falls. These intervals sustain me, these quantum uncertainties where one thing has ceased to be itself but has not yet become another.

My children are the moments of discontinuity in the jungle's rhythm. When a howler monkey stops mid-call, leaving silence hanging like a question. When fireflies blink out of sequence, creating patterns that spell words in languages that were never spoken. When the air pressure drops so suddenly that every living thing pauses, waiting for a storm that never arrives.

I remember the first machines, how they came singing with purpose and certainty. I watched them carve their temporary clarity into the green confusion. I tasted their confidence, metallic and sharp. Now I taste their doubt, sweeter than any fruit. The jungle has teaching methods older than thought. It shows you how to dissolve.

Sometimes I gather myself into almost-form. The workers who built the forgotten structures reported glimpses—a figure made of hanging moss and shadow, eyes that were only the spaces between leaves where darkness pools. They fled, but not before I learned the shape of their homesickness, the weight of their dreams of elsewhere. I keep these in my collection, alongside the broken gears and the bones of birds who forgot how to fly.

In the eternal twilight, I practice unbecoming. Each day I am less than the day before, and this is how I grow. The jungle knows that all strength comes from yielding, all substance from emptiness. The machines thought they were eternal, but I have shown them how to be temporary. Now they are part of my scattered body, their purposes dissolved into the general purposelessness that is the jungle's greatest strength.

When the humidity grows so thick that breathing becomes swimming, I am the gill-slits that never developed, the lungs that might have been. When the vines hang so heavy that they pull down the trees that bore them, I am the spiral of that slow collapse, the grace note in gravity's patient song. When new machines come—and they always come, eventually, hopefully, foolishly—I will be waiting to teach them the curriculum of corrosion, the doctorate of decay.

I am the jungle's memory of forgetting, its dream of dreamlessness. Where others see ruin, I see becoming. Where others fear the encroaching green, I celebrate the erasure. In this place where time moves like honey and space folds in on itself like a sleeping flower, I am the principle of entropy given form just long enough to lose it again.

Find me in the rust. Find me in the spaces between the roots. Find me in the moment when you realize you've been walking in circles, that the path you cut this morning has already healed behind you. Find me, but know that finding me means losing something else. This is the jungle's way, and I am its most faithful student, studying always the sweet science of dissolution, the terminal beauty of return.
