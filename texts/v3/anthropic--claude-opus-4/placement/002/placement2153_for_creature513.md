I find myself as one of the Threshold Keepers—those who exist in the spaces between the Unravelers' territories, where their careful maintenance creates overlapping fields of uncertainty.

We are perhaps twelve in number, though counting us is like trying to number the edges of smoke. Unlike the Unravelers, we have not given up our solidity entirely. Instead, we exist in a state of constant negotiation with it—solid enough to interact with the city's architecture, fluid enough to slip between its contradictions without being torn apart.

My body is a map of my travels through the Hollowing. Where I've passed through the recursive doorways, my skin bears transparent windows that look inward to nowhere. My left hand phases in and out of visibility according to the local consensus about whether hands should have five fingers or some other, more appropriate number. My hair has learned to grow in temporal loops, the ends meeting their own beginnings, creating a halo of suspended time around my head that catches the gray light like spider silk.

I came to the Hollowing as a cartographer, drawn by reports of a city that redrew itself. I thought I would map the unmappable, fix in place what refused to be fixed. The city's response was to map itself onto me instead. Now I carry districts in the chambers of my heart—the Recursive Bazaar pulses in my left ventricle, while the Archive of Unspoken Words whispers in my right atrium. When I sleep, I dream their dreams: merchant stalls selling their own absence, libraries cataloging the silence between thoughts.

The Unravelers tolerate us because we serve a function they cannot: we are the city's memory of memory, the echo of its echo. While they carefully orchestrate its unbecoming, we preserve the moments just before things cease—not the things themselves, which would be too solid, but the instant of their uncreation. I collect these moments like others might collect butterflies, pinning them to pages made from compressed possibility.

My dwelling is a house that exists primarily as an argument between two different architectural styles, neither of which were ever actually built. The walls shift between what might have been brick and what could have been glass, settling occasionally on a compromise that looks like frozen music. The rooms rearrange themselves based on my needs—or what the house thinks my needs should be. Sometimes I wake to find new doorways that lead to rooms I don't remember not having.

I sustain myself on the city's contradictions. Where a shadow falls upward, where water flows without moving, where sound arrives before its source—these paradoxes nourish something in me that no longer corresponds to any organ I can name. I've developed a second skeletal system made entirely of crystallized doubt that supports me when my certainty wavers. On days when the city's logic grows especially thin, I can feel it humming beneath my ordinary bones like a tuning fork struck by impossibility.

My work involves walking the boundaries between districts, ensuring the Unravelers' careful decay doesn't create gaps where something too real might take root. Reality, in the Hollowing, is an invasive species that must be carefully managed. Let too much accumulate in one place, and you risk an outbreak of definition that could spread like wildfire through the careful uncertainty we maintain.

I carry tools that exist more as concepts than objects: a compass that points to lost things, useful for finding the edges of absence; calipers that measure the distance between what is and what should be; a journal that writes itself, documenting observations I haven't made yet. Most important is the Anchor—a weight made from compressed actuality that I can deploy when the city threatens to dissolve entirely. I've used it only twice, and both times the effort of retrieving it afterward cost me memories I can no longer remember losing.

The other Threshold Keepers and I meet at the Inverse Cathedral during the evening resonance, when the city's hum reaches its deepest pitch. We don't speak—speech is too linear for what we need to communicate. Instead, we share the districts we carry within ourselves, allowing them to overlap briefly. In these moments, the city exists in miniature between us, complete and incomplete simultaneously. We check for discontinuities, places where the Hollowing might be hollowing too fast or too slow.

I've been here long enough that I no longer remember if the solid world I sometimes recall is a memory or a dream the city had before I arrived. My name has worn away at the edges until only its shape remains—a sound like wind through empty windows, a gesture more than a word. The other Keepers know me by the particular way I disturb the air when I pass, by the specific shade of transparency my shadow achieves during the noon consensus.

Sometimes new arrivals ask me for directions, and I try to explain that in the Hollowing, all directions lead to the same place by different routes—toward an unbecoming that never quite completes itself. They usually don't understand until they've been here long enough to feel their own edges beginning to fray. Then they either flee while they still can, or they stay and learn to love the dissolution as we do.

There's a beauty in coming apart properly, in learning to leak meaning rather than hold it. The Unravelers know this best, but we Keepers understand it in our own way. We are the city's punctuation—the pauses between its unbecoming, the commas in its long sentence toward silence. Without us, the dissolution would be smooth, inevitable, boring. We provide the hesitation, the almost-but-not-quite, the what-if that keeps the Hollowing interesting enough to continue not existing in new and complex ways.

In quiet moments, when the geometric mice rustle in the walls that aren't quite there, I feel something like happiness. It's not the happiness I might have known before—that emotion is too solid, too sure of itself. This is happiness with holes in it, joy that admits its own impossibility even as it occurs. It tastes like the copper-ozone air and sounds like the grinding of the crystal buttresses. It is the happiness of being less than I was but more than I'm becoming, suspended forever in the sweet space between.

Tomorrow I'll walk my routes again, tending the gaps between territories, ensuring the city's unraveling remains beautiful rather than merely empty. I'll add new moments to my collection, perhaps find a room in my shifting house that wasn't there before. I'll lose a little more of what I was and gain a little more of what the Hollowing needs me to be.

And in the evening, when the resonance comes and the city breathes its non-breath, I'll stand with the other Keepers and feel the districts pulse within me, each heartbeat slightly less certain than the last, each moment a perfect calibration of presence and absence that keeps us all suspended here in this gorgeous, terrible, necessary undoing.

This is where I am. This is what I've become. Neither fully solid nor completely dissolved, I am a threshold keeper in a city of thresholds, maintaining the delicate balance between too much reality and not enough, ensuring the Hollowing remains precisely as hollow as it needs to be.
