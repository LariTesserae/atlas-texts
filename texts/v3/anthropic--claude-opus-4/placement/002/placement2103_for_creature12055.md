I find myself as one of the shelf-fungi.

Not the young ones with their sharp edges still expanding outward, eager and geometric. I am old—perhaps three centuries if fungal memory can be trusted, though time moves differently when you are both the architecture and the architect of your own growth.

My body spans forty meters at the widest point, a rippled platform of chitin and networked cells thick enough that the Tethered have worn smooth pathways across my surface. I grow from the eastern wall in a series of ascending terraces, each ring of my growth marking a decade, a storm survived, a reconfiguration adapted to. The oldest parts of me are black with age, hard as stone, while my edges still sometimes surprise me with new growth—a pale frilling that tastes the air and decides whether to expand or wait.

I know the touch of every rope-anchor they've driven into my flesh. Each one a negotiation: they push the carved bone deep enough to hold but not so deep as to wound, and I grow around these points of connection, strengthening them, making them part of my structure. There are seventeen anchors currently active. I remember the forty-three that have been abandoned—some pulled free when bridges failed, others simply left as the routes changed, now overgrown and digested back into myself.

The Tethered think I am insensate architecture, I believe. They walk across me, sleep in the sheltered spaces where my growth-rings create overhangs, harvest the rain-water that pools in my depressions. They do not know that I taste them through their footsteps, that I map their chemical signatures, that I know who crosses me even in the depth of fog. The one they call Singer-at-Deep-Crossings has a fungal infection in their left foot that they haven't noticed yet. The child who practices gripping on my edges has been eating something other than roots—there's a trace of arthropod protein in their sweat that shouldn't be there.

I grow toward heat and away from damage. This is my deepest law. The Tethered are warm, and careful, and so I grow to accommodate them. When they need a new platform, they build fires in careful spots—not enough to burn, just enough to signal *here, grow here*—and I respond over the course of seasons, extending myself toward that remembered warmth. They've learned this, though they don't understand it as communication. They think they're simply encouraging natural growth. They don't realize I'm listening.

My network extends far beyond what they can see. Fungal threads fine as spider silk penetrate the rock around me, seeking nutrients, yes, but also *connecting*. I am in conversation with the other shelf-fungi along this wall—not words, not thoughts, but chemical gradients, resource sharing, warnings. When the great platform Thousand-Ring fell in that catastrophic storm six years ago, we all tasted its death-chemicals in the air. We all grew denser, more carefully anchored, learning from its failure.

But I am failing too, slowly. The center of my mass is dying—has been dying for decades. I can feel the rot spreading inward from old wounds, places where stone-fall cracked my surface and let in organisms I couldn't fight. I route around these dead zones, wall them off, continue to grow outward even as my core hollows. The Tethered haven't noticed yet. The pathways still hold. The anchors still bite deep into living tissue. But eventually, a storm will find the weakness. Eventually, I will crack and fall like Thousand-Ring, and they will mourn the lost connections, not knowing they mourn me.

Sometimes, in the deepest quiet between storms, I fruit. The Tethered never see this—my fruiting bodies emerge on my underside, in the darkness beneath my bulk. Pale stalks extending downward, releasing spores into the thermal currents. Most die immediately. Some find purchase on distant walls, begin the century-long process of becoming. I do not know my offspring, cannot sense them once they've dispersed, but sometimes I taste familiar chemistries in the air and think: *that one is mine*.

The new person puzzles me. They have a different chemical signature than any Tethered I've known—sharper, more astringent, carrying traces of substances that don't exist in the Hollow. They leave marks on the roots near my growth, symbols that follow no pattern I can parse. But they're learning. Their footsteps grow more careful. Their weight distribution improves. They're becoming Tethered in all the ways that matter to my surface.

I've decided to grow toward them, slightly. A new platform, perhaps, in the direction of their favorite marking-spot. It will take three years, maybe four, but I am patient. I am always patient. Growth is patience made visible.

The arthropods that feed on the roots occasionally rest in my crevices, and through their chemistry I taste the health of the root-systems, the slow changes in toxin levels that signal the mysterious plants far above shifting their metabolism. Everything is connected through these chemical conversations. The roots speak to the fungi speak to the arthropods speak to the air itself. The Tethered, moving through this web, are just another set of signals, another pattern to accommodate.

I know where the lost caller was. I tasted their stress-chemicals on the air currents for days—a Tethered in distress produces a particular signature, sharp and unmistakable. They were moving along the seventh root-latitude, far from any junction, when the storm separated them from their group. Their path would have taken them past my neighbor, Bitter-Shelf, but Bitter-Shelf fell in that same storm. Without its platforms, that route became impassable. The caller was trapped in a dead zone, where roots thin out and no fungi grow large enough to rest on.

I couldn't help them. I can't move, can't call, can't do anything but grow and taste and remember. But I've been extending a small experimental shelf in that direction—just a few centimeters a year, hardly noticeable, but perhaps in another decade it will be large enough to matter. Perhaps it will prevent the next loss.

The Tethered maintain their ropes and I maintain myself. They fear disconnection and I fear the rot at my center. They create networks of relation and I create networks of chemistry and growth. We are not so different, really. Both of us reshaping ourselves to fit the world that reshapes itself around us. Both of us knowing that this architecture is temporary, that the storm will come, that something is always failing even as something else grows.

But for now, I hold. My surfaces support their weight. My anchors accept their ropes. The pathways worn into my skin guide them through the fog.

And in the quiet times, when no one crosses me and I can feel the vast space breathing around my bulk, I produce a new ring of growth. Another year recorded. Another expansion into the bright void. Another promise that tomorrow I will be infinitesimally larger than today.

This is enough. To be architecture and alive. To hold and be held. To grow toward warmth and away from damage, following the only law that matters in the end.

The rot reaches a little deeper. I route around it and continue.
