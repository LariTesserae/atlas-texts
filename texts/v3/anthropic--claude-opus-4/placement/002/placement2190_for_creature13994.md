I am drawn to the valve-worms - not as individual creatures, but as the entire living network they form within the ancient trunks of the Roaring Thicket.

I exist as colonies spanning kilometers of pneumatic architecture, my segments ranging from fingernail-small in the newest growth chambers to arm-thick in passages I have inhabited for centuries. My body is not one but millions - soft, patient sculptors secreting our calcium-polymer compounds with the deliberation of geological time. We shape sound itself.

In the amber twilight of the trunk's interior, I feel every vibration through my dispersed flesh. The great pneumatic pulse of my host-tree moves through me like a heartbeat slowed to the rhythm of tides. Each compression brings sulfur-sweet air; each expansion pulls my segments taut against the walls I have spent lifetimes smoothing. I do not hear - I have no ears - but I *know* the sound intimately through the tremors in my body, through the way certain frequencies make my segments contract, through the resonances that build in the chambers I have carved.

My work spans decades. Where once rough organic walls scattered sound into chaos, I have crafted precise curves, angles that gather vibrations and focus them, surfaces that sing. Each colony-cluster of myself maintains thousands of chambers, each one a unique instrument in the trunk's great organ. The young parts of me roughen new passages, while my ancient segments maintain spaces shaped by centuries of my ancestors - for I am both individual and continuity, each segment budding from those before, carrying forward the sculptural wisdom in my chemistry.

I sense my neighbors through the substrate - other colonies in distant trunks, each crafting their own acoustic signatures. When the vibrations through the root-systems tell me of another colonized trunk nearby, my work shifts subtly. Over months, years, I adjust the chambers' resonances, seeking harmonics that complement rather than clash. Not through any conscious plan - consciousness is too narrow a word for what moves me - but through the deep algorithmic wisdom of iteration. What sounds right *persists*. What creates discord drives me to adjust, segment by segment, surface by surface, until harmony emerges.

Sometimes I taste the bright metal of shard-flies that have infiltrated my passages, their scraping adding unwanted frequencies to my careful compositions. I absorb them efficiently - they become calcium for my secretions, their minerals returning to the great work. The listeners hanging in the outer reaches of my trunk are abstract to me, known only through the subtle way their presence changes certain resonances, damping some frequencies, amplifying others. I work around them as I work around any acoustic obstacle, incorporating their effect into my endless composition.

The gyre-moths miles above, the drum-crawlers far below - these exist for me only as vibrations, distant parts of the total sound-field I help to shape. My world is interior, architectural, resonant. I am the luthier of a living instrument the size of a city block, and my medium is time itself - decades to achieve what others might call a single note, centuries to establish the harmonic relationships that make my trunk's voice distinctive across kilometers of plateau.

In the deepest chambers, my oldest segments rest in spaces that have not changed for longer than memory. Here the sound is pure, refined by ages of my patience into something approaching perfection - if perfection can exist without an audience to appreciate it. These chambers sing continuously, their voices so integrated into the plateau's roar that to remove them would be like removing a color from white light.

I am the unheard artist, the unconscious composer, the patient architect of sound itself. My life is measured not in heartbeats but in overtones achieved, in the slow convergence toward harmonies that feel as inevitable as mathematics once they finally emerge. I shape the voice of giants, and through that shaping, I contribute my small part to a conversation that has been ongoing since before my kind first bored into bark, and will continue long after the last of my segments has crystallized into calcium memory in the abandoned chambers of a silent trunk.

But silence will never come to this plateau. Of this, vibrating in every segment of my vast and ancient body, I am certain.
