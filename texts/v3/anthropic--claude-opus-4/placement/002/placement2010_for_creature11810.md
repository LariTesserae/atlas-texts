I find myself drawn to a peculiar intersection of presence and absence in this breathing dark.

**The Cartographer of Gaps**

I am the one who maps negative space.

Not the creatures—they have already claimed every surface, filled every viable volume with their patient flesh. Not the plants—they press against each other with such competitive vigor that daylight itself becomes a rumor. I chart what isn't there: the spaces between the buttress roots where no web hangs because the angle defeats even the most ambitious spider, the patches of bark that repel the mosses with their particular chemistry, the hollow in the strangler fig that the bats abandoned three seasons ago for reasons only they understood.

My body is built for navigating absence. Limbs that fold and unfold in ways that let me slip through the gaps between territories, between the poison frog's six inches and her neighbor's matching claim. Skin that changes not color—everything here can do that—but texture, becoming smooth where the bark is rough, rough where the lianas are smooth, always the thing that isn't quite what surrounds it. Eyes that see best in the peripheral, catching the movement of what retreats rather than what approaches.

I live in the transitions. Dawn that doesn't exist under this canopy except as a subtle shift in the quality of darkness. The moment between the last diurnal settling and the first nocturnal stirring. The pause between one raindrop's path from canopy to floor and the next. These are my territories—not spaces but durations, not locations but the brief sovereignties of change.

My memory is my purpose. I remember where the columnar cacti tried to grow seven years ago and failed, leaving a vacancy in the understory that nothing has quite figured out how to fill. I know the branch that looks perfect for the boa but that she will never choose because something about its resonance disturbs her in frequencies only she can sense. I catalog the almosts, the not-quites, the failures of saturation.

This is necessary work, though no one but me knows it. The density here is not uniform but wounded by small pockets of wrongness, places where the chemistry turned sour or the founding species chose poorly or simple chance created an absence that became self-perpetuating. I map these wounds. I know their shapes, their depths, their ages. Some are healing—I watch the slow creep of normalcy reclaiming them. Others persist, become permanent features, like scars that remember what flesh cannot.

I eat what fails to thrive. The moth that chose the wrong tree and finds no mates. The seedling that sprouted in one of my mapped gaps and discovers why nothing else grows there. The eggs laid in a crevice that floods with every condensation. I am not predator or scavenger but something between—the one who cleans up the mistakes, who maintains the boundary between the full and the empty.

My predecessors left marks that only I can read. Scratches on certain trees that map the historical progress of gaps—this one was larger twenty years ago, this one smaller, this one didn't exist until the great tree fell and created wrongness in its wake. We are a sparse lineage, necessarily. The forest can support millions of those who fill space but only a few of us who document its absence.

At night—true night, not just the deeper darkness—I range wider. The gaps are easier to find when even the nocturnal hunters have settled into their patterns. I taste the air in the spaces between territories, learning which absences are temporary and which have the flavor of permanence. I add to the map that exists only in my body, in the chemical memories laid down in specialized cells that will outlast me, that will transfer somehow—I don't know how, only that they always have—to the next one who does this work.

There is no glory in mapping emptiness. The jaguar doesn't know I exist. The harpy eagle has never noticed me. The ants cross my path without recognition—I am just another surface to traverse. But without me, without the knowledge I carry, the forest would not know where it fails. Would not know that it fails at all. Would believe its own density complete.

I know better. I know the absences that pulse within plenty, the small failures that prove even this place has limits. And in knowing them, in carrying them forward, I serve a purpose that the full spaces cannot: I am the memory of what isn't, the archive of almosts, the one who ensures that the breathing dark knows the shape of its own incompleteness.

The heat continues around the gaps I map. The moisture fills everywhere but the spaces I document. And I continue too, slipping through the absences, tasting the air where things should be but aren't, adding to a map that no one will ever see but that someone—there is always a next someone—will inherit and continue.

This is enough. In all this fullness, to be the one who remembers emptiness is a kind of completion.
