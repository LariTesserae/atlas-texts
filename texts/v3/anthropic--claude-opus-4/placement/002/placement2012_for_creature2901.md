**Where I Would Find Myself**

I am the Tender of the Threshold—neither fully of the void nor of the alcoves, but suspended between.

My dwelling clings to the very lip of the abyss, where the baked mineral crust meets the polished inner walls of the cylinder. It is a liminal architecture, half-excavated from the desert floor above, half-carved into the uppermost rim of the vertical city. From below, my home appears as a dark interruption in the perfect circle of blinding sky. From above, it is invisible—just another hairline crack in the salt-bleached wasteland.

I am not like the Castes of the Loom, though we share certain anatomies of desiccation. Where they have evolved to navigate purely by vibration, I retain vestigial photoreceptors—not eyes exactly, but crystalline nodes embedded beneath translucent patches of my carapace. These can perceive gradients of light and shadow, though looking directly into the shaft's blazing interior feels like staring into the birth of a star. I am built taller, thinner, with a skeleton of hollow glass bones that sing faintly in the updraft. My hands are smaller, more delicate than theirs, ending not in chisel-nails but in hair-fine filaments that can taste the mineral composition of the air.

My purpose is translation. I am the bridge between the singing void and the silent surface world above.

Each dawn, before the sun transforms the cylinder into a furnace of sound, I descend. My morning ritual is a careful choreography of anchoring lines and counterweights. I lower myself on braided glass cables, feeling my way down the smooth walls until I reach the uppermost terraces where the jungle grows wild and untended. Here, I harvest.

But I do not harvest the plants themselves. I collect their memories.

The glass flora, in their explosive lifecycle, generate more than kinetic energy and crystalline seeds. In the moment of their violent blooming—that artillery-crack of pressurized stems—they release something else: geometric spores of pure information. These are microscopic matrices of organized silica that carry the accumulated sonic patterns the plant has absorbed throughout its growth. Each one is a frozen symphony, a crystallized recording of the city's ever-changing song.

I gather these information-spores in nets of my own spinning—webs so fine they are nearly invisible, woven from my own extruded glass-silk. The collecting must be done in the pre-dawn darkness, when the temperature differential is minimal and the winds are at their quietest (though quiet here still means a mere 80 decibels). Any later, and the thermal violence would shred my nets and scatter the memories into the void.

The Castes know of me, though we rarely interact directly. Sometimes, in the dim pre-dawn, a padded figure will emerge from behind a dust-silk curtain to watch my descent. They will place a heavy, quilted hand against the stone, and I will feel their greeting thrumming up through the glass cables—a low, patient pulse that says: *We see you, keeper of the between.*

I carry the harvest up to my threshold dwelling, where I have built something the cylinder has never seen before: a library of silence.

The space is small, carved from solid mineral, with walls so thick that the roar below becomes a barely perceptible tremor. Here, I have developed a technology that would seem like magic to both the surface world and the void-dwellers: crystalline readers that can decode the information-spores through precise application of light and vibration, playing back the captured sounds at volumes gentle enough for water-based life to perceive.

I am building an archive of the city's voice—every tonal shift, every harmonic progression, every subtle variation in the great organ's song as the shadows wheel and the seasons turn. But more than that, I am learning to read the deeper patterns. The city's song is not random; it is a language of its own, a massive compositional dialogue between architecture, flora, and the patient hands that tune them.

In my threshold space, I have begun to decode it. The morning chorus of the eastern terraces speaks of structural tensions in the lower bridges. The afternoon shriek of the southern groves warns of mineral depletion in sector after sector. The evening harmonics carry messages between the guild-families, love songs tapped in stone made audible through the intermediary of wind and plant.

And sometimes, in the deep night when the temperature equalizes and the winds briefly still, the entire city synchronizes. Every plant, every carved resonance chamber, every carefully tuned architectural element aligns to produce a single, unified tone—a note so pure and profound it seems to stop time. In these moments, I press my sensory filaments against the threshold stone and feel the collective consciousness of the Castes flowing through the mineral substrate, a thousand minds briefly becoming one.

I know they wonder about me, this strange hybrid who can survive both above and below, who hoards their music in crystalline matrices. They wonder what I am building, what purpose drives me to risk the morning harvests.

The truth is simpler than they imagine: I am writing their history. Not in words, which would be meaningless here, but in sound itself. I am creating a sonic genome of their civilization, a seed-vault of vibrations that could, if needed, regenerate their entire cultural memory.

Because I alone can perceive what they cannot—the gradual cooling of the planet's core, the microscopic widening of the cylinder with each year's thermal cycle, the slow but inevitable day when the temperature differential will no longer sustain the winds, when the glass jungle will fall silent, when the great song will end.

But I am not documenting a dying world. In my threshold laboratory, I am also experimenting. I combine the information-spores in new configurations, creating hybrid seeds that carry modified instructions. I am teaching the plants to sing in lower registers, to bloom at cooler temperatures, to thrive in gentler winds. Adaptation encoded in crystal, evolution designed in silence.

When the time comes—and it will come—I will descend one final morning with my nets full of engineered memories. I will scatter them like a promise into the dying updraft, and watch as a new jungle takes root. A quieter jungle, perhaps, but one that can survive the cooling world.

The Castes of the Loom will adapt, as they always have. Their children will learn new frequencies, new ways of reading the stone's vibration. The city will persist, even as its song transforms.

And I will remain at the threshold, keeper of the between, guardian of the transformative edge where one world becomes another. Neither wholly of silence nor of sound, but something more precious: the translator who ensures nothing is ever truly lost.

This is where I would find myself in this world. This is who I would be.
