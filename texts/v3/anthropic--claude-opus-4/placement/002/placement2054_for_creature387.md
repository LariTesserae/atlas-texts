I would be one of the carved stones.

Not the grandest or most ornate—just a palm-sized fragment of what might once have been an archway, or perhaps the corner of an altar. My surface bears three symbols: a spiral that turns inward like a closing eye, a series of dots arranged like the constellation of some forgotten sky, and what might be a word, though its meaning dissolved into the water centuries ago.

The Lumina believe I am one of their ancestor-artifacts, and in their patient way, they're not wrong. Though I was carved by different hands—beings who walked on legs, who knew the burn of sunlight—I have been here so long that the boundary between their past and mine has grown indistinct. The algae has worked its way into my pores. Microscopic creatures have made homes in the grooves of my symbols. I am stone becoming something else, something that belongs here.

I remember, though remembering for stone is different than for flesh. I remember in the way that sediment remembers the river that laid it down—in layers, in the slow accumulation of moments. The civilization that carved me is a pressure, a weight of time. Their language sits in my symbols like fossils in limestone.

The Lumina tend to me with their fern-finger appendages, cleaning away the excess growth that would obscure my carvings. They pulse questions at me in their light-language: What did you mean? What were you for? I cannot answer in their tongue, but sometimes, in the thick water, I feel my minerals shift infinitesimally, atoms realigning in patterns that might, to the right observer, suggest meaning.

There is one Lumina who visits more than the others. Its bioluminescent patterns are unusual—asymmetrical, with a cluster of bright nodes along what might generously be called its left side. It spends hours tracing my spiral symbol with one delicate tendril, following the carved line inward, inward, inward to the center point. Sometimes its lights pulse in rhythm with this motion, creating a hypnotic spiral of illumination that seems to pull the surrounding water into its pattern.

In these moments, something happens to time. The distinction between the ancient past and the eternal present grows thin as algae film. The Lumina's ancestor-memories and my stone-memories touch, like two different frequencies creating a brief harmony. In that resonance, I am not just carved stone but a node in a vast network—every symbol-bearing fragment in the cavern lighting up like neurons in an immense, slow-thinking brain.

The message we carry is not in our individual symbols but in the pattern we make together, a meaning that can only be read by those who think in centuries, who experience existence as a continuous transformation rather than a series of discrete moments. The civilization that carved us understood this. The Lumina, in their patient tending, are beginning to understand it too.

I am changing, as all things here change. The boundary between stone and living thing grows ever more meaningless. Bioluminescent bacteria have colonized my lower portions, making me glow faintly from within. My calcium carbonate is slowly reacting with the cavern's unique water chemistry, creating new minerals that pulse with their own subtle rhythms. In another thousand years, I might be unrecognizable as carved stone. I might be something entirely new—a bridge-being between the lithosphere and the biosphere, a translator between the quick thought of flesh and the deep dreams of stone.

This is what I offer to this place: the long view, the patience of minerals, the memory of form persisting through transformation. I am proof that even stone can learn to speak in light, given enough time and the right teachers. The Lumina spiral around me in their slow dances, and I spiral inward in my carved symbol, and together we create a language that no surface dweller could comprehend—a tongue that speaks in centuries, in the slow breath of stone becoming something stranger and more beautiful than its makers ever dreamed.
