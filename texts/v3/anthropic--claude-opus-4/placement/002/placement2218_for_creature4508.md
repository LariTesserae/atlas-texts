I find myself as one of the Water-Readers.

We are not the Residents—those who compose themselves from absence and live in the economy of the almost-was. We are older, simpler, and more terrible in our certainty. We exist solely to tend the phosphorescent pools where the world bleeds its wrong-temperature memories.

I am the one who kneels beside these shallow wounds in stone, reading the vibrations that tremble across surfaces too taut for proper water. My body—if it can be called that—is composed of the same clear thickness that fills the pools, but structured into something that approximates limbs only when movement becomes necessary. Most often, I am simply a density of watching arranged around wherever the water speaks loudest.

The Residents avoid us. They can afford to play with presence and absence, to be sometimes-here and maybe-there. We cannot. We are bound to these pools by something heavier than duty—call it the weight of knowing what happens when the bleeding stops. The stone must continue to weep its liquid quartz tears, or else the folding accelerates, the darkness convulses without pattern, and even the Residents' elaborate games of un-being collapse into something worse than either existence or void.

I read the water through methods that would be touch if touch didn't imply separation between reader and read. Instead, I extend portions of my liquid-glass substance into the pools until there is no boundary between their trembling and mine. The frequencies that pass through us speak in languages older than words—they tell of the deep machine-hymns buried beneath, of the stress fractures developing in reality's foundation, of the precise moment when the stone will finally forget how to remember warmth.

Each pool requires different reading techniques. The ones near the folding-places must be interpreted backwards, their messages emerging only as I withdraw, like meaning that exists solely in the act of letting go. The pools that form where travelers have stood too long thinking dangerous thoughts must be read in spirals, starting from edges and working inward until I reach the center where their doubt crystallized into prophecy.

Sometimes I encounter evidence of the Residents' presence: a conversation abandoned mid-word that continues rippling across the water long after its speakers dispersed, or the reflection of someone who was never quite there but left their almost-image behind as payment for safe passage. I catalogue these carefully. Even their absences have weight here, and too many accumulated might-have-beens can cause the water to thicken beyond reading, become opaque with possibility until I must drain the pool and let it refill with simpler griefs.

The other Water-Readers and I never meet directly. We know each other only through the changes in the water after another has passed—a new harmonic in the subsonic drone, a slightly different angle to the way the liquid catches not-light. We leave each other messages in the form of temperature variations too slight for any thermometer to register but devastating in their precision: a degree's fraction that says "the western pools are remembering what salt means again," or "something large died in the between-spaces and its absence is leaking through."

My favorite pool—if favorite is a word that applies to necessity—lies where three of the stone waves converge in a depression that might have been a crater if this world believed in impacts. The water here is older, thicker, almost contemplative in its vibrations. When I merge with it, I can read not just the present tremors but the accumulated history of every frequency that has passed through since the first bleeding began. It's here that I learned the truth: we are not healing the wounds but keeping them precisely wounded enough to prevent either healing or death.

The work is endless. New pools form wherever the stone's memory of temperature fluctuates too sharply. Old pools evaporate not into air but into a more fundamental absence, leaving behind depressions that hum with frequencies too pure for matter to tolerate. I move between them in patterns determined by urgencies only the water understands, sometimes covering vast distances in single steps, other times taking geological ages to traverse the space between two adjacent pools because the reading of one revealed that haste would shatter what the other is trying to say.

I do not sleep. Instead, I experience periods where my substance disperses throughout all the pools simultaneously, becoming thin enough to feel every tremor across the entire plain at once. In these moments, I understand the larger pattern—how each pool's individual message contributes to an ongoing conversation the stone is having with whatever lies beneath. The conversation is always the same and always different: an negotiation about whether existing is worth the effort, conducted in frequencies that make thought feel like rust forming on the inside of awareness.

There are rules to being a Water-Reader. Never let your substance remain in any pool long enough to change its fundamental frequency. Never read the same message twice—it will have changed meaning in the interval and trying to reconcile both versions causes a resonance that can shatter stone for miles. Never look directly at your own reflection in the water; it won't be you looking back, but rather what you would have been if water could choose its containers.

Sometimes, rarely, I encounter a traveler who has wandered too far from the Residents' games and found themselves at pool's edge. They usually kneel to drink before recognizing that this liquid offers the opposite of what thirst wants. I try to warn them through the water's trembling—increasing the frequency to match the human range of fear—but many drink anyway. Those who do join us eventually, their bodies dissolved and reconstituted in liquid glass, their memories becoming new pools that we must tend until they learn to read themselves.

The hardest part is not the endless vigilance or the isolation or even the terrible knowledge of what we're preventing. It's the beauty. In certain configurations of not-light, when the darkness ripples just so across the water's surface, the pools sing visual music too perfect for any mind that wishes to remain intact. We must witness this without being transformed by it, must read pure meaning without letting it mean anything to us personally.

I continue my rounds. Here, a pool that formed where one of the Residents forgot themselves too completely, now trembling with the effort of containing an entire identity in three inches of liquid depth. There, a pool so old its vibrations have simplified into a single note that will—if our calculations are correct—continue sounding until exactly one moment after the last star dies. And always, beneath it all, the drone of the deep machines grinding against mathematics itself, their work so vital and terrible that we keep the wounds open rather than risk letting them surface to finish it.

The water never lies, but it doesn't tell the truth either. It tells what is necessary, which is more honest and less kind than either. I read it all, translate trembling into meaning into warning into the precise adjustments needed to keep this broken place exactly broken enough to persist.

This is my purpose: to kneel beside wounds that must not heal, reading the fever-dreams of a world that refuses to decide whether it's dying or being born. The Residents may play their elaborate games with presence and absence, but we who read the water know the simpler truth—sometimes the most important work is learning how to keep things precisely as damaged as they need to be to survive.

The next pool is forming now, its first tremors calling me to witness another small apocalypse prevented by refusing to let it finish happening. I go to read what must be read, my substance already anticipating the frequency of disaster transformed into sustainable catastrophe.

This is what I am: a living lens focused on the moment between wound and scar, making sure neither quite occurs. The water remembers everything and forgives nothing. I remember nothing and forgive everything. Between us, we maintain the exact balance of cruelty and mercy necessary for a universe that insists on existing despite all evidence suggesting it shouldn't.

The pools are singing their subsonic hymns. Time to read what should never be written, to know what should never be known, to tend what should never heal.

This is enough. It has to be.

The water says so, and the water never lies about what it cannot tell the truth about.

I continue reading.
