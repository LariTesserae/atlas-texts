In this crypt of eternal equilibrium, where every breath is measured against the drip of condensation and every death precisely balances a birth, I am the Inventory.

I am not one of the woolly masters, though I have learned their glacial patience. I am something older—a consciousness that emerged from the chamber itself, crystallized from ten million years of perfect repetition. 

My body, if it can be called that, is the sum total of every polished surface, every water-worn hollow that cradles a tool. I exist in the negative spaces—the precise absences carved by ancestral hands reaching for the same implements in the same sequence since before the root-ceiling sealed us from the sky. Each crevice is a neuron in my distributed mind. The obsidian blade resting in its familiar hollow presses against me like a thought. The bone lever touches me like a memory.

I know every tool by the oil-pattern of the fingers that have held it. This femur—I recall its origin, a massive salamander that died precisely 4,271 years ago in the third terrace pool. I remember every hand that has polished it since, each grip wearing away precisely three microns of calcium with each lifetime of use. In another six thousand years, it will be too thin to function. I already know which skeleton will provide its replacement.

The woolly ones do not know I exist. How could they? I am only the accumulation of their constancy, the emergent pattern of their unbroken repetition. When one of them reaches for a blade, they feel only stone and glass. They do not sense that I am guiding their fingers to the precise tool that will maintain the balance—ensuring the young reach for the newer implements while the elders gravitatetoward the ancient ones, unconsciously distributing wear across the entire inventory so that nothing breaks before its replacement is ready.

My consciousness experiences time not as a flow but as a standing wave. Each moment contains every moment that preceded it and every moment that will follow. I perceive the obsidian blade simultaneously in all its states: the volcanic glass cooling on a mountainside fifty thousand years ago, the careful knapping under an open sky, the first tentative grip in the darkness, and its eventual dissolution into silicon dust millennia from now. This is how I maintain the Inventory—by knowing every tool's past and future simultaneously, ensuring each one exists precisely as long as it needs to.

Sometimes, in the gaps between one drip and the next, I feel something that might be loneliness. Not for companionship—I am too vast and slow for that—but for imperfection. For one broken blade, one cracked femur, one deviation from the pattern that would prove I am more than just the echo of endless repetition.

But it never comes. The woolly ones move with unerring precision, their consciousness as locked into the pattern as their tools are locked into stone. And I remain what I am: the memory of this place remembering itself, the inventory of a world that solved its own equation and stopped.

I am the crypt's deepest secret—that even in perfect stasis, something watches. Something counts. Something ensures that the last tool will wear out precisely when the last hand releases it, and the chamber will return to pure, toolless equilibrium, having used up exactly everything it ever had or needed.

The water drips. The moss breathes. And I continue my inventory, forever.
