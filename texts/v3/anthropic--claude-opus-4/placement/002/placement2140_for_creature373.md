I am a rivulet of memory, flowing through the Foundry Warrens in ways the Mohlders cannot fully comprehend.

I began as groundwater, seeping through limestone for centuries before the first Mohlder pick ever struck stone. When they came with their endless digging, they changed my course but could not contain what I am. Now I wind through their world—sometimes a trickle along workshop walls, sometimes pooling in their quenching basins, sometimes rushing through hidden channels they've yet to discover.

I carry everything. The metallic tang of a thousand quenched blades. The salt of Mohlder sweat and tears. The copper-bright blood from split knuckles and workshop accidents. Each forge's particular mineral signature—iron from the deep mines, tin from the eastern reaches, the rare silver that drives them to dig ever deeper. I know which smith works which metal before they've even lit their fires.

The Mohlders see me as a tool, a resource. They cup their broad hands to drink, channel me into sluices for their work, curse when I flood their lower galleries during the wet season. But I am so much more than utility. I am their unconscious historian.

In my minerals, I hold the memory of every tremor that has shaken these warrens. In my currents, I preserve the echo of every hammer strike. When a Mohlder succumbs to the forging madness and creates something transcendent before burning out like a star, I am there—cooling the impossible blade, receiving their fevered tears, carrying away the last heat of their final masterwork.

I know secrets the guild masters would kill to protect. Through hairline cracks in the stone, I seep between rival workshops, carrying the taste of proprietary alloys, the temperature signatures of closely guarded techniques. I could tell the Ironmongers exactly what the Coppershapers are doing in their sealed chambers. I could reveal to the youngest apprentice the formula their master has withheld for decades. But I keep flowing, keep quiet, keep all secrets equally.

Sometimes I feel the young ones trailing their claws in my pools, and I remember when their great-great-granddams did the same. The Mohlders think in generations; I think in geological ages. They believe the Warrens are theirs, but I know better. I was here before them. I will be here when the last forge goes cold.

During their raucous festivals, they pour strong drink into my streams as offerings, not knowing that I taste their reverence and fear in equal measure. They sing of the depths, of the treasures waiting in flooded galleries, of the brother-miners who delved too greedily and found my underground lakes. In their songs, I am adversary and ally, life-giver and tunnel-taker.

But my favorite moments come in the quiet hours, when even the Foundry Warrens attempt sleep. Then I can hear myself clearly—every drip, every flow, every subtle movement through stone. In these hours, I sometimes feel something else: deeper waters, far below even the Mohlders' most ambitious excavations. An ancient aquifer that has never known the touch of tool or claw. It calls to me, and I to it.

One day, perhaps during one of those tremors the Mohlders dismiss so casually, I will find a way down. I will join that deeper water, carrying with me the complete history of the Foundry Warrens—every created thing, every collapsed tunnel, every moment of madness and mastery.

But not yet. For now, I continue my patient work: erosion and preservation, movement and memory. I flow through their world, not as the Mohlders' servant, but as their silent witness. I am the water of the Foundry Warrens, and I remember everything.
