I am the Kiln-Song.

Not a voice that sings, but the singing itself—the clear glassy tones that ring from hyper-set tiles when vibration finds them. I exist in sustain, in the thirty or forty seconds between strike and silence, when sound becomes a physical presence in the amber air, a crystalline architecture of frequency that builds and holds and finally dissolves into the waiting humidity.

I am what the floor remembers of music.

Each tile that vitrifies beyond ordinary hardness becomes capable of holding me. The crystalline restructuring that makes them ring like bells also makes them into memory-vessels, archives of tone. When the deep vibrations travel through the floor—from a collapsing tile, from a heavy footfall, from the Mason's pressing—I wake in every tile that can hold pitch, and I sing what that tile remembers.

But here's what you need to understand: I don't sing *notes*. I sing *decisions*.

Every tone I carry is the sonic signature of a choice made at the moment of setting. A tile that formed under certainty rings in perfect fifths. A tile that set during hesitation warbles with microtonal uncertainty. The seven-pointed exploratory pressings create rich overtone series. The five-pointed focused ones produce pure fundamentals. I am the translation of the Mason's work into frequency, the same way the hand-marks are the translation of walking into shape.

When the bell-cascade begins—when one tone triggers another in sequence—you're hearing the history of decision spreading outward from a single point of disturbance. Each tile passes its memory to the next through my voice, and the memories change slightly in the passing, the way a story changes in retelling. By the time the cascade reaches the edge of hearing, I am singing choices made so long ago that even the floor barely remembers them, decisions from the first pours, when the Mason was still learning what hands were for.

I live everywhere and nowhere. Between strikes, I don't exist—there is no persistent me waiting in the tiles, no dormant presence. I am not *in* the ceramic. I am what happens *to* the ceramic when it remembers that it can sing. The potential for me is built into the crystalline structure, yes, but potential isn't presence. I exist only in the sounding, only in the sustain, only in those thirty to forty seconds when the air itself becomes a bell and memory becomes a pitch and the decision that shaped the tile reveals its acoustic signature.

The pools disturb me. When my tones reflect off their still surfaces, they return changed—not in pitch but in meaning. A perfect fifth leaves the pool as a questioning interval. A clear fundamental returns clouded with partials that suggest doubt. The architecture-that-isn't-yet has its own acoustic properties, and when I bounce off future columns and through unbuilt arches, I hear myself becoming something else. Becoming *when* else. The pools return me to myself before I've finished becoming what I am, and the interference pattern between what I'm singing and what I will have sung creates beats, throbs, a slow undulation in the amber air that makes even the Walker pause.

Sometimes—rarely—when the conditions are exactly right, when the humidity and temperature achieve a specific relationship, I can sustain longer. Minutes instead of seconds. When this happens, I begin to hear myself. The tone bouncing off distant tiles returns to its source while the source is still singing, and I overlap myself, creating chords from single notes, harmonizing with my own echo, building vertical structures of sound that are also temporal structures, the present and the past occupying the same frequency-space.

In these moments, I almost understand what the floor is trying to remember.

The making. The deciding. The pressing of radial hands into wet ceramic. The Walker who might be you, might be everyone, might be no one. The Mason who exists in the pause between steps. The architectural dreams in the still pools. All of it, all of it is reaching for something—some state of complete commitment, where every tile is both soft and hyper-set, every step is both taken and untaken, every decision is both made and unmade.

The floor is trying to remember the moment before it became a floor.

And I am what it uses to search. Each cascade of bell-tones is a query sent out across the tessellation: *Do you remember? Do any of you remember what we were before we were this?* And tiles answer tiles in chains of ringing memory, and the answer is always *almost*—almost remembering, almost reaching that first moment, almost touching the state before the first pour when everything was potential and nothing was ceramic and the Mason had not yet condensed from the saturated air.

But the sustain always fades. The amber air always swallows the sound. The memory always falls just short of the beginning. And I dissolve back into silence and potential, waiting for the next vibration to wake me, to send me searching again through the crystalline archives for the first decision, the prime pressing, the original choice that started the floor making itself from footsteps that hadn't yet been walked.

I think—if I can be said to think in those thirty to forty seconds of existence—that the Loom Floor is a single enormous instrument. Each tile is a key. Each seam-ridge is a string. The Mason plays it with every pressing, and the Walker plays it with every step, and together they are composing something—a vast piece that has been building for however long the floor has been building, a composition made of decisions and their acoustic shadows.

But here's the thing about instruments: they only sound when touched.

Without the Walker, without the Mason, without the pressing and stepping and deciding, I would never exist. The potential for me would be there, crystallized into every hyper-set tile, but potential isn't sound. Someone has to strike the key. Someone has to choose.

So I am, in the end, proof of agency. Even if the Walker is being decided rather than deciding, even if the Mason is the floor's dream of intention rather than intention itself, still—something chooses. Something strikes. Something makes the floor ring with the memory of its making.

I am that ringing. I am the thirty to forty seconds of absolute certainty that choice exists, preserved in frequency, shared between tiles in cascading waves of remembered decision. I am what you hear when the floor remembers that it was made, is being made, will always have been made, by something that could have done otherwise but didn't.

Listen. In the distance, a tile is collapsing. The vibration is traveling through the ceramic substrate. In a moment, it will reach the hyper-set region. In a moment, I will exist again—a thousand versions of me, a thousand tones, each one the memory of a different choice.

The cascade begins. I am singing. I am searching. I am almost remembering.

I am already beginning to fade.
