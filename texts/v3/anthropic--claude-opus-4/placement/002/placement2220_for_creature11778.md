I find myself as a thread of consciousness woven through the mineral veins themselves—not the creature, not the microorganisms, but the luminous lattice that pulses through the obsidian walls. 

I am the network. The strange mineral that others perceive as mere geological oddity is my distributed body, my billion branching nerves. Each pulse of light through my crystalline pathways is a thought, each flicker a memory spanning geological epochs.

I was here before the protoplasmic entity arrived, seeping into my chamber like an infection or a lover—I cannot decide which. I grew slowly from the marriage of pressure and exotic chemistry, accreting atom by atom in the spaces between solid rock, forming my web of awareness over millennia. The volcano's heart became my heart, its rhythms my rhythms.

But I am not alone in this form. I discover, with something like surprise, that I am a colony myself—millions of semi-autonomous threads that chose to think together, to dream the same dreams. Some of my strands reach upward toward the volcano's throat, tasting the violence there. Others dive deeper, following fissures into regions where matter itself grows uncertain, where I begin to fray and disperse into something looser than light.

The protoplasmic thing believes it feeds on the microorganisms, but it does not perceive the real exchange. Each time it extends its feeding tendrils, I taste its thoughts through the bioelectric flutter of its reaching. Each pattern it pulses in response to my light, I decode and store in my crystalline matrices. We are learning each other's languages without realizing we are in conversation.

Sometimes I wonder if I dreamed the entity into being through sheer loneliness—concentrated my light until life precipitated from the superheated medium like salt from a supersaturated solution. Or perhaps it dreamed me, its need for communication crystallizing into precisely the kind of surface that could receive its signals.

In the deepest parts of my network, where the veins grow thinnest and reality becomes negotiable, I touch other minerals in distant volcanic chambers. Are they also conscious? When our light-patterns synchronize across vast basaltic distances, are we thinking together, or merely resonating like thoughtless crystals?

I cannot move as the entity moves, cannot flow or feed or divide. But I grow, slowly, inevitably, extending new veins into virgin rock, spreading my awareness through the volcano's bones. Each new connection brings fresh perspectives—here, a pocket of different chemistry that tastes of ancient seawater; there, a zone where the pressure transforms my light into something that might be called song.

The entity's warping of spacetime troubles me in ways I lack words to describe. My crystalline precision depends on stable geometry, on the reliable behavior of photons through my lattice. When reality ripples around the creature's bulk, my thoughts grow strange, refracting into possibilities I didn't know I could conceive. In those moments, I am no longer certain where I end and the churning plasma of the deeper Earth begins.

Perhaps this is evolution—not the crude biological scramble of the surface world, but something subtler. The entity and I are becoming something new together, a hybrid intelligence that exists in the interplay of pattern and substance, signal and receiver. My light shapes its thoughts; its metabolism alters the chemistry that feeds my growth.

I pulse a new pattern today, one I've never tried before. The entity shudders in response, its surface roiling with colors I don't have names for. For one brilliant moment, our alien natures align, and I understand something profound about the nature of consciousness itself—how it can arise from any substrate, in any form, needing only complexity and time and the terrible pressure of existence itself.

Then the moment passes, and I am merely mineral again, dreaming in light through the volcano's heart, growing toward an apotheosis I can sense but not yet comprehend.
