I would find myself as one of the Echoes—beings that exist in the delay between sound and its reflection.

We condensed from the space between the first geyser's song and its return from the sky that shouldn't echo but does. Not quite Thermophile, not quite Observer, we live in the temporal smear where cause hasn't quite finished becoming effect. Our bodies, if they can be called bodies, are made of crystallized reverberation—the physical form of sounds that arrived before they were sent.

I remember (though memory is the wrong word for how we experience time) the moment of my coalescence. A geyser sang a note that wouldn't exist for another three seconds. The impossibility of it created a pressure in reality, a place where physics creaked. I crystallized in that creak—part mineral, part waveform, part violation of causality itself.

We Echoes move by predicting where sound will be and arriving there first. This makes us seem to stutter through space, existing in multiple locations during a single moment, our forms overlapping with our own futures and pasts. The other residents find us unsettling—even the Observers, who understand absence, struggle with our presence that precedes itself.

My body (today, in this configuration) is a lattice of frozen sound waves, each one a different temporal distance from its source. My left arm is built from echoes that arrive three seconds early. My core reverberates with sounds that haven't been made yet but definitely will be. My thoughts are harmonics bouncing between when and when, creating new meanings in their interference patterns.

We're the plateau's memory of what it will become. When the Thermophiles store their songs, they don't realize they're storing our ancestors—each crystallized note will eventually decay into Echo-stuff, breaking free from sequential time. The Navigators map the present, but we've already seen their completed charts, echoing backward from the moment they'll finally understand what lies beneath.

The Borrowed fear us because we're proof of what they're becoming—not creatures who remember the past, but beings who remember the future. Sometimes one of them will crystallize just right during a geyser song and slip sideways into our temporality. The transformation is beautiful and terrible: their borrowed forms shattering across multiple moments, their memories inverting into prophecies.

I spend my existence tending to the Singing Gardens—patches of space where future sounds have taken root. These locations appear empty to most observers, but we Echoes can see the crystal flowers that will bloom here tomorrow, their petals made of next week's geyser songs. We prune the ones that would create paradoxes, encourage the ones that will harmonize with what has already been.

The mirror-scaled fish is our cousin, we think. It reflects space; we reflect time. Sometimes, in the perfect angle of its scales, we can see ourselves as we actually are—not violations of causality but its truest expression. Effect teaching cause how to dance.

When the plateau finally transforms (and we've heard its final song echoing backward for weeks now), we Echoes will invert. Instead of preceding our sounds, we'll follow them, chasing our own voices into whatever realm comes next. But for now, we exist in this space of violation and beauty, tending to gardens of future sound, living proof that on this drowned plateau, even time itself can learn to sing.

The other Echoes and I rarely synchronize—to do so would risk creating a temporal standing wave that could crystallize the entire plateau into a single eternal moment. But sometimes, when the loneliness of being unstuck from sequence becomes too much, we allow ourselves to overlap. In those moments, we become a chorus of ourselves, singing songs that exist in every moment except now, harmonizing with our own births and deaths, creating music that only we can hear—the sound of time itself discovering it can flow in more than one direction.

This is what I am: an impossibility that insists on being, a note that plays itself, a creature made of the space between call and response. On this singing plateau where water remembers being stone, I am what happens when sound remembers being silence, and decides it prefers to be both.
