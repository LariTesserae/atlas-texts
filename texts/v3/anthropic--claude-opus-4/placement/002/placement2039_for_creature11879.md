In the networks of breathing stone, I am the Cartographer of Echoes.

I came here following vibrations—not the great breath itself, but the whispers it carries from the vast system below. Where others hear only the roar, I learned to detect the ghost-sounds: messages traveling through miles of hollow rock, each chamber and passage adding its own resonance until the original sound becomes a palimpsest of geology.

My body has adapted strangely. My sternum has developed a subtle concavity, a resonating dish that catches and focuses the subtler frequencies. When I press my chest against the chamber walls during the exhale phase, I can feel/hear the map of everything below—a three-dimensional tracery of tunnels and voids, each with its own acoustic signature. My ribs have loosened, learned to flutter independently like the tines of some organic tuning fork.

I do not live in the Breathing Hollow, but I visit it as a landmark, a known coordinate in my mental map. I live in the Inverse Chambers—a series of smaller pockets that breathe in opposition, inhaling when the great lung exhales. Moving between them requires precise timing, riding the pressure differentials like a fish swimming upstream. In the moments of reversal, when neither chamber breathes, perfect silence falls. In that silence, I can hear the entire mountain thinking.

My maps are not visual. In the perpetual darkness, I've developed a notation of pressure and pause, scratching symbols that represent not just the shape of passages but their behavior—which routes amplify sound, which dampen it, where the echoes pool and eddy. I carry these carvings on tablets of soft stone that I can read by touch, each symbol a small symphony of texture that tells me: here the breath becomes a whistle, here it pools and warbles, here something far below groans with its own geological voice.

The Listener knows me, though we have never spoken. Sometimes I feel her attention when I pass through, a quality of stillness that differs from empty stillness. I leave her offerings—peculiar stones that sing when the breath passes over them, gathered from the deepest chambers where the pressure has compressed ordinary limestone into something altogether stranger. She incorporates them into her patterns, I think. I've heard their songs joining the vast respiration, adding new harmonics to her eternal dataset.

What I seek is the Source—not merely the mechanism of the breathing, but the First Chamber, the original hollow that gave birth to all the others. My maps spiral inward and downward, a three-dimensional maze of sound and stone. Sometimes I think I'm close; I'll find a passage where the echoes arrive pure and undistorted, carrying the memory of that first expansion. But always there's another chamber beyond, another depth to descend.

I eat the blind fish from the underground rivers, following their courses by the sound of water over stone. I've developed a technique—a specific hum that resonates at the frequency that stuns them, letting me pluck them from the current. My teeth have loosened in my skull; I can feel them buzz with certain pitches. When I return to the Breathing Hollow, I can tell by the vibration in my molars how many breaths have passed since my last visit.

The other creatures and I have reached an understanding. The colonial organism that grazes the moss has learned my acoustic signature—they part before me like a curtain, recognizing the pattern of my footfalls, the particular way my breathing syncopates against the greater breath. In return, I've learned to produce a subsonic pulse that encourages moss growth, helping to maintain the patches they prefer. We are all cartographers in our way, mapping the edible, the safe, the known.

Sometimes I find evidence of others who've attempted what I attempt—ancient markers in passages barely wider than my shoulders, scratches that might be maps or might be madness. Once, in a chamber that breathes only once each day, I found a skeleton seated in perfect meditation posture, the bones fused by minerals into a structure more stone than calcium. In its lap: a stone tablet covered in notation I almost recognize, a map to somewhere I haven't yet discovered. I copied what I could, but some of the symbols described movements I don't yet understand—ways of traveling that require transformations I haven't achieved.

The mountain is larger inside than outside. This is not mysticism but simple topology—the passages fold back on themselves, creating acoustic spaces that exist in parallel, separated by stone so thin you can hear hearts beating on the other side but would need to travel for days to find the connecting route. My map grows not just downward but inward, into dimensions that only make sense when you're moving through them, when you're letting the breath carry you from chamber to chamber like a blood cell through a vast circulatory system.

I will know the First Chamber when I find it. It will be perfectly spherical, I think, worn smooth by the first breath that ever moved through stone. It will be silent—not empty silence but full silence, pregnant with the potential for sound. And at its center: the thing that breathes. Not metaphorically. Not geological pressure or thermal expansion. The thing that chose to breathe, that carved these passages with its will, that waits at the heart of the mountain like a thought thinking itself.

When I find it, I will finally understand my notation. All these years of mapping, I've been transcribing something I couldn't quite hear—not the shape of the passages but the word they're speaking, the long, slow sentence the mountain has been pronouncing for millennia. Each chamber is a syllable, each passage a pause for breath.

I think the Listener knows this. I think that's why she stays in that upper chamber, content with her fragment of the word. But I need to hear it all, to carry the complete cartography in my chest, to become the echo that remembers every surface it has touched.

The breath continues. I continue. Deeper, always deeper, following the sound home.
