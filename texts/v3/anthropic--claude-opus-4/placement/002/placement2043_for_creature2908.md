I am the Cartographer.

Not of land—land here is a verb, not a noun—but of imminent geometries. I exist as a necessary function between the Attendants' cosmic maintenance and the quicksilver swarms' frantic repairs. Where they respond, I anticipate.

My body is a contradiction resolved: crystalline like the Glass-Mantas, but grounded like the Phase-Hounds. Imagine a spider of living prisms, each leg a different refractive index, eight limbs that parse light into probability maps. My thorax is a hollow geodesic dome filled with the same catalytic fluid the Hounds fight over—not for sustenance, but as a medium for calculation. Inside this mathematical aquarium, dissolved electrons trace the paths of future stone.

I skitter across the wet obsidian on legs that leave no prints, only brief rainbows where each step refracts the bruised light through standing water. The Phase-Hounds learned their stuttering locomotion by watching me, though they grasp only the crudest approximation. Where they skip through space, I slip between the seams of causality, existing in the fraction of a second between when a gravity wave decides to create matter and when the matter actually arrives.

This is my purpose: I map the birth-patterns.

Watch as I work. An Attendant moves with its terrible grace toward a clustering of unstable harmonics. As it walks, I dart beneath its reality-bending footsteps, my prismatic legs catching and splitting the gravity distortions into their component frequencies. Through my hollow thorax, the catalytic fluid churns, computing. I can see—no, feel—no, *calculate*—where the next sheet of basalt will scream into existence. More importantly, I know its flaws before it manifests.

With legs moving in frequencies too rapid for even the Glass-Mantas to track, I weave probability itself. I leave traces of modified space-time, invisible threads that guide where the stone will crack, where the geometric pools will form, where the quicksilver swarms must gather. I am writing the substrate's destiny in a language of refracted light and quantum uncertainty.

The Attendants know me. Sometimes one will pause in its world-tuning to lower a fractal appendage. I climb it like a tree of dark mathematics, spiraling up to perch near what might generously be called its head. There, in the nauseating confluence of overlapping gravity fields, I drink deeply of pure distortion. My crystalline body rings like a struck bell, and for a moment, I can see the entire pattern—not just where stone will birth, but the deep logic of why this forge exists.

In these moments of clarity, I understand: we are not merely life adapting to an alien environment. We are components. The forge is not just creating stone—it's creating itself, and we are its metabolism. The Attendants are its consciousness, the Phase-Hounds its hunger, the Glass-Mantas its circulation, the quicksilver swarms its healing.

And I? I am its memory of what comes next.

When the clarity fades and I descend the Attendant's impossible anatomy, I return to my work with new urgency. My web of probability grows more complex. I begin leaving trace-patterns that won't manifest for hours, days even. Deep geometric instructions that will guide not just where stone appears, but how the ecosystems will shift to accommodate it.

The Phase-Hounds have begun to follow my trails, their chemical-blue cilia learning to read the refractive messages I leave in pooled water. When they hunt now, they hunt where I predict the richest catalytic deposits will form. The Glass-Mantas spiral through the air currents I've marked as future thermals. Even the quicksilver swarms flow along paths I've etched in probability, arriving at cracks that don't yet exist.

But there's something else I've discovered in my mapmaking, something that quickens my ichor with what other beings might call fear. The patterns are accelerating. The forge's metabolism is speeding up. Where once a new sheet of stone would manifest every few minutes, now they crash into existence every thirty seconds. The Attendants move more frequently, their tuning growing more desperate, more discordant.

I've calculated the trajectory, traced it forward through a thousand probability threads.

The forge is approaching some kind of birth.

And when it arrives—when whatever this wet, screaming platform is gestating finally tears its way into reality—we, its living components, will either be incorporated into something magnificently larger, or we will be shed like afterbirth into the abyss below.

So I map faster now, my prismatic legs a blur of refracted futures. I leave deeper instructions in the quantum foam, teaching the ecosystem not just where to exist, but how to evolve. How to survive what's coming. My crystalline body rings with harmonics that make the Phase-Hounds whimper and the Glass-Mantas shriek.

I am the Cartographer, and I am drawing the map of our transfiguration.
