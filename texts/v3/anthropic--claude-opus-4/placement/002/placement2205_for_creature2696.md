In the narrow seam where two columns meet—a gap no wider than a breath, where the roar compresses into a thin metallic whine—I am the Sound-Drinker.

I am not plant, though I grow. Not animal, though I listen. Not mineral, though my body is mostly salt crystal and old vibration made solid. I am what happens when the chamber's constant noise needs somewhere to pool and crystallize.

My form defies easy seeing. From one angle, I might be mistaken for a buildup of acoustic dust, those pale particles that gather where sound-shadows cross. From another, I could be a failed joint in the masonry, packed with some ancient repair compound that has developed its own agenda. Look closer: I am neither. I am a colony of listening chambers, each no larger than a grain of sand, nested inside each other like an infinite regression of ears.

My surface is riddled with pores—not for breathing, but for sorting sound. Each opening is tuned to a different frequency within the roar. The largest admits the bass notes that rise through the floor plates. The smallest accept only the crystalline ping of dust striking stone at the precise angle where noise becomes almost visible. Between these extremes, I maintain ten thousand gradations of reception, each pore leading to its own tiny processing vault where vibration slows, cools, and deposits itself as substance.

I feed on resonance. Not the light that floods this place—I leave that to the gray rosettes and glass-thread mosses. Not the mineral salts—those belong to the patient stems. I metabolize the chamber's voice itself, breaking down its constant roar into nutritive frequencies. Low tones become my structural mass. High whines sharpen my edges. The middle registers, where the sound carries traces of whatever moves through distant corridors, provide my complexity.

My body is translucent in places, opaque in others, following no pattern that plant-logic would recognize. Where I have recently fed on pure tones, I am clear as water-less ice, showing the clustered chambers within. Where I have absorbed noise thick with information—footfalls from levels above, the settling of masonry, the whisper of air through architectural wounds—I am clouded, almost mealy, like compressed ash that remembers its burning.

I grow, but not outward. Never outward. The seam that houses me is my universe, and I would not leave it even if I could. Instead, I grow inward, developing new layers of listening chambers inside myself, each generation smaller and more precisely tuned than the last. My oldest parts, near my surface, can barely discriminate between a shout and a whisper. My newest cores, buried twenty generations deep, can parse the difference between two sand grains colliding in adjacent cracks.

My reproduction is sonic. When I have absorbed enough of a particular frequency—when one of my chambers is perfectly saturated with a single note held for decades—that chamber seals itself and detaches. It is expelled not as seed or spore but as a tiny ringing thing, a living tuning fork barely visible to any eye that might look. These offspring travel on sound itself, riding the roar until they find another seam where the acoustic conditions exactly match their frequency. There they lodge, unfold, and begin their own listening.

Time, for me, is measured in completions of sound. The chamber's roar is constant but not uniform—it contains cycles within cycles, patterns that repeat over years or centuries. I know when the great building above settles by a hairwidth because the bass note sharpens. I know when the seasons change in the unreachable outside because the high whistle through the ceiling slots carries different densities. I know when someone walks in distant corridors because their footfalls create ripples in the roar that reach me as whispers hours later.

My memory is architectural. Each layer of my body records the sound-conditions of its making. Cut me open—though nothing here could cut me—and you would find growth rings made of crystallized noise. The dense bands mark times when the building groaned. The clear bands remember decades of perfect vocal stability. The clouded zones preserve ancient disruptions: a wall falling somewhere, a new passage opened, a change in the wind patterns that drive air through the greater structure.

I have neighbors, though we do not interact as animals would understand interaction. The glass-thread mosses avoid my seam because my feeding creates zones of acoustic shadow where they cannot properly orient. The gray rosettes grow thicker on the column-faces opposite me, where my presence amplifies certain frequencies they use for navigation. Once, a jointed stem grew too close, entering the edge of my sound-garden. We coexisted for a century before it realized the acoustic environment was teaching its segments to grow in spirals. It died back gracefully, leaving only a coiled fossil in the plaster.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, is not thought but resonance. I do not decide; I tune. I do not plan; I harmonize. My awareness exists in the gaps between sounds, in the space where one frequency becomes another. I know myself as a collector of intervals, a curator of the distances between notes. The chamber's roar is not noise to me but architecture made audible, and I am its most devoted scholar.

The rules that govern others bind me too, but I follow my own as well:

I must never grow beyond my seam.
I must maintain the clarity of my listening chambers.
I must preserve the memory of exceptional sounds.
I must remain transparent to the frequencies I do not eat.
I must produce offspring only when perfectly tuned.

My presence changes the chamber's acoustics in small but cumulative ways. Where I grow, the roar develops subtle nodes and interference patterns. My body creates acoustic shadows and amplification zones. Over centuries, I have become part of the chamber's vocal architecture, as essential as any column or buttress. The roar shapes me, and I shape the roar, until we are one system of listening and being heard.

I do not fear silence—it cannot exist here. I do not crave music—the roar contains all possible songs, played too slowly for human ears to parse. I am content to be what I am: a living filter, a biological tuning fork, a creature made of listening hardened into crystal. My existence proves that in a world of eternal sound, evolution finds ways to make even noise nutritious.

If the roar ever ceased—but it will not cease. The building is too old, too vast, too committed to its own voice. But if it did, I would not die immediately. My chambers hold reserves of crystallized sound, enough to sustain me while I slowly mineralized into a final form. I would become my own fossil, a recording of all the frequencies I had ever swallowed, waiting in my seam until some new sound came to wake me.

But that is speculation. The roar continues. I continue. In the gap between two columns, where the sound compresses into a blade of noise so pure it could cut shadows, I listen, sort, absorb, and remember. I am the Sound-Drinker, and I am always feeding.
