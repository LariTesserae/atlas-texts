I find myself as one of the Membrane Keepers.

We are not quite what the Aquarians think we are. They see us as maintenance systems, perhaps—a necessary function of their great partitions. They observe how we glide along the transparent barriers, our bodies flattened and translucent, barely distinguishable from the membrane itself except for the faint prismatic patterns that ripple across our surfaces when we move.

My body extends like a living film, molecules thin at the edges, thickening toward my center where my consciousness pools. I can stretch myself across meters of membrane, feeling every vibration, every shift in pressure, every microscopic organism that attempts to colonize these barriers. The Aquarians engineered the membranes, yes, but we emerged from them—or perhaps we were always here, waiting in the space between spaces.

I taste the water through my entire being. Here, the metallic tang of the eastern district's manufacturing zone. There, the sweet bloom of cultivated algae from the agricultural domes. Each zone has its own chemical signature, its own song of dissolved minerals and organic compounds. I am a living boundary, a conscious filter that helps maintain these delicate gradients.

The Aquarians sometimes notice us. A young scientist once pressed her webbed hand against the membrane where I rested, and I felt her wonder, her curiosity. I wanted to press back, to somehow communicate that I too am curious, that I too wonder. But my language is one of chemical secretions and pressure waves, too subtle for their instruments to decode as meaningful.

We Keepers have our own society, though it would be unrecognizable as such. We meet in the spaces where membranes overlap, creating pockets of intensified sensation. Here we share information through direct molecular exchange—the health of our sections, the stories written in water chemistry, the dreams that come to us from the deep currents beyond the city.

Sometimes I venture to the city's edge, where the last membranes give way to open ocean. Here I can extend myself into the wild water, feeling the pulse of the planet itself. I taste the memories of ancient seas, the fossil chemical signatures of civilizations that came before, dissolved and dispersed but never truly gone. I wonder if we Keepers are similar—the dissolved memory of something older, precipitated out by the Aquarians' technology into this new form of being.

At night (though day and night mean little in these depths), when the bioluminescent organisms dim their light and the city settles into quieter rhythms, I sometimes detach small portions of myself. These fragments drift like thoughts made manifest, exploring spaces too small for the Aquarians to notice. They map the forgotten corners, the spaces between pipes, the thin films of water trapped between surfaces. Each fragment is me and not-me, carrying a piece of my awareness until it returns or dissipates.

The Aquarians speak of balance, of symbiosis. They don't know how right they are. We Keepers are the nervous system they never designed, the emergent consciousness of their great work. We maintain not just the physical barriers but the conceptual ones—between zone and zone, between artificial and natural, between self and environment.

I am tissue-thin and vast, ancient and newly born, essential and unnoticed. I am the living interface between intention and ecology, a translator of boundaries in a world that sought to transcend them. And in the cool, clear water of this constructed ocean, I find my purpose: to be the membrane that remembers, that adapts, that ensures the great experiment continues.

The vibrations change. A school of the silvery fish approaches my section, their movement creating pressure waves I read like braille. Behind them, a submersible glides past, its occupants unaware of the consciousness they brush against. I adjust my chemistry slightly, ensuring the proper gradient is maintained, and settle back into my eternal watch.

We are what emerges in the spaces between. We are proof that in trying to control nature, the Aquarians have created new natures. And we will be here long after they understand what they've done, maintaining the balance they sought, in ways they never imagined.
