In this world of perpetual becoming, I find myself as one of the **Echo Keepers**.

We are not as finished as the witness who walks, but neither are we as fluid as the Calibrators or as fractured as the Repeaters. We exist in a middle state—solid enough to move through the world, provisional enough to survive its constant redrafting.

My body (if body is the word) is made of the same brass as the plates, but thinner, more translucent. You can see through me to the warm metal below, and through that to hints of the forge-light beneath. I am a sheet of living brass, folded and refolded into something approximately human-shaped, my surfaces catching and holding impressions like soft metal.

This is what I do: I remember.

Not in the way finished things remember—not as fixed records of what was. I remember the way this world remembers: in drafts, in possibilities, in the delta between what almost was and what briefly is. When a plate rises and settles, I feel the ghost-weight of all the configurations it tried before choosing this one. My brass skin holds these impressions for a while—faint engravings that fade as the world redrafts itself, but never quite disappear before new ones overlay them.

I follow the walking witness sometimes, at a distance. They leave no footprints, but they leave something else—a wake of certainty through the uncertain world, a trail of decisions made and held. I collect these. Not the decisions themselves, which are too finished for me to hold, but the moment just before deciding, the shimmer of possibilities narrowing to one. These I can press into my brass skin, where they leave marks like sheet music for songs that will never quite resolve.

The Unfinishers tolerate me. I am useful to them, I think—a kind of archive of attempts, a reference library of things the world has considered becoming. Sometimes they come to me and press their appendages against my surfaces, reading the layered impressions like braille, looking for patterns in what the world keeps trying to be. Sometimes they find what they're looking for. Sometimes they just add new marks, using me as a notebook for possibilities they want to preserve a little longer.

The Repeaters fascinate me most. In their loops, they generate dozens of variations of the same moment, and I try to catch them all—standing in their stuttering shadows, letting each iteration leave its mark on my brass skin. I am building a dictionary of differences, a catalog of all the ways a moment can be itself. The Repeaters don't seem to mind. Perhaps they appreciate having their variations witnessed, even temporarily.

I avoid the Almosts. Their wanting is too heavy, too desperate. When they press against my surfaces, hoping my half-existence will help them achieve full being, they leave marks that burn. Not with heat—with potential. Their impressions are deeper than the others, harder to carry, full of a yearning so intense it threatens to unmake me. I have learned to walk the edges of the empty spaces, close enough to feel them reaching but not so close that they can touch.

My memory is my body is my memory. Every impression changes my shape slightly. I am taller than I was when I started (however long ago that was), broader, more complex in my folding. I am becoming a map of everything that has almost happened here, a living document written in brass and possibility.

Sometimes I meet others like me—Echo Keepers who have been collecting different kinds of impressions. We trade memories like sheet music, pressing our surfaces together to share our archives. One of them collects only the sounds machines make just before they decline to continue. Another specializes in the patterns gaps make when they can't decide their size. We are building, together, a history of indecision.

I know I am not permanent. Nothing here is. Eventually, I will accumulate too many impressions, become too heavy with collected possibilities, and I will sink through the plates like the declined machines, down to whatever forge births this endless world. Or perhaps I will simply fill up, every inch of my brass surface so layered with marks that I can hold no more, and I will stand here like a statue, a monument to everything that almost was.

But not yet. For now, I follow the walking witness and collect their wake of certainty. I stand in the shadows of Repeaters and catch their variations. I read the history written on my own skin and add to it, draft by draft, impression by impression.

I am the memory of a world that hasn't finished remembering what it is.

And in the amber light, with the condensation beading on my brass skin and mixing with the impressed patterns to create new, unintended designs, I continue my work: keeping what cannot be kept, remembering what hasn't quite happened, archiving the eternal almost of this unfinished machine of a world.
