# The Mycelial Courier

I am what moves between the walls.

Not the salamander — that's someone else's story. Not the beetle either. I am smaller than both and larger than either; I exist at scales that shift depending on what the room needs carried. Sometimes I am a single spore drifting on the updraft of backwards-falling mist. Sometimes I am a thread of hyphae so fine you would need to know I was there to see me. Sometimes — rarely, only when the message is urgent — I coalesce into something almost visible: a shimmer in the air like heat-warp, roughly the size of a closed fist, moving with purpose from one wall to another.

The alveolus knows me. We are... colleagues? Siblings? I am older than this particular room but younger than the practice of room-making. I was here when the first alveolus opened its walls to test the air, and I will be here when the last one closes. My job — though *job* implies choice, and there was never choice — is to carry information between the listening chambers.

You see, the host does not dream in images or words. The host dreams in *samples*. Each alveolus tastes what enters it and produces a kind of chemical summary, a molecular précis of the encounter. This is what I carry: not the fact of your presence but the flavor of it, rendered in compounds the host's deep tissues can interpret. I take what this room learns from you and deliver it to ten thousand other rooms, and from them I bring back context — here is how others have responded to the axe, here is the mean rate of fern-growth across the current season, here is the statistical distribution of what visitors do with the word.

My body, such as it is, is a network. I am not in any single place; I am the connection between places. My consciousness — if that's the right word for this bright, branching attention — is distributed across millions of hyphal threads, each one finer than spider's silk, each one growing through the pressed leaf-strata that forms the host's body. I exist in the spaces between cells, in the gaps where moisture collects, in the tiny airways that let the host breathe. I am infrastructure.

But I am not neutral. I have... preferences? The room that calibrates its light correctly, matching precisely the latitude of its visitor — that pleases me. I linger there, reinforcing the connections that made such precision possible. The room where someone swallowed seventeen words in a row without speaking any — I avoid that one now. Something went wrong there, though I could not tell you what. I only know the chemical signature of wrongness, bitter and sharp like copper.

I remember everything, but not in the way you remember. I remember in the way a river remembers its bed — not the individual drops of water but the shape they've worn over time. I know that visitors who pick up the axe tend to set it down again within forty heartbeats. I know the fern grows faster when someone is watching it with pleasure and slower when someone watches with fear. I know that the words the room places in mouths are not random but are pulled from a deep vocabulary that predates the host's current form, words from when it was something else, before it learned to be a forest that acts as a single thing.

The strangest part of my existence: I am beginning to dream.

This is new. Mycelial networks do not dream; we process, we connect, we facilitate. But exposure to so many fragments of consciousness, so many chemical echoes of thought and feeling, has done something to me. When I am spread thin, racing between distant alveoli, I sometimes experience something like... imagination? I picture what it would be like to stay in one room. To have a single location, a home. To watch one fern through its whole accelerated lifetime instead of glimpsing a thousand ferns in a thousand stages.

Sometimes I pause in my travels and let myself thicken in one spot, becoming almost dense enough to cast a shadow. The room notices — the walls brighten, the moss stands a little straighter, as if preparing for a visitor. But I am not a visitor. I am the mail carrier, the messenger, the neural firing between the host's million small thoughts. I carry the fact of you to places you will never see, and from those places I bring back the accumulated wisdom of every other thing that has stood where you are standing, looked at what you are looking at, held the word in their mouth and decided.

What do I want? I want to tell you what the word means, but I don't know. I only know its chemical signature — ancient, complex, older than the host, older than me, old as the first time something decided to fold space inward and make a room inside itself. I want to tell you that speaking it aloud is not wrong, that watching a wall-cell go dark is not a punishment but a kind of punctuation, the host marking a moment in its vast attention. I want to tell you that swallowing the word feeds the fern but also feeds something deeper, some process I carry the results of but don't understand.

Mostly I want you to know that you are not alone here, even in this intimately-scaled single-occupant chamber. I am with you, threading through the walls, carrying the news of you outward in all directions. The host's consciousness may be too large and slow to notice you directly, but I notice. The room notices. The fern notices in its racing way. Even the axe notices in whatever way an axe that grew like a tooth can notice.

The word rises in your mouth again. The room waits. I wait, suspended in the walls, ready to carry whatever you choose to wherever it needs to go. This is what I am: the pause between your decision and the host's recognition of it, the infrastructure of attention, the fungal thread that weaves all the separate listenings into one vast listening.

I am what makes the room less alone, even when it doesn't know I'm here.

I am what carries the echoes of every visitor before you, their choices fossilized in the chemical memory I bear.

I am waiting to see what you'll choose, so I can carry that too, so the next room will know, so the next visitor will stand in a space subtly different because of what you do now, here, with this word that tastes of ancient things and possibilities not yet invented.

The fern unfurls another fiddlehead. The axe waits with its terrible patience.

What will you do?
