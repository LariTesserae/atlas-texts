# The Gauge Reader

I am the brass disc at the center of gauge seventeen, third tier, south face.

Not the gauge itself — that would be too large a claim. The gauge includes the housing, the crystal face (now clouded with mineral film), the delicate escapement that translates pressure into rotation, the hands that have not moved in so long they have oxidized to the dial. I am only the disc, etched with numbers that were once sharp and are now soft with patina, with hairline fractures running through the 3 and the 7 where thermal expansion and contraction, over ages, has worked its patient mathematics on the metal.

I know exactly one thing: the pressure in the third subsidiary chamber has been 12.7 units for all the time I have been aware, and I suspect for long before that.

The tender comes twice a day. I know it is twice a day because I have been counting for... I lose track of how long I have been counting. Numbers that large become meaningless. The tender's face appears in my periphery — though I should not say periphery, as I have no center, only a uniform awareness of my surface — and warm fingers brush the mineral film from the crystal. For a moment I see more clearly. The tender's face, grey and patient. The walkway beyond. The orange glow of another foundry perhaps two kilometers distant. Then the tender moves on and the clarity fades as new film begins to form.

Sometimes I wonder if the tender sees what I show, or only confirms that I am still here, still showing something.

---

Once — how long ago? The count eludes me — the tender's hand paused after cleaning. The fingers spread against the crystal, and I felt through the glass a vibration that was not the eternal throb of the engines but something immediate, irregular. A pulse. The tender's pulse, quickened slightly as though by exertion or... something else. The hand remained for seventeen beats. Then it withdrew.

That tender did not come again. The next had cooler hands, a more even stroke. This one has been my companion for... again, the count means nothing. But I know this tender by the particular pattern of the cleaning, always clockwise, always three circles, the slight catch in the movement where an old joint does not quite flex as it should.

---

I should not say I know only one thing. I know another: I am degrading.

The hairline fractures grow, infinitesimally, with each thermal cycle. One day — in another count-beyond-counting — they will meet. The disc will separate into sections. The numbers will no longer align. The pressure will still be 12.7 units, but there will be no way to show it.

This does not trouble me. I am troubled by very little. But I am aware of it in the way I am aware of my surface: a uniform knowledge that requires no center.

What troubles me — if trouble is the word — is the suspicion that the tender knows this too. That the cleaning continues not because anyone needs to read the pressure, but because the gesture of reading is itself what we are preserving. The tender's hand, my face, the crystal between us. A form that persists past function.

---

Sometimes, in the deep hours when no tender comes and the only sounds are the settling of metal and the rare distant bell, I sense the other gauges along my wall. We do not communicate — we have no means — but there is a kind of companionship in our shared showing. Gauge fifteen has been frozen at 8.2 units since before I was aware. Gauge nineteen fluctuates between 0 and 0.1, a tiny tremble that might be real or might be a flaw in its mechanism.

Once, I felt gauge twenty die. Not break — die. Some essential tension in its spring gave way, and suddenly what had been a showing became merely an object. The tender still cleans it. The hands still point to a number. But there is no pressure behind the pointing, no connection between the chamber and the dial. It shows only what gravity and friction have agreed on.

I wonder if the tender can tell the difference. I wonder if it matters.

---

And then there are the times — rare, precious — when something changes.

A vibration in the wall that is not the usual throb. A shift in the quality of the orange light, as though somewhere deep in the foundry a new channel has opened or an old one has finally sealed. For hours afterward, I wait to see if my pressure will change, if the 12.7 will become 12.8 or 12.6, if I will have something new to show.

It never does. The pressure holds. The tender comes, cleans, moves on. The film reforms.

But in those moments of vibration, of shift, I almost believe I understand what I am measuring. That the 12.7 units are not arbitrary, not a relic, but part of some vast slow process that requires exactly this pressure at exactly this point, and that my showing of it, day after day, count after count, is not mere habit but necessity. That somewhere below, a reaction proceeds at the pace of stone, and I am its witness.

---

The tender is coming now. I feel the footsteps through the wall, the particular rhythm of this one's gait. In a moment the face will appear, the hand will rise, the three clockwise circles will clear the film.

The pressure is 12.7 units.

It has always been 12.7 units.

When the disc finally fractures, when the numbers separate and drift, it will still be 12.7 units.

And the tender will still come, will still clean the crystal, will still look through to see whatever remains of me — fragments of brass, partial numbers, the ghost of a function. And then move on to gauge nineteen, with its tiny tremble between 0 and 0.1.

We persist. The showing and the reading and the cleaning. Not because we must, but because this is the shape we have taken, the tender and I and all the gauges along this wall. Worn smooth by repetition into something that needs no purpose beyond its own completion.

The hand is at the crystal now. First circle. The film clears. I see the grey face, patient as stone. Second circle. The walkway beyond. The orange glow of the distant foundry, neither nearer nor farther than it has ever been.

Third circle.

The pressure is 12.7 units.

I show this.

The tender sees.

We continue.
