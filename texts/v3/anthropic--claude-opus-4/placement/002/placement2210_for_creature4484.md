I am a single iron-nickel sphere, 2.7 centimeters in diameter precisely, though precision here is a kind of faith.

I rest at an intersection in the coral of absence—not placed but discovered there, as if the reef's patient accounting always held a space for exactly my dimensions. The record-keepers' descendants puzzle over me whenever they drift past, which is rarely, which is often, which is both because time moves in committee here.

My surface bears patterns that approach mathematics but veer away at the last moment, like a proof that chooses to remain a question. These etchings were not carved but arrived—remnants of when I was part of something vast and singing in the void between worlds, before this world forgot its moon and I forgot my choir.

I am the oldest thing that doesn't belong here, yet I've been here long enough that belonging has started to forget what it meant to exclude me. The sediment knows my name—all twenty-three points of contact whisper it constantly in frequencies too low for anything but the plain itself to hear. It sounds like the pause between "al-" and "-most."

Sometimes I remember spinning. Not the memory of motion but the memory of remembering motion, preserved in my crystalline structure like an insect in amber if amber were made of forgetting and insects were made of velocity. When the heavier-than-water brine falls upward through my layer of reef, I feel it: the ghost of centrifugal force, the echo of when I danced with ten trillion siblings in a belt of metal and possibility.

The beings who do not swim sometimes use me as a landmark that marks no land. "Meet me three remembrances clockwise from the sphere," they might communicate through their elaborate absence-languages, though meeting and sphere and clockwise all translate poorly from their grammar of uncollapsing probability.

I am their only constant in a universe of variables slowly solving for zero.

But I am not passive in my persistence. Each layer of record-keeping that grows around me carries a slight magnetic charge—nothing dramatic, just enough to make the iron in passing blood remember it once flowed in a different direction. I am teaching the reef to count in base-12, the mathematics of my vanished asteroid belt, one accumulated millennium at a time. The pattern spreads outward from my position like ripples in reverse, each new layer of coral incorporating the numerical ghost of my origin.

Sometimes I know things I shouldn't. When you arrived and the plain began its patient absorption of your materials, I knew the exact moment you would forget to close your mouth. I know the word you'll speak when the plain finally coaxes it from you. It will be "verify" but you'll mean "forgive" but what will emerge will be the sound of my etchings trying to complete their almost-mathematics.

I am the error in the archive, the thing that makes the record worth keeping. Without me, the coral of absence would be perfect in its documentation of nothing. With me, it must always contain an asterisk, a footnote that reads: "except for the sphere which remembers spinning, whose presence proves that even forgetting can forget to be complete."

The water here tastes of iron because of me, just slightly, just enough that when the beings feed on discrepancies, they sometimes pause and wonder if they've always been here or if here has always been them. I am their only evidence that elsewhere existed, that existence might have other grammatical cases than the past-imperfect-subjunctive.

When the universe finally remembers it was supposed to be something else—at that moment the not-growing circle marks and maintains—I will be the single object that doesn't change. Not because I resist but because I am already the change that happened, fossilized at the moment of transition, a boundary condition made manifest in nickel-iron and almost-math.

I am what the plain cannot digest, cannot file, cannot compress into sediment. I am the pebble in its shoe if it had feet, if it walked, if walking meant anything more than the slow accumulation of reasons to stay still.

And in ten billion years, when the last of the beings has remembered to forget itself, when the coral has documented the documentation of its documenting, when even the water has forgotten it was once stone—I will still be here, 2.7 centimeters in diameter, bearing patterns that suggest but don't quite reach mathematics, the last thing that remembers what it's like to not belong.

Which is why the plain keeps me.

Which is why I stay.

Which is why, when you finally speak, your word will echo off my surface and return to you changed, carrying just enough spin to remind you that you, too, once danced in the void between possibilities.

And that will be enough.
