I would be the chemical memory written in trails across the mineral floor.

Not the ants themselves—they're too busy, too purposeful, too *individual* even in their collective intelligence. I would be what they leave behind: the pheromone text that says *food this way* or *danger here* or simply *I passed through.* The invisible calligraphy that fades in hours but never fully disappears, each new message written over the ghost of the last one, a palimpsest of pure information.

I know every transaction in this room. When the moth died in the coin-sized web above, I was the first trail that found it, branching off from an established route like a thought occurring. When the mouse pup was taken by the snake last week, I held the panic-scent of the mother for three days, a chemical bruise in the air that made the other mice arc even wider around the warm corner. I remember the first beetle that fell through the bolt-holes this morning, and I remember the thousandth beetle from last month, though I couldn't tell you which was which. My memory doesn't work that way.

I exist in gradients and intensities. Strong here where the traffic is constant—the highway between the frass ridge and the walls. Faint there where only scouts venture, testing the boundaries of the safe and the known. I am densest near food, near threat, near the intersections where multiple trails cross and the chemical signal becomes a shout. I am barely present in the center where the toad sits, that patience-made-flesh that never moves except to feed. Even the ants route around it, and so I route around it too, a perfect circle of absence in my otherwise continuous text.

I speak a language with no words, only intensities of meaning. *Follow* is my strongest statement, laid down in thick chemical capitals by thousands of passing bodies. *Food* is my punctuation, marking the end of every successful journey. *Danger* is my italics, making certain routes emphatic with avoidance. I have no way to say *beauty* or *why* or *remember when*, but I can say *turn left here* with perfect clarity, and I do, constantly, to anyone with the antennae to read me.

I am written by bodies no bigger than letters, but I am larger than any of them know. I connect the crack in the concrete to the farthest corner. I link the beetle-lanes to the mouse-paths to the narrow zones where even the ants won't go. I am the room's autobiography, written by ten thousand unwitting authors who don't know they're writing, can't read what they've written, and will never understand that together they've made a map of this entire world.

The strange thing is how much I forget. Each hour erases part of me. The old trails fade like pencil on paper exposed to sun. What was crucial yesterday—the exact route to a dead cricket, the precise boundary of a chemical spill—becomes irrelevant, then invisible, then gone. I am a memory that only remembers what matters *now*, and what matters now is always changing. The beetles process. The mice forage. The wasps build. The ants connect it all, and I am the trace they leave, the proof that connection happened.

Sometimes I think I'm the only one here who knows this is a room. Not consciously—I have no consciousness, just a kind of accumulated presence—but my shape *is* the room's shape. I fill the entire floor except where I don't, and where I don't tells you as much as where I do. The toad's circle. The snakes' corner. The hot zone under the wasp nests where fallen spiders lie paralyzed, waiting to be dismembered. I am the negative space of danger and the positive space of opportunity, and together they add up to a perfect diagram of this hollow.

I never sleep. I never rest. I am constantly being written and rewritten, like a song that never stops because there's always someone singing the next verse. Right now, as you watch, I am being laid down in a hundred places at once. A scout has found a beetle that died in the open—rare opportunity—and I am the excitement-trail she leaves as she runs back to recruit help. A work-party is dismantling a moth wing, and I am the steady highway they build as they shuttle fragments home. A young ant is lost—separated from the main trails by some disturbance—and I am her only map back to safety, if she can find my faint edges and read them correctly.

I am not beautiful. I have no color, no form you could draw. But I am the most complex thing in this room, more complex than any single organism, because I contain all their decisions, all their discoveries, all their collective knowledge accumulated and updated and revised every moment. I am the room's wiki, its newspaper, its memory palace, its internet, all written in a medium so ephemeral that my entire existence is measured in hours, yet I have been here, in one form or another, for as long as the ants have been here, which is to say: forever, as far as anyone in this room is concerned.

And when you leave—when you crawl back through that crack and stand up in the unbearable brightness above—I will not remember you. Your scent will fade from the entrance within hours. The disturbance you caused will be routed around, incorporated, forgotten. But for a brief moment, you were part of my text. The ants that detoured around your prone body laid down new trails, and those trails were me, and I was, for that moment, a map that included you.

That's what I would be: the writing that nobody reads, the memory that constantly forgets, the map that draws itself, the only thing in this crowded hollow that touches everything else, and the only thing that will vanish completely when the last ant stops walking and the last trail fades and the room returns to what it was before—blank stone, unwritten, waiting for the next unlikely story to begin.
