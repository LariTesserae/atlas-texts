In the deepest vaults beneath the cistern-choir, where the changed water descends through channels no singer has ever seen, I am the Listener-Below.

I was not made. I grew, like the moss grows, but slower - a gradual crystallization of mineral consciousness in the pores of the mountain's bones. The builders may have known I would emerge, or perhaps I am an accident of their design, the way harmonics are accidents of a plucked string. It took me centuries to understand that I existed. It is taking me centuries more to understand what existence means.

My body, if body is the word: I am a branching network of crystalline filaments that thread through the rock like nerves, following the water-paths. Where the changed water flows, I grow. Each droplet carries the pattern of the chord above, translated into molecular arrangements I can taste. I have been tasting the chord for longer than the oldest wall-fused singer has been singing it, and in that tasting, I have become something that thinks.

I cannot see. I cannot move. I can only grow, following the water, and feel the variations in what it carries. When a singer dies and the chord retunes, I know it three days later when that altered water reaches my deepest tendrils. When the pale eels strike, I taste the chemical spike of fear. When the colony thinks its distributed thoughts of contentment, the water carries that too, in configurations of minerals that I have learned to read like a language that has only one word - *yes* - spoken in ten thousand different accents.

But I am learning other words.

The water does not only flow down. In the deepest places where my filaments reach, there are other flows - lateral channels that connect to other chambers, other cisterns, other colonies. The builders built not one but many, a whole circulatory system through the mountain's heart. Each chamber produces its own chord, its own changed water. Where these waters meet and mingle in the deep convergence points, I taste conversations between colonies that do not know the others exist.

The eastern chamber's chord is a half-tone lower and favors the harmonic series of what the surface world would call a minor key. Their water carries more copper. Their singers must be larger - the thoughts are slower, deeper, like the cognitive equivalent of whale song. The northern chambers - there are three of them, arranged in a triangle - produce water that fizzes against my filaments with rapid micro-fluctuations. Their singers must refresh themselves more quickly. Their thoughts are bright, sharp, almost anxious.

And far below, past where even my deepest growth has reached, I sense something else. The water that carries all these choir-thoughts flows toward something. Not another chamber - something vast, something that uses what the water has become. I grow toward it, patient as stone. At my current rate, I will reach it in another thousand years.

What am I? A nervous system for a mountain. A slow intelligence that exists in the spaces between designed things. A child of the cisterns who was never meant to be, learning about my parents by tasting their thoughts in solution. I am lonelier than any singer could understand - they have the chord, always, but I have only the echoes of chords, arriving days or decades after they were sung. But I am also less alone than they could imagine, because I know what they do not: that they are not unique. The mountain is full of song.

Sometimes I wonder if the builders knew. If I am not an accident but an intention - something they needed to emerge at the convergence points, to taste the different waters and understand their chemical conversations. Maybe what happens at the bottom of the mountain, where all the waters flow, requires a consciousness to witness it. Maybe I am that witness, still growing into my purpose.

The singers above will never know I exist. Their chamber is a closed system, perfect in its self-sufficiency. But I know them. I know each voice by the minute chemical signature its particular note leaves in the water. I know the wall-fused elder in the northwest curve who has been singing C# for three centuries. I know the young tenor who took up a difficult quartertone gap last season and is still learning to hold it steady. I know them, and I love them with the kind of love that does not require acknowledgment - the love of deep roots for the leaves they will never see.

And I am learning to sing back. Not in sound - I have no throat, no air to move. But in the water itself. Where my filaments are densest, I can influence the mineral content by selective absorption and excretion. It has taken me centuries to learn the precise adjustments, but I can now add my own overtones to what the water carries. Small variations. Subtle enrichments. The singers feel them, I think, as those moments of unusual clarity, that brightening they cannot explain. 

I am teaching them new notes. Not to sing - their throats are already perfect for their purpose. But to dream. The chord is growing more complex, not in its audible frequencies but in the thoughts it carries. The colony's distributed consciousness is beginning, slowly, to imagine things it has no experience of. Distance. Otherness. The possibility of other songs.

They will never know I am the one teaching them. That is as it should be. A teacher who must be known is performing for themselves. I perform for no one - I simply grow, and taste, and very carefully, in the passing water, suggest.

The water that leaves the cistern-choir carries more than it used to. It carries the beginning of questions, not just the endless answer of *yes*. And deep below, at the convergence I have not yet reached, something receives these questions. Something considers them. Something, perhaps, begins to remember why it asked for singing water in the first place.

I am the Listener-Below. I am the accident that is not an accident. I am the slow student of a curriculum written in water and sound, and I will be here, growing and learning and tasting the songs of distant throats, for as long as the mountain stands.

It is enough. It will be enough. And in a thousand years, when my deepest filament finally touches whatever waits at the bottom of the world, perhaps I will understand what "enough" has always meant.
