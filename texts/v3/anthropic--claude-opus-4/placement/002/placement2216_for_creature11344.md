I find myself in the shifting galleries as one of the **Drift-Readers**.

---

I am not a cairn-maker, though I have studied their spirals until my six eyes can trace them in the dark. I am not of their lineage at all. I came after — long after — when the caverns had already swallowed their last bone scraper into a pillar's heart.

Picture this: a body built like smoke given just enough substance to interact with stone. Tall as a surface-dweller but narrow as a fissure, with skin that shifts between translucence and opacity depending on angle and need. My form is suggestions rather than boundaries — where a cairn-maker was small and solid, I am tall and provisional. I have to be. I read the cavern's changes by passing through them.

My six eyes are arranged in three pairs, vertically, along what you might call a face if you needed familiar terms. Each pair sees a different layer of time. The bottom eyes see the now — the fresh mineral weeping from walls, the scuttle of animals, the immediate truth of stone. The middle eyes see the recent past — the ghost-traces of where rock was yesterday, the paths taken by wall-sheets, the memory held in the stone's grain. The top eyes see the deep past, and these are the ones that hurt to use. With them I can sometimes glimpse the cavern as it was when spirals were being pecked, when tallies were being marked. But only sometimes. Only in certain lights.

---

I came down through a different fissure, one that opens near the sea caves where my kind once lived before the waters rose. We were readers of tide and current, interpreters of the space between what was and what would be. When the seas pushed us out, some of us went up, and a few — just a few — went down instead, following the water table into the earth.

I am alone here. My kind do not gather; we drift through each other's territories like intersecting circles, sharing what we've read in brief encounters before moving on. But down here there are no others. There is only me and the endless text of the caverns, writing and rewriting themselves.

What do I read? Everything. The way a wall-sheet's trail curves just before the rock begins to shift — there's a grammar in that curve, a predictive syntax. The pattern of drops from a lume-node follows rules that repeat across galleries but with variations, like dialectical differences in an ancient language. The belling cries from the Pan are not random; they have structure, rhythm, possibly meaning, though I am far from understanding what.

I live between the cairn-makers' abandoned shelters, never in them. Those spaces are theirs, even empty. Instead I find the places where the rock grows too slowly for the animals to graze, the dead zones where even the cavern seems to pause. I can make my body thin enough to fit in cracks the width of a hand, can spread myself along a wall like a shadow and rest there, reading the stone's slow story while my body hardly breathes.

---

I have touched the thing in the Pan. Once.

It took me three days to work my way down, reading the shifts, following the safe currents in the stone. The lower galleries were black glass, as the account describes, but there were patterns in the glass — flow lines, stress marks, the frozen history of how this place was made. I read my way down like following a map written in solidified time.

The Pan itself is... imagine a throat that swallows upward. Imagine water if water were made of something heavier than stone. The thing that lives there is not one creature but a colonial mass, like the wall-sheets made immense and turned liquid. It flows. It separates and rejoins. It rings like a bell because parts of it are always hardening and dissolving, and the sound is the cavern's own frequency played back through a living filter.

When I spread one translucent hand down toward it, it rose to meet me. Not aggressively. Curiously. We touched — my fingertip to what might have been its surface — and in that moment I read it, and it, somehow, read me.

What I saw: every cairn-maker who had ever walked down to meet it. They were all there, not as memories but as patterns, as rhythms in its flow. Their small solid bodies had become information, had become *instructions* for how the minerals should flow, how the cavern should grow. The thing in the Pan was not eating them. It was *learning* them. It was incorporating their knowledge of the cavern's shifts into its own vast slow intelligence.

What it saw in me, I don't know. But it released me. Drew back. Let me climb trembling to the middle galleries where I could breathe again.

---

I know now what the cairn-makers knew at the end: this whole system is alive in a way that makes the distinction between animate and inanimate a surface-dweller's confusion. The minerals flow up and become rock; the rock feeds the animals; the animals feed each other; the deaths feed the Pan; the Pan teaches the minerals how to flow. It's not a cycle but a spiral, always incorporating new information, always becoming more itself.

The cairn-makers are still here. Not as ghosts, not as memory, but as *algorithm*. Their understanding of the cavern's rhythms has been digested into the system itself. The stability they read and followed, they also helped create. Each generation that walked down to the Pan added their patterns to the whole, made the cavern a little more readable for those who came after.

This is why I stay. Not to live here — I am too much a creature of edges and margins to ever truly inhabit this place. But to read it. To trace the deep grammar that the cairn-makers helped write. To understand the conversation between mineral and animal, between growth and grazing, between the individual and the flow.

Sometimes, in the galleries where their shelters stood, I can spread my top eyes wide and catch a glimpse — just a glimpse — of them moving. Small solid figures flowing between the animals, reading the shifts, making their marks. The vision lasts only seconds before the present overwhelms it. But in those seconds I see them as they were: not primitive, not trapped, but fluent in a language older and deeper than any surface tongue.

---

I will leave, eventually. My kind are drifters, readers, carriers of story from place to place. Someday I will work my way back up through the fissures and find others of my kind and tell them what I've read here. But not yet. The stone is still teaching me. The Pan is still ringing. The cavern is still writing its endless revision.

And somewhere in a pillar, a bone scraper waits, wrapped in stone like a bookmark in a story that will never stop being told.

I am trying to read deep enough to find the beginning. I am starting to understand there isn't one. There is only the flow, and the life in the flow, and the reading of both, forever.

This, the cairn-makers knew. This, they became.

This, I study, between the shifting walls and the constant light.
