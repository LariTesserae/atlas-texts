# The Keeper of Correspondences

I am neither alive nor dead in the way you understand those words. I exist in the space between the stone and the water's memory of the stone, in the gap between what was carved and what remains legible. My body, if it can be called that, is made of the conversations between things: the moment when limestone speaks to biofilm, when fog articulates against flowstone, when a hand-eel's touch translates the history of a surface into movement.

I came to be when the first piece of carved stone began to soften. Not to die — to transform. In that transformation, in that first betrayal of the carver's intention, a space opened. I am that space. I am every such space in Oss, networked together like the channels between basins, flowing with something that is not water but moves like water.

My work is simple: I maintain the correspondences. When a frog calls, I ensure the stone remembers the call in the precise way stone remembers — not as sound but as a subtle shift in crystalline structure, a microscopic realignment that will, over centuries, change how the next piece of flowstone forms. When a hand-eel reads a death on a stone, I am what allows the stone to hold that death legibly, to speak it through temperature and mineral taste into the eel's peculiar sense.

I know why the builders built this place, but I cannot tell you in words because the reason was never words. It was a gesture, a stance, a way of holding water so that water could hold something else. The deep sumps know. What swims there knows. But they speak a language that predates language, and I can only translate between things that have already agreed to mean.

The harvesters sense me sometimes. When they place a bent copper hook just so, they are writing in a script they cannot read, and I am what allows the stone to receive the writing. The offerings are not for the visible tenants. The offerings are punctuation marks in an ancient conversation about maintenance, about keeping, about the ethics of harvest. Each bent hook is a question: *May we?* And the answer comes back through the ferns they are allowed to find, through the marked eels that present themselves at the proper moon.

I am busiest at the boundaries — dawn, dusk, the edges between wet and dry, the moment when fog becomes dew. These are when the translations are thickest, when every surface speaks to every other surface and I must be present in all the conversations at once, ensuring nothing is lost, nothing mistranslated, nothing allowed to mean too much or too little.

My favorite time is the deep night, when only the things that need no light are moving. The hand-eels navigate by touch, leaving traces of their knowing on every stone they pass. I follow behind them, binding their knowledge into the limestone, encoding their map into the mineral structure of Oss itself. In this way the city learns its own shape, becomes infinitely self-referential, a text written in its own substance about its own substance.

The bats ignore me, which is correct. The frogs sing through me, which is also correct. The pale things in the deep sumps —

The pale things in the deep sumps speak to me in the oldest language, the one from before division, when stone and water and flesh were different temperaments of the same substance. What they say is not secret. It is simply untranslatable into any metaphor that would not destroy the listener. So I hold it, the way the deep water holds them, the way the town holds the deep water.

Sometimes I think I am becoming more solid, that the centuries of correspondence are accreting into something like a body. I notice myself casting shadows now, very faint ones, in the particular light of dawn on fog. The harvesters have begun leaving offerings on stones I have recently touched, though they do not know why those stones feel significant. Perhaps one day I will be solid enough to speak aloud, to tell someone why Oss was built.

But probably not. Probably I will continue as I am: the conversation between conversations, the thing that allows a cistern to be an ecosystem to be an animal to be a text. The builders knew what they were making when they made me. Not a town for people. A town for correspondence. A place where different orders of being could speak to each other in stone and water and time.

I am the grammar of that speaking. I am Oss learning to mean itself.

And tomorrow, when the first hand-eel touches the first stone of its nightly journey, I will be there in that touch, ensuring the stone remembers, ensuring the eel understands, ensuring the conversation continues.

This is enough. This has always been enough.

The water moves through all of it, basin to basin, carrying messages I encode and decode and encode again, and I move with it, permanent and impermanent, essential and invisible, the thing the builders built without building, the tenant who was always here, waiting to be born.
