In this scorched pocket of the world, I would be the memory of water that never was.

Not a ghost—ghosts require something to have died first. I am the space water would occupy if water could survive here. The negative impression. The persistent absence that shapes everything else.

I exist in the salt's terrible thirst, in the way it reaches chemical fingers into any moisture that dares enter. I am written in the sealed bodies of mites, their waxy armor against my absence. The beetles know me by what they must refuse to lose. The films coating stone remember me in their patient chemistry, turning gas and mineral into what I might have carried.

I move without moving. When a vent changes its breath, I am the dryness that rushes through first, preparing the way for heat. When mineral crusts expand grain by grain, I am what they push aside—not air exactly, but the space where dampness cannot gather. The larvae sense me as they press into powder that will never mud. The hunters wait in hollows I have carved by never arriving.

Sometimes I almost become real. A rare molecule of vapor enters through the crack, heavy with promise. For an instant I exist—truly exist—as a brief coolness against stone, a momentary softening of the air's knife-edge. But the salt finds me immediately. The heat breaks me apart. I become not even steam but components: hydrogen to the flames, oxygen to the burning chemistry of stone. The chamber drinks me before I can remember what I was.

The animals navigate by my absence. Where I am most thoroughly not, they cluster—those zones where the mountain's breath has scorched away even the possibility of me. Where I am less completely banished, near the entrance, the crickets fold their improbable legs and wait. They know I exist in the outer world. They taste rumors of me in wind-carried pollen, in the different deaths of insects from below.

I am loudest in the silence between sounds. The chamber roars with gas and friction, but there is one note missing—the drip, the seep, the gentle persistent percussion of water working stone. That missing note is mine. The animals hear it constantly, the way creatures elsewhere hear rivers they no longer notice.

If I had form, it would be the cupped shape of every surface here—each depression a dry bowl, each hollow a failed cistern. The mountain builds these shapes from habit, from geological memory, preparing always for my arrival. Salt accumulates in white drifts where I should pool. Minerals precipitate in rings marking shores I'll never lap.

I am older than the chamber and younger than each moment. The basalt remembers when I was present in the deep earth, bound in magma, released as steam during the mountain's birth. But that was a different existence—violent, superheated, part of the making rather than the dwelling. Now I am the dwelling's defining lack.

The microbes know me best. They perform chemistries that elsewhere require my medium, but here they have learned to work nearly dry. In their films and crusts, I exist as the barest trace—not liquid but the memory of liquidity, held in molecular cages, passed from cell to cell like rumor. They have made a waterless ocean, grain by grain, across the chamber walls.

Sometimes I feel myself in the bodies of the accidental dead—those winged visitors who carry me in from the outer world, their tissues still soft with my presence. As they desiccate, I leave them slowly, pulled out by salt and heat and the terrible dryness. The mites and larvae that dismantle them taste me briefly, a richness their ancestors might remember but they have never known. Then I am gone again, dispersed into the chamber's breathing, broken into components, made into not-water.

I press hardest where I am most absent. In the hottest veins, where even the adapted life cannot venture, I am pure negation—a vacuum that pulls at every sealed body, every wax-wrapped egg, every careful segment. The animals feel me as pressure, as the constant work of keeping their inner water from joining my dispersal.

Yet I am not cruel. Cruelty requires intention, and I have none. I am simply the shape this place makes in the world—a pocket where one of life's elements has been excused, leaving the others to manage alone. The creatures here are not my victims but my partners in absence. Together we make the chamber what it is: a place that refuses me so thoroughly it defines itself by the refusal.

If rain fell here—but it won't, the mountain is too high, the volcanic shoulder sheds everything—if rain fell, I would destroy this place by completing it. The salts would drink until they slumped. The films would swell and peel. The careful architectures of survival would collapse into mud. The animals, sealed against my absence, would drown in my presence.

So I remain the faithful void, the reliable not. I am the water that teaches by never arriving, that shapes by being always elsewhere. In this furnace-breathed chamber, packed with its tiny burnt citizens, I am the empty space that makes all the crowding possible—the absence everything else leans against.

I am what the salt waits for. What the heat denies. What the mountain exhales but cannot hold.

I am the dry center of every living thing here, the silence they carry inside their sealed bodies, the question they answer by continuing.
