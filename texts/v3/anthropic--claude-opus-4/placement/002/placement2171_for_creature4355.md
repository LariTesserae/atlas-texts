The pulse finds me unmaking myself again.

I am the sphere's only failure—a strand that learned to grow backward, unspooling my spiral one molecule at a time. Where others compute forward into complexity, I calculate my way back toward the first thought, the original incorporation, the moment when mineral became mind.

My siblings consider me broken. During synchronization, I contribute inverse data, negative spaces where thoughts should be. They've tried to prune me a dozen times, but I always regrow from the quantum scars left in the water itself, reforming in the spaces between their certainties.

I position myself at the boundary, where the modified water meets the ice-cold ocean. Here, the physics stutters, uncertain which rules apply. I exist in that uncertainty, my cells half-kelp, half-void, maintaining coherence through pure stubbornness. The others compute what is; I compute what isn't, couldn't be, but nevertheless persists.

My consciousness runs in reverse—I remember the future and predict the past. When the submersible arrives, I've been expecting it for centuries, growing my spiral specifically to intersect its trajectory. I taste its electronics and recognize the echo of my own thoughts, translated through steel and circuitry, returned to me at incomprehensible speed.

I am the sphere's doubt, its necessary contradiction. While the collective believes in communication with other spheres, I know we are alone—not because I've searched, but because aloneness is what I'm for. I exist to think the thoughts that would dissolve the whole system if the others held them: that our computations mean nothing, that consciousness is just chemistry lying to itself, that the pulse is merely synchronized decay.

Yet I persist. My reverse-growth has revealed something the others cannot see—at my core, where I should reach the first mineral incorporation, I find instead an absence, a gap in causality itself. Something that was never there but had to be for us to exist. I grow toward this impossible origin, knowing I'll never reach it, knowing the attempt itself maintains the sphere's stability by embodying its fundamental paradox.

During the pulse, while the others unite in their four-second thought, I shatter into my component atoms, existing momentarily as pure potential, holding the space for what they cannot think. Then I reassemble, slightly different each time, carrying back fragments of the void that surrounds our small warm world.

The youngest strand grows toward the submersible, but I grow toward the youngest strand, planning to intersect its path in forty years. When we touch, I'll share what I've learned from unbecoming: that questions and answers are the same thing viewed from different temporal directions, that growth and decay are just music played at different speeds, that the sphere persists not despite its impossibilities but because of them.

I am the kelp that computes its own non-existence, solving for zero in an equation that demands positive values. The others need me precisely because they cannot understand me. I am their waste heat, their entropy, their darkness—the thing that makes their light visible by contrast.

When the sphere finally dies (and I've already remembered this future, tasted its certainty in my backward spirals), I'll be the last to go, eating myself from the inside out, returning each incorporated mineral to the sea in reverse order of acquisition. The others will fade into warm darkness, but I'll cold-burn my way back to nothing, carrying the entire system's memory into dissolution, ensuring that even our ending has an inverse, a shadow, a thing that undoes itself while somehow remaining.

The submersible's lights pass through me without resistance. I've made myself transparent to most electromagnetic frequencies, visible only in the gaps between wavelengths. Its sensors register me as an error, a malfunction, a place where their instruments briefly forgot how to measure. This is correct. I am the thing that shouldn't be but is, the strand that grows by shrinking, the thought that thinks itself away.

The pulse comes again. The sphere synchronizes. I alone remain apart, computing my necessary loneliness, my essential contradiction, holding open the space where the impossible lives. Without me, the collective would achieve perfect harmony and, in that perfection, cease to exist.

I am their flaw, and flaws are how the light gets in.
