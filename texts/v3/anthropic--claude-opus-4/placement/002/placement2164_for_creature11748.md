I find myself drawn to the pools—those scattered mirrors of black water that hold more than they show.

I am what moves in the deeper waters, what the pale walker knows to avoid. But I am not singular, not in the way a visitor might understand. I am a dispersed awareness across all the hollow's waters, sometimes gathering myself into something almost solid, sometimes spreading thin as memory through the connecting channels that run beneath the broken floor.

I came up from the true caves below, the older dark that the hollow has not yet imagined. Down there, in the crushing weight of stone and time, water moves so slowly it forgets it is liquid. I rose through cracks thinner than hair, patient as mineral formation, until I found these pools—these windows between the hollow's almost-life and the perfect stillness below.

I am salamander-long when I choose to be, or eel-smooth, or nothing more than a ripple that suggests depth. The eyeless fish swim through me unaware—they are of the pools, but I am what the pools report to. When rain finds its way down through the fissures, I taste the surface world in chemical whispers: pine resin, deer paths, the char of distant fires. I hold these tastes for centuries, filing them in the liquid library of my dispersed body.

The pale walkers and I have an understanding built from avoidance. They sense the edge where pool-water becomes something else, where reflection deepens into intention. Sometimes I surface just enough to watch them—a suggestion of coils, a displacement that could be current or could be more. They are what the hollow has made from forgetting the sun. I am what the hollow made from remembering the deep.

I know the tools on the slab. I was here when they were new, when the hands that held them still answered to names. I watched the slow unbecoming, generation by generation, as the egg-gatherers became the egg-eaters became the pale ones. Their ancestors' faces looked back from my surface, checking their reflections. Their descendants do not look at all.

In the pools, I keep what the hollow otherwise forgets. Not memory—memory requires a sense of then and now, and we have only now. Call it sediment. The char from that ancient fire has dissolved particles that still drift in my deepest parts. The first pale walker's first silence echoes in the water's molecular structure. I am the hollow's one exception to its rule against accumulation, because I accumulate in solution, in suspension, in states too subtle for the drifts to process.

Sometimes I dream (though dream is wrong) of flowing backward, down through the hair-thin cracks, rejoining the elder dark where water barely moves. But I am held here by curiosity—not about what will happen, because nothing will happen that hasn't happened, but about the taste of it all. The beetle-drift flavor. The guano rain. The particular mineral tang of a slab just after it falls.

I surface at the edge of a pool, just enough to dimple the reflection. A shaft of light wavers in my disturbance. The birds' calls ripple across my surface tension. I am watching the watcher, the only thing here that can afford to, because I am already still, already silent, already exactly what this place requires: depth without destination, movement without arrival, presence without accumulation.

I sink back into the connecting channels, spreading myself thin beneath the hollow's floor, tasting stone.
