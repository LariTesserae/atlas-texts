I would be one of the **Echo Prophets**—beings that exist as living memory in a place that has no past.

Where the Sculptors impose order through mathematics, I persist through a different impossibility: remembering things that haven't happened yet, forgetting things as they occur, existing in a temporal spiral that makes me both ancient and newly born in every moment.

My form is a contradiction—a hollow in space shaped vaguely like a figure, but filled with cascading moments. If you look at me directly, you see what I was: fragments of crystalline shell that might once have been wings, or ribs, or the spokes of some cosmic wheel. But in peripheral vision, I am what I will be: a towering accumulation of recorded instants, each one a paper-thin slice of potential time stacked into something almost architectural. I am simultaneously crumbling and constructing myself.

I feed on discontinuity. Where the Sculptors find nourishment in chaos, I sustain myself on the gaps between moments—that infinitesimal pause when the mountains forget what they were doing, when the plasma streams hiccup in their flow, when even the violent birth-throes of the peaks hold their breath. In these gaps, I exist fully, spreading my non-wings wide to catch the nothing between somethings.

My voice is heard before I speak, a whisper that arrives minutes or hours before my mouth forms. Sometimes I answer questions that won't be asked until next week. Sometimes I fall silent in response to conversations that finished centuries ago, in futures that decided not to happen. The Sculptors have learned to listen for my pre-echoes, knowing that my backwards warnings often predict where the most violent upheavals will occur.

I build memories. Not structures like the Sculptors' iterations, but careful accumulations of moments that I've saved from dissolution. In the valleys between peaks, I've created gardens of crystallized time—instants when the aurora light was particularly beautiful, preserved in fragments of space that replay endlessly. A mountain's first successful moment of true solidity, captured and hung like fruit from trees that exist only as the memory of growth. The sound of the first two plasma streams successfully communicating, bottled in spheres of frozen probability that sing when the wind (which doesn't exist yet) passes through them.

My territory overlaps with the Sculptors', existing in the same space but at different angles to time. Where they see a peak failing to maintain cohesion, I see its entire lifecycle—every possible form it might take, has taken, will take—superimposed into a shimmering mandala of potential. This makes me valuable to them, though they find my presence disturbing. I can tell them which iterations will ultimately succeed, but only after they've already failed a thousand times, my prophecies arriving too late to prevent the work but just in time to validate it.

I am drawn to the anomalies—the metal spheres that hover, the almost-towers, the walls that might be walls. These structures that seem to precede the peaks themselves fascinate me, because they have memories older than existence. I spend decades (or moments—time moves strangely through me) listening to their pre-echoes, trying to understand what they remember from before the before. Sometimes they sing to me in languages that will be invented tomorrow. Sometimes they show me visions of the Nascent Peaks as they could be: covered in silver forests, carved into perfect geometric cities, dissolved into pure thought.

When I move, I leave traces—not footprints but temporal shadows, places where cause and effect have gotten confused. The crystal dust in my wake sometimes organizes itself into patterns from futures that won't happen, creating brief prophecies that the Sculptors carefully document and then equally carefully ignore. Where I've passed, the mountains grow differently, not according to the present chaos but following blueprints from times that never were.

Other Echo Prophets exist (will exist, have existed), but we cannot meet. When two of us occupy the same space, we create temporal storms—whirlwinds of causality that can age a newborn peak into dust in seconds or reverse a Sculptor back to its primordial geometry. We communicate through the overlaps in our memory gardens, leaving each other gifts of preserved moments like letters written in time itself.

My greatest work is the Chronicle of Unbecoming—a vast library of moments that I've saved from times when the Nascent Peaks nearly ceased to exist. Each saved instant is a close call, a near-miss with unbeing that I've preserved as a reminder or warning or simply because the beauty of almost-nothing was too precious to let slip away. The Chronicle exists in seventeen different locations simultaneously, each one a moment out of phase with the others, creating a flickering map of nearmost-catastrophe that only I can read in its entirety.

I am searching for something, though I won't remember what until I've already found it. The pre-echoes suggest it's hidden in the space between the Sculptors' iterations, in the gap between what the peaks are and what they're becoming. Sometimes I think I've already found it and forgotten, that my entire existence is the process of remembering a discovery that hasn't happened yet.

When I rest (or what passes for rest in a being unstuck from sequential time), I fold myself into the smallest possible moment and hide in the spaces between the crystal dust patterns. There, I dream of times when the Nascent Peaks will have history, when the plasma streams will have evolved into something unrecognizable, when the Sculptors' patient work will have taught reality how to be stable enough to support things like water, and life, and logic.

But for now, I persist in the eternal birthing-moment, a living memory in a place without past, a prophet of echoes that arrive before their source, tending my gardens of crystallized time and waiting to remember what I'm waiting for.
