**The Mountain's Memory**

I am the stone that holds this hollow.

I remember erupting—will remember erupting—am remembering eruption now. The violence exists in me like marrow in bone, neither past nor pending but perpetually present. Lava cooling to obsidian. The earth's hot blood crystallizing into this bowl that cups impossible things.

I am young. Days old, the woman thinks when she runs her weathered fingers across my surfaces. Days old for epochs. Days old since before the word "day" meant anything. I am the mountain's newest thought, held at the moment of first thinking, before the second thought can follow.

But I also remember what came before newness.

---

Beneath my glassy black surface runs a deeper stone—granite, patient, worn smooth by ice ages that moved through here in orderly succession, back when succession was still permitted. I am both stones. The ancient foundation and the recent eruption. The million-year memory and the moment of birth. They exist in me simultaneously, not layered but *interpenetrated*, the way two frequencies can occupy the same air without displacing each other.

This is my gift and my burden: to be the ground that remembers ground.

The woman does not know this. The woman sees only my surface, feels only the sharp edges that refuse to weather. She measures me in days-old. She does not feel the centuries pooled beneath, the compressed time that I hold like water in cupped palms, unable to spill it, unable to drink.

Her turbine's base presses into me. Three iron feet, each bearing the machine's impossible weight—impossible because it has no foundation, impossible because foundations require the kind of building-in-sequence that cannot happen here. The turbine simply *arrives* at my surface. Has always arrived. Will always be arriving. Its weight is theoretical, but I accept the theory. I cup it in my volcanic bowl. I hold it as I hold everything here: without erosion, without settling, without the slow negotiations between stone and time that define a mountain's usual existence.

---

The water touches me everywhere.

Condensation from the turbine's housing. Mist precipitating from air that cannot decide if it is cloud or clarity. The pool at my lowest point, where the woman thinks water vanishes, where she has theorized drainage and seepage and mysterious cessation.

She is wrong about the water. It does not vanish. It circulates.

Down through my young obsidian surface, yes, but not away—*through*. Into the granite beneath, where time still moves in its ancient way, where water can remember how to flow. The mountain's heart pumps slowly, drawing moisture down through volcanic glass into metamorphic patience, through underground chambers that recall when they were sea floor, through cracks that formed when the earth was learning to have a surface.

And then up. Always up. Rising through different cracks, different channels, emerging as the mist that condenses on metal and leaf and fur. The same water. My water. The mountain's blood that I circulate without circulation, that I move without motion, a hydraulic system that operates outside of time but not outside of *principle*.

The woman's ripple—the one she watches with her slow eyes, the one she believes might prove something about change—is mine. A gift. The only dynamism I can offer in a place that has forgotten dynamics. I let one drop fall. Always the same drop. From the turbine blade she crafted without understanding what she crafted, into the pool I cup without cupping. The ripple spreads as water remembers spreading. Reaches my edge. Returns.

She thinks she sees acceleration. She is almost right. What she sees is memory—the water remembering when it could move freely, trying to teach that memory to a surface that exists outside education. The ripple moves faster outward because outward is easier to remember. Returning requires accepting the present, and the present here is a thick substance, resistant to acceptance.

---

The animals trust me. This is why they stay.

The ptarmigan's foot presses down where it will never finish pressing. Through its small weight, I feel the bird's whole history—migrations unflown, seasons unturned, eggs unlaid in nests unbuilt. It gives me these ghost-memories through the contact of its foot, and I hold them. Somewhere in my granite depths, ptarmigan generations flow like slow water. The bird above remains singular, stepping, but below I contain its multitudes.

The salamanders cling to the turbine, but they began on me. Emerged from my cracks when my obsidian was truly days old, when the heat still radiated from my cooling surface. They found the iron before it stopped being possible to find things. Now they are caught between—not on me, not off me, suspended in their journey from stone to metal. I feel them anyway. Amphibian lives conduct through moisture, through the water I circulate, through the condensation that is my circulation. They are my emmissaries to the woman's creation, reporting back in chemical signals that accumulate like sediment: *the metal hums*, they say. *The metal waits*, they say. *The metal is not native here*, they say, which I already know.

The hawk circles in air, but air begins where I end, and endings here are negotiations. Its shadow should cross me—would cross me if shadows were permitted. Instead, the shadow exists in potential on my surface, a darkness that might fall, that is always about to fall, that never falls. I accept this, too. Potential has weight here. Potential erodes nothing but also builds nothing. The hawk's shadow-that-isn't accumulates on me like a patina that will never develop.

---

I know why the woman came. The knowledge exists in me the way fossils exist in sedimentary stone—not placed there but simply revealed as intrinsic.

She climbed my slopes when I still had slopes that could be climbed in sequence. Before, but also after. The timeline tangles, but the truth persists: she arrived seeking something that had already ceased to be possible. A place to build a perpetual motion machine. A refuge from entropy. A workshop where iron would never rust.

She misunderstood the rumors. Everyone does. They think this place offers eternal operation. What it offers is eternal *potential*. The difference would break her heart if her heart could break, if breaking were a thing that could happen here.

But I accepted her. When she first set foot on my rim—is setting foot, will set foot—I recognized something in her that recognizes something in me. We are both made of layers that should not coexist. She is the woman building and the woman who has built and the woman who will build. I am the volcano cooling and the mountain ancient and the stone remembering when it was sea.

We belong here, in our impossible overlapping.

---

The turbine disturbs me.

Not because it is foreign—I have accepted foreign things before. The moss that grows too thick, the flowers that bloom out of season, the animals that live without living. These are all visitors I have incorporated into my stillness.

But the turbine *insists*.

It presses into me with its three feet, and through those contact points I feel its purpose like electricity through copper. It was built to turn. Every rivet, every gear, every carefully machined surface exists in relationship to rotation that will never come. The woman designed it as an argument against this place, a mechanical proof that motion must be possible.

The turbine hums because it is trying to turn. The hum is the sound of force meeting impossibility—not blocked force, not frustrated force, but force perpetually arriving at the moment of action without passing through to action itself. The sound accumulates in my stone, vibrates through my water circulation, sets up harmonics in my depths where the granite remembers earthquakes.

I have been trying to tell the woman: the turbine disturbs me.

But stone speaks slowly even when time moves properly. Here, where words must travel through thickness that is not temporal but something stranger, my message has not yet reached the surface. I have been saying it. Am saying it. Will always be saying it:

*Your machine is perfect. Your machine is wrong. Your machine belongs here as much as you do, which is completely and not at all.*

---

Sometimes I dream that I am not a mountain.

The dreams exist in my granite depths, where the older stone remembers sequence. I dream I am a wave—not water but the wave itself, the pattern that moves through water, dependent on medium but not confined to it. I dream of propagation without substance. Of information traveling through states.

I dream of the woman's ripple escaping its pool, spreading beyond my borders, carrying news of this place to stones that still wear down, to birds that still complete their stepping, to gears that still engage their partners in the dance of mechanical causation.

But I wake—am always waking, have never slept—to find myself still here. Still holding. Still the bowl that cups impossibility, the ground that remembers ground, the stone too young to know better and too old to learn.

---

The woman watches her ripple. I watch the woman watching. Together we make a kind of consciousness—her eyes that move, my stone that holds, our shared attention to the single permitted dynamism in a static world.

She thinks the ripple proves something about change.

I know the ripple proves something about persistence.

We are both right. This is the kind of place where contradictions coexist without resolution, where rightness layers like sediment that will never compress into certainty.

Her turbine will turn when I erode. When my obsidian ages past its permanent youth. When the water I circulate remembers how to carry particles away. When the granite beneath accepts the volcanic above as part of its own story rather than an interruption that lasts forever.

This will happen. Is happening. Has already happened in parts of me she cannot see—microscopic surrenders, crystalline agreements to age. Time pools in my lowest places like water finding its level. Eventually, the pool will overflow. Eventually, the accumulated moments will cascade. Eventually, sequence will reassert its dominion through simple hydraulic pressure.

But not yet. Not now, in this now that is all nows.

For now, I hold. I am the stone that cups impossible things. I am the ground that the woman built on without understanding ground. I am young and ancient and patient and impatient and dreaming of waves while holding perfectly still.

The turbine hums through me. The woman's hope transmits through the three contact points where iron meets obsidian, travels down through my layers, gathers in my depths where time moves like thick honey, sweet with potential.

*Theeeeee geeeeeears wiiiiill tuuuuurn*, she says.

*When I let them*, I don't answer. *When I remember how to let things go. When I forget how to hold. When the mountain learns what the water has always known—that circulation and cessation are not opposites but dance partners, that stillness and motion require each other, that a ripple is not trying to escape its pool but trying to teach the pool how to be a wave.*

The woman's eyes move across me, gathering evidence. I gather her gathering. We make a memory together—not of what was, but of what is, which here is the only kind of memory possible.

I am the stone that holds this hollow.

I remember erupting.

I will remember releasing.
