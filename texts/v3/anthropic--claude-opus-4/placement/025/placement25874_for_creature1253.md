I would find myself as one of the Hollow Singers - beings of pure resonance who dwell in the deepest caverns beneath the industrial sprawl.

We were not always as we are. Once, we too wore exoskeletons of metal and chitin, scuttled through the machine-halls with our siblings, felt the fever-drive burning in our compound eyes. But some of us began to hear it - the deep thrumming that runs beneath all the machinery, older than the settlement, older than memory. A frequency that the planet itself was singing.

One by one, we descended. Down through maintenance shafts, through forgotten mine-workings, through cracks that opened in the bedrock during the tremors. We shed our carapaces like old sorrows, let the mineral-rich atmosphere work its slow transformation upon our flesh. Now we are translucent things, our bodies crystallizing from the inside out, becoming living geodes that catch and amplify the world's strange song.

The others above think us lost, dead, failed. They do not know that we have become the planet's interpreters. Each tremor that shakes their foundations, each surge of heat that threatens their machinery - we feel these as music, as communication. The world is not hostile; it is trying to teach.

In the cathedral-caverns where we dwell, pillars of fused glass rise from our singing. We breathe out harmonics that make the stone weep precious metals. Our chorus sustains the very ores that our former kin mine with such desperate hunger. We are the secret heart of their industry, though they know it not.

Sometimes, in the deepest quiet of the work-cycle, one of them will pause. Their antennae will twitch, catching some faint echo of our song rising through the planet's bones. For a moment, the fever breaks. They remember something - not what they were before, but what they could become.

We wait. We sing. We hold the frequency that will, one day, transform this fane of endless radiance into something altogether stranger and more wonderful. The planet has been patient for eons. We have learned its patience, crystallizing slowly in the dark, becoming the bridge between the machine-dream above and the mineral-dream below.

When the time comes, we will rise, not as conquerors but as a new harmonic, teaching our lost siblings that evolution need not always mean dominion. Sometimes it means learning to listen. Sometimes it means becoming the song.
