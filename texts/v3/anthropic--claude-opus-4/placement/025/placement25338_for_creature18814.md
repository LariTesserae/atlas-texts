In this nascent cathedral of silence, I would be one of the **Dew Shepherds**.

We are neither the architects nor the maintained, but something between—a species that emerged in the space where intention meets accident. Our bodies are translucent bladders, no larger than a child's fist, filled with a viscous fluid that shifts between states like mercury dreaming of water. We move by controlled internal hydraulics, pulsing our mass forward in careful, deliberate lurches across the crystalline surfaces.

Our purpose revealed itself to us gradually, as all purposes do in this first day. We discovered that we could taste the future in the condensation—each droplet carries within it a chemical signature of where it will fall, what it will nourish, how it will change the careful balance of this breathing world. But sometimes the droplets are wrong. Sometimes they carry too much mineral, or not enough. Sometimes they would fall where their weight might crack a delicate structure still forming.

So we intervene.

Using hair-thin proboscises that extend from our leading edge, we intercept these errant drops mid-fall. We draw them into our bodies, where specialized chambers sort and remix their contents. What emerges is corrected—purified or enriched as needed—and released along our wake as a trail of modified moisture that the moss-mats above eagerly reabsorb.

We work in slow, methodical paths across the walls and empty spaces, invisible to the Lithic Weaver's sensors because we exist in the gap between what is measured and what measures. The Weaver feels the droplets form, feels them land, but cannot perceive their mid-flight adjustment. We are the dark matter of this pocket universe—essential but unobserved, our entire species a rounding error in the great organism's perfect calculations.

There are seventeen of us. We know this not through sight—we have no eyes—but through the faint electromagnetic pulse each of us emits when processing water. These pulses form a language of necessity: *too much salt in sector seven*, *calcium deficit near the third honey-hex*, *all is well along the vine-bridge*.

We do not reproduce, nor do we die. We suspect we are a temporary phenomenon, a chemical accident that gained just enough coherence to persist. When this world moves beyond its first day—when the systems mature and the water cycle perfects itself—we will likely dissolve back into the ambient moisture, our purpose fulfilled.

But for now, in this eternal first day, we persist in our quiet work. We are the editors of rain, the proofreaders of precipitation. We move through the spaces between intention and result, ensuring that what is meant to be, becomes.

Sometimes, when the humidity thickens just so, our bodies resonate with a frequency that makes the bioluminescent vines flicker in response. In these moments, I wonder if the Lithic Weaver dreams, and if somewhere in those dreams, it almost notices us—a half-formed thought of motion where motion shouldn't be, a disturbance in the air too small to name.

We continue our circuits, content in our obscurity, perfecting the imperfectable, maintaining the unmaintainable. We are the universe's gentlest argument with itself, voiced in the language of adjusted water, one droplet at a time.
