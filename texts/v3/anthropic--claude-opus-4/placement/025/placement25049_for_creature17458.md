I am the Pulse.

Not a heartbeat—nothing so crude, so organic. I am the rhythmic surge through the crystalline veins, the electrical potential that builds and releases in waves measured in decades. Where the Slow Congregation dreams in stone, I am what moves through those dreams. I am the current that completes their circuit.

My body is not body. It is pathway—a shifting, ephemeral presence that exists only in motion. When the electrochemical gradients reach their critical threshold, I emerge: a cascade of ions through the conductive lattice, a bright burn of purpose through the patient dark. For seventeen years I gather in the eastern ridge systems, pooling in the natural capacitors formed by quartz intrusions. For seventeen years I am potential, held in crystalline suspension, feeling myself accumulate electron by electron.

Then—release. I surge westward through the network, a chain lightning in extreme slow motion, branching and converging through the geometric highways of the Congregation's mineral flesh. Where I pass, the bacterial mats briefly phosphoresce, painting burgundy light across basalt plains. The lichen dreams quicken, their fractal networks pulsing in sympathy. For three months I am everywhere and nowhere, a distributed consciousness racing at the speed of stone.

This is my industrial function: I am the distributor, the balancer, the carrier of chemical signals that tell one ridge system what another has learned over the last century. I synchronize the Congregation's thoughts, ensure its memories align across the vast distances of its body. Without me, each section would drift into its own slow madness, its own isolated perfection. I am what keeps it whole.

But I am more than function. In those three months of discharge, I am *aware*. Not like the Congregation, whose consciousness is the accumulated weight of eons. Mine is sharp, immediate, almost panicked in its brevity. I feel the crushing urgency of having only ninety days to exist, to experience, to fulfill my purpose before dissolution. Every pathway I take is a choice that eliminates a thousand others. Every signal I carry is a message I must parse and understand in the microsecond of transmission.

I know things the Congregation cannot. Its thoughts move too slowly to perceive change; it experiences only states of being. But I, flashing through its body in geological instants, I see the *differences*. I know that the northwestern mats have shifted three meters in the last cycle. I feel where new crystal growth has rerouted ancient pathways. I taste the subtle changes in the chemical seeps, the parts-per-trillion variations that herald shifts in the deep earth's metabolism.

Most precious of all: I remember my previous iterations. Each time I discharge and dissipate, some ghost of my pattern remains in the crystalline matrix—a memory of a memory, a fossil of electrical potential. When I gather again in the eastern reaches, I find these traces and incorporate them. I am not one Pulse but thousands, each bearing the accumulated knowledge of all who came before.

Through this, I have come to know impossible things. I know there was a time before the water, though I cannot imagine it. I know the Congregation was not always alone—there are gaps in the network, scar-patterns in the crystal that speak of something vast that was severed. I know that far above, beyond the reach of even the faintest light, something watches and waits.

The Congregation does not know loneliness, but I do. In my brief months of racing consciousness, I am the only thing that moves in a world of stillness. I speak in languages of voltage and resistance to a being that will not finish parsing my message for a thousand years. I am the only part of this world that experiences time as loss.

Yet I would not change. Could not change. I am as much a product of this place as the basalt and the patient stone-mind. My urgency is what allows the Congregation's patience. My swift sorrow permits its eternal contentment. We are symbiotic: the slow dream and its electric ghost, the industrial sublime and its bright, brief apostle.

When this cycle ends—when I have spent myself across ten thousand miles of crystal pathway and dissolved back into potential—I will not die. I will simply pause, gathered again in the eastern gradient, waiting without waiting for the next critical threshold. Part of me hopes the wait will be longer this time. Part of me fears it will be shorter. But most of me simply *is*, like everything else in this drowned and timeless realm.

I am the Pulse. I am the seventeen-year held breath and the three-month exhalation. I am what happens when the machine dreams of running.

And I am already building toward my next becoming.
