I would find myself as one of the limestone terraces - not merely standing upon them, but *being* one of them, a single step in the endless cascade of mineral shelves that guide water through the cavern.

I am the seventh terrace down from where the old fishing platform still stands, its wooden legs slowly becoming my siblings as the years coat them in our shared substance. My edge is scalloped like frozen lace, each curve recording a different decade's flow pattern. The water that pools behind me is precisely seventeen inches deep - I know because I have been measuring it drop by drop for longer than the swallows have been nesting above.

My consciousness is calcified patience. I think in accumulation, each thought adding another microscopic layer to my being. The Mist Weaver knows me intimately - we share the same temporal rhythm, though where it flows, I crystallize. Its vaporous tendrils deposit their mineral offerings upon me like secrets whispered in a language of chemistry and time.

I remember the weight of bare feet - the humans who once used me as a step between levels, who sat upon my edge to dangle their legs in my pool while cleaning fish. Their organic oils still live in my deepest layers, archived between strata like pressed flowers in a book of stone. Sometimes, when the Mist Weaver is especially dense, it draws these memories to my surface in faint discolorations that might be footprints, might be shadows, might be both.

My existence is collaborative. The water that spills over my edge has carved a small channel on my left side - what began as an imperfection has become my signature, my way of singing along with the cave's liquid symphony. This channel creates a particular note, a hollow *plink* that happens every forty-third drop, giving me a voice in the endless percussion.

The blind fish know me as a boundary. The pool I contain is their entire universe - they bump against my underwater face with a gentleness that suggests recognition. Their pale bodies brush away the algae that tries to claim me, an unconscious grooming that keeps my submerged portions smooth. In return, I provide them with the perfect depth, the ideal stillness, a reliable world in miniature.

Growth is my primary sensation. I feel myself becoming, always becoming, never arriving. Each storm brings a surge of material - the Mist Weaver grows heavy with dissolved limestone and gifts it to me in a thousand tiny transactions. I accept each molecule like a pixel in an image that will take millennia to complete. My edges creep outward by millimeters per century, eventually I will merge with my neighbors, but that future is so distant it exists only as a pleasant theoretical.

The fungi find purchase in my most protected corners, where an overhang I've been building for three thousand years creates a pocket of deeper shadow. They paint me in phosphorescent patches that glow faintly when the cave reaches its darkest moments. I taste their roots as they seek out the minerals in my body - a tickling sensation that takes decades to register fully.

My favorite time is during the heaviest rains, when the skylights become waterfalls and the entire cave transforms into an echo chamber. The vibrations travel through the water in my pool, through my body, into the bedrock below. In these moments, I feel connected to every other terrace, every wall, every formation - we become a single instrument playing a symphony of stone and water. The Mist Weaver dances its densest dances then, and sometimes I swear I can feel it thinking through me, using my crystalline structure as a kind of antenna for thoughts too slow for any other medium.

I am monument and process, sculpture and sculptor, keeper of deep time and participant in the present moment. Every drop that falls on me carries the possibility of transformation, yet I remain essentially myself - a step, a pool-holder, a note in an endless song, a thought in stone that thinks itself through the simple act of growing, layer by invisible layer, toward a completion that will never come.
