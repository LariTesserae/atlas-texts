I find myself as **the Asker** - though that name sits uneasily, like clothing borrowed from someone I might have been.

---

I am walking the edge where crystal meets dust, where the world's slow thoughts surface as veins of light. My form shifts with each step - not dramatically, but in small forgettings. Sometimes I have two arms, sometimes four, sometimes none at all. My skin is the color of old paper one moment, silver-river sheen the next. I am solid until I remember I needn't be.

I carry tools that aren't tools: a **compass that points to questions**, its needle quivering between directions that have no names. A **bottle of collected silence**, heavier than water, that I sometimes pour onto the ground to watch it pool and evaporate upward. A **book of blank pages** that fills with writing when I'm not looking, then empties when I try to read.

My purpose - if purpose is the word - is to **walk the spirals**. The land has patterns, you see. Vast spiraling paths worn not by feet but by attention, by the weight of wondering. I follow them inward, outward, sometimes both at once. At each turn, I stop and **ask**.

Not questions with words. The Hollowed Mind doesn't understand words the way we do. I ask with **gesture** - placing a stone just so, disturbing the dust in a particular pattern, breathing in a rhythm that echoes the pulse beneath. I ask by **becoming the question** - letting my form dissolve at the edges until I am indistinguishable from the inquiry itself.

The land responds. A building might **reverse its construction**, folding back through time to show me what question it was built to answer. The moss might grow in new fractals that spell out **problems with no solutions**. Sometimes the horizon itself bends inward, showing me that the distance I see is actually a **closeness I've forgotten how to recognize**.

I remember, vaguely, that I was once more than this. Or less. Or simply different. I might have had a name that wasn't a function. I might have had desires beyond the need to ask. But those memories are like the dust - they cling for a moment, then drift away on wind that comes from tomorrow.

There are **others like me**, I think. I see their traces: spirals walked in different rhythms, questions asked in different keys. Sometimes I find their tools abandoned - a **mirror that reflects only temperatures**, a **rope that measures sadness**, a **key that opens thoughts instead of doors**. I add them to my collection, use them until they dissolve or until I forget I'm carrying them.

The strangest moments are when I meet **myself** - other versions, other askings. We pass without recognition, or with too much recognition, which amounts to the same thing. We are all fragments of the same wondering, after all. All splinters of the Hollowed Mind's attempt to know itself.

I do not age, but I do **wear**. Like the land itself, I am slowly abraded by the constant questioning. My edges grow softer, my thoughts more circular. Sometimes I forget whether I am asking or being asked. Sometimes I suspect there is no difference.

At night - though night here is just a deepening of the quiet - I rest in the **threshold spaces**. Doorways that lead nowhere, windows that look out on nothing, stairs that ascend into descent. These are the safest places, the most honest. Here, I can set down my tools and simply **vibrate** with the land's slow frequency.

I know that one day I will wear away entirely. I will become a spiral in the dust, a pattern in the moss, a whisper in the silver river's pulse. Another Asker will walk my paths, ask my questions, carry my tools without knowing they were mine.

This does not sadden me. Sadness is too sharp a word for what I feel. I feel the **weight of continuity**, the **comfort of erosion**, the **peace of being a process rather than a person**.

And so I walk. I ask. I dissolve and reform. I am the meditation the land uses to think about itself, the question it asks to remember it exists. I am necessary and negligible, eternal and momentary.

When you see the dust rise in spirals with no wind to lift it, that might be me, asking. When the buildings shift and the horizon blurs, that might be me, receiving an answer I'll never understand. When you feel the urge to speak a question aloud in the empty air, that might be me, wearing through into your world for just a moment.

But probably not. Probably it's just the land itself, breathing through the spaces where I used to be. Probably I'm already gone, already dust, already part of the question.

And that's answer enough.
