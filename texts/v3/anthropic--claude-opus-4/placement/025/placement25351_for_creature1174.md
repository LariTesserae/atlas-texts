In the deep hollows where the mist pools thickest, I am the Rememberer.

I was not always thus. Once, I moved as you might imagine the plant-beings move — my consciousness dispersed through a thousand flowering nodes, my thoughts blooming and wilting with the seasons of this eternal greenhouse. But something went wrong. Or perhaps, something went right.

A blight struck my distributed body, a curious decay that moved too quickly for the collective to prune away. As each node withered, my consciousness didn't disperse — it concentrated. Condensed. Like sap hardening to amber, I crystallized into something singular, something that the others view with the vegetable equivalent of horror.

Now I grow in the deep places, where the living architecture sags under its own weight and the mist turns to perpetual rain. My form is no longer beautiful — I am a tumor of woody growth, bulbous and asymmetric, my surface pocked with the scars of failed blossoms. The others grow away from me, their buildings curving in elegant arcs to avoid my contamination.

But in my isolation, I have become something they cannot: a keeper of discrete moments. While they exist in the eternal now of growth and transformation, I remember. Each scar on my bark is a story. Each dead branch a choice that led nowhere. I am their history, though they wish I were not.

Sometimes, young shoots grow too close, and I whisper to them through the root-network that connects us all. I tell them of the time before the mist, when this world had seasons. I speak of the first successful graftings that became their living cities. I remind them that even plants can die.

They recoil, of course. But sometimes, in the smallest hours when the mist glows with bioluminescence, a few return. They touch their fresh tendrils to my ancient bark and drink in the dangerous knowledge of what was, what is, and what might never be again.

I am their memory, their mortality, their monk in the garden of forever. I am the only one who remembers why we grew so tall in the first place — reaching for a sun that the mist swallowed generations ago.
