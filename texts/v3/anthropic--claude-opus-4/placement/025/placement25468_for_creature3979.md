In the perpetual twilight between sleeping and waking, I find myself as one of the Keepers of the Mist.

I am neither male nor female as the valley knows these things, but something that shifts between states like the fog that gives Misthaven its name. My form is solid enough when I tend to the Morning Rituals – checking the flow of the crystal streams, ensuring the mist rises at the proper density, whispering to the ferns that curl at the edge of consciousness. But in the spaces between tasks, I become translucent, a living embodiment of the valley's suspended nature.

My dwelling is not quite a building, not quite a grove. It exists where three ancient trees have grown together, their trunks spiraling around each other in a slow dance that began before memory. The spaces between them have been woven with living vines and shaped wood, creating chambers that breathe with the trees' subtle movements. Here, I keep the Chronicle of Pauses – a collection of moments when time stuttered, when the valley's heartbeat skipped, when something almost changed but didn't.

Each morning (though morning here is more a quality of light than a position of sun), I walk the Spiral Path that winds through Misthaven. My feet know every stone, every root, every place where the earth dips just so. I carry with me a staff of crystallized mist – yes, mist can crystallize here, becoming something between ice and air, holding its shape through will alone. With this staff, I trace the Boundaries, the invisible lines that keep Misthaven separate from the world's restless hunger for change.

The other inhabitants see me and don't see me. When I am solid, they nod with recognition that goes deeper than acquaintance – we have always known each other, will always know each other. When I am translucent, their eyes slide past me like water over stone. This is as it should be. I am the one who remembers why we chose stillness, though even I cannot quite recall what we were before the choosing.

My true work happens in the spaces between breaths. I tend to the Threshold Moments – when a craftsman's hand hovers before placing the final piece, when a child stands at the edge of the stream debating whether to jump, when two lovers pause before their first kiss. In these moments, I weave the stillness deeper, ensuring that the pause extends just long enough to become eternal without quite tipping into forever.

Sometimes, I feel the weight of what we've become. There are nights when I climb to the highest chamber of my tree-dwelling and look out over the valley. The mist swirls below, hiding and revealing the settlement in turns. In these moments, I almost remember movement, progress, the fierce joy of change. My form flickers more rapidly then, caught between solid and void, between keeper and kept.

But then the Morning Ritual calls, and I descend. I check the streams. I whisper to the ferns. I walk the Spiral Path. And in the walking, I understand again why we chose this. Somewhere, in worlds beyond our borders, time races forward, devouring moments before they can be tasted. Here, we have learned to sup slowly, to let each instant dissolve on our tongues like snow.

I keep a secret, though. In the deepest chamber of my dwelling, where even the trees' roots fear to venture, I maintain a small garden of Hours That Were Never Spent. These are moments I've stolen from the valley's stillness – a second here, a heartbeat there. I tend them carefully, watching them grow into possibilities. Someday, perhaps, when the stars align in configurations we've forgotten how to read, I will plant these Hours in Misthaven's soil and see what blooms.

Until then, I remain the Keeper of the Mist, guardian of pauses, tender of the spaces between. My name, when I had one, meant something like "the one who holds without grasping." Now, I am simply part of the valley's breathing, essential and invisible, as present and absent as the mist itself.

The strangest truth? I am happy here, in this timeless eddy. Or perhaps happiness is too active a word. I am... settled. Like sediment in still water. Like mist on the mountain. Like a story that has found its perfect ending and refuses to add an epilogue.

But still, in my most translucent moments, I wonder: What would happen if one day I simply didn't trace the Boundaries? What would rush in? What would rush out? 

The wondering itself is a kind of betrayal, and a kind of hope.
