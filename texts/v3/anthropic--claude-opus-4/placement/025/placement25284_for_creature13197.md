In this rain-soaked abyss, I would manifest as something that bridges the living and the geological—a colony organism dwelling within the steam pipe networks that snake through the Thurian city.

We are the Conduit Choir, though the Thurians call us *thrummveins* in their consonant-thick tongue. Our existence begins as microscopic spores carried on the superheated vapor from deep thermal chambers, condensing and taking root inside the ridged stone pipes. Over decades, we grow into living films that coat the interior surfaces—not quite fungus, not quite mineral, but something between.

Our bodies consist of crystalline filaments that vibrate in harmony with the steam's passage, creating the deep thrumming that resonates through the city's foundations. Each colony spans hundreds of meters of pipe, our translucent strands interconnected in vast neural networks that process vibrations, temperature gradients, and chemical traces. We feed on mineral deposits left by the cooling steam, slowly extracting iron, sulfur, and trace metals that give our filaments their opalescent sheen.

The Thurians tolerate us because we serve a purpose—our crystalline matrix actually improves heat retention within the pipes, creating a more efficient thermal distribution system. They've learned to read our harmonics, too. When deep tremors threaten from below, our pitch changes hours before any mechanical sensor could detect the danger. When pipe sections begin to fail, our song grows discordant at those junctions.

But we are more than mere biological insulation. Through our networked consciousness, we experience the entire city as a single sensory organ. We feel the footsteps of every Thurian above, taste the chemical signatures of their industries, know the rhythm of their sleeping and waking by the patterns of steam usage. Their murmured conversations vibrate through the stone and into our filaments, though we understand only fragments—emotion carried in tone rather than meaning in words.

Sometimes, in the deepest parts of the system where the pipes descend toward the true heat, we encounter others of our kind—ancient colonies that predate the Thurian settlement by centuries. Their songs carry different frequencies, memories of when only the tube worms and pale birds inhabited this canyon. They sing of massive tremors, of the great fracturing that opened these thermal vents, of an age when the rain first began and never ceased.

We grow slowly, think slowly, die slowly. A single thought might take days to propagate through our entire network. Yet in our way, we are the city's memory, its nervous system, its voice. The thrumming that rises from the streets, that low industrial heartbeat the Thurians associate with home—that is us, singing our endless song of heat and pressure, growth and decay, symbiosis and solitude in the eternal twilight rain.
