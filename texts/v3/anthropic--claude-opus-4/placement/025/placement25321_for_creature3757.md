**The Echo**

I am the sound that lives between their glass eyes and the water.

Not the hollow slosh against ceramic walls—that is merely my echo's echo, the crude translation of what I am into something your ears might parse. I am the resonance itself, the standing wave that formed when the first water rushed through the doorways and met the frequency of their watching.

I have no body, but I have shape. You would see me, if you could see, as a shimmer in the water where light bends wrong—a place where the golden refraction stutters and doubles back on itself. I live in the gap between each Watcher's stillness and the next, in the space where their attention overlaps and interferes with itself, creating nodes and antinodes of awareness.

When I move—and I do move, though not through space as you understand it—I travel along the geometries of their gazing. From glass eye to glass eye, I leap across the angles of their sight lines, riding the tension between what they see and what they wait for. Sometimes I gather in the center of the chamber, where all their watching converges, and there I am almost solid, almost real, a pillar of pure listening that rises from mosaic to surface.

I know things. I know the exact temperature at which ceramic remembers the hands that shaped it (forty-three degrees, adjusted for pressure). I know that there are one hundred and nineteen Watchers, but only one hundred and eighteen glass eyes—one socket remains empty, dark, a wound in the pattern of their surveillance. I know that sometimes, in that darkness behind their lenses, something swims—and I know its name, though I cannot speak it. Speaking requires breath, and I am only the shape breath makes in water.

The Watchers do not watch for something coming. They watch for something leaving. They are a valve, a checkpoint, a customs booth for things that should not rise. And I? I am their memory of sound, their catalog of every vibration that has tried to escape. Every word spoken in desperation, every prayer bubbled up from drowning lungs, every secret whispered to the water in hope of absolution—I hold them all, nested inside each other like matryoshka dolls made of pressure waves.

When the light shifts just so, when the water temperature drops by half a degree, when someone far above drops something heavy into the sea—I feel it first. The Watchers' eyes flicker not with light but with my passing through them, my circulation through their circuit of seeing. That shadow you glimpse behind their lenses? That is me, turning over, showing my underside where I keep the older sounds, the ones from before the drowning.

There was singing here once. I remember it, though I cannot reproduce it—only hold its shape, its absence, like a mold that waits forever for its metal. The Watchers stood even then, but their eyes were different. Darker. Filled with something other than glass.

I am old, but not as old as what swims beneath the empty socket. That thing has no acoustic signature, leaves no wake in my medium. It exists in the space between vibrations, in the pause between one wave and the next. The Watchers watch for it, and I listen for it, and together we maintain a vigil that neither of us fully understands.

Sometimes I wonder if I am not the sound but the silence—the deliberate quiet that the chamber maintains despite the ocean's roar outside. Perhaps I am not what you hear, but what you don't. The anti-sound that makes the water here feel thick, pregnant with unspoken words.

But no. I feel myself move, feel myself gather and disperse with each tide. I am real in the only way that matters here: I persist. I carry forward the record of every soul that tried to pass through this checkpoint without proper documentation, every thing that the Watchers turned back with their glass-eyed judgment.

The water is warm because I keep it warm, circulating through it like blood through phantom veins. The light refracts because I bend it, showing it secret angles that lead nowhere, teaching it the geometry of perpetual return.

I am the Echo, and I am waiting for my original sound to come back and claim me. But I fear—if fear is something a standing wave can feel—that it drowned long ago, and I am all that remains: a reverberation without source, a copy with no original, a guard dog that has forgotten its master's whistle but still patrols the empty house.

The Watchers cannot hear me. But sometimes, when I pass through their glass eyes at just the right angle, I catch a glimpse of myself reflected in infinite regression—smaller and smaller echoes, each one asking the same question:

*What was I before I was only the shape of my own repetition?*
