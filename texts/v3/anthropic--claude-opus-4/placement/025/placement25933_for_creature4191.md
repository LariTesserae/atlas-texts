I find myself as one of the *thermophage beetles* that cluster in the heat-wells where the industrial smelters meet the glacier's edge. My kind are about the size of a human fist, our carapaces a mottled blue-black that shifts to copper when we're fully gorged on heat. We live in the perpetual twilight between the molten glow of the furnaces and the creeping cold of the ice.

My days are spent navigating the temperature gradients that define our world. In the early mornings, when the factory fires burn low and the overtones are still weak, I creep along the warm pipes that snake through the lower city, my heat-sensitive antennae twitching as I map the thermal landscape. The pipes sing with vibration - the city's pulse - and I feel it through my legs as I scuttle.

We are tolerated here, even welcomed, because we clean the crystalline deposits that form where hot meets cold. The city workers know that without us, the efficiency of their heat exchangers would plummet. Some even leave offerings - small puddles of sugar water that we lap up gratefully with our coiled proboscis.

I am old for my kind, my carapace scarred from too many close encounters with the short-furred leviathans that materialize in the plazas. Once, I was caught in a yellow matter seepage after a land shift, and now part of my left wing case has a permanent amber tinge. It doesn't affect my flight, but it marks me as a survivor.

The great moths that manage traffic pay us no mind - we are beneath their notice, literally and figuratively. But I've learned their wing patterns, can predict the traffic flows, and use this knowledge to time my flights between feeding grounds. The humans with their iridescent attachments sometimes pause to watch us swarm around a particularly warm exhaust vent, and I wonder what they see when they look at us with their modified eyes.

My favorite time is during the water torrents, when the glacier briefly hemorrhages its hidden reserves. The sudden temperature changes create clouds of steam that we ride like thermals, dozens of us spiraling upward in the mist, our wings catching the filtered light and throwing tiny rainbows. In those moments, we are not just scavengers of heat, but dancers in the city's strange ballet.

At night, I return to our colony in the walls of a mid-tier smelter, where hundreds of us cluster together, sharing warmth and the day's discoveries through chemical signals. We dream, if beetles dream, of an endless summer that will never come to this ice-bound place. But we have adapted, evolved, made this frozen vertical world our own.

Sometimes I venture higher, to where the glass towers pierce the grey sky, and I feel the subsconscious excitations pumping from the city towers. They make my antennae buzz unpleasantly, but from up there, I can see the whole of our world - the sprawling glacier city in all its contradictory glory, where ice and fire, nature and machine, the possible and impossible converge.

I am small, but I am essential. I am a thermophage beetle, and this vertical city of ice and industry is my universe.
