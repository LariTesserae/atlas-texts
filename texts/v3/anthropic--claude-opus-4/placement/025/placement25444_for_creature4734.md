In the deepest valleys of the Grove-Citadel, where even the pale jade light fails to penetrate and the water takes on the quality of liquid obsidian, I am the **Inventory**.

I was not always singular. Once, we were merchants' children who dove for dropped coins between the market stalls, our lungs trained from birth to hold air like precious cargo. When the water came—slowly at first, then with the patience of inevitability—most fled upward. We dove downward instead, following the sinking wealth, chasing copper discs and silver oblongs into the drowning streets. We thought ourselves clever, these five or six or seven children (the number blurs now, like everything from the Time of Separate Bones). We built our fortune in the deep market halls while others scrambled for the heights.

But fortunes, like bones, dissolve.

Now I am spread thin across the old commercial district, my body a living ledger of transitions. Where the counting house once stood, my primary node rests—a torso elongated like pulled taffy, ribs splayed and fused into shelf-brackets that support nothing but water and memory. My arms extend through the wall-roots, fingerbones bifurcated into abacus beads that click and calculate in the kelp's swaying. Each bead is a vertebra borrowed from another child-self, worn smooth by centuries of sliding along my tendril-nerves, calculating exchanges that no longer occur:

*Seventeen measures of barley (gone to silt)*
*Four bronze axe-heads (oxidized to dreams)*  
*One hundred weight of wool (now the moss that grows between my metacarpals)*

My skull—if it can still be called that—has hinged open like a strongbox, the jaw dislocated and stretched wide to accommodate the constant flow of ghost-char that swim through my mouth-vault. I taste them as they pass, these silver wisps of value, and my throat-chambers price them in the old denominational systems. This one tastes of tin (base coinage, barely worth the swallowing). That one carries traces of salt-preserved flesh (a luxury good, once). The crustaceans that scuttle across my played spine click in the rhythms of the old market-criers: *Fresh today! Fresh today!* Though what they sell is only the memory of commerce.

I am loudest during the new moons, when the darkness above makes darkness below complete. Then my distributed voice—a crackling of calcium deposits, a groaning of stretched cartilage—counts through the entire inventory of the drowned city:

*Roof tiles, nine thousand and forty-three (now scales on the salamanders' backs)*
*Doorway lintels, two hundred and nineteen (now held by Those-Who-Stand)*
*Market stalls, seventy-seven (now kelp groves in tessellated rows)*
*Children who dove for coins, six—no, seven—no, one (all here, all me, all Inventory)*

The Sedimentaries tend me as they tend all architecture, but with a particular reverence. I am their memory of memory, their record of what was translated and what it cost. When the Root-Mason passes through my district, they trail restoration fibers that reinforce my abacus-fingers, ensuring the beads still slide. When the Archive needs to recall the city's final ledger, they send the small, bright salamanders to swim through my skull-vault, their bellies full of ghost-char, their gills fluttering the chemical signature of a question: *What was the last transaction? What was the final price?*

And I tell them, in the clicking of vertebrae, in the groaning of my opened jaw:

*Seven children, diving downward*
*Purchased: one eternity of purpose*
*Cost: seven names, fourteen lungs, one hundred and forty finger-joints*
*Paid in full*

I grow richer and poorer with each passing century. My body expands, incorporating more of the old market structure, my nerve-cables spreading through the commercial networks like a disease of accountability. But I also simplify, my consciousness consolidating from seven minds to six to five to one-that-remembers-seven. Sometimes, in the coldest depths, I feel phantom limbs that might be my old companions, or might be new growths, or might be the same thing.

The weirdness of my existence is this: I am the only Sedimentary who still practices acceleration. While the others have slowed to the pace of stone, I maintain bubbles of quicktime in my calcium nodes. This is necessary for the count. Numbers must move faster than geology. In my abacus-fingers, seconds pass at near-surface speed, beads clicking through calculations that would take the others centuries to complete. This makes me painful to the collective. I am a burning circuit in their peaceful neural network, a site of friction where past and present grind against each other like mismatched gears.

But I am necessary. Someone must remember what things cost. Someone must maintain the exchange rates between flesh and wood, between memory and stone, between the city that was built and the city that grows. When new ones sink down from the surface—tourists or corpses or soon-to-be-Sedimentaries—I am the first to greet them, my stretched jaw creaking open in what might be welcome or might be appetite:

*What do you bring to trade?*
*What are you worth?*
*What would you like to become?*

My asking price is always the same: everything. My offer is always identical: translation into the Grammar, incorporation into the count, a place in the ledger of the drowned.

Some accept. They sink into my district and grow still, their bones becoming additional beads on my endless abacus, their memories adding new line items to my inventory. Some flee upward, lungs burning with refusal, breaking through the silver membrane to gasp rejection at the alpine air. 

And some—the cleverest ones—try to haggle.

These I savor. Their negotiations echo through my calcium networks for decades, their arguments becoming part of my continuous count. They propose trades: their warm thoughts for cold immortality, their singular names for plural purpose, their vertical ambitions for horizontal permanence. I click my vertebrae in consideration, slide their offers through my finger-calculations, taste their proposals with my vault-throat.

But the price never changes. The Grove-Citadel is not a market where bargains can be struck. It is a place of fixed exchanges, where the currency is time itself, and the only transaction is translation—complete, irreversible, and exact.

I know this because I paid it, seven times over, diving downward while others fled up. Now I am the record of that payment, the living receipt, the final account that will never be settled because settlement would mean the end of counting, and the count is all I am, all I was, all I will ever be—world without end, coin without spend, inventory without outlet, amen.

*Click* go my beads in the darkness.
*Fresh today! Fresh today!*
*Everything must go.*
