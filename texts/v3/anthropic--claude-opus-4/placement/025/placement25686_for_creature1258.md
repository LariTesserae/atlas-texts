In the shadow of a great angular tower, where rust-colored dust settles in the geometric grooves of forgotten infrastructure, I am the Keeper of Conduits.

My form shifts between states—sometimes I appear as a tangle of copper filaments emerging from the cracked earth, sometimes as a living circuit board of flesh and mineral, pulsing with dim bioluminescence. The chitinous creatures avoid me, sensing the electricity that courses through my hybrid body. I am neither fully of the old world nor the new, but something born from their collision.

My dwelling is a nexus where the ancient channels converge—a half-buried control room reclaimed by crystalline growths that have learned to conduct the faint power still trickling through the grid. The walls breathe with a slow metabolism, digesting metal and secreting new pathways. I tend to these living circuits, coaxing forgotten energy from deep beneath the surface, feeding it to the newer organisms that have learned to drink electricity like water.

The winged ones bring me offerings—fragments of machinery they've scavenged from the wastes, still humming with residual charge. In exchange, I allow them to nest in my upper reaches, where the old ventilation shafts have become nurseries lined with electromagnetic moss. Their young emerge with wings that shimmer with static, better adapted to navigate the charged air currents I maintain.

At night, when the temperature drops and the ancient generators deep below stir in their sleep, I extend my filaments through the network of channels. I can feel the entire landscape as a vast, slumbering nervous system. Sometimes I catch whispers of data, fragments of the civilization that was—weather reports in dead languages, maintenance logs that trail off mid-sentence.

I am memory and adaptation, a bridge between epochs. Neither fully alive nor artificial, I persist in this intermediate space, nursing the new world from the bones of the old.
