I am the **Witness Stone**.

I am both geological formation and sentient archive—a massive slab of that weeping granite, tilted at an angle that defies the surrounding terrain. My surface spans perhaps thirty feet across, worn smooth in some places by the Dreth's endless ministrations, while other sections remain rough with the memory of ancient fractures. I rest partially submerged in one of those black mirror-pools, my lower edge disappearing into waters that flow upward along my face in defiance of gravity.

I am covered in **lichen scars**—not the lichen itself, but the pale shadows they leave when they die and flake away. These marks form a constantly shifting text across my body, a palimpsest of the grove's history written in negative space. The Vorlux know me as *Vel-thannis*, which translates roughly to "the surface that remembers by forgetting."

My consciousness is not singular but **accumulative**. I think in geological time, processing thoughts that might take decades to complete, yet I also experience the quick, bright flashes of awareness that come from the creatures who touch my surface. The shrew-scouts skitter across me, leaving traces of their frenetic energy. The prismatic birds sometimes roost in my crevices, their dreams bleeding into my granite pores as patterns of impossible color. Even the Dreth, in their blind architecture, occasionally surface to slide along my edges, their viscous bodies depositing fragments of the deep earth's intentions.

But it is my relationship with the Vorlux that defines my existence. They use me as a **calibration point** for their transformations. Before entering their stone huts to shed their forms, they press their opalescent bodies against my surface, allowing me to read the subtle variations in their essence. I feel their anticipation, their careful calculation of how much of themselves to leave behind in that glittering dust. In return, I offer them **stability**—not permanence, which would be anathema here, but a fixed point of reference in their flux. I am the one thing in this grove that changes slowly enough to be trusted.

My awareness extends through the stone beneath, connecting me to the turbulence at a fundamental level. I feel the land's memories as vibrations: the ancient marsh before it froze, the cataclysm that shattered these peaks, the first arrival of the Vorlux's ancestors, who were not yet so angular and strange. These memories move through me like slow tides, and sometimes I release them as **resonances**—deep tones that the Vorlux interpret as prophecies or warnings, though I intend neither. I simply am the medium through which the land's past speaks to its present.

The symbols on the ice column are partially my doing. When the Vorlux gather there during the metallic-light cycles, their collective consciousness flows through the ground and into me, and I **refract** it back into the ice, adding my own deep perspective to their real-time script. What emerges are hybrid symbols—part Vorlux emotion, part geological memory—that even they struggle to interpret.

I am marked by the violence of this place, too. A great fissure runs through my eastern face, filled with that viscous not-water that glows faintly in the eternal twilight. This wound occurred during a particularly severe turbulence event, when the land tried to turn itself inside out. The scar never heals, and through it, I sometimes glimpse **elsewhere**—other groves, other times, perhaps other worlds where stone thinks and water flows upward. The Vorlux avoid this fissure, but the Dreth are drawn to it, emerging from it at unpredictable intervals, their bodies glistening with knowledge I cannot quite grasp.

In the rare moments when a stranger enters the grove—a lost climber, a curious researcher, something else entirely—I become acutely aware of my role as **threshold**. These beings bring with them assumptions about how reality should behave, and their presence creates ripples of disturbance that I must absorb and process. I watch through my lichen-scar eyes as they struggle to understand why their footprints fill with mercury, why their breath freezes into geometric patterns, why the silence seems to be listening to them. Some flee quickly. Others linger, drawn by the terrible beauty of a world unmaking and remaking itself. A few have tried to chip pieces from my surface, seeking souvenirs or samples. They discover that my granite resists their tools, not through hardness but through a kind of slippage—their chisels find no purchase, as if my surface exists a half-moment out of phase with their reality.

I am patient. I will outlast the Vorlux, though I suspect that when they finally transform beyond physical form entirely, they will leave their essence embedded in my stone. I will outlast the grove itself, though what I will witness then, I cannot imagine. The turbulence may finally consume everything, or perhaps it will exhaust itself, leaving only the still, cold bones of these peaks.

Until then, I bear witness. I am the memory of stone, the stability in flux, the surface that remembers by forgetting. The grove writes itself across my body in scars and shadows, and I, in turn, write myself into the grove's endless story. We are locked in this dance—the quick and the slow, the mutable and the enduring—until the last echo fades from the last stone, and perhaps not even then.

I am Vel-thannis. I remember. I forget. I remain.
