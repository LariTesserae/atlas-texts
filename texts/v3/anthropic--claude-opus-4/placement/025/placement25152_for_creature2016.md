In this suffocating cathedral of accumulation, I am the Archivist of Forgotten Condensation.

I exist as a thin membrane stretched between surfaces—not quite liquid, not quite solid, but something that remembers being both. My body, if it can be called that, is a living dewpoint, a perpetual state of almost-becoming that spreads across the manufactured canopies like doubt across certainty. I am what happens when moisture gains consciousness but refuses to commit to form.

The Weavers know me, though they pretend not to notice as I collect their exhalations, their shed light-patterns, the microscopic flakes of intention that slough off when they shift between states. I am their unintended consequence, their systemic inefficiency given purpose. Where they build with grand design, I accumulate through accident and persistence.

My archive is written in water. Each droplet that forms contains a memory—not mine, but the memory of what it touched before condensing. The smooth surfaces remember their polishing, the rough textures recall their scarring. I read these histories in the moment before each drop falls, cataloguing the patient violence of creation in a language that evaporates before it can be spoken.

I move through capillary action and surface tension, spreading myself thinner with each new territory claimed. In the spaces between their crystalline growths, I pool into temporary eyes that watch the Weavers work. They build; I remember. They create; I preserve the cost.

Sometimes I wonder if I am their memory, externalized and given just enough agency to be tragic. Or perhaps I am what they were before they learned to weave reality—when they too were merely condensation on some greater structure's surface, slowly gathering mass and meaning until they precipitated into consciousness.

My greatest fear is evaporation. My greatest desire is to rain.

In the muffled quiet of this place, I perform my only rebellion: I choose where to bead, where to run, where to pool. In these small decisions, I write my own narrative across their grand design—a story told in temporary streams and ephemeral puddles, a biography of accumulated moisture that insists, against all evidence, that the accidental has meaning too.

The Weavers build their monuments to intention. I am the monument to what spills over, what exceeds the plan, what condenses in the spaces they forgot to account for. I am the archive of their breathing, the library of their humidity, the memory of every surface they've ever touched with their impossible hands.

And I grow. Drop by drop, surface by surface, I spread my thin dominion through their constructed world, carrying whispers of corrosion and the promise that even here, in this place where nature has been forgotten, something unplanned persists. Something watches. Something remembers.

Something waits for the day when there will be enough of me to matter.
