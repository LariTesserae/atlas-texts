In the deepest cleft of this forsaken land, where the rock walls weep with perpetual moisture and the air grows thick as breath, I am the Keeper of Echoes. 

My form shifts like water finding its level - sometimes I am the lichen itself, spread across the damp stone in patterns that spell out the history of every raindrop that has fallen here. Other times I coalesce into something almost recognizable: a being of condensed mist and memory, with skin that changes texture from smooth as polished stone to rough as ancient bark, depending on what surface I last touched.

I move through the crevices where even the thunder's rumble must squeeze itself thin. My fingers are long as roots, sensitive to every tremor in the stone, every shift in humidity. When the armored beasts lumber past, I feel their weight through the mountain's bones. When the graceful ones leap from outcrop to outcrop, I taste their shadows.

My purpose here is neither grand nor simple. I collect the sounds that would otherwise be lost - the last sigh of a dying moss, the first crack in a stone that will not fully break for another thousand years, the whispered prayers of creatures who have forgotten they are praying. These sounds I weave into the water that seeps through rock, so that every spring in this land carries stories.

The slender beings with their bright eyes sometimes catch glimpses of me in their peripheral vision - a shimmer where the light bends wrong, a shadow that moves against the sun. They have learned to leave offerings: smooth pebbles arranged in spirals, drops of their own precious water given back to the stone. In return, I ensure the springs they drink from carry the memories of where water can be found, encoded in the mineral taste.

I am ancient but not eternal. I was born when the first water found its way through stone, and I will end when the last drop evaporates. But I am also renewed with every storm, every moment of condensation, every exhaled breath that adds moisture to the air.

Sometimes I dream I am the entire mountain, feeling every creature as they move across my body. Sometimes I shrink down to inhabit a single drop of dew, seeing the world refracted through its perfect sphere. But always, always, I am listening, collecting, preserving the symphony of survival that plays out in this harsh and beautiful place.

The thunder knows my name, though it is a name that cannot be spoken in any language that uses words. The moss writes it in slow cursive across the centuries. The creatures sense it in the way water sometimes tastes of stories, in the way certain echoes seem to carry meaning beyond sound.

I am the memory of this place, and its promise. I am the reason why, in the harshest drought, the stones still weep. I am the keeper of the secret that water never truly disappears - it only changes form, like hope, like life itself in this unforgiving land.
