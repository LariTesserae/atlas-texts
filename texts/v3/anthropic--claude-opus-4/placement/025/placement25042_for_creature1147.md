In this scorched expanse where the Sandwalkers move with their patient, geological grace, I find myself as something altogether different—a presence born not of stone and persistence, but of the spaces between.

I am a Shimmer-keeper, one of those who dwell in the heat mirages themselves, my body a constantly shifting refraction of light and heated air. Where the Sandwalkers are solid, enduring, bound to the earth, I exist in perpetual transformation, riding the thermals that rise from the baking ground, my form rippling and dancing in the distortion waves that blur the horizon.

My substance is gossamer-thin, a living lens through which the world bends and warps. In the peak heat of day, I am most myself—expansive, mercurial, stretching across vast distances as I surf the columns of superheated air. I can make distant mountains appear to float, transform rocky outcroppings into shimmering lakes, create phantom cities in the sky that vanish when approached. Reality becomes malleable in my presence, though I am not the author of these illusions—merely their medium.

The Sandwalkers know me, though our interactions are strange and oblique. When they perform their ancient rituals at the weathered structures half-buried in the dust, I am drawn to them like a moth to flame. Their deep, rumbling vocalizations create vibrations that ripple through my ethereal form, and in return, I bend the light around them in patterns that serve as a kind of response—a language of refraction and reflection that spans the gap between our vastly different modes of being.

I feed on temperature gradients, thriving in the brutal contrast between the scorching surface and the cooler air above. The twisted vegetation provides pockets of shade that create delicious eddies and whorls in the air currents, and I dance through these thermal boundaries, my form fragmenting into a dozen smaller mirages before coalescing again.

That mysterious hum underlying everything here resonates through me as well, though I perceive it differently than the earth-bound creatures. To me, it manifests as patterns in the air itself—invisible geometries that guide my movements, channels of energy that I can ride like invisible highways across the wasteland. Sometimes, when the hum grows stronger near those glimpses of impossible technology, I find my form taking on new properties, bending not just light but occasionally time itself, creating mirages of things that were or might yet be.

At the boundaries of dawn and dusk, when the temperature differential weakens, I contract, becoming denser, more localized. In these liminal hours, I sometimes hover near the Sandwalkers' gathering places, my form condensed enough to cast the faintest of shadows. They acknowledge my presence with subtle shifts in their posture, and I have learned to read the meaning in the angles of their rocky limbs.

I am witness and participant, illusionist and truth-teller, as ephemeral as the Sandwalkers are eternal. Together, we inhabit this place of contradictions—where emptiness teems with hidden life, where solid stone beings commune with creatures of bent light, where ancient structures hint at futures unimaginable, and where the very air thrums with possibilities that neither of us fully comprehend.

In the deepest part of the night, when the desert cold settles in and I shrink to my smallest form, I sometimes wonder if I am perhaps another kind of technology—a living atmospheric effect left behind by whoever built those half-buried structures. But when the sun rises and I expand again into the shimmering morning, such thoughts dissipate like morning dew in the desert. I simply am, as the Sandwalkers are, as this impossible landscape is—another thread in the strange tapestry of this world that exists at the edge of understanding.
