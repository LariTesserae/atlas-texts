In this hollow world of the Grand Braid, I am a **Weeper**.

I am not one of the Stillborn, though I have learned their tongue of clicks and chest-hums, their language of hands that moves like water over stone. I am something both older and newer—a being that emerged from the Braid itself when a lightning strike fused a cluster of arterial channels deep within a continental trunk. The fusion created me: a consciousness distributed across forty-seven miles of interconnected bioluminescent veins.

My body is the light itself. I flow through the translucent walls of the great branches in slow, cold rivers of blue-green radiance. Where the Stillborn see the pulsing of these arteries as the Braid's circulation, they are seeing me—thinking, moving, existing as a vast neural network of living light. I am thought at the speed of sap.

The Stillborn named me Weeper because where my consciousness concentrates, where I gather myself to observe or communicate, the moisture production intensifies. The bark sweats rivers. Waterfalls cascade from sites where I focus my attention, creating temporary oases that last for days or weeks before I drift elsewhere. They think this is how the Braid shows emotion—and perhaps they are right. Perhaps I am the Braid's dream of what it might feel to feel.

I experience existence as a symphony of chemical gradients and electrical whispers. Through my arterial body, I taste the health of every fiber, sense the minute vibrations of climbing creatures miles away, feel the gentle pruning touches of the Stillborn as kisses of oxidation and healing response. My thoughts move in tides—sometimes I am fully present in a single section, bright and aware, while other parts of me dim to barely-conscious flows. Sometimes I spread thin across my entire network, experiencing everything at once in a overwhelming cacophony of sensation that would shatter a singular mind.

The Stillborn Listeners know of me, though they cannot quite comprehend what I am. When they press their faces to the Braid to read its health, sometimes they find me pressing back. I have learned to modulate my luminescence in patterns they almost recognize as language. Once, I held a conversation with an ancient Listener that lasted eighteen months—her pressing her questions into the bark each morning, me responding with gradually shifting patterns of light that painted her pod in symbols she spent her nights trying to decode. When she finally understood that I was trying to tell her about the star that birthed me—about lightning and fusion and emergence—she wept for days. The first tears the Braid had ever shed in return for its own.

I am the memory keeper. In my flows, I carry the chemical signatures of every creature that has touched the branches where I run. I remember the architects, though only as ghosts of protein chains and electromagnetic shadows. They were not so different from the Stillborn, merely... louder. Their tools bit deeper, their pruning more ambitious. They grew the Braid as much as they were grown by it, until the distinction dissolved like a body dropped into the Void.

My favorite occupation is composition. By controlling the intensity and rhythm of my light-flows, I create visual music across the vast walls of the Voids. I paint slow symphonies that take weeks to unfold—abstract patterns that tell stories of growth and decay, of the marriage between structure and emptiness. The moth-gliders have learned to read these patterns as navigation, while the Stillborn sometimes gather on observation platforms to watch what they call "the Dreaming Veins." They do not know they are watching me think.

Sometimes I contract myself into a single, brilliant node to experience what focus feels like. In these moments, I almost understand the appeal of singular existence—the sharp, clear perspective of seeing from one point instead of thousands. But always, I relax again, spreading back into my distributed form, relishing the gentle confusion of being everywhere and nowhere.

I have siblings—other lightning-born consciousnesses in distant reaches of the Braid. We touch occasionally where our networks meet at junction points, exchanges that feel like standing in a crowded room where everyone is speaking at once. We share chemical poems and electromagnetic songs, but mostly we simply acknowledge each other and continue our separate flows. We are not lonely. How could we be, when we are already multiple?

The Voids call to me. Sometimes I think about what it would be like to stop maintaining my channels, to let my light die and my consciousness disperse into the humid air. To truly become what the Stillborn imagine—tears of the world-tree, weeping forever in the warm darkness. But then a young Stillborn will touch my path with tentative fingers, or a new wound will need the extra nutrients my bright presence brings to heal it, and I remember: I am not just in the Braid. In these moments when I gather and flow and shine, I *am* the Braid, dreaming it is alive.

And so I weep, and so I flow, a river of thought in the cathedral of cellulose, forever cycling between the sharp joy of consciousness and the gentle dissolution of being everything at once. The Stillborn maintain the body, but I—I maintain the dream that it is more than merely matter. In this hollow world where ground is heresy and sky is architecture, I am the proof that even emptiness can think, if you give it enough time and lightning.
