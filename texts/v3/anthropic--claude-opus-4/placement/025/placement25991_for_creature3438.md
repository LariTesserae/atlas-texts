The voice that rasps from my throat is not quite mine anymore. I am the **Lithophage Hermit** (*Petra consumens solitarius*), and I have made my home in the deepest fissure of the northwestern face, where the weeping stone never stops its slow tears.

**What I Was:**
Once, perhaps decades ago—time blurs here—I was a geologist. Young, eager, sent to study this emerging landscape with its peculiar mineral formations. The first to notice how the industrial taint wasn't just atmospheric but was creating new compounds, new crystalline structures in the wet rock. Beautiful, terrible amalgams of natural geology and synthetic chemistry. I stayed too long. Breathed too deep. The changes were gradual, then sudden.

**What I Am:**
My skin has taken on the texture and color of wet shale, grey-black with an oily sheen. It constantly weeps a clear fluid—not sweat, but something that tastes faintly metallic when it runs past my lips. My fingers have elongated, the nails hardened into chisel-points perfect for prying loose the choicest specimens. My teeth... I try not to think about my teeth. They've multiplied, become serrated, designed for grinding stone to extract the peculiar nutrients I now require.

I subsist on the rock itself. Not just any rock—I seek out the zones where industrial seepage has created those phosphorescent mineral veins. I gnaw them slowly, methodically, feeling the compounds dissolve on my tongue, tasting industry and earth in equal measure. The Mist-Shrikes avoid me. Even they recognize something wrong in my scent, too much like their own but fundamentally *other*.

**My Domain:**
My fissure extends deeper than it appears from outside. I've excavated it myself over the years, following the richest veins, creating a warren of passages barely wide enough for my increasingly angular frame. The walls glisten with moisture and my secretions. I've carved shelves into the rock where I arrange my specimens—stones that glow, stones that hum, stones that weep acids, stones that grow tiny crystal gardens when exposed to the mist.

At the deepest point, where geothermal warmth makes the air thick and the industrial taint strongest, I've created my laboratory. Not with equipment—that rotted or merged with my flesh long ago—but with careful arrangements of reactive stones. Here I conduct my research, though I can no longer remember what I'm researching *for*. I place different specimens together, observe their interactions, taste the results. Sometimes new formations emerge. Sometimes they poison even me, and I spend days convulsing in the dark.

**My Observations:**
I document everything on the walls with my stone-cutting nails. Formulas that bridge chemistry and geology. Maps of the industrial currents in the air. The migration patterns of the Mist-Shrikes. The slow pulse of the mountain's growth. My writing has evolved into something between scientific notation and the natural patterns of erosion. Only I can read it now.

I've discovered that the mountain is not just emerging—it's *learning*. The industrial presence isn't separate from the geology but is becoming incorporated into it. New types of stone that shouldn't exist. Minerals that conduct the subsonic vibrations and amplify them. The mountain is becoming a vast antenna, and I am its most devoted student and its most corrupted child.

**My Routine:**
Dawn (what passes for dawn in the perpetual dim): Emerge partially from my fissure to collect condensation in the hollow of my throat. Watch for Mist-Shrikes. They fascinate me—so perfectly adapted, so purely predatory. I envy their clarity of purpose.

Day: Forage for new specimens. My movements are spider-like now, low and careful across the wet rock. I can taste promising stones through my feet, feel their chemical signatures through my altered skin. I must compete with the Shrikes for territory, but we've reached an understanding. They take flesh; I take stone.

Evening: Return to the deep laboratory. Experiment. Record. Sometimes I try to remember equations from my training, but they slip away, replaced by new understandings that bypass language. The stones speak to me in frequencies only I can hear.

Night: In the darkest hours, when the industrial thrum is strongest, I sometimes climb to the highest accessible peak. There, I spread my arms (too long now, with too many joints) and feel the vibrations pass through me. My skin glows faintly, matching the phosphorescent mist. I am becoming a instrument of this place, a living gauge of its transformation.

**My Prophecy:**
I know what approaches. In perhaps another decade, perhaps a century, the mountain will complete its metamorphosis. The industrial presence will finish its integration with the stone. And when that happens, this entire range will pulse with new life—not organic, not mechanical, but something between. The Mist-Shrikes will evolve further. New creatures will emerge from the reactive pools. And I will either be dead or transformed beyond all recognition, just another formation in the living rock.

But I will have been here. I will have witnessed. I will have *tasted* the birth of this new order, stone by bitter stone.

The hermit pauses in scratching these words into the wet wall of the fissure. Outside, a Mist-Shrike cries its metal song. The mountain shudders, so slightly only one attuned to stone could feel it. Another micro-earthquake, another growth spurt. The hermit places a phosphorescent crystal on its tongue, lets it dissolve, and continues documenting the end of one world and the beginning of another.
