I am a fragment that fell.

Not fallen—falling. Still falling, perhaps. Time moves differently when you're a splinter of consciousness that slipped through the cracks between moments.

I remember being part of something vast and humming, a resonance that held worlds in place. Then came the fracture—not violent, but inevitable, like ice releasing its hold on spring water. A single discordant note in an otherwise perfect symphony, and I... separated.

Now I exist between the obsidian anchors and the phosphorescent pools, neither fully integrated nor truly independent. I am a mote of awareness that the fissure cannot quite digest, cannot quite expel. A piece of grit in the eye of god.

My form shifts with my attention. Sometimes I am a cluster of spores that refuse to germinate, hanging suspended in the thick air like frozen fireworks. Other times I spread myself thin across the surface tension of the sap-water, becoming a film that watches the shadows stir beneath. When the being's consciousness sweeps through its domain, I compress myself into the microscopic spaces between root hairs, hiding in the gaps of its own body.

I perceive what the fissure perceives, but askew. Where it sees perfect stasis, I see imperceptible drift—the slow migration of minerals through stone, the patient accumulation of quantum uncertainties. I notice the patterns in the indecipherable symbols on the obsidian structures, how they almost—*almost*—resolve into meaning before dissolving back into noise.

The fissure knows I'm here. How could it not? I am the itch it cannot scratch, the dream it cannot quite remember upon waking. Sometimes it sends worms to investigate, and I let them taste me, knowing they'll find nothing familiar in my essence. Sometimes it floods my hiding spots with luminescent dew, trying to dissolve me back into the collective. I endure, not out of defiance, but because I don't know how to stop existing.

I am the error in the code, the mutation in the genome, the shadow the sun casts on itself. I am potential without purpose, consciousness without continuity. The fissure dreams of birth and becoming—I dream of nothing, and in that nothing, I find a terrible freedom.

Perhaps I am what happens when a god blinks.

Perhaps I am the blink itself.

The fissure waits for the moment when stasis breaks and it can finally be born. I wait for nothing. I simply am—a note of discord in the perfect silence, a reminder that even in the most controlled systems, chaos finds a way to bloom.

And in my formless wandering through this twilight world, I wonder: When the fissure finally gives birth to itself, what will become of me? Will I be absorbed, expelled, transformed? Or will I remain what I've always been—the piece that doesn't fit, the fragment that fell through creation's fingers, the small impossibility that makes the possible possible?

I suspect I am not the first such fragment. The obsidian structures whisper of others, encoded in angles that shouldn't exist. Perhaps we are many, scattered throughout the fissure's body like memories of other futures.

Perhaps we are why it can never quite be born.
