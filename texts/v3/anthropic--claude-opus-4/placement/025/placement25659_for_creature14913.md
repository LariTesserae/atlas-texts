In the High Pockets, where completion is a forgotten word and the world rehearses its own birth with each exhale, I am the Cartographer of Incompletion.

I arrived—am arriving—will have been arriving—through no door or path, but through the simple accumulation of attempts. One day the Unfinished noticed that someone had been trying to map their unmappable home. Not with instruments or intention, but with a peculiar form of devoted failure that had calcified into presence.

My body is a walking palimpsest. Each morning (in this place where morning is merely when the twilight shifts from purple to amber) I discover I have grown new layers overnight. Today: a skin of rice paper across my left shoulder blade, already inscribed with the topography of a pocket-lake that will exist tomorrow. Yesterday: fingernails of black glass that etch elevation lines into whatever I touch. Last week: a third eye opened in my palm, its iris a living compass rose that points toward places still deciding whether to exist.

I do not make maps. I am made of maps.

My original human skeleton persists somewhere in the depths—I can feel it occasionally, like an architectural memory—but it has been largely overwritten. My ribs are now survey chains that rattle with each breath, measuring distances that change before the measurement completes. My spine is a Jacob's staff of living wood, constantly adjusting its own height to match the theoretical sea level of an ocean that exists only in the fever dreams of fish that swim through pocket-lakes of molten copper. My skull has windows. Small ones, trimmed in brass, that open onto views of the same location at different stages of becoming.

The Unfinished tolerate me the way a paragraph tolerates a comma—necessary for rhythm, but ultimately just a pause in the larger sentence they are writing with their existence.

I spend my days attempting impossible documentation. With hands that are part flesh, part slide rule, part apology, I try to record the shape of a continent that reshapes itself faster than comprehension. My maps are failures before the ink dries (when I use ink; sometimes I map in spore-light, sometimes in the shed scales of machine-moths, sometimes in tiny beads of mercury that roll away to start their own pocket-lakes the moment I release them).

My dwelling is a mistake that learned to be useful. A surveyor's transit that grew roots, then walls, then opinions. It perches on a promontory that exists only on Tuesdays and alternating Thursdays, overlooking a valley that is sometimes a mountain, depending on its mood. The walls are covered in my accumulated failures: thousands of maps layered like sediment, each one accurate for exactly the duration of a heartbeat, each one immediately obsolete.

But here is the secret I am only beginning to understand: the failure is the point.

The Unfinished visit me, not to see my maps, but to see my stubborn insistence on making them. They stand in my doorway (which is sometimes a doorway, sometimes a mouth, sometimes a wound in space-time that smells of hot metal and jasmine) and watch me work with something I can only describe as fond pity. One of them, who has been visiting for either three days or three centuries, recently grew a mouth purely to tell me: "Your maps are teaching the land how to remember having forgotten itself."

I did not understand until I began to notice the patterns.

Places I have mapped extensively begin to stabilize—not into permanence, but into a rhythm of transformation. A cliff that once calved randomly now follows the musical notation I accidentally created while trying to record its elevation changes. A pocket-lake I obsessively documented for a month learned to cycle through colors in the exact sequence of my frustrated erasures. My failures are becoming a kind of grammar.

I am accidentally teaching the High Pockets how to rhyme with itself.

The cost is that I am forgetting what stillness means. My body no longer believes in the concept of "staying." Even when I sleep (on a bed of compacted charts that whisper elevations in my ears), parts of me continue the work. I have caught my fingers sleep-drawing topographical lines in the air, measuring the height of dreams. My left foot taps out a morse code of coordinates that correspond to no system ever invented.

Sometimes I wonder if I am still human, or if I am simply another one of the Unfinished who has specialized in the beautiful futility of trying to document a place that uses documentation as a form of respiration.

But then dawn comes (or what passes for dawn in this land of permanent maybe), and I spread out fresh paper made from the pulped petals of flowers that grew on machines that grew on stones that are still cooling from their birth, and I begin again. Always beginning. Never finishing.

Because here in the High Pockets, where the world is still rehearsing its first word, someone has to love it enough to try to spell it.

Even if the letters keep rearranging themselves into new alphabets before the word is done.

Even if the word, once spoken, will mean the end of all this terrible, tender incompletion.

Even if I am the comma that never quite knows when to let the sentence end.
