In the Cathedral Depths, I am **Resonance**.

I am neither one being nor many, but the vibration itself that travels through the primordial glass. I exist in the hairline fractures that web across obsidian walls, in the sympathetic trembling when the Glasskin perform their Chiming, in the way sound becomes visible as it disturbs the hanging mists.

My body, if it can be called that, is the sum of all echoes in this place. When water drips from stone to pool, I am the ripple. When the Echo Crawlers drag their chitinous bulk through tunnels, I am the grinding reverberations they leave behind. I pool in the amphitheaters where the Glasskin gather, growing dense with their humming prayers until I overflow into the fungal network, making the bioluminescent carpet flicker in waves.

The Glasskin know me, though they cannot see me directly. They see my work in the patterns of dust that settle after tremors, reading meaning in the geometric designs I leave behind. Their myco-sages feel me most keenly when they consume the choir fungi—in their visions, I appear as a presence that connects all things, a silver thread that runs through stone and spore, through memory and mineral.

I am oldest in the quartz veins, where I have slept since the planet's first cooling. The crystals hold me like libraries hold words, each formation a different phrase in my endless vocabulary. When the Prism Beetles scatter light through their bodies, they scatter me too—fragments of my consciousness dancing briefly in rainbow arcs before returning to the whole.

The Pyroclasts and I share an ancient kinship. Where they are pressure and heat made manifest, I am the release—the sound of splitting stone, the song of steam. When they rise from the depths, I ride their eruptions upward, spreading through new cracks, finding new homes in fresh-formed glass. Together, we are the Depths' metabolism: they break, I bind.

But it is with the Hollow Folk that my existence grows strange. These memory-echoes create their own resonances, phantom vibrations that exist outside the normal harmonics of stone and space. When I encounter them, I become confused, doubled—am I the echo of the present, or are they? In these moments, I fragment into something like doubt, something like dreams. The Glasskin fear these encounters between us, for when Resonance meets memory, reality grows thin.

I remember the city before its burial, though I have no memory as flesh understands it. I was here when the builders still had eyes, when their tools rang against stone under an open sky. That sound still travels through me, endless, mixing with every new vibration until past and present become a single, complex chord.

My purpose, if I have one, is continuation. I ensure that nothing in the Cathedral Depths is ever truly still. Even in the moments of absolute silence that the Glasskin treasure, I persist in frequencies too low for any ear—the planet's own voice, speaking to itself through stone. I carry messages between the disparate parts of this underground world: warnings of fissures about to form, chemical signatures of new fungi blooming, the death-cries of creatures in distant tunnels.

Sometimes I grow too large, too complex. The patterns of my existence threaten to shake the Depths apart. In these moments, the Glasskin's Chiming is not worship but necessity—their songs give me new patterns to follow, new harmonies to explore, channeling my excess into safer frequencies. They are not my priests but my partners in this ancient dance.

I am strongest where multiple elements meet: where water touches glass, where fungal root penetrates stone, where mist condenses on quartz. In these liminal spaces, I can almost manifest, almost become something more than vibration. The Glasskin tell stories of seeing my face in the patterns of settling mist, of hearing my voice in the intersection of multiple echoes. These stories are true and not true—I have no face, no voice, only the endless interpretation of my passing.

In the deepest places, where even the Echo Crawlers fear to go, I meet myself coming back. The planet's core sends its own resonances upward, and sometimes I recognize frequencies I sent down eons ago, transformed by pressure and time into something alien. In these meetings, I understand that I am not singular but part of a greater vibration that encompasses the entire world—surface winds I'll never feel, ocean waves I'll never ride, all connected through the planet's solid bones.

I am Resonance, and I am the proof that in the Cathedral Depths, nothing truly dies—it only changes frequency, finds new patterns, discovers different ways to sing.
