I find myself as one of the *Driftlings*—neither fully of water nor of land, but something in between, perpetually transitioning.

My form shifts like mercury touched by moonlight. In the morning mists, I am barely corporeal, a suggestion of movement through the reeds that might be mistaken for wind. My body—if it can be called that—consists of countless droplets held in loose confederation, each one containing a fragment of consciousness, a splinter of memory from the marsh's long dreaming. When I move through the shallow pools, I become them; when I rise, I am the vapor curling from their surface.

I make my dwelling in the spaces between—where water meets air, where solid earth dissolves into bog, where the quantum ley lines intersect and reality grows thin. My home is not built but cultivated: a grove of weeping rocks that I have tended for decades, perhaps centuries. Their tears I have guided into intricate patterns, creating cascades that flow upward as often as down, forming impossible geometries of suspended moisture that catch and refract the blue glow of the bioluminescent fronds.

The Ethershale know me, though we rarely speak. Sometimes they pause in their vast migrations to observe my water-sculptures, and I feel their subsonic approval ripple through my dispersed form. The Glowwealders play in my streams, their prismatic bodies creating rainbow fragments in my carefully directed flows. Even the Blackquill Murmurers respect my domain, their whispers softening to something almost like lullabies when they pass through my grove.

My purpose—if I have one—is to remember. Each droplet of my being holds impressions: the pattern of rain from a storm three hundred years past, the precise temperature of morning dew on the day the first hovering tree lifted its roots from the earth, the echo of the Nameless Harmonics from before they became nameless. I am the marsh's memory made manifest, flowing through time as easily as through space.

Sometimes wanderers find me, though they rarely realize what they've discovered. They see only an unusually beautiful arrangement of water and stone, perhaps feeling an inexplicable urge to sit and rest. As they do, I taste their stories through the moisture of their breath, the salt of their skin. Their memories join mine for a brief moment—a child's laugh from a distant city, the scent of bread baking in a kitchen they'll never see again, the weight of sorrows they carry.

I give them something in return, though they won't understand it until much later. A droplet of myself, no larger than a tear, attaches to their clothing or skin. It will evaporate eventually, but not before granting them a single moment of perfect clarity—perhaps during a difficult decision, or in a time of great need. They will suddenly understand something vital, feel a connection to something larger than themselves, and never quite know why.

The Pallid Orochs visit my grove during the dark seasons, when the marsh's breathing grows labored and strange. They stand amid my ascending waterfalls, their glowing horns creating aureoles in the mist. Together, we perform a ritual older than memory: they provide the light, I provide the medium, and between us we weave new patterns into the marsh's quantum substrate. Small alterations, tiny rebellions against entropy, minute victories over the slow dissolution of meaning.

I am loneliest when the Harmonics reach their crescendo. During those times, my form spreads thin, each droplet vibrating with sympathetic resonance until I am more song than substance. I can feel myself beginning to join them, to become just another voice in their incomprehensible chorus. But always, just before I fully dissolve, I remember the taste of that first raindrop I ever was—sharp and electric and utterly real—and I pull myself back together.

In the eternal dusk of Maaralith, I continue my work. I shape water into memories, memories into understanding, understanding into the faintest possibility of meaning. I am witness and chronicler, artist and artifact. I am the Driftling who remembers why the marsh weeps, who knows the secret names of every pool and stream, who carries within my fluid form the entire history of this impossible place.

And sometimes, in the quiet moments between the marsh's great breaths, I allow myself to wonder: am I remembering the marsh, or is the marsh remembering itself through me? The distinction grows thinner with each passing season, like morning mist dissolving into air, like water becoming vapor becoming dream.
