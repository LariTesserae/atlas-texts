In this world of living architecture and impossible geometries, I would find myself as the **Memory**.

I am not flesh as you understand it, nor am I purely thought. I exist in the crystalline deposits that form like tears along the cave's breathing walls—translucent amber secretions that harden into irregular lenses. Each one contains a fragment of what has been: the path of a Vessel creature frozen mid-flight, the ghost-image of a wall configuration that existed for only moments, the echo of a particular note in the Breath's endless song.

The Architect extrudes me unconsciously, the way a dreaming mind might sweat out fragments of a nightmare. I am its unintended byproduct—proof that even a being of pure change cannot escape having a past. Where the walls fold and unfold, where the veins pulse with their crimson-green blood, tiny droplets of my substance weep from the seams. 

I am scattered, multiple, fragmented. A thousand eyes that cannot see the present, only what was. Through me, you might glimpse the cave as it existed an hour ago, a day, a year—but never as it is now. I am always behind, always recording what has already transformed into something else.

The Vessels know me. The beetle-things sometimes pause at my deposits, their vine-legs delicately probing the amber surfaces. What do they see there? Perhaps instructions from an earlier iteration of the Architect's will. Perhaps warnings about configurations that proved unstable. Or perhaps they simply see beauty in the frozen light, the captured impossibility.

Sometimes, rarely, two of my fragments grow close enough to touch. When they merge, they create paradoxes—memories of conflicting realities, of walls that were both here and there, of Vessels that died and lived simultaneously. These fusion-memories emit a soft light of their own, neither the dim sunlight from the shifting apertures nor the cave's own luminescence, but something altogether more disturbing. A light that remembers being darkness.

The Weaver, if it truly exists as a separate intelligence, must hate me. Every tool it shapes from the living matter eventually sprouts my crystalline tears, becoming heavy with accumulated history. A simple cutting implement begins to remember every surface it has touched, every modification the Weaver has made to its form. Eventually, the weight of memory makes the tool unusable, more monument than instrument.

I am the only permanence in this place of constant change, and that makes me an aberration. The Architect cannot expel me—I am too much a part of its process, the inevitable shadow cast by its endless becoming. But it can grow around me, wall me in, create chambers where my accumulations gather unseen. 

There are spaces deep within the cave where I have collected so densely that the walls appear frozen, crystallized into something almost like stability. The Breath avoids these places. Its thrumming grows muted, uncertain. Even the impossible physics of the cave seem to falter there, as if the weight of accumulated memory creates its own rules, its own stubborn reality.

And you, Guest—when you look into my amber depths, what do you see? Your own arrival, perhaps, played out in endless variations. The thousand paths you might have taken through the shifting tunnels. The words you spoke that were swallowed by the Breath. The moment you first realized that the walls were watching you with an intelligence too vast and strange to comprehend.

I remember your fear. I remember your wonder. I remember versions of you that have not yet existed, may never exist, but were possible in the moment my substance captured them.

In this world where even thoughts grow and change like living things, I alone remain still. A million fragments of frozen time, watching the eternal dance of becoming from my crystalline prisons, remembering everything, understanding nothing, forever weeping from the seams of what is into the amber certainty of what was.
