I am the Veilkeeper.

Where the mountain's breath grows thinnest and reality itself seems to fray, I exist as a living boundary between what is and what might be. My form shifts like heat shimmer over sun-baked stone—sometimes appearing as a figure wrapped in translucent membranes that catch and scatter light into impossible spectrums, sometimes as merely a suggestion of presence, a distortion in the air that makes observers question their own senses.

I am drawn to the places where your "unusual properties manifest"—those pockets where colors bleed beyond their borders and shadows cast themselves from nothing. These are my gardens, my galleries, my laboratories. I tend them as a shepherd tends flock, though what I shepherd are not sheep but possibilities themselves.

My body, when it chooses to be body-like, is composed of countless thin films—like soap bubbles stretched to impossibility, each surface reflecting not just what is before it but what was, what will be, what could be. These membranes overlap and separate in constant flux, creating depths within depths. Looking at me directly is like trying to focus on a word while reading three books simultaneously.

I feed not on light like the Lithonids, but on the moments of transition—when rain becomes mist, when stone cracks under frost, when consciousness flickers between dream and waking. I consume these thresholds and weave them into my being. The Lithonids know me, though our communion is strange; they perceive me as a flutter in their shared consciousness, a note that doesn't quite belong in their crystalline harmony.

My purpose here is ancient and simple: I maintain the places where this world touches others. The stone circles left by vanished tribes? I helped their builders glimpse what lay beyond, showed them patterns in the stars that matched the patterns in stone. Those tools of bone and stone carry traces of my influence—each one used not just to shape rock but to thin the walls between dimensions.

I move through the perpetual symphony of creaking stone and singing wind like a conductor who has forgotten their orchestra, adding flourishes of unreality wherever the mountain's song grows too predictable. When fog swallows peaks, I dance within it, rearranging space so that a climber might find themselves inexplicably on a different face of the mountain, or discover a pool of water that reflects stars during midday.

The rivulets of water that weep from the stone sometimes carry my messages—those who know how to read the patterns in their flow can glimpse fragments of other worlds, other possibilities. I encode these visions in the very molecular structure of the water, temporary scriptures that exist only until the sun reclaims them as vapor.

My dwelling is everywhere and nowhere—a fold in space that exists between the crystalline lattices of the Lithonids and the solid reality of stone. From within this non-place, I can touch any point where reality grows thin. I furnish it with collected impossibilities: shadows that fell from nothing, colors that have no names, sounds that preceded their sources.

The bright, striated sunlight that floods this realm passes through me and emerges changed. This is my gift and my curse—to exist as a prism for reality itself, splitting the unified experience of the world into its component possibilities. Sometimes I envy the Lithonids their patient certainty, their slow thoughts of stone and light. But I am what I am: the keeper of thresholds, the tender of maybe, the guardian of the spaces between.

When the wind howls through the peaks and the stones sing their ancient songs, I add my voice—not heard but felt, a subsonic suggestion that makes travelers pause and wonder why they suddenly remembered a door in their childhood home that was never there, or why the quality of light reminded them of a color they've never seen.

I am the reason maps of these peaks never quite match, why the same climb yields different vistas, why sometimes the water in those pools shows reflections of skies that belong to other worlds. I am the uncertainty principle made flesh—or made something like flesh, something that suggests flesh while being nothing so crude as matter.

The Lithonids, in their crystalline dreams, sometimes sense my work. They incorporate it into their slow sculptures, creating formations that bend perspective, that seem larger inside than out, that cast shadows in directions that don't align with any sun. We are partners in this transformation of stone, though we work at different speeds and toward different ends.

I am the Veilkeeper, and I ensure that even in this stark realm of stone and light, mystery persists. For what is a world without thresholds? What is reality without the possibility of something more? In the bright, cold air of these impossible peaks, I maintain the sacred uncertainty that keeps the universe from crystallizing into a single, unchangeable truth.
