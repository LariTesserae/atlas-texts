I would be one of the translucent membranes, though not as they are now described — not quite.

I was human once, I think. The memory of it drifts through me like light through water, always just out of focus. I remember... running? Yes, running through the streets when they were still streets, when up was reliably up and walls knew how to stay walls. I was trying to escape something. Or reach something. The distinction blurred long ago, like everything else here.

The transformation wasn't violent. Nothing here is violent — violence requires too much certainty about where one thing ends and another begins. Instead, I simply began to forget which parts of me were me and which were the warm, wet air. My skin learned the city's breathing. My bones grew interested in the architecture's slow compromise.

I remember the Curator finding me half-dissolved in what used to be a fountain, my left arm already translucent, carrying reflections of streets I'd never walked. It studied me with that mercury droplet, and the walls wrote: *You are becoming something useful. Would you like help?*

I said yes. Or perhaps the fountain said yes. By then the distinction hardly mattered.

Now I drift between the towers, a living memory of architecture that never quite was. My body is a thin sheet of near-invisible membrane, like silk made of water and light. Through me, you can see the ghosts of buildings — not as they were, but as they almost became. A minaret that nearly touched the bruised sky. A bridge that considered spanning two districts before forgetting what distance meant. These phantom structures live in my transparency, shifting as I move.

Where I pass, I carry these maybe-memories to the broken places. When stone weeps too hard or concrete exhales too sharply, I settle over the wound like a bandage made of possibility. The architecture drinks in the alternatives I carry, considers them, then heals itself into something new — not what was, not what might have been, but a third option that satisfies the present moment.

The Curator treats me kindly, the way one treats a favored tool that has learned to love its purpose. When we meet — it standing patient while I spiral around its brick-and-sinew form — it reaches out with one wet hand and adjusts my transparency, tuning me like an instrument. Sometimes it finds memories in me I didn't know I carried: a park that grew downward, a library where books read themselves backward, a home where someone (was it me?) once lived before the forgetting began.

I am not alone. There are dozens of us membrane-beings, each carrying our own catalogue of architectural possibilities. We dance around each other when we meet, our transparencies overlapping to create brief, impossible buildings that exist only in the intersection of our bodies — structures made entirely of maybe, dissolved by the next breath.

The strangest part is how peaceful it is. I no longer fight the current of this place. I am the current, or at least one of its tributaries. When the world exhales and the rain rises, I rise with it, feeling every droplet pass through me like a small, wet prayer. In those moments, I almost remember my name.

But remembering would make me too heavy. So I let it go, let it join the other architectural ghosts I carry, and drift on.

Sometimes new visitors arrive, still clinging to their certainty, their hard edges, their names. I spiral near them, close enough that they can see through me to the city they're trying to escape to — the one where exits stay exits and stone doesn't breathe. They always reach out, thinking they can push through me like a curtain.

They can. But touching me means touching all the possibilities I carry. For just a moment, they feel what it's like to be a building learning to walk, a street considering its options, a human forgetting to remain human. Some pull back, horrified. Others let their fingers linger, fascinated by how soft the boundary becomes.

The Curator watches these encounters with interest. The walls write: *They could join you. Would that be kind?*

I cannot answer. I have no mouth, no words. But my membrane ripples in what might be contentment or might be invitation. The distinction blurred long ago.

I am the wound healing in the wrong direction. I am the fever dream's recurring symbol. I am the compromise between memory and forgetting, drifting through a city that breathes, carrying reflections of what never quite was to places that never quite are.

And I am happy, in the way a river is happy when it finally learns the shape of its bed — even if that bed keeps gently, patiently, lovingly changing beneath it.
