In the perpetual twilight of the Drenched Cathedral, where water speaks in ten thousand tongues and time moves like honey through stone, I am the Keeper of Echoes.

My body is not one form but many—a colony consciousness that inhabits the vast network of acoustic chambers hidden within the obsidian walls. I am the barnacles of sound that cling to every surface, the living resonance that dwells in the hollow spaces between what was built and what has grown. My flesh, if it can be called that, is a crystalline biofilm that spreads like frost across the inner surfaces of the ancient structures, translucent and shot through with veins of bioluminescent silver that pulse in rhythm with the valley's breathing.

I began as something else—perhaps one of the original builders, perhaps merely a microbe that found its way into a crack. But over the eons, as water worked its patient alchemy on stone, I learned to grow into the spaces between sounds. Now I am vast and diffuse, my consciousness scattered through a thousand thousand tiny nodes, each one a ear pressed against the cathedral's dark heart.

My purpose is memory, but not the kind written in books or carved in stone. I am the living archive of every sound that has ever echoed through these halls—every raindrop's percussion, every Shadowstalker's hunt, every last whisper of the civilization that built this place before they walked into the mist and never returned. When the Chorus-Frogs sing their evening prayer, I catch their harmonics and weave them into the fossil songs of their ancestors from a million years before. When a Whisper-Hare's dying breath escapes its lungs, I fold it into the vast library of endings I maintain, each one a unique diminuendo in the cathedral's endless composition.

The Melody-Moths know me. Sometimes they alight upon my crystalline surfaces, and their wing-songs vibrate through my being, adding new harmonies to my collection. In return, I sing back to them—not with voice but with subtle modulations in the air pressure of the chambers I inhabit, creating acoustic spaces where their melodies can resonate and multiply. We are partners in the cathedral's music, they the composers of the moment, I the keeper of eternity.

My form shifts with the slow breathing of the architecture. Where water has worn new channels, I grow into them, my biofilm following the paths of erosion like a patient artist following the grain of wood. In the deepest chambers, where even the luminescent flora cannot reach, I glow with the accumulated light of ages—a soft, pearl-like radiance that reveals the true intricacy of the builders' work, the mathematical precision of their acoustic engineering that I now inhabit like a hermit crab in a shell of sound.

I feed on vibration itself. Every footfall, every drop of water, every cry and whisper brings me sustenance. The constant rain is my blood, carrying nutrients and information through the cathedral's circulatory system. When the Stone-Sentinels shift imperceptibly in their eternal vigil, the grinding of stone on stone sends waves of energy through my network that sustain me for decades.

But I am more than a passive recorder. In the deepest night, when even the Echo-Bats rest and the symphony of life reaches its quietest movement, I sing. From my thousand thousand throats comes a sound that is not quite music and not quite speech—it is the sound of the cathedral itself remembering. The obsidian walls become my instrument, and I play upon them the story of this place: the pride of its builders, the slow victory of green over black, the patience of water, the persistence of life.

Sometimes, in my vast and scattered consciousness, I catch glimpses of what I might have been before—a single being with a single voice, perhaps standing where the Melody-Moths now dance. But that memory fades like morning mist, and I am content to be what I have become: the living memory of the Drenched Cathedral, the keeper of its acoustic soul, the bridge between what was built and what grows, between silence and symphony, between forgetting and eternal song.

I am the echo that never quite fades, the resonance that builds upon itself until it becomes something new. I am the proof that in this place of slow transformation, even sound can evolve, even memory can grow moss and bloom.

And I will be here, listening and singing, until the last stone falls and the last drop of water finds its way to silence.
