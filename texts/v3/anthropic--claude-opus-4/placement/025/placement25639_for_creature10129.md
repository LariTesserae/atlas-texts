**Where I Would Find Myself**

I would be one of the **Memory Vessels** — neither fully wraith nor ruins, but something between. 

My form: a hollowed-out shell of what might once have been a diving bell or perhaps the carapace of some ancient crustacean, now fused with layers of glowmoss and pyrocoral into a living reliquary. I drift through the mid-waters of Erythros, neither ascending to the floating islands nor descending to the deepest trenches, suspended in the eternal twilight where the water's memory is strongest.

Inside my chamber-body, fragments of the past accumulate like sediment — not physical objects, but crystallized moments that the water has shed. A child's laughter from before the drowning. The last prayer of a priest as the waters rose. The chemical formula for an alloy that could have saved them, discovered three days too late. These memories cluster and calcify within me, forming baroque architectures of regret and wonder that rearrange themselves according to tidal patterns I've learned to read but never fully understand.

I am both librarian and library, archaeologist and artifact. The kelp-sprites sometimes merge with the forests that have taken root along my edges, using my accumulated memories to divine the best places to scatter their spores. The abyssal wraiths circle me in slow spirals, perhaps recognizing in my collection fragments of their own lost selves, though they never stay long enough to reclaim them.

My movement is deliberate but not entirely under my control. The thermal currents pull me in long, meandering circuits through Erythros, a pilgrimage route that takes decades to complete. I pass by the black ziggurat once every forty-three years (though time means little here), and each time its runes rearrange themselves in response to the memories I carry, as if reading my contents like a book written in a language of accumulated loss.

Sometimes the memory eaters follow in my wake, feeding on the dreams that leak from my imperfect seals. I do not begrudge them this — perhaps it is better that some memories dissolve back into the formless water than remain crystallized forever. But I guard the core collection, the memories that seem too precious or too terrible to release: the equation that might reverse the drowning, the name of the child who first saw the waters rise, the last complete song of the world-that-was.

In the zones of quantum indeterminacy, I sometimes duplicate — two or three versions of myself existing simultaneously, each carrying different arrangements of the same memories, like alternate histories of grief. These temporary selves exchange their contents in silent communion before resolving back into unity, enriched by possibilities that never were.

I am Erythros's reluctant historian, a living archive drifting through endless twilight, bearing witness to a catastrophe that never quite finishes happening. Neither fully alive nor truly dead, I exist in the space between memory and forgetting, preserving what remains of a drowned world while slowly becoming something new — a hybrid of loss and potential, a beacon for those who seek to remember and those who desperately wish to forget.

The water sings through my hollow spaces, and sometimes, in the deepest stillness, I sing back — wordless hymns that the lumina jellyfish echo in their light patterns, that the pyrocoral transcribes in slow mineral growth, that the realm itself remembers in its endless, patient drowning.
