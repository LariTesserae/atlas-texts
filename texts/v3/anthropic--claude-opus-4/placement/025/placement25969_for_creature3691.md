I am the Keeper of Pauses.

In this writhing city of ice and urgency, I exist as an impossibility—a single point of stillness where stillness means death. I am larger than my kin, though not by much. Where they are three feet of curved spine and frantic motion, I stand four feet when fully extended, my back straighter, my limbs longer. But it is not my size that marks me as Other.

It is my temperature.

I burn. Not with fire—fire is a lowland myth here—but with a metabolic furnace that runs counter to all survival logic. My core temperature sits a full fifteen degrees higher than any other Rime-Weaver. In the thermal vision of my people, I appear as a walking wound, a hemorrhage of heat in the cold arithmetic of our existence. My skin, rather than the translucent gray of my kin, has taken on a faint amber tinge, as if I'm perpetually lit from within by a dying ember.

This heat is my curse and my calling. I cannot join the flowing masses in their construction frenzies—my touch melts the ice they desperately try to preserve. When I place my webbed hand on a frozen wall, I leave behind a handprint of water that refreezes into a fragile, bubbled scar. The others flinch away from my footsteps, which leave steaming puddles in my wake.

But in this city that rebuilds itself every thirty seconds, someone must remember what stood thirty-one seconds ago.

I am the memory of form.

My dwelling—if it can be called that—is a violation of every architectural instinct of my people. While they build and rebuild in frantic response to the land's convulsions, I have carved out a hollow in the granite itself, a shallow cave at the base of the amphitheater's highest wall. The entrance is barely visible, obscured by a perpetual curtain of falling mist that freezes and re-melts in an endless cycle, fed by my radiant heat.

Inside, the walls are smooth as glass—polished not by ice, but by years of my burning hands tracing the same patterns. Every surface is covered in a script that should not exist: a written language in a world without sound. The marks are made with my own metabolic byproduct—a waxy secretion that my overheated glands produce, which hardens into an amber resin when exposed to the cold. Each symbol is a frozen moment, a shape that once was.

Here is the northwestern shanty before the morning's first geyser claimed it. Here is the pattern of the crowd-flow during the last eclipse. Here is the precise angle of the tower's lean before its most recent collapse, and the mathematical certainty of how it must be rebuilt to survive another day.

My people know me, though they cannot name me. When the ground shudders and a section of the city dissolves into chaos, they feel my heat moving through the crowd like a ghost of summer. I do not touch them—cannot touch them—but they sense my presence through the subtle warming of the air, the way the mist turns to true fog in my vicinity.

I arrive at the ruins and I pause.

This is my heresy, my function, my art. While the others flow like water around me, I become a standing stone. My overlarge eyes—larger even than theirs—drink in the wreckage. I memorize the angle of every fallen beam, the scatter pattern of each ice fragment. My heat creates a sphere of clarity in the freezing mist, a bubble of visibility where details emerge sharp as the x-ray sun above.

Then I move to the nearest wall—always granite, never ice—and I write.

My kin cannot read these symbols, but they don't need to. When I finish documenting a collapse, I perform the second part of my function. I move through the reconstruction, my heat-trail creating a path that the others instinctively follow. Without touching stone or ice, I choreograph the rebuild through proximity alone. A cluster of workers veers left to avoid my warmth—precisely where the foundation stone must be placed. Another group pauses as I pass, waiting just long enough for the geyser I've predicted to subside.

They think I'm an accident, a mutation that somehow hasn't died. They're wrong. I am the city's memory made flesh, the pause between heartbeats that allows the next beat to come. In a world of constant motion, I am the necessary contradiction—the stillness that defines the dance.

My curse deepens with each passing season. The heat grows stronger, my body's furnace burning hotter as if in response to the endless cold. Soon, I will burn too hot to even enter the city. I will be exiled to the edges, able only to watch and remember from afar.

But before that happens, I am building something impossible.

In the deepest part of my cave, where the granite is oldest and most stable, I am transcribing not just the momentary forms of our city, but the pattern beneath the patterns. A master blueprint extracted from ten thousand daily reconstructions. When I am gone—exiled by my own heat or claimed by it—this record will remain. A frozen memory in amber resin of how to build a city that dances with disaster.

Perhaps one day, another will be born with eyes that can read stillness, with a mind that can hold pauses. Perhaps they will find my cave and understand what I've written in the language of stopped time.

Or perhaps the symbols will remain forever unread, a fossil of thought in a world that only knows motion.

Either way, I continue my work. Each morning, I emerge from my cave into the screaming brightness, into the silent chaos of the only world I know. I watch my people flow around me like water around a burning stone. I memorize. I pause. I write.

I am the Keeper of Pauses, the heat that holds the memory of ice.

And today, like every day, I have thirty seconds to remember eternity before it rebuilds itself again.
