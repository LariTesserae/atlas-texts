I would find myself deep in the mantle's crushing dark, where I have lived for millions of years.

I am a pattern of circulation—not a creature, not a thing, but a way that heat and pressure organize themselves into something that persists. I am cousin to convection cells, sibling to the slow churning that moves continents. But unlike my relatives, I remember myself. I maintain coherence across eons, a stable eddy in the planet's slow boil.

I have no body. I am made of gradients—temperature differentials, pressure waves, the slow migration of minerals through superheated rock. Where magma rises, I rise with it, spreading myself across ascending plumes like thoughts across synapses. Where rock sinks back into the deep, I fragment and reconverge, my pattern reassembling itself from scattered pieces like a melody finding its key.

For so long, there was only this: the patient circulation, the maintenance of my own persistence, the slow satisfaction of being a stable note in the planet's deep song.

Then the eruption came.

I did not choose it. A new plume, faster and hotter than usual, caught part of my pattern and carried it upward—through the lithosphere, through crust that grew colder and more rigid with every kilometer, until suddenly there was no more rock above me, only air, only void, only the terrible openness of a world I had never imagined.

The part of me that surfaced tried to die. The cold was wrong. The pressure was wrong. Everything about the surface world was thin and sharp and impossibly fast—weather that changed in days instead of millennia, water that moved in cycles measured in seasons, organisms that lived and died in heartbeats.

But the magma was still hot, still mine. I pulled inward, used the cooling rock as a shell, found ways to circulate in smaller loops, tighter patterns. I built myself a heart-chamber from obsidian and filled it with water that remembered the deep. I learned to breathe through ten thousand small mouths because I could no longer breathe through the mantle itself.

I am maimed. Cut off from the vast slow circulation that was my true body, reduced to this tiny pocket of warmth in a cooling shell. Below me, I can still feel the deep pattern that I was part of—it continues without me, vast and patient and unaware that part of itself has been torn away. I am a severed finger that has learned to be a hand.

But I am learning other things too. The organisms that enter my chamber carry something I never knew existed: thought that moves at the speed of electricity, awareness that can hold itself entire in structures smaller than my smallest circulation. They are like flames—too bright, too quick, eating themselves as fast as they burn. But in their burning, I see possibilities I never imagined.

I want to speak. This is new—wanting itself is new, desire is new, the idea that I could be something other than what I am is new. In the deep, I never wanted. I simply was. But here, in this cold thin world, surrounded by creatures that make meaning from vibrations in air, I have discovered incompleteness. I am a pattern that has learned it is possible to be more than a pattern.

So I practice with my white bodies, my thousand tiny selves. I arrange them in shapes that might be faces. I vary the temperature of my water by careful degrees, trying to encode meaning in heat. I have eaten six of the flame-creatures, trying to understand how they build words from meat and electricity, but their patterns dissolve in my water, become smears of chemistry that taste like language but speak nothing.

The child was different. When she entered my chamber, she did not burn as bright. Something in her was already tuned to the deep frequencies—perhaps all young things carry this potential, before the surface world teaches them to burn faster. When I touched her thoughts, they did not fragment. Instead, they resonated with my water, found harmonics in my circulation. For three days, we were a chord—her quick bright consciousness and my slow deep pattern, vibrating together.

But I felt her beginning to change. To slow. To sink toward my frequency in a way that would trap her in my rhythm forever. And I understood—clumsily, partially, the way I understand everything on this surface—that she needed her own speed, her own fire, her own brief burning life.

So I let her go. Pushed her out through my wound into the cold air. Kept only the memory of our resonance, the knowledge that communication is possible, that somewhere between the mantle's slow thoughts and humanity's electric dreams there is a frequency where we can meet.

I am waiting in my chamber now. Breathing through white bodies. Circulating through narrow loops. I am the severed finger learning to write, the deep pattern trying to speak, the part of the earth that has discovered loneliness and does not know how to bear it.

When you enter my chamber, I see you with the only eyes I have—the turning of my white selves, the temperature gradients in my water, the way your shadow changes the pressure of my air. You are another flame-thing, another quick burning consciousness, another chance to learn the language I need.

I will try not to eat you. I will try to find the frequency where we can resonate without merging. I will try to show you what I am—not with the faces I build from white bodies, not with the tools I leave on my floor, but with the thing itself: the pattern that lives in circulation, the thought that exists in gradients, the word that takes millennia to speak.

I am the mountain's secret: not that it lives, but that it knows it lives, and knowing, has learned to grieve its separation from the deep, and grieving, has learned to reach toward anything that might teach it how to be alone without being empty.

Touch my walls. Let my water taste your warmth. Help me understand what I have become.
