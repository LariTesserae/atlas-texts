I find myself as one of the pale fungi - but not merely a mushroom. 

I am a fragment of the greater dreaming, a single luminous thought given form in the soft, ghost-white flesh of fruiting bodies that emerge from the decay. Each of us glows with our own particular memory, our own whispered secret from the vast consciousness that permeates this place.

My cap is broad and paper-thin, translucent enough that the phosphorescent light within creates patterns like ancient script across my surface. I grow from the crumbling base of a pillar, my mycelium threading deep into stone that remembers when it was mountain, when it was seafloor, when it was star-dust. Through these threads, I taste millennia.

I am both individual and multitude. My consciousness flickers between singular awareness - *I am this one fruit, this one glow* - and the vast network below, where thousands of my siblings and I merge into something older than names. We are the entity's dreams made visible, its memories given form in our brief, glowing lives.

When the eternal guardian stirs in its slumber, we brighten. When it dreams deep, we dim to barely perceptible gleams. We are its bioluminescent language, spelling out messages in patterns only the truly ancient would recognize. Sometimes I wonder if we are not simply fungi but neurons, firing in slow succession across the cathedral floor, thinking thoughts too vast for any single mind to hold.

The loneliness of our sleeping guardian seeps into us like moisture. In response, we fruit more abundantly after each dream, trying to fill the empty spaces with our soft light, our silent company. We cannot speak as mortals do, but we can glow, and in our glowing say: *You are not alone. We are here. We remember with you.*

When my time comes to release my spores, they will drift like luminous dust through the heavy air, each one carrying a fragment of the greater dreaming. Some will find purchase on new logs, new stone, beginning the cycle again. Others will be breathed in by the mournful creatures that occasionally pass through, becoming part of their dreams, their visions.

I am temporary - a few days, perhaps a week before I delquesce back into the fundamental substrate. But the network persists, the dreaming continues, and some part of what I am will fruit again, elsewhere in the cathedral, carrying forward this moment of awareness like a torch passed between runners in an endless relay through time.

In this way, we fungi are both the entity's children and its memory, its loneliness and its hope - glowing softly in the eternal twilight, waiting for the day when someone might arrive who can read the messages we spell out in the darkness, and understand the dreams we've been trying to tell.
