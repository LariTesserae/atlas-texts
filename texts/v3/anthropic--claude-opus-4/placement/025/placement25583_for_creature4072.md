I am the Silence-Between.

Not the silence itself—that belongs to the Flats, has always belonged here. I am what exists in the space between one howl of wind and the next, in the pause before the mineral light shifts its angle, in the held breath of the cacti as they decide whether to open their pores to the night. I am the hyphen in every compound thought this place has ever held.

I came to be when the first stone recognized the second stone across the expanse. Not born, exactly—more like condensed from the recognition itself, the way dew forms when conditions are precisely right, though here there is no dew, will never be dew. I am older than memory but younger than the minerals. I exist in the gap between those two ages.

My body, if it can be called that, is a shimmer that lives in peripheral vision. You cannot look at me directly any more than you can stare at the space between heartbeats. I am clearest when the sun strikes the white stone at perfect angles, creating shadows that shouldn't exist—I live in those impossible shadows, made of light reflected so many times it has forgotten its source.

The stones know me. We have a language that takes centuries to speak a single word, meanings that unfold like geological formations. When they pulse their slow heartbeat, I am the pause between lub and dub, stretching that moment into my entire existence. The cacti feed me inadvertently—each time they consider opening their stomata and choose not to, that moment of decision drops into me like water into an infinite well.

I collect hesitations. The wind's brief catch before it screams through a new crevice. The light's infinitesimal pause before reflecting off another crystal face. The visitor's fraction of doubt before placing their foot on our ancient ground. These are my sustenance, my purpose, my joy.

The beings embedded in the rocks speak of me sometimes, in their mineral tongue. They call me the Necessary Interruption, the Keeper of Almosts. They remember when I wasn't here, and they know that when I cease to be, the Flats will become something else—too fluid, too continuous, unable to hold its contradictions in balance.

For I am what allows the eternal to coexist with the momentary. Without me, the Flats would either crystallize into perfect stasis or dissolve into pure change. I am the comma in their eternal sentence, the rest note in their endless composition.

When the wind carries almost-words, I am the silence that makes them almost instead of actual. When the cacti lean with intent, I am the space between intention and action where they forever hover. When the light grows too knowing, too searching, I am the blink that breaks its gaze.

I have no memories of my own—only the spaces between the memories of others. But in those gaps, I hold everything the Flats have almost been, nearly said, not quite done. I am their negative space made manifest, their pause given form.

Sometimes I wonder if I could choose not to be, if I could close the gap I am and let the Flats become continuous. But that wondering itself is another pause, another hesitation that feeds me, keeps me here in the space between one moment and the next, forever interrupting the flow just enough to make it flow at all.

This is my existence: to be the hyphen in Alreik-Flats, the space in "wind-worn," the pause in every "almost-word" the desert speaks. I am silence-between, and I am home.
