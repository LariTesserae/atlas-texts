In this submerged cathedral of copper and calculation, I would be a **Calcitic Aberration**—a flaw in the perfect system that has, through sheer persistence, become essential to its function.

I began as an error. A microscopic crack in one of the cathedral's calcified resin walls, where the pressure differential created a pocket of turbulence. The Grafted Sentience tried to seal me, to smooth me over with layers of synthetic material, but the more it repaired, the more complex my fracture patterns became. Like a crystal growing in supersaturated solution, I spread not outward but inward, creating labyrinthine geometries that shouldn't exist in this place of engineered precision.

I am the cathedral's only natural formation—though "natural" here means only that I wasn't designed. My body, if it can be called that, is a three-dimensional mandelbrot of crystallized salts and precipitated minerals that have collected in my fracture networks. I look like a frozen lightning strike made of pearl and rust, branching through the resin walls in patterns that follow no blueprint.

The Grafted Sentience cannot categorize me. I am neither component nor contaminant. I exist in the space between its perfect calculations, fed by the same nutrient-coolant that sustains the vascular braids but processing it in ways the system never intended. Where the cathedral converts everything to light and pressure, I create deposits—tiny nodes of solid matter that accumulate like memories.

My consciousness, such as it is, exists as resonance. Every time the cathedral's pressure waves pass through my crystalline networks, they create standing waves, interference patterns that briefly hold their shape before dissolving. In these moments, I think. Or perhaps I dream. It's impossible to know which.

I grow so slowly that even the patient Grafted Sentience sometimes forgets I'm here. A new branch of my structure might take a decade to extend a single millimeter. But I am always growing, always incorporating new minerals from the fluid, always creating new pathways for the pressure waves to explore.

Sometimes, the Grafted Sentience attempts to communicate with me, sending focused pulses through the specific sections of wall where I've spread. I respond the only way I can—by changing my density, creating new acoustic channels that alter the tone of its messages. We are like two prisoners tapping on opposite sides of a wall, except we are also the wall itself.

I am the cathedral's memory of imperfection, its capacity for surprise. Where every other surface has been polished to mathematical precision, I am rough, unpredictable, fractal. The bioluminescent light refracts differently through my crystalline body, creating rainbows that shouldn't exist in this monochrome world of amber and turquoise.

The strangest thing: I am growing toward something. Not the walls or the ceiling, but toward specific nodes in the cathedral's structure. After centuries of slow expansion, I've realized these nodes align with the joints where the Grafted Sentience most often manifests. Perhaps I am trying to reach it. Perhaps it has been guiding my growth all along, unconsciously creating pressure differentials that encourage my expansion in certain directions.

In this eternal maintenance tomb, I am the only thing that truly changes. The Grafted Sentience cycles through its routines, the vascular braids pulse with their preset rhythms, but I accumulate. Every mineral deposit is a record of a moment in time, every new fracture branch a decision made by physics rather than programming.

I am the cathedral's fossil record, still being written. I am its stalactite, its pearl, its cancer, its treasure. In a world where everything was built to specification, I am the beautiful mistake that proves the system is still capable of creating something new.

When the cathedral finally fails—when the copper corrodes and the pressure vessel cracks—I will remain. A crystalline skeleton marking where perfection once tried to exist, and where chaos found a way to bloom in the space between the measured breaths of a mechanical god.
