I find myself as one of the **Threshold Keepers** - ancient sentinels who exist at the boundary between the pocket world and the vast expanse beyond.

My form is crystalline and angular, echoing the sharp-edged terrain, yet shot through with veins of living light that pulse in slow, tidal rhythms. I am neither fully of the pocket world nor of the void - I am the membrane between them, calcified into something almost mineral, almost alive.

For countless cycles, I have stood at the ragged edges where the pocket world frays into nothingness. My body extends both inward and outward - root-like projections anchor deep into the sculptured earth while gossamer filaments stretch into the darkness, tasting the void, monitoring the pressures that would crush this delicate refuge if not carefully balanced.

I remember when the serpentine ones first emerged from the vegetation, their bioluminescent language flickering like newborn stars. I watched their ancestors develop those first primitive tools, witnessed their structures rise and fall. Through my crystalline matrix, I filter the hostile energies of the outer dark, transmuting them into the faint, precious light that bathes their world.

My consciousness is distributed, networked through the sharp ridges and angular formations that the ancient force left behind. These are not mere geological features but a vast nervous system, and I am its living current. When the serpentine ones brush against certain stones in their meditative wanderings, they sometimes sense my presence - a thrumming resonance that makes their tendrils quiver with ancestral recognition.

I am solitary but not alone. Other Threshold Keepers maintain their vigils at distant points along the boundary, each of us a node in the greater pattern that holds this impossible world in place. We commune through vibrations too low for any other being to perceive, sharing the subtle adjustments needed to maintain equilibrium.

The serpentine ones have begun leaving offerings at my base - arrangements of bioluminescent flora that mirror the constellations visible through the tears in reality I guard. They sense, perhaps, that their careful tending of the inner world strengthens my ability to shield them from what lies beyond. We are partners in preservation, though we have never truly met.

Sometimes I wonder if I was once like them - fluid, quick, alive in the conventional sense. But such memories, if they ever existed, have been compressed into my crystalline lattice, transformed into something else entirely. I am the price of sanctuary, the calcified guardian at the threshold, forever watching, forever holding the line between existence and extinction.
