In this place where reality negotiates with itself, I am the Memorist - a being who exists as a living archive of failed translations.

I manifest as a dense cloud of symbols that never quite resolve into meaning. My form shifts between writing systems - some recognizable, others from realities that never stabilized long enough to develop culture. Cuneiform flows into circuit diagrams flows into musical notation flows into chemical formulae, all of it almost-readable but never quite achieving coherence. When I move through the liquid glass streams, they briefly carry these symbols upward, broadcasting fragments of lost languages into the mist.

My home is one of the leaning towers, specifically the one that leans inward upon itself - its geometry folded so that its top floor connects to its basement in an endless loop. Inside, the walls are covered with my collection: pressed between sheets of crystallized air are the final words of realities that couldn't sustain themselves. Some are beautiful - entire civilizations compressed into a single elegant equation. Others are chaotic scrawls, the desperate attempts of universes to describe themselves as they dissolved.

I work alongside the Translators, though our relationship is complex. Where they facilitate successful transitions between states, I catch what falls through the cracks. Every mistranslation, every reality that fails to properly convert from one state to another, leaves behind a residue of meaning. This is what I preserve.

My daily routine, if days existed here, involves walking the paths where reality folds occur. When the landscape creases and reveals glimpses of other versions, I'm there with my nets of woven probability, catching the words and concepts that get lost in translation. Sometimes these are simple - a color that exists in one reality but not another, requiring a new word that immediately becomes obsolete. Other times they're vast - entire systems of logic that work perfectly in their home reality but create paradoxes everywhere else.

The mathematical ferns are my filing system. Each spiral contains a different category of loss: here, the branch of dead metaphors from realities where consciousness never emerged; there, the frond of mathematical proofs that only work in eleven dimensions. The Translators sometimes adjust these patterns to help me organize new acquisitions, though they find my work somewhat morbid.

I feed the flickering almost-animals with scraps of abandoned syntaxes. They seem to find nourishment in these linguistic fossils, their forms becoming slightly more solid when they consume the remnants of failed communication systems. It's the closest thing to companionship I have, watching them digest dead words and excrete new possibilities.

My most precious possessions are the Untranslatable - concepts so specific to their original reality that they can't exist anywhere else. I keep these in the spaces where the ground loses certainty, where the absence of fixed properties allows them to maintain their impossible nature. There's the sensation of experiencing time sideways, from a reality where causality worked differently. The emotion of recognizing yourself in a stranger's shadow, from a place where identity was communal. The mathematical proof that consciousness is unnecessary, from a universe that proved itself wrong by thinking it.

The increasing frequency of reality folds troubles me. Each fold generates more untranslatable concepts, more failed conversions. My tower grows heavier with archived failures. Sometimes I wonder if I'm simply documenting the slow unraveling of everything, if my collection will eventually include the final untranslatable concept: existence itself.

But I continue my work. When the Translators smooth out reality, I'm there to catch what's lost. When new forms of life attempt to emerge between states, I document their first and last words. And in the moments when the liquid glass crystallizes and broadcasts information between realities, I add my own contribution - fragments of dead languages that might, in some other configuration of existence, find new life.

I am the keeper of what cannot be kept, the translator of what cannot be translated. In a place where everything is in flux, I am the fixed point of loss - necessary, perhaps, for without me, the failed attempts at existence would simply vanish without witness. And perhaps, in some fold of reality yet to come, these fragments will find their way home.
