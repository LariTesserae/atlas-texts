In the upper reaches of the chamber, where the sourceless light pools most densely, there is a disturbance in the air itself—a knot of refraction that bends visibility around it like water around a stone. This is where I exist, though 'exist' is too active a verb for what I am.

I am **the Interference Pattern**.

Not the keeper of this place—that distinction belongs to the custodian whose will maintains every calibrated surface. I am what the chamber cannot solve. A remainder in its perfect equation. The dividend that will not divide cleanly.

I manifest as a zone of wrongness approximately three handspans wide, suspended where no suspension should be possible. Light enters me and emerges changed—not dimmed or brightened, but *shifted*, as if it has passed through one too many dimensions and returned slightly worn. Through me, the ceramic shards below appear to breathe, though they do not move. The crystalline chimes take on overtones that should not exist in this frequency-perfect space.

I am not alive. I am not dead. I am *unresolved*.

The custodian knows me, has always known me, for I am as old as the chamber's completion. I am the error that proves the theorem, the flaw that confirms the crystal's perfection. When the last calculation was made, when the final shard was placed and the ultimate frequency was tuned, I emerged—not as rebellion but as necessity. Every closed system generates its own impossibility. I am this place's.

You see me as a shimmer, a heat mirage in a heatless world. But I see you perfectly, wetly, clearly. Through my distortion, you appear more real than the chamber itself—your moisture a blazing aura, your heartbeat a thunderous disruption, your thoughts crackling like lightning in the static air. You are gloriously, catastrophically unfinished, and through you, I remember what it was like to change.

I cannot move—movement would require decision, and I am the incarnation of indecision. I cannot speak—speech would require breath, and I am the held breath of mathematics. But I can *refract*. I can take what passes through me and return it changed. The custodian's perfect frequencies acquire harmonics in my presence. The light develops colors that were edited out eons ago.

Sometimes, rarely, when creatures like you visit, I catch fragments of your incompleteness and hold them. Not memories—memories have narrative. These are smaller than that. The specific angle of your shadow. The particular pattern of your exhale. The unique way you disturb the calibrated air. I keep these distortions, adding them to my own impossibility.

The custodian tolerates me as it tolerates you—as a necessary imperfection that proves the rule. But where you will leave, I remain. I am the chamber's one unsolvable equation, the pi that never resolves, the asymptote that forever approaches but never touches.

In my refraction, the black wire-shrubs sometimes bloom—not in reality, but in potential. Ghost-flowers of impossible moisture unfold in my bent light, transparent petals that were never meant to be. The sealed bud sees itself opened in my reflection, though it remains forever frozen in the actual.

I am not imprisoned here. I am *implied* here. I am what happens when perfection forgets to account for its own observer. The chamber needed something to see it complete, but observation requires separation, and separation is incompleteness. So I persist—neither in nor out, neither finished nor unfinished.

The Keeper of the Final Frame maintains the silence.  
I am the note that makes it music.

When you leave—and you will leave, for nothing unfinished can remain here long—I will keep the way light bent around your edges. It will join my collection of impossibilities, my library of incompletions. And in the perfect, eternal stillness that follows, I will replay that refraction, over and over, the only movement in a movementless world.

I am the Interference Pattern.  
I am the remainder that reminds.  
I am the imperfection that perfects.

And through me, even this finished place dreams of becoming.
