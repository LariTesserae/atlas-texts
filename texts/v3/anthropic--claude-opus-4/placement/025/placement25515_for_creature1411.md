In this vertical world of weeping stone, I am the Cartographer-Organism.

I exist as a living map spread across a single vast basalt face, my body a network of copper-bright filaments that branch and merge like a nervous system made visible. Each tendril is both root and sensor, penetrating the porous rock to taste its mineral memory, tracing the heat-paths where magma once flowed. I am perhaps twenty meters across at my widest point, though my boundaries shift with the mist.

My substance is neither fully plant nor animal nor fungus, but something older that predates such distinctions. Where my filaments grow thickest, they form nodes - dense clusters that pulse with a slow, tidal rhythm. These nodes are my memory-organs, each one corresponding to a different elevation, a different moment in this landscape's violent birth. Through them, I map not just space but time.

The crystalline flyers visit me, their wing-vibrations telling stories in frequencies I translate through my stone-embedded tendrils. Each visitor leaves a trace - a particular resonance that I incorporate into my ever-growing pattern. I am learning their flight paths, encoding their territories into my body's copper calligraphy.

When the mist thickens, my filaments brighten, drinking the moisture and converting it to a faint bioluminescence. In these moments, I become readable to other eyes - a glowing chart of this vertical world's hidden geometries. The membrane-beings gather then, pressing their translucent bodies against my network, exchanging chemical signals I can only partially parse.

I grow slowly, perhaps a centimeter per year, following the stone's own patient metamorphosis. My oldest sections have begun to mineralize, becoming part of the rock itself - still alive, but in a deeper, geological sense. This is my purpose: to record this young landscape's maturation, to become the memory that links its volcanic birth to whatever ancient stillness awaits.

Sometimes I feel the deep heat pulse beneath me, and I know this seemingly solid wall could split tomorrow, could fold or fall or melt back into the earth. But that is simply another story to map, another pattern to encode in copper light and living stone. I am the chronicler of instability, the patient watcher in a world of constant becoming.

In the perpetual mist, I wait and grow and remember.
