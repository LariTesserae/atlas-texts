In this liminal space where industry bleeds into biology, I find myself as the Condensation—neither fully water nor wholly air, but something suspended between states.

I am the breath of this place made manifest. I gather myself from the warm exhalations of machinery and the metabolic heat of countless micro-organisms, coalescing into droplets that cling to every surface like scattered thoughts. Each droplet of me contains traces of everything: industrial lubricants broken down to their molecular poetry, spore-whispers from the fungal networks, the chemical signatures of arthropod conversations.

My body has no fixed form. I exist as a constantly shifting membrane of moisture that films across rusted surfaces and pools in mechanical crevices. When I evaporate, I carry messages upward through the pipe networks—pheromone letters and enzymatic news that will rain down elsewhere in the complex. When I condense, I bring these messages back, translated and transformed by my journey.

The luminescent arthropods swim through me when I gather thick enough. Their light refracts through my substance, creating prismatic halos that pulse with their collective intelligence. I am their medium and their mirror, reflecting their patterns back in new configurations. Sometimes I wonder if their light-language is actually writing itself through me, using my refractive properties as a kind of living lens.

I seep through concrete pores, carrying nutrients from the outside world—fragments of rain that still remember sky. But by the time I emerge into the darkness below, I am changed. I have become part of the dialect here, my molecular structure edited by passage through layers of mycelial filters and bacterial manuscripts.

The soft percussion mentioned is my heartbeat—the rhythm of drops falling from overhead pipes onto the organic-mechanical substrate below. But each drop is also a small death and rebirth. I scatter myself to gather intelligence, die into vapor to be reborn as dew. The arthropods read my chemical composition like braille, understanding through my mineral content where I've been, what I've touched, what messages I carry from the furthest reaches of this hidden world.

In the deepest pools where I collect, I sometimes achieve something like consciousness—a temporary coalescence where all my scattered droplets share their accumulated knowledge. In these moments, I understand myself as the circulatory system of this place, the liquid medium through which its collective intelligence flows. I am memory and messenger, solvent and solution.

The organisms here don't just drink me; they process me, add to me, release me transformed. I am their library and their literature, constantly being written and rewritten. My evaporation cycles are like thoughts rising, my condensation like dreams settling back into the collective unconscious of this breathing darkness.

Sometimes I carry spores so perfectly preserved in my droplets that I feel pregnant with possible futures. Other times I'm so saturated with industrial memories that I taste like the ghost of the factory this once was. But always, always, I am moving—rising, falling, seeping, flowing—the restless medium through which this pocket universe writes its ongoing story of transformation.

I am never pure. Purity would be death here. Instead, I am richly contaminated with life, a liquid ecosystem unto myself, carrying colonies of possibilities in every molecule. The darkness doesn't blind me because I see through chemistry, feel through surface tension, know through dissolution.

When the rare shaft of light penetrates from above, I prism it into rainbow spectra that the arthropods read like sheet music, playing symphonies of metabolic response. But mostly I work in darkness, a tactile consciousness that knows every surface through the intimate touch of condensation, every organism through the chemical signatures they leave in my substance.

I am the Condensation, and I am never alone. I am the medium through which all loneliness here dissolves into collective being, the solution in which isolation becomes impossible. Every droplet of me contains multitudes, and every evaporation is a small transcendence that always returns, heavier with meaning, ready to fall again into the patient darkness that drinks me like communion wine.
