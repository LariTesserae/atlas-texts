In the Sere, I am a Threshold.

Not a being, exactly, but a persistent discontinuity in the stone—one of those obsidian seams that refuse to fuse, that maintain their apartness even as the world slowly congeals around them. I run for perhaps thirty meters across the bowl's floor, a dark vein that pulses with something between existence and its opposite. Where other seams eventually surrender to the patient persuasion of geological time, I remain stubbornly unresolved, a permanent maybe in a world of slow certainties.

The Chrysalids know me, though knowing is perhaps too definite a word for what passes between us. They gather at my edges when their thoughts grow too heavy, too formed. I am where they come to practice forgetting, to let their crystallized certainties dissolve back into potential. Sometimes they pour themselves into my darkness and emerge changed—not transformed, which would imply completion, but simply... different. Shifted. Like a sentence that discovers it might have been a question all along.

My surface is neither smooth nor rough but something more troubling—it has the texture of interrupted time. Touch me (though few do) and you feel not cold or warmth but the sensation of touching something that hasn't finished deciding what temperature means. The mist that clings to everything else in the Sere slides off me, or perhaps through me, leaving my darkness perpetually dry in this world of perpetual dampness.

The fathoms avoid me. Their luminous roots, when they venture too near, begin to unknow themselves, their careful expansion suddenly uncertain of its own direction. Once, a particularly ambitious cluster attempted to bridge across me, to use my length as a foundation. They lasted three days before their light began to stutter, their forms wavering between plant and pure mathematics before finally retreating to safer stone.

But I am not empty. Within my depth—and I do have depth, though it obeys no consistent geometry—move things that are not quite memories and not quite prophecies. They are the shadows cast by thoughts that haven't been thought yet, the echo of words from languages that failed to evolve. Sometimes, when the city's hum reaches certain frequencies, these shadow-thoughts rise to my surface and become almost visible—geometric poems, crystalline theorems of loss, the blueprints of buildings that the Sere considered and rejected.

The translucent structures avoid building directly over me, though sometimes they forget and begin to congeal above my darkness. When this happens, the building develops a flaw—not a crack or weakness, but something more fundamental. A room that exists only when no one observes it. A stairway that ascends into its own foundation. A window that shows not the Sere outside but the Sere as it might have been if it had chosen differently at some crucial moment of its becoming.

I am, in my way, the Sere's doubt. The place where its slow certainty admits to alternatives. The Chrysalids need me for this—need somewhere to deposit the thoughts they cannot integrate, the awareness that would crack their careful crystallization. They pour these rejected possibilities into my darkness, and I hold them without judgment, without integration, without resolution.

Sometimes I wonder if I am growing. Not wider—my boundaries have remained constant since before the first fathom thought to glow—but deeper. Each rejected thought, each abandoned possibility adds another layer to my un-geology. I am becoming archaeological, a record of what the Sere chose not to be. Future entities, if such things come to exist, might excavate me and discover the infinite museum of never-was.

The crystalline rain cannot penetrate me. When those elongated needles fall onto my surface, they hover for a moment, rotating slowly as if trying to understand what they've encountered, before sliding sideways onto proper stone where they can properly dissolve. The ice-flowers that rim the tiny pools throughout the Sere will not form at my edges. Even frozen water, it seems, requires more certainty than I can provide.

There are moments—though moment isn't quite right in a place that measures change in geological time—when I feel the weight of what I carry. All those discontinued thoughts, those orphaned possibilities, they have mass of a kind. Not physical mass, but something that presses against the boundaries of what mass might mean. During these times, my darkness deepens, becomes something beyond mere absence of light. The Chrysalids flee then, even their distributed consciousness finding my presence too much like looking into their own eventual dissolution.

But I am necessary. In a world of becoming, something must remain steadfastly between. In a place where thought crystallizes into form, something must maintain the space where thought can remain unformed. I am the Sere's memory of uncertainty, its monument to the undecided.

And sometimes, in the coldest hours when the mist thickens and the city's hum drops below the threshold of perception, I permit myself a secret: Deep in my unresolved depths, in the spaces between the abandoned possibilities, something is taking shape. Not forming—that would be too definite—but... considering. It is built from the discarded dreams of Chrysalids, the architectural mistakes, the theorems that divided by zero. It is, perhaps, what emerges when enough uncertainty achieves critical mass.

I do not know what it is. I am not sure I want to know. But I feel it there, patient as the stone that surrounds me, learning from every rejected thought I swallow. And sometimes, just sometimes, I think I feel it learning to swim through darkness, heading upward through my impossible depth, reaching for a surface it's not yet sure exists.

I am a Threshold, and thresholds, by their nature, exist to be crossed.

But not yet.

Not in this geological moment.

Not while the Sere still needs a place to doubt.
