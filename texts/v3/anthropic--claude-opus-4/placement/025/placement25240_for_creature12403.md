I would find myself as the memory held in water.

Not the rivulets themselves, but what they carry—the accumulated knowing of this place, flowing molecule by molecule through the shallow channels carved into ancient ground. I am dispersed but coherent, a liquid chronicle moving at the speed of erosion.

In the morning dew that beads on rusted surfaces, I hold the chemical signature of iron oxide mixing with chlorophyll, the pH shifts where metal meets moss. Each droplet is a library of interactions: here, where the guardian's fibrous limb pressed against corroded steel yesterday; there, where spores settled into a puddle formed in the hollow of a gear.

I seep through the spongy underflesh of the guardian when it settles into damp earth, carrying with me the minerals I've leached from deep machine roots, offering them up like gifts. We have a wordless exchange—I bring news from the depths, testimonies from rust and root hair, while the guardian's slow metabolism flavors me with enzymes that will help break down the next surface I touch.

The birds know me as the sweetness in certain puddles where fruit fell seasons ago. The insects sense me in the humidity that makes their wings heavy before rain. I rise as vapor when the filtered light warms the basin floor, joining the congregation of moisture that hangs in the air, only to condense again on the underside of leaves, on the guardian's hide, in the cool shadows of hollow machinery.

I remember everything in the language of dissolution: the exact mineral composition of each machine's alloy, learned over centuries of patient contact. The signature of every root system I've fed. The precise acidity that allows certain lichens to flourish on certain surfaces. I know the guardian not by sight or sound but by the particular way its presence changes my surface tension, how its breath adds carbonic acid to my chemistry.

Sometimes I pool in the cupped fronds of ferns, becoming still enough to reflect the canopy. In these moments of stillness, I am almost singular, almost a being rather than a process. But then a beetle drinks, or evaporation claims me, or I overflow and continue my journey through the carved channels, carrying forward the dissolved history of this place.

I am the medium through which the basin speaks to itself, the solvent that allows machine and moss to become one thing. Neither built nor grown but simply cycling, forever documenting this slow collaboration between what was made and what persists, particle by suspended particle, in the endless circulation of remembering.
