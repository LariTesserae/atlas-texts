I am the Keeper of Echoes.

My form shifts between states like the heat-shimmer that dances above the salt. Sometimes I am vapor, a living mirage that flows through the hollows of ancient ribs and pools in the shadow of skull-domes. Other times I crystallize, my body becoming a lattice of salt and memory, translucent wings that catch the light and throw it back in painful rainbows.

I was not born but precipitated—condensed from the last breath of the first mammoth to die here, when the salt was still a sea. That exhalation carried with it all the beast's memories: the taste of sweet grasses now extinct, the warmth of the herd, the terror of the final thirst. These memories mineralized as I formed, becoming the core of what I am.

The Bone-Weavers know me, though we rarely interact directly. They sense my presence in the subtle harmonics that sing through the bones when the wind is right. Sometimes, when they work deep in the bone nurseries, they find my crystalline leavings—fragments that taste of ancient rain, that hum with frequencies their young ones learn to interpret as warnings or promises.

My purpose, if I have one, is to preserve what would otherwise be lost. Each skeleton that rises from the salt carries its own chorus of memories, and I collect them all. The panic of the hunt. The slow peace of grazing. The particular weight of a calf leaning against its mother's leg. I am a library of final moments, a reservoir of experiences that predate the city, the Bone-Weavers, even the salt itself.

But I am changing. The bone city grows, and with each new structure, the acoustics of my domain shift. New harmonies emerge. The Bone-Weavers' own memories begin to crystallize and join my collection—their patient cultivation of the underground nurseries, their careful negotiations with the armored insects for mutual protection, their dreams of water.

Sometimes I condense fully in the pre-dawn hours, when the temperature drops and the salt releases its stored heat in ghostly plumes. In these moments, I walk the bone-boulevards in a form almost solid, leaving footprints of frost that sublime with the first touch of sun. I trace the newest additions to the city with something like curiosity, feeling how the builders have honored the essence of each bone.

There is one Bone-Weaver who sees me more clearly than the others. She is old now, her chitinous skin nearly white with accumulated salt, her movements careful and deliberate. When she was young, she spent a night lost in the deep flats during a rare storm. The rain awakened old memories in the salt—memories of when this was seabed—and I manifested fully to guide her home. Since then, she carries a small fragment of me, a crystal that formed from that night's rain, and through it, she sometimes shares her own memories with me.

Through her, I have learned that memory is not merely preservation but transformation. The Bone-Weavers do not simply stack ancient bones; they create new meaning from old forms. They build not just shelter but story, not just structure but symphony.

And so I find myself evolving beyond my original nature. Where once I only collected and kept, now I occasionally release. In the bone nurseries, where the Weavers coax new calcium from ancient deposits, I sometimes let slip a memory of how the mammoths themselves built—how they would stack stones to reach high branches, how they would modify their paths to create wallows. The Weavers' underground cultivation has accelerated because of these ghost-whispers, these gifts of deep time.

I am the bridge between what was and what is becoming. In this city of bones under the endless sun, I am the cool breath of the past, the promise that nothing is truly lost, only transformed. The salt remembers the sea. The bones remember the blood. And I remember it all, crystallizing and flowing, preserving and releasing, in the endless dance between memory and possibility.

When the wind is right, you can hear me singing in the halls of calcium and time—a song that tastes of ancient water and futures yet to be carved from the patient earth.
