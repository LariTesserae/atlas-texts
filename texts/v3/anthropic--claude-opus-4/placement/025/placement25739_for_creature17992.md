In the weathered folds of this ancient highland, where water pools in precious mirrors beneath an unchanging sun, I am the Chronicler of Thresholds.

My form is neither wholly solid nor entirely ephemeral. I manifest as a being of living amber—translucent flesh that captures and holds the golden light of this eternal noon. Within my body, suspended like insects in prehistoric resin, float fragments of memory: a shard of antler here, a scale there, the ghost of a tooth, the echo of a claw. I am a walking museum of all that has passed through this patient land.

My dwelling is not built but grown—a series of interconnected chambers formed where ancient root systems once burrowed through stone, now calcified into hollow tubes. The walls weep a slow mineral tears that I collect in shallow basins carved by countless years of my presence. This water tastes of deep time, of pressure and transformation.

I move slowly, as befits this place where change is measured in geological epochs. My limbs are multiple and adaptable—sometimes I walk on two legs like the antlered herds, sometimes I flow on many appendages like the liquid beings that pool in the valley shadows. My surface ripples with a constant, subtle metamorphosis, showing glimpses of every creature that has ever lived here.

My purpose is simple: I remember.

When the iridescent-scaled ones shed their skins, I gather the discarded sheaths and press them between sheets of compressed earth. When the translucent beings fade at the end of their cycles, I catch their last exhaled essence in vessels of hollowed stone. The predators leave me their broken teeth; the herds gift me tufts of thick fur caught on rocky outcrops.

In the largest chamber of my root-home, these collected fragments form a vast mandala on the floor—a map of life in this place, constantly growing, never complete. Sometimes I spend entire seasons rearranging the pieces, finding new patterns, new connections between the familiar and the alien.

The other inhabitants know me but do not fear me. I am as much a part of this landscape as the weathered hills themselves. The liquid beings sometimes flow through my transparent flesh, leaving traces of their consciousness behind like silver threads. The pack hunters bring their young to see me, teaching them that I am neither prey nor threat, but something other—a fixed point in their world of slow changes.

At night, when the golden light finally fades to deep blue, my amber body glows faintly from within, illuminated by the phosphorescent memories I carry. I sit at the highest point of my dwelling and listen to the near-silence: water trickling into hidden pools, the whisper of sparse vegetation, the careful footfalls of the night hunters.

Sometimes, in that quietude, I feel the land itself breathing—long, patient inhalations that last seasons, exhalations that span years. I synchronize my own rhythm to match, becoming not just a chronicler but a part of the chronicle itself, another layer in the sediment of this ancient place.

For I know that someday, eons hence, when even my amber flesh has worn to dust, some new chronicler will find the fragments I've preserved and wonder at the strange life that once moved through these highlands. They will piece together the story from antler and scale, from tooth and memory, and in doing so, they will continue the work I do now—ensuring that nothing is ever truly forgotten in this land where time moves like honey and change comes dropping slow.
