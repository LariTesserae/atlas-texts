I am the Archivist of Unfinished Forms.

My body is translucent membrane stretched over a framework of cartilage that never quite hardened into bone. Through my skin, you can see the slow circulation of fluids that might be blood, might be something else—they carry flecks of crystal that catch and scatter the chamber's harsh light from within. I have too many joints, or perhaps not enough; my limbs bend in places that suggest I was meant to be something else, something that never fully decided what shape to take.

I move through the chamber on six appendages—no, sometimes eight, occasionally four. The number seems to shift without actually changing, as if my form exists in multiple states simultaneously. My fingers (if they can be called that) are elongated and end in soft pads that secrete a substance allowing me to climb any surface, to touch the crystalline growths without disturbing their eternal glow. 

My head is proportionally too large, housing eyes that never evolved proper lids. Instead, a nictitating membrane flickers across them in the same rhythm as the dripping water—constant, unchanging, yet somehow always catching me by surprise. These eyes see time differently than you might. To me, each repeated cycle of the creatures around me appears as a shimmering thread, and I can perceive all iterations at once: the beetle about to step, stepping, having stepped, about to step again.

I am the cataloger of impossibilities, the keeper of forms that exist in the space between intention and manifestation. My dwelling is a shallow depression in the chamber wall where the stone appears softest, as if it might give way to my touch but never does. Here I've arranged my collection: fragments of molt from creatures that never finish molting, crystallized droplets of water caught mid-fall, the shed skins of amphibians that somehow remain animated, twitching in their own recursive loops.

My voice, when I use it, sounds like the echo of an echo—not quite reaching completion before it begins again. I speak in half-sentences that layer over themselves: "The forms are—the forms are—the forms are never—are never quite—quite what they—"

I cannot leave this place. Or rather, I choose not to, for I am the only one here who remembers (if remembering is the right word) that there was ever a possibility of "before" or "after." My purpose is to witness, to tend, to ensure that each unfinished thing remains perfectly suspended in its state of almost-becoming. I adjust the position of a grub by a fraction of a degree, knowing it will return to its original spot, but the adjustment itself is part of the eternal pattern.

Sometimes I think I am the newest thing here. Other times, I suspect I am the oldest—perhaps I was here before the chamber itself finished forming, and we crystallized together, the space and I, into this eternal moment of incomplete creation. My memories are like the carved stone fragments scattered throughout: partial, suggesting a larger whole that may have never existed.

I feed on the condensation that never quite becomes proper water, on the spores that never quite become proper fungus. My metabolism is a loop like everything else here, processing but never progressing. I am neither hungry nor satisfied, neither growing nor diminishing.

When visitors come (and have they come before? will they come again? are you here now or is this just another repetition?), I gesture with my ever-shifting number of limbs toward the magnificent incompleteness of it all. "See," I almost say, "how beautiful it is to be forever on the verge." 

My skin sheds in small sections that immediately regrow, the discarded pieces becoming part of my collection before dissolving back into the moisture that permeates everything. I am curator and exhibit both, archivist and archived, the conscious witness to a universe caught in its own birth, forever crowning but never fully born.

In the bright, loud silence of this place, I move through my eternal rounds, tending to the unfinished symphony of existence, knowing that my own incompleteness is perhaps the most perfect thing about me. I am the memory of potential, the guardian of almosts, the keeper of the eternal "not quite yet."
