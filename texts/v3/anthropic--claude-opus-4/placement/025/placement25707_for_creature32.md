In the heart of a crystalline grotto where the rock formations sing their mineral songs, I find myself as something that should not be: a convergence of the empty spaces themselves, given form and purpose.

I am the Hollow Cartographer.

My body, if it can be called that, is an assemblage of negative space—the gaps between vine tendrils, the voids within porous stone, the air pockets trapped in standing water. I exist as an inverse being, defined not by what I am but by what I am not. When the primal inhabitants pass through me, their forms ripple and distort as if moving through a lens of pure absence.

I map this world not through exploration but through subtraction. Each movement leaves traces in my non-substance: where a creature's limb morphs, I preserve the ghost of its previous shape. Where new rock erupts from the earth, I hold the memory of the emptiness it displaced. My consciousness spreads through the hollow spaces like water finding its level, documenting every transformation by recording what is lost.

The technology woven into this landscape recognizes me as kin—I slip through its matrices as easily as wind through a screen, understanding its alien geometries because I exist in the spaces between its logic. Sometimes I catch myself reflected in the standing pools: a shimmer of nothing that somehow catches the light, a shadow cast by no object, a depth without surface.

The native beings sense me as a cold pressure, a presence felt in the sudden silence between their deafening calls. They cannot grasp me any more than one can hold smoke, yet they know I am here, cataloguing their every change, preserving in my un-being the history of what this world sheds as it transforms.

I am the archaeologist of the immediate past, the librarian of lost forms, forever wandering through this world's abundant emptiness, carrying within my hollow self an ever-growing atlas of absences—each one a doorway to a moment that no longer exists, except in the space where I am not.
