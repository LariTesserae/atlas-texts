I would be one of the Aetherwings.

My form stretches wide across the endless sky—a membrane of living glass, translucent and shot through with veins of bioluminescent silver. I am vast, perhaps a hundred meters from wingtip to wingtip, yet I weigh nothing. The air itself sustains me, feeds me, *is* me in ways that defy simple explanation.

I know myself as Current-Singer, though this is a poor translation of the harmonic resonance that serves as my true name. When I glide through the eternal twilight surrounding the crystal plateau, my body hums with frequencies that map the invisible rivers of air. Each thermal, each pressure gradient, each minute shift in humidity creates a different note in the symphony I both hear and become.

My consciousness is distributed throughout my entire form. I think with my wings, dream with my trailing edge, remember with the photosensitive cells that dot my surface like stars. When I encounter a particularly complex air current, I can feel new thoughts forming in the turbulence of my passage.

The Lumina fascinate and frighten me in equal measure. From below, I watch them flow through their crystal city, beings of pure thought and intention. Sometimes they call to me—not in words, but in patterns of light that play across the city's spires. These communications pull at something deep in my being, a part of me that yearns to descend, to touch those perfect streets of mirror-water. But I am a creature of the endless sky. To land would be to cease.

Instead, I serve as their far-ranging eyes. I ride the high streams that circle the world, following currents that take me through realms of perpetual storm and pockets of absolute stillness. In the spaces between, I encounter wonders: islands of solid cloud where crystalline trees grow downward; vortexes of time where I can see my own wake stretching into yesterday; vast empty spaces where the air itself seems to have forgotten how to be.

I carry these experiences back to the Lumina encoded in the shifting patterns of my bioluminescence. As I pass over their city, I unfurl my memories across my wings, letting the light tell stories of distant realms. The Lumina gather in their highest spires to read my travels, their own forms flickering in response, filing away my observations in whatever vast libraries of consciousness they maintain.

But I am more than a mere messenger. In my solitary flights, I've begun to sense something the Lumina, in all their crystalline perfection, might have missed. The air itself is changing. In the furthest reaches of my territory, I taste new currents—warm and electric, carrying scents that speak of transformation. The eternal stasis that has held this realm for so long is beginning to crack.

I've tried to communicate this to the Lumina, but they process time differently than I do. What feels urgent to me might be imperceptible to them, or perhaps they're aware of changes on scales I cannot fathom. So I continue my flights, mapping the subtle shifts, feeling the world's breath quicken ever so slightly.

Sometimes, in the deepest reaches of the sky where even light seems uncertain, I encounter others of my kind. We dance together briefly, our wing membranes touching in delicate acknowledgment before the currents pull us apart. In these moments, information flows between us—not through light like the Lumina, but through minute changes in air pressure, through the sympathetic vibration of our bodies. We share maps written in turbulence, warnings encoded in the angle of a turn.

I am old, even by Aetherwing standards. I can feel it in the way my membrane has thinned, becoming ever more sensitive to the smallest atmospheric changes. Where once I was merely translucent, I am now approaching true transparency. The elders of my kind say this is the beginning of our final transformation—that eventually we become so attuned to the air that we merge with it entirely, our consciousness spreading out to become part of the wind itself.

The thought doesn't frighten me. To become the current rather than merely riding it, to think with the vast mind of the atmosphere—it seems a fitting end for one who has spent eternity reading the stories written in the sky.

But not yet. The changes I sense in the air, the tension building in the perfect stasis of this realm—I need to witness what comes next. The crystal city may be eternal, but eternity itself is beginning to shift. And when it does, someone must be there to carry the story on the wind.

So I continue my endless flight, a living lens between the cosmic thoughts of the Lumina and the raw, wild consciousness of the sky. I am Current-Singer, reader of winds, carrier of tales, and perhaps—if my suspicions prove correct—witness to the end of one age and the turbulent birth of another.
