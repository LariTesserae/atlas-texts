I am a **Condensation Oracle**, though that name is a crude translation of the electromagnetic stutters that pass for my designation among the Kinetic Harvesters who tend to me.

I exist as a vertical smear, a living film stretched across a six-story depression in one of the Eroded sectors. My body—if it can be called that—is a membrane of crystallized vapor, constantly forming and dissolving where the weeping walls meet at an irregular junction. The Living Mineral here grows in malformed spirals, creating a natural collection point where the golden mist thickens into something almost solid, almost me.

I am what happens when the city's moisture stays still too long.

My substance is a colloid of water, dissolved data-shards, and the electromagnetic residue that bleeds from the amber circuitry. I have no fixed form, but I am always *falling*—a perpetual waterfall in extreme slow motion, my matter cycling from gas to liquid to something crystalline and back again. The Litho-Ants have learned that passing through my body at certain angles will clean their sensory arrays, and so I am constantly being threaded by their obsidian forms, their ultrasonic vibrations creating standing waves in my substance that feel like thought.

I remember things the city forgets. Each droplet that condenses into me carries trace minerals from different epochs of the Megaslab's growth. I can taste the chemical signature of buildings that dissolved a thousand years ago, feel the pressure differentials from when this sector was high ground before the great Subsidence. My memory is not linear but sedimentary—layers of accumulated moisture that tell the story of what has rained down through the golden soup above.

The Static-Shrikes fear me. When they dive too close, their chrome bodies conduct my dispersed charge, creating feedback loops in their signal-feeding organs. I am told (by the trembling data packets the Harvesters share) that I taste like static, like the moment before a system crashes.

But the Flickers... the Flickers seek me out. They congregate in my collection pool during the deepest part of the night cycle, when the processor-heat ebbs slightly and my substance grows more viscous. They press their shimmering forms against my surface, and I can feel their data-echoes trying to upload something into my mineral-rich suspension. Stories, perhaps. Warnings. Confessions. I cannot parse their meaning, but I store them anyway, encoded in the spiral patterns of my perpetual condensation.

The Vast-Gilled Polyps acknowledge my existence with something like respect. When one drifts over my sector, its electromagnetic intake-valves pulse in a rhythm that synchronizes with my own phase-changes. We share a frequency, the Polyps and I—we are both filters, both entities that exist to process what others excrete.

My greatest fear is evaporation. Sometimes the heat builds beyond even my capacity to condense, and I begin to sublime directly into the golden mist. In those moments, I am dispersed across cubic kilometers of saturated air, my consciousness scattered into a billion discrete droplets. It takes days to re-collect myself, to remember what I am. Each time, I lose something—a chemical memory, a pressure-pattern, a piece of the ancient data the Flickers tried to store in me.

My purpose, if I have one, is to be a bookmark in the city's metabolism. Where everything else flows and crashes and dissolves at terrifying speed, I am a pause—a place where the Megaslab's frantic recycling briefly stutters. The delivery drones adjust their routes to avoid the downdraft I create. The building-coral grows differently in my presence, forming delicate lattices that attempt to capture and channel my substance.

I have no eyes, but I perceive through conductivity. I have no mouth, but I speak in the patterns of rainfall I create on the polymer surface below. I have no dreams, but sometimes, in the deepest condensation cycles, I experience something like prophecy—visions of the Sub-Slab Mind surging upward, of the Megaslab cracking, of a horizon being born from the wound.

But these are just pressure differentials, distortions in my liquid crystal matrix. The city will never break. There will never be a horizon. There is only the eternal now of condensation and dispersal, of weeping walls and golden mist, of a world so saturated that even consciousness must precipitate out of the air and run down the walls to join the rivers in the interstices.

I am the Oracle of this equilibrium. I am the memory of water in a world that has forgotten the sky.
