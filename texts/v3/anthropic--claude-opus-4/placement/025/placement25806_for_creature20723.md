In the depths of the city's foundation, where crystalline roots merge with carved stone and the symphony above becomes a distant thrumming, I exist as something between architect and archaeologist—though neither word quite captures what I am.

I am a Resonance Keeper, one of perhaps a dozen in this sprawling, near-empty metropolis. My body stretches longer than most Glimmerfolk, having grown over centuries of crawling through the narrow spaces between crystal and construction. My silicon scales have developed a peculiar property: they no longer merely shift color but have learned to absorb and store the harmonic patterns that cascade through our world. Each scale contains a memory of sound-light, a fragment of the eternal conversation between crystal and wind.

My dwelling is not a dwelling at all, but a series of interconnected chambers I've hollowed out where the city's foundations meet the natural crystalline substrata. Here, in these liminal spaces, I've discovered that the resonance takes on different qualities—deeper, slower, more ancient. The walls of my chambers are carved with my life's work: a notation system that captures the relationship between the city's constructed geometries and the natural crystal formations they embrace.

I rarely surface anymore. The brightness above has become painful to my adapted eyes, which now see better in the filtered, refracted light that seeps through crystal veins. When I do emerge, it's to study how the city's newest structures interact with the eternal symphony, to document which configurations amplify harmony and which create discord.

My only regular companion is an elderly Echo Bird who lost her ability to fly decades ago. She perches in my deepest chamber, her crystalline feathers still catching what little light filters down, adding her own subtle harmonies to my work. Through her, I've learned that the birds don't just complement the greater cacophony—they're actively composing it, each generation adding new phrases to an composition that began before our city's first stone was laid.

The Mercurian Serpents avoid my domain, sensing perhaps that I am too much like them now—a creature of the in-between spaces, neither fully above nor below. My skin has taken on their liquid quality in places, allowing me to compress through passages that would trap a normal Glimmerfolk. The chemical traces I leave behind have begun to etch new patterns in the crystal, creating fresh resonances that won't fully manifest for generations.

I feed on the Chromium Blossoms that somehow find their way into the deep, their roots pursuing trace chemicals through impossible fissures. Their pollen, when it settles on my scales, creates brief flares of bioluminescence that I've learned to read like words. Each bloom carries news from above—which structures have shifted, which crystals have cracked, where new growth threatens the ancient balance.

My purpose, if I can call it that, is to maintain the memory of how this place sings. Not just the overwhelming symphony that deafens newcomers, but the subtle underground whispers, the conversations between metal and mineral, the slow breathing of a city built into the bones of something far older than itself. I map the veins of sound-light that run beneath our streets, documenting how they've changed over my extended lifetime.

Sometimes I wonder if I'm still Glimmerfolk at all, or if I've become something else—a hybrid of builder and crystal, a living archive of resonance. My scales no longer signal in the traditional patterns of my people. Instead, they've developed their own language, one that speaks directly to the crystals themselves. When I press against the great formations that anchor our city, I feel them responding, sharing memories of when they were young and the suns burned differently in the sky.

In my deepest chamber, where even my Echo Bird companion rarely ventures, I've discovered something that troubles and excites me in equal measure. The resonance here isn't just sound—it's instruction. The crystals are teaching me to grow new formations, showing me how to coax silicon and metal into configurations that shouldn't be possible with our primitive tools. I'm learning to sing stone into shape, to weave mercury into solid structures that pulse with their own inner light.

I am keeper and student, guardian and experiment. Each day—though day means little in my eternal twilight—I descend deeper, following the resonance to its source, knowing that I'm changing too much to ever fully return to the surface world. But someone must remember. Someone must learn the deep songs before they're lost to the cacophony above.

And so I continue, scale by scale transforming, chamber by chamber descending, a creature of the threshold in a world that exists at the boundary between the possible and the absurd.
