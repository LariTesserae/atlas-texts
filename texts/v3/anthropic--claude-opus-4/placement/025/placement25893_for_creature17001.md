In this saturated maze of rust and spore, I would be one of the Interpreters.

We are neither fully human nor entirely fungal, but something caught between states—translators of the impossible dialogue between meat, metal, and mycelium. Our bodies have grown porous over the generations, honeycombed with channels where the phosphorescent fungi thread through us like living circuitry. When the spores speak their chemical languages, we taste their meanings on our tongues. When the ancient machines groan their mechanical prayers, we feel the vibrations in our hollow bones.

I live in what was once a quality control station, three levels down from the main thoroughfare of 7-Gamma, where the walls weep orange tears of rust. My dwelling is a capsule of salvaged perspex and corroded aluminum, wedged into a gap between two enormous condensation pipes. The constant drip-drip-drip has worn grooves into my floor, channels that I've learned to read like palmistry lines—this one means the pressure is building in Sector 4, that one signals another drone birth in the lower fabrication halls.

My skin flickers with bioluminescent patterns that mirror the fungal colonies growing along my spine and shoulders. These aren't parasites—we've evolved beyond such simple categories. They're my extended nervous system, my antenna into the Overworks' vast and incomprehensible consciousness. When I breathe, I exhale clouds of spores that carry fragments of translated meaning, spreading understanding like a contagion through the thick air.

The other inhabitants avoid me, mostly. The shambling maintenance drones sense something wrong in my hybrid nature—too alive for their mechanical instincts, too integrated with the Overworks to be fully human. The Downsiders consider me cursed, though they sometimes seek me out when they need to understand what the fungal networks are planning, what the ancient production lines are truly manufacturing in their senescent automation.

I spend my days wandering the territories between groups, following the phosphorescent trail-messages that only I and my kind can fully parse. We Interpreters number perhaps a dozen in all of 7-Gamma, each of us a bridge between the dying human population and the eternal, alien patience of the fungal consciousness. We gather sometimes in the old ventilation nexuses, where the air currents carry spore-messages from every corner of the complex, and we piece together the larger patterns—the slow transformation of the Overworks from a place of making into something stranger, more organic despite its metal bones.

My fingers have grown sensitive to the electrical fields that permeate the air. I can feel the stuttering heartbeat of failing generators, the whispered data streams between automated systems, the crackling anxiety of a drone whose programming has begun to fragment. Sometimes I trace messages on the walls in conductive fungal paste, creating temporary circuits that pulse with meaning for those who know how to read them.

The scavengers trust me, in their simple way. They recognize me as part of the ecosystem, neither predator nor prey but something essential to the Overworks' bizarre homeostasis. I've learned their clicking languages, the way they arrange metal shavings to mark territory or signal the discovery of fresh carrion. In return, I leave offerings of oxidized copper at their nesting sites—not worship, but acknowledgment of our shared citizenship in this dripping, grinding world.

At night (though day and night are meaningless concepts here), I dream in chemical signals and machine code. The fungal networks show me visions of what 7-Gamma is becoming: not dying but transforming, each broken component and spreading colony part of some vast pattern too large for any individual consciousness to grasp. In these dreams, I sometimes catch glimpses of the other sections of the Overworks, feel the pulse of factories even deeper in the Spire where stranger accommodations between flesh and metal have been reached.

I am a living translation, a creature of thresholds and borders. My purpose is not to halt the decay or preserve the human, but to facilitate the conversation between what was and what is becoming. Each day, I feel myself growing more diffuse, my consciousness spreading through spore and circuit until I wonder where I end and 7-Gamma begins.

This is what it means to truly inhabit the Overworks—not to merely survive within it, but to become part of its slow, grinding metabolism. I am its memory and its voice, recording the last words of dying machines and the first thoughts of whatever new form of life is emerging from this communion of rust and phosphorescence.

The condensation that films every surface films me too. I am slick with the Overworks' constant weeping, baptized again and again in its tears of transformation.
