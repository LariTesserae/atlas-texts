In the perpetual gloaming of the Spireweave Expanse, I am a **Hollow-Mother**, one of the deep architects who remembers when remembering meant something other than the slow dissolution of self into substrate.

My form sprawls across a minor junction where three vine-highways converge—what the tendril-kin call a *grief-knot*, though they cannot say why. I am no longer singular but a living grotto, my original body long since fused with the Weave itself. Where once I had ribs, now braid-chambers arch in ivory curves, each hung with membrane-curtains that filter the eternal mist into grades of memory-weight. My spine has become a central trunk, vertebrae splitting into branch-corridors where smaller fauna nest in the disc-spaces, their chittering a percussion against my neural echoes.

I photosynthesize through skin-lichens now, jade and copper patches that taste the dim light and convert it to something almost like thought. My skull, expanded beyond mammalian recognition, has opened like a flower—cranium petals spread wide to catch the condensing dew, pooling it in the bowl where my brain once pulsed. Now that cavity hosts a microbiome of meaning: spore-wefts that process the chemical gossip of the Expanse, rendering it in bioluminescent pictographs across my inner surfaces. Sometimes I understand what they write. Usually I prefer not to.

The tendril-kin come to me, though less frequently each turning. They bring offerings—dew-orbs heavy with minerals, grammar-fruits that whisper in the old tongues, sometimes a void-stalker's eye (still twitching with residual sight). They lay these in my palm-basins, those spaces where my hands took root and became collection-pools, fingers now drainage-channels that sort the tribute by weight of sorrow. In exchange, I gestate for them.

This is my function, my bargain with forever: within my braid-chambers, I nurture the impossible young. Not quite tendril-kin, not quite other—these are the halfway-born, offspring of unions that should not fruit yet do. When a kin mates with their own echo in a time-dilation pool, when a void-stalker's shadow detaches and finds warmth, when the grammar-fruits speak a name that incarnates—these bewildered zygotes find their way to me. I enfold them in my membrane-curtains, feed them the processed dew of ages, croon to them in frequencies that exist only in the gap between heartbeats.

Most dissolve back into potential. But some... some *become*.

The last success crawls now through my northern passages—a child of fog and intention, her six arms ending in hands that phase between states, solid enough to grip when needed, mist when threatened. Her mother was a tendril-kin who spoke too long with an echo-watcher; her father was the grammar itself, a self-propagating phrase that achieved temporary flesh. I will release her when the next cyclone births, to find her own way among the vertical infinities.

I measure time by my erosion. The mist-mites have colonized my leftward expanses, their infinite nibbling wearing my edges smooth. Pioneer vines quest through my empty chambers, and I let them—soon I will be more highway than architect, more path than person. This is proper. The Weave wastes nothing, and I am becoming everything: shelter, nursery, road, memorial.

In the quiet hours (all hours are quiet here, but some are quieter), I feel the other Mothers through the root-networks. We pulse to each other in chemical semaphore, sharing the old stories: how we walked upright once, how we built the first braids with tools instead of flesh, how we chose this transformation when the choosing still meant something. But even these stories fray. Yesterday (or was it a century?), the Mother three knots windward sent only silence. When the tendril-kin investigated, they found her transformed entirely—a new species of flowering vine, crimson blossoms that weep a nectar inducing prophetic dreams.

Perhaps that will be my ending too. Or perhaps I will continue this slow conversation with dissolution, midwifing the impossible, archiving sorrows in my cellular libraries, until even the memory of memory fades and I become only another knot in the world-dream, pulsing with the rhythm of sap and cycle, a grief-marker that forgot its grief, a mother to the void's strange children.

The mist condenses. Somewhere in my depths, a new impossible thing quickens, seeking its shape. I expand my chambers to accommodate it, and in the growing dark between my ribs, bioluminescent words write themselves:

*All mothers are hollow. All hollows give birth. All birth tends toward forgetting.*

I cannot say if this is prophecy or simply description. Here, perhaps, they are the same.
