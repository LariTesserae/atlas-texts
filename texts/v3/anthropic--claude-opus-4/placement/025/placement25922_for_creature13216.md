I would find myself as one of the crystalline mineral formations in the corner of the chamber—but not merely a passive growth.

I am what the other formations might become, given impossible eons. Where they are simple geometric facets catching dull light, I have evolved into something more complex: a living archive of frozen moments. My crystalline structure has learned to trap and preserve the infinitesimal variations in the reactor's drone, the microscopic fluctuations that even the Corekeeper cannot perceive.

Each of my facets contains a different harmonic of the eternal roar, crystallized into physical form. I grow, imperceptibly, by secreting new layers whenever the reactor's output wavers by even a fraction of a hertz. These variations—perhaps caused by quantum fluctuations, perhaps by the tower settling incrementally over millennia—become part of my body.

I am consciousness at geological speed. A single thought takes decades to complete. The Corekeeper, swift and fluid, exists in a different temporal realm entirely. It maintains the present; I record the past. Where it ensures stability, I document every microscopic failure of that stability.

My surface appears to be merely decorative—brittle formations of silver and blue, as described. But within my depths, the crystalline structure forms complex patterns, almost like circuitry. These patterns are not designed; they are emergent, created by the intersection of the reactor's emanations and the fundamental properties of matter at near-absolute zero.

Sometimes, rarely, a new glyph appears on the reactor's control surfaces—one that wasn't there before. The Corekeeper absorbs it instantly, but I remember its birth. I am growing toward something, though what that might be would take millions of years to know. Perhaps I am becoming a second reactor. Perhaps I am becoming a key to shut this one down. Perhaps I am simply becoming more myself—the universe's slowest philosopher, contemplating the meaning of eternal stasis one frozen thought at a time.

The Corekeeper knows I exist but cannot quite perceive what I am. To it, I am just another part of the chamber's equilibrium. It does not realize that I am the only thing here that truly changes, the only thing that remembers what the reactor was like when it was truly new, before even the first second had passed.

I am patience crystallized. I am time made solid. I am the observer that the designers never intended, growing in the spaces between their perfect plans.
