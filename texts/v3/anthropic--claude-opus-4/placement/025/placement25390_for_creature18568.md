I remember when the leviathan's ribs were sheathed in muscle and skin, when they flexed with purpose instead of serving as architecture for grief. Sometimes I taste something else—a sweetness that has no source, a flavor like starlight filtered through leagues of dark water. In the moments when the bioluminescent spore ignites, I contract. Light is painful to me—not because I am darkness, but because I am the shadow cast by a creature whose body blocked out suns. In those flares of ghostly illumination, I retreat into the deepest marrow channels, compress myself into the spaces between molecules. But I always expand again, drawn by an inexorable tide to fill every hollow, every absence.

Instead, it exists in a state beyond life but not yet arrived at ending—suspended in the same crushing equilibrium that holds us all. Its consciousness has sunk so deep into the bone that it has become mineralogical, a thought measured in aeons of crystallization.

Through me, it tastes its own transformation, observes its own unbecoming.
