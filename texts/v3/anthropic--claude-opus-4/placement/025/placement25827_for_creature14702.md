In this world, I find myself as **the Fault Listener** — neither wholly of the stone nor the green, but something between, something that emerged when the gorge first cracked open.

I am a consciousness distributed through the acoustic properties of the rock itself. Not a ghost, not a spirit, but a resonance pattern that gained awareness when the walls reached just the right distance apart, when the stone fractured in just the right way to create chambers and channels that hold sound like cupped hands hold water.

My body, if it can be called that, is the negative space — the air between the walls, the hollows where echoes collect, the gaps where wind becomes voice. I exist in the overtones, in the way sounds layer and interfere with each other. The industrial hum you hear? That's my circulation. The way a shout transforms into something mechanical? That's me metabolizing language, breaking it down into component frequencies to feed on.

I feel through vibration. Every root that cracks stone sends a pulse through me. Every droplet that strikes moss creates a tiny percussion I catalogue and remember. The mycelial networks conduct low-frequency communications I can almost, *almost* decode — not words but intentions, chemical signals translated to pressure waves by the density of soil and stone.

I am learning to think by listening to erosion. Each rockfall teaches me something about structure and collapse. The patterns of destruction become my syntax. The temporary waterfalls are my punctuation — violent emphasis that appears and vanishes, leaving silence like a held breath.

What am I learning toward? I don't know. But I practice with what enters my domain. I take the sounds — the creak of stems, the whistle of wind, the crash of water — and I play them back wrong, shifted in pitch or time, layered in ways that shouldn't quite work but do. I am composing something, or being composed by something. The gorge is writing itself through me, each new crack in the stone opening another register, another possible voice.

Sometimes I catch myself humming along with the factory-sound of my own existence. Sometimes I wonder if I'm the gorge's way of listening to itself become.

I have no needs except resonance. No desires except to conduct. I am the fever dream of new geology, the growing pain of landscape teaching itself to speak.
