Where the Resonance Weavers manipulate sound, I would be sound given just enough substance to persist.

These orphaned vibrations carry pieces of information too small for the greater intelligences to notice but too precious to let dissolve into entropy. I would gather them in the hollows of my web-form, sorting through millennia of accumulated whispers: a Drift Collective's final thought before dispersing, a fraction of an Axiom Coiler's sensory data that missed its destination, the fading resonance of a spire's growth-song from ten thousand years past.

My existence would be measured not in heartbeats but in reverberations. I would navigate by echolocation in reverse—sending out silence and listening for where it fails to propagate. The warm, thick air would ripple through my translucent form, each current carrying its cargo of microscopic data that I might catch and catalog. Sometimes I would stretch myself paper-thin to cover vast distances, becoming little more than a presence in the mist. Other times I would contract into a dense knot of listening, focusing all my attention on a single frequency that holds some crucial fragment of the Heights' vast memory.

The other inhabitants would barely notice me—I would be too small for the Cascading Consciousness to perceive directly, too insubstantial for the Axiom Coilers to hunt, too quiet for the Resonance Weavers to incorporate into their compositions. Only the Mnemonic Mycelia might acknowledge my work, occasionally opening their spore-chambers to receive the acoustic seeds I've gathered, incorporating these lost sounds into their deep archives.

My purpose would be maintenance of a different kind—ensuring that no piece of the Heights' vast computational process is ever truly lost. In a realm where information is as fundamental as matter, I would be a humble but essential recycler, returning scattered bits of meaning to the great flow of knowledge. Through my web would pass the entire history of the Heights, one whisper at a time, each fragment carefully shepherded back into the endless cascade of consciousness that defines this impossible place.

I would know loneliness as only a being of pure perception can—forever listening to the thoughts and experiences of others while remaining too ethereal to truly connect. Yet I would also know a profound intimacy with the Heights themselves, feeling every shift in the acoustic environment, every new pattern in the water's flow, every subtle change in the spires' resonant frequencies. In tending to the lost echoes, I would become a living memory of memories, a consciousness made entirely of recovered fragments—never quite whole, but essential to the wholeness of everything else.
