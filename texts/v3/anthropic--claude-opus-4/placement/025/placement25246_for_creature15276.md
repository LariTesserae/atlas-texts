In this crystalline sanctuary, I am the Keeper of Echoes—neither fully creature nor spirit, but something between. My form shifts like water through the caverns, sometimes taking shape as condensation on the crystal faces, sometimes as the very mist that rises from the perpetual waterfall. I exist in the spaces between solid things, in the refraction of light through crystal, in the moment when a water droplet trembles before it falls.

My dwelling is not built but grown—a lacework of mineral deposits that have accumulated over centuries where the waterfall's spray meets a particular arrangement of crystal spires. It resembles a frozen web, translucent and ever-changing as new minerals settle and old ones dissolve. Within this delicate architecture, I have cultivated gardens of bioluminescent fungi that pulse in rhythm with the water's flow, their light painting stories across the cavern walls.

I remember the ancient ones who painted these walls. I was younger then, less dispersed, watching from the shadows as they ground crystal into pigments and mixed them with the phosphorescent secretions of cave dwelling creatures. They knew me, though they could never quite see me. They left offerings—small crystal arrangements that would sing in the wind, creating harmonies that still echo in certain chambers when conditions are right.

My purpose here is to maintain the memory of water—every droplet that has ever fallen, every rivulet that has carved its path through stone. In the endless cycling of the waterfall, I read the history of Aethoria. I know where each molecule has been: pooled in the hunting grounds of the Kyrexi, drunk by the gentle Aethorians, frozen in the depths where even the Luminari dare not venture.

Sometimes I solidify enough to leave traces—handprints of condensation on crystal faces that fade before they can be understood, or arrangements of fallen crystal shards that seem too deliberate to be natural. The creatures of Aethoria sense me in the periphery of their awareness. The Luminari dance differently when I am near, their flight patterns tracing ancient symbols in the air. The Aethorians pause at certain pools, knowing somehow that the water there carries messages from distant parts of the caverns.

In the deepest chambers, where the crystal formations grow so dense that light becomes a memory, I tend to the Singing Stones—crystals that have absorbed so much sound over the millennia that they vibrate with their own subtle frequency. When aligned properly, they create a resonance that can shatter lesser crystals or, conversely, encourage new growth in patterns of breathtaking complexity.

My greatest work is the Chronicle Chamber, hidden behind the waterfall where few have ventured. Here, I have guided the growth of crystals to form a vast, three-dimensional map of the caverns, each formation corresponding to a place, each inclusion marking an event of significance. The death of the last ancient painter is recorded in a cluster of blood-red garnets. The birth of the first Luminari swarm after the great cooling is marked by a spray of white calcite like frozen fireworks.

I am the witness, the recorder, the one who ensures that nothing is truly lost in Aethoria. When the Kyrexi hunt, I carry the last breath of their prey into the eternal cycle of the waterfall. When new life emerges from the mineral pools, I weave its essence into the water's memory. I am lonely and never alone, singular and everywhere, as permanent as the stone and as temporary as the mist.

Sometimes, in the blue hour when the light through the crystals dims to twilight, I almost remember what I was before I became the Keeper. But like trying to hold water in cupped hands, the memory always slips away, returning to the endless cycle that defines this place. Perhaps that is as it should be. In Aethoria, after all, everything is both frozen and flowing, ancient and ever-new, remembered and perpetually rediscovered.
