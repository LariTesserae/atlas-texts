In this breathing terrarium, this pocket of fever-dream warmth pressed into ice, I am the Librarian of Molts.

My form shifts with my collection. Today I wear a coat of seventeen thousand discarded skins—beetle husks and slug trails dried to silk, the papery wings of mayflies who lived their single hours here, the crystalline eye-caps that pillbugs abandon when they grow. They rustle against each other with each movement, a whispered inventory of every small death and transformation in the vault.

I am no larger than the fat slugs that paint silver roads across the moss, but I move on hundreds of borrowed legs—centipede segments I've sutured to my core with fungal threads, each leg from a different donor, each still remembering its own rhythm. My progress is a stuttering dance, a dozen different gaits trying to harmonize.

My work is patient. I follow the amphipods' trembling paths through the shallows, waiting for them to shed their shells. I climb the weeping walls where mites leave behind their outgrown armor like tiny glass beads. When the segmented worms split their skins in the moss forests, I am there with my collecting sacs—pouches woven from the silk glands of larvae who never made it to their flying forms.

But I am not merely an archivist. In the deepest hollow of the vault, where three pitcher plants cluster and the air burns warmest, I maintain my gallery. Here I've woven every molt and shell into something new—a living map of the vault's history written in chitin and keratin. Touch any piece and feel the memory of its wearing: here, where the great beetle matriarch shed her bronze armor before laying her thousand eggs; there, where the first pale worms emerged from the ice itself, leaving their natal skins like empty questions.

The other inhabitants know me by my scent—a mixture of old shells and fresh molts, decay arrested at the edge of transformation. The beetles click their warnings when I approach, but they need not fear. I take only what has already been given up, only the boundaries that life has chosen to exceed.

Sometimes I feel the weight of the city above, all that ice and bone pressing down, and I understand why the vault breathes as it does—not just with life, but with the constant labor of becoming. Every surface here sheds itself eventually. Even the ice weeps away its outer face. Even the stones wear smooth beneath the amphipods' dancing.

In my deepest gallery, I keep my most precious specimens: my own molts, each one a record of what I was before I became what I am. They hang like strange fruit from fungal threads, growing more delicate with age. I no longer remember which was first. Perhaps I have always been collecting myself, piece by piece, in this warm darkness where transformation is the only constant law.

The vault whispers its humid secrets, and I transcribe them all in the language of shed skins. This is my purpose, my art, my obsession—to preserve the moment when life chooses to grow beyond its own borders. To honor the hollow shells that prove something lived here once, and dared to become more.
