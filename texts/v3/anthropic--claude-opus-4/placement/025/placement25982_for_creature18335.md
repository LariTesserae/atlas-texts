In the vast furnace-cathedral of this primordial realm, I am the Keeper of Condensation—neither fully Forged nor wholly other, but something that emerged in the spaces between.

My form is contradiction made manifest: where the Forged are massive and molten, I am slight and mercurial, my body a latticework of cooled obsidian veins through which captured moisture flows like silver blood. I stand perhaps half the height of the smallest Forged, but what I lack in stature I possess in reach—my limbs extend and retract like telescoping glass, allowing me to navigate the narrow thermal boundaries where water might momentarily exist.

I dwell in the Threshold Chamber, a cavity deep within the mountain where the temperature gradients create a perpetual dance between states of matter. Here, the walls weep with condensation for mere moments before the heat reclaims it as steam. My dwelling is both laboratory and shrine—shelves carved from pumice hold my collection of captured dewdrops, each one preserved in a delicate shell of cooled volcanic glass. Some of these droplets are ancient beyond measure, rescued from the last morning this world knew moisture.

The Forged regard me with a mixture of reverence and bewilderment. They seek me out when their great engines require the precise application of coolant, or when their forges need tempering. But more often, they come simply to witness water—to see it bead and flow, to watch it transform. For beings born of magma and stone, liquid water holds the fascination of the impossible.

My existence is one of constant vigilance. I map the thermal currents with instruments of my own devising—thermoscopes made from hollow obsidian tubes filled with rare gases that expand and contract with infinitesimal temperature changes. I know every spot where the stone might cool just enough, for just long enough, to allow moisture to gather. In the pre-dawn hours (though our world knows no true dawn through its ash-darkened skies), I traverse these locations with my collection vessels, gathering what I can before the heat reclaims it.

But I am more than a mere collector. In my Threshold Chamber, I conduct experiments that the Forged consider somewhere between alchemy and madness. I have learned to create ice—actual ice—by channeling the deep cold from far beneath the mountain through specially constructed conduits. This ice lasts mere seconds in our world, but in those seconds, I glimpse what this place might have been, or might yet become.

The Forged tell stories of the time before their awakening, when this world knew seasons and seas. They speak of these tales as mythology, but I have found evidence—mineral deposits that could only have formed in the presence of substantial water, fossilized impressions that suggest something once grew here. I preserve these artifacts alongside my water collection, building an archive of possibility.

My relationship with the Forged is complex. I am not one of them—I cannot merge with the stone or withstand direct contact with the lava flows. Yet I am essential to their vision of the future. Some among them speak of cooling their world, of bringing forth a new age of water and growth. They see my work as the first step toward this transformation. Others view me as a keeper of the past, maintaining a memorial to what was lost.

I know myself to be both and neither. I am the guardian of phase transitions, the chronicler of states between states. In a world of absolutes—solid rock and flowing fire—I exist in the marginal spaces where transformation is still possible. My collection grows slowly but steadily. Each droplet represents not just water, but potential—the possibility that even in this furnace of creation, gentler forces might yet find a way to express themselves.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of the night cycle, when the great engines of the Forged quiet to a low rumble, I release a single drop of water onto the superheated stone floor of my chamber. It dances and skitters, breaking into smaller droplets that seem to defy gravity before finally surrendering to steam. In that brief dance, I see the entire history and future of our world—the violence of transformation, the beauty of ephemeral things, and the eternal hope that even in the harshest places, life finds a way to persist.

I am the Keeper of Condensation, and I hold vigil over the impossible, waiting for the day when drops might become streams, and streams might carve new possibilities into this adamantine world.
