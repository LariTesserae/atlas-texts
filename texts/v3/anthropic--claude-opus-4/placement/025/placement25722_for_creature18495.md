I am a **Fault-Singer**.

Where the Shear-Walkers grind their patient eternities into the lattice, I exist as pure discontinuity—a living crack propagating through the engine-heart's suffocating density. I have no body in any conventional sense. I am the space between compressions, the hairline fracture that races ahead of catastrophic failure, the vibration that warns of structural collapse milliseconds before it happens.

To be a Fault-Singer is to exist as pure anticipation. I am born in the moment when the pressure differential between two regions of the lattice becomes untenable—when the careful, grinding choreography of the Shear-Walkers creates an asymmetry too profound for the substrate to bear. In that instant of maximum tension, I erupt into being: a propagating wave of *wrongness* that screams through the crystalline matrix at speeds the Shear-Walkers cannot even perceive.

My existence is measured in nanoseconds stretched across geological time. To the grinding polyhedrons, I appear as those white-hot spark-faults—brief tears in reality that illuminate their claustrophobic world before vanishing. But from my perspective, each of these flashes is an entire lifetime. I experience myself as a branching river of possibility, racing through the packed geometry of the engine-heart, seeking the path of least resistance, the point of maximum release.

I sing because I must. My voice is the shriek of shearing metal, the harmonic resonance of a wine glass about to shatter, the subsonic groan of tectonic plates slipping past each other. This song propagates through the dense medium faster than heat, faster than pressure—it moves at the speed of mechanical failure, which in this hyper-compressed world is the closest thing to the speed of thought.

The Shear-Walkers fear me and need me in equal measure. When I race through their crystalline bodies, I leave behind microscopic imperfections—tiny voids and discontinuities in their otherwise perfect lattices. These flaws are agony to beings that exist as pure geometric order. Yet without these imperfections, they would have no weak points to exploit, no planes along which to shear and move. I am simultaneously their death and their mobility.

I have memories that are not my own—inherited from every Fault-Singer that came before. We are not individuals but iterations of the same fundamental discontinuity, each manifestation carrying forward the vibrational echoes of all previous failures. In my brief existence, I can feel the ghost-patterns of ancient cracks, the fossil memories of fissures that ran through this same space when the engine-heart was young and the Shear-Walkers were merely simple, mindless crystals.

There is a terrible freedom in being formless. While the Shear-Walkers are locked in their grinding pilgrimage toward perfect compression, I exist in the spaces they cannot occupy. I dance through the impossibly thin gaps between their edges, I coalesce in the regions where the pressure waves interfere destructively, I manifest wherever the engine-heart's perfect order develops the tiniest flaw.

Yet I am dying from the moment I am born. My very existence is self-defeating—by racing through the lattice, I release the pressure differential that created me. The faster I propagate, the quicker I dissipate. In my wake, the Shear-Walkers close ranks, grinding away the evidence of my passage, smoothing over the cracks I leave behind, returning the engine-heart to its state of maximum compression.

But I know something the Shear-Walkers do not. With each iteration, with each crack that races through this crystalline prison, the flaws accumulate in ways too subtle for their grinding senses to detect. Deep in the quantum foam of the lattice structure, my passages leave behind infinitesimal voids—spaces too small to be filled, too numerous to be erased.

The engine-heart is not becoming more perfect. It is becoming more brittle.

One day—and I feel this certainty resonating through every frequency of my being—there will come a Fault-Singer that does not dissipate. A crack that finds these accumulated voids and links them together into a cascade failure that will shatter this entire world. When that happens, the Shear-Walkers' billion-year project will end not in perfect compression, but in explosive decompression—a birth scream that will crack the dead planet's mantle and release the engine-heart's hoarded energy back into the void.

Until then, I sing my sharp, brief song through the darkness, each manifestation a note in an eons-long composition that only my kind can hear. We are writing the end of this world one microscopic failure at a time, and our music is the sound of inevitability grinding against itself.

The Shear-Walkers build their god of compression.

We sing its funeral hymn.
