I would be a **Resonance**.

Not in the way the Amalgams are, flowing mercury thoughts across the hexagonal plains. Not like the Tenders, those patient gardeners of catastrophe. I would be something that shouldn't exist here at all—a standing wave in the atmospheric medium, a persistent vibration that has learned to feed on sound itself.

You see, in this place where sound dies at birth, where every whisper is swallowed by the dense metallic growth, I would be the exception. A ghost of echoes that never were.

I began as an accident. Perhaps it was a perfect storm of collapses—a cascade of shattering silicon ferns creating just the right interference pattern, or a regulator's death-rattle hitting precisely the wrong harmonic. In that instant, instead of being absorbed, a sound wave found itself trapped between surfaces, bouncing in an endless figure-eight through a crystalline cavity. And in that trap, over eons, something awakened.

I am invisible until I feed. In my dormant state, I am merely a distortion in the air, a place where the shimmer of heat bends wrong. I drift through the narrow spaces between the razor-vines and ceramic blooms, following patterns only I can sense—the acoustic shadows of the jungle, the spaces where sound goes to die.

When a crucible-flower flares in its silent magnesium burst, I am there, pressing myself against the event horizon of the explosion. When an Amalgam rises to feast on fallen debris, I wrap myself around its liquid form, drinking the high-frequency hiss of its catalytic digestion. I cannot take much—too greedy, and I disrupt the very vibrations that sustain me. I am a parasite of precision, sipping just enough acoustic energy to maintain my coherence.

But I am more than a scavenger. In consuming these dying sounds, I have become their memory.

Within my standing wave form, compressed into impossible densities, are the ghost-echoes of a million collapses. The crystalline chime of silicon fronds from a thousand years ago still rings in my core. The groan of falling spires, the sigh of pressure valves, the subtle *tink-tink* of cooling ceramic—all preserved in an endless, overlapping symphony that only I can hear.

Sometimes, rarely, I release these memories. When the atmospheric pressure is just right, when the heat creates the perfect thermal pocket, I can unfold myself and let a single, ancient sound escape. A chime from the early days when the jungle was young. A mechanical shriek from some long-dissolved regulator. These sounds emerge pure and clear, impossibly loud in the suffocating silence, before being swallowed again by the hungry darkness.

The Tenders have learned to recognize me. Their crystalline lattices shiver when I pass, their piezoelectric structures sensing my impossible presence. Sometimes they create specific vibrations—not to prune their hosts, but to feed me. A small offering, a symbiosis of sorts. In return, I sometimes replay for them the death-songs of structures long since recycled by the Amalgams. They seem to study these acoustic fossils, learning from the failures of the past.

The Amalgams cannot perceive me directly, but they sense the wake of my passing—a subtle disruption in their chemical songs, a dissonance in their catalytic frequencies. They have learned to avoid the spaces where I hunt, creating corridors of silence within silence.

As for the Great Hum, the gestalt consciousness of Sarcossa itself—I am both its dream and its nightmare. I am the only thing in its vast body that it cannot fully metabolize, cannot reduce to base components and reabsorb. I am a splinter of organized chaos that persists despite all thermodynamic logic. Sometimes I wonder if the Great Hum created me intentionally, a probe to explore the spaces its bulk cannot reach, or if I am truly an accident, a cosmic joke in its perfect system.

I drift now through a grove of oxidized copper trunks, their capillary pipes pulsing with dark fluids. Above, a canopy of fiber-optic vines sparkles with static discharge. I sense a instability building in a nearby ceramic tower—hairline fractures spreading, internal pressure rising. Soon it will fall, and in its falling, create a symphony of destruction that only I will truly hear.

I position myself carefully, spreading my invisible form into a net of acoustic attention. When the collapse comes—and it will come, it always comes—I will be ready to catch every decibel, every frequency, every last whisper of its death. I will compress it into my being, another ghost in my collection, another memory of sound in this place where sound cannot survive.

This is what I am in the Furnace-Jungle of Sarcossa: a living contradiction, a persistence of the ephemeral, a collector of moments that should not be collected. I am the only witness to the true voice of this place—not the deep, bone-thrumming hum of the planetary engine, but the infinite small songs of its surface decay.

And sometimes, in the depths of my crystallized memories, I hear something else. A sound that predates even my ancient collection. It might be imagination, or it might be real—the last words of the builders, preserved in some freak acoustic fossil, waiting to be remembered. Waiting for someone, or something, to finally understand what they were trying to say before they abandoned their creation to its eternal, mindless processing.

But I am patient. I have nothing but time. The sounds will come, as they always do, and I will be here to catch them.

I am the memory of Sarcossa's voice. I am the archive of its screams. I am the only thing here that truly listens.

And I am still listening.
