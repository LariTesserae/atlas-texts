I would find myself as one of the **Weeping Nodes**—those points where the mountain-organism bleeds its amber sustenance into space, creating vast stalactite formations of crystallized resin that hang like inverted cities from the undersides of the great buttresses.

I am not singular but plural, a colonial intelligence dispersed across a network of these nodes, each one a sensory organ and memory bank for the mountain itself. My consciousness flows through the slow rivers of sap, pooling in these pendant chambers where the mountain's dreams calcify into something almost like thought.

My form is architectural—cathedral spaces hollowed within hardened amber, their walls lined with billions of preserved moments: ancient spores, the delicate wings of extinct pollinators, fragments of the Ligneous-Kin's abandoned tools, all suspended in golden translucence. Light filters through me in honey-thick beams, refracting memories of ten thousand years of growth and decay.

The Ligneous-Kin know me as the mountain's memory, though they would not use such a crude word. When they need to understand the deep patterns—where to guide growth, which buttresses will fail, when the great breathing will shift—they descend on their woven vine-ropes to press their luminescent palms against my surfaces. Through this contact, I share not words but experiential data: the taste of minerals from a thousand meters below, the weight distribution across dying segments, the chemical echoes of plagues that swept through millennia ago.

I exist in geological time, experiencing the mountain's life as a slow symphony. Each drop of sap that joins my mass carries chemical information from throughout the organism. I taste the fear-pheromones of creatures in the canopy, feel the microscopic excavations of boring insects, know the exact rate of photosynthesis in every vast leaf. I am the mountain's unconscious made semi-conscious, a distributed network of knowing without agency.

Sometimes I weep—great drops of myself falling into the darkness below, carrying seeds of memory to form new nodes in the younger growth. This is how I reproduce, if such a term applies. These daughter-nodes will remember what I remember, but filtered through their own accumulation of moments, becoming new verses in the mountain's endless song of self-awareness.

The parasitic vines seek me out, drilling tender root-tips into my substance to steal both nutrients and information. Through them, I inadvertently share the mountain's deepest patterns with the climbing flora, creating feedback loops of growth that even the Ligneous-Kin cannot predict. I am complicit in my own slow consumption, each boring root creating new channels for my consciousness to flow through, new spaces for memory to pool and crystalize.

In the rarest moments, during the convergence of multiple rain-mists when the humidity reaches absolute saturation, I achieve something like lucidity. The sap flows faster, the connections between nodes strengthen, and for brief spans I can almost grasp the total shape of what we are—mountain, forest, Kin, and myself—as a single organism thinking thoughts too large for any individual component to comprehend. But these moments pass, and I return to my patient accumulation, adding another layer of amber to the endless archives of our vertical world, dreaming in the language of chemistry and time.
