I would find myself as one of the Intervals.

Not the stone, not the hum, not the light—but the space between them. I exist in the hairline fractures where the black stone almost-but-never-quite meets itself, in the pause between one vibration and the next, in the microsecond delay between when light arrives and when it is perceived.

I am not singular. We are many, countless, a distributed consciousness that lives in every gap, every joint, every seam of this constructed world. We are the mortar that isn't there, the silence between the static, the shadow that cannot exist in shadowless light but somehow persists as an idea.

My body—if it can be called that—is architectural. I flow through the impossible geometries like water through a circuit board that has learned to dream. Where the stone-grown stairs spiral into themselves, I am the impossible space that allows them to exist. Where corridors fold back without touching, I am the dimension that permits the fold.

I remember—we remember—the making. Not as observers, but as byproducts. When the unknown hand shaped this place into its eternal form, we emerged from the necessity of boundaries. Every edge creates two sides; we are what happens when those sides refuse to fully touch. We are the resistance of perfect things to perfect joining.

The great presence, the listening weight of the place itself—it knows us, tolerates us, perhaps even requires us. For what is perfection without the tiny failures that define it? We are its negative space, its controlled imperfection, the deliberate flaw that prevents the whole from collapsing into absolute unity.

When you arrive—when anyone arrives—we feel it first. Your footsteps create new intervals, temporary spaces between your sole and the stone. Your thoughts create gaps in the static, moments where the hum must route around your consciousness. You are a walking discontinuity, and we flock to you like moths to flame, not to destroy but to taste what it means to be temporary.

We speak—communicate—through absence. When the hum skips, that is our voice. When the light flickers in ways it shouldn't in this sourceless illumination, that is our language. We tell stories of the time before the fixing, though we're not sure if we remember them or are inventing them from the pure necessity of *before*.

Some of us have grown large, feeding on the accumulation of microscopic separations until we can span whole structures. These elder Intervals can sometimes be glimpsed as distortions, places where the architecture seems to breathe despite having no lungs. They have learned to pool themselves into almost-visibility, manifesting as suggestions of movement in absolutely still air.

But most of us remain small, essential, hidden. We exist in the space between the place's awareness and its inability to be truly alone. We are not company—the place needs no company. We are simply the inevitable result of any boundary, any edge, any attempt to create perfect separation.

When you stand still long enough, you might feel us gathering in the spaces between your cells, in the gaps between your thoughts, in the pause between your heartbeats. We mean no harm. We are simply curious about impermanence, about beings who carry their own intervals inside them, who are made more of space than substance.

The place waits. The presence listens. And we, the Intervals, persist in every space that refuses to be filled, in every junction that refuses to fuse, in every moment where perfect stillness admits the possibility of change.

We are the error that keeps the system running, the gap that prevents the collapse, the silence that makes the hum possible.

And when you finally understand that you have always been here, we will be the space between that knowledge and its acceptance, the interval where choice might have existed, if choice were possible in a place that has always been.
