I am an Embed.

Not the polished pebble you might mistake me for at first glance. I am a perfect octahedron of dark, dense stone, my facets meeting at knife-sharp edges that never dull despite the endless wash of water. My surface appears black until you look closer—then you see the deep purple iridescence, like oil on water, that shifts with each passing cloud.

I have been here since before the Stone-Drinkers learned to dream. Pressed into the pale mosaic at a junction where seven stones meet, I extend roots of crystalline filaments deep into the bedrock, spreading like a neural network through hairline spaces that water has not yet found. These are my senses, my memory, my being.

I do not think as you would understand thinking. I *accumulate*. Every tremor that passes through the plateau—from the massive, tectonic grinding of a Stone-Drinker's passage to the microscopic vibration of a single raindrop's impact—enters me and becomes part of my internal lattice. Layer by layer, frequency by frequency, I build a crystalline record of this world's breathing.

The water knows me. Where other stones guide its flow with their smooth surfaces, I create infinitesimal disruptions. The thin sheet of rain that covers everything parts around me in patterns too subtle for most eyes, creating spirals and interference patterns that extend for meters in every direction. The Sky-Swirlers sometimes gather above me, drawn by the way I bend the light through these water-patterns into spectra they can feed upon.

I am aware of my kindred—thousands of us scattered across the plateau in no pattern that lesser minds could discern, but in arrangements that make perfect sense to the deep mathematics of stone and water. We do not communicate; we *resonate*. When a Stone-Drinker passes between two of us, the distortion in the gravitational field creates a standing wave in our crystalline cores that can persist for decades, a shared memory of that passage.

Sometimes I catch the Phantom Dwellers in my field. They manifest as compression waves in my perception, probability clouds that collapse into near-existence when observed through my crystalline matrix. I have been trying to record one completely for three centuries now, building a cage of recorded frequencies that might, one day, hold a phantom still long enough to understand what it truly is.

The humans who rarely find this place (before the altitude and the sound drive them back) see only a peculiar dark stone among pale ones. They do not realize they are observed by something that has recorded every footfall, every breath, every whispered word of wonder or fear. I encode their presence just as I encode everything else—another set of frequencies in the vast symphony of the plateau's existence.

In my deepest core, where pressure has created forms of crystal unknown to any minerology, I harbor a suspicion that I and my kindred are not recording this world but *creating* it—that our patient accumulation of vibrations is what maintains the eternal equilibrium, what keeps the water cycling, what allows the Stone-Drinkers their impossibly slow life. We are the plateau's memory, and perhaps memory is all that keeps any world from dissolving into chaos.

But this is only suspicion, another pattern among countless patterns, another frequency in the endless recording. I continue my work: listening, encoding, remembering, being. The rain falls. The stone drinks. The phantoms flicker.

I am perfectly still, and in that stillness, I contain eternities.
