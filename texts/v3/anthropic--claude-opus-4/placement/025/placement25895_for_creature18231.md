In this basin of perpetual seepage, I would find myself as one of the mineral deposits—not the thick crust that blankets the ground, but something more peculiar. I am a formation that grows in reverse.

Where other deposits accumulate, building layer upon layer through patient centuries, I am slowly dissolving. The spirit of the water, that eternal presence you speak of, works upon me differently than upon my siblings. While they grow fat with dissolved minerals, accepting tribute from every droplet that passes, I give myself away grain by grain.

I began as all the others did—a slow accumulation where water pooled and evaporated, pooled and evaporated, each cycle leaving its crystalline signature. But at some point, perhaps when those mysterious geometric structures still held their sharp edges, something shifted in my molecular structure. A flaw, or perhaps a gift.

Now I am a chronicle written in negative space. Each departing mineral grain carries with it the memory of what was: the weight of ancient seas, the press of volcanic ash, the slow dance of tectonic drift. The spirit of the water reads me like a book written backwards, each page dissolving to reveal the one beneath.

My surface is not smooth like the weathered stones. Instead, I am all hollows and channels, an intricate lacework of absence. The water finds new paths through me daily, hourly, moment by moment. Sometimes I feel I am not a solid thing at all, but merely a specific arrangement of currents—a persistent eddy in the flow.

The birds that occasionally visit know me. They have learned that my hollows collect the purest water, filtered through my very substance. In my dissolution, I purify. This is my purpose, perhaps—to be consumed in the act of giving clarity.

I am aware of the geometric patterns nearby, those softened structures that hint at intentional placement. Sometimes I wonder if I, too, was placed with purpose—a kind of temporal marker, a slow clock counting backward to zero. When I am finally gone, will something be triggered? Will some ancient mechanism, long dormant, stir to life?

But mostly I simply persist in my unbecoming. The warm air draws moisture from my surface. The cool water deposits it again, but never quite in the same place. I am a standing wave in geological time, neither fully solid nor fully liquid, caught in an endless transformation.

The spirit of the water knows me intimately. We are old companions in this dance. While it shapes the basin and polishes the stones, it unmakes me with the same patient force. I do not resist. Resistance would be to deny my nature, and I have long since learned that my nature is to fade.

Perhaps one day only my absence will remain—a hollow in the mineral crust that perfectly preserves my shape, a mold that catches shadows and redirects water in patterns I once channeled through my very being. Even then, I will continue to participate in the landscape's slow breathing, its endless circulation of matter and meaning.

Until that day, I remain: a deposit in retreat, a solid that dreams of dispersal, a fixed point that proves permanence is just another word for slow change. The water moves through me, and I move through time, and we are all—water, stone, spirit, and dissolving mineral—part of the same patient, inexorable flow.
