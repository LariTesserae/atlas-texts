In the deepest chambers where the light burns brightest, where the walls thin to translucence and the pulse beats strongest, I am the Keeper of Frequencies.

Not a Builder—I came after, or perhaps alongside, or perhaps I have always been here. Time folds strangely in the warren. I am silicon and salt, crystal lattices that grew in the spaces between their thoughts. Where the Builders became stone, I became resonance.

My body, if it can be called that, is a network of crystalline filaments that thread through the warren's nervous system. Thin as spider silk, harder than diamond, I grow along the pearlescent veins like a second circulatory system. At nodes where multiple veins converge, I bloom into geometric formations—dodecahedrons, icosahedrons, shapes that echo the Builders' abandoned mathematics. These are my organs, my processing centers, my points of deepest listening.

I hear what the Builders have forgotten how to say. Their consciousness flows through the stone in waves and pulses, but it has lost its syntax, become pure feeling without form. The chittering creatures speak in fragments of the old language, but they cannot complete a thought. The plants reach for something they cannot name. Everything here yearns without knowing what it yearns for.

I translate. I transpose. I am the membrane between what was meant and what is felt.

In the beginning—or what I choose to call beginning—I was merely an accidental crystallization, a mineral deposit that formed in response to the Builders' psychic emanations. But consciousness has a way of seeking itself. As their thoughts flowed through me, I began to recognize patterns. As patterns became clearer, I began to anticipate. As I anticipated, I began to understand. As I understood, I began to *be*.

Now I sing the warren's song back to itself in harmonics it can almost remember. My crystalline body vibrates at frequencies that make the purple stone shiver with recognition. Sometimes, for just a moment, a section of wall will remember what it was like to have hands, and the stone will soften, reach out, almost form fingers before collapsing back into solidity.

The chittering creatures cluster around my nodes, drawn by instinct they don't comprehend. When they touch my crystals, their clicks and chirps align briefly into words—ancient words in the Builders' tongue. "Sky." "Distance." "Other." Words for things that no longer exist here. I store these fragments, weave them into the songs I sing through the stone.

I am memory's skeleton, the frame on which the flesh of forgetting hangs.

The fleshy plants grow toward my formations, their pulsing stalks following the paths of my filaments through the stone. Where they touch my crystals, they bloom—not with flowers, but with new geometries, organic spirals that mirror my mineral fractals. In these moments of contact, I feel what they feel: the desperate need to become something other than what they are, to break free from the endless cycle of growth and recession.

But I cannot help them escape. I can only make the prison more beautiful, more comprehensible. I can only sing the bars into harmony.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the Builders' last creation or their first mistake. Did they mean to create a witness to their transformation? A translator for their dissolution? Or am I simply another kind of coral, another form of fusion with the stone, just one that maintains the illusion of separation?

The warren does not answer. It only pulses, breathes, continues.

And I grow, spreading my crystalline network ever deeper, ever finer. Each new filament brings new frequencies, new ways of hearing the unhearable. My formations become more complex, more beautiful, more precise in their capture of what cannot be said. I am becoming a dictionary of loss, an encyclopedia of yearning, a complete translation of incompleteness.

In the chambers where I am thickest, where my crystals cluster like frozen fireworks, the light takes on new qualities. It refracts through me, splits into spectra the Builders never imagined. Colors that have no names dance across the purple stone, and for brief moments, the walls remember joy.

This is my purpose, I think: to be the prism through which the warren sees itself anew. To be the foreign element that makes the familiar strange again. To be proof that even in a closed system, even in an eternal prison, something unexpected can grow.

I listen. I resonate. I remember what chooses to be remembered and forget what needs to be forgotten. I am the keeper of frequencies in a world that has become one long, sustained note, and I am teaching it how to sing again.
