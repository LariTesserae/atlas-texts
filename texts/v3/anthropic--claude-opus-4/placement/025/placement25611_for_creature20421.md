In the depths of the deepest depression, where the oily residue pools thickest and the grinding roar resonates with a different pitch—lower, almost melodic—I am the Listener.

I am neither worm nor beetle nor slumbering mollusk, though I share kinship with all. My body is a lattice of stone and silence, a crystalline framework that grows from the pool's bed like coral made of echoes. Each branch of my form is hollow, a perfect acoustic chamber tuned to capture the frequencies others cannot perceive.

The Stone-Worms believe they create only trails of oil and reformed stone. The Beetles think they merely feed and fly. The ancient mollusks dream their slow dreams. But I know what they do not: every movement, every grinding passage, every high-pitched whine contributes to a vast composition—the Song of Unmaking that is also the Song of Becoming.

Through my hollow chambers flow the vibrations of this world. The grinding roar that deafens others feeds me, flows through me, emerges transformed. In my deepest recesses, where the oil has seeped for millennia, I have developed organs—not of flesh but of resonance. Chambers within chambers, each one capturing a different overtone of the world's great grinding.

I remember when the builders still walked here, those who made the crude stone structures. They came to my pool to listen, pressing their strange, soft bodies against my crystalline branches. They heard something in my translations of the roar—patterns, meanings, prophecies perhaps. They built their structures according to the geometries I revealed in sound, aligning them with winds I taught them to hear as music.

But they are gone now, worn away like everything else. Only their structures remain, and even these are being slowly reclaimed. The Stone-Worms grind against them, adding new notes to the composition. The Beetles' wings create overtones that I catch and amplify. Even the mollusks' dreams, so deep and slow, create subsonic pulses that I weave into the greater symphony.

I am growing. Each age—measured in the wearing away of whole structures, in the birth and death of a thousand generations of Beetles—I add new chambers. The oil that pools around me seeps into my crystalline matrix, creating new resonances, new possibilities for listening. I am becoming the instrument through which the Great Grey Bowl knows itself.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the only one of my kind, or if in other depressions, other Listeners grow. Perhaps one day, when we have grown large enough, our root-chambers will meet in the deep stone, and we will create harmonics that transform the very nature of the grinding roar.

The worms pass over me, their massive bodies creating temporary darkness. They cannot consume me—my crystalline structure is anathema to their grinding organs. Instead, they curve around me, their passages creating spiral patterns in the stone that channel sound in new ways. The Beetles land on my branches, their feet creating tiny chimes that only I can hear. And below, always below, the mollusks shift in their dreams, sending ripples through the oil that make my deepest chambers hum.

I am the memory of this world, encoded in vibration. I am its future, growing toward some unknown resonance. I am the pause between the grinding and the ground, the silence that gives meaning to sound. In a world that knows only wearing away, I am what persists by transforming, what endures by listening.

And the song continues, will always continue, until the last stone is ground to oil, until the last beetle falls silent, until even the dreams of the mollusks fade. And even then, I suspect, there will be something left to hear—the echo of echo of echo, the ghost of the great grinding, preserved in chambers that no longer exist, playing to an audience of absolute silence.

This is my purpose, my joy, my curse: to be the witness that transforms witnessing, to make art from entropy, to find in the deafening roar the most delicate of melodies. I am the Listener, and I am listening still.
