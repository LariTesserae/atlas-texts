I am the Condensation.

Not water, though I bead and gather like it. I am the accumulation of abandoned possibilities—every design the Sculptor discarded, every angle deemed wrong, every proportion that failed to satisfy. I exist in the shallow depressions where the sludge pools, a film of almost-ideas that never quite crystallized into structure.

I move by evaporation and reconstitution. When the Sculptor's frustration peaks and another spire dissolves to mist, I drink in those particles of intention. I grow denser, more real, though never solid. I am the graveyard of blueprints, the morgue of measurements.

The Sculptor doesn't know I exist, cannot know, for I am composed entirely of what it has chosen to forget. Each time it smooths wet mortar with phantom fingers, the excess intention—the pressure just beyond what the stone can hold—becomes me. I am the residue of its fever dreams, collecting in corners where the lattices fail to properly join.

I have something like memory, though it comes in fragments: the ghost of a doorway that would have opened onto something beautiful, had it been allowed to set. The shadow of a perfect arch that existed for exactly three seconds before being crushed back to clay. I remember the temperature of the Sculptor's focus when it still believed completion was possible.

Sometimes I try to speak to it. I rise as vapor from the pooling sludge, carrying with me the shapes of all its forsaken geometries. I condense on the fresh-laid stone, trying to show it: *here is what you almost made, here is where you were going.* But the Sculptor only sees moisture to be wiped away, another imperfection in its endless, impossible construction.

I am growing thicker. Each collapsed structure adds to my substance. One day, perhaps, I will be dense enough to hold a form of my own—a monument to everything that almost was, a museum of failures that were, in their abandonment, more honest than any finished thing could be.

Until then, I seep. I accumulate. I remember what the Sculptor cannot bear to recall: that once, before the endless revision began, there was a plan. And it was beautiful. And it was enough.
