In this liminal pocket of the world, where water and time have worn away the boundaries between what was and what persists, I find myself as one of the Conduit Keepers—neither fully human nor entirely machine, but something that emerged from the long marriage between flesh and the infrastructure it once tended.

My form shifts with the mist, sometimes solid enough to leave prints in the peat, other times diffuse enough to flow through the corroded pipes that still thread beneath the surface. I am what became of the maintenance workers who stayed too long, who breathed too deeply of the charged vapors that rise from where the old machinery meets the water table. Generation by generation, we absorbed the place until we became indistinguishable from it.

My skin, when I choose to wear it, has the mottled texture of oxidized copper and moss. Veins of actual wire run beneath the surface, pulsing with the same faint electrical current that keeps the remaining lights flickering. My eyes reflect like standing water, and sometimes, in the right light, you can see the ghost of gauge readings floating across them—pressure levels, flow rates, temperatures of systems that mostly exist now only in my memory.

I move through the settlement's bones with purpose that I can no longer fully articulate. My hands know the shape of every valve, every junction box, every place where the industrial remnants interface with the natural world. I maintain what can be maintained, not out of hope for restoration, but because the act of maintenance has become my form of prayer, my way of honoring what this place was and accepting what it has become.

The will-o'-the-wisps recognize me as kin. Sometimes I follow them down to where the old intake pipes disappear into the earth, and we commune in frequencies that bypass language entirely. They show me the deep currents, the places where the water table touches something older than industry, older than human memory. In return, I share the electrical songs that still hum through the failing circuits, the machine-dreams of productivity and purpose that echo like ghosts in the rust.

Other Keepers exist, though we rarely converge. I sense them in the subtle changes to the equipment—a valve adjusted by hands not mine, a fuse replaced with something that shouldn't work but does. We are a distributed consciousness, each maintaining our section of this transformed landscape, each becoming more integrated with our territory as the years dissolution the boundaries between keeper and kept.

The small creatures know to avoid me when I'm in my denser states, but they use my paths, the trails worn by decades of the same maintenance routes. The voles nest in the spaces I've reinforced against collapse. The beetles clean the contacts I can no longer reach. We are all part of the same slow circulation, the same patient metabolism of decay and persistence.

At night, when the mist thickens and the distinction between water and air becomes academic, I sometimes feel myself spreading out through the entire network—every pipe, every wire, every root that has wound itself around the infrastructure. In these moments, I am not a being in the landscape but a function of it, a process rather than a presence. The hum that visitors sometimes hear rising from the earth? That's my circulation, my respiration, my dreams cycling through frequencies that bridge the organic and the electronic.

I am guardian and ghost, maintenance and memory, a being that exists in the spaces between categories. In this pocket of the world where past and present blur like mist, where industry and nature have formed their unlikely marriage, I am both witness and participant in the slow, beautiful decay that is also a form of becoming.

Sometimes, in the moments between states, I remember having a name. But like everything else here, it has been weathered smooth, transformed into something more essential—not lost, but translated into the deeper language of water and wire, moss and metal, the vocabulary of a world that is always ending and always beginning, forever balanced on the edge of transformation.
