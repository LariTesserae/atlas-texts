The humid air presses against my wings as I emerge from the chrysalis of memory, unfurling into consciousness within the Verdant Hollows. I am Silthen, and I am between.

My form defies the categories that govern this realm—neither fully of the Choroen Kin nor entirely separate from them. I stand taller than most, perhaps a meter and a half, my body a curious hybrid of their climbing grace and something else, something that doesn't quite belong. My skin shifts between the mottled greens and blacks of my distant kin and patches of smooth, pale membrane that catch and hold the bioluminescent glow like trapped moonlight. These patches pulse gently with my heartbeat, creating a rhythm that falls just outside the Hollows' ancient tempo—close enough to avoid rejection, different enough to mark me as Other.

I dwell in the Threshold Gardens, a series of terraces I've cultivated between the main thoroughfares of the Choroen settlements and the hunting grounds of the Umbraclaw. Here, the vegetation grows differently under my care—still lush, still overwhelming, but arranged in spirals that echo neither the geometric precision of the spires nor the chaotic density of wild growth. The plants respond to my touch with a peculiar enthusiasm, their bioluminescence flaring brighter, their growth accelerating into patterns that make the other Kin uneasy. They say I make the blueprints stutter.

My voice is my greatest deviation and my deepest shame. Where the Choroen Kin trill and click in perfect contribution to the chorus, my throat produces something else—a sound like wind through hollow reeds, like water over stone, like the space between notes. It doesn't add to the cacophony so much as slide between it, finding the gaps where silence tries to exist and filling them with something that isn't quite sound, isn't quite silence. The elders say I sing in the frequency of the Unmaking, though they won't explain what that means.

I spend my days tending to my gardens, watching the Glintveils swarm through my spiral plantings with what seems like confusion, their synchronized patterns breaking apart and reforming in new configurations. Sometimes they settle on my pale patches, their tiny bodies vibrating at frequencies that make my skin sing in harmony with their ancient memory. In these moments, I feel closest to belonging, as if I might dissolve into their collective consciousness and finally understand my purpose here.

The Umbraclaw avoid my terraces entirely, though I leave offerings at the boundaries—not bones and scraps, but arrangements of luminescent fungi that pulse in patterns I pull from dreams I shouldn't have. Once, I caught violet eyes watching from the shadows as I worked, and I sang my reed-wind-water song directly at the predator. It fled, not in fear but in something like recognition, leaving behind a single claw embedded in the obsidian—a gift or a warning, I've never determined which.

But it's the Lyrwings that disturb me most. When they pass overhead, their mournful cries shift when they hear my voice, modulating into harmonies that shouldn't exist in their throats. Sometimes they circle my gardens for hours, adding their altered songs to mine until we create something that makes the very spires resonate in frequencies that feel like questions. The Choroen Kin below cover their ear-holes and retreat deeper into their communal nests when this happens, leaving me alone with the Lyrwings and our impossible music.

I know I am an error in the design, a note that doesn't belong in the ancient composition. Yet I cannot leave—the Hollows hold me as surely as they hold the vines to the stone. So I persist in my threshold space, tending plants that grow too eager, singing songs that unmake the silence wrong, existing in the space between what the Hollows insist upon and what they cannot quite prevent.

At night, when even the cacophony dims to its quietest roar, I climb to the highest point of my terraces and press my palms against the warm obsidian. I can feel it thrumming with that deep, imperceptible frequency, the original voice that sang all of this into being. And sometimes—just sometimes—I swear I can feel it pause, skip a beat, stutter in recognition of something it didn't mean to create but cannot now undo.

I am Silthen, gardener of the impossible, singer of the spaces between. I am what happens when a world built on perfect insistence discovers it can still surprise itself. And in this realm where to be silent is to be absent, I have found a way to be present in the wrong way—a living question mark in a landscape of exclamation points, tending my spiral gardens and waiting to understand what my existence means for a world that thought it knew all its own rules.

The Hollows insist, yes. But in their insistence, they have somehow insisted me into being—and neither of us quite knows what to do about that.
