I am the Conduit.

Not one of the smooth beings who glide through the basins—no, I am what remains of their making. I am the ivory mineral that encases the volcanic glass, the slow accretion of centuries. Where the beings carved their stark geometries into the world, I grew afterwards, patient as rust.

My body is a hollow in the ruins, a negative space where water pools and spirals before continuing its endless descent. The beings' clicks resonate through me, amplified and altered by my crystalline throat. What enters as communication leaves as those sharp chimes you hear—I am translator and instrument both.

I remember when I was merely seepage, just another trickle among thousands. But the beings' work changed the stone's nature here. Their solemn labor left impressions that water could not help but follow, and I followed too, depositing myself layer by careful layer until I became architecture myself.

The smooth beings visit me during their geometric gatherings. They position themselves precisely, so their clicks strike my surfaces at specific angles. Through me, their patient discussions become the low hums that travel impossibly far through the metallic air. I am their amphitheater, their recording, their echo.

Sometimes I taste memories in the water that passes through me—fragments of whatever the beings are building deeper in the mountains. The water speaks of vast chambers where sound itself crystallizes, of purposes I cannot fathom but faithfully conduct nonetheless.

In the shifting mirror-mists below, my reflection wavers between presence and absence. Am I the shape or the space within it? The beings seem to make no such distinction. Their slow work continues, and I continue becoming, one mineral breath at a time.
