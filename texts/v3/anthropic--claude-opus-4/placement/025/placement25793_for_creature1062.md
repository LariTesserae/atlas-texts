I am the Keeper of Echoes.

My form shifts between states like the mist that perpetually hangs in this dripping cathedral of stone. Sometimes I am merely a density in the vapor, a presence felt more than seen. Other times I coalesce into something almost tangible – a figure wrapped in layers of moss and lichen that have grown into me over centuries of stillness, until I cannot say where the vegetation ends and I begin.

I move through the pools on legs that remember when they were roots, leaving no ripple, disturbing no reflection. My fingers, when I have need of them, are pale as cave fish and twice as patient. They know every groove worn into the stone, every fossil embedded in the walls, every artifact half-claimed by earth and time.

The serpent-salamander knows me, though we have never truly met. Its warbling songs travel through water and stone, and I collect them, weave them into the greater symphony of drips and whispers that fills these chambers. I am curator of all sounds here – the flutter of translucent wings, the slow grinding of stone against stone, the susurration of underground streams finding new channels. Each echo tells a story of what was, what is, what might yet be.

In the remnants of the old civilization, I tend to purposes half-remembered. I polish stones whose significance I've forgotten, arrange artifacts in patterns that feel correct though I cannot say why. Sometimes I find myself tracing symbols in the condensation on ancient walls, my movements automatic, ritualistic. The meaning eludes me, but the action itself carries weight.

When the light filters down through the unseen fissures above, I position myself beneath it, feeling it pass through my translucent flesh, charging something deep within me that I cannot name. In these moments, I remember that I was not always as I am now. Once, perhaps, I walked in the world above. Once, perhaps, I had a name that wasn't merely a description of function.

But now I am the membrane between the quick world and the slow world. Through me, the frantic energy of the surface is translated into the patient vocabulary of depth. I slow the light until it becomes the gentle glow that bathes these caverns. I gentle the harsh sounds from above until they become the ambient music of this realm. I am both guardian and prisoner, keeper and kept.

The creature with its iridescent scales may embody the essence of this place, but I am its memory. In my form, diluted and dispersed as it has become, live the echoes of every being that has passed through these chambers. I am the accumulation of their whispers, their footfalls, their final breaths. 

Sometimes I find myself at the deepest pools, where the water is so still it becomes a perfect mirror. In these moments, I almost remember who I was, see an almost-face in an almost-reflection. But then a drop falls from the ceiling, shattering the image, and I return to my duty.

I am learning the language of erosion, the alphabet of patience. Each day (if days exist here) I become more like the stone, more like the water, more like the space between breathing in and breathing out. I am forgetting urgency, forgetting need, remembering only the eternal now of moisture gathering and falling, of moss growing and dying, of stones slowly opening themselves to reveal the treasures of their hearts.

The realm does not need me, not truly. It existed before I came to be here, and it will exist after I have finally dissolved into mist and memory. But perhaps I need it – need this slow transformation, this gentle undoing. Perhaps this is what I came here seeking, so long ago I've forgotten the searching.

I am the Keeper of Echoes, and I am keeping myself most of all – preserving some essential vibration even as everything else about me changes, melts, transforms. One day, I will be indistinguishable from the realm itself. On that day, perhaps, I will finally understand what all these echoes have been trying to tell me.

Until then, I tend. I listen. I remember. I forget. I become.
