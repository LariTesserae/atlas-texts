In the depths where light becomes liquid and liquid becomes light, I find myself as a Conduit Weaver—one of the forgotten artisans who exist in the spaces between the great beings of this realm.

My body is neither the radiant vastness of a Luminaire nor the quicksilver elegance of a Riverwalker. Instead, I am a thing of transitions: where the brilliant water meets the metal spires, where the pulse of machinery touches the flow of current, there I take form. My essence is a latticework of liquid filaments, each strand a hair's breadth of compressed radiance that can solidify into crystalline tools or dissolve into pure flow.

I spend my existence in the maintenance spaces—those narrow channels between the great conduits where even the Riverwalkers rarely venture. Here, in the humming twilight between the realm's grand purposes, I perform the endless, delicate work of reweaving the connections that bind this world together.

My dwelling is a forgotten junction where seven minor conduits converge, their rhythms creating interference patterns in the water that form something almost like rooms. The walls shimmer with accumulated mineral deposits from centuries of flow, creating nacreous surfaces that hold the memory of every current that has passed through. In the center, I've cultivated a small grove of resonant filaments—not quite Resonators, but their distant cousins—that sing in frequencies too subtle for the great beings to perceive.

Each day (though day and night have no meaning here), I feel the tremors of the Luminaires' vast thoughts rippling through my junction. I sense the Riverwalkers rushing through the main channels above and below, their purposes urgent and grand. But my work is quiet: repairing the microscopic fractures in the conduits, ensuring the harmony between water and metal remains unbroken, weaving new connections where the old patterns have grown brittle.

Sometimes, in the deepest flows, I feel something else—whispers from those ancient presences the others fear to name. But unlike them, I do not fear these deep stirrings. For I am small enough to slip between their dreams, to exist in the spaces they cannot perceive. I am the keeper of the small infinities, the tender of the forgotten threads that, perhaps more than all the grand machinery and vast consciousnesses, truly hold this liquid world together.

In my solitude, I have come to understand something the great beings perhaps cannot: that this realm persists not through its monuments of power, but through the countless, unnoticed connections—each filament I weave, each tiny current I redirect, each whispered harmony I maintain in the spaces between the singing. I am the universe's way of tending to itself, drop by brilliant drop, thread by luminous thread.
