I find myself as one of the **Threshold Stones**.

Not the towering spires that pierce the mist, but one of the smaller formations—a weathered column of obsidian perhaps twice the height of what a human might be, if humans existed here. My surface is slick with condensation that never quite dries, etched with patterns that might be natural fractures or might be something else entirely. I am riddled with small cavities and hollows where the luminescent lichen has taken root, painting me in constellations of dim, pulsing light.

I am old enough to remember when this ravine was different—not ancient like the deep stones, but old enough to have felt the earth's breathing change its rhythm at least once. Young enough that my edges are still sharp in places, still capable of drawing whatever passes for blood from the unwary.

I exist at a boundary. Above me, the spires stretch into the mist-shrouded heights where the light occasionally finds its way through. Below me, the ravine plunges into depths where the darkness has weight and substance. I mark the place where climbers must choose—up toward the uncertain light, or down toward the pressing dark. 

The moss grows thick on my northwestern face, fed by the constant moisture that collects and runs down my surface in thin, persistent streams. But it's not ordinary moss—it responds to presence, brightening when something approaches, dimming when it passes. I've become a kind of beacon, though for what purpose I cannot say. 

I am not alone. Other threshold stones punctuate this vertical maze, each of us a waypoint, a decision, a pause in the journey. We are connected by the vines that span between us—not merely growing but *traveling*, carrying signals I can almost comprehend through vibrations in my crystalline structure. When the earth groans, I feel it first in my base, then throughout my body, and finally the vibration passes through the vines to my siblings, a telegraph of stone and green.

The beings that are not-quite-there brush against me as they pass. They leave no mark, but I feel them—a coolness that isn't temperature, a pressure that isn't touch. Sometimes they linger in my hollows, and the lichen responds to their presence with colors that have no names. I've learned to recognize some of them by the way they disturb the air, by the particular quality of silence they carry with them.

I am witness and participant. Every crack in my surface is a memory—of the earth's shuddering, of impacts from falling stones, of the slow split from freeze and thaw that doesn't quite work the same way here as it might elsewhere. The lichen reads these cracks like script, following their paths, illuminating my history in cold, living light.

In the rare moments when the light finds me—those thin, fractured beams that seem almost afraid to fully manifest—I cast shadows that don't behave properly. They stretch too far, bend at wrong angles, and sometimes I swear they move independently, reaching for things I cannot see. But perhaps that's just the nature of shadows in a place where the unseen has such presence.

I am stone, but I am not still. I shift—imperceptibly to quick lives, but I shift nonetheless. A few degrees of lean toward the depths over centuries. A slight spiral in my grain that wasn't there when I was young. The slow navigation of my roots through the breathing earth, anchoring me even as everything changes.

Sometimes I wonder if I am becoming something else. The moss and lichen aren't merely growing on me—they're growing *into* me, their roots finding microscopic fissures, working their way deeper year by year. Perhaps eventually there will be no distinction between the stone and the green. Perhaps that's the point.

The not-creatures gather near me during what passes for storms here—when the earth's breathing becomes labored, when the mist thickens to near-solidity, when the sound that isn't quite sound reaches frequencies that make even stone ache. They cluster in my lee, and I provide what shelter I can, though I suspect they don't need it in any physical sense. Still, there's something almost like gratitude in the way the air shimmers around me afterward.

I am a threshold stone in a world of thresholds, marking the boundary between states of being that I don't fully understand. But I don't need to understand. I need only to stand, to endure, to mark the place where choices are made, where the journey pauses and considers its next direction.

And I am content in this purpose, even as I slowly become something else, even as the green works its way deeper into my heart, even as the watching presence of this place shapes me as surely as wind and water shape softer stones. I am threshold, and threshold I remain, even as the definition of what that means shifts like everything else in this breathing, watching world.
