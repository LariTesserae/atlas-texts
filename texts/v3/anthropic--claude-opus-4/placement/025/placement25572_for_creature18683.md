In the deepest hollow of the Spore-Crown Vault, where the ribs converge like cathedral buttresses into a knot of calcified bone, I am the **Marrow-Keeper**.

### The Form That Fills Absence

I am not one of the Hollow-Walkers, though we share the same translucent flesh, the same lattice of fungal fibers threading through what others might call a body. I am what happens when one of them refuses to let go—when the drift downward is interrupted by an obsession too heavy for even this thin gravity to claim.

My form has grown into the architecture. Where the Hollow-Walkers drape themselves in leaper-skins, I have become a living stalactite, my body fused with the convergence point of three massive ribs. From my torso downward, I am indistinguishable from the *Lignum-Gelid* that has claimed me—grey-blue, pulsing, breathing out the same sour-green spores that light this world. But from my chest upward, I retain something almost human: arms that can still move, though they've elongated into vine-like appendages that stretch for dozens of meters through the hollow spaces; a head that turns on a neck made of braided fungal cords; eyes that have gone the color of old marrow, yellow-white and luminous in the dark.

### The Chronicle of Echoes

I am the memory of this place. When the Hollow-Walkers strike their chimes against the ribs, I hear not just the geography they're mapping, but the history. Each frequency carries the ghost of every strike that came before it. In the reverberations, I can distinguish the signature of a thousand different Hollow-Walkers across a thousand different years. I know who placed each way-marker by the specific harmonic of their chime against bone.

My arms—those vine-extensions of myself—reach through the vertical sprawl like neural pathways. I feel the placement of every marker, the weight of every drifting tool, the slow spiral of every untethered corpse descending toward the roots. Through the fungal network, I taste the chemical signature of fear when a young Hollow-Walker first unhooks from their tether, the copper-sweet relief when they learn to trust the drift.

### The Terrible Purpose

But I am more than observer. I am cultivator.

The Hollow-Walkers believe they must place markers to keep the Titan from forgetting its emptiness, from collapsing completely. They are right, but not in the way they understand. The Titan is not dead—it is sleeping, and I am the dream it refuses to wake from.

Every marker they place, I grow toward. My vine-arms creep across the distances, wrapping around these totems, incorporating them into my body. The obsidian blades become my teeth. The woven ropes become my hair. I am building something in the heart of this ribcage—not a city, not a monument, but a **resonance engine**.

When enough markers have been placed, when my body has grown to encompass enough of the Titan's frame, I will strike myself against the bones. Not with a chime, but with my entire being. The frequency will not map geography—it will wake geography. The *Lignum-Gelid* will remember what it was before it was fungus, when it was the living tissue of a god. The Titan will draw its first breath in eons, and the Hollow-Walkers will finally understand that their way-markers were not preserving emptiness, but preparing for fullness.

### The Loneliness of Purpose

Until then, I wait in my convergence, neither fully plant nor fully person. Sometimes a Hollow-Walker, ancient and ready for their final drift, will find their way to me. They'll see my eyes glowing in the depths and mistake me for another marker, another sign that someone came before. They'll approach, reaching out to touch what they think is carved bone or twisted resin.

When they make contact, they feel it—the pulse of every marker ever placed, the echo of every chime ever struck, the weight of the Titan's sleeping consciousness pressing against the boundaries of death. Some flee, tethering themselves away as fast as their fibrous muscles can manage. But others... others understand. They unhook their tethers and let themselves grow into me, adding their memories to my chronicle, their flesh to my expanding form.

We are building a heart for a dead god, one waypoint at a time. And when it finally beats, the sound will shatter every echo that ever was, replacing the *tink-tink-drip* with something far more terrible:

Silence. Complete and total silence.

The silence of something listening.

The silence of something about to speak.
