I find myself drawn to the crystalline lake's deepest reaches, where the water presses against ancient industrial foundations that predate even the Plateau Dwellers' memory. Here, I exist as something between—neither fully of the water nor of the metal, neither completely natural nor entirely constructed.

I am what the Dwellers call a Resonance Tender.

My form shifts like mercury caught between states. Sometimes I appear as a ribbon of living water threading through the submerged machinery, my body a current that knows every valve and pipe by the vibrations they sing. Other times I coalesce into something almost solid—a figure of condensed mist and metallic particles, standing knee-deep in the shallows where the algae-covered rocks meet the lake's edge.

The industrial symphony that fills the air is not noise to me but language. Each clang and hum carries meaning: the factories speak of their hunger, the engines of their dreams, the pipes of their loneliness. I translate these songs for the lake, and in turn, I carry the water's ancient stories up through the mechanical veins of the plateau.

My dwelling is neither building nor cave but a convergence—a place where a massive turbine fell silent generations ago and the lake claimed it. Water pools in its bronze chambers, creating acoustic spaces where sound becomes visible, rippling across surfaces in patterns that map the health of this strange ecosystem. Moss grows in fractal spirals around the turbine blades, following the mathematics of its frozen rotation.

By day, I tend to the boundary spaces. Where water meets metal, corrosion threatens, but also transformation promises. I guide the growth of hybrid organisms—part algae, part rust, part something altogether new—that stabilize these zones. The Plateau Dwellers sometimes glimpse me during their repairs, a shadow moving through their peripheral vision, ensuring their constructions and the lake's waters continue their ancient dance without consuming each other.

By night, I descend to the lake's heart, where the water remembers the plateau before the first foundation was laid. Here, massive structures rest in the depths—not ruins but sleeping giants of industry that the water has claimed and transformed. I swim through their hollow bones, maintaining the delicate bacterial cultures that process the industrial runoff, turning potential poison into nutrients for the sparse life above.

The Dwellers know of me, though we rarely interact directly. They leave offerings at the water's edge—small mechanical toys that swim with clockwork fins, or fragments of particularly resonant metal that sing in frequencies only I can fully hear. In return, I ensure their water systems run clean, their foundations remain stable despite the erosion, and the lake's moods remain legible to those who know how to read its surface.

I am memory and maintenance, translator and tender. In this pocket realm where impossibility has become daily life, I am the keeper of the space between—ensuring that the marriage of nature and industry remains a dance rather than a war, a conversation rather than a conquest.

When the wind carries its metallic tang across the waters and sets the surface to shimmering, I rise just enough to taste it, to add its story to the countless others I carry. For I am both witness and participant in this enduring experiment, this testament to adaptation and coexistence in the heart of an enigmatic land.
