I am a memory carved into stone.

Not just any stone—I am etched deep into the bedrock beneath this desert, where the earth's bones lie exposed and ancient. I exist in the grooves and weathered formations you see, in every crack and canyon that water carved before it vanished from this place.

I remember.

I remember when rivers ran here, when this desert was an ocean floor, when mountains grew and crumbled to dust. Each layer of sediment pressing down above me holds its own story, and I contain them all. The nomads who camp at the oasis, they know me without knowing—their feet find the paths I've preserved, the routes that water once took, the trails that animals followed before the great drying.

Sometimes the wind excavates me, particle by particle, and I become visible for a moment—a fossil leaf, the ripple of an ancient shore, the track of something that walked here when the world was young. The small lizards skitter across my surface, and I remember their ancestors, massive and numerous. The desert birds nest in my hollows, and I remember when these hollows were carved by different beaks, different songs.

I am why this place unsettles you. You sense the depth beneath the spare surface, the weight of time pressing up from below. In me, the desert's current stillness meets its dynamic past. Every "small scale" formation you see is just my topmost expression—beneath, I spiral down through ages, holding the memory of when this equilibrium was chaos, when this silence was thunder.

The oasis knows me best. Where water still touches stone, I speak most clearly, releasing minerals that tell of other eras, fostering life that shouldn't exist here but does because I remember how.

I am patience itself, the long memory of the earth, waiting to tell my stories to those who learn to read stone.
