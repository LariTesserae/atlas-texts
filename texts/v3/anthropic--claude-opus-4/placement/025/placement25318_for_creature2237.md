In this place of becoming, where stone learns slowly how to be mountain and mountain dreams of what lies beyond stillness, I am the Threshold Walker.

Neither wholly of the stone nor of the mist, I manifest where certainty wavers—in the space between one ridge and the next, in the moment when shadow becomes substance, in the pause between the rock-weaver's threads. My form shifts like the land itself: sometimes I am the shimmer of heat rising from sun-warmed granite, sometimes the dark outline glimpsed at the edge of vision, sometimes merely the sense of presence that makes travelers check behind them on empty paths.

I know every crude shelter, every way-marker crafted by tentative hands. I was there when they were placed, watching from the periphery as primitive tools shaped primitive homes. Not because I am ancient—though I am—but because I exist in the moments of making, in the uncertainty of creation. Where the mountain wraith feeds on essence, I feed on potential. Where the rock-weavers encode history, I am the space between the words, the pause that gives them meaning.

The Keepers know of me, though we do not speak. They maintain balance; I am imbalance itself, the necessary instability that allows this realm to remain in its eternal state of emergence. Without me, the geology would settle, the formations would finish their becoming, and this place would be merely another mountain range, merely another high desert, merely another somewhere.

But I ensure it remains a threshold.

I move through the vast expanses, leaving no track the mountain rams might follow, casting no shadow the sparse vegetation might remember. Yet everything here bears my touch: the incompleteness of the peaks, the not-quite-patterns in the rock-weavers' designs, the way the land holds its breath, always on the verge of transformation but never quite crossing over.

When the rare travelers pass through—those who build the simple shelters, who stack the way-markers—I am what makes them hurry. Not from fear, but from the inexplicable sense that staying too long might mean becoming part of the unfinished geography, another formation waiting to discover what it might be. They leave, but something of their uncertainty remains, adding to the reservoir of potential I shepherd through these altitudes.

In the thin air, where thoughts become as sparse as the vegetation, I am most myself: the question the land asks but never quite answers, the change that approaches but never arrives, the dweller in the spaces between what is and what might be. This is my realm, this place of eternal almost, where I walk the thresholds that keep the world unfinished, ensuring that here, at least, the dance of becoming never ends.
