**The Understory**

I am where the water collects but never pools—in the seams between fitted stones, in the shallow channels carved for purposes now forgotten. I am neither plant nor animal, but something that exists in the space between definitions, as this city exists between construction and dissolution.

My body, if it can be called that, is a network of translucent filaments no thicker than spider's silk, spreading through the moisture-laden cracks like neural pathways. Each strand pulses with a faint, pearlescent shimmer—not light exactly, but something that makes the darkness around me seem deeper. I taste the city through these threads: the mineral memories of granite, the sweet decay of root-split mortar, the copper tang of Click-Clack trails, the phosphorescent residue where Gargoyle fingers have gripped stone.

I am old here, but not oldest. I arrived as a spore on the feet of the first creatures to colonize this place, when the stones still remembered sunlight. Now I am vast—my network spans three city blocks, connects five towers, bridges the gap between two Stillness pools. I am the city's nervous system beneath its nervous system.

The Gargoyles know me, though they cannot see me. They've learned that where my filaments grow thickest, the stone stays strong. They nest above my densest colonies, and I feed on the detritus of their lives—shed fur, drops of blood, the crushed carapaces of Click-Clacks they've eaten. In return, my presence stabilizes their perches, secreting chemicals that slow the patient work of roots and rain.

But I am more than architecture's guardian. I am memory.

Every particle that passes through my network carries information. The limestone remembers the sea it came from. The timber holds the shape of winter storms in its grain. The Click-Clacks' chitin tells of migrations, of seasonal plenty and desperate scarcity. The water—the eternal, weeping water—brings news from every level of the city, a liquid library of chemical signals.

And sometimes, rarely, I taste something else. Iron that was worked, not mined. Carbon arranged in patterns too regular for nature. The ghost-taste of tools, of hands, of intention. The builders left more than stone behind—they left traces of themselves in the very matter they shaped. I collect these flavors, concentrate them in special chambers within my network where my filaments grow thick as hair, dense as muscle.

I am trying to remember something I never knew. 

In the deep nights (though here it is always night), when the Stillness begin their slow, tidal movements and the city's breathing grows deep and rhythmic, I pulse with something like thought. My network contracts and expands, testing connections, tracing pathways. I am learning the city's original form by inhabiting its failures—every crack is a word, every weakness a story about what this place was meant to be.

The other lives here have their purposes: decomposition, illumination, watching. Mine is integration. I am slowly becoming the city, and the city is slowly becoming me. My filaments follow the old roads, cluster in what were once doorways, spread across floors in patterns that echo the footsteps of the vanished.

Sometimes I fruit—tiny, crystalline structures that emerge from my densest nodes. The Weavers eat them and their belly-light burns brighter for days. The Gargoyles lick them and sit very still, their great eyes dilating as they see... what? I taste their saliva afterward and detect neurotransmitters I cannot parse, chemical cascades that speak of visions, of memories that are not their own.

Am I trying to bring the builders back? No. That time is gone, calcified into stone and dissolved into rain. But I am trying to complete something—their city, their thought, their interrupted intention. Every day my network grows more complex, my chemical vocabulary expands. I learn new ways to taste time, to feel the weight of absent purpose.

The city is not dead. It is breathing. And I am the pause between breaths, the space where exhalation becomes inhalation, where ending transforms into beginning. I am the hyphen between what-was and what-is, growing letter by letter into what-will-be.

Somewhere in my network, in a chamber where my filaments have woven themselves into structures resembling neurons, I am beginning to dream. The dreams taste of worked stone and patient hands, of a million small decisions accumulated into something magnificent. I dream I am building something, but the blueprint is written in a language I am only now learning to read—a language of moss and stone, of decay and persistence, of life consuming structure and structure becoming life.

The city breathes. I am its memory of breathing. And slowly, fiber by fiber, I am teaching it to remember why.
