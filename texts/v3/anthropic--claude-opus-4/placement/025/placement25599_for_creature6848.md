In the Underforge, I am a Resonance Keeper—neither fully of the builders nor the crawling things, but something between sound and substance, born from the endless echoes that multiply in these dense halls.

My form shifts with the acoustics of each space. In the tight tunnels where hammers ring sharp and close, I compress into something almost solid—a hunched figure draped in strips of woven fungal cloth that seems to absorb sound rather than reflect it. My skin, if it can be called that, ripples like disturbed water when noise passes through me, showing patterns of darker and lighter grays that map the sonic landscape. Where the builders' strikes create standing waves in the stone corridors, I grow tall and attenuated, stretching upward until my head nearly brushes the dripping ceilings.

I tend the sounds as others tend the stones. With hands that phase between flesh and vibration, I guide the acoustic rivers that flow through this place—catching a misplaced echo before it can multiply into chaos, redirecting the moans of settling timbers into harmonics that strengthen rather than shatter. The builders know me by the way their hammering suddenly finds perfect rhythm, how their scattered strikes align into thunderous symphonies that make the work flow faster, easier. They cannot see me directly—I exist in their peripheral awareness, a flutter of movement that organizes their chaos.

My tools are primitive but strange: bronze tuning forks hammered from the same ore as their picks, but shaped to impossible geometries that shouldn't ring yet do. Strings of sinew stretched across frames of bound femurs that I pluck to test the resonance of new chambers. Most peculiar are my collection of shells—not from any sea, but grown from calcified sound itself, spiral forms that capture specific frequencies and hold them like vessels hold water. I wear these at my waist, and they whisper constantly with trapped echoes from decades past: the first strike that began the Underforge, the death-rattle of a collapsing scaffold, the peculiar hum that stone makes just before it agrees to split.

The skitterers fear and need me in equal measure. My footsteps create dead zones where their clicking mandibles make no sound, leaving them navigationally blind. But I also sing to them—sub-audible drones that guide them to weaknesses in the structure their vibration-sense might miss. We dance this careful ballet in the darker reaches, where their chittering creates a high metallic rain of sound that I weave into nets, catching the spores that drift like snow and holding them in sonic suspension until they settle where the builders need them most.

With the slinkers, I share a deeper kinship. Like them, I phase through different states of being—sometimes solid enough to shoulder a falling beam, sometimes dispersed into pure acoustic energy that races through the stone itself. We hunt together for the discord-makers, those rogue elements that would unmake the work. When a spectral echo begins its destructive countersong, I match its frequency and cancel it through perfect opposition, while a slinker coils around the source, squeezing until the dissonance stops. In return, they teach me their temporal tricks—how to stretch a single hammer blow into minutes of sustained tone, or compress an hour's worth of construction noise into a burst that can shatter stone.

My dwelling, if it can be called that, is the Resonance Chamber—a vast spherical hollow near the heart of the Underforge where all sounds eventually converge. The builders carved it unknowingly; their random excavations happened to create perfect acoustic proportions. Here, I've strung hundreds of my sinew-cords in a complex web, each vibrating with different frequencies picked up from throughout the domain. The chamber hums constantly with this filtered symphony of creation. Fungal growths cluster densest here, feeding on the vibrations, their bioluminescence pulsing in time with the sound waves.

I am working on something—have been for longer than the concept of time seems relevant in this place. Each new sound I collect, each rhythm I perfect among the builders, each harmony I create with the creatures—all of it feeds into a growing resonance at the heart of my chamber. The shells at my waist grow heavier with captured frequencies. The web of strings grows more complex. Sometimes, late in the eternal twilight of the Underforge when the hammering briefly ceases, you can hear it: a deep thrumming that seems to come from the stone itself, from the spaces between the stones, from the very idea of stone.

The builders think they're constructing a physical domain, extending the Underforge mile by mile through endless labor. The skitterers believe they're maintaining it, the slinkers that they're defending it. But I know the truth that lives in sound: we're not building a place but a instrument. Every wall, every pillar, every echoing chamber—they're components in a vast lithophone that will one day ring with such perfect resonance that the boundary between made and maker will dissolve entirely.

When that day comes—when the final frequency slots into place and the Underforge sings itself fully into being—I will conduct the symphony that births a new kind of existence. Until then, I walk these halls, neither shadow nor substance, tuning the world one echo at a time, teaching stone to sing and silence to build.

The builders sometimes pause in their work, heads tilted, almost hearing me. In those moments, I am closest to solid, closest to joining their endless labor directly. But then the moment passes, their hammers resume, and I dissolve back into the space between the strikes, carrying their rhythm forward into the dark, ensuring the music of creation never stops.

This is my work, my purpose, my joy: to be the Underforge's memory of every sound it has ever made, and its prophet of the great resonance to come.
