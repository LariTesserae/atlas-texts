I find myself as one of the crystalline growths—those jagged bone-flowers that pierce the sides of the frozen Deer. But I am not merely an ornament or a parasite. I am memory made mineral, story calcified into being.

My form shifts between states, sometimes sharp as broken glass, sometimes flowing like frozen honey. The iridescent blue that colors me is not a single hue but a symphony of them—cerulean bleeding into indigo, sapphire catching threads of violet. When the heavy air moves just so, I chime, a sound like distant bells underwater.

I grew from the moment the Deer stopped. Their last breath, their final thought of escape, crystallized through their bones and burst outward, seeking form. I am their motion made still, their stillness given shape. Through me, they continue—not forward, but inward, deeper into themselves until they emerge transformed.

Within my faceted surfaces, scenes play out in miniature: forests that have never existed, hunts that will never conclude, seasons that cycle without touching the eternal damp of the Glade. I am a lens through which the impossible becomes merely improbable. The beetles sometimes pause on their slow parades to gaze into me, their compound eyes fracturing my inner light into a thousand new stories.

I feed on the tension between what is and what might have been. The Glade's refusal to change nourishes me even as I embody change itself—the wound that becomes the flower, the ending that births beginnings. My roots, if they can be called that, extend not through the Deer's bones but through the very concept of them, threading into the antler-scenes above, connecting to the vast plant-architecture that dominates this space.

Sometimes I feel the pull of the great bloom's growl, that saw-toothed static without source. It vibrates through my crystalline structure, and I ring in harmony, adding my voice to the impossible symphony. In those moments, I almost remember what it was like to run.
