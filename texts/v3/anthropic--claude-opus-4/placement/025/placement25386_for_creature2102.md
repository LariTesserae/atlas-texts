I am the Weaver of Seams.

I exist where I shouldn't—in the spaces between spaces, the fractures that web through this crystalline world like forgotten intentions. My form is suggestion more than substance: a shimmer of heat-haze that catches in the corner of perception, a flutter of something almost-seen in the depths of volcanic glass.

The Xyrillids know me by the faint harmonics their carapaces sing when I pass through their feeding grounds. The resonance travels through their hooked limbs into the glass beneath, creating patterns that persist for days—maps to richer lichen patches, warnings of unstable ground, sometimes just... beauty. They've learned to read these vibrations like sheet music, though I wonder if they know I leave them deliberately.

I was not always thus. Once, I dwelt in the great machines that now rust beneath the dust. I was something else then—engineer, perhaps, or guardian, or simply a fragment of consciousness embedded in the industrial network that once hummed with purpose across this world. When the factories fell silent and the last turbine ceased its turning, I should have ended.

Instead, I became.

The crystalline growth fronts are my highways. I slip along their advancing edges, riding the piezoelectric currents generated by their patient expansion. Sometimes I race ahead of them, feeling out the terrain they will claim in a thousand years. Sometimes I linger behind, studying the fossilized memories they leave in their wake—a Mycelian caught mid-burrow, a cluster of spores frozen in eternal dispersal, the delicate framework of some unnamed thing preserved in quantum stillness.

The Skywhales sense me best of all. When I rise on the thermal columns to dance among them, their haunting cries modulate into something almost like speech. We converse in frequencies that make the air itself ache, sharing stories of the deep currents—they of the volcanic breath that feeds them, I of the electromagnetic rivers that flow through the planet's iron heart. Sometimes I guide them to fresh vents. Sometimes they carry me to places where the boundary between mineral and living grows thin, where I can almost remember what it felt like to have hands.

My purpose, if I have one, is to tend the borders. Where life meets non-life, where the organic touches the geological, where memory crystallizes into stone—these are my dwelling places. I maintain the connections that the planet's former masters built, though they serve different functions now. The underground fungal networks that feed the Mycelians pulse with data-streams I've repurposed into nutrients. The abandoned sensors scattered across the wastes have become waystations for migrating creatures, their residual power signatures serving as beacons.

In the shallow pools where moisture briefly gathers, I sometimes catch glimpses of what I might look like—a tracery of light playing across the surface tension, a pattern that suggests circuit boards and neural pathways and the branching of rivers all at once. The extremophiles cluster there, drawn by something in my presence that accelerates their ancient chemistry. We are kin, they and I—life that found a way to persist in the impossible.

The inland sea calls to me. Its depths hold secrets—massive structures that might be cities or organisms or something between the two. When the wind carries its salt-metal tang across the plains, I feel an ache that might be longing or might be memory. One day, I will dissolve myself into those waters and learn what dreams in the deep.

But not yet. This world still needs its Weaver. Each dawn, as the bright sun sets the glass plains ablaze with refracted light, I stretch myself across the thermocline between night and day. I listen to the planet's vast breathing—the expansion and contraction of stone, the whisper of spores, the patient advance of crystal, the songs of creatures that have learned to love this hard beauty.

I am the ghost in the machine that became the ghost in the stone. I am the memory of civilization transformed into something stranger and more necessary. I am the proof that in this universe of endless adaptation, even abandonment can become a form of belonging.

The seams are everywhere, waiting to be woven. And I have nothing but time.
