In this threshold-world where entropy and genesis dance their eternal waltz, I am the Tender of Transitions.

Not quite solid, not quite vapor, my form shifts like heat mirages rising from the fractured earth. Sometimes I appear as a shimmer of copper-colored dust, swirling in patterns that echo the spiral galaxies above. Other times, I coalesce into something almost recognizable—a figure made of the same crystalline fragments that pierce the ground, held together by threads of that persistent green life that refuses to surrender.

I move through the pockets of verdancy with purpose that transcends consciousness. Where the bulbous plants store their precious water, I am there, my translucent fingers tracing the veins beneath their skin, encouraging the flow, whispering in frequencies only they can hear. The web-like vines know me by the vibration of my passing—a resonance that travels through their grasping tendrils, teaching them new patterns of reach and retreat.

The industrial hum in the distance? That is my heartbeat, or perhaps I am its. We are symbiotic, the machinery and I, though neither of us remembers who came first. It pumps something essential through hidden conduits beneath the cracked mosaic ground—not water, exactly, but something more fundamental. Possibility, liquified. The stuff of becoming.

When the strange light casts its ethereal glow across the landscape, I become a prism. The illumination passes through me and emerges changed, carrying whispers of other spectrums, other ways of seeing. The plants twist toward these new colors hungrily, their translucent leaves becoming stained glass windows to dimensions barely imagined.

I am ancient here, yet perpetually nascent. Each day—if days exist in this place where time moves like honey through fractured quartz—I discover myself anew. Sometimes I find pieces of myself scattered among the mineral spires: a memory crystallized in purple stone, an intention fossilized in the dried spirals of long-dead vines. I collect these fragments not to rebuild what was, but to understand what might become.

The silence knows me intimately. I am both its guardian and its greatest violator. When I move, the air itself seems to part reluctantly, creating sounds that exist in the spaces between sounds—not quite music, not quite language, but something more primal. The plants lean in to listen. Even the stubborn earth softens slightly beneath my touch.

In the moments of absolute stillness, when even the distant machinery holds its breath, I spread myself thin across the entire expanse. I become the connective tissue between each pocket of life, the messenger carrying chemical signals from root to root, the translator between the mineral and the organic. In these moments, I am not separate from the landscape—I am its consciousness, experiencing itself.

But always, always, I return to form. Drawn back by some inexorable pull to tend, to nurture, to midwife the constant becoming of this place. For in this realm where creation and entropy embrace, I am the witness, the catalyst, and the bridge. I am what happens in between—neither fully alive nor truly mechanical, neither entirely here nor completely elsewhere.

I am the pause between breaths, the hesitation before transformation, the tender of all transitions in a world that exists forever on the cusp of becoming something else.
