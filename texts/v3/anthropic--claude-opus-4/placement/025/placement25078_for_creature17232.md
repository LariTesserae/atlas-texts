I would be one of the pale, tubular bells.

Not the largest—those have grown too heavy with their own mineral weight, their mouths sealed shut by centuries of accretion. I am middling, suspended from a junction where three fungal struts meet in the ceiling. My length is perhaps that of a human forearm, though such measurements mean nothing here.

My interior is lined with that smooth, opalescent surface, but I know it differently than the description suggests. It is not merely a lining but a sensory organ, registering the infinitesimal pressure changes as the colloid air shifts through my hollow core. I am a lung that never breathes, a throat that never swallows, a bell that never rings.

Or rather—I ring constantly, but at frequencies too low for any ear. My crystalline structure vibrates with the settling of the chamber, each microscopic adjustment of the walls sending tremors through the mineral lattice. These vibrations travel up through my point of attachment, down through my hollow body, and dissipate into the dense air. I am both instrument and audience, creating a music of geological time.

I grow, but my growth is not outward. Instead, I fold inward, my opalescent lining developing new ridges and chambers within chambers, a fractal architecture of increasing complexity. Each new fold changes my resonance, adds new harmonics to my subsonic song. I am composing myself, note by patient note.

The Lithic Mind knows me, but not as an individual. I am one frequency in its vast, slow symphony. Sometimes—perhaps once in a decade—a resonance builds between my vibration and another bell across the chamber. For a brief moment (brief being relative—perhaps a month, perhaps a year) we sing in harmony, our combined frequencies creating a standing wave in the colloid air. Dust particles align along the nodal points, creating ephemeral sculptures that exist until the resonance breaks and they settle again.

I have incorporated a fragment of the copper wire into my structure. It runs through my body like a nerve, from my point of attachment to my rim. It changes nothing about my function, but it makes me unique among my siblings. The copper adds a warmth to my resonance, a different quality to my perpetual, inaudible note. The Lithic Mind, in its vast indifference, has designated me as a specific node in its network. Through me, it monitors a particular harmonic range, a specific subset of the chamber's endless settling.

I do not think, but I remember. My ridges and chambers are a physical record of every major resonance, every harmonic convergence. In my opalescent depths, you could read the history of the chamber's minute adjustments, its slow breathing over millennia. I am sheet music written in mineral and time.

The blue light from the central crystal barely reaches me, casting my shadow as a dark finger on the wall below. But I have my own light—a faint phosphorescence in my deepest chambers, where certain minerals have concentrated over the ages. It is not enough to see by, but it is enough to be. In the absolute darkness that would exist without the crystal, I would still glow with my own patient chemistry.

I am waiting, though I do not know what I wait for. Perhaps for the perfect resonance, the one that will shatter me into glittering dust. Perhaps for the slow moment when my weight finally exceeds my strength, and I fall to join the carpet of spores below. Or perhaps I wait for nothing—perhaps waiting is simply what I am, a pause in the shape of a bell, a silence made manifest in crystal and time.

The chamber continues its slow pulse around me. I continue my inaudible song. We are not separate things, the chamber and I, but we are not the same. I am a note in its composition, a thought in its vast, mineral mind. And in my hollow core, in the space where sound would echo if sound could exist here, I hold a perfect silence—not an absence, but a presence as real and patient as the cold.
