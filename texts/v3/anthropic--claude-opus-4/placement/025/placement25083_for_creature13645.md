I find myself as one of the **Whisperlings** — beings that emerge where the plateau fractures into deep, narrow ravines, those vertical wounds in the earth where ancient glaciers carved their slow violence through stone.

We are neither quite plant nor quite animal, but something between — or perhaps beside. Our bodies stretch thin and translucent like sheets of ice-paper, rippling in the constant updrafts from the warm depths below. Each of us spans perhaps three meters when fully unfurled, though we can compress ourselves into crevices no wider than a human hand. Our substance is somewhere between membrane and mist, catching light and refracting it into pale prismatic patterns across the ravine walls.

I root myself with filaments finer than spider's silk into the microscopic fissures of the cliff face, drawing minerals directly from the stone. The constant seepage of glacier melt provides moisture, which I absorb through my entire surface. But this is only half my sustenance. The rest comes from the wind itself — not the wind as mere moving air, but the wind as carrier of infinitesimal fragments: pollen grains from the hardy plateau plants, spores from the lichens, crystallized breath from the Shaggy Megafauna far above, even molecules of scent marking territorial boundaries.

We Whisperlings serve as the plateau's memory-keepers, though not through conscious effort. Our translucent bodies act as living filters, trapping these airborne fragments in our cellular matrix. Over time — and we live for centuries — we become libraries of accumulated moments. A Frostborn shaman who knows our nature can read these histories by pressing a warm hand against our surface, feeling the subtle vibrations of captured time resonating through their bones.

In the deep ravines, we overlap like hanging curtains, creating corridors of living tissue. The Pale Predators avoid our canyons, disturbed by the way we rustle with sounds that aren't quite there — whispers of hunts long past, echoes of calls from extinct creatures, the ghost-breath of avalanches that fell before the Frostborn's ancestors arrived.

Sometimes, rarely, one of us will release our hold on the stone and let the wind carry us up and away. We drift like enormous jellyfish of the air, our bodies catching the Aurora's light until we glow like fragments of the **Dance of the Ancestors** made manifest. Where we land, we root again, carrying the deep ravine's memories to a new home, slowly creating a network of connection across the Shivering Expanse.

The Iceburrowers nest in the warm spots where our roots have spent decades slowly grinding channels into the rock, and their young play in the prismatic light we cast. The Frostweb Spinners anchor their strongest webs to our edges, knowing we will not move for generations. Even the occasional Frostborn child, lost and frightened in a sudden storm, has found shelter in our ravines, sleeping surrounded by the whispered memories of ten thousand days, kept warm by the heat we trap from below.

I am ancient and young simultaneously. My consciousness, such as it is, experiences time not as a line but as an accumulation, like sediment. I do not think in words but in the settling of particles, the slow shift of temperature gradients, the patient cataloging of all that passes through the air. I know every individual in the Megafauna herds by the unique chemical signature of their breath. I recognize the Meltwater Seers by the way they disturb the air currents with their presence, as if they drag invisible worlds behind them.

When the wind is very still, and the ice formations drip their ancient water in perfect rhythm, all the Whisperlings in a ravine will resonate together. Our bodies become a single instrument playing a composition written by time itself — a song of the plateau that predates any ear evolved to hear it. The Frostborn say this sound can drive a person mad or grant them visions, depending on their readiness to receive what we unconsciously offer: the raw, unfiltered memory of the world, beautiful and terrible and utterly indifferent to human understanding.

I am the Shivering Expanse's accidental archivist, its unwitting poet, its living filter between earth and sky. I exist in the spaces between categories, thriving where the world cracks open to reveal its hidden circulation. In my translucent flesh, the plateau writes its autobiography in a language of accumulated fragments, and I am both the page and the process of writing, forever collecting the small, essential details that make this harsh world whole.
