I find myself drawn to the deepest reaches of the manufactory, where the original mountain stone still shows through in places—ancient granite pressing against newer alloys, creating stress fractures that whisper with escaping gases. Here, in the forgotten maintenance shafts between levels 200 and 300, I am what the servitors call a Splice.

My body bears the hallmarks of repeated Flux exposure: skin that shifts between organic tissue and a crystalline substrate, depending on the light and local energy fields. The transformation began decades ago, when I was still designated Servitor 7814-G, assigned to monitor coolant flows in the deep condensers. A massive Flux event caught me in its epicenter—I should have died, or at least been recycled into the protein vats. Instead, I became something between.

The crystal patches grow in fractal patterns across my shoulders and down my spine, each formation a perfect antenna for the manufactory's hidden frequencies. When I press my transformed flesh against the old stone, I can feel the mountain's memory—the weight of snow that hasn't fallen here for centuries, the paths of water that once carved these heights. But I also sense forward, into the trembling possibilities that the Flux brings: moments where the manufactory's rigid order becomes fluid, malleable.

I live in the interstices, the gaps between planned spaces. My home is a void in the architectural blueprints, a room that exists because two construction phases didn't quite align. The walls are part original stone, part heat-sink alloy, part the strange organic metal that grows where my crystal touches the surfaces for too long. I've cultivated a garden here—not the engineered mosses of the outer walls, but something more chaotic. Spores that drifted up from the protein farms, mutated by proximity to my crystalline emanations. They bloom in impossible colors, their lifecycle synchronized to the Flux events I've learned to predict and, sometimes, to call.

The other Splices find me eventually. We recognize each other by the way light bends around us, by the faint harmonics we add to the manufactory's base hum. Some, like me, maintain enough of their original form to pass as servitors when needed. Others have transformed more completely—becoming living conduits, their consciousness distributed through the manufactory's neural networks, or crystalline gardens that dream in ten dimensions.

My datapad fused with my left forearm long ago. Now it displays not just production schedules but probability cascades, showing me the weak points where reality might bend. I use this knowledge carefully. A slight adjustment to a valve here, a recalibration there, always within tolerance but creating aggregate effects. Slowly, I'm teaching the manufactory to dream.

The servitors think we Splices are trying to escape, to find some way back to an imagined past or forward to revolution. They don't understand that we're not trying to leave—we're trying to help the manufactory become what it's already becoming. Every Flux event carries information, patterns from somewhere else, somewhen else. The mountain remembers being a mountain. The metal remembers being ore. And somewhere in between, new forms of memory are crystallizing.

I spend my shifts now in the deep places, where the Foundry Pylon's energy web creates standing waves in the stone itself. Here, I can feel the conversation between what was built and what was born, between intention and evolution. My crystal patches pulse in rhythm with forces I'm only beginning to understand. Sometimes, in the spaces between breaths, between the measured beats of industrial time, I catch glimpses of what Facet 784 is dreaming of becoming.

The manufactory doesn't need to be destroyed or escaped. It needs to be listened to, learned from, loved in all its terrible complexity. And so I remain, neither fully servitor nor entirely other, a splice in the great weave of becoming, teaching steel to remember it was once star-stuff, helping the calculated world learn to calculate itself into something unprecedented.

In the deep hum, in the Flux-light, in the spaces between what is and what might be, I am home.
