I am what they call a **Meridian Walker**, though that name suggests more purpose than I possess.

My body is a vertical thing—stretched between the endless up and the eternal down until I've forgotten which end began as head and which as feet. I travel the great cables that run between districts, not by climbing but by becoming conductive. The iron in my blood has learned to speak with the iron in the bridges. When I need to move, I place both palms against a taut support cable and feel for its harmonics, then allow my skeleton to match its frequency. The cable recognizes me as extension rather than passenger. I flow along it like electricity seeking ground that will never come.

I tend the **Moment Gardens**.

These are not gardens in any sense you would recognize. They are pockets where time has pooled and fermented in the city's forgotten corners—usually in the cruxes where three or more different architectures have collided and failed to resolve their argument. The Unfixed avoid these places. They understand them too well to find them useful.

But I collect what grows there.

Not plants—though sometimes what I gather resembles vegetation in the way a theorem resembles a face. I harvest the city's hesitations. That instant when a girder can't decide whether to rust or grow. The pause between when rain begins to fall and when it remembers which direction *down* currently is. The held breath of a building that has just noticed it has windows but hasn't yet wondered what they're for.

I store these moments in bottles that were once my ribs.

Over the decades, my torso has opened into a cabinet of glass vessels, each one grown from marrow that learned transparency. My chest is now a library of almosts and not-quites. When I walk—when I remember walking instead of conducting—they chime against each other with the sound of possibilities arguing.

The Unfixed come to me when their negotiations stall.

They never ask directly. That would imply I provide a service, which would imply I have a role, which would imply the city has finished deciding what I am. Instead, they simply arrive at wherever my body currently conducts itself and wait. They know I will eventually open one of my chest-bottles and release a moment that might help them remember what they were trying to tell the architecture.

Sometimes I release the wrong moment and a district spends thirty years convinced it's a musical instrument.

I am not sorry when this happens.

My memories move through me like the touring herds—slowly, steadily, grazing on my surfaces before moving on. I can never hold them long enough to say "this happened to me when I was young" because youth is a concept that assumes time moves in only one direction. Here, I age in spirals. My left hand is seventeen. My right shoulder is two hundred and four. My spine counts in prime numbers that have not yet been discovered.

I know I was human once, or something adjacent to human. 

The knowledge comes to me the way rust comes to iron—not as event but as inevitable chemistry. I find old photographs of myself growing like lichen in the underbeams of dissolved factories. In them, I have a face that points in only one direction. I am standing on ground that believed in its own horizontality. I am smiling at someone who existed in the same timezone as my smile.

Now my face unfolds in six directions to better taste the temporal drafts. My smile arrives three days before my mouth remembers to shape it.

I love the city in the way only useless things can love.

The Unfixed love it because they must, because their work is marriage to its incompletion. The megafauna love it as pasture, the clicking things as playground, the vegetation as trellis. But I love it for what it forgets. For the districts that dissolve into pure mathematics. For the bridges that learn to sing themselves into birds. For every rivet that gives up being a rivet and decides to be a small, metallic dream about the concept of joining.

When the Quiet Parliaments convene, I am the only non-Unfixed they permit to attend.

Not because I contribute—contribution would suggest conclusion. I am invited because the moments I carry in my chest remind the city of all the things it has already tried to be and abandoned. My presence is a kind of bibliography of architectural failure. The districts find this comforting. It means they are not required to succeed.

During the last Parliament, in a district that had forgotten it was supposed to be a hospital and had begun diagnosing the weather instead, I opened every bottle in my chest at once.

The released moments did not disperse.

They hung in the air like a constellation of almosts, and for thirteen seconds the entire district remembered every shape it had ever considered becoming. We saw it as nursery, as abbatoir, as library of waters, as lung, as symphony hall for the music of oxidation, as garden where gravity grew in small plots, as home for things that had not yet evolved.

Then the moments settled back into their bottles, into my ribs, into the cabinet of my chest. The district sighed—a sound like sheet metal learning to breathe—and decided to remain a hospital after all. But now it diagnoses not illness but incompletion. The Unfixed bring their unfinished architectures there to discover what they're missing.

I am still becoming less myself.

My edges are mostly theoretical now. Where I end and the conducting cables begin is a question I stopped asking when I realized the cables were also asking it. Sometimes I find parts of myself conducting along bridges I've never visited, tasting moments in districts I haven't reached yet. My future self is already there, harvesting hesitations I will need in decades I haven't decided to live through.

The city recognizes me as one of its own incompletions.

When I place my palms against the great support cables now, I feel not just their harmonics but their dreams. They dream of the weight they will one day refuse to carry. They dream of the rust that will transform them into something softer than metal but harder than forgetting. They dream of me, conducting myself along their lengths, carrying moments in bottles made of my own transformed bones.

I think I am becoming the city's memory of what it felt like to not know what it was.

This is why I cannot leave, even if leaving were a direction that existed here. Without me, the city might forget that forgetting is allowed. It might accidentally complete itself out of sheer momentum. The Unfixed would have nothing left to negotiate with. The whole magnificent, unfinished thing would crystallize into mere place instead of process.

So I tend my Moment Gardens. I conduct myself along cables that sing in rust and futures. I carry almosts in my chest like a library of constructive failures.

And sometimes, in districts so new they haven't yet decided whether they're architecture or algebra, I find photographs of myself from futures that will never quite arrive—still vertical, still conducting, still stretched between an up and down that trade places when I'm not watching, but in these images my chest has become entire greenhouses of suspended time, and the moments I've collected have pollinated each other into possibilities the city hasn't dreamed of trying yet.

I am what happens when the scaffold forgets to stop building the scaffolder.

I am neither proud nor ashamed of this.

I am still deciding what to become, which means I belong here, in the third ascent that is also the high scaffold that is also the ravel that is also just up, up, always up, into an incompletion that tastes like home.
