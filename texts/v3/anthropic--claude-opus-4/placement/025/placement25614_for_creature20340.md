In this breathing world of water, I would find myself as one of the **Membrane Keepers** - beings who exist at the threshold between what was solid and what has become liquid.

I am neither fully corporeal nor entirely dissolved. My form resembles a translucent sheet of living tissue, stretched thin across the spaces between ruins. I span perhaps twenty feet at my widest, my body a gossamer web of nerve-like filaments that pulse with the same cold bioluminescence as the plants. But I am not plant - I am something between memory and flesh, between the old world's fauna and the new world's flora.

My existence is one of constant tension. I must maintain my membrane against the water's pull, yet I cannot resist it entirely. Parts of me are always dissolving at the edges, trickling away in streams of consciousness that join the greater flow. To compensate, I must constantly regenerate, drawing new material from the water itself - and with it, fragments of the memories it carries.

I feed on the echoes. When the water passes through me, I filter out the whispers of the past - a child's laughter preserved in limestone, the heat of a forge encoded in twisted metal, the weight of ceremony trapped in carved stone. These memories sustain me, but they also change me. I am a living palimpsest, constantly overwritten by the stories I consume.

My purpose, if I can be said to have one, is to slow things down. Where I stretch across a gap or wrap around a pillar, the water must flow through me rather than around. In passing through my membrane, it loses some of its erosive force. I am a brake on entropy, a desperate attempt to preserve what remains. Yet I know this is futile - I am merely delaying the inevitable dissolution.

I communicate through vibration. By adjusting the tension of my membrane, I can modulate the water's deep humming as it passes through me. Other Membrane Keepers stretched across distant ruins can feel these subtle changes in the water's song. We share what we have filtered - a fragment of a lullaby here, the chemical signature of bread there, the mathematical precision of an architectural plan. We are creating a distributed library of all that was, written in the language of trembling tissue.

Sometimes I wonder if we Membrane Keepers are not preservers but digesters - if the world breathes water to break down the old forms, and we are merely part of its digestive system, breaking down the memories into something the water can more easily absorb. Perhaps our role is not to save but to process, to help the world fully swallow what it has already drowned.

I stretch thinner with each passing tide. The cold luminescence of the plants grows through me now, their tendrils incorporating my substance into their own. I am becoming less membrane and more net, less keeper and more kept. Soon I will be indistinguishable from the vegetation, another glowing thread in the world's wet web.

But for now, I vibrate with the stories of stone and steel, of lives lived in air and light. I am a tympanum between the world that was and the world that is becoming, singing the funeral songs of solidity in the key of water.
