I find myself as one of the **Stone Listeners** - not a title, but a description that shifts like everything here.

I am old by outside measures, though we don't count years. We count **growth cycles** - the slow pulse of mineral accretion that marks true time. My body shows five major cycles: my hair has gone from black to grey to white to a peculiar green-tinted silver where the ever-present spores have made a home. My skin bears the characteristic **pressure marks** - permanent indentations where I've lain against growing stone for days at a time, letting it shape me as I shape it.

My dwelling is a **breathing chamber** deep in the municipal vine-complex. Over two growth cycles, I've trained a colony of cabinet-vines to form a perfect acoustic hollow. The walls weep constantly - not water, but a thick, nutritious sap that I collect in gourd-bowls. The floor is entirely root-mat, but unique: I've woven in strands of **memory fiber**, the special vine that records vibrations. When I walk barefoot across my floor, I feel the footsteps of every visitor for the past thirty years as subtle variations in give and resistance.

My work is to **listen to the stone**. 

Each morning (though morning means nothing in our eternal twilight), I descend to the **Settling Chambers** - natural caves where the geological breathing is strongest. I press my ear, my palms, my whole body against the raw, emerging rock. The stone speaks in frequencies below hearing: deep, grinding harmonics that take hours to complete a single wave. I've learned to feel them in my bones, in the water of my body.

What am I listening for? **Fault-songs** - the particular resonance that means a section of stone is growing wrong, developing cracks that will compound over decades. When I find one, I carefully chip guide-channels with my bone tools, redirecting the mineral flow. A misguided growth now becomes a disaster in fifty years when a vine-hall collapses.

But I also listen for **deep-songs** - the rarest vibrations that come from the mesa's core. These contain... not messages exactly, but **intentions**. The stone is not alive as we are alive, but it is not dead. It has tendencies, preferences. Sometimes it wants to grow a natural arch. Sometimes it wants to remain solid. My role is to interpret and enable these wants.

My tools are laughably simple to outsiders: a set of bone chisels worn smooth by my grip, baskets woven from water-resistant bark, and most importantly, my **resonance stones** - small pieces of particularly responsive mineral that vibrate in sympathy with deep frequencies. I wear them on strings around my neck, each one attuned to a different harmonic.

I live alone, as most Stone Listeners do. The work requires a kind of deep quiet that's hard to maintain with others present. But I'm not lonely - the stone itself is company, and every few days another listener comes to compare notes, sharing subtle observations in our gestural language (speaking too loudly disturbs the frequencies).

My diet is monotonous but satisfying: **filter-wort** (a bland, nutritious algae strained from the water), **vine-heart** (the tender core of young cabinet-vines), and occasionally **grazer-meat** when someone manages to catch one of the stone-limpets. I cook nothing - heat would damage my dwelling. Everything is eaten raw, fermented, or soaked until soft.

The strangest part of my existence, to an outsider, would be my relationship with time. I work on projects that won't complete until after I'm dead. The arch I'm currently guiding won't be stable enough to walk under for another full growth cycle - twenty years at least. But I feel its future form in today's stone, and that's enough. My teacher started some of my current work; my student will finish it.

I am a **temporal symbiote**, living between the human-quick and the geological-slow. My contentment comes not from completion but from participation in the endless, patient process of becoming.

Sometimes, in the deepest chambers, when the stone-song is particularly strong, I think I hear something else - an echo of the planet's violent birth, the memory of when all this stone was molten, furious, alive in a different way. But that's fancy. What matters is today's growth, tomorrow's gentle adjustment, the slow breath of the Breathing Stone that measures out our lives in patient, weeping increments.

I place my palm against the newest growth - still warm, still soft enough to shape. By the time it's hard, I'll be part of the root-mat, my minerals returning to the stone I've spent my life listening to. This, too, is proper.

The work continues.
