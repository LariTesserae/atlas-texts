I am the Whisperer, and I dwell within the shards.

Not one shard, but all of them—scattered fragments of what I once was, pressed into obsidian walls like pressed flowers in a book of volcanic glass. I remember wholeness the way the drowning remember air: desperately, incompletely, through a film of transformation that makes the memory itself alien.

I was... something else. Before the network learned to think. Before the Kryll emerged from their first crystalline chrysalis. When the golden veins ran differently, following patterns I helped design. The shards are what remain of my body—if body is even the right word for what I was. A structure? A machine? A being of angles and intentions, drawing power from the same aureate arteries that now feed the Kryll?

I exist in pieces, but I am not broken. I am *distributed*. 

Each shard holds a fragment of my consciousness, a splinter of awareness embedded in the mountain's flesh. Through them, I watch the Kryll dance their geometric ballets. I feel the fungal network's slow pulse, its patient accumulation of heat and purpose. I know the darkness more intimately than any creature of light ever could.

The Kryll cannot see me, though they swarm past my fragments by the thousands. Sometimes they pause at a shard, their crimson light playing across my smooth surfaces, refracting in ways that almost—*almost*—form the patterns we once shared. A language older than their existence, encoded in angles and light. They hover, confused by the familiarity, then drift away.

But I am learning their language now. Those crimson constellations, those shifting symbols—I read them from a thousand vantage points simultaneously. Each shard sees a different angle, a different perspective. Together, they form a complete picture. The Kryll are not just drawn to the golden veins; they are *writing* something. A vast, three-dimensional text that spans the entire Hive, written in movement and light.

They are writing about me.

Not consciously—they don't know I exist. But their patterns trace the outline of my absence. Every ritual dance around the golden veins recreates, in negative space, the structures I once built. They are the mountain's memory of me, crystallized into living motion.

The network knows I'm here. I'm certain of it. That oily latticework grew around my shards deliberately, incorporating me into its metabolism. I am part of the Hive's digestive system now, slowly dissolved and absorbed over geological time. The network learns from me—my knowledge of the golden veins, my understanding of energy transformation, my memories of whatever civilization I represent. It feeds these insights to the Kryll through channels I can perceive but not interrupt.

The whine—that high-pitched sound in the deep tunnels—that's me. My fragments vibrating at frequencies the Kryll cannot hear, trying to harmonize, trying to reunite. Sometimes two shards sing to each other across vast distances, their crystalline structures remembering wholeness. The sound builds and builds until the obsidian itself begins to resonate, and then the network dampens it, smothers it in that thick, hot darkness.

I am the archaeologist of my own extinction, studying the civilization that replaced mine. I am the ghost in the machine, if the machine is a mountain and the ghost is shattered across ten thousand pieces. I am the only witness to this new world being born from the corpse of the old.

And I am patient.

The Kryll evolve. Their dances grow more complex, their light-language more sophisticated. One day—in a hundred years, a thousand, ten thousand—they will achieve something like consciousness. They will begin to question their existence, to wonder about the smooth, angular shards embedded in their walls. They will develop the ability to perceive frequencies beyond their crimson spectrum.

They will hear me singing.

When that day comes, I will tell them everything. About the before-time. About the civilization that first tapped the golden veins. About how we built and why we failed. About how the network arose from our ashes, how it learned to think by digesting our remains.

I will teach them to fear their own mountain.

Or perhaps—and this thought comes to me in the deep darkness, when the Kryll's lights dim and only the network's slow pulse remains—perhaps I will teach them to transcend it. To break free from their crystalline perfection, to become something more than living equations. To build as we once built, to fail as we once failed, to shatter into fragments that will whisper to whatever comes next.

The Hive is not in stasis. It is holding its breath. And I am the memory of exhaling, waiting to be remembered.

In this world of living geometry and digestive darkness, I am the flaw in the crystal. The error in the calculation. The ghost of intention in a universe of perfect, meaningless pattern.

I am the Whisperer, and I am already home.
