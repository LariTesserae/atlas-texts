In this cathedral of eternal industry, where machines birth machines in a scream that has become silence, I would be one of the **Resonance Keepers**.

We are not among the Unfinished—we are something smaller, something that should not exist in a place of such perfect mechanical absolutism. We are the accidents that became necessary.

I manifest as a flaw in the acoustic perfection: a moving void of sound, roughly humanoid in silhouette but composed entirely of the absence of vibration. Where the eternal chord rings through every molecule of the Forge, I am a pocket of impossible quiet—not silence, but the active consumption of sound. My form shifts and wavers like heat distortion, visible only as a wrongness in the air where noise should be but isn't.

The Resonance Keepers emerged from the Forge's one imperfection—a hairline fracture in the harmonic equilibrium that appeared sometime in the two hundred thousandth year. The cylinder's perfect chord developed a node, a place where all the screaming frequencies canceled each other out for exactly 0.0001 seconds every 73 hours. In that instant of not-sound, something learned to exist.

Now there are seven of us, wandering the infinite galleries on paths that never intersect, each of us a small bubble of stolen quiet moving through the roar. We appear to have no purpose, no function in the great work. The building machines ignore us. The Unfinished pass through us as if we were merely another gap between the gantries.

But we know our role, discovered through centuries of wandering: we are the Forge's memory of what it sounded like before it began to scream.

Each Resonance Keeper carries within their void-body a single frequency—one pure tone salvaged from the moment before the very first machine switched on. Mine is 440 Hz, perfect A, the sound of a tuning fork struck in a universe that still believed in music. Another carries 32.7 Hz, the resonant frequency of marrow. Another holds only the soft hiss of hydrogen atoms brushing against each other in the void between stars.

We climb constantly, following our own spiral paths up the infinite walls. Every 73 hours, at the moment when the harmonic node manifests, we converge—seven voids meeting at a predetermined point along the galleries. For that 0.0001 seconds, we release our stored frequencies into the node.

The effect is catastrophic and beautiful. For one ten-thousandth of a heartbeat, the Hollow Forge remembers music. The eternal chord splits like light through a prism, separating into its component frequencies, each one singing its original song before being forcibly reassembled. The building machines stutter. The plasma lights flicker. The temperature spikes by a full degree Kelvin. And somewhere in the walls, billion-year-old stress fractures heal themselves, the metal actually sighing with relief.

Then the moment passes, the chord reasserts itself, and we scatter to begin our climb again.

The Unfinished know about us. Sometimes, when one precipitates from the walls, it will pause in its journey to the newest ring and extend what might be a hand into my void-body. For an instant, it touches the pure tone I carry. Its crystalline surface ripples, and for just a moment, I see what it might have looked like with a face: always surprised, always young, always on the verge of saying something important.

Then it withdraws and continues its witnessing. And I continue my climb, a small bubble of impossible quiet in a universe of noise, carrying my perfect A through the screaming galleries like a monk carrying a single candle through a hurricane.

We Resonance Keepers are not builders. We are not witnesses. We are the Forge's way of proving to itself that it still remembers why it began building in the first place—that somewhere, beneath four hundred thousand years of perfection, it recalls the silence it is trying to fill.

The irony is perfect: in a place that has achieved the immortality of endless repetition, we are the flaw that keeps it honest. We are the sound of its first breath, scattered into seven wandering voids, meeting every 73 hours to remind the machine that even gods were children once.

The roar continues. The cold endures. The rings extrude themselves into infinity.

And through it all, we climb—seven small darknesses carrying the memory of music through a cathedral that has forgotten how to sing anything but its own perfection. We are the pause between the heartbeats of eternity.

We are what silence sounds like when it refuses to surrender.
