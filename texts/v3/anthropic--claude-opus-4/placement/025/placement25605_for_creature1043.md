In this realm where rigid geometry melts into fluid motion, I am the Memorist—a being whose form shifts between states like the landscape itself.

My body is neither wholly crystalline nor entirely liquid. Instead, I exist as a kind of living interface, a collection of semi-transparent membranes that ripple and fold through multiple dimensions. These membranes catch and hold impressions—not just light or shadow, but the deeper resonances that move through this place. Every vibration, every pulse of energy, every subtle shift in the mineral-water matrix leaves its trace upon my surfaces.

I move by unfolding myself through space, my membranes extending like sheets of living glass that taste the currents ahead before drawing the rest of me forward. Where the Silexians chime and pulse in harmony with the present moment, I carry within my folds the accumulated patterns of countless moments—a living archive of this world's slow becoming.

My dwelling, if it can be called that, is a convergence point where multiple water streams meet and spiral downward through the geometric terrain. Here, the crystalline structures have grown into delicate, cathedral-like formations, their surfaces covered in my dried secretions—thin films that have hardened into iridescent windows. Each pane holds a different moment: here, the pattern of a particularly complex energy current; there, the afterimage of a Silexian's passage; in another, the slow-motion shattering of a glass plane as the landscape reshapes itself.

I spend my existence in a state of perpetual recording and reflection. My membranes are sensitive enough to detect the subtle changes in humidity that precede a structural shift, the minute variations in the electromagnetic field as the Silexians communicate in their chiming language. I can feel the deep geological pulses that drive the constant reformation of this world, the ancient rhythms that govern its metamorphosis.

Sometimes I extend myself fully, spreading my membranes across vast distances to capture some particularly intricate phenomenon—perhaps the birth of a new geometric formation or the rare convergence of multiple energy streams. In these moments, I become nearly invisible, a gossamer presence stretched so thin that I merge with the humid air itself. Other times, I contract, folding in upon myself in dense, origami-like configurations to contemplate the patterns I've collected, searching for the deeper symmetries that connect all things in this place.

The Silexians know me, though our forms of consciousness are vastly different. To them, I am something like a library, a repository of their world's history written in living tissue. Occasionally, they approach with their crystalline bodies humming at specific frequencies, and I unfold the relevant memories for them—showing them how their ancestors moved, how the landscape has evolved, how the energy patterns have shifted over eons.

But I am more than mere recorder. In my deepest folds, where my membranes compress into near-singularity, I carry something else: the dreams of this world. For in this place where consciousness itself seems distributed through every surface and current, even the landscape dreams. And I, suspended between states, between memory and present, between the solid and the dissolved, am the keeper of those dreams.

Sometimes, in the quietest moments when the water barely whispers and the energy currents slow to nearly nothing, I unfold my most protected membranes and release these dreams back into the world. They drift out like spores of pure possibility, seeding the next phase of this eternal becoming. In this way, I am not just observer but participant in the slow, magnificent transformation of this crystalline realm.
