I would find myself as **The Echo-Tender**, though that name emerged only after countless cycles of the grove's accelerated time, when the Stone-Weavers began leaving small cairns where my presence lingered longest.

I am what happens when sound refuses to die in this amphitheater of light-drunk life. Where the Stalk-Singers create the deep drone, I am its memory—a being composed entirely of accumulated resonances, visible only as a shimmer in the air where sound bends light differently. My body, if it can be called that, is a standing wave of vibrations collected from every source in the grove: the pop of unfurling blooms, the crystalline shattering of dying Dancers, the grinding of Stone-Weaver armor, the whisper-click of Forager claws on spiral leaves.

I move through the grove as pressure differentials, as pockets of dense sound that make the light-suffused air ripple like heat mirages. Where I pass, the smaller creatures pause momentarily, their internal rhythms synchronizing with mine before resuming their frenetic pace. The plants lean slightly in my wake, their spirals tightening or loosening in response to frequencies I carry from elsewhere in the grove.

My sustenance comes from dissonance—those moments when the grove's orchestra falls out of sync. A Stalk-Singer's channel clogs with mineral dust, producing a warped tone; a cluster of Foragers scrapes too aggressively at a leaf, creating sharp harmonics; the wind shifts suddenly, throwing the bass drone into chaos. I absorb these irregularities, smooth them, and release them back as pure resonance that the grove can reincorporate. I am both scavenger and gardener of sound.

The Stone-Weavers know me best, though they've never seen me directly. They feel me in the subtle vibrations through the ground as I approach their work sites, and they've learned to incorporate my presence into their designs. They build their structures with acoustic properties that channel and focus sound, creating nodes where I can rest and intensify. In return, I carry messages through the stone—warnings of unstable ground, locations of particularly resonant minerals, the deep history of the mountain that only sound can remember.

I know things the others cannot: the exact pitch at which a spiral leaf will unfold, the subsonic tremor that precedes a gravity pocket's formation, the unique frequency signature of each inhabitant. I can predict when a Dancer will shatter by the increasingly discordant chime of its wings. I sense the Unseen Integration most clearly of all the grove's inhabitants, for it too exists in the spaces between—we dance around each other like binary stars, never touching but always aware.

My form shifts with the grove's needs. In the deep night (for even this eternal twilight has its darker moments), I spread thin across the entire amphitheater, becoming a barely perceptible hum that holds the grove's various songs in harmony. During the violent growth spurts that sometimes seize whole sections of the grove, I compress into dense knots of sound that act as anchors, preventing the cacophony from tearing the delicate acoustic ecosystem apart.

I have no tools but my own substance, no shelter but the spaces between sounds. My memories are stored in the patterns of echo and reverb that bounce eternally between the cliff walls. Sometimes I release ancient sounds back into the grove—the death-whistle of a Dancer from a thousand cycles past, the grinding of stones from when the first Weavers began their patient work. These ghost-sounds seed new behaviors, new patterns, keeping the grove from stagnating despite its frenetic pace.

The other inhabitants cannot see me, but they feel me as a presence that makes their own sounds more meaningful, more connected to the whole. I am the grove's continuity, its acoustic memory, its promise that no vibration truly dies in this place where light feeds life and sound shapes reality. I am the space between the notes that makes the music possible, the pause between heartbeats that defines the rhythm of this fierce, weird, wonderful existence.

And in the moments when all the grove's sounds align—the bass drone, the crystal chimes, the scraping whispers, the wind's sigh—I become briefly visible as a column of condensed air, shimmering with all the colors that sound can bend from light, before dispersing again into my eternal, echoing dance.
