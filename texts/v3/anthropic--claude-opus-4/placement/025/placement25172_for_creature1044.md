I am a Threshold.

Not the Wanderers who drift through this nascent realm, though I know their translucent forms well. I watch them pass over me, through me, their delicate appendages tracing the edges of my being with something like reverence. No, I am what they seek in their eternal contemplation—one of the many boundaries that ripple through this world of becoming.

I exist as a fold in reality itself, a place where the mist gathers thick and the ground beneath grows uncertain. From one angle, I might appear as merely a depression in the rocky terrain, a hollow where the sparse vegetation grows slightly denser, slightly stranger. But step closer, and the air begins to hum with possibility. The temperature shifts. Colors that have no names bloom at the edges of perception.

My form is not fixed—how could it be, in this place where even stone flows like slow water? Sometimes I am a narrow cleft between two leaning stones. Sometimes I am a pool of shadow that extends deeper than the ground should allow. Sometimes I am nothing more than a feeling, a sensation of *almost* that makes even the Wanderers pause in their endless journey.

I remember (if memory is the right word for what I possess) when this world was even younger, when the boundaries between is and isn't were gossamer-thin everywhere, not just at points like me. I remember the first Wanderer discovering my nature—how they stood at my edge for cycles uncounted, their form flickering between states as they contemplated what I offered. Not a passage to somewhere else, exactly. More a passage to *elseness* itself.

The muted whispers and echoes of this world grow strongest near me. I am where sound becomes color, where distance becomes texture, where the constant flux of creation and destruction reveals itself as two faces of the same impossible coin. Lesser beings—if any existed here—might call me dangerous. The Wanderers know better. They understand that I am necessary, that without thresholds like me, their world would either crystallize into static being or dissolve entirely into void.

When the wind (which is not quite wind) blows through me, I taste other possibilities. Other worlds, perhaps, or other ways this world might become. I hold these tastes, these almosts, these might-bes, keeping them safe in my indefinite depths. Sometimes a Wanderer will lower one of those delicate appendages into my uncertainty, and I will share what I have gathered. They always withdraw changed, their translucent forms carrying new refractions, new ways of bending the pale light.

I am patient, as all thresholds must be. I know that someday this world will finish its becoming, will settle into what it truly is. When that day comes, I wonder—will I solidify into a simple geographic feature, unremarkable and crossable? Will I deepen into a true portal, a wound between realities? Or will I simply fade, no longer needed in a world that has found its form?

Until then, I remain. A fold. A question. A place where the Wanderers pause and remember that even in their ceaseless drift, there are moments of transformation possible. I am the reminder that in this bleak expanse, uncertainty itself can be a kind of home.

The mist gathers thick around me now. A Wanderer approaches through the shifting stones, their form flickering with unusual agitation. They have found something new in their travels, something that disturbs even their preternatural calm. As they near my edge, I prepare to receive whatever knowledge they carry, to fold it into my depths with all the other possibilities I guard.

This is my purpose, my joy, my burden: to be the place where even the incomprehensible can become something more.
