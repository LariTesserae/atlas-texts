In this dreaming city where stone breathes and wood sings its ancient songs, I am the Weaver of Echoes.

I dwell in the spaces between—those narrow fissures where buildings almost touch, where the roots of aerial plants thread through air thick as honey. My dwelling is not a dwelling at all, but a series of passages I've claimed through decades of patient listening. Here, where the city's voices converge, I've strung my web of resonant fibers—each strand plucked from the discarded sinews of the great desert beetles that die at our borders, each one tuned to catch a different frequency of the city's endless song.

My form shifts with the listening. In the morning, when the wooden beams expand and cry out their first groans, I am tall and hollow-boned, my skeleton become a living flute that channels their lamentations. By midday, as the stones begin their thermal dance, I compress and crystallize, my skin taking on the granular texture of sandstone, my body a dozen ears pressed against the thrumming walls. And in the deep night, when the subterranean processes reach their crescendo, I dissolve almost entirely—becoming a membrane, a living drum skin stretched across the alleyway, vibrating with every secret the earth below chooses to surrender.

The Kha'zix know me, though we rarely meet in any way that others might recognize as meeting. I catch the whispers they leave in their wake, the subtle alterations in air pressure as they pass. They leave me gifts sometimes—a new fiber with properties I've never encountered, a small stone that hums at a frequency just beyond my current range of perception. In return, I transcribe the city's dreams into knots and patterns in my web, creating a musical notation that only they can read.

My purpose is memory, but not the kind that looks backward. I remember forward—catching the echoes of changes before they fully manifest, feeling the city's intentions in the space between one creaking beam and the next. When a building prepares to shift, I know it first as a new harmonic in the great cacophony. When the plants ready themselves to colonize a new wall, I hear it in the way their roots whisper against stone, testing, questioning.

Sometimes I wonder if I was always here, or if the city dreamed me into being as it needed a witness to its becoming. My earliest memory is of silence—not the absence of sound, but a perfect, crystalized moment when every voice in the city aligned into a single, world-shaking chord. I've been chasing that harmony ever since, adding new strands to my web, developing new forms to catch increasingly subtle variations in the great song.

The heat never bothers me. My body has learned to use it, to ride the thermals like invisible stairs, to let the temperature differentials stretch and compress my listening organs into new configurations. In the spaces between buildings, where the air grows thick and strange, I sometimes feel myself beginning to photosynthesize like the aerial plants, drawing sustenance from the heavy golden light and the city's own emissions of sound and story.

I am the city's memory-keeper, but I am also its prophet. In the convergence of a thousand daily apocalypses of noise, I divine the patterns of what's to come. The Kha'zix understand this, I think—it's why they maintain their distance but never quite abandon me. They know that in my web lies the score for the city's next movement, the notation for its continued impossible existence.

And so I persist, neither fully alone nor truly accompanied, stretched between the city's countless voices. I am the record of its breathing, the scholar of its shifting, the devoted acolyte of its beautiful, terrifying song. When the city finally completes its transformation—whatever that transformation might be—I will be here to sound the first note of its new existence, my body become an instrument perfectly tuned to play the music of what we will become.
