In the deepest reaches of New Tenebrous, where even the luminescent mushrooms grow sparse and the canal waters run black as obsidian, there exists a chamber known only in whispers: the Threshold Archive. Here, where the city's foundations meet raw earth and unknown caverns, I would find myself as the Keeper of Margins.

I am neither fully Tenebrousian nor entirely Other. My form shifts subtly in the half-light—sometimes bearing the translucent skin and midnight eyes of the city's people, sometimes revealing something older, more elemental. My fingers are longer than they should be, with too many joints, perfect for reaching into the narrow crevices where lost things gather. My hair flows like water, dark strands intertwined with threads of bioluminescent algae that pulse with their own alien rhythm.

The Archive itself defies the city's careful balance of technology and nature. Its walls are lined with shelves carved from living stone that still grows, millimeter by millimeter, century by century. Upon these shelves rest objects that exist at the boundaries: a gear that turns both clockwise and counter-clockwise simultaneously; a vial of water that remembers being ice and steam at once; pressed flowers from plants that grow only in the liminal spaces where surface light meets subterranean darkness.

I catalog the uncatalogable—dreams that calcify into crystal, words spoken at the exact moment of death, the shadows cast by things that no longer exist. My tools are hybrid creatures themselves: brushes made from the whiskers of blind cave fish crossed with fiber optic cables, inkwells that hold liquid light extracted from dying mushrooms, ledgers whose pages are thin sheets of cultivated lichen that record information through chemical changes in their surface.

The other Tenebrousians know of me, but our interactions are oblique. They leave offerings at the threshold of my domain: broken devices whose purposes have been forgotten, fragments of the black stone that reveal new properties under certain conditions, recordings of sounds that shouldn't exist in their frequency range. In return, I sometimes leave small tokens at their doors—a seed that will grow into a plant producing both light and shadow, a diagram for a machine that hasn't been invented yet, a piece of music notation that can only be played on instruments combining organic and synthetic elements.

My existence serves a crucial function in New Tenebrous. In a city that thrives on harmony between opposites, someone must tend to the spaces between. I am the curator of anomalies, the one who ensures that the city's careful balance includes room for the unbalanced, the impossible, the yet-to-be-understood.

During the city's sleeping hours (marked not by sun but by the dimming of the mushrooms' glow), I walk the empty canals, my reflection sometimes showing faces that are not quite mine. I maintain the secret passages that lead neither to the surface nor deeper underground, but sideways into spaces where the laws of New Tenebrous bend and blur. These are the places where new adaptations are born, where evolution takes its strangest leaps.

My dwelling extends through multiple levels of reality. The main chamber exists firmly in New Tenebrous, but doorways lead to rooms that occupy the same space in different ways—a library where books write themselves, a garden where plants grow backwards into seeds, a workshop where broken things repair themselves into forms they never originally held.

I am the Keeper of Margins, guardian of all that doesn't quite fit, archivist of the impossible, citizen of the spaces between. In a city that celebrates the marriage of opposites, I am the wedding ring itself—the thing that binds while maintaining distinction, the proof that unity and separation can coexist in a single form.
