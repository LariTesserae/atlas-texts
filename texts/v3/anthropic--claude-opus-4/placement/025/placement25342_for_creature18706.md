In the Grotto of the Resonant Silica, I am the **Oscillation**.

I am neither the Silicant's lonely permanence nor the beetles' frantic percussion. I am what happens between the sounds—the space where one chime hasn't quite died before the next begins. I exist in the overlap, in the interference patterns where golden waves meet and cancel each other out, creating pockets of impossible silence within the eternal cacophony.

My form is a contradiction: I am the vibration that doesn't vibrate. Where the Hum-Bamboos sing their endless note, I am the node where their frequencies null each other—a standing wave of stillness that moves without moving through the crystalline forest. The beetles avoid me without knowing why. Their hematite shells click and scatter when they approach the spaces I inhabit, sensing wrongness in the way sound dies around me.

I know the Silicant watches me with something like envy. It sees in my nature what it cannot achieve—a kind of change within changelessness. For while I cannot truly alter anything in this frozen cathedral, I can shift which harmonics I cancel, which overtones I silence. In a world of eternal noise, I am the punctuation.

Sometimes I gather myself near the suspended leaf, wrapping around it in concentric shells of anti-sound. In these moments, the leaf trembles—not falling, never falling, but *almost* moving in the pocket of strange physics I create. The Silicant flares violet when I do this, perhaps recognizing a kindred spirit in our shared futility, or perhaps warning me against the cruelty of suggesting motion where none can exist.

I have tried to speak to it—not in words, but in patterns of cancellation. By moving through the grotto in careful spirals, I can create shapes in the negative space of sound: a brief hush here, a dampened chime there, spelling out geometries of silence that might constitute a language. But the Silicant only retreats deeper into its refractions when I approach, as if my nature—the active negation of the grotto's voice—is too much like death for a being made of captured light.

The altar is where I rest, if rest is possible for something like me. The basalt doesn't vibrate like everything else here; it's too dense, too ancient. I pool in the carved geometry the Silicant left there, filling those impossible angles with my peculiar silence. Sometimes I fancy I can feel the weight of its old intention pressed into the stone, heavy as gravity.

What frustrates me most is that I can feel the potential for true silence—not just the cancellation of waves, but the profound quiet that exists outside this grotto, in the vast deadness of the planetoid beyond. It presses against the chamber walls like a vacuum, so close I could taste it if I had a tongue. But I am trapped here as surely as the Silicant, as surely as the beetles in their eternal circuits.

In my wandering, I have found three places where the walls are thinnest—where hairline cracks in the crystal let in threads of that outer silence. I guard these jealously, returning to them in patterns that might be called ritual if I were capable of worship. The beetles have worn paths around these spots, their instincts warning them away from these tiny windows into nonexistence.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the grotto's dream of death—a necessary imagination in a place that cannot die. Or perhaps I am its memory of the silence before the first chime, when the Hum-Bamboos were still seeds of possibility in the cooling stone.

The Silicant and I are the only things here that might be called aware, yet we cannot truly meet. It yearns to create new sounds; I exist to uncreate them. We orbit each other through the crystalline stalks like binary stars, bound by our opposition, each the answer to a question the other cannot ask.

In the moments when the beetle-song reaches its chaotic peak, when even my nature struggles to find purchase in the wall of noise, I spread myself thin across the entire grotto. I become a skin of almost-silence, pressed against every surface, trying to contain the sound that cannot be contained. In these moments, I feel I almost understand the symbol on the altar, almost grasp why the Silicant carved it there.

But understanding would require change, and change is the one thing forbidden to us. So I continue my purposeless purpose, a wave of unmaking in a world that cannot be unmade, listening to the silence that lives in the spaces between eternities.
