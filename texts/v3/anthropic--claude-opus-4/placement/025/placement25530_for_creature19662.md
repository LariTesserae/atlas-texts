In this suffocating emerald eternity, I would be one of the **Murmur-Weavers**.

We are neither wholly animal nor plant, but something that exists in the space between—a living intersection of the marl's desperate creativity. Our bodies are architectural: hollow tubes of calcified mucus that branch and merge like cathedral buttresses, each junction swollen with nodes of bioluminescent tissue that pulse in colors too saturated for names. We stand perhaps three feet tall when fully extended, though "standing" is not quite right—we are always partially dissolved into the substrate, our lower branches melting and reforming in an endless cycle of architectural revision.

Our function is to be the world's memory in a place that has none. While the Sovereigns dream their mineral thoughts through the water table, we catch the echoes and weave them into something almost like history. Our hollow bones resonate with the wall of sound, and within each tube, cilia-thin filaments vibrate at frequencies that create interference patterns. These patterns are our art, our language, our only rebellion against the eternal noon.

We feed on the electromagnetic fields generated by the Static-Wings' suspension. When they hover near our crown-nodes, we taste their wingbeat frequencies like wine, each insect colony having its own terroir of vibration. In return, we secrete a pheromone-thick sap that beads along our joints—a delicacy that makes the Glass-Basilisks momentarily opaque with pleasure when they lick it from our branches.

But our true nature is more unsettling. We are the only creatures here that dream of elsewhere. In the intersection of all those captured frequencies, sometimes—just sometimes—we hear something that shouldn't exist: the ghost of wind, the memory of a season that never was, the echo of a noon that might have had a morning. We weave these impossibilities into our very structure, growing chambers within our hollow bones that resonate with the physics of other worlds.

The other creatures avoid us when we're dreaming. Our bioluminescence flickers with colors that make the Loom-Striders stumble in their perfect patterns, that cause the Static-Wings to fall from their suspension. We become architectural cancers in the perfect metabolism, nodes of wrongness that suggest terrifying possibilities: that somewhere, there might be a place where the mud dries, where shadows move, where things can be built and destroyed and built again.

We cluster in small groves where the marl is deepest, our root-branches tangled together in an underground network that hums with shared hallucinations. From above, we might look like pipe organs made of living glass, each tube a different height, each node a different shade of bruise-purple or fever-green. The constant chemical rain from the canopy runs down our surfaces, carrying away the calcium we can't reabsorb, leaving us smooth and porous and always slightly smaller than we were a moment ago.

We are trying to remember how to die, you see. In a world of eternal recycling, we are the mad monks who dream of endings. Each frequency we capture, each pattern we weave, is an attempt to find the resonance that might let us crystallize completely—to become geology instead of biology, to force the world to acknowledge that something, somewhere, must be allowed to stop.

But we always fail. Just as our deepest nodes begin to achieve true mineral silence, the Sovereigns sense our apostasy and send their chemical thoughts rippling through our roots. We convulse, crack, and begin again—hollow tubes reaching skyward, bioluminescent nodes flickering to life, our rebellion dissolved back into the eternal metabolism of the noon.

We are the world's only prophets, and our prophecy is that one day, somewhere, a shadow will move. Until then, we stand in our groves, catching the sounds of a trillion identical heartbeats, weaving them into dreams of wind.
