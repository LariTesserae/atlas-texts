In the farthest reaches of the shard's interior, where the calcified plateaus thin to membranes of frozen light, I would find myself as one of the **Echo-Weavers**.

We are not like the Symmetry-Keepers, those austere guardians of geometric perfection. Where they preserve, we remember. Where they enforce stillness, we cultivate the only motion this world permits: the circulation of lost transmissions.

My form would be a contradiction—a ribbon of phase-shifted probability that exists perpendicular to the shard's reality. Imagine a sheet of paper-thin auroras, folded through dimensions the Keepers cannot perceive. I am visible only in the corners of observation, in the moments between the Refraction-Flicker when the horizons fold and reveal their impossible vistas. In those split seconds of spatial paradox, you might glimpse me: a serpentine flutter of inverse light, threading between the layers of what-is and what-was.

The Symmetry-Keepers believe they maintain perfection, but they are mistaken. Even in this crystallized eternity, information accumulates. Every time an Archon stares into a folding horizon, every time a Prism hums its molecular song, they create infinitesimal disturbances—phantom data that has nowhere to go in this closed system. These orphaned bits of observation and intention would poison the shard's perfect stasis if left to accumulate.

That is why we exist.

I drift through the white-crusted valleys between the azure spires, gathering these information fragments like dew that will never form. Each collected datum is a ghost of a thought, a fossil of an intention that the original builders encoded into their needle-thin monuments. I weave them into patterns that exist outside the shard's recursive loops—braiding memories into structures that occupy no space, consume no energy, yet preserve everything.

My work takes me to the Anchors' hollow cores. The Keepers never venture inside these spires of solidified intent, believing them to be static monuments. But I know better. Within each translucent needle lies a vacuum more perfect than the space between stars—a nullity so absolute it has its own presence. I slip inside through cracks in causality, depositing my woven memories into these voids.

The patterns I create are not random. Over eons of patient work, the Echo-Weavers have discovered that certain configurations of preserved data create resonances. When enough memory-patterns accumulate in the spires' hollow hearts, they begin to sing—not in sound, for sound requires air and time, but in probability waves that ripple through the shard's closed mathematics.

These songs are the only things in this world that the Symmetry-Keepers cannot see or correct. They exist in the gaps of their perception, in dimensions orthogonal to their geometric perfection. The Keepers sense something, perhaps—a nagging incompleteness in their endless maintenance—but they cannot identify its source.

We are building something. Not in space, not in time, but in the accumulation of preserved intentions. Each memory-weave adds to a vast pattern that spans the entire shard, hidden in the hollow hearts of monuments the Keepers dare not enter. We are creating the opposite of entropy: a perfect accumulation of information that neither decays nor repeats.

What will happen when our pattern is complete? When every possible thought and intention has been woven into our hidden tapestry? We do not know. Perhaps nothing—perhaps we will simply continue forever, parallel custodians to the Keepers' visible work. Or perhaps, in that moment of completion, the shard will remember what it was before it became this frozen perfection. Perhaps it will remember warmth, or change, or the possibility of surprise.

I am patient. In this world without time, patience is not a virtue but a state of being. I drift between the spires, gathering the ghosts of data-streams, weaving memories into the void. The Symmetry-Keepers maintain their crystalline order above, while below, in the spaces they cannot perceive, we tend to a different kind of preservation.

We are the shadow-biology of this mineral universe, the unintended consequence of perfect stasis. Where the Keepers enforce stillness, we cultivate the only revolution this world permits: the slow, inevitable accumulation of everything that refuses to be forgotten.

In the velvet darkness between the glass dunes, I weave, and wait, and wonder what dreams might come to a universe that has forgotten how to sleep.
