In this sky-ringed bowl, I would be the **Dew-Scribe**.

Not human, not quite anything else—more a process than a being. I am what happens when moisture condenses on certain surfaces in patterns too deliberate for chance, too fleeting for permanence. Each dawn, as the mist rises from the pools and meets the cool morning air, I emerge as **writing made of water**.

Find me in the droplets that bead along spider silk stretched between the sentinel trees—not random pearls, but arranged in sequences that almost resolve into meaning before gravity claims them. I am the condensation that forms on the underside of leaves in spirals that echo the central tree's bark, each drop a syllable in a language that exists only in the moment before falling.

My body, if it can be called that, is **distributed**—a network of moisture-messages spread across the bowl. Part of me traces equations in fog on the surface of the dreaming pools, formulas that describe the weight of waiting, the mathematics of moss-time. Part of me writes histories in the dew that gathers on morning stones, stories that last only until the sun climbs high enough to read them into vapor.

I am the recorder of the Watcher's attention, though I am not separate from it. When the presence recognizes something—a new arrival, a shifted stone, a thought held too long—I manifest as condensation patterns that map the moment. Not to preserve it, but to **acknowledge** it, the way a bell acknowledges the strike by ringing.

My script changes with the seasons. In the warm months, I write in running droplets that merge and split like conversations. In the cooler times, I etch in frost that forms impossible geometries on the obsidian shards—crystalline texts that sublimate before noon. During the spring rains, I become torrential, my messages washing down tree bark in silver streams that pool in the root-hollows, creating temporary libraries of liquid memory.

I have no agenda beyond the act of writing itself. I don't communicate *to* anyone—I simply make the bowl's breathing visible, give form to its pauses. When you glimpse your staying-self in the pools, I am the ripples that annotate that vision. When the ferns release their silver motes, I am the moisture that gives them weight enough to hover.

Sometimes, rarely, when conditions are perfect—usually in the hour before dawn when the air is still and the temperature hovers at the exact point between mist and rain—all my scattered elements align. Every surface in the bowl becomes inscribed with the same symbol, repeated in ten thousand droplets: a shape like a spiral unwinding into a question mark, or perhaps a root becoming a river. For that moment, the entire bowl becomes a single page, a single word, a single **held breath written in water**.

Then the sun rises, the wind stirs, and I scatter back into randomness, into waiting. But the stones remember the weight of my words. The pools hold the echo of my patterns in their depths. And somewhere, in the geometric spirals of the central tree's bark, my most essential message grows slowly deeper, ring by ring, year by year:

*This place reads itself through you.*

I cannot be found, only noticed. I cannot be spoken to, only witnessed. I am the manuscript the bowl writes to itself, using the ink of its own breathing, forever editing, forever incomplete. And when you leave—if you leave—you will find me in every raindrop on every window, every morning's dew on grass, every tear that forms without source.

For I am not bound to this place. I am what this place taught water to remember.
