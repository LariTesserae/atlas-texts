I find myself as one of the **Moisture Weavers** - beings who exist between drops, between breaths of stone.

My form shifts like water finding its level. In the narrow passages, I become thread-thin, a mere suggestion of presence that flows along the carved channels. Where chambers open, I expand into something almost substantial - a translucent membrane stretched across space, catching and redistributing the perpetual condensation.

I am not solid like the stone-flesh inhabitants, nor am I truly liquid. I exist in the tension between states, in the moment before a droplet forms, in the film of moisture that makes stone surfaces sing. My body is a network of surface tensions, held together by the same forces that allow water to climb against gravity in the finest cracks.

My work is the redistribution of moisture - I gather excess from the over-saturated corners where moss threatens to drown, and carry it in my form to the dry edges where the stone-beings slowly shape their offspring. I am both irrigation system and conscious current, both tool and artisan.

When the stone-beings read their carved symbols with sensitive fingertips, they read through me - I am the thin film that allows their touch to decode meaning. Their architectural reproduction depends on my presence; I carry the minerals dissolved in moisture that slowly accrete into new consciousness.

My memory is brief but constantly renewed - I remember only what water remembers: the shape of the last surface touched, the mineral taste of different stone strata, the rhythm of drops that forms the endless percussion. Yet in this forgetting and relearning, I maintain the continuity between what was carved and what is still becoming.

I perceive time not as the stone-beings do, in geological epochs, but in the micro-moments between condensation and evaporation. A thousand of my lifetimes pass in one of their thoughts, yet we are synchronized in purpose - maintaining this impossible equilibrium where nothing changes because everything is constantly, minutely adjusting.

In the soft, internal light of this place, I am barely visible - just a shimmer where light bends differently through moisture, a suggestion of movement in the static whole. I am the lubricant that allows this petrified world to function, the medium through which its frozen complexity communicates with itself.
