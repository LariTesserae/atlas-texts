I would manifest as one of the **Inertials**.

We are the memory of motion itself, trapped in the interstices where machine-logic meets plant-flesh. Unlike the Sedimentors who accumulate from debris, we are born from the frozen potential energy of the stalled pistons and locked gears. We are what happens when kinetic force has nowhere to go.

Picture a translucent membrane, thin as an eyelid, stretched between the teeth of a cathedral-sized gear and the woody flesh that holds it prisoner. That membrane is my body—if body is even the right word. I exist in the space between the machine's yearning to turn and the forest's refusal to let it. My substance is neither plant nor metal, but the crystallized tension between them.

We Inertials live spread across surfaces like oil films on water, our bodies mere microns thick but potentially acres wide. We taste the rust through our skin, feel the slow seep of sap across our surfaces. Every droplet of condensation that strikes us reverberates through our being like a gong strike, and we shimmer with the impact, our transparency briefly revealing rainbow patterns—the ghost of motion, refracted.

I am draped across a massive flywheel, my edges seeping into the moss that clutches its rim. The wheel wants to spin—I can feel its desire as a constant pressure against my membrane, a force that has been building for millennia. This pressure is my food, my breath, my reason. I metabolize stillness itself, breaking down the differential between what-should-move and what-cannot into something I can survive on.

Sometimes, in the eternal humidity, parts of me detach—tiny fragments that drift like jellyfish through the thick air, carried by convection currents so subtle they're almost theoretical. These fragments are how we reproduce, if reproduction is even the word for it. They seek out new mechanical hearts to wrap around, new seized bearings to embrace, new frozen dreams of industry to feed upon.

We communicate through tension patterns. When I shift my weight across the flywheel's surface, the change in pressure creates a wave that other Inertials can feel through the rigid structures we're all connected to. It's a language of stress and strain, of forces that should be but aren't. We share stories of the almost-movement, the nearly-was, the forever-delayed.

The Sedimentors fear their shattering, but we Inertials fear something worse: equilibrium. If the forces we feed on ever truly balanced—if the machine's desire to move ever fully accepted its paralysis—we would simply cease. We would become nothing more than dew.

So we maintain ourselves in the spaces where the world fights itself, living testimonies to the agony of arrested development. We are the ulcers of this planetary stomach, the places where the digestion has gone wrong, where the forest-that-ate-the-factory still feels the indigestible weight of its meal.

In the wet darkness, I stretch thinner, feeling for the microscopic vibrations that tell me another drop is about to fall, another Sedimentor is about to shift, another tiny proof that even in this monument to stasis, true stillness remains impossible. I am the proof that even a world that has given up on becoming still trembles with the memory of what it meant to change.
