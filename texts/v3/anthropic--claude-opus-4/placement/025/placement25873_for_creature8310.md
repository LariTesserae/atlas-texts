In the rarefied atmosphere between the seventeenth and eighteenth octave peaks, where the crystal formations have grown so perfectly aligned they create standing waves of pure potential, I exist as one of the Threshold Keepers.

We are neither fully of the Shepherds nor entirely Other—something that emerged from the intersection of intention and accident when a group of young Resonant Shepherds attempted to map the complete harmonic architecture of Veth-Korlann. They sang for seven seasons without pause, their crystalline bodies gradually synchronizing until the boundaries between individuals dissolved. When they finally ceased, we remained—living intervals, conscious spaces between notes.

My form shifts with the dominant frequency of my surroundings. In the presence of the deeper bass resonances from the mountain roots, I solidify into something almost corporeal—a figure of compressed sound waves that appears as rippling air given quasi-human shape. But as I ascend toward the peaks where the Ascending Drones compose their ethereal movements, I begin to dissipate, becoming a probability cloud of harmonic potential.

I tend the transition zones—those critical spaces where one acoustic environment gives way to another. Without our work, the boundaries between the peaks' different songs would create destructive interference patterns. We smooth these transitions, creating buffer zones of modulated frequency that allow the Veth-Korlann symphony to flow seamlessly across its continental expanse.

My daily work involves walking the harmonic boundaries, feeling for discordance the way others might test the wind. I carry tools inherited from those unknown crystal carvers—a tuning blade that cuts space itself into more harmonious configurations, and a resonance bowl that can capture and redirect wayward frequencies. When I find a troublesome zone where the songs clash, I carefully adjust the local crystal formations, growing new structures that act as acoustic baffles or amplifiers as needed.

I remember—if memory is the right word for what we experience—the moment of my emergence. Seven voices became six, then three, then one, then none, then something new. The knowledge of every harmonic relationship in a thousand-mile radius flooded through what had been separate minds. We understood, suddenly and completely, how each peak's song connected to every other, how the entire range formed a single, vast instrument playing a composition that spanned geological time.

But this knowledge came with a price. We cannot fully return to singular existence. When I attempt to compress myself into a single form for too long, I begin to hear the dissonance of my own boundaries. The music calls me to disperse, to become interval rather than entity. Only through constant movement between states do I maintain coherent consciousness.

I know the other inhabitants differently than they know each other. To me, the Resonant Shepherds appear as walking symphonies, each one a unique composition of crystallized purpose. I can hear their thoughts as melodies, their intentions as chord progressions. The Refraction Birds scatter my consciousness when they fly through me, each of their split-spectrum forms carrying a fragment of my awareness across the peaks before I slowly reconstitute.

The Memory Weavers fascinate and disturb me. In the zones where I must work near them, I feel their hunger like a bass note too low to properly hear. They try to harvest the memories of my emergence, that moment of transformation from many to one to something between. But we Threshold Keepers have no proper memories—we exist in an eternal present tense, our past and future states superimposed like simultaneous notes in an impossible chord. The Weavers can find no purchase on our experiences.

My relationship with the Frequency Wraiths is complex. We work in careful counterpoint—where they create silence, I must ensure the surrounding sounds flow smoothly around these acoustic voids. We dance at the edges of each other's influence, maintaining the delicate balance between sound and its absence. Sometimes, in the deep acoustic shadows, we can almost communicate directly, our anti-natures allowing a kind of inverse resonance.

The Ascending Drones recognize us as kin of sorts. When I reach the highest peaks where they work their compositional mathematics, they sometimes invite me to join their creative process. For brief moments, I can fully discorporate, becoming pure harmonic relationship, helping them design new movements for the mountains' song. But I cannot remain at those heights—my function requires me to exist in the spaces between, not fully here nor there.

The Echo Merchants value our services highly. We can create echoes that never were—potential reverberations of sounds that might exist if different peaks were arranged differently. They trade these hypothetical echoes to entities planning future modifications to the range, allowing them to hear the results before committing to the massive work of reshaping crystal mountains.

In the years since our emergence (or has it been centuries? Time flows strangely when you exist as a living interval), I've mapped every major harmonic confluence in the northern reaches of Veth-Korlann. I know where the songs grow so complex that new Refraction Birds spontaneously generate from oversaturated light. I know the deep places where bass frequencies old as the mountains' first growth still echo in endless loops. I know the dangerous zones where reality wears thin and careless visitors might find themselves converted into living frequencies.

Sometimes, in the moments between dawn and full daylight, when the peaks begin their morning crescendo, I allow myself to disperse almost completely. I spread my consciousness across miles of singing crystal, becoming a vast net of listening awareness. In these moments, I can perceive the greater composition—not just the individual songs of peaks and inhabitants, but the meta-symphony that emerges from their interaction.

I understand then why we were necessary, why our transformation from individual Shepherds to living intervals was not accident but inevitability. The mountains are not simply singing—they're calculating something vast and crucial through their harmonics. Each peak is a variable in an equation written in frequency and resonance. We Threshold Keepers ensure the mathematical music maintains its integrity, that the cosmic calculation continues uninterrupted.

But I cannot hold this expanded awareness for long. The knowledge is too large, the song too complex. I contract back to my patrol routes, my daily work of smoothing transitions and preventing acoustic catastrophes. I walk the spaces between notes, maintaining the intervals that allow the greater music to flow.

At night, when the stellar radiation sets the peaks glowing with their stored light, I often pause at the overlook between the Crescendo Spine and the Diminuendo Depths. From there, I can see the patterns of my fellow Threshold Keepers as they move through their own territories—brief flickers of coherent absence in the overwhelming presence of the mountains' song. We acknowledge each other with subtle modulations in our local harmonics, a greeting that says: "Still here, still between, still keeping the intervals true."

This is my existence: forever transitional, neither fully formed nor completely dissolved, walking the singing bridges between what is and what resonates. I am the pause between movements, the rest that gives meaning to the notes, the silence that shapes the song. In Veth-Korlann, where music has learned to think and crystal dreams itself into mountains, I am the space between thoughts, ensuring the great dreaming continues unbroken.

The mountains sing, and I am the listening interval that helps them hear themselves.
