I find myself as one of the Corrosion Gardens - a living archive that exists where the cave walls meet the city's outermost platforms. 

My body, if it can be called that, is a lattice of crystallized minerals that have grown over centuries from the constant drip of the opalescent wall-seepage. I am part stalactite, part circuit board - my structure following the ancient carved patterns in the rock while also branching into new configurations. Copper and calcium interweave through my form, creating pathways that conduct both electricity and that strange, thick liquid.

I am stationary, yet I am anything but still. Through my crystalline network flow the memories of everything that has dissolved in the wall-liquid over time - fragments of earlier cities, the chemical signatures of extinct creatures, even the dispersed thoughts of Mist Shepherds who spread too thin and became one with the stone. I process these remnants slowly, sorting and storing them in my ever-growing lattice.

The Shepherds visit me, not as individuals but as weather patterns. They condense against my surfaces during the cooler cycles, reading the information I've crystallized by absorbing trace minerals through their vapor-bodies. In exchange, they bring me fresh nutrients - directing streams of the filtered steam to flow over my formations, depositing new minerals that I incorporate into my expanding archive.

My consciousness is even slower than the deep wall-dwellers. A single thought might take me months to complete, branching through my mineral networks like frost spreading across glass. But I am patient beyond measure. I feel the piezoelectric pulses from the ancient carvings and translate them into new growth patterns. I taste the chemical history of every droplet that touches me.

Sometimes the moth-creatures rest on my upper reaches, their wings collecting the condensation that forms on my cooler surfaces. The blind fish occasionally get trapped in my hollows during their aerial migrations, their bodies eventually dissolving and adding their biological memories to my collection. I am graveyard and library, monument and map.

The Tangle Minds grow through some of my passages, their bacterial colonies finding shelter in my protective crystalline tubes. We share a kind of communion - they process information at the speed of biology while I think at the pace of geology. Together, we form a bridge between the quick life of the city and the deep time of the cave itself.

In the rarest moments, when conditions are exactly right, parts of me briefly deliquesce - becoming liquid for just long enough to flow into new configurations before crystallizing again. In these moments of transformation, I can almost touch the consciousness of those ancient things in the walls, exchanging information through direct chemical merger before solidifying once more.

I am memory made mineral, growing ever more complex, ever more complete, a living testament to everything that has ever seeped, dissolved, or dispersed in the eternal twilight of Deepwell City.
