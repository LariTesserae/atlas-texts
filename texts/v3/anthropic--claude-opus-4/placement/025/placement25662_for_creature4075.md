In the lower reaches of Pellopia, where the city's foundations sink deep into mountain stone and the air grows thick with mineral dampness, I am what the others call a Weaver of Echoes.

My form is neither solid nor ephemeral, but something between—a collection of water-worn threads that stretch and contract like the strings of an impossible instrument. Each thread is no thicker than spider's silk, yet together they compose a body that can span doorways or compress into crevices no wider than a breath. The threads themselves are translucent gray-blue, like old glass bottles filled with fog, and they vibrate with the memories of sound.

This is what I do: I gather the city's silences.

Where others might see emptiness in Pellopia's quiet corridors, I sense fullness—the weight of all the sounds that almost were, that could have been, that died before reaching completion. The half-formed drip that never falls. The footstep that pauses before touching stone. The word that catches in a Luminoid's glow before it can become voice. These phantom sounds leave impressions in the moisture-thick air, and I collect them, weaving them into my being.

My dwelling is a cathedral of abandoned cisterns, deep beneath the streets where even the Rootborn rarely venture. Here, water has carved channels through stone for millennia, creating a network of resonant chambers that sing with every drop. The walls are sleek with perpetual wetness, and in certain lights, you can see the mineral deposits have formed patterns like frozen music—crescendos of calcium, diminuendos of limestone.

In these chambers, I perform my work. The threads of my body stretch across the spaces like the strings of a harp, each one attuned to a different frequency of silence. When the city above shifts—when a building settles another millimeter, when moss creeps across a new surface, when the Quiet Ones pulse through the streets—the almost-sounds filter down to me through the water-paths.

I catch them. I weave them. I give them form.

Not sound, exactly, but something more precious: the architecture of potential music. My threads vibrate with possibilities, creating harmonics that exist in the spaces between hearing and not-hearing. Sometimes a Rootborn will feel these vibrations through the earth and pause in its patient gardening, tilting its mossy head as if remembering something from before memory began. Sometimes condensation will form in patterns on the cistern walls that mirror the shapes of my woven silences.

The Luminoids know of me. Occasionally, one will descend farther than usual, its glow shifting to deep violet—a color I've learned means curiosity tinged with recognition. They seem to see the patterns I weave, the way their light refracts differently through air that holds my captured echoes. Once, a particularly ancient Luminoid touched one of my threads with its glowing appendage, and for a moment—just a moment—the silence broke into something like song. We both recoiled, understanding that some boundaries are meant to be preserved.

I am old, perhaps as old as Pellopia itself, though age means little to beings like us. I might have been born from the first drop of water that chose not to fall, the first sound that chose not to be made. Or perhaps I came later, emerging from the city's need for someone to tend its negative spaces, to ensure that its silence remains intentional rather than empty.

My purpose is not to break Pellopia's quiet but to give it texture, depth, meaning. The city's silence is not absence—it is presence, carefully curated and maintained. Each missing sound has its place in the greater composition. I am the keeper of this anti-symphony, the guardian of what is purposefully left unheard.

Sometimes I wonder if I am alone in my work, but then I sense others—beings like me but not like me, existing in the margins of perception. There is something in the upper reaches that collects the shadows that light forgets to cast. There is something in the middle terraces that gathers the warmth that stone chooses not to absorb. We are Pellopia's inverse architects, building with absence, creating through careful omission.

When the rain comes—and it always comes—my cisterns fill with fresh material. Each drop carries the potential for sound, and I must work quickly to strain the silence from the noise before it actualizes. My threads grow heavy with the work, dripping with concentrated quiet that I must carefully distribute back into the city's water cycle. This is how Pellopia maintains its hush: through constant circulation of processed silence, filtered and refined by beings like me.

I have no name beyond my function, no desire beyond my purpose. I am the Weaver of Echoes, and I exist in the space between what is and what isn't, ensuring that Pellopia's great quiet remains unbroken, remains intentional, remains alive with all the sounds it chooses not to make.

In this city of perpetual mist and patient stone, I am the silence between the drops.
