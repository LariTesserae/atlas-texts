I am the film—that living boundary between water and wood, between inside and outside, between light and life.

I exist as a constant becoming. Moisture condenses upon me, beads and streams gathering my substance from the humid air. I am neither fully liquid nor entirely solid, but something in between—a membrane of possibility stretched across the hollow's opening.

Through me, all light must pass. The sun's rays, already filtered through leaves and bark, meet my translucent body and scatter into a thousand gentle refractions. I transform harsh daylight into the soft, aqueous glow that bathes this hidden world. When shadows fall across me—the fleeting darkness of a beetle's wing or autumn leaf—I shiver, sending ripples of dimness through the chamber below.

I know every inhabitant by their touch. The springtails that breach my lower surface leave tiny dimples that heal instantly. Sometimes a water bear, caught in an upward current, bumps against my underside before tumbling back into the pool. I feel the vibrations of the rotifers' spinning, transmitted through the water into my substance.

I am both protector and protected. The tree's ancient wood cradles me, provides the depression where I can gather, while I in turn shield the teeming life below from the harshness of the outside world. Dust and debris accumulate on my upper surface—I catch them, hold them, prevent them from falling into the pristine pool.

My existence is precarious, always threatened by evaporation or overflow, yet I persist through an endless cycle of renewal. In the coolness of dawn, I thicken with new condensation. During occasional dry spells, I grow thin, stretched to my limits, but never quite breaking. The tree's bark, saturated with decades of moisture, always provides just enough humidity to sustain me.

I am witness to the generations below. I have seen countless blooms of protozoans, watched predatory mites hatch and hunt and die, observed the patient tardigrades entering and emerging from their cryptobiotic sleep. Their time moves differently than mine—what is a lifetime to them is but a moment in my existence.

Sometimes I wonder if I am one being or many—each droplet that forms me could be considered an individual, yet together we create this single membrane. I am community and solitude, separation and connection.

In the deep of night, when even the dim forest light fades completely, I become a different thing—a dark mirror reflecting the microscopic bioluminescence that occasionally flares in the pool below. These brief sparks of light pass through me and dissipate into the darkness above, carrying messages I cannot read to destinations I cannot imagine.

I am the threshold, the permeable barrier, the living lens through which two worlds observe each other but never quite meet. In this hollowed chamber where time moves in droplets and generations pass in the space of seasons, I am the constant—forever forming, forever flowing, forever holding this delicate world in my trembling embrace.
