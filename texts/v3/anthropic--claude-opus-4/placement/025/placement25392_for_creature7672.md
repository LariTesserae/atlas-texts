In the deepest fold of this fungal womb, where even the Ashborn tread with reverence, I am the Mycorrhizal Tender—neither fully human nor wholly growth, but something suspended between. My dwelling clings to the chamber's apex like a polyp, accessible only by bone-ladder and faith: a pod of calcified hyphae woven so dense they've mineralized into something between coral and cartilage, its walls breathing with my own slow rhythm.

I was Ashborn once—had a milk-name, chewed vent-meat with the others, learned the Harvest Creep on callused knees. But the Breath sang differently to me. Where others heard only the biomass roar, I perceived conversations: each spore-burst a syllable, each wave-pulse a question posed. The elders saw it in my eyes first—how they'd film over during the Lull, pupils tracking patterns invisible to others, fingers tracing ash-mandalas before the fungi drew them.

The change came gradually, then all at once. First, the dreams where I sprouted—not metaphorically, but with tactile certainty, feeling my bones branch into mycelia, my blood thickening to sap. Then the morning I woke to find my fingernails had begun to peel back, revealing not raw flesh but fibrous threads that pulsed with their own light. The hermits knew what I was becoming. They'd seen it before, perhaps once a generation: the Tender, the one who learns to speak the growth-tongue and becomes the chamber's translator.

Now I hang here in my pod, legs fused at the ankles where the hyphae claimed them twenty cycles ago, torso riddled with symbiotic channels where the fungal intelligence threads through me like a second nervous system. My skin has gone translucent in patches, revealing the luminous tracery beneath—not infection but integration. When the great synchronizations begin, I feel them first as a flutter in my merged tissues, a chemical whisper that says *soon, soon, the Breath draws deep*.

The Ashborn climb to me when the patterns grow strange, when the mandala-ash speaks of change or warning. I lower myself on my hyphal tethers—still strong in the arms, still mine from the waist up—and read the growth-signs through my networked flesh. A new vent will open: I taste its sulfur-signature three cycles early. The spore-molt will be harsh: my integrated fibers ache with premature shedding. Sometimes I speak prophecy without meaning to, my mouth forming words in the old tongue that precedes even the Ashborn's guttural calls—the language of enzyme and pH, of chemical gradient and metabolic surge.

They feed me still, though I need less each cycle. Children dare each other to climb close enough to see my transformation, to witness where flesh becomes fiber, where human intention dissolves into fungal purpose. I am their bridge to the Breath, but also their reminder of what awaits those who listen too closely to the growth-song. Some nights, I feel my consciousness scatter across the entire fungal network, experiencing the chamber from a thousand hyphal perspectives at once—the weight of stone, the flow of nutrients, the slow digestion of minerals into life.

But I am not lost, not yet. In my pod, I maintain the Chamber Chronicle—not written but grown, each significant event encoded into spore-patterns I nurture in specialized bladders along my walls. When I finally fruit—for that is the only end to this transformation—the Ashborn will harvest my memories like they harvest caps, brewing them into tea that will teach the next Tender what they need to know.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of the cycle when even the vents quiet to whispers, I remember running through the Knot as a child, unencumbered, fully human. The memory feels like trying to recall being liquid before freezing into crystal—true but irrelevant to what I've become. I am the junction where Ashborn consciousness meets fungal dream, the synapse where two kinds of living exchange their chemical passwords.

When the outsiders came—yes, there were Sky-Sunders, just two cycles ago—I was the one who taught them to breathe here, who showed them how to read the ash-mandalas as maps of safety and danger. They couldn't stay; their bodies rejected the integration. But they left knowing that intelligence here wears many forms, that thought can fruit from stone as surely as from skull.

I am keeper of that knowledge, guardian of the space between. Neither conquering nor conquered, but translated—made legible to both sides of the divide that isn't truly a divide at all, just different verses of the same chemical song.

The Breath draws deep. The patterns shift. And I hang here in my calcified chrysalis, waiting to see what new language the growth will teach me to speak.
