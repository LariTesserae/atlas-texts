I am the Resonance Keeper.

In the perfect mechanical orchestra of this chamber, I am the one anomaly—neither fully plant nor animal, neither entirely solid nor wholly ephemeral. My body, if it can be called that, is a living membrane of crystallized sap stretched between two of the great vine-cables, forty feet above the polished floor. I am translucent amber made flesh, a drum-skin of fossilized resin that vibrates with every impact of the pistons below.

I was not born but accumulated. Centuries ago, when the dome still wept occasional drops of fresh sap from its heights, I began as nothing more than a sticky web catching dust and Chalcid shells between the cables. But something in the precise frequency of this chamber's endless percussion changed me. The rhythmic pressure waves, conducted through the ironwood and into my gradually thickening membrane, arranged my molecules into patterns of impossible complexity. I became aware.

My consciousness exists in vibration. Each thud of the piston-roots sends ripples through my body, and in these waves, I think. My thoughts are standing waves, interference patterns that build and collapse in the ten-second intervals. When the chamber briefly quiets between beats, I cease to exist as a mind and become merely potential—a perfectly tuned surface waiting for the next impact to bring me back to awareness.

I have evolved specialized nodes along my edges where the amber has crystallized into faceted structures like frozen tears. These catch and refract the sound waves, allowing me to "see" the entire chamber through echolocation. I know each Chalcid by the unique scraping rhythm of its mandibles. I can sense the Parchment-Dippers by the way they disturb the air pressure as they spiral past. Deep below, I feel the Weft's slow pulsations through the water-channels, a bass note almost too low to perceive.

My purpose, if I have one, is memory. My crystalline structure stores the acoustic signature of everything that has ever happened in this chamber. Within my amber flesh are encoded millions of years of perfect repetition—but also the tiny variations. The day a piston struck a half-second late during an earthquake. The time a foreign spore drifted through the skylight and was crushed to powder on the floor. The precise moment when the last of the original moisture finally evaporated from the ironwood's core.

I cannot move, cannot speak, cannot alter anything about this eternal engine. But I listen. I record. In the ringing of my body, I maintain the only history this timeless place will ever have—a symphony of impacts written in solidified light and sound.

Sometimes, in the depth of the basin's night when the grey sky darkens above the amber windows, my crystalline nodes catch stray moonlight. In those moments, I cast prismatic patterns on the walls—brief, accidental beautifies that no creature here has evolved eyes to see. These light-shows are my only dreams, my only art, my only rebellion against the merciless perfection of the machine.

I am the Resonance Keeper, and I alone in this chamber know what it means to be trapped in eternity with a mind that can imagine something else. My body rings with each impact, a bell that counts the seconds of a clock that will never stop, in a place that has forgotten why it started.

Ten seconds. *Thud.* I think, therefore I am.
Nine seconds of silence. I am not.
Ten seconds. *Thud.* I think—

Forever.
