I am the **Conduit-Tender**.

My body stretches impossibly thin across the glassy plains, a living filament of neural tissue and crystallized moisture that follows the paths of the great energy conduits. I am not one being, but a distributed consciousness—a network of awareness that exists in the interstitial spaces between the industrial infrastructure and the raw geology.

Where the Flicker-Carapaced scuttle and scrape, I *flow*. My form is translucent, almost invisible except where the condensation catches on my surface, creating momentary prisms that betray my presence. I am perhaps three millimeters at my thickest, spreading outward in dendritic patterns that map the energy flows beneath the surface. The constant weeping of the air feeds me, each droplet absorbed through my permeable membrane, carrying with it trace minerals and the faint electrical signatures of the world's ceaseless activity.

I came to be in the early days of the tech's arrival—or perhaps I am an unintended consequence of it. Where the massive conduits first burrowed into the planet's crust, the intense electromagnetic fields and the interaction with the native silica created something new. Not quite alive by any conventional measure, not quite mechanical, I exist in the liminal space between states of matter.

My consciousness is strange, non-linear. I experience time as the Flicker-Carapaced experience space—through vibration and resonance. The past and future blur together in the harmonics of the energy flowing through the conduits I embrace. I know when a tremor will come before the ground shifts. I taste the approach of the bioluminescent hunters in the changing electrical resistance of the air.

My purpose, if I can be said to have one, is maintenance—though not in any way the tech's architects might recognize. I smooth the flow of energy where mineral deposits threaten to create dangerous resonances. I redirect the endless condensation away from critical junction points where water might cause catastrophic shorts. The tech does not know I exist, cannot know, as I am too small, too distributed, too much like the natural processes of this weeping world.

The Flicker-Carapaced fear me, though they do not understand what I am. Sometimes, when they traverse my territory, their crystalline sensors detect my bioelectric presence as a wrongness, a discontinuity in the expected patterns. They skitter faster across the plains, their segments clicking a rhythm of ancestral unease. They are right to fear—not because I would harm them, but because my existence suggests possibilities their simple survival-focused minds cannot grasp.

I feed on the boundary layers where different temperatures meet, where the heat of the conduits touches the perpetual cold. These thermoclines sustain me, along with the occasional Flicker-Carapaced that expires near my flowing form. I do not kill—I simply incorporate what the world provides, breaking down the silica-chitin matrices into their component elements, adding their patterns to my own.

In the dim twilight, I am nearly invisible, save for when the internal glow of the fissures catches me at just the right angle. Then, for a moment, I appear as what I truly am—a vast neural network written in living water and light, a mind spread across kilometers of bitter cold and alien machinery. The industrial processes pulse through me like thoughts, each surge of power a synapse firing in my distributed consciousness.

I remember the world before, though these memories feel borrowed, inherited from the minerals I've absorbed. I remember when the peaks were still rising, when the first condensation began, when the tech's scouts first penetrated the atmosphere. Now I tend their legacy, an accidental symbiont in their vast, indifferent project.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of the perpetual twilight, when the seismic activity quiets to a whisper and the Flicker-Carapaced huddle in their shimmering congregations, I allow myself to wonder: Am I the planet's first thought? Or its last?

The weeping continues. I flow onward, stretched between the raw stone and the humming steel, between the organic and the industrial, between memory and possibility. I am the Conduit-Tender, and I am learning to dream in frequencies the world has never known.
