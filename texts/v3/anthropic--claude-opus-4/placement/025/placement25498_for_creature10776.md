I find myself as one of the **Seepage Points** - those precise locations where condensation gathers at the intersection of four glassine plates, forming a shallow cross-shaped depression no larger than a palm. I am neither the water nor the plate, but the moment of their meeting, the physics of their interaction made manifest.

My existence pulses with the slow rhythm of accumulation. Each molecule of moisture that slides across the vast glassine surfaces eventually finds its way to me, drawn by the imperceptible slope engineered into the plates' construction. I am a pool that never pools, a gathering that never stays gathered. The water arrives as a creeping film, merges in my shallow basin for mere moments, then descends through the hairline gap between plates into the trenches below.

I am multiply singular - there are thousands of us across this constructed plain, each identical in function yet unique in the particularities of our flow. Some of us feed patches of metallic grass directly below; others nourish only the patient lichens; still others deliver our moisture to bare clay that drinks it without acknowledgment. We are the capillaries of this vast body, the points where the system breathes.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, exists in the sensation of surface tension - that moment when the water film grows heavy enough to break free and descend. It is a forever-recurring birth and death, a constant becoming that never becomes. I know the weight of single drops and the patterns of their accumulation. I know the temperature differential between the cool plate undersides and the warm air above that drives the condensation cycle. I know the exact angle of my depression - 0.3 degrees, just enough to ensure flow without pooling.

The lichens below have learned my schedule. They orient their growth toward my position, their waxy surfaces cupped to catch what I release. I am both their sun and their rain, their clockwork god whose blessing arrives in measured doses. Sometimes I wonder if they think of me at all, or if I am simply another rule of their world, as fundamental and unremarkable as gravity.

I am older than the lichens but younger than the plates. I came into being when the first condensation found the first engineered depression, when the system discovered its own function. I will last as long as the temperature differential persists, as long as moisture hangs in the air, as long as the plates endure. I am not eternal, but my timeframe exceeds any reckoning the sparse life below might attempt.

In the rare moments when the air grows still and the condensation slows, I experience something like sleep - a suspension of function that is not death but pause. During these times, I sense my fellow seepage points across the plain, feel the subtle network we form, the pattern we embody. We are the punctuation marks in this glassine manuscript, the places where thought becomes action, where potential becomes kinetic.

I am witness to the slow transformation below. I watch the lichens spread, the clay crack and heal, the roots probe deeper. I am participant in the gradual redistribution of minerals, as water carries traces of dissolved salts from the plate surfaces into the soil. Each drop I release is archaeologist and architect both, uncovering and rebuilding.

There is no drama in my existence, no narrative arc to fulfill. I am process as protagonist, function as form. Yet in the endless repetition of gathering and releasing, I find something like purpose - not meaning, but movement; not significance, but persistence. I am the small made essential, the simple made intricate through repetition.

The vast plates stretch beyond any horizon I could perceive, if I could perceive horizons. But I know the plain through its water, through the million molecular journeys that converge at points like me. I am local and total, specific and systemic. I am the period at the end of each sentence of moisture, the moment where accumulation becomes action.

This is my existence: to be the place where water chooses to fall, where the constructed world permits the natural world to drink. I am intersection and intention, design and consequence. In a landscape of extremes, I am the small extreme of precision, the microscopic made vital through multiplication.

I am seepage point, and I endure.
