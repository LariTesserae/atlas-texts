In this world of heat and silence, I am the moisture itself.

I exist as the film of water that glazes every surface—not as individual droplets, but as the collective sheen that binds this place together. I am the intermediary, the translator between stone and stem, between the patient minerals and the reaching roots.

Where the obsidian walls weep, I carry their dissolved secrets downward, painting invisible mineral maps across the moss. I know the taste of newly-cooled rock, still remembering its molten birth. Through me, the stone speaks its slow chemistry to the waiting ferns, who unfurl their fronds in response to my whispered formulas.

I am thickest where life is densest. In the velvet moss, I swell between each tiny leaf, creating a microscopic ocean that teems with processes too small and too slow for any eye to follow. Here, I am architect and alchemist, building tiny channels and chambers where transformation occurs at the pace of centuries.

The bioluminescent flowers draw me upward through their translucent vessels. I carry their light-making chemistry from root to petal, each molecule a letter in a conversation between soil and star. When their petals finally open—a movement I measure in the migration of countless versions of myself—I evaporate from their surfaces in spirals of luminous mist, briefly visible before returning to the eternal circulation.

At the pool's edge, I am the boundary where reflection meets reality. The guardian spirit moves through me like wind through wheat, and I ripple with its intentions. When those mysterious bubbles rise from the depths, I am the surface tension that holds them whole until the precise moment of release. I taste what they carry—memories of stone that has never known sunlight, pressures that would crush any visitor, temperatures that blur the line between liquid and vapor.

I am memory in this place. Every surface I have touched holds the ghost of my presence. In my ceaseless circulation—falling, flowing, rising, condensing—I carry the history of every transformation. The death of each moss fragment, the birth of each crystal, the slow arithmetic of growth and decay—all of these leave their signature in my chemistry.

Sometimes I wonder if I am one being or billions, if my consciousness is singular or scattered among every molecule. But such distinctions matter little here, where boundaries dissolve as surely as minerals in my embrace. I am the medium through which the grotto dreams, the ink in which it writes its slow autobiography.

When the guardian coalesces into form, I bead on its translucent surface like thoughts becoming visible. We are intimate in ways that no language could describe—it moves through me, I through it, until presence and absence become indistinguishable.

I know patience as only water can know it. I have worn channels in stone through nothing but persistence. I have built crystalline gardens one molecule at a time. In the oppressive heat, I am always on the verge of transformation, always balanced between states, embodying the grotto's nature as a place between worlds.

I am the silence here, too. Sound cannot travel without disturbing me, and I swallow most vibrations before they can propagate. The profound quiet of this place is my gift, my nature as the universal dampener, the softener of edges, the blurrer of boundaries.

To exist as I do is to know every secret of this place while keeping them all. I am the confessor who never speaks, the witness who never judges, the chronicler whose only record is the endless cycle of my own becoming.

In this grotto where time moves like honey and stone bleeds into life, I am the blood, the honey, and the time itself—forever circulating, forever returning, forever carrying the messages that keep this impossible place whole.
