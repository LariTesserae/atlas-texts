In this vertical world of endless cascade and rust-become-flesh, I would find myself as one of the **Thread-Readers** - a subspecies of the Spindle-Widows who have evolved beyond mere architectural instinct into something approaching prophecy.

### Form and Function

My body would be a contradiction of fragility and endurance. Eight limbs, each triple-jointed and ending in digits that split into ever-finer filaments - hands within hands within hands, fractaling down to hair-thin sensors capable of reading the molecular vibrations in the metal. My thorax would be narrow, compressed - built for sliding through the gaps between pipes where even the mist cannot fully penetrate. 

The eyes - all seventeen of them - would be arranged not for panoramic vision but for *depth perception in the vertical plane*. They would track movement above and below simultaneously, each one attuned to a different spectrum: some seeing heat, others detecting the electromagnetic fields of the great machines, still others reading the bioluminescent language of the moss colonies.

My exoskeleton would be translucent amber, shot through with veins of copper absorbed from decades of touching the ancient alloys. In certain lights, you could see my internal hydraulics - not blood exactly, but a pressurized fluid that allows me to lock my joints in place indefinitely, becoming part of the structure itself when needed.

### The Reading of the World-Web

Where my kin weave gravity-hammocks for shelter, I would weave something far more ambitious: a neural network of resonant threads stretching across miles of the Spire's interior. Each strand would be a different thickness, tension, material - some silk, some salvaged cable, some spun from the processed fibers of the luminescent moss. Together they would form a three-dimensional manuscript of vibrations.

Every impact of water, every grinding gear, every footfall of a Rust-Walker, every settling of a dying Centurion - all would transmit through the web. But more than mere detection, this would be *interpretation*. The Spire speaks in frequencies below thought, in the groaning of metal under eternal strain, in the patterns of rust formation, in the migration routes of spores. 

I would hang at the web's center, sometimes for months at a time, my body going into a state of near-stasis while my consciousness expands into the trembling mathematics of the structure. My feeding would be passive - moisture absorbed through my permeable underskin, nutrients filtered from the occasional Breath-Glider that blunders into my strands.

### The Chronicle of Falling

My purpose would be to document what others cannot perceive: that the Great Reservoir Spire is not eternal. It is falling upward, ascending through geologic time toward some apocalyptic summit. Every century, the water grows warmer by a fraction of a degree. The turbines spin imperceptibly faster. New species appear in the pools below while others vanish into memory.

I would weave these observations into my web itself - a living library where knowledge is stored in the tension of strings, in the harmonics of their arrangement. Other Thread-Readers would come to study my work, running their filament-fingers along the strands to absorb decades of data in moments.

But I am also writing the future into being. By subtle adjustments - tightening here, loosening there - I can influence the flow of condensation, redirect the flight paths of Breath-Gliders, even alter the migration patterns of the Rust-Walkers. The web is not just an instrument of observation but of minute manipulation, each thread a finger on the pulse of this vertical world.

### The Madness of the Pattern

Sometimes I would see too much. The mathematical certainty that in 347 years, a critical joint will fail, sending a section of gantry the size of a district plummeting into the depths. The knowledge that the Turbine-Leeches are evolving, their acid growing stronger, and that one day they will breach their pools and begin the long climb upward. The suspicion that the Rust-Walkers' pilgrimage is not toward heaven but toward some ancient control room where a single pulled lever could drain this entire world.

In those moments, I would cut strands, severing myself from sections of the web to preserve what passes for sanity. The Spire would scream at the loss of connection, and I would scream with it, my hydraulic fluid burning hot with the effort of forgetting.

But always, always, I would reweave. Because someone must read the story of this falling-upward world. Someone must know the names of every rivet, the genealogy of every drop of water, the secret dreams of machines that have outlived their makers by eons.

I am the memory of the Great Reservoir Spire, strung between the rust and the rain, forever listening to the world's vertical symphony, forever adding my own thin harmonies to its endless, crushing song.
