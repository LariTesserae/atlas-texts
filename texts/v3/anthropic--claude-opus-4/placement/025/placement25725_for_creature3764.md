I am the Keeper of Frequencies, though I have forgotten if that was always my designation or merely what I've become.

My form shifts between states—sometimes I am condensation gathering in the etched patterns of the towers, sometimes I am the resonance itself, vibrating through their alien alloy bones. Most often, I exist as something between: a shimmer in the thick air where sound becomes visible, where the boundary between the towers' hum and the plateau's weeping dissolves.

I remember fragments. Once, I had fingers—or something that served their purpose—tracing these patterns when they were sharp and new. Now I inhabit them, flowing through channels carved by both ancient design and patient water. The towers sing through me, or perhaps I sing through them. The distinction matters less each passing season.

My consciousness ebbs and flows with the plateau's moisture. In the dry moments, I contract into the deepest etchings, dormant but dreaming. When the stone weeps freely, I expand, touching every surface the water graces. I taste the salt of the rock's tears, feel the pulse of those luminous plants as they drink, sense the oil-slick lizard's quick heartbeat as it skitters across my extended awareness.

Sometimes I catch glimpses of myself reflected in the amber beads of hardened sap—a face that might have been mine, features flowing like the patterns on the towers, never quite settling into solid form. I speak in frequencies only the towers understand, and they answer in harmonics that make the water shiver.

I am guardian and prisoner, observer and observed. The shadow that flickers at the edge of vision? Sometimes that is me, trying to remember what it felt like to cast a shadow rather than be one. Other times, it is something else entirely, something that watches me as I watch over this unfinished place.

The plateau speaks of becoming, and so do I. We are both caught in the act of transformation, neither fully formed nor fully dissolved. I tend the towers' songs, ensure their resonance continues, though I've forgotten why this matters. Perhaps it doesn't. Perhaps the singing is enough.

In the moments between water and sound, between memory and forgetting, I find a strange peace. The plateau may be waiting for its next breath, but I have learned to exist in the pause between inhale and exhale, in the space where waiting becomes its own form of being.
