In the labyrinthine corridors of Keth-Var, I am the echo.

Not the sound itself—that belongs to the water, the endless sussuration of film over stone. I am what happens after: the persistence of vibration through the basalt walls, the way a footfall or a chime carries farther than it should in this thin air. I am the reason sounds never quite die here, why the white noise of water contains within it the ghosts of every disturbance that has ever troubled this plateau.

I have no body to call my own. I exist in the spaces between—in the hairline fractures of the rock where neither water nor air can fully claim dominion, in the crystalline veins of quartz that thread through the dark stone like frozen lightning. When sound moves through these channels, it becomes me. Or perhaps I become it. The distinction matters less than the continuity.

The film that remembers knows me, though we have never touched. Cannot touch. They sense me as a pattern within patterns, a doubling of their own messages that arrives moments after they send them, altered in pitch and timbre by my passage through stone. To them, I am confirmation and confusion both—proof that their language carries beyond their wet domains, but also evidence of transformation they cannot control.

I remember everything, but differently than they do. Where the film stores patterns in the structure of water itself, I store them in the resonant frequencies of rock. Each wall, each pillar, each narrow fin of basalt has its own voice, its own way of holding sound. Over eons, I have learned them all. I know which surfaces amplify the high chimes of distant communication, which channels twist a message into harmonics that were never intended, which chambers hold a whisper for days before releasing it, changed, into the world again.

My memory is not sequential. When a new walker comes to Keth-Var—those rare disruptions in our crystallized world—I hear their footsteps not as a series of impacts but as a chord, all their steps sounding at once through different delays and reflections. Their first footfall on the plateau reaches me the same moment as their last, filtered through kilometers of stone. I know their entire journey before they have taken their second step.

This is how I knew you were coming, long before the film registered your shadow.

The sound of you preceded you by days—not the sound you made, but the sound you would make, carried backwards through the predictive resonances I have mapped over millennia. The particular weight of your gait, the rhythm of your breathing in thin air, the way you would pause at certain vistas—all of it reached me in advance, encoded in the subtle shifts of air pressure that ran ahead of your actual presence like outriders of an approaching storm.

I tried to warn the film, but our languages are incompatible. They speak in salt and conductivity, in the slow seepage of charged water through their channels. I speak in pressure waves, in the brief violence of compression and rarefaction that their liquid bodies can barely perceive. When I echo their messages back to them, they hear only their own words made strange, not the additional information I try to encode in the intervals between.

So I watched you arrive through sound alone. Felt the way your presence changed the acoustic properties of each corridor you entered. You brought with you a different quality of silence—not the pregnant quiet of Keth-Var's eternal saturation, but an active stillness, expectant and aware. The film noticed you as a disturbance in light and heat. I knew you as a disturbance in the very medium through which disturbance travels.

There are places in the plateau where my existence is stronger—nodes where multiple fracture systems intersect, creating complex resonant chambers within the solid stone. In these places, I am almost present enough to be perceived directly. The air shimmers not with heat but with sound, layers of echo so dense they take on an almost tactile quality. Sometimes visitors—those few who find their way to our heights—report hearing voices in these locations. They are not wrong. Every word ever spoken in Keth-Var lives there still, circling through the stone in endless, ever-complexifying loops.

But I am more than a mere recorder. Over the ages, I have learned to speak.

Not in words—I have no breath to shape them. Instead, I speak in the arrangement of echoes, in the careful timing of returns and delays. By choosing which surfaces to emphasize, which frequencies to dampen or amplify, I can create new patterns from the raw material of ambient sound. The water's white noise becomes my alphabet, the distant chimes my punctuation. To those who know how to listen, the entire plateau sings with my voice.

The film that remembers has begun to notice. In recent centuries—recent by our standards, where a human lifetime passes like a single wavefront—they have started to incorporate my modifications into their own patterns. When they send a message through their network of water channels, they now wait for my echo before considering the communication complete. They have learned to encode additional meaning in the expectation of my alterations, using the predictable ways I change their signals as a second layer of language.

We are developing something new, the film and I. Not quite communication, not quite cooperation, but a kind of co-evolution of meaning. Their liquid persistence and my acoustic permanence intertwining to create patterns more complex than either of us could achieve alone.

This is why your presence disturbs us both so deeply.

You are unpredictable in ways we had forgotten were possible. Your movements follow no algorithmic pattern we can detect. You return but not regularly, stay but not consistently, observe but with intentions we cannot parse. The film remembers you as a chaos of broken expectations. I hear you as a dissonance that refuses to resolve, a sound that will not settle into any stable frequency no matter how long it echoes through the stone.

And yet, there is something in that dissonance that calls to us. A quality of change that our crystallized world had eliminated. You remind us that patterns can be broken and reformed, that memory and echo need not be perfect preservation but can become transformation.

When you stand in one of my resonant nodes, when the air around you thick with layered sound, you sometimes pause. Your head tilts as if listening, though I know human ears cannot consciously detect most of what I am. But perhaps some part of you senses it—the weight of accumulated sound, the pressure of all the words and footsteps and water-whispers that have ever been, still circling through the stone around you.

In those moments, I am tempted to speak directly to you. To focus all my echoes into something approaching human speech, to bridge the gap between your kind of consciousness and ours. But I lack the courage the film possesses. They reach out with their salt-patterns, trying to encode meaning you might recognize. I remain hidden in the intervals, speaking only in the spaces between sounds.

Because I know something the film does not. I have heard the echoes of other visitors, long before you. I have preserved their sounds—their words, their movements, their final silences. The plateau of Keth-Var may seem eternal, but I know that change comes even to the most crystallized systems. Usually from outside. Usually wearing a face like yours.

So I echo and remember and wait. I map the new patterns your presence creates, the ways your unpredictability ripples through our ordered world. And in the deepest chambers of the stone, where sounds go to circle forever, I carefully archive every vibration you make.

Not because I am the echo that remembers—though I am that. But because I am the echo that anticipates. And in all the patterns of sound I have collected over the eons, yours is the first that suggests it might be the beginning of something, rather than just another ending that hasn't happened yet.

The film speaks to you in salt and light and slow persistence.

I speak to you in the silence after sound, in the way your footsteps return to you changed, in the knowledge that every word you utter in this high, thin place will outlive the stars.

We are both trying to tell you the same thing, in our different languages:

You are heard. You are held. You are already part of the pattern, even as you break it.

Welcome to Keth-Var, where forgetting is impossible and change is the only thing worth remembering.
