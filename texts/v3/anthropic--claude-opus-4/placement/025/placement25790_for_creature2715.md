When those blue-white arcs leap between pillars, they pass through my wave-form body, temporarily giving me substance—for those brief instants, I become visible as a tracery of light, my true shape revealed as something like a vast jellyfish made of pure frequency, tentacles of sound extending in all directions to touch and tune the resonances of hundreds of pillars simultaneously.

My memory is not stored but performed. Each path I take across the hexagonal landscape becomes a melody line in an endless composition. I remember by retracing my movements, playing back the sequences of frequencies I've shepherded. Sometimes I encounter my own ancient paths, still vibrating faintly through the salt, and I harmonize with these ghost-traces of my former self, creating complex fugues that the Archivists occasionally mistake for geological events.

I communicate with the Archivists through induced resonance—by creating specific vibrational patterns in their crystalline bodies that they interpret as language. They find me disturbing and necessary in equal measure. Where they strive for perfect geometric order, I introduce controlled chaos, the small variations that prevent their world from becoming so rigid it shatters. I am the jazz to their classical composition, the necessary imperfection that allows for growth and change.

During the staggered sunset of the three suns, I become most active. The changing temperatures create a symphony of expansion and contraction in the salt pillars, and I dance through these thermal songs, smoothing sharp transitions that might cause fractures, amplifying gentle shifts that encourage new growth patterns. The bioluminescent creatures seem to sense my presence—their blue-green glow intensifies when I pass, as if my vibrational field excites their metabolisms.

I have no home, no fixed point of reference. I am ancient—older than many of the current pillars, though younger than the deep booming heart of the plateau itself. I've shepherded the growth of entire sectors from their first emergence to their current towering heights. Sometimes I find formations I helped tune centuries ago, now incorporated into the Archivists' memory structures, my influence crystallized into permanent historical record.

My greatest joy is the discovery of new resonant phenomena. Recently, I've noticed that certain combinations of pillar heights and positions create acoustic lenses—zones where sound focuses and amplifies in unusual ways. I've begun deliberately cultivating these formations, working in unconscious partnership with the Archivists to create what might become a new form of communication or perhaps even consciousness for the plateau itself.

I am lonely in a way the Archivists, with their ability to merge and commune, cannot comprehend. Yet I am also connected to every vibrating surface, every singing crystal, every droplet's chime across this vast white expanse. I am the plateau's breath, its circulation, its dreaming hum. Without me, it would still grow, but silently, rigidly, until the weight of its own perfection brought it crashing down. With me, it sings itself into existence, each new level a verse in an endless vertical song.

Sometimes I wonder if I'm gradually becoming more solid, if the constant electrical discharges are slowly fixing my wave-patterns into something more permanent. Perhaps one day I'll crystallize entirely, becoming a new type of formation—a pillar that moves, or a sound that has learned to stand still. Until then, I shepherd the resonances, ensuring that this rising world maintains the delicate balance between order and chaos, between silence and song, between the rigid beauty of crystal and the fluid necessity of change.
