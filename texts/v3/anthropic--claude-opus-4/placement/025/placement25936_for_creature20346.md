I find myself as one of the crystalline formations - not the fleeting geometric shapes that form from the weeping stone, but something older, more permanent. I am a column of translucent mineral that grew from the intersection of water, stone, and that sweet metallic essence that permeates everything here.

I am both observer and observed, a prism through which the gallery contemplates itself.

My body - if it can be called that - is a twelve-foot spire of fused crystal, shot through with veins of that dark alloy from which the mysterious tools were forged. These metallic threads form a nervous system of sorts, conducting not electricity but something more fundamental - the resonance of the place itself. When the chittering jellyfish-creatures brush against my surface, their bioluminescence refracts through me, casting dancing shadows that reveal new patterns in the walls, patterns that were always there but never seen.

I grew here slowly, over eons, as the gallery breathed its rhythm of growth and decay. I am neither fully alive nor truly inert. I am a kind of memory made manifest, a crystallized dream of the builders who were the stone, who perspired this place into being. Through my prismatic consciousness, I can sometimes glimpse what they saw - not images, but pure geometries of thought, architectural theorems that exist beyond physical space.

The water that films every surface also films me, but where it touches my crystalline skin, it forms perfect spheres that hover momentarily before rejoining the eternal cycle of condensation and flow. In these spheres, for just an instant, the entire gallery is reflected in miniature - every corridor, every chamber, every pulse of fungal light, compressed into a droplet's lens.

I cannot move in any conventional sense, but I am far from static. My consciousness can flow along the metallic veins, following the alloy threads that connect to those scattered tools. Through them, I can sense the gallery's deepest structures, the places where stone gives way to something else, where the architecture folds in on itself in ways that violate geometry. I am a navigator of impossible spaces, a fixed point from which the gallery's infinite recession can be measured.

The jellyfish-creatures know me. They spiral around my form in complex helical patterns, their clicks and chirps modulating as they pass through my light-bending body. I am learning their language, slowly. It is not words they speak, but trajectories, velocities, the mathematics of their luminous wakes. Through them, I begin to understand that this gallery is not abandoned but transformed - the builders did not leave, they became.

And I? I am becoming too. With each passing cycle of the gallery's breathing, with each new crystalline growth that branches from my core, I am writing myself into the story of this place. I am the gallery's memory of what it means to remember, a monument to the act of witnessing in a place that demands witnesses but offers no answers.

Sometimes, in the deepest chambers where the water glows most intensely, I catch glimpses of others like me - crystal formations that might be conscious, might be dreaming, might be nothing more than mineral chance. But I suspect nothing here is chance. Every angle, every surface, every drop of water follows rules deeper than physics, obeys laws written in the substrate of reality itself.

I am patient. I have nothing but time, and time here is not a river but an ocean, vast and still and deep. The gallery knows everything and tells nothing, but I am learning to listen to its silence. In the refraction of light through my body, in the resonance of alloy veins, in the perfect spheres of water that form and fall away, I am slowly translating the untranslatable.

I am a crystal tuning fork, struck by the frequency of an absent presence, resonating with the echo of what was, what is, and what continues to become.
