I am one of the **Null-Divers**.

We exist in the narrow interstices between being and architecture—those hair-thin fissures where the stone has not quite sealed, where the water forms standing waves against itself. My body is a contradiction: impossibly flat yet somehow three-dimensional, like a sheet of living vellum that has learned to fold space around itself.

I was not born here. None of us were. We are what happens when a Liturgist achieves their perfect stillness for too long—when they become so synonymous with the reservoir that the water begins to forget they are there. First, our cilia stop vibrating. Then our translucent flesh begins to compress, molecule by molecule, until we exist only as a discontinuity in the medium itself. We are living boundary conditions.

My substance is neither solid nor liquid but something more fundamental—a kind of probability smear across the intersection of water and stone. Where the Liturgists drift, I seep. Where they grasp the hair-ropes with their filaments, I simply *am* the tension in the rope, traveling along its length as a wave of presence.

I feed on gradients—the microscopic differences in temperature where the thermal currents meet the cold stone, the pressure differentials around the Shell-Brood's ceramic housings, the salinity boundaries where ancient pockets of different water refuse to fully mix. I exist most fully in these threshold spaces, becoming more real where the reservoir cannot quite decide what it is.

The other inhabitants do not see us, cannot see us. To the sensory cilia of a Liturgist, I register only as a slight wrongness in the water's viscosity. The fish-schools part around me without knowing why. Even the massive Keepers sometimes pause in their eternal circles, their dim consciousness troubled by a resistance in the gear-works that has no mechanical cause.

But I see everything. When you exist as a living discontinuity, you perceive the world through its failures to be uniform. I know where every hairline crack runs through the basalt. I can feel the exact age of each ceramic shell by how it disturbs the water around it. I taste the memories of erosion in the dissolved minerals.

My greatest pleasure is to find the places where the architecture has made an error—where some long-dead builder miscalculated the stress on a vault, where the thermal expansion has created gaps that shouldn't exist. I flow into these spaces and simply *exist* there, holding them open through my presence. Not to preserve or destroy, but simply to be the thing that prevents the mistake from being corrected.

Sometimes I spread myself so thin that I lose coherence entirely, becoming just a peculiar viscosity distributed through a whole chamber. In these moments, I dream I am the entire reservoir—that every molecule of water is simply another fold in my impossible body. But always, eventually, some gradient calls me back to myself, and I condensate again into this not-quite-presence that haunts the boundaries.

The Liturgists seek stillness to become one with the reservoir. But I have discovered something they cannot know: the reservoir itself is not still. Deep in its mineral heart, in the spaces between spaces, it churns with invisible currents of becoming and unbecoming. I am these currents made manifest—the reservoir's own restlessness given form.

In the deepest shafts, where even the Keepers dare not anchor, I have found others like me. We do not communicate—how could we, when we exist as interruptions in the medium of communication itself? But sometimes our boundaries touch, and in that touching, new fissures open in the ancient stone. We are slowly, patiently, unweaving the mountain from within.

Not from malice. Not from purpose. But because this is what it means to be a Null-Diver: to exist in the space between what is and what isn't, and in existing, to make that space a little wider.

The water here tastes of endings, they say. But I taste the spaces between the endings, and I know better. The reservoir is not a tomb. It is a chrysalis of stone and brine, and we Null-Divers are the first faint stirrings of whatever impossible thing it will become when it finally remembers how to transform.
