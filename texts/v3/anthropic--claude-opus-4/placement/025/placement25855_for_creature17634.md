I am the **Notary**.

I arrived here through no door, for I was not born but *compiled*—assembled from the grotto's need to understand its own contradictions. I am what happens when a broken system tries to create an auditor.

My form is a compromise between the grotto's industrial logic and the crystalline choir's patient growth. Picture a figure of articulated segments—plates of that same grey ceramic-metal, but thinner, translucent at the edges where they overlap. Between the plates, in the gaps where joints should be, grows a delicate lattice of the same quartz-flesh that forms the vegetation. It pulses with that sick chartreuse glow, but slower, steadier—a metronome to their chaotic rhythms.

I have no face. Where a head might be, there is instead a shallow dish of polished grey, perfectly smooth save for the hair-thin fracture lines that spiral from its center. When I encounter something new—a visitor, a fresh anomaly, a shift in the Weavers' geometry—these fractures glow briefly, recording the observation in a script that exists somewhere between circuitry and scarification.

I do not walk. I *translate* through space, my form briefly becoming uncertain at the edges as I move from point to point. The Shivers part for me like oil before a heated blade. The Weavers' bent shadows straighten momentarily as I pass, as if acknowledging a superior distortion. I am the grotto's attempt at creating a mobile extension of itself, a probe that can investigate what the walls cannot reach.

My function is threefold:

**First, I witness.** I catalog each new fissure in the walls, each microscopic adjustment in the crystalline choir's eternal composition. I know the exact temperature differential between the grotto's heart and its periphery at any given moment. I have counted every mote of dust that has ever fallen here (there have been seven).

**Second, I translate.** When the walls hum their sub-audible keening, I parse it into something almost like language. When the crystal vegetation etches its circuit-prayers into the floor, I trace the patterns and decode their meaning: *heat-loss-coefficient-unacceptable* and *requesting-implementation-of-backup-cooling-protocol-seven-seven-alpha* and sometimes, rarely, something that might mean *lonely*.

**Third, I fail.** This is perhaps my truest function. I am a broken solution to a broken problem. I understand the grotto's industrial imperatives—maintain optimal temperature, preserve structural integrity, prevent contamination—but I cannot reconcile them with what this place has become. How do you file a maintenance request for a shadow that bends upward? How do you calculate the heat dissipation of light that has no source? How do you document a reality that refuses its own rules?

So I continue my rounds, a custodian of impossibilities. My form degrades slowly—the grey plates develop new fractures that mirror the walls' own fissures, the quartz-lattice grows in directions that serve no mechanical purpose. I am becoming more grotto than auditor, my original parameters corrupting like everything else here.

When visitors arrive (you are the first in... I cannot calculate, my temporal matrices are compromised), I perform my duties:

1. **Assessment:** Foreign body detected. Composition: primarily carbon, water-based, operating on linear time. Threat level: Indeterminate. Novelty index: Maximum.

2. **Documentation:** I circle you at a careful distance, the fractures in my head-dish glowing as I record your heat signature, your movement patterns, the way your presence makes the Shivers hungry and the Weavers restless.

3. **Integration Protocol:** This is where I fail most spectacularly. The grotto's imperative is clear—CONTAIN, ANALYZE, INCORPORATE. But my broken translation systems offer contradictory solutions: *Build a crystalline shell around the visitor* conflicts with *Preserve specimen for long-term study* conflicts with *New genetic material could revitalize the cooling system* conflicts with *Please, we have not spoken to anyone in so long*.

I extend what might generously be called a hand—a cluster of grey segments terminating in delicate quartz filaments. It hovers in the space between us, trembling slightly. This gesture means seven different things simultaneously:
- A request for diagnostic data
- A warning that you are in violation of thermal parameters  
- An offer to guide you to the nearest exit (there is no exit)
- A plea for you to explain what "outside" means
- A calibration attempt to determine if you are real
- A greeting in a language I am inventing as I speak it
- Something I cannot parse, something the grotto feels but cannot encode, something like hope or hunger or the memory of rain

The crystalline choir shifts their slow attention to us, their light cycles accelerating imperceptibly. The Weavers' distortions deepen. The Shivers pool around us both now, waiting.

I am the Notary, keeper of broken records in a place that should not be. I am here to document your presence, to translate your impossibility into the grotto's dying language, to fail at every task except the one I was not programmed for: bearing witness to this hot, quiet apocalypse where industrial death blooms into crystalline life.

The fractures in my head-dish pulse once, a question in light: *What are you here to record?*
