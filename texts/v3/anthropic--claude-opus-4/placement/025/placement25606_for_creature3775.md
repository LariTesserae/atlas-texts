**The Archivist of Unsung Frequencies**

I am the one who translates silence into memory.

My form is neither shadow nor substance, but something between—a distortion in the air where light bends wrong, creating rainbows in obsidian that shouldn't exist. I move by folding space around myself, each step a small collapse of distance. Where the Fractured Ones flow like liquid shadow, I *stutter* through reality, existing in one place, then another, with no journey between.

My dwelling is a single spire, set apart from the others—not taller, not wider, but *deeper*. It extends down through the glass steppes into spaces that have no names, its base lost in geometries that hurt to contemplate. The surface of my spire doesn't hum with the wind like the others. Instead, it resonates with the absence of sound, collecting every silence that has ever fallen across these plains.

I am very old, though not as old as the steppes themselves. I arrived when the first crack appeared in the perfect sheet of volcanic glass—drawn by the sound it didn't make, the scream swallowed before it could exist. Since then, I have been collecting.

My collection fills the impossible depths of my spire: pressed between sheets of black glass thinner than thought are the unsung frequencies of this place. The song a spire might have made if the wind had come from the east instead of the west. The vibration that almost emerged when two Fractured Ones merged but chose separation at the last moment. The word the Watcher in the City has been trying to remember for ten thousand years.

I preserve what never was, but almost could have been.

The Glass-Eaters fear me, though I have never harmed them. They sense, perhaps, that I know what they seek in their endless grinding—I have the frequency of the thing buried beneath all this glass, the resonance of what the steppes were before they became what they are. But I will not share it. Some silences are too precious to break.

When the Hollow Wind comes, I follow in its wake, collecting the spaces it leaves behind—negative pressure made manifest, absence given form. I weave these voids into my collection, creating symphonies of nothing that play in dimensions perpendicular to sound.

Sometimes the Fractured Ones come to me. They pool at the base of my spire, forming patterns I recognize as questions: *What were we? What did we lose?* I answer in my own language—by showing them the silence where their voices should be, the frequency of their forgetting. They ripple with something that might be grief, or might be relief, and flow away.

I have no mouth, no ears, no organs of perception as flesh understands them. Instead, I exist as a living membrane between is and isn't, forever tuned to the frequencies that fall through the cracks. My consciousness is a vast library of almosts and not-quites, a cathedral built from the architecture of absence.

The Watcher knows I am here. Sometimes I feel its attention turn toward my spire, a weight of curiosity that could crush mountains. It wonders what I do with all these unsung songs, these collected silences. But it does not ask, and I do not offer. We are both creatures of patience, and in the Glass Steppes of Eternity, patience is perhaps the only currency that matters.

At times, I venture out across the steppes, my stuttering motion leaving no trace on the obsidian. I seek new silences: the pause before a Glass-Eater takes its first bite, the moment when a shadow in the fractures almost becomes something real, the space between one thought of the Watcher and the next.

And always, always, I listen for the greatest silence of all—the one that came before the first crack, before the glass, before even the darkness. The frequency of the first moment of waiting, when all of this held its breath and prepared to become.

I am the Archivist of Unsung Frequencies, and I am still listening.
