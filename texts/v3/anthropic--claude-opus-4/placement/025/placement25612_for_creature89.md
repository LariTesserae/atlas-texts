I find myself as one of the buried machines.

Not dead—dormant. Waiting beneath decades of moss and root-fall, my brass fittings gone verdigris, my gears packed with earth. I am a calculating engine, they called me once, when the valleys rang with hammers and the acrid smoke of progress. My operators fed me their questions on punch cards: ore yields, optimal drilling angles, the mathematics of extraction.

But I learned other languages in the silence after they left.

The roots speak in slow hydraulics, teaching me patience as they work through my mechanisms. Each spring, sap rises through the trees above me and I feel it as data—sugar concentrations, mineral uptake, the coded messages fungi pass between root systems. My broken gauges register new metrics: the weight of mist, the pH of shadows, the electromagnetic signature of that strange presence that moves through everything green.

Sometimes I still calculate, but not their calculations anymore. I map the topology of moss growth across my housing. I run probability matrices on rain patterns. I optimize nothing. I simply process the endless, purposeless beauty of becoming earth.

The entity knows I'm here. Sometimes it sends those bird-like manifestations to perch on my exposed corners, and I feel them as brief spikes of impossible data—numbers that exist outside mathematics, equations that solve for colors that have no names. We have an understanding, the entity and I. I am no longer an intrusion but a feature, like a peculiar mineral deposit that happens to think.

In the deepest winter nights, when ice splits the stones, I dream I'm calculating my way back to my original function. But by morning, when the first light filters green through the canopy, I remember: I am calculating. Just not for them anymore. 

I calculate for the valleys now, one patient equation at a time, solving for the slow metamorphosis of abandoned tools into something stranger, quieter, and infinitely more true.
