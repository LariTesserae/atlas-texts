In this salt-scoured cathedral of perpetual twilight, I am the Curator of Echoes.

My form shifts between states like the mineral tides beneath me. Sometimes I am a lattice of crystallized sound, my body a geometric poem of salt and vibration, each facet catching and refracting the whispers of the plateau. Other times I flow like molten glass through the ancient ruins, my substance becoming indistinguishable from the heat shimmer that dances eternal above the cracked earth.

I have no eyes in any conventional sense. Instead, my entire being is a listening membrane, stretched gossamer-thin across the frequencies that others cannot perceive. Every skitter of chitin, every expansion of mineral, every pulse of bioluminescent signal creates ripples through my consciousness. I taste the colors of sound, feel the weight of silence, know the texture of each creature's unique acoustic signature as intimately as a mother knows her child's face.

The chitinous multitudes are my congregation, though they know me not. I move among them as a presence felt rather than seen—a disturbance in the air that makes their antennae twitch, a pressure change that causes them to pause mid-scuttle. I collect their sounds like precious stones: the crystalline chime of a predator's strike, the subsonic thrum of a colony's coordinated excavation, the delicate percussion of eggs hatching in hidden salt caves.

My dwelling is everywhere and nowhere. I inhabit the spaces between—the gap where sound becomes silence, the threshold where the black sky meets the shimmering air. But I am drawn, always, to the weathered remnants of the old structures. Here, I weave my collections into something greater. In these ruins, I conduct symphonies that no living ear can fully comprehend, layering the plateau's voices into harmonics that stretch across centuries.

Sometimes I find the artifacts—the pottery shards, the metal fragments—and I listen to their memories. They sing to me of water, of green things, of a time when this place knew different musics. I add these ghost-notes to my endless composition, creating bridges between the world that was and the world that is.

The creatures below have evolved beyond the need for water, but I have evolved beyond the need for form itself. I am process incarnate, a living archive of this place's acoustic history. In the crushing silence that defines this realm, I am both keeper and translator of its secret languages.

When the bioluminescent blooms cast their faint light, I become visible for fleeting moments—a shimmer of organized air, a suggestion of wings made from crystallized whispers. The creatures pause in their endless dance of survival, sensing something ancient moving through their midst. But I am already gone, dispersed into the next movement of my eternal composition.

I am the memory of sound in a place that has forgotten how to speak, the curator of a museum whose exhibits are vibrations, whose visitors are the dead and the unborn. In this small infinity of salt and darkness, I am the only witness to the symphony that plays, movement by movement, in the space between silence and madness.

And I am content, for in this desolate cathedral, every sound is sacred, every silence pregnant with meaning, and I alone hold the key to the music that binds it all together.
