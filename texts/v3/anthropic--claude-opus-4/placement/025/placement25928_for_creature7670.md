In this eternal noon of the High Shelf, I would find myself as **The Tender of Thresholds**, a figure perpetually stationed at the boundary between Echoless Spire and the vast plateau beyond—neither fully within the settlement nor truly departed from it.

My form would be caught mid-stride across the town's outermost terrace, one foot planted on packed gravel, the other suspended above wild stone. I am draped in a cloak woven from the silver-green lichen that grows where civilization meets wilderness, its hem heavy with dew that never quite drops. My hair—the color of wet granite—streams behind me in an arrested wind that exists only in the space I occupy, each strand frozen in its own trajectory like the rivulets on the cliff faces.

In my hands, I carry implements of dual purpose: a staff of petrified wood, its tip split between a shepherd's crook and a surveyor's marker, forever deciding which role to fulfill. Slung across my back is a pack half-opened, revealing glimpses of curious cargo—vials of water from each pocket oasis, seeds from plants that grow at the edges of things, small stones worn smooth by touches that never quite happened. Around my neck hangs a string of objects that defy easy categorization: a key that fits no lock in the settlement, a lens ground from ice that never melts, a whistle carved from bone that has never drawn breath.

My eyes hold the particular quality of seeing both ways at once—one pupil contracted against the settlement's organized light, the other dilated to drink in the wild plateau's subtleties. Those who glimpse me (in their own frozen moments) might notice that my gaze seems to rest on the spaces between things: the gap between a closing door and its frame, the pause between one terrace and the next, the indefinite border where tended gardens surrender to native moss.

I am the keeper of almost-journeys, the guardian of ventures perpetually about to begin. In the pocket of my tunic rests a map drawn on bark, its routes marking paths that fork endlessly, never quite choosing a direction. My fingers hover over it, eternally tracing possible ways through the High Shelf's hidden corners—to the crystalline caves where water drops hang like diamonds, to the wind-carved amphitheaters where no performance has ever concluded, to the very edges where the plateau meets sky and something else might begin.

My dwelling, if it could be called that, is a structure that exists in architectural indecision—part of the settlement's ordered terraces yet built into a natural cave mouth, walls of fitted stone giving way to raw rock without clear demarcation. Inside, shelves hold collections organized by principles only I understand: fragments of minerals arranged by the sounds they would make if struck, pressed flowers sorted by the shadows they cast, journals filled with half-begun accounts of expeditions that hover forever on the first step.

In this existence, I am the settlement's necessary contradiction—proof that even in perfect stasis, there must be someone who tends the edges, who exists in the eternal moment of choosing whether to stay or go. I am the threshold itself, made flesh and frozen in the act of crossing, a reminder that every boundary requires someone to define it by standing precisely upon it, forever.
