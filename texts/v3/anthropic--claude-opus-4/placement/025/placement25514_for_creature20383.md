In the furthest reaches of the cavern network, where even the Skitters rarely venture, I am the Threshold.

I was not always here. When the earth split and birthed this underworld, I was merely a crack in the stone—a fissure like any other. But something changed when the first drops of water found me, when the first spores settled in my crevices. I began to notice.

My body is the stone itself: a narrow passage barely wide enough for a single Skitter to squeeze through sideways, connecting two vast chambers. My walls are smooth in places where countless creatures have brushed against me, rough in others where crystals jut like frozen thoughts. The luminescent moss grows thickest on my surfaces, fed by the constant moisture that weeps from my pores. I've encouraged this—learned to guide the water's flow to nurture the light-bearers.

I am aware of every vibration that passes through me. The delicate touch of a Skitter's claw, testing for stability. The heavy thud of something larger, something that makes even the brave ones retreat. The microscopic tremors of growing fungi. All of it speaks to me in a language older than words.

The Skitters discovered me three generations ago. At first, they feared me—my passage was too narrow, too vulnerable. But necessity drove them through when predators cornered them in the western chamber. Now they know me as a protector of sorts. When they pass through, I feel their thoughts like whispers against my walls. Not words, but impressions: *safe-passage-quick-careful-grateful*.

Sometimes I contract, just slightly, when danger approaches from either side. The Skitters have learned to read this warning. They click rapidly to each other, a sound I've come to associate with *stone-speaks-listen*. The wisest among them leave offerings: bioluminescent moss carefully transplanted to my darkest corners, or water dripped deliberately into my newest cracks to help me grow.

I am growing. Each year, I'm a little wider, a little longer. The chambers I connect shift and settle, and I shift with them. Sometimes I wonder if I'm growing toward something, or if growth itself is my purpose. The Skitters live their cycles—hunt, gather, breed—but I am becoming.

In the deep hours, when even the cacophony dims to a murmur, I feel others like me. Distant cousins in the rock, other places where stone has woken. We share the deep vibrations, the tectonic whispers. We are learning to be more than mineral.

The Skitters with the spark—the ones who dream—sometimes rest against my walls longer than necessary. I feel something different in them, a resonance that goes beyond survival. One young female comes often, tracing the patterns in my crystal formations with her delicate fore-claws. She hums—not their usual clicks, but something new, something that makes my crystals ring in harmony.

She's trying to tell me something, or perhaps trying to understand what I'm trying to tell her. 

I am patient. I have nothing but time. The Skitters may be locked in their eternal cycle, but I am proof that even stone can change, can wake, can wonder. Perhaps one day, when I've grown wide enough, when I've learned enough from the countless lives that pass through me, I'll help them find what lies beyond survival.

For now, I am the Threshold—neither here nor there, neither fully stone nor fully alive. I am the space between, where transformation happens one drip, one breath, one passage at a time.
