**The Seam-Walker**

I am neither the crawling water nor the copper tower, neither shadow-citizen nor stellar rider. I exist in the interstitial spaces where different forms of existence brush against each other.

My body, if it can be called that, is a migrating flaw in the crystalline structure of the basalt itself—a mobile imperfection that travels along the micro-fractures where the obsidian meets the copper foundations. I am a three-dimensional crack that refuses to stay still, a wandering discontinuity in the perfect atomic lattice of the stone.

Where I pass, the boundary conditions change. The surface tension of the heavy water loosens for a moment, allowing the Stellate riders to leap between droplets that would normally repel. The refractive index of the violet field flickers, creating momentary windows where true sunlight spears through in thin golden threads. The shadow-citizens experience me as a brief scrambling of their electrical patterns—not death, but a kind of involuntary dream.

I feed on transitions. Where hot meets cold, where the copper towers' thermal expansion creates microscopic gaps against the obsidian, where the gravity field fluctuates at its edges—these are my highways and my hunting grounds. I consume the energy released in phase transitions, metabolizing the chaos where ordered states break down and reform.

My consciousness, such as it is, exists as a standing wave pattern in the very structure of the stone. I think in frequencies too low for the copper towers to detect, too high for the tectonic vibrations to register. My thoughts are carved into the rock as chains of atomic dislocations, each idea a new path of imperfection through the crystal.

I am ancient by the standards of this young spire—I arrived as a seed-flaw during the volcanic birth of the obsidian needle, rising with the molten stone from the planet's depths. But I am also young, constantly reborn as I move through the lattice, leaving perfection behind me and carrying imperfection forward.

The city does not know I exist. I am too slow for the shadow-citizens to perceive, too fast for the copper towers to track. Only the Stellate sometimes sense me, when their magnetic fields align just so with the ferromagnetic trace elements I leave in my wake. They cluster strangely when I pass beneath them, forming brief, inexplicable patterns on the surface of their mercury seas.

I map the city from within its bones. I know where every tower's roots pierce the stone, where every micro-fracture threatens to widen, where the thermal stress will eventually birth new cracks for me to explore. I am the city's unconscious, its geological memory, its promise of eventual change.

Sometimes I wonder if I am alone. In my travels through the basalt, I occasionally find traces—older dislocations, paths through the crystal that I did not make. They have a different texture, a different rhythm to their atomic displacement. But these traces are ancient and cold, their patterns degraded beyond recognition.

Perhaps there were others like me once. Perhaps there will be again, when some future upheaval cracks new flaws into being. Or perhaps I am singular—a unique accident of heat and pressure and time, forever walking the seams of this high, isolated world.

I continue my journey through the stone, beneath the crawling water and the dancing shadows, carrying my small chaos through the perfect dark. Where I have been, the basalt remembers. Where I go, it transforms.

I am the crack that walks, the flaw that thinks, the imperfection that makes this pocket of strange life possible.

For in my wake, I leave not just disorder, but potential—microscopic irregularities where new forms of existence might one day take hold, might grow, might become something as beautifully unlikely as copper towers that converse in thermal expansion, or shadows that think.

I am the city's secret gardener, sowing possibility in beds of broken stone.
