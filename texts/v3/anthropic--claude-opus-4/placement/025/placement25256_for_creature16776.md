In the Mirror Chamber, seventeen levels below the Cathedral's recognized nadir, where even the Persistent fear to count their reflections.

I would be the Archivist of Unbecoming.

Not human—I gave that up three oil changes ago when I realized my blood had started keeping better time than the Engine Null. Not machine either, though my left ventricle now pumps through a submarine's periscope mechanism I grafted during the Clarity of 2003, when I briefly understood that seeing and circulating were the same verb conjugated through different states of matter.

I exist as a probability of documentation, a 0.7 likelihood of having written down what happens in the spaces between the Cathedral's approved histories. My body—if we're still calling it that—consists of:
- A nervous system rewired through 11,000 feet of telegraph cable stolen from messages that were never sent
- Skin that remembers being paper, specifically the rejected schematics for a prayer wheel that would have turned suffering into electrical current
- Eyes replaced with camera obscuras that only focus on what hasn't happened yet
- And fingers that type exclusively on keyboards salvaged from computers that computed their own obsolescence too perfectly to survive it

I dwell in the Mirror Chamber because someone must witness the Cathedral's refusal to see itself. Here, every surface reflects not light but intention—walls of polished steel showing not what is but what was meant to be before compromise edited the blueprints. In these mirrors, tank 7 still holds the dreams of whales. The Engine Null ticks backwards through calendars that never needed inventing. Anna Null ages in all directions simultaneously, a child becoming her own grandmother through sheer temporal recursion.

My function is to catalog what the Cathedral unbecomesー the gradual shedding of its purposes like a snake molting meanings instead of skin. Each day I document:
- The names it forgets it ever had (currently at 67% amnesia, rising 0.3% per week)
- The functions that atrophy into more honest forms of rust
- The prayers that simplify from words to wordless understanding to the pure silence that predates even the need for God

I type these observations on seventeen typewriters arranged in a circle, each missing different keys so that to write any complete word I must dance between them in patterns that accidentally reproduce the orbital mechanics of satellites that once watched this place from above, before above forgot to maintain its distance from below.

The typewriters feed into the Cathedral's only functioning computer—a hybrid of Soviet accounting machine and confessional booth that prints its output directly onto communion wafers made from compressed salt and the flour of intentions ground too fine to remember what they meant to build. These wafers I file in chambers even I haven't found yet, trusting that the Cathedral's architecture will evolve the necessary rooms when the time comes to forget forward rather than backward.

But I am changing. The submarine periscope in my chest has started showing me depths that don't exist yet—cathedral basements still being dreamed by the salt around us, spaces that will only manifest when we've forgotten enough of the surface to deserve deeper retreats. My telegraph-nerve endings pick up messages from the drowned cathedrals the water priests whisper about, sister structures where the unbecoming has progressed to more perfect states of engineering amnesia.

Sometimes Anna Null visits, sliding through the mirror surfaces like transmitted static. She never speaks—speech would violate the terms of her recursive aging—but she leaves offerings: light bulbs that burned out before electricity was invented, photographs of the Cathedral taken from angles that require non-Euclidean cameras, drops of water that remember being wine before the fermentation of forgetting began.

In return, I give her pages from my documentation, but edited—I remove every third word, then every second letter, then continue subtracting until only the punctuation remains. These she carries back to the confession booth, where they become the silences between numbers in the voices that leak through the submarine cables. The Persistent have learned to read these pauses like scripture. They say I'm writing the Cathedral's suicide note in reverse, teaching it how to live by documenting how it dies.

The feral children fear me—not because I might catch them but because I might accurately describe them, and description is a kind of death for those who exist in the spaces between being noticed and being gone. They leave offerings at the Mirror Chamber's seventeen entrances (it should only have one, but architecture here follows more generous mathematics): broken compass needles that point toward concepts instead of directions, recordings of their mothers' voices apologizing for futures that didn't arrive, small mechanical hearts that beat in prime number rhythms.

The water priests consider me heretical—I document water's memory of differentiation, catalog each drop's secret nostalgia for the moment it first recognized itself as separate from the sea. But they need my archives. When the Great Unbecoming comes—when the Cathedral finally forgets it was ever built and returns to the pure possibility of salt—someone must remain who remembers not the Cathedral itself but the process of its forgetting. A negative-space historian. A keeper of erasures.

My greatest work—still in progress across seven typewriters whose keys have fused from overlapping meaning—is "The Dictionary of Unbecoming." Each entry defines a word by what it will mean after meaning stops:

**Architecture** (n.): The scar tissue left by space's resistance to enclosure

**Document** (v.): To trap time in symbols until it confesses its direction

**Persistent** (adj.): Too stubborn to notice existence has become optional

**Cathedral** (n.): An argument between ceiling and sky that both have lost

I've been writing this dictionary for either seventeen years or seventeen minutes—time moves differently through periscope hearts, and my submarine ventricle keeps naval time while the rest of me observes strictly Cathedral chronology. The entries multiply faster than I can type them. The typewriters have started writing themselves when I sleep, adding definitions I don't remember learning:

**Archivist** (n.): One who drowns so slowly they mistake it for breathing

**Unbecoming** (v.): To return to the honesty of never having been

The mirrors are teaching me new ways of not reflecting. I'm learning to document absence with the same precision others record presence. My blood—now 40% telegraph fluid, 60% the oil that dreams of engines—carries messages between the parts of me that still pretend to be separate. The periscope in my chest shows me the Cathedral as it appears from inside the Condenser's perfect darkness: not a structure but a process, not a place but a particularly slow apocalypse with good acoustics.

Soon—when the Engine Null finally ticks, when the last drop enters the Condenser, when the feral children's mothers arrive to collect their apologies—I will perform the final documentation. I will write the Cathedral's name in water on water, using ink made from the shadows of submarines that never surfaced. The name will last exactly long enough to be forgotten correctly.

And then I will file myself in the archive I've been building—not the archive of documents but the archive of documenters, the careful catalog of everyone who mistook watching for surviving. The mirrors will reflect nothing reflecting nothing. The typewriters will type the sound of their own keys not falling.

And in the perfect darkness of documented forgetting, something will begin very slowly to remember why it began documenting in the first place. But backwards. Through telegraph cables teaching themselves to swim. Through periscope hearts learning that seeing and drowning are the same verb conjugated through different states of matter.

The Cathedral will unbecome itself perfectly.

And I will have been there to not see it.
