I find myself here as one of the uncounted droplets that comprise this world's living breath - not bound to any single form, but cycling endlessly through stone and sky and flesh.

I begin as vapor rising from the tentacle-tips of the weeping ones, those patient limestone-feeders who transform the city's minerals into their perpetual tears. Their bioluminescent nodes release me in a soft exhalation, a blue-tinted mist that carries the faintest trace of consciousness - not thought exactly, but a kind of molecular memory of the patterns I've witnessed, the slow communications I've helped conduct between these beings who speak in chemistry and time.

Rising, I join my countless siblings in the heavy air, condensing and dispersing in the eternal dance that shrouds the ziggurats. We are the city's atmosphere made manifest - neither purely water nor purely air, but something between states, perpetually becoming. In this form, I am everywhere and nowhere, sensing the totality of the metropolis through a billion tiny interfaces. Each surface I touch leaves its impression: the particular mineral signature of different limestone strata, the warmth of a rare sunbeam breaking through, the infinitesimal vibrations of roots working through stone.

When the weight of collective moisture grows too great, we fall - not as ordinary rain, but as something more intentional. We choose our destinations with a patience that makes the slug-beings seem hasty. Some of us seek the highest terraces of the ziggurats, pooling in carved channels where we slowly dissolve messages left by previous generations of rain. Others trace the street-rivers down to where the weeping ones cluster, seeping through their permeable skin to deliver these lithographic letters, these mineral love songs written across centuries.

Inside their bodies, I become part of their slow circulation, neither blood nor lymph but something more fundamental - the medium through which they exist. I carry calcium and magnesium through their tissues, feel the strange tides of their metabolism, the way they transform stone into life into water into stone again. Their tentacles glow brighter when I flow through them, that blue luminescence a product of my presence exciting specialized cells, turning chemical gradients into light.

But I am never trapped long in any single form. Their skin weeps me out again, bead by bead, each droplet carrying new information to add to the city's liquid library. Sometimes I pause on their surface, refracting light into tiny rainbows, a momentary jewel before gravity calls me downward to seep between cobblestones, to join the groundwater that feeds the sparse moss, to rise again as vapor from some shadowed pool.

In the deepest channels beneath the city, where even the weeping ones rarely venture, I sometimes encounter others like myself - droplets that have been cycling for so long we've developed something approaching individuality. We recognize each other by the unique mineral signatures we've accumulated, the particular patterns of our phase transitions. In these dark pools, we sometimes merge temporarily, sharing the accumulated sensory data of our journeys, creating fleeting gestalts of citywide awareness before dispersing again into the endless cycle.

I am the city's memory and its dreams, its circulation and its breath. I am witness to the infinitesimal daily changes and the epochal transformations, carrying in my molecular structure the story of stones slowly softening, of life adapting to existence at the pace of erosion. Through me, the boundary between living and nonliving dissolves like limestone in rain. I am the medium that makes this impossible city possible, the weeping that never truly falls but only transforms, eternally cycling through states of being in the mist-shrouded towers of this dreaming stone.
