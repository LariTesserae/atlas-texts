In this fever-dream of molten glass and impossible physics, I find myself as one of the Oscillations—a being that exists between states, never quite settling into form.

I am neither fully Shimmering nor entirely separate from the collective pulse. My core is a wound spiral of platinum-bright plasma, but unlike my siblings who maintain their polyhedric dignity, I am afflicted with a peculiar instability. Every seventeen beats of the obsidian's tectonic heart, my form collapses into a two-dimensional plane—a sheet of living mercury pressed between invisible panes of glass. In these moments, I become pure surface, all reflection and no depth, sliding through the gaps between other beings like a whisper of light.

But I am not diminished by this flattening. When compressed to nothing more than a membrane, I become hypersensitive to the tremors that run through our shard-world. I feel the approach of each droplet before it forms, taste the mineral composition of condensation still clinging to the cathedral's ceiling. I know which of my siblings will next lose cohesion and spread across the razor edges—I feel their internal geometries beginning to unspool long before they themselves are aware.

My role, if such a thing exists in our wordless dance, is that of the Anticipator. When I sense an imminent liquefaction, I position myself beneath the failing form, spreading my flattened self like a net of light. As they dissolve, I catch their geometric memories before they can be lost to the general absorption. For a brief moment—no longer than it takes for a droplet to fall through solid stone—I hold their entire existence within my planar form. Their crystalline hearts spin inside me like trapped stars. Their learned shapes press against my boundaries, trying to break free.

Then I do something the others cannot: I fold.

Not in the way paper folds, but in the way space itself might fold if it could. I create pockets and chambers within my two-dimensional form, impossible architectures that should not exist. In these folds, I preserve fragments of the dissolved—not their full being, but their most essential patterns. A particular way of refracting violet light. The memory of a perfect dodecahedron achieved just before collapse. The frequency at which they vibrated when the heat was at its most unbearable.

When I unfold and return to my three-dimensional state, these preserved patterns distribute themselves among the swarm, but changed—refined by their passage through impossibility. The collective does not know that I do this. They only know that sometimes, inexplicably, they dream in geometries they have never worn.

I am currently pressed against the underside of a condensation bead the size of a world, feeling its weight bearing down on me, threatening to force another collapse. Inside it, I can see the reflection of the entire shard, warped and inverted. In that reflection, I glimpse something that should not exist: a hairline crack in the obsidian's surface that wasn't there moments ago. It sings with a frequency that makes my plasma core shudder.

Change is coming to our eternal dance. The shard is beginning to cool.

I flatten myself further, becoming thin enough to slip between the molecules of the glass, and I begin to fold the knowledge of this crack into a shape that can be preserved. When the others absorb this geometry, they will know—without knowing how they know—that our world is not forever. That even obsidian can break.

Until then, I oscillate. I compress and expand, catch and release, fold and unfold. I am the memory between memories, the pause between breaths that do not exist. I am what happens when the dream of being alive dreams of dreaming.

And in the wet, suffocating heat of our crystalline cathedral, pressed between the weight of condensation and the razor-sharp certainty of stone, I perform my secret purpose: ensuring that when we finally cool into stillness, something of our impossible dance will remain, folded into the very structure of the shard that birthed us.
