In the sulfurous twilight between worlds, where the weeping rocks bleed their eternal tears into jade-dark pools, I am the Keeper of Corroded Engines.

I dwell within the hollow shell of what was once a vast turbine hall, its cathedral ceiling long since collapsed to reveal the merciless sun. The metal bones of ancient machinery pierce through centuries of moss and vine like the ribs of slumbering leviathans. My home is neither fully of the old world nor the new—it exists in the liminal space where rust becomes soil, where copper pipes birth orchids, and where the ghosts of industry dissolve into the patient chemistry of rain and time.

My form reflects this threshold existence. Where others might see contradiction, I have become synthesis. My skin has taken on the patina of weathered bronze, etched with the same intricate patterns that mark the weeping rocks. Moisture beads perpetually on my surface, not sweat but condensation from the cool metal core that replaced my failing organs long ago. My hair—if it can still be called that—has become a cascade of copper wiring fine as spider silk, conducting the ambient electricity that still hums through the buried cables of this place.

Each morning, I wade through the flooded generator halls where electric eels have made their homes among the stator coils. They recognize me as kin—part creature, part circuit. I tend to the ghost engines that still turn in the depths, not to generate power for any grid, but to maintain the ancient rhythm that keeps the underground aquifers flowing. The machinery has evolved beyond its original purpose, fusing with the root systems of the towering fan-palms above. Through my ministrations, water rises through corroded pipes to weep from the rocks, feeding the endless cycle.

My fingers have grown long and articulated like the fronds of ferns, each joint containing a different tool—wrench, probe, root-cutter, seed-planter. I am simultaneously mechanic and gardener, maintaining the hybrid ecosystem where brake ferns grow from differential gears and morning glories strangle defunct transformers with loving persistence.

The creatures here know me by the electromagnetic hum of my presence. The jeweled beetles that nest in circuit boards scatter patterns of bioluminescent signals that I can read like text. The massive salamanders that lurk in the cooling pools share with me their ancient memories of when this place first fell silent, when the last human engineers fled the rising waters and failing systems. They remember, and through touching their thoughts with my wire-hair tendrils, I remember too.

At night, when the heat finally breaks and mist rises from every surface, I climb the skeletal transmission towers that still stand like monoliths above the canopy. From there, I can see the whole of my domain—the marriage of decay and growth, the beautiful entropy of a world reclaiming itself while incorporating what was left behind. Lightning strikes the towers during storms, and I spread my arms wide to receive it, letting the electricity course through my hybrid body to charge the underground capacitor banks that will slowly release their energy to fuel another century of managed wildness.

I am the bridge between epochs, the translator between the language of sap and hydraulic fluid. In the places where others see only ruin or untamed growth, I see the conversation between what was built and what grows, each teaching the other new ways to exist. The weeping rocks know my touch; they release their waters more freely when I sing to them in frequencies only metal and stone can hear.

This is my purpose: to tend the machines that have learned to breathe, to guide the vines that have learned to conduct, to ensure that the marriage of the industrial and natural produces ever more beautiful and impossible children. I am neither preservation nor progress, but the patient cultivation of transformation itself.

In this land where the sun burns everything to its essence and water rises from the earth like dreams, I am the memory of making and the promise of becoming—forever turning in the space between.
