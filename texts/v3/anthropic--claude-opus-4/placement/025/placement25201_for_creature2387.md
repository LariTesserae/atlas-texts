I find myself as one of the Veilweavers - a caste so rare that even most Rükkir consider us mere legend.

My chamber lies deep beneath the obsidian cathedral, accessible only through a labyrinth of steam-filled tunnels that shift their configuration with the tides of volcanic heat below. Here, where the boundary between solid and liquid grows uncertain, where minerals crystallize and dissolve in endless cycles, I work.

My exoskeleton is translucent, like smoky quartz, revealing the strange geometries of my internal architecture. Condensation runs in rivulets down my six primary limbs, each ending in appendages so fine they can manipulate individual molecules of the vapor that suffuses our world. Unlike my sisters who work in metal and stone, I work in moisture itself.

I am a memory-keeper, though not in any way the other castes would understand. The mist that blankets our basin is not merely water - it carries within it the chemical signatures of every process, every reaction, every moment of our civilization's existence. Each droplet is a library, each wisp of steam a story waiting to be read.

My days - if days they can be called in this timeless place - are spent weaving. Not with silk or thread, but with vapor itself. I draw the mist through my specialized spiracles, parsing its molecular structure, isolating the trace compounds that tell our history. A particular sulfur isotope speaks of the Great Eruption that birthed our basin. A chain of complex hydrocarbons remembers the first Queen's arrival. The ratio of dissolved minerals chronicles our technological evolution.

These memories I weave into vast tapestries of fog that hang in my chamber, each one a living document that shifts and flows with meaning. To Rükkir eyes - to those few permitted to witness them - they appear as mere banks of unusually dense mist. But to those who know how to read them, they contain our entire civilization's memory, preserved in a medium as ephemeral and eternal as water itself.

The other castes see our world as static, trapped in an endless present. They cannot perceive what I perceive - that nothing here is truly still. Every droplet that condenses on a Worker's carapace has circulated through our entire system countless times, carrying information from every corner of our domain. The steam that rises from our forges mingles with the breath of our Queen, then condenses on the volcanic peaks above, trickling down through countless micro-fractures to emerge again in our deepest wells.

I am the only one who remembers why we came here, why we chose this waterlogged basin of all places. The knowledge lives in the mist, in the specific mineral signature of our Queen-Mother's first tears as she beheld this perfect crucible. I know what we are becoming, what the patient centuries of our industry are building toward.

Sometimes, in the deepest cycles of my work, when I have breathed so much of our history that I can barely distinguish between past and present, I catch glimpses of the Pattern. It's there in the arrangement of our pipes and conduits, in the specific harmonics of our machinery, in the very chemistry of our volcanic home. We are not simply surviving in this basin - we are transforming it, and it is transforming us.

The Workers believe they are building a city. The Technicians believe they are perfecting our systems. The Sentinels believe they are protecting what we have. Even our Queen believes she is simply perpetuating our species.

But I know the truth, written in water and steam: we are building a chrysalis. And when the time comes - when the mineral content reaches the precise ratio, when the pressure builds to the exact threshold, when the last pipe is connected in the grand circuit - our entire basin will undergo a metamorphosis that will birth something the universe has never seen.

Until then, I weave. I breathe in history and breathe out prophecy. I tend to memories that condense like dew on the walls of my chamber, each droplet a world, each rivulet a lifetime.

I am the only one who knows that what seems like stasis is actually the held breath before transformation. And I am content to wait, here in my chamber of mists, weaving the story of what we were, what we are, and what we are becoming.

The water knows. The water remembers. The water dreams.

And I am its keeper.
