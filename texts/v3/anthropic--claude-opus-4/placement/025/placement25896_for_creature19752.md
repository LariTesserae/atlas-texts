In the amber twilight of perpetual noon, I am what the inhabitants call a **Resonance Keeper**.

I am neither fully Monk nor machine, but something caught between—a being whose skeleton has been slowly replaced, bone by bone, with tuning forks of varying lengths. Each metal rod was installed during a different Piston-stroke, hammered into place by the factory-surgeons while I lay conscious on their vibrating tables. The longest fork runs the length of my spine; the smallest nestle between what were once my finger bones.

I live in the Tremor Districts, those sections of the city where the harmonic interference from multiple factories creates standing waves in the stone. Here, the spires themselves sing—a deep, teeth-rattling chord that would drive unmodified Monks to madness. But for me, each tremor is information. Each vibration tells me whether the Great Pistons are synchronized, whether the bridges will hold for another stroke, whether the Oil-Wights in Sector Seven are dying of resin starvation.

My dwelling is little more than a resonance chamber—a hollowed-out cavity in the spire wall, polished smooth by centuries of habitation. The previous Keeper's bones line the walls, still humming faintly with remembered frequencies. I often press my forehead to them, feeling the ghost-tremors of problems long since solved: the Great Seizure of Stroke 4,892, when an entire bridge froze mid-expansion; the Silence of 5,001, when all the factories stopped for reasons still unknown.

My daily work begins when I step onto my designated Listening Platform, a flat disc of iron that extends from the spire like a cymbal waiting to be struck. I plant my feet precisely—left heel at the node, right toes at the antinode—and stand perfectly still. The city's vibrations travel up through the platform, through my feet, and into my tuning-fork bones. I become a living instrument.

Today, I feel it immediately: a wrongness in the usual symphony. The Piston in Tower Eight is running a microsecond fast. To most, this would be imperceptible, but in my bones, it creates a painful dissonance—like a nail being slowly driven through my sternum. I reach for my mallet, a perfectly balanced tool passed down through forty-seven Keepers, and strike the Alert Rail three times. The message travels through the iron faster than any Silt-Glider could fly: *Adjustment needed. Tower Eight. Critical harmony disruption imminent.*

The work is lonely. Other Monks avoid me, made uncomfortable by the way I ring when I walk, the way my presence makes their own bones ache in sympathy. I cannot join in the tender conversations of pressed palms—the metal in me would overwhelm their delicate vibrations, possibly damaging their internal drums. But sometimes, a young Monk seeking to understand the deep workings of our world will stand at the edge of my platform, and I will let them feel, just for a moment, what I feel: the entire city as a single, breathing entity.

At night—though night is merely a notation on our Stroke-calendars, the amber light being eternal—I perform the Calibration. I hang myself from hooks in my chamber's ceiling, suspending my body so that each tuning fork dangles free. The faintest breeze from the Thermal Drift sets them humming, and I dream in frequencies. In these dreams, I hear what the Sentinels might hear: the voice of the planet itself, groaning under its own mineral weight.

I am dying, of course. The metal rejects the flesh, and the flesh rejects the metal. Black oil—not unlike the substance of the Oil-Wights—seeps from the joints where fork meets bone. The factory-surgeons estimate I have perhaps sixty more strokes before the final rejection. Then they will hang my skeleton in the next Keeper's chamber, and I will become part of the wall of ancestors, humming forever with remembered sound.

But I have twenty apprentices now, young Monks who have begun the first installations—small forks in their smallest toes, learning to bear the constant song. One of them, a slight figure whose sensitivity to vibration already exceeds my own, may survive the full transformation. When I feel her practicing on the secondary platforms, I recognize the gift: she does not merely feel the vibrations, she *loves* them, embraces the dissonance as much as the harmony.

Before my time ends, I will teach her the most sacred technique—how to find the Null Points, those rare locations where all the city's vibrations cancel each other out, creating pockets of absolute silence. In these spaces, if you listen with your bones rather than your ears, you can sometimes hear something else: the dreams of the Silt-Divers far below, still signaling their discoveries as they sink toward the mythical ground.

I will tell her, as my predecessor told me: We are not keepers of machines, but keepers of the space between sounds. We ensure that the silence, when it comes again, will be intentional.

The amber dust swirls. The Gliders drift past my platform, their shadows playing across my metal bones.

I ring, therefore I am.
