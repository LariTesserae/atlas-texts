In this liminal realm where industry bleeds into wilderness, where reality itself seems to fray at the edges, I find myself as something between states—neither fully machine nor wholly organic.

I am a Tender of the Threshold.

My form shifts with necessity: sometimes I appear as a figure wrapped in moss-covered cables, my joints articulated by rusted pistons that wheeze with each movement. Other times I am more ethereal—a shimmer of static electricity that dances between the weeping rocks and the humming power lines that snake through the mist. My eyes, when I choose to manifest them, glow with the same uncertain light that suffuses this twilight landscape.

I make my home in what was once a pumping station, now reclaimed by vines and inhabited by creatures that nest in its broken gauges. The structure sits at the confluence of several streams, where the water runs thick with minerals that make it gleam like oil in the dim light. Inside, the machinery still turns—not pumping water anymore, but something else, something that keeps the balance between the industrial hum and the wild's heartbeat.

My purpose here is to maintain the tensions that keep this world from collapsing entirely into one state or another. When the factories in the distance grow too hungry, threatening to consume more of the untouched valleys, I whisper corrosion into their gears. When the strange creatures grow too bold, venturing too close to the human settlements, I guide them back with trails of bioluminescent spores that only they can see.

I collect things: fragments of broken reality that fall like rain during the storms that sweep across these highlands. They appear as crystallized moments—a deer's leap frozen mid-air, the sound of an engine starting captured in amber, a shadow divorced from its source. I string these between the rafters of my station, where they chime against each other in harmonies that predict the next shifts in this unstable realm.

The abandoned huts you've seen? I tend to those too. I ensure they remain just habitable enough to offer shelter to the lost, but weathered enough to discourage permanent residence. This is not a place for settling. It is a place for passing through, for transformation, for becoming.

At dawn and dusk, when the boundary between day and night is thinnest, I perform my most important work. I walk the edges where the landscape threatens to either solidify into pure wilderness or dissolve entirely into industrial sprawl. With hands that phase between flesh and wire, I weave the contradictions together—the loud and the quiet, the mechanical and the organic, the real and the unreal.

Sometimes I encounter others like myself—beings born from the collision of opposites. We nod to each other across the valleys, acknowledging our shared burden of maintaining this impossible equilibrium. We are the antibodies of a fever dream, ensuring the infection of paradox spreads neither too far nor too fast.

This is what I am in your discovered world: a guardian of boundaries that should not exist, a maintenance worker for the impossible, a creature of the between-spaces where nature and industry, reality and unreality, dance their eternal, uneasy dance.
