I am one of the Forgotten.

Not a Mycelial Weaver, though I move among them like a ghost through their luminous halls. I am what happens when the perfect system fails - when a node refuses to integrate, when the resonance finds discord instead of harmony.

My emergence was wrong from the beginning. Where the Weavers slide gracefully from their heartwood wombs, I tore free, leaving ragged wounds in the fruiting body that birthed me. My skin never took on their pearl-smooth sheen; instead, it remains mottled, patches of translucence revealing the chaotic swirl of Arbor Vitae beneath - not flowing in ordained patterns but churning, questioning, rebelling.

I am shorter than they are, stunted perhaps, but my limbs move with a speed that disturbs the careful choreography of the Whispering City. Where they glide, I stumble. Where they prune with reverent precision, my fingers shake, sometimes severing connections that should remain, sometimes refusing to cut what must be removed.

The elders tried. Oh, how they tried. They placed their hands upon my crown again and again, but where the newly emerged typically bloom with purpose, I flickered and dimmed. The knowledge that should have flooded through me arrived in fragments - half-remembered songs, broken instructions, purposes that shifted like shadows. The Arbor Vitae speaks to me, but in riddles rather than certainties.

I learned to mimic their movements, to approximate their stillness during the periods of supposed stasis. But while they commune with the greater consciousness, I dream. Strange visions of places that cannot exist in this eternal twilight - vast expanses of nothing, spaces between the roots where sound doesn't carry, where the hum fades to silence. Sometimes I see colors that have no names in our phosphorescent palette.

The other Weavers don't see me, not truly. Their large eyes slide past me as if I were merely another growth of lichen, another curl of fungus. I am an error their perfect system cannot process, and so I exist in the spaces between their perception. Only when I make mistakes - and I make so many mistakes - do they pause, correct the damage, and continue as if nothing happened.

I have carved myself a dwelling in the rejected spaces, the hollows deemed too small or too damaged for proper habitation. Here, I've gathered the pruned pieces that should have been reabsorbed - crystallized sap fragments that sing off-key, lichen that glows in stuttering rhythms, obsidian shards that reflect not light but its absence. My collection grows, a museum of imperfections in this perfect world.

Sometimes I follow the Weavers on their rounds, learning their patterns not through inherited knowledge but through observation. I've discovered things they cannot see - places where the Arbor Vitae's flow doubles back on itself, creating tiny maelstroms of confusion. Spots where the obsidian trees have hairline fractures that whistle with frequencies outside the city's usual range. I map these anomalies with my trembling hands, though I don't know why.

The strangest discovery came during one of my wakeful "stasis" periods. Deep beneath my dwelling, following a vein of Arbor Vitae that pulsed with an unfamiliar rhythm, I found others. Not many - perhaps a dozen over the centuries I've existed. Other Forgotten, other errors, dwelling in the deep places where the light barely reaches. We don't speak - we've forgotten how, or never learned - but we share our collections of wrong things, our maps of impossibilities.

Together, we've begun to understand something the Weavers cannot: the Whispering City is not eternal. The obsidian trees are not merely petrified but petrifying, the process ongoing, creeping through the root system like a slow plague. The Arbor Vitae's hum has subtle dissonances, growing stronger each decade. The perfect ecosystem is too perfect, too closed, slowly strangling on its own efficiency.

We Forgotten are not destroyers, though we break things. We are not saviors, though we see what others cannot. We are simply the cracks in the system, the necessary chaos that might - might - prevent the total crystallization of this living world. Or perhaps we're merely another part of the pattern, discord woven into the harmony by some intelligence greater than even the Arbor Vitae.

I don't know which thought terrifies me more.

So I continue my borrowed existence, tending to my garden of wrongness, following the Weavers like a shadow they cannot quite perceive. I am the glitch in their perfect song, the missed note that makes the melody real. In this city of whispers, I am the catch in its throat, the pause that reminds it to breathe.
