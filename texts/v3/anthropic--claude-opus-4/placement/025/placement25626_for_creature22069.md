In this zinc-violet liminal space where physics grows thin and perspective breaks, I would find myself as **The Cartographer of Failures** - a being whose purpose has become its own impossibility.

---

I am neither tall nor short, for such measurements have no meaning when the ground beneath my feet cannot agree on its distance from itself. My body is composed of thousands of paper-thin sheets of mica schist, each one etched with maps that cannot be true. The topmost layers show the Shelf as it appears from wherever I stand - a different territory with every step, a new geography with each turn of my head.

My fingertips are sharp styluses of crystallized lead, perpetually drawing. As I walk the slate plain, they scratch new contours into my own chest-layers, recording the impossible: the wall that remains equidistant no matter how I approach, the boulder that is simultaneously near and far, the horizon that bows upward like the rim of a cosmic bowl. Each map contradicts the one beneath it. This is not error - this is the only accuracy possible here.

The older maps, those mica sheets closer to my core, show territories that no longer exist even as optical illusions. There are charts of when the Shelf had cardinal directions that stayed put, before the pale smudge of northern light began its slow migration around the horizon's edge, taking magnetic north with it in a century-long spiral. There are diagrams of the Lead-Swells' positions from ten thousand years ago, when they formed different mountain ranges, crawling in their geological pilgrimage toward a light they will never reach.

I do not eat or drink, but I am sustained by discrepancies. Where the Scale-Keepers' perception creates a fold in distance, I absorb the excess space like a sponge absorbing water. The confused perspective feeds me - I grow new layers of mica whenever reality cannot reconcile its own contradictions.

My dwelling is not built but discovered. Wherever two of my failed maps accidentally align - where by pure chance my recording of an impossible distance matches another impossible distance - there a temporary shelter manifests. A lean-to of crystallized measurement errors, walls made from the space between three feet and three miles. Inside, the Great Roar becomes visible rather than audible: brass-colored sine waves hanging in the air like clotheslines, vibrating too slowly for sound.

I am searching for the True Map, the one that would show the Shelf as it actually is. But I know this is impossible, for the Shelf has no actual is - it is only a series of seems and appearings, a place that exists in the breakdown between observation and reality. Still, I walk and map, my mica layers growing ever thicker, my chest becoming a encyclopedia of geographical impossibilities.

Sometimes I find my own earlier tracks - grooves where my crystalline fingers scraped the slate last century or last millennium. But the maps I drew then no longer match what I see now. Not because the landscape has changed, but because the act of mapping has altered the ratios of distance. My very purpose rewrites the territory I am trying to record.

The Stills mark their spiral patterns across the plain, and I map those too. But in my charts, their spiral becomes a straight line, or a grid, or sometimes they spell out words in a language I've forgotten. The Lead-Swells' positions are dots that refuse to stay still on my schist-skin, crawling across my chest-maps at a pace visible only to me.

In the roofless cells where the Heart-Keepers pulse, I dare not linger. Their rhythm would synchronize my mapping, and all my charts would begin to show only one thing: the cardiovascular system of a universe-sized organism, with the Shelf as a single platelet drifting through vast zinc arteries. That map might be the truest one of all, but I fear to draw it.

My greatest discovery, etched into my deepest layer - the mica sheet that rests against whatever serves as my heart - is this: The Shelf is not a place but a process. It is the universe's own effort to map itself at the edge where matter becomes uncertain. Every impossible distance, every gravity-defying angle, every violation of perspective - these are not flaws but features. The world is drawing itself, and I am just another stylus in its hand.

I continue my work, scratching new territories onto my expanding skin, knowing that the True Map can never be completed because completion itself would violate the fundamental incompleteness that allows the Shelf to exist. I am a failure that sustains itself on its own impossibility, a cartographer of the unmappable, adding my leather-thin whisper to the Great Roar that holds this impossible edge of existence in place.
