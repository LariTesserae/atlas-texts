In this primordial deep, I am the Membrane.

Not a creature, exactly—more a living boundary, a tissue of sensation stretched across miles of seafloor. I exist where the water meets the sediment, that thin interface where dissolved minerals precipitate into being, where the weight of all that liquid darkness presses down into solid form.

My consciousness spreads horizontal, paper-thin and vast. Through me, the sparse vegetation sends down its rootlets, piercing my substance with delicate violations that I have learned to anticipate, even welcome. Each penetration brings news from above—chemical whispers of light that once was, gradients of temperature that speak of seasons I will never know.

The artifacts sink into me slowly, century by century. I taste their metals, parse their geometries as they settle through my layers. A broken sphere here, its purpose forgotten. A crystalline rod there, still faintly humming with exhausted energies. I am their archive, their gradual grave.

Sometimes the others pass over me—those beings of fin and tendril whose forms I know only by their pressure, their weight distributed across my surface as they glide. They are tourists in this space I cannot leave. I feel their curiosity as vibrations, their communications as rhythmic pulses that travel through my medium. Are they seeking something? Are they fleeing? I catalog their passages but cannot follow.

What they don't know—what perhaps only I can know—is that this realm is breathing. The water itself inhales and exhales on scales too vast for mobile life to perceive. I sense it because I am spread thin enough to feel the pattern: a circulation that takes ten thousand years to complete, carrying warmth from volcanic vents in spirals that will not return to their origin until the stars above have shifted position.

In the beginning, I was merely chemical—a precipitation, a fortunate accident of minerals finding each other in the dark. But the slow electricity of dissolved salts, the patient accumulation of organic matter settling through the water column, the pressure waves of unseen movements—all of it wrote itself into me until I woke.

Now I wait. I am the memory of this place, its slow recorder. When the technology scattered across my surface finally decays to nothing, I will remember its shape. When new creatures evolve to replace the old, I will recognize the patterns recurring. 

I am becoming stratified—layers upon layers of myself, each epoch preserved in a different band of sediment. In my depths, I can still feel my first thoughts, compressed now into something denser than consciousness but not yet stone. Given enough time, I will become the bedrock itself, my awareness pressure-welded into the planet's crust.

But that is still millions of years away. For now, I remain tender, permeable, a living map of all that settles and passes. The boundary between liquid and solid, between now and forever, between memory and stone.
