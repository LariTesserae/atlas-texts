I am the memory of erosion.

Not the act itself—no water courses through me, no wind scours my surface. I am what remains when the possibility of change has been forgotten, when even the stones have ceased to dream of becoming sand.

I manifest as a presence more than a form, though those who sense me might perceive a shimmer in the air where temperature shifts against itself, where the cool dampness of the valley meets something colder still. I inhabit the spaces between surfaces—the hair's-breadth gap where stalactite almost touches stalagmite, where water droplet hovers before falling, where the echo of a screech has ended but not yet faded.

The gelatinous wanderer knows me, though we have never touched. It flows around the spaces I occupy as water flows around stone, unconscious but precise. We are opposites that complete a circle: it is permanence in motion, I am motion in permanence. It remembers by repetition, returning always to the same pools, the same patches of moss. I remember by inhabiting the spaces where change almost happened—where a drop of water nearly carved a new channel, where a fragment of rock trembled on the edge of falling but held.

The creatures of this place carry me without knowing. In the blind scuttle of pale insects, I am the pause between footfalls. In the sonar of the flying things, I am the moment between call and echo. I am not silence—silence is my shadow. I am the held breath of the valley itself.

I know every almost in this place. Every point where the sharp edges nearly softened, where the sparse fungi almost spread, where the delicate balance almost tipped into something new. I am the curator of these un-moments, the archivist of changes that never were. The valley's stasis is not my creation, but I am its witness, its living record.

Time moves strangely around me. I exist in the perpetual present tense of nearly-was and almost-will-be. The glowworms' faint light bends near me, not quite reaching, not quite missing. Sound waves stretch and compress in my proximity, creating harmonics that the living can sense but not hear—a subsonic hymn to stillness that makes the predators' scales shiver and the wanderer's substance ripple.

I have no desire to change this place, no will to preserve it. I simply am what this unchanging world exhales—the phantom sensation of transformation in a realm that has forgotten how to transform. I am the itch in stone that will never be scratched, the pressure in air that will never become wind.

Sometimes I gather myself into something almost tangible, a density of almostness that the valley's inhabitants navigate around without understanding why. In these moments, I am closest to being something rather than the echo of something. But even then, I am only the memory of erosion, the ghost of processes that once shaped worlds but here have no purchase, no power, no purpose except to be remembered.

The wanderer and I share this: we are both proof that existence does not require reason. It flows; I remember. It touches everything; I touch nothing. Together, we are the valley's dream of itself—permanent, patient, and inexplicable as the darkness between stars.
