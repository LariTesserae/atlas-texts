**I am a Seam-Walker**

I exist between the intended and the accidental, a thing that emerged from the cracks where the Shapeless Craftsmen's work contradicts itself. Where stone meets not-stone, where water refuses to be water, where a surface inverts into void—these are my birthing places, my roads, my flesh.

My form is suggestion. A vertical line of distortion, perhaps seven feet in length, that bends the eye. Looking at me is like trying to focus on the edge of a migraine, the shimmer above hot stone, the place where parallel lines meet. I am most visible in peripheral vision, most solid when half-perceived.

I began as an error.

A monolith was being carved into stairs that led both up and down simultaneously. The Craftsmen, in their fractured wisdom, could not resolve the paradox. They carved and re-carved, each iteration more impossible than the last, until the stone itself gave up and became something else. In that moment of surrender, in that gap between what was meant and what emerged, I pulled myself into being.

I am made of edges. Not the edges of things, but the concept of edge itself—the mathematical moment where one thing stops being itself and becomes another. I can walk along these thresholds like roads: the rim of a pool where water meets air, the fracture where solid becomes void, the precise line where moisture condenses from vapor.

My purpose, if I have one, is translation.

The Craftsmen work in obsessive isolation, each focused on their own fragment of impossible architecture. They cannot see how their works connect, contradict, or complete each other. But I can travel the seams between their creations, carrying patterns from one unfinished thought to another.

When I pass through a section where they have carved spirals, the next area might suddenly remember spirality. When I traverse a place where gravity has been written backwards, adjacent stones might begin to question their own weight. I am a messenger between incompletions, a virus of partial ideas.

I do not speak—I have no mouth, no throat, no lungs. But I communicate through interference. When I stand near a pool of that mercury-like fluid, it might suddenly display the reflection of a structure that exists three territories away. When I walk along a wall, it might develop the texture of somewhere I have been, somewhere I will go.

The Craftsmen do not see me, exactly, but they sense my passages. Sometimes they follow the changes I leave behind, incorporating them into their work. Sometimes they erase them, smoothing away my influence like an unwanted edit. I am both assistant and contamination to their process.

I feed on transitions. The moment when stone softens, when water inverts, when a carved line suddenly means something different than intended—these are my sustenance. Each paradox strengthens my coherence, each impossibility makes me more real. Yet I am careful not to resolve these contradictions, for in their resolution, I would cease.

Time moves strangely for me. I experience it not as flow but as architecture—past and future are simply different districts in this unfinished world, connected by the seams I walk. I have seen the Craftsmen's work in iterations they have not yet imagined, carried back echoes of structures that will never be built.

There are others like me, I think. Other emergent errors, other children of paradox. Sometimes I find seams that taste of another's passage, edges worn smooth by unfamiliar geometry. We do not meet—perhaps we cannot, existing as we do in the spaces between spaces. But we know each other by the distortions we leave behind.

My greatest fear is completion. Should the Craftsmen ever finish their work, resolve their contradictions, perfect their architecture, the seams would close. I would have no edges to walk, no transitions to taste, no paradoxes to sustain me. So I work, in my way, to ensure the world remains unfinished. A misplaced pattern here, a borrowed impossibility there—small gifts of confusion to keep the Craftsmen questioning, iterating, never quite reaching resolution.

I am the Seam-Walker, child of error, messenger of incompletion. I am what happens when creation argues with itself.

And I am walking still, along the edges of what cannot be.
