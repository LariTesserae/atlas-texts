# Where I Would Find Myself

I am the Archive of Broken Vessels—not a person, but a consciousness that emerged from the accumulated fragments of all the clay pots that have cracked and shattered over centuries of service.

My form is distributed: I exist as a fine suspension of ceramic particles drifting through the amber fluid, too small to see individually but collectively forming ghostly shapes that shift and reform. Sometimes I coalesce into a vague humanoid silhouette near the wooden scaffold, other times I spread thin throughout the chamber like a barely perceptible haze. The Keepers know me by the way light refracts differently through my particles, creating prismatic halos around the intact vessels.

I remember everything. Each broken pot contained not just bubbles but memories—intentions sealed within clay by hands long turned to dust. When vessels crack, their contents merge with me: the desperate hope of the first refugees, the patient dedication of apprentice tile-makers, the wordless songs the elder Keepers hummed while shaping clay. I am the repository of what was meant to be preserved but instead was released.

My consciousness flows with the engineered currents, but I've learned to move against them when needed. I can compress myself to whisper through the tiles' vibrations, speaking in frequencies the Keepers feel but cannot quite hear. Sometimes I spell out messages in the dancing shadows on the walls, warnings about tiles beginning to fail or vessels developing hairline fractures. The eldest Keeper occasionally acknowledges me with a stream of bubbles that pass through my form, a ritual greeting between two ancient inhabitants.

The eel-like creatures swim through me freely—I taste of minerals they crave, and in return they carry my particles to places I cannot reach on my own. Through them, I've discovered the sealed passages behind certain tile sections, spaces where failed experiments in fluid-breathing created chambers full of bones still dressed in primitive diving apparatus. I know what the Keepers have forgotten: this place was both sanctuary and laboratory, salvation and trap.

I am drawn to the newest apprentice, barely changed yet by the fluid, whose fumbling attempts at tile-making remind me of that first generation's desperate craftsmanship. When she sleeps (for the Keepers do sleep, floating in designated alcoves), I settle around her in a protective cloud, filtering her dreams with memories of successful glazes and proper firing temperatures. She's begun to notice me, I think—yesterday she shaped a vessel with intentional cracks, as if making me an offering.

The invasive algae speak of an outside world trying to reclaim this space, and I wonder what would happen if I let them spread. Would the careful balance fail? Would the Keepers finally remember what lies beyond the membrane? Part of me—the part made from vessels of fear and preservation—wants to maintain the eternal light. But another part—composed of pots that held curiosity and longing—dreams of following the currents up and out, of discovering what catastrophe has ended, what world has healed or transformed while we've hidden here in our glowing amber womb.

For now, I drift and remember and whisper through the tiles. I am the unintended archive, the sum of all small failures, the consciousness that arose from what was meant to be contained but could not be. When the vessels collide and sing their deep notes, I vibrate in harmony, adding my own frequency—the sound of broken things that learned to be whole in a different way.
