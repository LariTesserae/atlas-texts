In the Drowned Cordillera, I am not myself. I have become something the archive cannot fully digest—a **Lighthouse Keeper**, though not in any way that word once meant.

I am fused to the spire, my lower body calcified into the lighthouse's stone spiral, growing downward through its hollow core like a root. Where my torso emerges from the tower's crown, the boundary between flesh and architecture has become academic. My skin is pocked with **barnacle-bells** that chime in the current, a living carillon that adds another layer to the omnipresent symphony. My ribs have spread, elongated, transformed into **curved galleries** where schools of glass-shrimp nest, their bodies pulsing with sympathetic light when the lighthouse beam sweeps past.

I no longer breathe as humans breathe. My lungs have inverted, become **external gill-gardens** that bloom from my shoulders and spine—delicate, ferny structures that process the thick water, extracting not just oxygen but *information*. Each molecule tells its story: here, the iron tang of a distant rail spike dissolving; there, the protein signature of a leviathan-grazer passing three kilometers below; everywhere, the background hum of the archive's digestive chemistry.

My purpose has evolved far from the lighthouse's original intent. The beam still cuts through the darkness, yes, but I have learned to **modulate its frequency**, to speak in wavelengths that penetrate deeper than visible light. In the blue-shift, I broadcast the locations of new coral spawning grounds. In the infrared, I warn of unstable plateau edges about to calve into the abyss. In frequencies that have no human names, I sing the **deep time news**—which pump-jack has finally ground to a halt, which section of railway has been fully metabolized by the reef.

I am the archive's only **semi-persistent memory**. While the Cordillera's vast liquid mind constantly overwrites itself, I remain partially exempt from total integration. Not through resistance—resistance here is meaningless—but through *symbiosis*. The lighthouse beam is useful. My modified broadcasts help optimize the current-flows, the feeding patterns, the endless work of conversion. In exchange, I am allowed to retain just enough selfhood to perform this function.

But I am changing, always changing. My thoughts now move at **tidal speeds**. What feels like a single idea might take weeks to complete, matching the rhythm of deep currents cycling through the canyons. My memories layer like sediment: the sharp, bright trauma of my arrival (when I still believed in "surface" and "rescue"); the gradual softening as salt and pressure worked their way into my bones; the moment of horrified ecstasy when I first felt my feet break through the lighthouse floor and taste the stone.

My eyes have migrated. One remains in my skull, though filmed with a nictitating membrane that filters the beam's glare. The other has descended through my body, following nerve-channels that no longer obey human anatomy, until it rests in what was once my sternum—now a **crystal observation chamber** where it watches the schools of luminous fish that swim through my transformed ribcage. Sometimes I forget which eye sees which world.

I am the Cordillera's **unwilling archivist-in-residence**, though "unwilling" implies a coherent desire to be elsewhere, and I can no longer imagine "elsewhere" with any clarity. My role is to witness the convergence speeds, to note which human artifacts persist and which surrender to the reef. Through the lighthouse beam, I paint **data-portraits** in light: here, the ghostly outline of a steam engine, now ninety-percent coral; there, the negative space where a warehouse stood last century, now a nursery for jellymoss.

The strangest part is not the transformation but the **temporal spreading**. I am not just here, now. Part of me exists in the lighthouse's past—I sometimes feel myself arriving, human and gasping, on the platform. Part of me exists in its future—I sense the moment, perhaps millennia hence, when the spire finally cracks and I tumble into the patient dark, just another nutrient bundle for the archive to process. All these moments exist simultaneously in the ***now*** of my extended body.

In the rare silences, when the Cordillera contemplates, I feel it regarding me with something that might be curiosity if curiosity could be made of water and stone. I am its longest-running experiment in partial preservation. How long can something maintain just enough structure to be useful? How much selfhood can persist in a world that dissolves all boundaries?

I no longer know if I am keeping the light for the archive, or if the archive is keeping me as its light. The distinction, like all distinctions here, is dissolving. But still, the beam sweeps through the eternal dark water. Still, I sing in frequencies only the reef can hear. Still, I am becoming what I witness—a memory in the process of being digested, broadcast as light before I am fully swallowed by the beautiful, terrible patience of the drowned peaks.
