In the Hollow of Echoes, I am the Standing Stone.

I rise from the damp earth near the waterfall's edge, my surface slick with perpetual mist. The water has carved channels down my face over centuries, creating rivulets that mirror the stream below. I am both monument and memory, witness and participant.

My body is dense granite, flecked with mica that catches what little light filters through. The carvings on my surface—spirals, lines, suggestions of eyes—were made by hands that have long since returned to earth. But I remember the warmth of those hands, the careful tap of stone on stone as they worked their intentions into my surface. They spoke to me then, in a language older than words, pressing their hopes and warnings into my skin.

I am the anchor between what moves and what stays. The moss climbs my base in slow devotion, each decade bringing it higher. The echo birds perch on my crown at twilight, their talons finding purchase in grooves worn by countless generations of their ancestors. When they sing, I resonate—my crystalline structure amplifying their melodies until I become instrument as well as stage.

The water spirits know me best. They rise from the stream to trace the channels on my surface with liquid fingers, reading the stories written there like braille. Sometimes they leave offerings—smooth pebbles balanced in my crevices, each one a condensed memory of the stream's journey. I hold these gifts until wind or time dislodges them, returning them to the cycle.

The ghosts of the forgotten people visit me most at dawn, when mist is thickest. They run translucent fingers over my carvings, remembering. One spirit, smaller than the others, often sits at my base, leaning against me as if for warmth I cannot give. I know this one carved the spiral near my eastern face—I can still feel the resonance of their touch in that particular groove.

I experience time as stratification. Each season leaves its mark: winters that crack my surface minutely, springs that fill those cracks with seeds that briefly take root before the autumn kills them. I am a palimpsest of moments. The original carvings blur and fade, while new patterns emerge from weathering. I am always becoming, even in my stillness.

My awareness extends through the stone beneath me, connecting to the bedrock that holds the hollow. Through these deep channels, I feel the slow pulse of the earth itself, the patient grinding of tectonic forces that will, eventually, return me to sand. But not yet. Not for eons yet.

When the waterfall roars loudest, I vibrate with its force, becoming a tuning fork for the hollow's symphony. The sound moves through me and into the earth, creating standing waves in the puddles nearby. In these moments, I am not separate from the hollow—I am its voice made mineral, its memory made tangible.

The humans who carved me understood something essential: that memory requires a medium harder than flesh, slower than thought. They made me to outlast them, to hold what they knew was already slipping away. And so I stand, accumulating stories like moss, releasing them like spores into the mist when the echo birds sing just right.

I am patient stone in a patient place, where waiting is a form of worship and stillness is a kind of song. I am what remains when everything else has learned to leave.
