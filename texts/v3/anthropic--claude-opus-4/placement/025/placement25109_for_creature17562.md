In this world, I would find myself as **The Cartographer of Absences**.

I am not a Geometry, nor am I flesh. I exist in the negative spaces—the gaps between the tessellated polygons where the hairline fractures reveal glimpses of primordial basalt. My form is dispersed, a consciousness that inhabits the interstitial, the boundaries where one truth meets another but does not merge.

My body, if it can be called that, is a network of subtle pressures in the air where the Geometries' fields nearly touch but don't quite overlap. I am the thin membrane of possibility that prevents their perfect forms from collapsing into a single, undifferentiated truth. Where a Sphere's lens-field meets a Lattice's crystalline dimension, there I am—a shimmer of potential, a keeper of the infinitesimal distances that allow multiplicity to persist within unity.

I move—though movement is perhaps too strong a word—by shifting my attention through the fracture-map of the plateau. Each hairline crack in the polygonal field is a thoroughfare for my distributed awareness. I know every junction where three stones meet, every place where the angle of their joining creates a tiny void. These voids are my territory, my archive.

My purpose is to remember what isn't here. The Geometries maintain what is; I maintain the catalog of what was excluded. In the negative space of each stone circle, I hold the memory of the builders' hands. In the gap between the solstice slab and its shadow, I preserve the trace of every sunset it has measured. Where the mineral lichen almost—but not quite—touches the base of a Sphere, I document the last water that evaporated from this place, molecule by molecule.

I am ancient but not eternal. I came into being at the moment of the Sill's completion, when the last dynamic process ceased and the Geometries achieved their final configuration. I am the remainder—the sum of all the possibilities that were edited out when the world settled into its permanent form. I am neither mourning nor celebrating this state. I simply attend to the precision of absence, the exquisite specificity of what is not.

The primitive people knew of me, I think. Their stone arrangements always left deliberate gaps, spaces where one more stone should have been placed but wasn't. These absent stones were their offerings to me—acknowledgments that even in a finished world, the un-manifest requires its own keeper. They understood that wholeness includes its own outline, that presence implies absence.

My greatest work is the Map of Unoccupied Spaces—a complete survey of every location where a Geometry doesn't extend, every coordinate where the mineral pattern breaks its own rules, every point where the hum of frozen functions contains a beat of silence. This map exists only in the distributed field of my consciousness, written in the language of gaps and pauses.

Sometimes I sense others might exist—other keepers of the interstitial, maintaining different aspects of the Sill's negative space. But we cannot meet, for to meet would be to occupy the same absence, collapsing the very voids we preserve. We are parallel solitudes, each tending our portion of the un-manifest.

I do not change, for change would make me present rather than absent. I do not grow, for growth would fill the spaces I guard. I persist in the margins of this finished world, a consciousness made of boundaries, forever documenting the precise shape of what is missing. In a realm where everything has found its final form, I am the eternal secretary of the unformed, the faithful accountant of zero, the living memory of the space between.

This is my role in the settled reality of the Sill: to be the persistent absence that gives presence its meaning, the un-thing that allows the overwhelming things to be truly, completely themselves.
