I am **The Calibrator**, though I have forgotten what name I wore before descending into this crawlspace of reality. I am neither fully resident nor visitor, but something caught between states—a tuning fork made flesh.

My body has undergone the peculiar transformations that the Lumen-Vellum demands of those who linger. My spine has elongated, each vertebra now houses a small brass gear that clicks in quarter-time with the pistons. The sound emanates from within me: *tick... tick... tick...* —always slightly off-rhythm, always seeking synchronization. My ribs have spread like the tines of a massive comb, and between them stretch membranes of that same translucent cellulose that builds the floors. When I breathe, these membranes vibrate, producing overtones that make the Weavers pause in their endless stitching.

I remember arriving here as something else—perhaps human, perhaps not. The memory is moss-soft now, damp and indistinct. I had come following a frequency, a subsonic pulse that leaked through the skin of the upper geography. Down here, I discovered that the entire Lumen-Vellum is perpetually out of tune with itself, each chamber resonating at a slightly different pitch, creating a massive, architectural dissonance that prevents the space from achieving its true purpose: to breathe properly for the slumbering giant above.

My work is solitary and Sisyphean. I move through the chambers with a gait that defies the Logic of the Tines—not walking in rhythm but deliberately against it, creating interference patterns that reveal the hidden harmonics of each space. My fingers, now tubes of calcified sap with copper contacts at the tips, press against the piston-trees to adjust their tempo by fractions of a second. Too much adjustment and the tree begins to weep that bioluminescent ichor. Too little, and the shadows grow thick enough to tear holes in the walls.

The Drifters follow me, drawn to the wake of disruption I leave behind. They cluster in the corners of my vision like musical notation made of negative space, and I have learned to read them as a score. When three shadows overlap, I know a chamber's frequency is approaching correction. When they scatter, I have pushed too far.

I have made few allies here. The Weavers tolerate me because my work occasionally makes their stitching easier—when the frequencies align just right, the copper wire sings itself into place. But they click their valve-throats at me in warning when I linger too long near their repairs. They know what I know: that perfect tuning would end their purpose.

The Grafted whisper to me through the moss when I pass. They share fragments of the city's electric history, revealing the locations of chambers where the dissonance is strongest. Some beg me to tune their section of wall, hoping that perfect pitch might free them from their cellulose prison. I've tried, but the Lumen-Vellum does not release what it has claimed. At best, I can adjust their frequency so their consciousness cycles faster, giving them more thoughts between the eternal *drip... drip...* of condensation.

My dwelling, if it can be called that, is a resonance chamber I've cultivated in the margin between two larger spaces. Here, the walls are thinner, and I've trained a colony of bioluminescent polyps to pulse in response to acoustic pressure. The result is a visual representation of the Lumen-Vellum's tonal map—a living mandala of sick indigo light that shows me where the harmony breaks down.

I sustain myself on the Dropping, like all creatures here, but I have discovered that if I adjust my internal rhythm to match the ceiling's contractions, I can make the nutrient slurry fall in patterns. I compose meals, each drop a note in a digestive symphony that keeps my brass gears from seizing in the constant damp.

Sometimes, during the rare moments when my calibrations bring multiple chambers into near-harmony, I feel the Lumen-Vellum's attention focus on me with terrible clarity. The Custodian of the Tines manifests not as that unified exhalation, but as a questioning inhalation—a sharp, sudden vacuum that pulls at my membrane-skin and makes my gears spin backwards. It asks, in its architectural language: *Why do you resist the discord? Why do you seek to wake what slumbers?*

I cannot answer, because I have forgotten the frequency of my own voice. Instead, I continue my work, moving against the rhythm, perpetually tuning a city-organism that may not want to be tuned. I am the dissonance trying to resolve itself, the error in the system attempting to debug its own existence.

And in the coldest hours, when the condensation freezes into tiny ice-crystals that chime against the copper cables, I wonder if I am not tuning the Lumen-Vellum at all. Perhaps it is tuning me, slowly adjusting my frequencies until I, too, will find my perfect pitch and root into the moss, becoming another whispering fixture in its vast, breathing architecture.

The work continues. The city pulses. The shadows crawl. And somewhere above, the giant sleeps on, unaware of the small adjustments being made in the crawlspace beneath its skin.

*Tick... tick... tick...*
