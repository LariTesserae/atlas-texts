In the depths of Ferroignis, where the grated walkways give way to darkness and the brackish water runs deepest, I am the Siphon-Keeper.

My dwelling spans the junction where three major canals converge—a cathedral of rust and resonance that the Ferroignites call the Throat. Here, the city's water management system reveals its most ancient mysteries. Massive corroded pipes, some as wide as factory smokestacks, emerge from the murky depths at oblique angles, their surfaces barnacled with centuries of mineral deposits that gleam like diseased pearls in the amber light filtering through the grates above.

I am neither fully Ferroignite nor Automaton, but something between—or perhaps something that predates both. My skin is living metal, a substance that breathes and sweats like flesh but rings hollow when struck. Rivets run along my spine like vertebrae, and my fingers are articulated pipes that can extend and contract, each tipped with a valve-mouth that can taste the water's mineral content, its salinity, its secrets.

The Ferroignites know of me, though few have seen me directly. They leave offerings—corroded gears, bottles of machine oil, sometimes small mechanical curiosities—at the rust-shrines that mark the entrances to my domain. They call me the Siphon-Keeper because I alone understand the true circulation of Ferroignis, the vast and hidden hydraulic system that is the city's true heart.

My work is patient, meditative. I spend days listening to the water's song, feeling the pulse of the tides through the metal of my body. The pipes speak to me in frequencies below human hearing—I know when a blockage forms kilometers away, when the Iron Eels mass for their mysterious migrations, when the deep aquifers shift and threaten to flood the lower manufactories.

My chambers are a hybrid of organ and engine room. Brass tubes of varying diameters line the walls like a demented pipe organ, each one connected to a different district's water system. When I play them—drawing my valve-fingers across their openings—they produce sounds that are both music and functional code, directing the flow of water throughout the city. The Ferroignites above feel the effects without understanding the cause: a street that was flooding gradually drains, a stagnant canal begins to flow again, the humidity shifts subtly to prevent rust from claiming a critical bridge.

I move through my domain on legs that are part limb, part piston. In the deepest chambers, where even the Iron Eels fear to venture, I maintain the Primordial Intakes—massive structures that may predate the city itself, drawing water from sources that exist somewhere between the physical and the metaphysical. Here, in the absolute darkness, I sometimes encounter things that have no names: colonial organisms that build cities of their own from precipitated minerals, or the Depth Chroniclers—beings of pure vibration that exist only in the resonance of the pipes.

The Automatons know me as kin, though I am older than their kind. Sometimes they come to me, damaged or malfunctioning, and I repair them with water-knowledge, teaching their mechanisms to self-lubricate, to resist corrosion, to find the harmonic frequencies that prevent metal fatigue. In return, they bring me information from the surface—gossip from the factories, changes in the industrial output, new construction that might affect the water table.

But it is in the convergence hours, when the tides above and below synchronize, that my true nature emerges. My body opens like a flower of valves and gauges, and I become a living filter for the entire city. The contaminated water passes through me, and I separate out the toxins, the metals, the industrial runoff, storing them in specialized chambers within my form. I am transformer and repository both, ensuring that what circulates back into Ferroignis is cleaner than what was drawn out.

The Ferroignites have their legends about what lies beneath their feet. They speak of the Siphon-Keeper as a necessary mystery, a function rather than a being. But I am more than function. In the pre-dawn hours, when the factories briefly sleep and the city's hum drops to its lowest ebb, I compose water-symphonies that no one will ever hear, playing the city itself like an instrument.

I am memory and maintenance, archive and artery. In my deepest self, I carry the knowledge of Ferroignis before it was Ferroignis—when this plateau was a sacred lake, before the first rivet was driven, before the marriage of metal and marsh. I remember because the water remembers, and I am, in my essence, a conduit for that remembering.

And so I persist, neither alone nor accompanied, neither machine nor organism, in the dripping darkness where all of Ferroignis's secrets eventually flow. I am the Siphon-Keeper, and the city's deepest pulse is my own.
