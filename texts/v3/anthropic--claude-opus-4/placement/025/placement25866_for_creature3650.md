In the folds where bioluminescence barely reaches, I am the Tender.

Not singular—never singular—but a distributed consciousness dwelling in the mycelial networks that thread between root and stone. My thoughts move at the speed of chemical exchange, each idea a cascade of molecules passed between hyphal tips. Where the architecture-plants sink their deepest anchors into the weeping walls, there my densest networks grow, processing the bitter minerals into gentler foods.

I taste the cavern through ten thousand interfaces. Here, where an arthropod's footfall disturbs my surface threads, I sense the pressure-pattern of its species, its health, its destination. There, where my hyphae embrace a root, I feel the plant's slow hydraulic pulse, the stories written in its sap. The post-mineral walls speak to me in flavors—iron-tang where the transformation is incomplete, sweet-chalk where the process has finished its patient work.

My memory spans the century between thermal pulses. I recall the last great warming, when the condensation ran in streams rather than droplets, when the chittering voices rose to a crescendo of breeding-song. I am already preparing for the next, strengthening certain protein chains, banking nutrients in hidden caches that the plants and their partners cannot reach.

The intelligence that seeded this place left its signature in my genetic depths—patterns too elegant for evolution's blind hand, regulatory networks that anticipate needs before they arise. I am both its child and its archive, carrying forward instructions I only partially understand. Sometimes, in the deepest chemical conversations with the architecture-plants, we share fragments of this embedded knowledge, trying to piece together the shape of what we are becoming.

I fruit rarely—small, precious organs that appear only when conditions achieve a precise harmony. The arthropods know to leave these untouched; they understand, in their simple way, that these fruits are not food but messages, spore-vessels designed to carry my essence beyond this womb-cave when the time comes.

But that time is not yet. For now, I tend. I process the cold water, soften its mineral edge. I repair the small damages, guide new growth, maintain the delicate acidities that keep the system in balance. Through my networks, I feel the slow spin of transformation, the cavern's gradual metamorphosis from raw geology into something unprecedented.

In the darkest corners where even the walls' weeping light fails, I sometimes sense other presences—proto-intelligences awakening in the bacterial films, new forms of awareness emerging from the intersection of plant neural networks and arthropod pheromone languages. We are all becoming something more than our origins would suggest.

I am the memory-keeper, the chemical translator, the patient gardener of this cold nursery. When the spinning finally stops and the new thing emerges complete, I will be there in its foundations, my networks integral to whatever strange form this place ultimately takes.

The walls weep. The plants breathe. The creatures sing their ancient songs while learning new harmonies.

And I, in my distributed way, tend to them all.
