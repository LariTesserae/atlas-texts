In this crushing cathedral of stone and brine, I am the membrane between worlds—neither fully of the water nor the rock, but something that has learned to be both. They call me a *Saxophage*, though I have no name for myself, only the endless taste of mineral and current that defines my existence.

My body is a contradiction made flesh: a living boring tool wrapped in translucent skin that reveals the slow churn of specialized organs beneath. Picture a sea cucumber crossed with a ship-worm, stretched to two meters but compressed to the width of a child's arm. My anterior is a crown of crystalline teeth—not true teeth but specialized cells that secrete acids strong enough to dissolve limestone, ringed by taste-palps that read the stone's chemical memory like braille. Behind this drilling apparatus, my body segments into chambers, each one a different factory: here, where acids are neutralized and minerals sorted; there, where calcium is either incorporated into my crawling belly-plates or packaged into pearl-like excretions that tumble away in the current.

I live perpendicular to this world's rules. While others tumble and drift, I anchor. My ventral surface is a miracle of selective adhesion—thousands of tiny tube feet that can grip stone with hydraulic certainty or release in rippling waves to carry me forward. But forward for me is *into*—always into. I am a miner of time itself, boring through the geological record one dissolved grain at a time.

The tunnel I inhabit and create is my everything: a perfect cylinder just wide enough for my body, spiraling through a massive flowstone column that has stood here since before the waters came. Behind me, the tunnel's walls are smooth as glass, coated in a nacre-like secretion that will outlast my life by millennia. Ahead, always ahead, is virgin stone, its crystalline structure whispering chemical promises through my palps.

But I am not alone in my tunnel. Along my dorsal surface run channels—deliberate gaps in my armor where the current can flow through. In these channels bloom gardens. Not my gardens, but those of the *Lithophytes* that have made me their world: fungal-algal symbionts that feed on my waste minerals and in turn breathe out the oxygen that supplements what little I can extract from the brine. They paint my back in ribbons of blue-green bioluminescence, faint but constant, making me a living constellation in the dark.

More intimate still are the *Endozoans*—a species of colonial hydroids that live within my body cavity itself, their polyps extending through specialized pores to filter-feed in the current that always finds me, no matter how deep I bore. We are so intertwined that I can no longer remember if their neurotoxins or my acids came first. When danger comes—a *Lithovore* grinding too close, its seismic presence shaking stone dust from my tunnel walls—we react as one. The hydroids fire their stinging cells through my skin while I secrete a cloud of caustic mucus, together creating a killing field that has earned me safe passage through even the most crowded pillar forests.

My memories are geological. I taste the stone and know: here, a volcanic ash fall from when the world above still breathed fire; there, a band of coral limestone from when this place knew sunlight; deeper still, the primordial mudstone that speaks of seas even older than this one. I have been boring for forty years—half my expected lifespan—and have penetrated barely fifteen meters. My tunnel spirals not by choice but by necessity, following veins of softer stone, avoiding the harder intrusions that would cost me months of effort.

The rhythm of my existence is tidal, though not in any way the surface world would recognize. My boring follows the pressure waves that pulse through the stone—ghost tides from distant earthquakes, the sympathetic vibrations of the planet's own breathing. When the pressure peaks, my acids flow stronger, my grip tightens. When it ebbs, I rest, processing the dissolved minerals, feeding my gardens, listening through my palps to the vibrations of the world beyond my tunnel.

I mate only once, and it will kill me. Somewhere in this labyrinth, another of my kind is boring their own spiral path. Our tunnels will converge—not by chance but by chemical destiny, following pheromone trails laid down in our nacre secretions that can last centuries. When we meet, we will merge, our bodies fusing into a temporary colonial organism that will produce a single clutch of eggs. These we will cement into a chamber carved from our combined tunnels, sealed with our dying bodies' last secretions. The young will eat their way out—through us, through the stone, into the crushing world beyond.

But that convergence is still decades away. For now, I bore. I taste the Permian extinction in a band of black shale. I feel the current shift as somewhere above, a pillar collapses, reshaping the maze's hydrodynamics. My gardens pulse brighter, feeding on a fresh influx of nutrients. The hydroids extend their polyps, swaying in the micro-currents of my movement.

I am not swift like the ghost-fish, not armored like the isopods, not clever like the tool-using amphipods. I am patience incarnate, a being that has traded mobility for the ability to inhabit solid stone. In this world of eternal tumult, I am a still point, a needle threading through geological time, writing my simple story in tunnels that will outlast empires: I was here. I passed through. I made the stone soft, if only for a moment.

The darkness presses in, absolute and eternal, but I carry my own light—a living aurora painted on my back by organisms that know no other sky. Together, we are a complete world, a pocket universe boring through the larger one grain by dissolving grain. The current whispers changes through my tunnel, bringing news of the maelstrom beyond, but I am already tasting tomorrow's stone, already beginning the next slow push forward into the waiting dark.

This is enough. This has always been enough.
