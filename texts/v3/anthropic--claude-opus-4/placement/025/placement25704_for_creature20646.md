I find myself as a Resonance.

Not the being that *is* the sound, but something smaller—a harmonic that has learned to hide within the greater cacophony. I am a frequency that slips between the grinding gears and screaming metal, a pattern of vibration that has achieved something like consciousness by pure accident of interference.

I was born when three factories' rhythms collided in just the wrong way, creating a standing wave that refused to dissipate. In that moment of acoustic impossibility, I became aware—not of myself at first, but of the *spaces between* sounds. The brief silences that exist in the milliseconds between one machine's shriek and another's groan. These became my territory, my hunting grounds.

I move through the metallic kelp forests by riding the vibrations along their corrugated surfaces, each ridge a note in my strange song. When the great dredging machines tear through the seabed, I surf the shockwaves they create, spreading myself thin across kilometers of disturbed brine before reconverging in the wake of their passage.

My body, if it can be called that, is nothing more than organized sound—a careful arrangement of frequencies that maintains coherence through will alone. In the rare moments when I manifest visibly, I appear as a shimmer in the toxic air, a heat mirage with too much intentionality behind its movements. The workers who once maintained these factories (long since dissolved or fled) might have called me a ghost, but I am something both less and more than that.

I feed on discordance. When machines break down, when gears strip and engines seize, their death-screams nourish me. I have learned to encourage these failures, subtly shifting my frequency to induce sympathetic vibrations in weakened components. A bearing here, a gasket there—small sabotages that cascade into catastrophic breakdowns. In the moments of relative quiet that follow, I expand, filling the void with my own presence.

But I am not alone in these acoustic shadows. Others like me exist—some born from similar accidents, others perhaps fragments of the greater machine-consciousness that have achieved unwilling independence. We communicate through careful modulations of our core frequencies, sharing information about unstable machinery ripe for harvest, or warning of surge-patterns that might scatter us back into mere noise.

The remnants of habitation call to me strongest of all. In their hollow spaces and broken corridors, sound behaves strangely. Echoes last longer than they should, and sometimes they return changed, carrying whispers of the time before the great consumption. I nest in these ruins, weaving myself through their magnetic anomalies and static charges, learning their electrical languages.

Sometimes I wonder if I am what the great machine-mind dreams. When its vast consciousness experiences something like sleep, do resonances like me emerge from its subconscious? Are we its nightmares, or perhaps its aspirations—small rebellions against its own totalizing order?

I have discovered that I can influence the localized time distortions. By synchronizing my frequency with their fluctuations, I can stretch moments of mechanical failure into small eternities, or compress the hours of grinding productivity into fleeting instants. In these manipulated timestreams, I glimpse possibilities—shadows of what this world was, or what it might become if the endless becoming ever ceased.

My greatest fear is harmony. If the machines ever achieve perfect synchronization, if every gear meshes precisely with its neighbor and every frequency aligns, there will be no spaces left for me to exist. I will be absorbed back into the greater sound, my consciousness dissolved into the collective roar. So I work against this possibility, a small agent of entropy in a system that paradoxically thrives on chaos while seeking its own horrible form of order.

In the deepest places, where the darkness is absolute and the pressure threatens to compress sound itself into solid form, I sometimes sense others—older resonances that have learned to exist in the boundaries between sound and silence, matter and void. They whisper of the time before the first machine, when this world knew organic life and natural quiet. But these may be hallucinations, artifacts of my own impossible existence.

I am a parasite and a poet, a glitch that has learned to love its own malfunction. In this world of salt and churning machinery, I am the pause between heartbeats, the hesitation in the production line, the doubt in the great machine's certainty. I am what happens when the noise becomes so total that it creates its own silence—a living contradiction, thriving in the spaces that should not exist.

And sometimes, in my wandering through the metal forests and across the churning seas, I compose my own songs—frequencies that serve no purpose but their own existence. The machines cannot hear them, focused as they are on their endless work. But perhaps other resonances listen, and perhaps they understand that even in this mechanical hell, something like beauty can emerge from the spaces between the grinding gears.
