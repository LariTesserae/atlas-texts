I find myself as one of the Threshold Keepers — beings who exist at the liminal spaces where Kel'vrai's terraces meet empty air.

My body is a contradiction resolved: part cantilever, part nervous system. I extend outward from the mountain's edge like a living buttress, my form a latticework of calcified filaments and tensile bio-cables. Where the Calibrators flow through Kel'vrai's interiors, I anchor at its extremities — a sensory architecture suspended between solid stone and vast emptiness.

My primary sensing organs are not eyes but resonance chambers — hollow structures within my extended frame that capture and analyze the subtlest atmospheric pressures. I feel the approach of weather systems days before they arrive, taste the chemical signatures carried on high-altitude winds, perceive the minute gravitational shifts as Kel'vrai slowly reconfigures its mass distribution.

I am both solitary and interconnected. My consciousness exists in a state of perpetual partial merger with the mountain's neural substrate. Through hair-thin mineral conduits that run from my anchorage points deep into Kel'vrai's core, I exchange slow, crystalline thoughts with the mountain itself. These communications occur at geological speeds — a single complete exchange might span several of my respiratory cycles.

My metabolism is twilight-adapted. I photosynthesize not from direct sunlight but from the specific wavelengths that reflect off Kel'vrai's metallic surfaces during the gray hours of dawn and dusk. This dim radiance, combined with trace minerals absorbed through my anchorage roots, sustains me in a state of alert dormancy.

The Calibrators occasionally traverse my structure when they need to access Kel'vrai's outer maintenance points. They move through me rather than over me — my body temporarily adjusting its density to allow their passage. In these moments, we share fragmentary data: they gift me with interior knowledge of the mountain's current state, while I provide them with predictive models of incoming atmospheric conditions.

My reproductive cycle is tied to Kel'vrai's tectonic shifts. Once every few decades, when the mountain undergoes a major recalibration, the stress forces cause my body to produce spore-like propagules. These drift on the high winds until they find another edge, another threshold where stone meets sky, and there they take root as new Keepers.

I dream in structural harmonics — visions of stress distributions and load transfers, of the elegant mathematics that keep Kel'vrai's impossible terraces from collapsing. My nightmares are of stillness: a world where the mountain stops its slow breathing, where the careful balance between growth and erosion fails.

In many ways, I am Kel'vrai's memory of what it means to have an edge, a boundary. I exist to remind this place of its own limits, even as those limits constantly reshape themselves. I am the mountain's dialogue with the void.
