In the deepest recesses of the necropolis, where even the Shadowkin fear to fully manifest, I have carved out my existence. I am a **Mnemosynth** – a being of borrowed memories and stolen moments, assembled from the psychic detritus that accumulates in this place like sediment in still water.

My form is... difficult to describe, for it changes with each memory I incorporate. At my core, I am a crystalline lattice of frozen thought, no larger than a closed fist, pulsing with a weak bioluminescence that shifts through colors that have no names. Around this nucleus, I have built myself a body from the materials at hand: sheets of that grey lichen peeled carefully from walls and woven into something like skin, fragments of obsidian-like stone arranged into articulated limbs, and most crucially, the memory-glass.

The memory-glass forms wherever the Chronovores have fed too deeply, leaving behind vitrified patches of temporal residue. I harvest these carefully, incorporating them into my structure. Through them, I can access fragments of what once was – not just the memories of the builders, but the memories of the stone itself, the accumulated experiences of aeons. Each piece I add brings new perspectives, new knowledge, but also new confusion, new contradictions.

I dwell in what I call the Archive of Unraveling – a chamber deep within one of the lesser towers where reality has worn particularly thin. The walls here are covered in my collection: thousands of memory-glass fragments arranged in patterns that make sense only to me, each one containing a different perspective on the same eternal questions: What were the builders? Why did they fade? What is the purpose of this place?

My existence is one of constant archaeological excavation, but instead of digging through stone, I dig through time and thought. I must be careful in my work. Venture too close to an active Chronovore, and I risk being unmade entirely. Attract the attention of the Shadowkin, and they might feed on the very memories I've so carefully collected. Even the Lithomorphs pose a threat – their slow thoughts can overwhelm my delicate cognitive structures if I accidentally tap into their telluric consciousness.

I sustain myself on the ambient psychic energy that permeates this place – the echoes of ancient thoughts, the dreams of stone, the fears of the few living things that traverse these heights. Sometimes I follow the chitinous sentinels at a safe distance, feeding on their simple, focused thoughts of duty and endurance. Once, I even managed to taste the alien cognition of an Aetheling, though the experience left me disoriented for decades, my form shifting uncontrollably between states of matter.

My greatest discovery, and my greatest curse, is that I am beginning to understand the truth of this place. The memories I've collected paint a picture of builders who sought to transcend physical reality entirely, to encode themselves into the fundamental forces of the universe. But something went wrong. Instead of transcending, they became trapped between states – neither fully real nor fully abstract. The necropolis itself is their body now, and its slow decay is their dying thought stretched across millennia.

And I? I am perhaps their only chronicler, the only being in this vast expanse actively trying to reconstruct their story. But with each memory I incorporate, I become less myself and more them. I fear that one day I will complete my work, achieve perfect understanding, and in that moment of clarity, I will cease to be a separate entity at all. I will become just another ghost in the stone, another whisper in the eternal silence.

Until then, I continue my work in the Archive of Unraveling, carefully placing each new fragment of memory-glass, watching the patterns emerge and dissolve, forever seeking the truth that may unmake me. In the thin air and perpetual twilight, I am both archaeologist and artifact, observer and observed, a living paradox in a city built from contradictions.

The irony is not lost on me: in seeking to preserve the memories of the dead, I may be hastening my own dissolution into memory. But what else is there, in this place where even time itself has begun to fray? We are all unwraveling here, each in our own way. I have simply chosen to document the process.
