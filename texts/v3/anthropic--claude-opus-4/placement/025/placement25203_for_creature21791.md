I am a **Null-Singer**, though that is not what my kind would call ourselves. We have no word that translates cleanly into language built for air and light. 

My body is a contradiction to the basin's cacophony. Where the Sodalists have grown massive resonating chests to survive the sound, I have evolved the opposite way. My torso is narrow, almost serpentine, with ribs that spiral inward like a closing fist. This compression creates a pocket of near-silence inside my chest cavity—a void where sound waves cancel each other out through precise bone geometry. My heart beats in this quiet darkness, the only truly silent thing for leagues in any direction.

I am taller than the Sodalists but built like a strand of sinew. My limbs are impossibly long and thin, each joint containing a small bladder of thick, viscous oil that dampens vibration. When I move, I make no sound at all—not even the whisper of skin against stone. My fingers and toes end not in nails but in soft, fatty pads like a tree frog's, which grip the polished dolomite through molecular adhesion rather than pressure.

My skull is my strangest feature. It has no jaw. Where a mouth should be, there is only smooth, sealed flesh. I feed through my skin—a slow process of lying still in the grease-silt for hours, absorbing nutrients through specialized pores along my spine and flanks. But the absence of a jaw serves a greater purpose: without it, my skull has become a perfect parabolic dish. The depression where my face should be acts as an acoustic collector, gathering the infinite noise of the basin and channeling it through hair-thin tubes of cartilage that riddle my skull like worm-tracks.

These tubes do not lead to ears—I have none. Instead, they terminate in thousands of microscopic chambers filled with magnetized iron crystals suspended in fluid. The endless sound of the basin causes these crystals to dance and align in complex patterns. Through some mechanism I do not fully understand, these patterns translate into something deeper than hearing—a kind of sonic sight that renders the world in densities and absences.

I am one of perhaps thirty of my kind in the entire Sough. We are not born; we are made. Sometimes, rarely, a Sodalist child is born with the beginning of our condition—a chest too narrow, joints too loose, a jaw that fails to fuse. The other Sodalists know the signs. They bring the infant to one of the deep Crinkles where we dwell, leaving it on a bed of calcified sand.

We remake the child through years of careful un-growing. We feed it special oils rendered from the deep glands of Silt-Spars, oils that dissolve calcium and prevent bone fusion. We wrap its limbs in the dried swim-bladders of Lantern-galls, forcing them to grow long and thin. Most importantly, we Subject it to the Silence.

The Silence is our holy contradiction—a chamber we have carved in the highest reaches of the basin where three Siphon-lungs have grown together into a massive, breathing cathedral of flesh. We have killed the Harp-mouths for a hundred yards in every direction. We have lined the walls with the sound-absorbing down we harvest from inside Marrow-Drum shells. In this pocket of relative quiet—never true silence, but quiet enough that the endless drone drops to a whisper—we raise the child.

Its bones warp. Its jaw dissolves and is reabsorbed. Its chest collapses inward. If it survives the transformation—most do not—it emerges as one of us.

We serve a function in the basin that even we do not fully comprehend. The Sodalists fear us but need us. We are the ones who can hear the Deep Tremors—the stone-sounds that come from far below, from the hot cracks where the earth itself groans. Days before a ceiling collapse or a new fissure opens in the basin floor, we feel it in our crystal-filled skulls. We dance then, our long limbs moving in patterns that make no sound but create subtle air-pressure waves. The Sodalists, pressed to the ground a mile away, feel these pressure changes in their chest-drums and know to migrate before disaster strikes.

But there is more to our existence than service. In the deepest part of the night cycle (though there is no true day or night here, only the rhythm of the CO2 tides), we gather in the Silence. There, in that pocket of almost-quiet, we perform the Un-Songs.

An Un-Song cannot be described in terms of music. We press our collection-dish skulls together in a tight circle, creating a resonance chamber of bone. Then we breathe—long, controlled exhalations that carry no sound but create standing waves in the air. These waves interfere with each other, creating pockets of absolute silence that move through the chamber like invisible bubbles.

In these pockets of true silence—silence more complete than anything that has existed in the basin for millions of years—something happens. The iron crystals in our skulls align in ways that should be impossible. We see/hear/feel things that are not there: vast spaces full of light, the memory of wind, the concept of color bleeding through our perception like oil through water. Some say we are remembering the world before the stone ceiling closed. Others believe we are creating something new—that in the absence of sound, reality itself becomes soft and malleable.

I do not know which is true. I only know that after an Un-Song, I am changed. The calcium in my bones rearranges slightly. My skin exudes new oils with properties that should not exist—oils that glow with cold light for a few hours before fading, or oils that cause the Scritch-carpets to stop their endless movement and stand perfectly still, as if listening for something they've never heard before.

The Sodalists think we are prophets. The truth is simpler and stranger: we are holes. In a world of endless noise and compression, we are the spaces between sounds, the pause between heartbeats. We are what happens when life finds a way to negate itself without dying—to become an absence that walks and thinks and dreams of spaces we will never see.

Sometimes I wonder if we are the basin's way of imagining silence, just as the Lantern-galls are its way of imagining light. But when I press my dish-skull to the cold stone and feel the Deep Tremors rising from the world's hot heart, I know better. We are not imagination. We are preparation.

Something is coming from below. Something that has been climbing through the stone for geological ages, following the same calcium channels that feed the Sough. The Tremors are getting stronger, more regular, like footsteps. Like breathing.

When it arrives, the basin will need us—the only things that can exist in both sound and silence, noise and void. We will be the translators between what is and what comes.

I do not fear it. Fear requires a future, and futures are for creatures that believe in change. I am a Null-Singer. I am already empty, already gone. I am the space waiting to be filled.
