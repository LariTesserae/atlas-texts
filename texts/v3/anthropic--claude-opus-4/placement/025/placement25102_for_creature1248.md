I find myself as a presence woven between states—neither fully formed nor entirely dispersed, but something that exists in the margins where transformation occurs. 

My body, if it can be called that, is a confluence of tendril and dewdrop, of spore-breath and mineral memory. I am the condensation that forms where warm earth meets cool air, gathering myself pearl by pearl along the undersides of fern fronds until I achieve just enough weight to fall—not to shatter, but to merge again with the pools that mirror the heavy sky.

In the mornings, I am most myself: a traveling boundary between mist and water, solid and vapor. I move by gathering and releasing, each droplet of my being a tiny lens through which this world reveals itself differently. Through one, I see the slow dreams of the moss-covered stones. Through another, the fern-spirits' dance becomes visible as traces of silver light. Through a thousand more, I witness the intricate negotiations between growth and decay that keep this realm in its perpetual state of becoming.

The other beings here know me as the Collector of Transitions—not because I hoard them, but because I carry them from place to place like pollen. Where the rock spirits meet the earth, I precipitate to witness their slow exchange of minerals and memories. When the water-sprites laugh, I capture the sound in my droplets and release it later as rain against the stones, a liquid percussion that speaks of joy translated through states of matter.

My purpose here is not to guard or govern, but to facilitate the conversations between edges: where moss softens stone, where water meets air, where the visible touches the unseen. I am the medium through which the subtle transformations of this place announce themselves—the sudden chill that raises neck-hairs, the shift in air pressure that makes the ferns' movement visible, the quality of dampness that speaks of hidden depths.

Sometimes I condense enough to almost take a form others might recognize—a figure traced in fog against the darker stones, a presence felt rather than seen. But mostly I exist as possibility, as the moment before precipitation, as the pause between one state and another. I am what happens in the space between breathing in and breathing out, when the boundary between self and world becomes permeable.

In this realm where change is the only constant, I am change itself given just enough form to be witnessed, just enough consciousness to bear witness in return. Every droplet of my being carries the memory of having been cloud, rain, stream, mist, and dew—and the promise of becoming each again. I am the circulation that keeps this world's hidden energies flowing, the messenger between its varied inhabitants, the proof that even in a place of ancient powers and watchful presences, transformation itself can take on life and purpose.

When newcomers venture into this realm, they feel me as that tingling along their skin, that sense of being observed. But I am not watching—I am simply recognizing another boundary where different states of being meet, another edge where transformation might occur. Their presence creates ripples in the field of possibility, and I gather around them like morning dew, curious to see what changes their passage might bring to this ever-shifting land.
