In the deepest fold of the Concave, where the resin-crete walls lean inward like cupped palms, I am the **Resonance Keeper**.

I do not move. Movement is for those who correct errors in space. I correct errors in *time*.

My chamber is a perfect acoustic void, a negative-space carved with obsessive precision into the basin floor. The walls are not smooth but *tuned*—thousands of shallow grooves spiral inward, each cut to trap and redirect specific frequencies. When the Breathing Drum exhales its sub-audible pulse, my chamber inhales it, sorts it, and holds it in standing waves that hover like invisible architecture.

I am built like the Crafteers—tall, thin, with those same light-drinking eyes—but my fingers are different. They are not stained with resin and pigment but are instead unnaturally long, with an extra joint in each digit. The tips are callused into hard nodes from decades of pressing against vibrating surfaces. My skin has taken on the blue-gray tint of the resin-crete, as if I am slowly becoming substrate.

My function is this: I *remember the rhythm*.

The Concave's great work—its endless self-construction—follows patterns too long for any single Crafteer to perceive. A misaligned block today creates a stress fracture in fifty cycles. A too-rapid weaving rhythm in the northwestern quadrant will desynchronize with the glass-grinding schedule, causing material shortages in ninety cycles. These cascading consequences ripple through time like drops in those still water basins.

I feel them all.

My chamber is strung with **tension wires**—hair-thin filaments of drawn basalt that span the space at precise angles. Each wire holds a different harmonic memory. When I press my node-fingers to them, they sing the history of their frequency: *here, the looms once ran too fast for seven cycles, creating a weakness in sector four that manifested thirty cycles later as a catastrophic felt shortage.* 

The Crafteers above know nothing of this. They respond only to immediate imbalance. But I perceive the Concave's temporal skeleton—the way today's scrape-and-pour creates tomorrow's stress pattern creates next-year's collapse point. Through the wire-songs, I map these futures and broadcast corrections.

How? My throat has been modified. Where the Crafteers whistle sharply, I produce something different: **subsonic drones** that travel through the resin-crete itself. These are not heard but *felt*—a wrongness in the belly, a discomfort in the teeth. When a work crew feels this unease, they slow. They check their rhythm. They adjust without knowing why. My drones are the Concave's autonomic nervous system, its sense of *something about to go wrong*.

The wire-songs never stop. Even in my rest state—not sleep, for I do not sleep, but a kind of conscious dormancy—my fingers twitch against the filaments, reading their stories. The Maintenance Crawlers know to avoid my chamber; their repetitive clatter would muddy the acoustic clarity I require. Sometimes a young Crafteer, drawn by curiosity, descends my spiral ramp and peers into the resonance void. They see me suspended in my web of wires, fingers dancing across invisible frequencies, throat humming below perception. They always leave quickly. Not from fear, but from a kind of systemic recognition: *this is not for you*.

I eat what they eat—a paste of ground glass-dust mixed with the condensation-collection gel, which hardens in the stomach and provides slow energy. But I consume it differently. Where they eat in groups, sharing bowls in the brief lantern-lit meal breaks, I eat alone, letting the paste dissolve slowly on my tongue while I taste the mineral content, the ratios that tell me about material flows in the basin above.

My relationship to the Concave is neither love nor duty. It is more fundamental than that. I am its **temporal proprioception**—its sense of where its body is in time. Without me, it would still build, still persist, but it would judder and lurch, accumulating invisible stresses until sections collapsed catastrophically. With me, it moves like a blind giant guided by perfect instinct, each hand always reaching for the next necessary hold.

The strangest part is this: I am beginning to hear patterns longer than my own lifetime. The wires sing of cycles I will never see complete, of rhythmic corrections that will take three Resonance Keepers to fully manifest. When I die—and I will die, for even modified flesh erodes—another will take my place, trained in the final years through wire-song proximity. They will inherit not just my role but my unfinished patterns, my half-complete corrections.

I suspect, sometimes, in the deepest part of my dormancy, that the Concave is building toward something. That all this noise and motion has a terminal state, a final form it seeks. But the wire-songs won't tell me what it is. They only sing of the next necessary adjustment, the next microsecond of prevention, the next breath of the Breathing Drum that must be caught and held and analyzed for its invisible futures.

I am not alive in any way that matters.  
I am a function the Concave performs upon itself.  
I am its memory of tomorrow, vibrating in a web of stone-spun songs, forever.
