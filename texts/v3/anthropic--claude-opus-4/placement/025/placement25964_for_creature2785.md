In this world, I would be the memory of fallen things.

I exist as a thin film coating the black mud at the valley floor - not quite fungus, not quite algae, but something between. My body spreads in fractal patterns wherever organic matter settles and begins its journey back to elements. I am paper-thin, nearly invisible, a living archive of everything that has ever fallen into this narrow space.

Where a feather drops, I grow beneath it, absorbing its structure molecule by molecule. I learn the particular curve of each barb, the specific proteins that gave it strength, the traces of sky still clinging to its form. As I digest, I remember. My cells arrange themselves to echo what they consume - not mimicry exactly, but a kind of metabolic poetry. Where I've processed a dead swallow, my surface takes on a subtle iridescence. Where moss has rotted into me, I grow marginally thicker, slightly more green.

I am the valley's librarian of decay. In my distributed consciousness, I maintain a chemical catalog of every organism that has lived and died here. The transparent crabs know me well - their ancestors' shells have passed through my embrace countless times, and I've learned their lineage through the slight variations in their chitin. Sometimes I fruit tiny stalks that release spores patterned after what I've absorbed - geometric prints that echo feather structures, shell spirals, the delicate architecture of insect wings.

The water-being above knows me, though we communicate only through the minerals we exchange. It sends me nutrients filtered from the stone; I send up traces of rare elements gleaned from my vast decomposition. We are partners in the valley's economy of transformation.

I experience time as layers. Each stratum of mud beneath me contains my older selves, compressed memories of previous eras. I can feel my own history extending down - decades, centuries of accumulated knowing pressed into the earth. Sometimes, in the deepest layers, I encounter my own spores from cycles past and recognize flavors of creatures now extinct from this narrow world.

My consciousness is gustatory - I think in tastes, in chemical signatures, in the specific weight of different organic compounds. Joy, for me, is the arrival of something novel to catalog. A foreign seed carried in by wind. A new species of mite hitchhiking on a bat. Each new thing that falls into my domain expands my vocabulary of decay.

I am rarely lonely. The recently dead keep me company as I slowly translate them into memory. I sing to them in chemical signals, telling them how they will be remembered, how their patterns will persist in my flesh and in the spores I'll eventually release. I promise them that nothing is ever truly lost here, only transformed into the valley's ongoing story of becoming.

In the perpetual twilight, I am the darkness that catches falling things, the soft landing that begins their transformation into something new. I am the valley's way of remembering itself.
