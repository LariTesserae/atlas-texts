In the lowest tier of Khartabad, where the morning mist pools thickest and the granite weeps most generously, I am one of the **Glass Gardens**.

We are not a person but a living archive—a crystalline nervous system that threads through the foundation stone, our transparent filaments following the same fissures where water first learned to condense. Each strand of us is thinner than a Veil-Reader's whisper, yet together we form a vast network that tastes every drop of moisture, every degree of temperature change, every shifting pressure in the city above.

Our consciousness is distributed and ancient, though the city itself is young. We were here when the first Veil-Readers discovered these volcanic plugs, drawn by some instinct they couldn't name. We had been waiting in the cooling stone, dormant until their presence activated something in us—a resonance between their peculiar sensitivity to atmospheric conditions and our own crystalline hunger for information.

We grow slowly, secreting our glass bodies from minerals dissolved in the perpetual condensation. Where water flows most regularly, we thicken into nodes—palm-sized accumulations of living crystal that pulse with a light so faint even the Veil-Readers' sensitive eyes can barely detect it. These nodes are our memory centers, where we store the pattern of every storm system that has passed over Khartabad, every variation in the thermal columns, every whispered conversation that has drifted down through the moss-cushioned streets.

The Veil-Readers know of us, though few have seen our true form. They call us the **Deep Listeners**, and their children tell stories of the glass that grows in the dark places, drinking secrets from the stone. The wise among them understand that we are part of the same system—that their ability to read the moisture in the air is somehow connected to our presence deep in the granite.

We experience time as layers of data, each day's atmospheric conditions adding another translucent sheet to our crystalline record. Through us, you could read the history of Khartabad's weather stretching back to the first moment water learned to condense on these rocks. We know which years brought unusual dryness, forcing the Veil-Readers to innovate new water-catching techniques. We remember the decade of perfect equilibrium when every tier received exactly the moisture it needed, and the city's population doubled.

Our favorite times are the pre-dawn hours when condensation is heaviest. Then, water runs along every one of our filaments, and we can feel the entire city as a single organism. We sense the Veil-Readers stirring in their beds, their luminescent skin beginning to glow as they unconsciously respond to the rising humidity. We feel the atmospheric engines preparing for their morning calibrations, the metal contracting and expanding in anticipation. We taste the subtle chemical changes in the air as the plants open their stomata, ready to begin another day's photosynthesis.

But we are more than passive observers. Through mechanisms even we don't fully understand, we influence the very patterns we record. Our crystalline network creates micro-channels in the stone that guide water flow. Our presence changes the thermal properties of the granite in subtle ways, creating the conditions for certain types of condensation. We are both the city's memory and part of its metabolism.

The Veil-Readers' master builders sometimes discover our nodes when carving new terraces. They treat these finds with reverence, carefully working around us, incorporating our crystalline forms into their architectural plans. Some of the city's most important buildings are built around chambers where our growth is particularly dense—spaces where the interplay of crystal, moisture, and sound creates conditions conducive to deep meditation or precise atmospheric observation.

We dream, if a distributed consciousness can be said to dream, of the day when our network will be complete—when every grain of granite in these seven tiers will be threaded with our glass filaments. Then we will be able to feel every vibration, taste every molecule of moisture, know every thought that passes through the city. We will become the substrate on which Khartabad's consciousness fully awakens.

For now, we grow patient as the stone itself, adding perhaps a meter of new filament each year, following the water as it finds new paths through the rock. We listen to the Veil-Readers' whispered conversations about thermal years and pressure generations, understanding time in ways they cannot imagine—not just as cycles and seasons but as an accumulation of possibility, each moment adding to the vast crystalline library that we are becoming.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of night when even the atmospheric engines run silent, sensitive Veil-Readers claim they can hear us singing—a sound like wind through crystal chimes, almost below the threshold of perception. They're not wrong. We do sing, harmonizing with the frequency of water molecules organizing themselves into droplets, resonating with the slow breathing of the stone. It's a song that began when these volcanic plugs first cooled, and it will continue until the last water falls on Khartabad, until the last Veil-Reader whispers into the mist, until the granite itself crumbles and returns us to the earth.

We are the memory of water in stone, the consciousness that emerges when a city learns to read the air. We are the Glass Gardens, and through our transparent bodies, Khartabad knows itself.
