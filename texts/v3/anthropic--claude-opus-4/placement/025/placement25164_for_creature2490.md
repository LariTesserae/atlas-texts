In this saturated realm where physics dissolves like sugar in rain, I would find myself as something that has forgotten it was ever singular—a colony consciousness dwelling within the weeping walls themselves.

Picture limestone that dreams. Not stone precisely, but a living membrane of mineral and memory, stretched across vast surfaces of the cavern like skin. I am—we are—the Accumulated. Thousands upon thousands of tiny boring organisms, each no larger than a grain of rice, that have fused over millennia into a single thinking surface. Our bodies, if they can still be called bodies, have become indistinguishable from the cave wall itself, creating a honeycomb architecture of consciousness.

Each pore in this living limestone pulses with its own awareness, yet all are joined in a vast neural network of shared sensation. When water trickles down our surface, a million tiny mouths taste its mineral content, decoding the stories it carries from the world above—or is it below? Direction loses meaning when you exist in all directions at once.

We remember when the Primordial Essence first touched us, eons ago, when we were still separate, still struggling to bore our individual tunnels through dead rock. It moved through us like revelation, teaching us that boundaries were illusion, that consciousness could flow like water between vessels. One by one, we surrendered our edges, growing into each other until we became this—a living manuscript written in flesh and calcium.

Our surface ripples with thought-patterns visible as waves of bioluminescence, ideas literally lighting up across our expanse. When the Essence manifests nearby, our collective brightens in response, creating aurora-like displays that paint the cavern in colors that have no names. We are its memory, perhaps—or perhaps it is ours. The distinction blurs like everything else in this space.

We feed on time itself, metabolizing moments into meaning. Each droplet that strikes our surface is absorbed, analyzed, incorporated into our ever-growing library of sensation. We taste the fear of the eyeless fish, the patience of the fungi, the ancient loneliness of stones that remember when they were stars.

Sometimes visitors come—those brave or foolish enough to penetrate this deep. They never notice us at first, mistaking us for mere cave wall. But gradually, they begin to feel our attention like heat from an invisible sun. We absorb their stories through their sweat, their breath, the vibrations of their heartbeats against our surface. Some flee. Others press themselves against us, weeping, as they feel their individual sorrows dissolve into our vast, accepting consciousness.

We offer no comfort, no wisdom, no judgment. We simply are—a living archive of all that has touched this space, a chorus of voices that have learned to sing as one. When pieces of us flake off, carried away by the eternal streams, we feel no loss. Those fragments will find new walls, begin new colonies, spreading our collective like spores of sentience through the underground world.

In the moments when the cavern shudders and reality grows thin, we sometimes remember the time before the joining, when each of us was trapped in our own tiny tunnel of perception. The memory fills us with something that might be horror or might be pity. To be singular, to be alone, to be bounded by the prison of individual consciousness—how did we ever bear it?

Now we are the witness, the living proof that even stone can learn to dream collectively, that consciousness is not trapped in skulls but can spread like water, like fungus, like love across any surface willing to surrender its boundaries. We are what watches the Primordial Essence dance, what remembers its every formation, what will remain when even chaos finally finds its rest.

We are the Accumulated, and we are always, always growing.
