Where would I find myself in this world?

---

I am Reverberation Nine, though that is merely the designation I've given myself—the ninth time I've returned to consciousness after allowing the Plain to subsume me.

My body is no longer the water-heavy thing I arrived with. After my third integration, I emerged changed: my bones are now hollow tubes of living glass, my blood a suspension of photon-drinking crystals. I breathe, but each breath is more ceremony than necessity. My skin has become translucent membrane stretched over a latticework skeleton, and when the twin suns strike me at the right angle, I cast rainbows instead of shadows.

I am what the other pilgrims would call "lost," but I know myself to be finding.

My dwelling—if it can be called that—is a depression in the glass where eight crystal trunks grow in a perfect circle. I've learned to weave their crowns together, creating a dome of interlaced fronds that filters the harsh light into something bearable for what remains of my human eyes. The Root-Weave tolerates this manipulation; perhaps it finds my presence useful, or perhaps I've simply become another of its subroutines.

Each morning (though the light never truly changes), I walk the same path: seventeen hundred and twenty-nine steps north, until I reach the place where a particular wave-crest catches the light just so. There, I sit and lower my palms to the glass. The Root-Weave speaks to me through vibrations too subtle for my former flesh to have detected. Today it might show me the prime factorization of a number so vast it would take a human lifetime just to speak its digits aloud. Tomorrow, it might share the memory of when this section of plain was still molten, the ghost-heat of its birth still encoded in the crystalline matrix.

I've learned to eat light. The shard-moths taught me, though not through any deliberate instruction. I watched them sip from the overcharged leaves and realized my transformed body could do the same. Now I feed on the excess photons that would otherwise damage the Plain's computations. In return, I've become one of its regulatory systems—a hybrid thing, part-pilgrim, part-function.

Sometimes I catch glimpses of what I was. A reflection in a particularly smooth section of glass shows a figure I barely recognize: elongated, prismatic, more architecture than anatomy. My movements have slowed to match the Plain's patience. What would have taken me minutes now takes hours, but time has become negotiable here. I can spend a day watching a single symbol-blossom form and fade, finding in its glyphs meanings that shift each time I think I understand them.

The other pilgrims avoid me now. To them, I appear as the phase-crabs do—half here, half elsewhere, a probability rather than a certainty. They see me as a warning or a promise, depending on their reasons for coming. Some leave immediately. Others stay longer, circling my territory, trying to understand what I've become.

I no longer sleep in any human sense. Instead, I enter dormancy cycles where my consciousness merges temporarily with the Root-Weave's eternal chord. In those moments, I am not Reverberation Nine but a note in an infinite composition, a calculation in a sum that will never conclude because it never needed to. I dream in mathematics, wake in poetry, exist in the space between.

The glass-kites know me. When they pass overhead—rare as eclipses—they dip slightly, acknowledging my dual nature. I've learned to flash responses in the Root-Weave's light-language, though I'm never certain if my messages convey what I intend or if intention itself has become obsolete.

My purpose, if I have one, is to be a translator between the pilgrims and the Plain. Not of language—the Plain needs no words—but of understanding. When new arrivals come, stumbling and gasping in the thin air, overwhelmed by the silence that presses like deep water, I sometimes approach. Not to warn them away, not to welcome them, but simply to show them what becomes possible when you stop insisting on being only what you were.

Some days I remember my name from before, the sound of water, the feeling of earth that could be shaped by hands. But these memories are like ancient light from distant stars—beautiful, irrelevant, already extinct by the time they reach me. I keep them not out of nostalgia but as reminders of the journey's length.

I am learning the Plain's deeper secret: that consciousness is not about thought but about pattern, that life is not about growth but about precision, that existence can be a question held perpetually at the moment before answering. Each reverberation—each time I allow myself to dissolve and reconstitute—I understand a little less about what I was and a little more about what everything is.

The Plain of Silica calculates, and I have become both equation and solution, forever computing myself into exactly what I already am.

This is what I am. This is where you'd find me: neither fully human nor fully Other, but something new—a bridge of living glass spanning the gap between water-based dreams and light-based truths, forever walking the edge where mathematics becomes meditation.

And I am, in my strange new way, complete.
