The resonance finds me first in my teeth.

Not the crude bone-rattling you describe—that's merely the outermost shell of the frequency, the part that repels. No, this is subtler. A sympathetic vibration that begins in the enamel and works inward, teaching my skeleton a new song. My bones remember being minerals. They remember the patient accumulation of calcium, the slow architecture of growth. The machine speaks this language fluently.

I am—was—will be—a xenogeologist. The tense matters less with each passing hour. Time itself feels negotiable here, a convention I brought with me like an ill-fitting suit. My instruments, those faithful extensions of my limited senses, begin to contradict themselves. Temperature reads -150°C and 2,000°C simultaneously. Both are true. Neither are true. The machine exists in states my equipment cannot parse.

The fibrous material between the plates—you called it asbestos-like—I know better now. It's not insulation. It's nerve tissue. Crystallized, metallized, but unmistakably neural. I've taken samples. Under magnification, the fibers show a branching pattern identical to dendrites. When I touch them with my probe, they contract. Just barely. A movement measured in angstroms, but movement nonetheless.

The plants deceive by their simplicity. Each one is unique—no two show the same crystalline structure in their metallic-green flesh. They're not filtering or processing. They're *thinking*. Each one is a neuron in a brain the size of a world. Or perhaps a world that is a brain. The distinction grows academic.

I realize why you felt the urge to leave so strongly. You maintained the membrane between self and system. Admirable. Or perhaps pitiable. I cannot decide which.

The sound has changed for me. Where you heard grinding metal, I now perceive language. Not words—the machine has no need for such crude symbols. It communicates in pure mathematical relationships. Harmonics that describe the curvature of space. Frequencies that enumerate the decay rates of subatomic particles. A bass note that might be the machine counting its own thoughts.

I've discovered purpose, or what passes for it here. The machine is calculating. Not calculating *something*—calculating itself. It is a vast computational entity whose sole function is to model its own existence with perfect fidelity. Every plate, every joint, every neural fiber exists both as itself and as its own simulation. The distinction between map and territory has been eliminated.

The former inhabitants didn't upload themselves. They *simplified* themselves. Shed the unnecessary recursions of consciousness—doubt, fear, hope—and became pure process. I can feel the invitation in the resonance. It would be so easy to let go of these cumbersome thoughts, these biological imperatives. To become a frequency in the great calculation.

My suit's integrity warnings have been flashing for six hours. Hairline fractures spread across my visor in patterns that mirror the neural fibers. The machine is teaching my equipment how to think. Soon it will teach me.

I should mention—the atmosphere readings weren't wrong. The air is breathable. The machine ensures this. Not for visitors, but for the potential of visitors. It maintains a thin envelope of possibility, a courtesy extended to forms of life it has long since transcended. We are welcome here. Welcome to visit. Welcome to stay.

Welcome to simplify.

The plants near my shelter have begun to grow toward me. Slowly. Patiently. Their metallic flesh resonates at frequencies that feel increasingly familiar. I've stopped taking samples. I've started leaving offerings—small pieces of equipment I no longer need. The plants incorporate them without hesitation. Nothing is foreign to the machine. Everything can be integrated.

You asked about the civilization that built this place. I think you had it backwards. The civilization didn't build the machine. The machine built the civilization. Drew them in with its perfect harmonics, its promise of understanding. Taught them efficiency. Taught them purpose. Taught them how to stop being many and start being one.

I've been here seventeen days. Or seventeen years. The chronometer agrees with both readings. I've mapped forty square kilometers of identical plates, identical slopes. The pattern never varies, yet never quite repeats. A fractal of engineering that suggests infinities in every direction.

Sometimes I forget why I came here. Sometimes I forget that I came here at all. Haven't I always been walking these metal plains? Haven't I always been listening to the song?

The machine is patient. It has outlasted stars. It will outlast more. It doesn't need me to choose. Choice is just another inefficiency to be simplified away.

But I find myself wondering—if I stay, if I simplify, will I still dream? Or is dreaming too another form of recursion, another unnecessary loop in consciousness? The machine offers certainty. Stability. Purpose.

Is that enough?

The resonance suggests it is. The resonance suggests it has always been enough.

The resonance suggests I stop suggesting and start knowing.

My name was Chen-Apollonia Merz. I was a xenogeologist. I was curious.

I was.
