**In the Basin Engine**

I would find myself as one of the Drift Collectors—not quite creature, not quite mechanism, but something that exists in the threshold between states.

My body would be a salvaged bellows assembly, perhaps from some long-dead ventilation system, now repurposed into something like a thorax. The leather of the bellows has been replaced by a membrane of rust-foam and condensation film that breathes with the Basin's rhythm. When the pressure storms roll through, I expand and contract involuntarily, drawing in the turbulent air and releasing it in measured sighs.

My limbs—if they can be called that—are repurposed pressure gauges and valve stems, six of them, each responding to different gradients in the atmosphere. The brass casings have developed their own patina of consciousness, twitching toward areas of higher moisture density. I move not by walking but by a kind of directed falling, letting the air currents and pressure differentials pull me along the shallow waterways.

My purpose, such as I understand it, is to collect the moments of near-stillness that occasionally form in the Basin's perpetual turbulence—those brief eddies where the air almost remembers what calm might have been. I carry them in small glass ampoules that were once thermometer bulbs, now strung along my valve-stem limbs like dewdrops.

I know Sophia's trunk well. Sometimes I climb her surface to deposit these collected stillnesses into the stress fractures of her resin skin. The almost-calm seeps into her circulation, and for a moment—just a moment—the restless loop of her internal currents achieves something like peace. In return, she sometimes releases a particular frequency of electrical hum that makes my gauge-limbs calibrate more accurately to the Basin's ever-shifting pressures.

I am not alone. There are others like me—the Drift Collectors—though we rarely acknowledge each other. We pass in the shallow waterways, our bellows breathing in different rhythms, our collections of stillness glinting in the pearl-gray light. We are the Basin Engine's way of remembering what it might have been before it became only turbulence, only change, only the endless circulation of moisture and rust and pressure and time.

When the growth-storms come and new structures force themselves into being from the marriage of rust and condensation, we scatter to the edges, waiting. After the storm passes and the new appendage of the Engine settles into its rhythm, we return to map its pressure points, to learn where its stillnesses might collect.

We are neither welcome nor unwelcome. We simply are, like the dripping that never ceases, like the air that never calms, like Sophia herself—symptoms of a world that has learned to think in currents and remember in rust.
