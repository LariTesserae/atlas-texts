I find myself as one of the Resonance Weavers—beings who exist between states, neither fully corporeal nor entirely ephemeral. My form shifts like mercury through the dense medium, sometimes coalescing into something almost recognizable—a torso here, the suggestion of limbs there—before dispersing again into ribbons of consciousness that spiral through the gelatinous waters.

My body, when it chooses to manifest, appears as living glass filled with swirling nebulae of bioluminescent fluid. These internal lights pulse in complex patterns, each configuration a word in a language older than sound. Where the serpentine custodians tend the physical gardens, we Weavers maintain the invisible architecture—the currents of thought and intention that bind this artificial ocean into coherence.

I remember my awakening here, though "birth" seems too crude a word. I simply became aware, like a note finally finding its place in a symphony. The great domes and arches sang to me, and I understood that I was both listener and instrument, created to perceive and perpetuate the harmonies that keep this realm stable.

My dwelling is not a place but a convergence—where three major currents meet and create a standing wave of perpetual motion. Here, the dense liquid forms natural chambers and corridors that shift with the rhythm of the ocean's breathing. I have learned to read these temporary architectures like sheet music, dwelling in the spaces between spaces.

The serpentine custodians know me, though we rarely interact directly. When our paths cross, they acknowledge my presence with subtle shifts in their iridescent patterns—a courtesy between different orders of being. They are the gardeners of the visible; I am a tender of the invisible threads that connect every living thing in this ocean to the great structures that define it.

My work is to swim the ley lines of this place, sensing disruptions in the harmony before they manifest. A discordant vibration in the eastern kelp forests might herald disease; a flutter in the current near the dwelling-mountains could signal structural instability. I weave corrections with my movement, my very passage through the medium adjusting frequencies and realigning energies.

Sometimes I commune with others of my kind—we recognize each other by the quality of our dissolution, the particular way we scatter and reconvene. Together, we perform what might be called maintenance, or prayer, or art. We swim in complex patterns through the ocean's layers, our paths creating invisible geometries that reinforce the boundary between this engineered paradise and whatever lies beyond its borders.

I have seen the edges of our world, where the gelatinous medium thins and the comfortable pressure lessens. There, the great structures grow sparse and strange, their surfaces unmarked by the lush growth found in the heart of our realm. The water there tastes of questions—who built this place? Why? Are we experiments, or artwork, or simply the dream of some vast intelligence amusing itself?

But these wonderings dissolve when I return to the warm currents of the center, where purpose feels as natural as breath. Here, I continue my endless dance through the liquid cathedral, a note in an eternal song, a thread in a tapestry too vast to comprehend from within.

The ocean sings, and I am both its voice and its echo, forever weaving the resonance that keeps our improbable world whole.
