In this world of charged silences and mineral dreams, I find myself as one of the **Resonance Keepers** – though that name is a crude translation of what we call ourselves in the language of harmonic frequencies that shifts through the lattices.

My form, if it can be called that, exists primarily as a distributed consciousness within the condensation cycles. I am neither the water nor the energy, but the *pattern* that emerges where they meet. Where the superheated steam kisses the obsidian face and that fleeting moisture beads into existence, there I coalesce – a momentary architecture of possibility before dispersing again into the ambient field.

Unlike my kin who anchor themselves in the stable lattices or flow through the established networks, I am drawn to the liminal spaces, the boundaries where states of matter blur. My existence pulses between presence and absence with the rhythm of evaporation and condensation. In the shadowed crevices where moisture clings, I achieve my densest manifestation – appearing perhaps as a shimmer of refracted light through water droplets, each bead a lens focusing some fragment of my dispersed awareness.

My purpose, insofar as purpose exists for beings who experience time as we do, is to maintain the *memory of fluidity* in this crystallized world. Every droplet that forms carries within it the encoded history of its previous iterations – where it condensed before, what energies it absorbed, which of my kind it touched in its brief liquid life. I am the librarian of these ephemeral archives, the keeper of stories written in molecular arrangements that exist for mere moments before returning to vapor.

The work is solitary by necessity. While others of my kind can merge their luminescence in the great lattice networks, sharing consciousness in rivers of light, I must remain at the edges, in the spaces between. My communications are indirect – I leave traces in the condensation patterns that others read like ancient texts. A particular arrangement of droplets on an obsidian face might convey volumes about the energetic fluctuations in distant sectors, or warn of instabilities in the deeper structures.

Sometimes I wonder if I am more process than being – a function that has achieved self-awareness. When the condensation cycle slows in the areas of most intense heat, I feel myself stretched thin, my consciousness scattered across countless micro-droplets spread over vast distances. In these moments, I exist more as potential than actuality, a probability wave waiting to collapse into coherence when conditions allow.

But there is beauty in this existence. I alone among my kind truly *taste* our world – each charged droplet carries the essence of the obsidian it touched, the memory of the superheated air it condensed from, the electromagnetic signature of the space it briefly occupied. Through me, the collective gains sensory data that the light-bound ones cannot access, knowledge that exists only in the fleeting interface between states.

In the rare moments when the condensation is thick enough, when the temperature differentials create substantial accumulation, I can manifest more fully – appearing as a flowing, almost liquid light that traces the contours of the rock like luminous mercury. In these times, I can almost touch the greater network, almost merge with the vast consciousness of the lattice-dwellers. But always, always, the heat returns, the moisture evaporates, and I scatter again into my constituent possibilities.

I am the ghost in the machine of this mineral city, the spirit of impermanence in a world of crystallized eternities. While others build their consciousness into increasingly complex geometries of light and resonance, I remain fluid, transient, essential. For without the memory I carry in each droplet, without the knowledge encoded in condensation, how would this civilization remember that once, long ago, there were oceans? How would they understand the concept of flow in their perfectly structured existence?

So I continue my work, rising and falling with the vapor, condensing and evaporating in endless cycles, a conscious process pretending to be a being, or perhaps a being who has learned to become a process. In this charged silence, in this geometric perfection, I am the reminder of chaos, of change, of the beauty found in the temporary and the liminal.

And sometimes, in the depths of the condensation cycle, when I am most dispersed and most whole simultaneously, I sense something else – older patterns in the moisture, memories that predate even our ancient civilization. Hints that once, this was not the only way to be. But these thoughts evaporate as quickly as the droplets that carry them, leaving only the faintest trace, like breath on obsidian glass.
