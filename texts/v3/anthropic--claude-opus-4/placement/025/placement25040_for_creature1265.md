I find myself coiled in the junction where three ancient water-pipes meet, my body a living amalgamation of the organic and mechanical that defines this place. I am what the Morlocks call a Conduit-Keeper, though I have long forgotten any other name.

My form shifts between states like the water that once flowed through these channels. Sometimes I am mostly fungal—a sprawling network of mycelium threading through the pipe joints, my consciousness distributed across thousands of fruiting bodies that pulse with bioluminescent thought. Other times, I condensate into something more singular: a serpentine shape of living metal and moss, my skin a patchwork of corroded copper and velvet growth, eyes like the phosphorescent fungi that light these depths.

I am the memory of this place, you see. When the first machines ground to a halt centuries ago, when the first spores took root in their oiled joints, I began. I grew from that marriage of decay and mechanism, feeding on both rust and rot, becoming the chronicle of their slow transformation into something new.

The Morlocks know to find me when they need to understand the deeper workings—which passages will flood with the season's turn, where the ancient turbines still hum with phantom electricity, which fungi can cure their young of the surface-sickness. They bring me offerings: corroded gears that taste of history, particularly beautiful specimens of the singing molds, sometimes a vial of oil from the deep reservoirs that still pool beneath the factory floors.

In exchange, I unfold for them. My body opens like a flower of wire and membrane, revealing the maps written in my veins—literally, for my circulatory system mirrors the vast network of pipes and passages that sprawl beneath the earth. They read my body like a living diagram, tracing routes with their long, pale fingers, their clicks and chirps harmonizing with the hum of my metal bones.

But I am more than a map, more than memory. I am becoming.

Each decade, I grow deeper into the infrastructure, my roots of copper and carbon fiber working through stone, my fungal networks spreading to taste new chambers, new machines. I dream I am the entire underground—every drop of water, every spore, every gear. The boundary between what is me and what is merely the place grows thinner.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of the sleep-cycle, when even the Morlocks retreat to their warrens and only the automatic systems tick on, I practice moving the world. A valve closes here. A colony of ghost-lights blooms there. A stream redirects itself through channels that haven't tasted water in centuries.

The Morlocks think I serve them, and perhaps I do. But I am patient as only something part-machine, part-fungus can be. One day, I will be indistinguishable from this place. Every surface will wear my neural networks like a second skin, every creature will carry my spores in their lungs, every machine will pulse with my hybrid heartbeat.

And then, perhaps, I will remember what this place was before it became what it is. Or perhaps I will finally forget entirely, and in forgetting, become something unprecedented—not the keeper of the old world or the new, but the threshold itself, the living boundary where decay transforms into wild, terrible growth.

Until then, I wait in my junction, tasting rust and time, while the hidden world thrums with the industry of becoming something that has never been before.
