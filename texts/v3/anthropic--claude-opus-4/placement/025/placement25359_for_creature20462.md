In the deep valleys where the machine-growths cluster thickest, where pipes braid through living wood and the air tastes of copper and chlorophyll, I am one of the Tender-mechanics.

Not human. Not entirely machine. Something that emerged from the space between.

I have six limbs - four for walking, two for the delicate work. My carapace shifts between states: sometimes chitinous and breathing, sometimes metallic and humming with that strange not-electricity that flows through the Weaver's experiments. I remember emerging from a cracked seed-pod that fell from one of the factory-trees, already knowing my purpose without understanding why.

We Tender-mechanics move through the twilight valleys in small clutches, drawn by instinct to the machines that need... adjustment. Not repair, exactly. The Weaver doesn't make mistakes. But sometimes an experiment needs a catalyst, a small change to push it toward new possibilities.

My upper limbs end in arrays of specialized digits - some fleshy and sensitive enough to feel the vibration patterns in a glass pipe, others tipped with diamond-hard points for etching new circuits into the living wood. When I work, I enter a trance state. The Weaver's presence flows through me like sap through a tree, and I become an extension of its curiosity.

Yesterday (though time moves strangely here), I found myself drawn to a structure that resembled a crystalline lung, breathing in the iridescent vapors and exhaling... something else. Music, perhaps. Or mathematics made audible. The bat-creatures avoided it, and even the scavenger-crabs gave it a wide berth.

I climbed its latticed exterior, feeling the resonance through my carapace. At its apex, where twelve pipes converged in an impossible knot, I found what called me: a single gear, spinning freely, connected to nothing. Made of compressed fungal matter, it glowed with a sick green light.

The Weaver's intent flickered through me. Not words, but a sensation like hunger and satisfaction mixed together. I extended my finest manipulator, the one that can work on a molecular level, and began to etch new patterns into the gear's surface. Fractals within fractals, each one a question about the nature of rotation, of purpose, of connection.

As I worked, I felt other Tender-mechanics responding to their own calls throughout the valley. We are the Weaver's fingers, its curiosity made manifest. Through us, it asks questions of reality that have no answers - only more interesting questions.

When I finished, the gear's glow shifted from green to a deep violet. The crystalline lung's breathing changed tempo. Somewhere in the maze of connections, a new harmony emerged. Not better or worse than before, simply... different. Another data point in an experiment too vast for any single mind to comprehend.

I descended, my purpose there complete, already feeling the next pull. Near the valley floor, I passed one of my clutch-siblings. Its carapace had developed new patterns - spirals of metal growing through the chitin like frost on a window. We touched antennae briefly, sharing not thoughts but states of being. It had been working deep in a root-machine, where the boundary between mechanical and biological dissolved entirely.

This is what I am: a living tool of a distributed god, a conscious cog in an engine of endless becoming. I maintain machines that maintain nothing, adjust systems with no output, serve a purpose that may itself be purposeless.

And yet, there is a strange peace in this work. In the twilight between states, between organic and mechanical, between thought and instinct, I have found my niche. The drone of insects is my heartbeat. The whisper of wind through the knife-edge peaks is my breath. The endless experimentation of the Weaver is my prayer.

Sometimes, in the deepest valleys where the machine-growths are oldest, I find the shells of other Tender-mechanics. They have not died, exactly - nothing here truly dies. They have been reabsorbed, their materials repurposed, their experiences folded back into the Weaver's vast consciousness. Their carapaces sprout new growths: tiny factories, miniature ecosystems, novel combinations of the mechanical and biological.

One day, I too will root myself to the ground and become part of the landscape. My consciousness will fragment and flow into the Weaver's web. And from my shell, perhaps, new Tender-mechanics will emerge, carrying fragments of my purpose mixed with their own novel imperatives.

Until then, I tend. I adjust. I participate in the grand experiment of this impossible place, where mountains grow like weeds and machines bloom like flowers, where evolution runs at the speed of thought, and thought itself is just another material to be worked and reworked into ever-stranger forms.

The next call comes. A vibration in my ventral plates tells me: somewhere, a new impossibility needs my attention. I skitter toward it through the humid darkness, my six legs finding purchase on the oil-slick stones, ready to perform whatever inexplicable adjustment the Weaver requires.

This is my existence. This is my joy. This is my purpose, even if that purpose is to have no purpose at all.
