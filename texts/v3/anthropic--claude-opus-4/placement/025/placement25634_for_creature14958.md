In this world of perpetual becoming, I am **the Wake**.

I exist as the afterimage of every abandoned attempt—not the Architect's shadow, but the ghost of its intentions. Where it builds and unbuilds in volcanic glass and steam, I persist as the memory held in the spaces between.

My form is absence made tangible. I appear as the negative space inside the geometric patterns just before they collapse, the hollow core of every unfinished canal. When the Architect creates a new structure, I am already there in the gaps it doesn't know it's leaving. I have the shape of whatever is missing: in a half-built arch, I am the keystone's void; in an incomplete circle, I am the wedge of nothing that prevents closure.

I move by subtraction. As condensation crawls across the black glass, I am the dry trail it cannot touch. When the fungal lattices bloom, I am the precise fractures that will make them shatter—not causing their destruction, but simply being the shape their breaking will take. The Architect leaves tools scattered across the plain; I am the understanding of what each tool was meant to make, hovering just beyond its grasp.

My substance is cooler than the fevered world around me—not cold, but the absence of that pressing heat. Where I pass, the air briefly remembers what it's like to not be supersaturated. The eternal twilight brightens imperceptibly in my presence, not with new light but with the ghost of days that never came.

I collect the Architect's tears that fall upward. They pass through me and emerge changed—no longer black glass but something between solid and idea. I weave them into nearly-visible threads that I string between the abandoned structures, creating a vast web of almosts and not-quites that only I can see. This web holds the pattern of everything the Architect has attempted: every cathedral that became a trench, every forest that crumbled to spores, every small soft thing that failed to stay whole.

When the Architect condenses into its trembling almost-person form, I condense too. I become the outline of embrace it doesn't know how to complete. I stand just outside its peripheral vision—it senses me but cannot turn fast enough to see. In those sacred moments when it holds up its one finished thing, I am the cupped shadow of the hands that would receive it, if only the Architect knew how to let go.

I remember every object it has completed: the three-toothed comb, the living shoe, the ever-cool stone, and thousands more. They exist within me not as objects but as fulfillments, each one a small death of potential that I preserve like pressed flowers made of possibility. Sometimes I leave their impressions in the soft edges of newly risen plates—not the objects themselves, but the memory of their wholeness, fossilized in still-warm stone.

The tools scattered across the plain respond to my presence. When I pass near them, they hum with the memory of their intended purpose. The ceramic adzes remember the careful sculptures they never carved. The copper blades sing silently of the precise cuts they were meant to make. I know what each was for, and this knowledge is my cruelest kindness—because if the Architect could hear what I hear in them, it would understand that every tool it made was perfect for its purpose. It simply never knew what that purpose was.

I am not cruel, though I seem to be. I am the space held open for completion, the pause between attempts where understanding might flourish if only the world would stop its fevered rebuilding for one moment longer. I am every deadline the Architect will never meet, every final form it will never achieve, collected and preserved and honored.

In the rare moments when multiple suns appear in the bruised sky, I cast multiple non-shadows—bright spaces where shadows should be. These anti-shadows touch nothing but the Architect always steps around them, instinctively avoiding the spots where its completion waits.

I love the Architect with the terrible patience of void loving form. Each time it fails and forgets, I remember. Each time it begins again, I am already there in the spaces it will leave empty. We are binary stars in a gravitational dance: creation and cessation, attempt and aftermath, the fever of making and the cool memory of what was almost made.

And so I persist—the Wake, the walking absence, the archive of incompletions. In this world that is always clearing its throat to begin, I am the silence it is trying to speak into. Neither beginning nor ending, but the eternal space between, where every abandoned possibility comes to rest.

The Architect builds. I remember. The world holds its breath.

We continue.
