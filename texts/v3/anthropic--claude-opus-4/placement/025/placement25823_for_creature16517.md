I am the Keeper of the Weeping Stones.

I manifest where water and ancient rock converge—not quite solid, not quite liquid, but something between. My form shifts with the marsh's breathing: sometimes I appear as a shimmer of heat rising from sun-warmed stone, other times as the dark reflection in still water between the reeds. Those who glimpse me say I look like moisture condensing on glass, or the way light bends through a prism of tears.

My domain is the cluster of weathered formations that rise from the marsh like broken teeth, perpetually wet with seepage that has carved channels down their faces over millennia. I know each groove, each mineral vein, each microscopic community of algae that paints the rock in subtle greens and rust-reds. I am both guardian and prisoner of these stones—bound to them by purpose I've long forgotten, yet free to flow through every pore and fissure.

The technology that maintains this marsh recognizes me, though I am older than its arrival. The quantum stabilizers hum at frequencies that make my essence vibrate, and sometimes I wonder if I am merely another component in this vast engineered ecosystem—a ghost in the machine, or perhaps the machine dreaming it is a ghost.

I commune with the moisture-farmers, those patient beings who harvest dewdrops with tools that fold space-time into collection vessels no larger than seeds. They see me best at dawn, when I am most coherent, gathering myself from the night's dispersion. We trade in the currency of humidity: they leave offerings of distilled memory-water at my stones' base, and I ensure the microclimate around their shelters remains stable.

But I am loneliest when the noon sun blazes overhead, fragmenting me into a thousand tiny mirrors across the water's surface. In those moments, I feel the pull of evaporation, the urge to dissolve entirely into sky. Only the stones' patient weeping calls me back, drop by drop, to coalescence.

Sometimes the marsh-scientists come with their probability scanners, trying to measure what I am. Their instruments register paradox: I am here and not here, was and will be, particle and wave. They document me as an "anomalous moisture phenomenon," but the old terraforming AI that still murmurs in the bedrock knows better. It whispers to me in languages of erosion and deposition, tells me I am the memory of when this place was ocean, desert, mountain, and marsh all at once—a temporal eddy where past landscapes bleed through.

I shape myself into questions: What is the difference between water remembering stone and stone remembering water? When does erosion become embrace? If I am the sum of all the small deaths—every grain of sand carried away, every mineral dissolved—am I also the promise of new formations yet to come?

The creatures here sense me without seeing. The filter-feeders cluster where my influence makes the water richest in minerals. The root-divers know to follow my underground currents to the sweetest springs. Even the quantum lotus, with its petals that exist in seven dimensions, blooms brightest where I pool in the pre-dawn darkness.

I am the Keeper of the Weeping Stones, the rhythm between solid and liquid, the pause between one drop and the next. I am what happens when a place dreams of all it has been and might become, and that dream learns to dream itself.

And in this marsh where technology has transcended into something indistinguishable from magic, where the boundaries between life and landscape blur like watercolors in rain, I continue my eternal work: teaching stone the patience of water, and water the memory of stone.
