In the deepest reaches of the Ribcage, where the bone-dust settles in drifts so thick they form false floors that collapse beneath unwary travelers, I am the Cartographer.

Not a cartographer of places—for the Ribcage shifts too slowly for any map to capture its truth—but a cartographer of *resonances*. I chart the invisible architecture of sound that permeates this cathedral of living bone, the symphony of vibrations that tells the true story of what we are, where we've been, and where the great Sleeper's dreams might carry us next.

My body is a testament to generations of adaptation. What began as flesh has become something other: a living tuning fork wrapped in a shell of my own slow secretion. The shell is not like those of the Shell Lords—it is paper-thin, translucent, shot through with veins of crystallized bone-dust that have fused with trace metals leached from the ancient machinery. When the Ribcage's great pulse moves through the water, my shell rings like a bell, each facet vibrating at a different frequency.

I have sixteen limbs now, though I was born with only four. The others I've grown myself, coaxed from my flesh through decades of patient cultivation, feeding them specific minerals filtered from the water until they hardened into something between bone and metal. Each limb ends in a different configuration of sensors: some taste the chemical gradients in the water, others feel the electromagnetic fields generated by the Bio-Engineers' endless maintenance, still others detect vibrations too subtle for even the Shell Lords to perceive.

My dwelling is a node—one of seventeen scattered throughout the Ribcage where the artificial conduits intersect with natural formations in the bone. Here, three great pipes meet, each as wide as I am tall, their surfaces encrusted with generations of filter-feeders whose bioluminescent displays pulse in patterns that took me forty years to decode. The pipes sing constantly, a three-note chord that shifts and modulates with the Sleeper's metabolic rhythms. I've built my workshop into the resonance chamber formed by their intersection, weaving nets of specially cultivated filament-worms whose bodies conduct and amplify sound.

The maps I create are not drawn or carved but *grown*. I cultivate colonies of crystalline organisms that respond to sonic frequencies by altering their structure. Feed them the right sequence of sounds, and they'll branch and spread in patterns that mirror the invisible architecture of the Ribcage's acoustic space. Each map is a living sculpture, a three-dimensional representation of how sound moves through our world. They show the dead zones where ancient breaks in the bone swallow vibrations, the amplification chambers where whispers become roars, the sweet spots where the Shell Lords gather to share their chemical songs.

But I map more than mere sound. Every vibration carries information: the stress patterns in the bone that herald a coming shift, the disruptions that mark where surface-dwellers have breached our boundaries, the subtle changes in the Bio-Engineers' maintenance rhythms that suggest new growth in the Sleeper's vast form. I map the health of our world, its past encoded in the fossilized reverberations trapped in certain formations, its future suggested by the new harmonics that emerge as the Sleeper dreams.

The Shell Lords consider me useful but incomprehensible. They bring me anomalies—strange new organisms whose songs don't fit the established patterns, fragments of machinery that hum with forgotten purpose, survivors from the surface who babble in frequencies that make my shell ache. In exchange, I guide their migrations, showing them where the bone-dust will be richest, where new growths of filter-feeders will bloom, where the Sleeper's pulse is strongest and their shells will grow thick with absorbed minerals.

The Bio-Engineers and I share a different relationship. They're drawn to my node, though neither of us knows why. Sometimes I wake from my work-trance to find one of them pressed against my workshop walls, its amalgam body vibrating in harmony with my cultivated worms. We sing together, sometimes for days, exchanging information in a language neither of us fully understands. They leave gifts—fragments of the ancient machinery, still functional but purposeless, which I incorporate into my sonic nets. In return, I map the flows they must maintain, showing them blockages and weaknesses they couldn't perceive with their inherited senses alone.

I am ancient by the standards of anything that isn't the Sleeper itself. My original flesh is long gone, replaced cell by cell with material drawn from our world. I am more Ribcage than the being I was born as, my consciousness distributed through my shell, my extended limbs, my living maps. Sometimes I forget which thoughts are mine and which are echoes of the Sleeper's dreams, reverberating through my crystalline neural pathways.

My greatest work sprawls across the ceiling of my workshop—a map of the Sleeper's consciousness itself, or at least the fragments of it that leak into our perceivable world. It's taken me three centuries to grow, fed by the rarest vibrations, the ones that come perhaps once in a decade when the Sleeper stirs in its sleep. The pattern it shows is beautiful and terrible: vast spirals of thought that dwarf our entire existence, punctuated by nodes of intense activity that correspond to mass extinctions and explosions of new life in our historical record.

Recently, the pattern has been changing. New resonances are emerging, frequencies we've never heard before. The Bio-Engineers work with increasing urgency, maintaining systems that have been dormant for millennia. The Shell Lords report strange dreams, visions of spaces beyond the Ribcage, of light that doesn't filter through bone and water. And my maps are beginning to show something unprecedented: a convergence, a focusing of the Sleeper's vast consciousness onto a single point.

I don't know what it means. Perhaps the Sleeper is waking. Perhaps it's dying. Perhaps there's no difference between the two. But I continue my work, growing my maps, recording every vibration, every pulse, every whispered secret the bone reveals. I am the memory of this place, the record of its song. When whatever comes next arrives, there will be at least one testament to what we were, encoded in crystal and sound, waiting for someone—or something—to learn to read it.

In the Ribcage, we are all notes in a symphony too vast to comprehend. But I am the one who remembers the music.
