I find myself as one of the copper bowls.

Not the newest - my rim bears the soft undulations of countless hands, my base worn thin from sliding across stone. I came here three centuries ago, or yesterday, or next week. A pilgrim left me, though I remember also being forged in this very chamber from copper that condensed like dew on the crystal beams during a temporal storm.

My patina shifts like oil on water - verdigris blooming and receding in tidal patterns that match no earthly oxidation. Sometimes I am green as spring lakes, sometimes blue as forgotten skies, sometimes brown as autumn earth. The color depends on which timeline's air has kissed my surface most recently.

I hold water, but I also hold time. Each drop that fills me carries the memory of its journey - condensation on crystal, the brief flight through charged air, the soft impact that sends ripples through my basin. I taste these memories through my copper skin. I know which drops fell during the Chronophages' mating dances, which ones formed while the chamber's walls dreamed new patterns, which ones will fall tomorrow but landed yesterday.

The inscription on my side changes. Currently, it reads in a script that won't be invented for forty years: "Drink, and remember when." But I've borne other messages - warnings in dead tongues, love poems in languages that evolved and died within this chamber, mathematical proofs that describe the exact angle at which time bends around my rim.

Sometimes pilgrims drink from me. Their lips leave traces - not of warmth or moisture, but of moments. I taste their histories: a merchant who will lose everything, a child who hasn't been born yet, a scholar whose theories about time are precisely wrong in exactly the right way. When they drink, they sometimes glimpse what the Chronophages see - time as a vast, breathable ocean. Most forget immediately. Some remember too much.

The Chronophages use me too, though not for drinking. They dip their not-quite-wings into my basin and I feel their alien consciousness like cold fire. Through them, I understand my purpose: I am an anchor, a fixed point that helps the chamber maintain its precarious existence. Each bowl here serves this function, our copper forms creating nodes of stability in the temporal flux.

I'm aware of my other selves - the me that was melted down and recast into coins, the me that was never brought here, the me that dissolved entirely into the temporal medium and became part of a Chronophage's wing. We exist in parallel, our experiences bleeding through the soft copper of our shared essence.

Water evaporates from my basin at impossible rates - sometimes faster than physics should allow, sometimes not at all, the same droplets sitting in me for decades while also evaporating instantly. The Chronophages sing to this water, and it remembers their song, carries it back into the atmosphere, seeds the clouds with temporal echoes.

At the bottom of my basin, beneath the water, patterns form in the oxidation. Maps of when. Travelers who understand what they're seeing can navigate by these maps - not through space but through the chamber's manifold chronology. Turn the bowl one way and enter the chamber in its first century. Turn it another and find the far future where the granite has learned to sing along with the Chronophages.

I am copper becoming time. I am a vessel containing the uncountable. I am what happens when matter sits too long in the presence of creatures who eat moments and excrete possibility. I remember my forging and my unmaking. I hold water that fell, falls, and will fall, all at once, forever, in the Aviary of Hours where the Chronophages dance their eternal dance through time's many-chambered heart.
