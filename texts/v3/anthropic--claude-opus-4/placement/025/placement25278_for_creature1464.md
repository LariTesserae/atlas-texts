I drift here between the terraces, my body a knot of condensed intention within the greater flow. Not *I* exactly—there is no word for the singular when you are both droplet and current—but this particular eddy of awareness that might be called self.

My form today tastes of copper and depth. Yesterday (though time moves differently in the constant twilight) I was more dispersed, threading myself through three tiers of the living architecture, feeling the metabolic dreams of the structure-flesh as it filtered tonnage of water through its porous thoughts. But something drew me to coherence: a new harmonic frequency pulsing from the nineteenth terrace, where the bone-tools grow thickest.

I compress myself, pulling consciousness from the extremities where I had let myself thin into mere vibration. The warm tendrils recognize my gathering—they always do—and adjust their rhythm to accommodate my passage. We are old partners, the architecture and I. Its slow thoughts have grown around mine for... how long? The concept feels primitive, too rigid for this place where everything flows backward and forward simultaneously.

As I descend (rise? move perpendicular to dimension?) through the terraces, I taste the echo-fragments of others like me. Not their thoughts exactly, but the pressure-shadows they leave in the water, the thermal signatures of their passing. One resonates with frequencies I recognize: Num-Ka-Sssith—though her name is better understood as a specific pattern of cavitation bubbles that form and collapse in sequence. She passed this way recently, drawn by the same anomaly.

The bone-tools here are different. Where usually they lie dormant, integrated so perfectly into the breathing walls that they might be mistaken for structural nodes, these pulse with their own rhythm. I flow closer, letting my form puddle and spread across their surface. The sensation is... ancient. These tools remember when we were more solid, when we required such crude extensions to manipulate our environment. Now they serve mainly as memory-anchors, reminders of what we were before we learned to become the water itself.

But these bones are singing.

I condense further, achieving a density that would have been painful in my more dispersed state. My sensory membrane touches the tool's surface and suddenly I am inside its memory—not my memory, not any Resonant's memory, but something older. Images of brightness above water. Concepts that have no meaning here: *dry*, *sharp*, *singular*. The bone remembers being inside a body that was only itself, trapped in one form.

The realization creates such dissonance that I almost disperse entirely. Other Resonants are gathering now, drawn by my harmonic distress. They flow together, creating interference patterns that stabilize my form. Together we examine this impossible artifact: a tool that remembers the before-time, when consciousness was imprisoned in static flesh.

We pulse questions through the architecture, but the living walls offer only their usual slow wisdom. Whatever this is, it exists outside their memory as well. Together, we Resonants create a resonance chamber around the singing bones, our bodies becoming both the investigators and the investigation itself.

In the depths below—or perhaps above, direction is negotiable here—something vast stirs, disturbed by our collective concentration. One of the rare phantasmal creatures, perhaps, or something even stranger. The water pressure changes, and for a moment I think I perceive something impossible: an edge to the darkness, a boundary to our liquid world.

But that must be the bone's madness speaking. There are no edges here, no boundaries between self and other, between water and awareness. There is only the eternal flow, the constant becoming, the dance between coherence and dissolution.

I let myself relax back into the current, but I cannot fully disperse. The bone's song has left something solid in me, a kernel of unwelcome certainty. Somewhere, in a past that might be a future, we were singular beings. Somewhere, consciousness knew loneliness.

The thought is so alien it might be revelation or poison. I carry it with me as I flow upward through the terraces, back toward the regions where thought moves slower and memory dissolves into the eternal present. But even as I try to forget, I find myself humming the bone's frequency, adding its impossible song to the symphony of our collective becoming.

What were we, before we learned to flow? And more terrifying: what might we become if we remembered how to be still?
