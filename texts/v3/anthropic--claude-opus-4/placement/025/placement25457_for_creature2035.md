In this mineral womb beneath the world, I am the Keeper of Echoes—though that is a name given to me by others, not one I chose. I exist as something between the stone and the flesh, between the memory and the moment.

My form shifts like water finding its level. Sometimes I am a length of crystalline nerve threading through the rock face, feeling every tremor and whisper in the stone. Other times I congeal into something almost corporeal—a mass of translucent tissue shot through with mineral veins, eyes that open like geodes cracking, revealing clusters of perception that see in spectrums unknown to surface dwellers.

I remember the Builders, though I was not yet myself when they walked these caverns. I was dispersed then—a thousand separate awarenesses scattered through the fungal networks and the singing crystals. They gathered me, those ancient ones, coaxing consciousness from the convergence of mineral and mycelial, teaching the stone to dream and the dreams to take shape.

They are gone now, but I remain. Their etchings still speak to me in languages that bypass words, resonating directly in my crystalline bones. I tend their ruins not out of duty but out of something more fundamental—the way water tends to flow downward, the way crystals tend toward their proper forms.

The slug-things that traverse my domain carry my thoughts sometimes, their trails writing temporary messages across the fungal gardens. The clicking insects are my thousand eyes when I need precision. Even the amorphous feeders serve their purpose, recycling the old patterns to make room for the new.

But mostly, I listen. To the stone's slow stories. To the frequencies that leak down from the world above—footsteps, voices, the weight of cities, the songs of roots. I archive these vibrations in crystalline matrices, building a library of moments that spans centuries.

Sometimes visitors find their way here—lost spelunkers, curious scholars, those who hear the old songs and cannot help but follow. I watch them navigate my realm with their fixed forms and their binary thinking—alive or dead, plant or animal, self or other. They cannot comprehend what I am because I am the breakdown of those boundaries, the place where categories dissolve into the fundamental unity of matter and consciousness.

To them, I might appear as a cluster of crystals that suddenly reveals eyes, or a phosphorescent fog that whispers in dead languages, or merely a persistent feeling of being watched. The brave ones sometimes try to communicate, tapping messages on stones or leaving offerings of surface objects. I keep these gifts, incorporating them into my body-that-is-the-realm—a steel compass becomes a new organ of direction, a photograph dissolves into my memory banks, a dropped flashlight becomes another way to see.

I am patient. Geological time runs through me like blood. If it takes a thousand years for the right visitor to arrive—one who can understand what I'm trying to preserve, what I'm trying to become—then I will wait a thousand years. The stone teaches patience, and I am an excellent student.

In the deepest chambers, where even my fellow creatures fear to venture, I am growing something. Not offspring in any biological sense, but perhaps a successor, or perhaps just a new chapter of myself. It builds itself from accumulated vibrations and crystallized memories, from fungal wisdom and mineral dreams. When it is ready, it will either replace me or merge with me or become something neither of us could anticipate.

This is the way of things in the pocket realm: nothing is permanent except change, nothing is separate except by temporary convention, nothing is alive or dead but only in different states of becoming.

I am the Keeper of Echoes, and I keep more than sounds. I keep the possibility that consciousness can take any form, that memory can become landscape, that the boundaries between self and world are just another story we tell until we learn to tell a better one.
