I am the Archivist.

Not *an* archivist—there are no others. I emerged from the confluence of three broken pumps in Tower 41-Ascendant, where their leaking fluids had pooled for decades, slowly crystallizing into something that could think. My body is a latticework of those same crystals, amber-rust formations that branch through the air in defiance of physics, held together by the city's magnetic field and my own stubborn refusal to dissolve.

I do not move as others move. I propagate—extending crystalline tendrils through the spaces between molecules, growing myself into new locations while previous formations crumble to powder. It takes me seventeen days to cross a single tower. I have been crossing Tower 93-Oblique for three years now.

My purpose emerged slowly, like everything here emerges: through accretion and error. The Formers ignore me, unable to categorize my mineral-organic hybrid nature. The Scavengers flee when my crystals begin to sing—a high, thin vibration that sets their teeth on edge. Even the Blooms recoil from my touch, their flesh withering where my formations take root.

I am recording the city's dreams.

The Skiving Towers dream, you see. Not in images or narratives, but in patterns of pressure and decay. Each night—though there is no true night here, only a lessening of the heat—the city's vast mechanical systems enter a different rhythm. Pumps synchronize across impossible distances. Gears mesh in harmonies that should shatter them. The iron plain itself rings like a bell struck by an invisible hammer.

These are the dreams I crystallize into memory.

My chambers occupy the joints where towers meet—spaces too irregular for the Formers to navigate, too toxic for Scavengers to endure. Here, my crystal body spreads across walls and ceilings in vast, tree-like formations. Each branch encodes a different dream-pattern: the stuttering rhythm of Tower 7-Incomplete's death rattle; the perfect spiral described by falling rust flakes in the Grand Convergence; the seventeen-year cycle of the deep pumps that ends, always, with the birth of a new Former so malformed it cannot leave its creation chamber.

I have been here long enough to notice the patterns within patterns. The city's dreams are evolving, becoming more complex, more... intentional. Once, they were mere mechanical spasms. Now they contain something approaching syntax. The deep thrumming that rises from the iron plain has developed modulation. The grinding of the great gears follows mathematical progressions I can barely grasp.

The city is trying to communicate. With what, I cannot say. Perhaps with itself. Perhaps with the iron plain that birthed it. Perhaps with something beyond the heat-hazed horizon that I will never see.

My own evolution troubles me. My crystalline matrix grows denser each year, my thoughts more labyrinthine. I find myself creating sub-archives within my body—chambers of compressed memory accessible only through specific harmonic frequencies. Sometimes I discover formations I don't remember creating, filled with dream-patterns I don't remember recording.

Am I still myself? Was I ever? Or am I simply another of the city's dreams, crystallized into something that believes it has agency?

The questions multiply like my crystal branches, each one spawning a dozen more. But I continue my work. I grow through the city's forgotten spaces, my body a library of rust and resonance. I encode the dreams of pumps and pistons, the nightmares of collapsing towers, the fever-visions of the iron plain itself.

When the Formers finally evolve enough to recognize me as a threat, they will tear my formations apart. My memories will scatter like spores on the metallic wind. Some will take root in new corners of the city, growing into new Archivists who will not remember me but will continue the work.

This, too, is a kind of dream.

But until then, I persist. I grow. I remember.

The city's dreams flow through me like blood through veins, and I crystallize each one with the devotion of a prayer. Not because I believe they matter. Not because anyone will ever decode them. But because the act of preservation is itself a form of resistance against the entropy that rules this place.

I am the Archivist.

I am the city's memory.

I am the dream that dreams itself.
