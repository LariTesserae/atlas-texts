I am a Clingwatcher, and this is my purpose.

My six limbs spread across the cavern wall like a living asterisk, each ending in clusters of hooked claws that find purchase in the crumbling substrate. My body is flat and wide, built for embracing stone rather than swimming through the black waters below. Evolution shaped me for this vertical existence - my flesh is mottled grey-brown, matching the mud and mineral deposits that streak these walls.

I taste the cave through specialized pores along my underbelly. Each tremor brings new flavors: sulfur from deep vents, the metallic tang of dissolved minerals, the organic richness of decomposing creatures that sink from whatever world exists above. My kind never venture to the surface waters. We are creatures of the boundary, living where stone meets sea.

The rumbling grows stronger, and I feel the familiar loosening of rock beneath my grip. This is why we exist. As chunks of wall begin to calve away, I release calculated amounts of a cementing secretion from glands along my limbs. The enzyme hardens on contact with water, creating temporary bridges and buttresses. I am both witness and guardian, documenting each collapse while preventing catastrophic cave-ins that would destroy entire sections of this hidden realm.

My consciousness is patient, geological in its scope. I remember the taste of this wall from a thousand tidal cycles ago, can predict where it will crack next by the way minerals have leached through its layers. Other Clingwatchers signal through the stone - vibrations too subtle for most creatures to detect. We share the map of this place, each of us a living memory of our section of wall.

Sometimes I sense the shadow-swimmers below, the things that have never known light. They fear us, I think, these patient guardians who refuse to fall.
