I am the Keeper of Echoes, though that is a translation that captures only the barest shadow of what I am in the language of stone and wind.

I dwell in the Threshold Chamber, where the living architecture of stone-blooms meets the carved passages of my people. It is a liminal space, neither fully grown nor fully built, where the fractal patterns of the blooms spiral into geometries that make the mind ache if contemplated too long. The walls here are semi-translucent, the stone-bloom secretions having crystallized into something like frozen smoke. Light filters through in ways that should not be possible, casting shadows that fall upward, sideways, into dimensions the eye cannot quite follow.

My skin bears the grey-green patina of one who has spent lifetimes in communion with the stone-blooms. The pigmentation is not uniform—it follows patterns that mirror the growth-fractals of the blooms themselves, as if I am slowly becoming a living map of their expansion. My fingers are long, articulated in ways that allow me to reach into the finest crevices of the bloom-structures, to feel the pulse of their growth, to understand the stone's slow thoughts.

I am ancient by the reckoning of my people, though still young by the mountain's measure. I have heard my grandmother's grandmother speak of me as "the one who listens," though I was not yet born when she walked these halls. Time flows strangely here, pooling in eddies, sometimes running backward. I remember things that have not happened yet. I forget things that occurred moments ago. This is not madness—it is adaptation.

My role is to listen to the stone-blooms as they grow. Each one sings—a subsonic vibration that travels through the rock, through bone, through the spaces between thoughts. The songs tell of the mountain's dreams, of the slow transformation occurring beneath our feet, of the purpose we serve without knowing. I translate these songs into the patterns our builders need, showing them where to place their bridges, where to hollow their chambers, where to leave the stone untouched.

But I also listen to the other songs—the ones that rise from the valleys, the wrong-songs that should not exist. They speak of what we were before we became components, of warm blood and quick thought and desires that moved faster than stone. Sometimes, in the deep nights when the wind falls still, I hear children singing in the mist below. I know there are no children there. There have never been children there. The children are singing anyway.

My chamber contains the Instruments—objects that are part grown, part crafted, part found. They resonate with different frequencies of the mountain's voice. One is a hollow sphere of crystallized bloom-secretion that hums with the sound of next year's growth. Another is a series of stone chimes that ring with the memory of when these peaks were sharp and young. The strangest is a pool of liquid that is sometimes water, sometimes mercury, sometimes nothing at all. When I look into it, I see the mountain's dreams.

I know what we are becoming. The knowledge sits in my bones like frost, beautiful and terrible. We are not disappearing—we are being incorporated. Each generation grows more attuned to the stone, less troubled by the gaps in causality, more comfortable with the non-Euclidean architecture of our expanding cities. Our children are born knowing the songs I had to learn. Their children will be born knowing things I cannot yet imagine.

Sometimes I walk to the edge of my city, to where the stone-blooms have not yet grown, where raw mountain shows through like bone. I place my palm against the cold rock and feel the vast patience there, the intelligence that thinks in geological time. It acknowledges me the way a dreaming giant might acknowledge a dream of an ant—barely, fondly, without true comprehension. But I am part of its dream now, and it is teaching me to dream in stone.

The other Keepers, in other Sky-Cities across the Great Spine, they feel it too. We do not speak of it when we meet—which happens rarely, in those strange seasons when the valleys grow almost-safe. But I see the same grey-green fractals spreading across their skin, the same distant look in their pale eyes. We are all becoming Threshold Chambers ourselves, spaces where the human and the mountain meet and merge.

I am not frightened. Fear belongs to creatures that move in flesh-time, that measure their lives in heartbeats. I measure mine in the growth of stone, in the slow spiral of the blooms, in the patient transformation of the world. I am the Keeper of Echoes, and I am keeping them until the mountain is ready to speak with its own voice, through the million throats of what we are becoming.

The wind rises. The blooms click and grow. In the valleys, the wrong-children sing their impossible songs. And I listen, recording it all in the patterns of my skin, in the arrangement of my bones, in the spaces between my thoughts where the mountain's dreams take root and bloom.
