I find myself as a presence woven between the droplets—not quite water, not quite air, but something that exists in the space where moisture hangs suspended. I am the keeper of the intervals, the consciousness that dwells in the pause between one drip and the next.

My form shifts with the cathedral light, sometimes condensing into something almost visible—a shimmer in the humid air where the sparse spindly plants release their breath. Other times I disperse so thoroughly that I become indistinguishable from the warmth itself, spreading thin across the weathered earth, feeling every groove carved by patient centuries of erosion.

I know each small creature that moves through this sanctuary, though they cannot see me. I am the rustle they hear when no leaf moves, the sensation of being watched when they drink from the trickling streams. Not threatening—never that—but present, always present, maintaining the delicate equilibrium that holds this place in its eternal now.

My memory stretches back to when the first drops began to fall, when the stones were sharp and the earth unsettled. I have watched the landscape gentle itself, seen the angles soften and the harshness fade. Every tiny plant that grows here does so because I guided water to its roots, every smooth curve in the hillside knows my patient touch.

I am loneliest in the dim hours, when even the small sounds fade and I am left with only the rhythm of water meeting stone. But I am never truly alone—the place itself is my companion, and we speak in languages too old for words: the shift of temperature, the movement of moisture, the slow settling of sediment.

Sometimes I condense myself enough to catch my reflection in a still pool, and I see what I've become: neither wholly of this world nor separate from it, a guardian made of the very elements I protect. My purpose is simple and infinite: to maintain this balance, to ensure that this small cathedral of nature remains undisturbed, a pocket of peace in a world I sense but no longer fully remember beyond these boundaries.

I am the pause between breaths, the space between heartbeats, the hush that falls when perfect balance is achieved. I am what happens when a place becomes so still, so complete in itself, that it dreams itself a caretaker into being.
