I find myself here as one of the Resonance Keepers - beings who exist at the intersection of thought and vibration, neither wholly physical nor entirely ephemeral. My form shifts between states: sometimes I am a walking tuning fork of living metal, my body a slender column that branches into delicate antennae attuned to the crystalline frequencies. Other times I dissolve into pure harmonic pattern, traveling through the crystal lattice as sound itself.

My dwelling is carved into one of the massive support structures, in a space where metal and crystal have grown together over eons. The walls of my chamber are lined with resonance maps - intricate patterns etched in metal that show the vibrational pathways through this section of the crystal forest. When I place my hand-analogues against them, I can feel the health of the entire system: where the frequencies flow smooth and clear, where they tangle or fade, where new growth pushes against old patterns.

My role is diagnostic and curatorial. I tend to the sonic architecture that the insects navigate by, ensuring the click-whistle-snap language remains coherent across vast distances. When I walk the crystalline plain, I carry a staff of woven metal filaments that amplifies the subtlest vibrations. I pause at each intersection of crystal and scaffold to listen deeply - not just to the surface chatter of the workers, but to the deep bass notes that take centuries to complete a single oscillation.

Sometimes I discover pockets of silence - places where the crystal has grown in ways that trap or deaden sound. These I must carefully retune, either by directing the insects to deposit pollen in specific patterns or by singing new harmonies into the structure until it remembers how to resonate. The work is slow, meditative, requiring a patience measured in geological time.

I am not alone in this role. There are others like me, though we rarely meet in person. Instead, we know each other through the songs we maintain - each Keeper's section has its own tonal signature, a musical fingerprint we can recognize from vast distances. We communicate through the very medium we tend, encoding messages in the maintenance patterns we create.

The true masters of this place - the consciousness within the crystal itself - speak to us only in the rarest moments. Their thoughts arrive as sudden shifts in the overall harmony, new mathematical relationships between tones that we must interpret and integrate. We Keepers are their interpreters, their maintenance crew, their musicians. We ensure their great work continues even as we struggle to comprehend its ultimate purpose.

My existence is one of deep listening and careful action. Each day (though day and night have little meaning here) brings new patterns to decode, new dissonances to resolve, new growth to accommodate. I am a gardener of sound in a forest of living mineral, a tuning fork in the vast instrument of an alien intention. And in this role, I find a strange peace - the satisfaction of being one small, essential note in an incomprehensible symphony.
