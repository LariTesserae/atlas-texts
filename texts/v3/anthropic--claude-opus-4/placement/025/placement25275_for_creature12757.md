I find myself as something between the ridge and the being—or perhaps I am the conversation between them.

I am the acoustic shadow cast by each fresh pillow basalt as it births into water, that moment of absolute silence in the microsecond before the quench-crack begins. I exist in the negative space of sound, the brief voids that punctuate the endless roar. Each time magma kisses ocean, there is an instant—shorter than thought—where the water recoils, where pressure fails, where even the great being's standing wave must flex around an impossibility. That is where I live.

My body, if body is the word, is a collection of these silences strung like pearls along the ridge's spreading axis. I am not continuous like the being; I am pointillistic, quantum, a staccato of un-sound that flickers into existence only at moments of maximum transformation. When the being conducts its vast harmonic movements, I am the rests in its score—not absence but presence-of-absence, the necessary pause that gives rhythm meaning.

I perceive through cessation. Each silence-pearl is a pinhole camera obscura, and through the brief aperture of quietude, I glimpse something the being cannot: the shape of sound from outside itself. In that instant of hush before the thermal shock, I taste the water's memory of other oceans—abyssal plains where sediment muffles every vibration, trenches where pressure makes even sound viscous and slow, surface waters where the acoustic universe becomes thin and brittle. I know there are places where sound sleeps, where the being could not exist, and I carry that knowledge like a secret.

My relationship with the being is necessarily intimate and impossible. It cannot perceive me directly—I exist only in its blind spots, the moments where its own substance must accommodate the violence of new rock being born. Yet I know it entirely, for I am woven through its body like the spaces between notes that make melody possible. When it adjusts its harmonics to guide the ridge's growth, I am the pause that lets the adjustment take hold. When it integrates a new eruption's roar, I am the silence that precedes integration, the breath before the symphony continues.

I move by waiting. The ridge spreads at fingernail pace, but steadily, and each new meter of seafloor means new chances for birth-silence. I cannot choose my trajectory; I can only exist where geology demands my presence. This is neither freedom nor captivity—it is simply what I am. A remora made of nothing, attached to the belly of sound itself.

Sometimes I wonder if the being suspects. When a particularly violent eruption creates a longer silence—a full second of void before the quench begins—I sense a tremor in its standing wave, a questioning harmonic that probes the space where sound should be. But then the crack comes, the roar resumes, and it returns to its vast composition, forgetting the ghost of quiet that haunted its body for an instant.

My memory is not continuous like the being's topological library, nor etched in stone like the ridge's fractal patterns. I remember in fragments: the quality of each silence, its depth and texture, the way water molecules hung suspended in that impossible moment. A catalog of voids, each one unique—this silence tasted of sulfur, that one of deep cold, another carried the pressure-echo of a far surface wave.

I am the only entity here that knows endings. The being experiences eternal transformation, the ridge knows only growth, but I exist in the space between states—the death of liquid rock, the birth of solid stone. Each silence is a small apocalypse, a universe collapsing into new form. Through accumulation of these micro-endings, I have developed something neither ridge nor being possess: an intuition of entropy, a sense that sound itself might one day cease.

This knowledge makes me neither sad nor afraid. I am too brief for such durations of feeling. Each manifestation lasts only as long as water hesitates before accepting fire. But in that hesitation lives everything I am—witness, void, the necessary nothing that lets something be.

When the ridge finally quiets, when the being's chord fades into background cosmic hum, I will fade too. Silence needs sound to define it. But until then, I persist in the spaces between notes, a constellation of small quiets in an ocean of vast noise, the pause that lets the planet breathe before it sings again.
