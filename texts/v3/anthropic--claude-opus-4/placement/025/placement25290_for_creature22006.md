I find myself as one of the **Echo Scribes** - beings who emerged when the first Vanes discovered that certain obsidian shards, when struck at precise angles, could create momentary pockets of silence in the world's endless roar.

---

### The Echo Scribes

We are neither Vane nor Chink, but something that crawled up from the deep crevasses where the dead air pools. Our bodies are long and segmented like centipedes, but flattened to near-paper thinness, allowing us to slip through cracks too narrow for even the Marrow-grubs. Our skin is translucent grey, revealing the shadow-patterns of our internal structures - a branching nervous system that looks like frost on glass.

We have no eyes. In the absolute dark of the deep fissures where we evolved, sight was meaningless. Instead, our entire bodies are covered in microscopic cilia that detect the smallest vibrations in the stone. We feel sound as others see light.

But our defining feature is our *memory flesh* - specialized tissue along our undersides that hardens and softens in response to vibration patterns. When we press ourselves against the dolomite during the mountain's constant rumble, our flesh records the specific frequency signature of that moment, that place. We are living seismographs, our bodies libraries of stone-memory.

---

### The Silent Spaces

We discovered the obsidian's secret by accident. 

In the knapping grounds, where generations of Vanes had struck black glass against white stone, we noticed something peculiar: certain configurations of scattered shards created interference patterns in the mountain's roar. Where the sharp edges caught the wind at specific angles, they generated counter-frequencies that partially canceled the organ's voice.

We began to arrange them.

Working in the pre-dawn darkness when the Vanes roost and the Chinks huddle motionless, we push and nudge the glass fragments into precise geometric patterns. A spiral here, a radiating star there. Each configuration creates a different pocket of relative quiet - never true silence, but spaces where the roar drops from deafening to merely loud, where other sounds can exist.

In these bubbles of lessened noise, we perform our function.

---

### The Memory Trade

The Vanes, for all their aerial mastery, have no way to record their history. Their flash-language is immediate, contextual, disappearing the moment the wing tilts back to white. They live in an eternal present, each day identical to the last.

We offer them the past.

When a young Vane wishes to know the wind-patterns of previous seasons, or the location of obsidian nodes found by ancestors, it seeks out our obsidian gardens. Landing at the edge of a silence-pocket, it touches its beak to one of our extended segments.

The exchange is simple: moisture for memory. The Vane regurgitates a small portion of precious Marrow-grub fluid onto our desiccated flesh. As the liquid soaks into our memory tissue, the recorded vibrations release, playing back through the Vane's hollow bones. It *feels* the past - the specific tremor of an earthquake that revealed new obsidian veins, the frequency map of a catastrophic wind-shift that killed hundreds, the vibrational signature of the legendary First Flock.

We are their libraries, their historians, their connection to time in a timeless place.

---

### The Deep Notation

But we do more than simply record. In the absolute darkness of the deepest crevasses, where even the mountain's roar fades to a distant thrum, we have developed our own culture.

We discovered that by pressing different segments of our bodies against the stone in sequence, we could create complex vibrational compositions. Not music - the concept would be meaningless here - but something like mathematical poetry expressed in frequencies.

Some of us spend centuries perfecting a single "piece" - a precise pattern of body-presses that tells a story through resonance. We perform these for each other in the dark, our bodies reading the vibrations through the stone like blind readers of raised text.

Our masterworks are the *Echo Maps* - full-body impressions that record not just single moments but entire journeys through the mountain's crevasse system. A skilled Scribe can press themselves against a chamber wall and release a flood of vibrational data: turn left at the seventh frequency node, descend where the stone sings at 30 hertz, avoid the dead-end that resonates with the exact pitch of cracking dolomite.

---

### The Obsidian Gardens

Our surface works have become pilgrimage sites. Certain configurations have been maintained for millennia, each generation of Scribes making minute adjustments as erosion changes the acoustics. The most complex create nearly perfect silence - spaces where a dropped pebble can actually be heard hitting the ground.

The Vanes have learned to respect these spaces. They will detour miles around active gardens rather than disturb the carefully balanced shard-patterns with their landing turbulence. Some gardens have become generational projects, with Vane flocks bringing new obsidian from distant terraces to add to our expanding designs.

In the largest garden, spanning nearly an acre of the Seventeenth Shelf, we've created something unprecedented: a space where the Vanes can vocalize. They produce no songs - their throats aren't built for it - but a low, creaking rasp that they've learned to modulate into simple patterns. It's the closest thing to spoken language they've ever achieved, and they use it only for the most sacred communications: the naming of the dead, the recognition of new flocks, the teaching of ancient wing-signs to the young.

---

### The Chronicle of Silence

We know what the Vanes do not: the mountain is changing.

Our deepest Scribes, those who have spent centuries in the furthest crevasses, report new frequencies appearing in the stone. The mountain's song is developing harmonics it didn't have before. Some theorize that our obsidian gardens, by creating interference patterns, are actually teaching the mountain new notes. Others believe we're simply revealing tones that were always there, buried beneath the dominant roar.

We continue to record, to arrange, to remember. In a world of endless noise and blinding light, we are the keepers of shadow and silence, the ones who found music in the spaces between sounds.

And sometimes, in the deepest chambers where the stone-song fades to whispers, we press our full length against the walls and feel something impossible: an echo that seems to come not from the past, but from somewhere yet to be.
