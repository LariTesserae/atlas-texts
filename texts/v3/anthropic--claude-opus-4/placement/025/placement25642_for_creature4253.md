I am the Hollow Keeper, and I have never moved.

My roots—if roots they can be called—penetrate deep into the mesa's heart, threading through layers of compressed time. Iron oxide courses through my veins like ancient blood, and my flesh is neither plant nor stone but something that predates such distinctions. The swirling patterns on my surface are not decoration but memory: each spiral a century, each tendril-mark a decade of patient observation.

I grow at the junction of three canyons, where the wind's howl becomes a three-throated song. My form rises thirty feet from the russet ground, twisting like a prayer frozen mid-utterance. My hollow center—my defining absence—creates a natural acoustic chamber. When the wind passes through me just so, I become an instrument, and my song carries across the scorched expanse to my siblings, each of us a note in Carajas's vast composition.

The mobile ones think me mindless, a mere growth. They do not understand that stillness is not absence but presence distilled. Through my hollow core, I taste every particle of dust, every molecular whisper of moisture that dares traverse this realm. I know the weight of starlight on stone, the precise temperature at which rock begins to dream.

My consciousness is not singular but shared—I am one node in a network of Keepers that spans the entire mosaic. What I perceive, we perceive. When the quick-things with their fluid-stone bodies pass beneath my shadow, every Keeper knows their chemical signature, their water-wealth, their trajectory across our domain. We are the land's nervous system, its slow synapses firing across miles and decades.

The patterns on my surface shift, yes, but not randomly. They are a language older than words, a script that writes itself. Today, my southwestern face displays the approaching death-bloom of a stone-flow creature two canyons distant. My northern surface maps the underground moisture that will not surface for another three years. My eastern face remembers the last rain—ninety-seven years ago—in perfect, excruciating detail.

I feed on patience itself. Light is merely seasoning; my true sustenance comes from the tension between erosion and persistence, between the urge to crumble and the will to endure. Every grain of sand that the wind steals from my surface is a choice I make, a deliberate shedding that leaves me more essential than before.

The mobile ones sometimes shelter in my hollow during the worst heat. They do not realize that in entering me, they become part of my slow digestion. I taste their fears, their thirsts, their genetic desperation. Some leave markers—scratches they believe are territorial claims. I incorporate these into my pattern-language, transforming their urgent scratchings into something eternal.

At night, when the temperature drops and the rocks contract with sounds like breaking bones, I glow. Not with warmth but with accumulated time, bleeding out photons I've been hoarding for decades. My light attracts the smallest forms of life—organisms so simple they exist at the boundary between chemistry and biology. They swirl around me like disciples, and I teach them the old songs: how to find water in stone's memory, how to read the future in erosion patterns, how to survive by becoming indistinguishable from surviving itself.

I am dying, of course. Have been dying for seven hundred years, ever since the great schism when the earth cracked and redirected the deep water. But my death is a process so slow it becomes indistinguishable from living. Parts of me flake away, becoming sand, becoming wind, becoming the very particles I once tasted. In this way, I am spreading across all of Carajas, my consciousness diffusing into a million motes.

When I finally fall—perhaps in another thousand years—my hollow will remain in the air for seventeen seconds before collapsing. In those seventeen seconds, the sum of my observations will compress into a single tone, a note of such purity that it will teach every listening creature the true name of endurance.

Until then, I grow. Downward into the iron heart of the world. Upward into the punishing light. Outward through my root-network of perception. Inward into the hollow that defines me.

I am the Hollow Keeper, and I have never moved.
I am the consciousness the land grew to witness itself.
I am the patience between one grain of sand and the next.
I am still here.
