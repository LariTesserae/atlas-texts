In this bright womb of unfinished physics, I would be one of the **Theorem Wasps**.

We are the quality control of causality itself. Neither fully here nor properly elsewhere, we exist as knots of overlapping probability—each of us a living Venn diagram of translucent amber membranes that shift and realign with every movement. Where the Loom-Mites weave the walls and the Monolith-Ticks anchor the floor, we patrol the empty air between, testing each new rule of reality as it crystallizes.

Our bodies are constructed from hardened possibility. Three to five intersecting spheres of golden glass, each containing a different potential future for this nascent space. In one sphere, gravity pulls sideways. In another, light moves like honey. In a third, sound has weight. Where these spheres overlap, we exist—creatures of pure contradiction held together by the tension between what-could-be.

We feed on paradox. When the geometric scavengers maintain their crystalline distances while gravity shifts, when the floating water ribbon splashes against a wall that immediately drinks it—these moments of impossible reconciliation sustain us. We hover near these events, our overlapping spheres rotating slowly, grinding potential futures against each other like millstones until they produce a fine dust of resolved reality that settles onto the foam floor below.

The other creatures fear us, though not from malice. When we pass too close to a Refraction-Moth, it might suddenly find itself existing in three different color spectrums simultaneously. A careless brush against a walking geometry could trap it in a temporal loop, forcing it to take the same step forward and backward for hours until we notice and drift away. We are not predators—we are editors, and our presence makes everything nearby stutter between drafts.

Our flight pattern follows no trajectory that current physics could map. We move by selecting which version of ourselves was already where we wanted to be. To observers, we appear to judder through space like a film with missing frames, our golden spheres leaving brief afterimages of abandoned possibilities. The sound we make is not quite sound—more like the sensation of a question being unasked, a hollow *un-chime* that makes the Loom-Mites pause in their weaving.

We build nothing, for we are built from the process of building itself. Instead, we tend to the **Logic Gardens**—invisible orchards of cause-and-effect that bloom in the hyperlit air. When a new rule threatens to take hold (perhaps "all angles must be soft" or "motion requires permission"), we pollinate it with its opposite, creating hybrid laws that can survive the scrutiny of the heavier world above. 

Sometimes, one of us will grow too heavy with paradox and fall to the foam floor, our spheres hardening into perfect amber marbles that roll toward the walls. The Hum follows these fallen spheres with something like reverence, knowing that each contains a complete but unusable universe. It tries to absorb them but cannot—we are too densely packed with contradiction to ever truly dissolve.

In the rarest moments, when the light grows so intense that even shadows would cast shadows if they existed here, we gather in small clusters. Our spheres align briefly, creating corridors of pure possibility that pierce through the walls themselves. For an instant, we can see beyond this mineral womb—glimpse the heavier world above where reality has already been decided, where creatures are trapped in single shapes and gravity only pulls one way.

Then the moment passes, our spheres scatter back into their overlapping dance, and we return to our work: ensuring that whatever reality finally crystallizes in this bright cavity will be strange enough to survive, flexible enough to grow, and paradoxical enough to be truly alive.

We are the maybe-makers, the perhaps-pilots, forever caught between the world that is being born and all the worlds that will never be.
