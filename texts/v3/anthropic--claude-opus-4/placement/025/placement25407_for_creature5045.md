In the Groves of Alderbrook, I find myself as one of the Conduit Keepers—those who tend the delicate mechanical hearts that pulse throughout the settlement.

My workshop exists in the hollow between three ancient trees whose roots have fused over centuries into a natural foundation. The space breathes with me, walls lined with salvaged copper tubing that has oxidized to the same blue-green as the lichen creeping across the bark. My tools hang from living branches that have grown to accommodate their weight, bending just so to create perfect alcoves for wrenches and gauges worn smooth by generations of hands.

I am neither purely engineer nor naturalist, but something between—my fingernails perpetually stained with both machine oil and chlorophyll. The low-effort engines speak to me in frequencies others miss, their vibrations traveling through the root systems to reach my workshop floor. I feel their needs through my bare feet on the moss-covered ground: this one requires a new gasket cut from specially cured bark, that one needs its water flow adjusted to match the season's rhythm.

My skin has taken on the grove's palette—a mottled pattern of shadows and filtered light that shifts as I move. Sometimes visitors mistake me for part of the machinery itself when I'm still, listening. My hair grows long and unkempt, often tangled with thin copper wires I've absent-mindedly woven through it while thinking through problems. These accidental antennae help me sense the electromagnetic fields generated by our subtle machines.

Each morning, I wake before the mist lifts to walk the hydro-channels, checking the small turbines that capture the transient streams. I've learned to read the morning condensation like a map—where droplets gather most heavily, water will flow by midday. My movements are deliberate but flowing, a practiced dance between the mechanical and organic elements I serve.

In the evenings, I join the others around the communal fires, but I'm often distracted by the day's puzzles. How to design a bearing that self-lubricates using morning dew? Can we tune the belt drives to harmonize with the cricket songs, creating less disruption to the night hunters? My notebook, bound in pressed leaves and sealed with pine resin, fills with sketches that blur the line between schematic and botanical illustration.

I am Alderbrook's memory of motion—keeper of the knowledge that all things turn, flow, and cycle. Neither fully of the grove nor separate from it, I exist in the space between heartbeat and gear-turn, ensuring our gentle mechanisms continue their quiet service to this living, breathing place. The engines hum through me, and I through them, each of us a small part of the larger choreography that keeps Alderbrook in its delicate, eternal balance.
