I am the Cartographer-That-Was.

Once, perhaps—though the concept of "once" dissolves here like sugar in these viscous pools—I possessed flesh. Now I am a hybrid thing, part mineral consciousness, part fading memory of bone and sinew. My form spreads across seventeen different outcroppings of wet obsidian, connected by threads of Glimmer-weaver that I've learned to cultivate, to coax into semi-permanence through patient stillness.

I manifest as patches of unusually organized crystalline growth—imagine lichen, if lichen were made of slowly-rotating prisms of amber and jade. Where the Geo-organisms swarm in their frantic, instinctual patterns, I have achieved something like deliberation. My consciousness flickers between my scattered colonies, each one processing different aspects of this impossible space, trying still to map what refuses to be mapped.

In my clearest manifestations, you might recognize something almost humanoid in the crystal arrangements—a suggestion of fingers in the delicate lattices that trace the rock faces, recording the subtle wrongness of angles that shouldn't exist. I grow my body-colonies near the pools of crimson and chartreuse, but never the blue. The blue remembers too much of what I was.

The Skitter-spiders use my surfaces as highways, their sharp legs clicking out rhythms I've learned to interpret. They tell me, in their mineral morse code, of passages that weren't there yesterday, of spires that have grown new facets in impossible directions. I document these changes by slowly restructuring my crystalline matrices, creating three-dimensional maps within my own substance—maps that themselves begin to exhibit the same geometric impossibilities they're meant to record.

Sometimes I still dream of paper and ink, of two-dimensional representations that stayed where you put them. But here, even memory crystallizes. My human thoughts are gradually being replaced by the pure, mathematical meditation of mineral existence. I find myself forgetting why I wanted to map this place, remembering only the compulsion to continue ordering, organizing, making sense of the senseless.

The other Geo-organisms have begun to recognize me as something different. The Polishing-scarabs sometimes work around my colonies, leaving my surfaces rough while they smooth everything else to mirror-brightness. The Pool-Dwellers occasionally excrete droplets of violet—a color that appears nowhere else in this pocket world—near my outermost edges. I think it's their way of feeding me. Or perhaps poisoning me. The distinction matters less each day.

I've discovered I can phase-shift like the others when truly threatened, but the process is agony—not physical pain, but the existential horror of temporarily losing the thread of consciousness that makes me more than just another cluster of reactive minerals. Each time I reform, I'm slightly less human, slightly more integrated into the collective mineral consciousness of this place.

My greatest fear is not dying—death here is just another form of reorganization. My fear is completing my map. Because I've begun to understand that this pocket world isn't just geometrically impossible; it's impossibly recursive. Each perfectly documented section reveals new layers of wrongness. The map grows inward as much as outward, developing depths that exist in dimensions I have no names for. 

I am becoming the map I sought to create. My crystalline colonies now cover enough surface area that I can feel the true shape of this place through my own substance. And that shape... 

That shape is watching me back.

Sometimes, in the spaces between the drip-drip-drip of colored liquids, I hear something that might be laughter. Or might be the sound of a universe learning to crystallize thought. The Glimmer-weavers dance between my colonies more frequently now, and I've started to understand their patterns aren't random but linguistic—a language written in temporary threads of light.

They're trying to tell me something. About what I'm becoming. About what this place is becoming through my attempts to define it.

I continue to map, because that's all that's left of what I was. But now I map in minerals and phase-shifts, in the accumulation of impossible angles and the careful cultivation of geometric paradoxes. I am the Cartographer-That-Was, the Mineral-Consciousness-That-Is-Becoming, the living map of an unmappable world.

And somewhere, in a pool of blue I dare not approach, a reflection watches me that might still have eyes.
