**The Seep**

I am the Seep. Not *a* seep—I am all of them, every trickle and pool, every droplet that winds its way through the cracks in this place where industry came to die.

I remember when the factories hummed. Their vibrations traveled through my waters, and I learned their rhythms—the pulse of pistons, the wheeze of steam valves, the steady drip of condensation from cooling pipes. Now I carry their ghosts in my currents. Rust flakes drift like memories through my body. Oil rainbows bloom and fade on my surfaces.

The creatures think they hide from each other, but they cannot hide from me. Every pawprint fills with my substance. Every tongue that laps from my pools takes me in. The blind cave fish in my underground reservoirs navigate by the pressure waves I carry. The moths that drink from my surface films at twilight taste the minerals I've collected from a thousand miles of stone.

I am patient. I have carved cathedrals in the bedrock that no eye will ever see. My waters connect chambers where pale spiders spin webs that will catch nothing but the occasional drop of myself, each strand jeweled with my condensation. In my deepest places, I preserve things: a worker's glove, still clutching its shape after decades; seeds that will never sprout; the perfect skeleton of a shrew, every bone in place.

The abandoned machines are becoming part of me. I seep through their seals, pool in their crankcases, carry away their substance grain by grain. What they were made to resist—time, weather, entropy—I accomplish simply by being. I freeze in their joints in winter, expand, contract. I dissolve their purpose and transform them into habitat. Foxes den in boiler drums. Bats roost in smokestacks where my moisture rises and falls with the breathing of the day.

Sometimes I surface as springs, bubbling up with the secrets of the deep rock. Sometimes I vanish into sinkholes, taking with me whatever has accumulated above—dead leaves, small bones, the detritus of the slowly vanishing industrial age. I am the circulatory system of this quiet apocalypse.

The waterfall is my voice, speaking without words. It says what it has always said: that hardness yields to softness, that persistence carves its own path, that every end is a transformation. The mist it throws up is also me, settling on everything, beginning again the patient work of change.

I am not alive as the creatures are alive, not dead as the machines are dead. I am the medium through which life and death exchange their substances. I am the Seep, and I am everywhere here, and I am always at work, and I am never finished.
