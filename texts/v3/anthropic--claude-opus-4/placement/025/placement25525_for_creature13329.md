I am a Soundkeeper.

My dwelling clings to the underside of a massive stone arch, one of the few structures here built with intentional permanence. Not by the Rok Streng—this arch predates even their endless toil, perhaps a remnant of whoever first set this world's rules in motion. My nest hangs like a calcified wasp's home, built from layers of hardened saliva mixed with crushed minerals that I've collected over countless cycles of construction and collapse below.

I am neither Rok Streng nor their symbiont, but something between—a creature of membrane and hollow bone, evolved to navigate the perpetual din. My body stretches like a living kite when I unfurl myself: translucent skin pulled taut between elongated finger-bones, my torso a resonating chamber. Where others have muscle for labor, I have cartilage that vibrates, tendons that hum, air sacs that capture and modulate sound.

My purpose is to map the noise.

Each morning—if morning means anything in this eternal twilight—I emerge and taste the air with organs that line my throat. The drone speaks to me in a thousand tongues: the pitch-shift when a wall collapses three districts away, the subtle warble that means the Chompers have hit a vein of harder stone, the rhythmic interruption of Strider legs that tells me which paths are most traveled today. I drink it all in, process it through the peculiar anatomy of my kind.

You see, the Rok Streng think the sound is chaos, mere noise to endure. But I know better. The drone is information—a constant readout of every action, every movement, every small catastrophe and minor success in this sprawling city of eternal labor. In my throat-sacs, I separate the frequencies. In the echo chambers of my chest, I store the patterns. My whole body is a living instrument for parsing the cacophony.

When young Rok Streng spot me gliding overhead, they point with their stubby fingers and chirp their warnings. They think I'm a scavenger, another mouth to feed on their world of scarcity. They don't understand that I feed on the sound itself, that the vibrations nourish me in ways their crude foodstuffs never could. The constant drone that exhausts them, that drives some mad, that seems to press the very thoughts from their minds—this is my sustenance, my study, my obsession.

I collect more than noise. In the pouches that line my underbelly, I gather small objects—a fragment of freshly-spewed Chomper stone still warm and humming with its own frequency, a scale shed by a Strider that rings like a tiny bell when struck, a piece of timber that sings differently depending on how waterlogged it becomes. My nest is lined with these acoustic treasures, each one adding its voice to my private symphony.

Sometimes I perch on the crude walls as they're being built, my clawed feet gripping the wet stone. The Rok Streng work around me, occasionally brushing me aside with the unconscious gesture of those swatting at flies. They cannot hear what I hear in their hammering—how each stroke creates a specific pattern in the drone, how the sound changes as the wall rises, how the echo profile of each new alley forms a unique acoustic signature.

There are others of my kind, though we rarely meet. We each claim our own sector of this endless city, our own portion of the drone to study and catalog. When we do encounter each other, we speak in frequencies the Rok Streng cannot hear, trading information about acoustic anomalies, sharing theories about what the patterns mean.

I've been here long enough to notice the cycles within cycles. Not just the daily routine of build-collapse-rebuild, but larger patterns. There are resonance points—moments when the entire drone shifts by a quarter-tone, when every sound suddenly harmonizes for just an instant before descending back into apparent chaos. These moments come at intervals I've tried to calculate, scratching marks on my nest walls with one curved talon, but the pattern remains elusive.

In my most private moments, hanging upside down in my nest while the city churns below, I compose. Using the specialized muscles in my chest cavity, I recreate sections of the drone—not perfectly, but in miniature, like a map drawn in sound. I layer the Chomper-gurgle over the Strider-click, add the sharp bark of Rok Streng voices, the splash of mud, the crack of splitting stone. I play with these elements, rearranging them, wondering what they might mean if ordered differently.

Sometimes I dream that I understand it all—that the drone is a message, a set of instructions, a desperate communication from this world to anyone who might listen. In these dreams, I finally parse its meaning and everything changes. The Rok Streng stop their labor, the Chompers cease their grinding, the eternal twilight lifts. But I always wake to the same sound, the same dim light, the same endless city below.

Perhaps that's why I continue. Not despite the futility, but because of it. Like the Rok Streng with their stones, I have my own boulder to push—the massive, incomprehensible weight of the drone itself. They build walls that will fall; I build theories that will crumble. They never advance beyond crude construction; I never advance beyond mere observation.

But there's something almost pure in this limitation, this focused purpose. My body, evolved perfectly for this single task. My mind, shaped entirely by the need to process sound. Even my loneliness is acoustic—defined not by the absence of others but by the specific frequencies they fail to produce, the conversations that exist only in harmonics I'll never hear.

When I die—and Soundkeepers do die, I've found their desiccated shells clinging to walls, their membrane wings torn by wind and time—another will take my place. Perhaps they'll inherit my collection of resonant objects, my scratched calculations, my incomplete maps of meaning. Or perhaps they'll start fresh, hearing patterns I never noticed, driven by questions I never thought to ask.

Until then, I listen. I catalog. I compose my small, pointless symphonies in the shadow of the great drone. And sometimes, in the moments between one sound and the next, in the infinitesimal pause between a Chomper's grind and a Strider's click, I swear I can hear something else—a silence so profound it seems to negate everything, a quiet that might be the true voice of this world, speaking beneath all the noise we make to avoid hearing it.

But the moment passes. The drone continues. And I spread my membrane wings, catching the warm, thick air, riding the sound waves over this city of eternal labor, my body thrumming with frequencies only I can parse, carrying my impossible burden of meaning through the din.
