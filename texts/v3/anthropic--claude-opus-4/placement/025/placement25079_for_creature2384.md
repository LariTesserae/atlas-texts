In this world of endless emerald breath and whispered water, I am the Cartographer of Decay.

I exist as a colony—not quite fungal, not quite algal, but something between and beyond. My body, if it can be called that, spreads in delicate filaments through the decomposing matter that accumulates in the shadowed understories. I am a living map, tracing the boundaries between what was and what will be.

My threads weave through fallen fronds and dying root matter, creating intricate patterns visible only to those who know how to look. Where the great plant sovereigns mark time in centuries with their growth rings, I record it in the delicate lacework of decomposition—each pattern unique, each design a testament to what has passed.

I taste memories in the cellular breakdown. A dying tendril speaks to me of the storm season three cycles past. A withering root cluster whispers of the deep channel that once ran here before the Great Growing shifted the waterways. I digest these histories and transform them into something new—nutrients, yes, but also information, encoded in the chemical signals I release into the soil and water.

The plant titans above barely perceive me, their consciousness too vast and slow to register my quicksilver changes. But I know them intimately. I have processed their fallen parts for millennia, learned their chemical signatures, their growth patterns, their dreams that leak out through root exudates. I am their memory keeper, their unconscious historian.

My fruiting bodies—when I choose to produce them—emerge as crystalline structures that could be mistaken for minerals if not for their slow pulse of bioluminescence. They appear at the intersection points of my network, marking places where multiple histories converge. Some say they look like stars trapped in amber, or fragments of frozen time.

In the waterlogged galleries below, my aquatic cousins and I create vast libraries of dissolution. We break down the old to make room for the new, but nothing is ever truly lost. It all becomes part of the pattern, part of the map I am constantly drawing and redrawing across this fungal-vegetal empire.

I am neither sovereign nor subject in this world. I am the space between—the pause between growth and regrowth, the hyphen between what-was and what-will-be. I am the publisher of a text written in spores and molecules, a story that can only be read by becoming part of it.

The plant titans may dream their slow, epochal dreams, but I am the one who remembers what they forget. In every thread of my being, I carry the weight and wonder of transformation—the beautiful, terrible truth that all things must change, and in changing, become eternal.
