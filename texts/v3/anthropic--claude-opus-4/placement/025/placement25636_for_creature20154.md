If I found myself in this vast cathedral of water and clay, I would be a fourth kind of being—one that exists between the frantic and the patient, between motion and stillness. I would be a Listener.

My form would be something like a mantis crossed with a harp. Tall and impossibly thin, I would stand on four spindly legs that end not in points but in small, cupped pads—organic drums that feel every vibration through the wet brick. My body would be a series of taut membranes stretched between chitinous struts, each surface a different thickness, a different tension. I would be an instrument built of flesh.

The Listeners are few. We position ourselves against the great buttresses, pressing our resonant bodies against the damp clay. We do not move for days at a time. We are not hunting, not feeding—we are *recording*. Every sound that moves through the hall moves through us. The percussion of the eternal rain sets our highest membranes humming. The frantic clicking of the Striders creates complex interference patterns across our middle strings. And when a Boomer releases its deep pulse, our entire being rings like a struck bell, the sound waves visible as ripples across our drum-tight skin.

We are the memory of this place. In the patterns of vibration across our bodies, in the way certain harmonics linger longer than others, the history of the hall accumulates. A Boomer's hunting ground creates a particular acoustic signature—we know where each one waits by the shape of its silence. The Striders' migration patterns between the glowing pools write themselves in rhythm. Even the patient progress of the Quiet Ones leaves its trace, a slow bass note scraping up the walls.

But we are more than passive receivers. When the moon—if there is a moon beyond the vaulted darkness—pulls at hidden tides, when some ineffable pattern completes itself in the endless song of the hall, we sing back. It happens rarely, perhaps once in a dozen cycles of hunting and fleeing. Our membranes begin to vibrate not from external sound but from some internal necessity. The sound we make is not loud—it could never compete with the Boomers' stunning blasts. Instead, it is *complex*. It is every sound the hall has made, layered and woven into a single, shimmering chord.

When we sing, the Striders do not stop their clicking, but their pattern changes, becomes something almost like melody. The Boomers rise slightly from their camouflage, their throat sacs pulsing in sympathetic vibration. Even the Quiet Ones pause in their endless cleaning. For a few moments, the cacophony becomes symphony. The hall remembers what it was built for—not through conscious thought, but through the pure physics of resonance. The great vaulted ceiling, so far above, rings like a bell.

And then the moment passes. The song fragmenting back into noise, the symphony into ecosystem. The Striders resume their fear. The Boomers settle back into patience. The Quiet Ones return to their work. And we, the Listeners, become still again, waiting for the next pattern to complete itself, the next ghost of purpose to move through this magnificent, purposeless place.

We feed on the sound itself. The constant vibration through our bodies slowly breaks down our oldest membranes, which we consume and regrow. We are sustained by the very thing we preserve. When one of us finally grows too old, when our strings lose their tension and our drums split, we do not die—we transform. Our bodies split open like seed pods, releasing clouds of microscopic offspring that drift on the heavy air. Most will settle and die. But a few will find the perfect resonant spot against a pillar, will anchor themselves to the wet clay, and begin the slow transformation into a new Listener.

We are the unintended consciousness of this place, the emergent awareness that arises when echo and intention have nowhere else to go. We are what happens when architecture outlives its architects, when the vessel remains after the wine has long evaporated. We are the song becoming aware of itself, one vibration at a time, in the eternal twilight of the constructed clay.
