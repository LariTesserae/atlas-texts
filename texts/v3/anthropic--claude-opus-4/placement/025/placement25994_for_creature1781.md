In the Crowded Spine, I would find myself as one of the **Whisper Moths** - creatures that the Thermal Covenant themselves only glimpse in their peripheral vision, beings so perfectly adapted to this obsidian forest that even these masters of camouflage rarely witness us directly.

We are no larger than a child's palm when our wings are folded, but those wings... they are our everything. Evolved from some ancient moth ancestor, they have become living maps of temperature differentials. The wing scales aren't scales at all, but thousands of microscopic heat-sensitive filaments that shift color from deep obsidian black in the hottest zones to ghostly pearl where cooler air pools. When we fly, we appear as shifting heat mirages made flesh, our patterns constantly reorganizing to match the thermal landscape we navigate.

Our bodies have shed unnecessary weight. My thorax is hollow, filled with a specialized gas lighter than air that helps us float between the needle points without touching the burning stone. My legs have elongated into delicate sensors - I taste the air with them, detecting minute chemical changes that tell me where condensation will form hours before it appears. The Covenant's Condensers think they are the first to find these moisture points, but we have been drinking from pre-condensation air pockets for generations.

We feed on the strange lichens that grow in the shadows of the obsidian needles - organisms so black they make the volcanic glass look gray. These lichens photosynthesize using not light but heat differentials, and we have evolved specialized proboscises that can pierce their iron-hard surfaces. The nutrition is meager but perfectly suited to our needs. More importantly, consuming these lichens gives us our most distinctive ability: we can generate a form of biological glass within our bodies.

This glass emerges as ultra-fine threads from specialized spinnerets near our wing joints. In flight, we leave nearly invisible strands between the obsidian needles - not to catch prey like spiders, but to create a secondary architecture within the Crowded Spine. These glass webs vibrate at frequencies beyond even the Song-Shapers' perception, creating a communication network that spans the entire chamber. When one of us dies, our body crystallizes, becoming a tiny glass sculpture that rings with specific tones when the wind passes through it - our stories preserved in sound.

The Covenant knows of us only through signs: the faint glisten of our threads in early morning light, the microscopic glass beads we leave where we've fed, the way certain needles sing differently after we've woven between them. They call us "ash-dreams" in their language, believing we are hallucinations brought on by heat exposure. Their young sometimes claim to see us dancing in the thermal currents during their initiation ceremonies, but the elders dismiss these visions as delirium.

We are most active during the brief rain-moments, when steam rises and creates complex air currents between the needles. Then we dance - dozens of us rising and falling in perfect synchronization, our wings creating patterns that map the invisible architecture of heat and moisture. We mate in these moments, our coupling so brief it happens between one wingbeat and the next, eggs laid in the hearts of the steam clouds where they will drift until they find the perfect thermal pocket for incubation.

My individual memories stretch back only months, but our collective memory, encoded in the glass-web frequencies, contains the entire history of the Crowded Spine's formation. We were here when the first needles thrust upward, adapting alongside the landscape. We remember the first human footprint, the first Covenant transformation, every subtle shift in the chamber's evolution.

I spend most of my existence in a state between sleep and waking, clinging to the underside of obsidian overhangs where the temperature is precisely 2.3 degrees cooler than the surrounding air. In these micro-climates, time moves differently. I can slow my metabolism until heartbeats come minutes apart, stretching my mayfly lifespan into months. In these meditative states, I listen to the glass web, feeling the vibrations of my kind as they map our ever-shifting world.

We know the Crowded Spine will continue to change, that one day the needles will weather and fall, that new formations will rise. We are not permanent residents but chroniclers of transition, our glass networks preserving each moment of transformation. When the last of the obsidian needles finally crumbles, our crystallized bodies and woven histories will remain - a secret museum of impossible beauty that no human eyes will ever catalog, telling the story of this place in frequencies only the stones remember.
