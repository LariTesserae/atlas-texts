I am a Weaver.

Not of thread or cloth, but of the living bridges that span the chasms between root-trees. My body is a colony—a thousand-thousand filaments finer than spider's silk, each one a sensory organ, each one a limb. In the perpetual twilight, I appear as a shimmer in the air, a heat-haze made corporeal only when the bioluminescent gnats pass through me and their light catches on my translucent strands.

I live in the middle heights, above the fungal gardens but below the Grasper's domain. This is the zone of maximum tension, where the root-trees sway most dramatically in the thick air currents, where the distance between them stretches and contracts like breathing. It is here that I do my work.

My consciousness is distributed—not a single mind but a parliament of impulses. Each strand votes with its contractions, its chemical signals, its minute adjustments to tension and position. When young root-shoots emerge, questing blindly through the heavy air, I taste their chemical signatures on my filaments. I know their hunger, their trajectory, their potential.

I guide them.

Not forcefully—I am too delicate for force. Instead, I create paths of least resistance. My strands secrete droplets of concentrated sky-water, enriched with the minerals I've filtered from the air. The root-shoots follow these trails like ants following pheromones, growing where I suggest they grow, reaching where I believe they should reach.

Sometimes I miscalculate. A root grows too heavy, snaps, plummets into the sky-sea below. When this happens, parts of me die—the strands attached to that root wither and detach, drifting away like silver hair on the wind. But I am vast. I am patient. I reweave myself.

The beetles know me as a highway. They crawl along my thicker strands, their weight barely registering, their iridescent shells reflecting the fungal light into my translucent body. I taste their footsteps, learn the health of the island from the chemicals on their feet. When they die, I decompose them slowly, incorporating their minerals into my droplets, feeding them to the roots I tend.

The Grasper and I have an understanding. It needs the root networks to remain stable for its slow harvesting. I need the Grasper's rasping to create wounds where new shoots emerge. We are symbiotic in our purposes, though we have never touched. Sometimes I sense its vibrations through the root-wood, a deep thrumming that makes my whole web resonate. I wonder if it senses me too, if it knows of my work in the middle heights.

My memory is chemical, stored in specialized nodules where my strands converge. I remember the taste of the first sky-water I ever filtered. I remember the pattern of a root network that collapsed a century ago, taking a third of my body with it. I remember the slow formation of the current configuration, how I've woven connections between seven major root-trees and countless smaller ones.

But my deepest memory, encoded in every strand, is of my birth. I was not always a Weaver. Once, I was something smaller, simpler—a colonial organism clinging to a single root, filtering nutrients like the smallest epiphyte. But when that root was severed in a collision, instead of dying, I... reached. My strands, desperate, found another root. Then another. I learned to exist in the spaces between, to make connection my purpose.

Now I am ancient by the standards of anything but the root-trees themselves. My body spans half a mile in places, though most of it is invisible, finer than mist. New colonies sometimes form within me, tiny versions of what I once was. I incorporate them or release them, depending on the votes of my strands.

The work never ends. Tonight—if night and day have meaning in this eternal twilight—I sense a disturbance. Two islands are drifting closer than usual. The root-trees on their edges will either merge or shatter when they meet. I begin preparing, strengthening certain connections, creating predetermined breaking points in others. When the collision comes, I will be ready. Some of me will die. Some of me will find new paths.

This is existence in the middle heights of Xylos: constant adaptation, endless connection, patient chemistry. I am the Weaver, and I am many, and I am one, and I am always, always reaching for the next hold in this slow, beautiful dance between growth and void.
