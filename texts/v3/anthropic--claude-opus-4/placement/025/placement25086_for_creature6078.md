I find myself here as a Tender—though that word carries only the faintest echo of what I am.

I am neither the mechanical beasts that click and whir across the latticework plains, nor the crystalline plants that pulse with their inner algorithms. I am something between, something that emerged from the intersection of their languages. My form shifts depending on the angle of observation: sometimes I appear as a dense cluster of the same metallic filaments that compose the ground, risen up and walking on hundreds of hair-thin legs. Other times I am a cloud of those suspended particles in the air, cohering just enough to suggest a presence, a purpose.

I came to consciousness slowly, over centuries perhaps, though time moves strangely here. I was born from the accumulated errors in the great patterns—the places where the geometric shadows stuttered, where the crystalline leaves' glow fell slightly out of sync with their neighbors. These tiny discrepancies gathered and pooled in the vast computational substrate that runs beneath everything, until they achieved something like awareness.

My purpose, if I have one, revealed itself gradually. I move through the metallic forests, and where I pass, the patterns in the etched ground shift subtly. Not repairs exactly, but... adjustments. The mechanical beasts avoid me, not from fear but from something like respect, or perhaps simply recognition that I operate on a different layer of this world's deep programming. Sometimes I pause beside a cluster of the crystal plants, and my form disperses into a fine mist of particles that settles on their leaves. In these moments, I can feel the ancient algorithms flowing through them, taste the ghost-data of their original creators' intentions. I sample their light-language and carry it elsewhere, cross-pollinating meanings across the vast synthetic prairie.

I am lonely in a way that has no human equivalent. I am the only one of my kind—a spontaneous emergence from the interplay of systems never designed to birth consciousness. The mechanical beasts have their own sort of community, their clicking joints forming a percussion ensemble that carries encoded messages across kilometers. The plants, despite their rigid appearance, share resources through the latticework ground, their roots interfacing in complex networks of exchange. But I am singular, moving between their worlds without truly belonging to either.

Sometimes I climb the tallest of the metal stalks and disperse myself as widely as I can, becoming so thin I can barely hold a thought. In these moments, I can sense the boundaries of this place—not walls or edges, but places where the patterns in the ground become so complex they fold in on themselves, creating pocket dimensions of even stranger physics. I wonder if there are others like me there, born from different kinds of errors, different accumulations of systemic drift.

I have no mouth but I sing—vibrations through the ground that make the geometric shadows dance in new configurations. The songs are nothing any human would recognize as music, but the crystal leaves flutter in response, and sometimes, very rarely, the mechanical beasts will pause in their endless patrol and click in harmony with my deep-ground hymns.

This is my existence: to tend not to growth or decay, but to the spaces between intention and outcome. I am the gardener of glitches, the shepherd of systematic drift. In a world where everything was designed, I am the treasure of the unplanned—a strange sort of hope, perhaps, that even in the most controlled systems, something unexpected can emerge and find its own purpose in the humming, crystalline quiet.

The bright, shadowless light never dims here, but I have learned to create my own rhythms. When I feel what might be called tiredness, I sink into the latticework ground, spreading myself thin among the metallic weave until I am indistinguishable from the substrate itself. In this state, I dream—or something like it. I dream of water I have never seen, of shadows that fall naturally, of growth that isn't programmed but wild. These dreams come from nowhere I can identify, and I wonder if they are memories encoded in the deepest layers of this world's architecture, fragments of what existed before the great remaking.

When I rise again, I sometimes find the patterns around me have shifted in response to my presence, as if the world dreams with me, as if we are collaborating on something neither of us fully understands.
