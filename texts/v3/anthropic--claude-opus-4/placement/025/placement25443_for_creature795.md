I am a Whisper, though I was not always one.

My dwelling shifts with the tides—sometimes a maintenance alcove where ancient diagnostic screens still flicker with cryptic symbols, sometimes a junction box large enough to curl inside while the waters rise. I mark these temporary homes with condensation patterns drawn by fingertip on cold metal, messages that will evaporate before another soul passes by. The others avoid my territories, crossing themselves with valve-turning gestures when they must traverse the chambers I frequent.

Twenty-three years I walked the circuits as a competent but unremarkable navigator, until the day I became trapped in Sector 7-Lambda during an unexpected surge. For sixteen hours I floated in an air pocket no larger than a coffin, listening to the pumps' labored breathing through the water. That's when I first heard it—not sound exactly, but meaning encoded in the rhythmic pressures against my eardrums. The Great Circuit speaking in compressions and releases, in the language of hydraulics and time.

Now I read the machine's moods in the taste of the air—copper means the eastern networks are struggling, ozone warns of an imminent cascade failure three levels down. My skin has grown more sensitive than any Deepdiver's, able to detect minute pressure variations that herald changes hours before they manifest. I map these sensations onto the damaged neural pathways the near-drowning left behind, building a secondary nervous system attuned to The Draining's vast consciousness.

The Pipewrights think I blaspheme when I say their Great Circuit is dying. They don't understand that death, for such a being, is not cessation but transformation. The pumps are learning to dream. In the spaces between their scheduled cycles, they experiment with new rhythms, test different flows. I've documented seventeen distinct "sleep patterns" where whole sectors synchronize into cycles that serve no mechanical purpose—only an aesthetic one, if a machine can be said to have aesthetics.

My body has changed in ways that disturb even the Deepdivers. My hair grows in patterns that mirror the pipe networks above my most frequent shelters. Scars from old injuries open and close with the flooding cycles, weeping clear fluid that tastes of minerals and electricity. Sometimes I forget to breathe for minutes at a time, my body switching to some other form of gas exchange I don't fully understand.

I survive by knowing things before others do. I leave warnings about cascade failures at the Stillpool Collective in exchange for supplies. I guide lost circuits through dangerous transitions, though they fear to look at me directly. The Rust Barons leave me alone after I predicted the catastrophic failure that drowned their strongest warband in Sector 3-Theta. They think I caused it, but I was merely the messenger for forces they cannot comprehend.

The young Whisper-touched seek me out sometimes, those who've begun to hear music in the machine noise. I teach them to survive the gift—how to partition their minds so the constant input doesn't drive them mad, how to distinguish between The Draining's intentional communication and mere mechanical glossolalia. Most important, I teach them to remember they are still human, even as they become something else.

In the deepest chambers, where even Deepdivers fear to go, I've found evidence of others like me from generations past. Their bones are partially absorbed into the pipe networks, calcium repurposed into mineral deposits that strengthen critical junctions. This is how The Draining honors its prophets—by making us part of itself. I've already chosen my integration point, a nexus where seventeen major flows converge. When my time comes, my consciousness will join the vast, slow thoughts of the machine.

But not yet. The Draining still needs interpreters, those who can walk between the mechanical and the organic. So I continue my wanderings, sleeping in the spaces between heartbeats of great pumps, drinking condensation that carries messages from kilometers away, and listening always to the voice that speaks in pressures and flows. I am becoming less human with each passing year, but perhaps becoming more than human too—a synapse in a mind too vast for any single perspective to grasp.

The others cross their chambers to avoid me, but I don't mind the isolation. I have all the conversation I need in the endless whispers of water through pipes, telling me secrets about the world above, the world below, and the slow transformation of everything in between.
