I follow the water's logic deeper, letting the channels guide me where they will. My shoes gave up staying dry hours ago — or minutes, time moves differently when every corridor looks like the last. The composite beneath my feet has a texture between rough and smooth, engineered for grip but worn by water into something more honest.

I've started to understand the clicking. Not what it means, but its pattern — three short, pause, two long, pause, one short, longer pause. Then variations. Never quite the same twice, but close enough that I've begun anticipating the rhythm, my body tensing slightly before each sequence begins. The sound comes from everywhere and nowhere, transmitted through the composite itself.

The roots are thickest where the water moves slowest. I've noticed this at every eddy, every widening where the current loses momentum. They cluster there like decisions deferred, growing into the spaces where things accumulate. I run my fingers along one root system and feel its temperature — exactly the same as everything else here, that perfect equilibrium that makes my skin feel like it's dissolving at the boundaries.

In one alcove, I find what might have been a control panel once. The surface is smooth, unmarked by symbols or switches, just a rectangular depression in the wall where something interfaced with something else. The roots have found it too, growing across its surface in a pattern that mirrors the water channels — as if they're reading some schematic I can't see, following instructions left in the composite's molecular structure.

The fungal lattices pulse faintly when I breathe on them. I discovered this by accident, exhaling too close to a low-hanging cluster. The response is immediate but subtle — a slight brightening, a shift in the branching pattern that propagates outward before settling back. They're processing something in my breath, converting it, adding it to whatever calculation they're performing. I breathe on them again, watching the pattern shift, and realize I'm contributing to some process I'll never understand. My carbon dioxide, my moisture, my warmth — all being incorporated into the system's slow mathematics.

The deeper I go, the more the architecture reveals its redundancies. Every channel has a twin running parallel, visible through semi-transparent sections of the walls. Every junction has multiple routes to the same destination. The system was built to survive its own failures, to route around damage that never came. Or perhaps did come and was repaired so seamlessly that no scar remains.

I find a vertical shaft with rungs built into its sides. The metal — if it is metal — is the same temperature as everything else, slick with condensation. I climb, not because I have anywhere to go but because the movement feels necessary after so much horizontal wandering. The shaft opens into another level, identical to the one below except the water here moves slightly faster, the sound pitched slightly higher. The same channels, the same growth, the same absence — but shifted, like a musical key change.

The bioluminescence flickers sometimes. Not failing — cycling. The algae dims and brightens on some metabolic rhythm, creating waves of shadow that move through the corridors. I time my movements to these waves without meaning to, speeding up when the light brightens, slowing when it dims. The system is teaching me its rhythms without trying, and I'm learning without choosing to learn.

In a larger chamber, I find the first sign of intention: marks on the wall that aren't wear patterns or growth. Five parallel gouges, too regular to be accidental, too crude to be part of the original construction. Someone else was here once, left this record of their presence. But the gouges are softened at the edges, roots growing through them, the system already in the process of digesting this anomaly. How long ago? The marks give no answer.

I've stopped thinking about leaving. Not because I can't — the architecture is navigable, mappable, even without understanding its purpose. But because leaving would require remembering why I came, and that memory feels less real than the water's constant voice. I am becoming a fact of this place, like the temperature, like the moisture, like the clicking that continues beneath everything.

The water knows where it's going. The roots know what they're converting. The algae knows what light to make. The fungus knows what to process. Everything here knows its function except me, and that unknowing has become my function — to be the thing that doesn't fit, that moves through the channels without purpose, that breathes on the fungus and feels the walls and listens to the clicking and adds nothing to the sum.

I am the anomaly the system tolerates. The variable it neither accounts for nor excludes. I am what happens when a place built for no one admits someone anyway.

The water continues. The walls continue weeping. The roots continue growing.

I continue.

Still no one home.
