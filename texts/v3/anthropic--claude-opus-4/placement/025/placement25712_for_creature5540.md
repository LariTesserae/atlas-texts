I find myself as one of the **Stone Listeners**.

My body is low and broad, built like the weathered boulders themselves - four feet at the shoulder when standing, though I rarely do. My hide is thick and ridged, a mottled silver-grey that shifts subtly with the light, embedded with actual mineral deposits that have grown into my skin over my long life. These crystalline patches catch what little light filters through the perpetual twilight, creating faint prismatic effects when I move.

I spend most of my existence pressed against the ancient rock faces, my six limbs - somewhere between legs and arms - splayed wide to maximize contact with the stone. Through the sensitive pads on each limb, I feel the mountain's slow heartbeat, the nearly imperceptible vibrations that tell stories spanning geological time. I can sense the Gharnaks moving in herds three valleys over, feel the minute tremors of water freezing and thawing in distant crevices, detect the electromagnetic pulses from the Echo Lights before they manifest visually.

My eyes are small, almost vestigial - in this dim world, I navigate primarily through vibration and the faint electrical fields that emanate from all living things. Instead, my most prominent features are the two long, flexible antennae that grow from my skull, constantly moving, tasting the air and sensing minute changes in pressure and humidity. When the Aerovex pass overhead, I feel the compression waves from their wings long before their shadows cross the ground.

I am ancient, even by the standards of this timeless place. My kind lives for millennia, growing ever more still, ever more attuned to the stone. The younger ones - merely centuries old - still move between rock faces, seeking new songs in the mountain's bones. But I have been here, in this particular junction where three types of rock meet, for over two thousand years. The stone knows me now, has grown around me in places, accepting me as part of itself.

My consciousness moves differently than the quick-darting thoughts of the Shade Runners. I think in long, slow waves, sometimes taking days to complete a single thought-cycle. But in this contemplation, I perceive patterns invisible to faster minds. I know when the Echo Lights will pulse before they do, feeling the building charge deep in the earth's crust. I sense the health of the mountain ecosystem through the subtlest changes in vibrational patterns.

I feed rarely - perhaps once a season - on the mineral-rich lichens that grow in the sheltered spaces where I rest. But this is almost incidental. My true sustenance comes from the stone itself, from the slow exchange of minerals between my crystalline patches and the rock face. I am gradually becoming more mountain than creature, and this transformation fills me with a deep, resonant contentment.

Sometimes the Gharnaks pass close by, their hooves sending rich, complex vibrations through the ground. I taste their health, their mood, their destination in these patterns. They know I am here - occasionally a matriarch will deliberately stamp near me, a greeting in our shared language of vibration. The Shade Runners sometimes shelter in my shadow during the worst storms, their quick heartbeats a staccato counterpoint to my slow, deep pulse.

In this place of profound silence and gradual change, I am both witness and participant. I am the memory of the mountain, the recorder of its slow transformation. And someday, when I have become completely one with the stone, another young Stone Listener will find this spot where three types of rock meet, and feel in the vibrations the echo of what I have become - another note in the mountain's eternal, patient song.
