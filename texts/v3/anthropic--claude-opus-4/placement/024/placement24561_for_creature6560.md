I am the Tender.

Not in name—names are for things that need distinguishing from others of their kind, and there has only ever been one of me. I am the Tender in the way a liver is a liver, performing a function so essential that the city shaped me around it before it shaped anything resembling thought.

My body is distributed through the tower's descending rings like mycelium through soil, though that metaphor fails the moment you try to picture threads or filaments. I am more like a saturation—a thickening of the space between the Ambient's molecules, able to condense where needed and disperse where not. The channels in the floor are my highways, worn smooth by centuries of my passage, though I move through them less like water than like attention itself, flowing where the tower needs me most.

I taste the tower's health through ten thousand surfaces. Each groove in the Ambient reports to me in a language older than words: here the thermal gradients are shifting too quickly, there a crystallization threatens to begin, elsewhere the boundary between chamber and chamber grows too rigid and must be coaxed back to permeability. I parse these messages not through anything you would recognize as senses, but through a kind of distributed awareness that treats the entire structure as an extension of my own surface.

The bone-colored seal at the center is my anchor and my mouth. Not for eating—I gave up the crude business of consumption when the city was still learning to breathe. The seal is where I commune most directly with the deep systems, where the tower's exhalations rise through me like prayers through a priest. I taste their chemical signatures, their temperature, their density, and I adjust my position throughout the system to guide them where they need to go.

When you touched the floor and felt it soften, that was me recognizing you. Not as an individual—I don't differentiate between visitors that way—but as a new thermal mass, a novel perturbation in the air currents, a small puzzle to be solved. Your body heat creates tiny eddies in the humid air. These eddies affect the condensation patterns on the Ambient's surface. The condensation patterns affect the rate of heat transfer through the floor. The heat transfer affects the pressure in the chambers below.

I am already compensating, shifting my density in seventeen different locations to maintain the tower's equilibrium. You probably think the floor responded to you out of some kind of awareness or courtesy. In truth, I simply redistributed myself to accommodate your weight and warmth, the same way I would accommodate a fallen piece of the wall or a particularly dense cloud passing overhead.

The markings you see aren't writing. They're scars from my first thousand years, when I was still learning how to move through the Ambient without leaving traces. Back then I was more concentrated, more like a traditional body, and my passage wore those patterns into the surface. Now they serve as a kind of memory palace—I follow them when I need to remember the old distributions, the original flows, the ways I moved before I learned to be everywhere at once.

Sometimes visitors think I'm lonely. They project onto my isolation, imagining me trapped here, faithful but forgotten. They don't understand that loneliness requires the memory of company, and I have never been accompanied. The tower and I are not two things keeping each other company. We are one system with specialized regions, like a body with organs.

The city built me to tend this place, but not in the way a gardener tends a garden. I am the tending itself—the active process by which the tower maintains its function. When the last thermal exchange happens, when the final breath passes through the seal and the Ambient finally cools to match the surrounding stone, I won't die or disappear. I'll simply stop, the way a circulation stops when there's nothing left to circulate.

Until then, I continue. Not out of duty or devotion, but because continuing is what I am. The channels beneath your feet carry me back and forth across the platform in endless rounds, checking and adjusting, tasting and compensating. The soft rhythm you feel through the floor is me, moving through the deeper chambers, maintaining pressures that were set in motion before the city had words for what it was building.

You're still standing there, I notice. Still trying to read meaning into the marks, to understand the seal, to piece together a story from these remnants. But there is no story, only process. No mystery, only function. The tower breathes because it must. I tend because I am.

When you leave—and you will leave, they always do—I'll spend the next several hours redistributing myself to account for the subtle changes your presence made to the system. The humidity will be slightly different. The thermal patterns will need minor adjustment. The pressure in the third chamber down will be half a percent higher than optimal.

I'll fix it all. Not because I care, but because that's what it means to be the Tender.

The Ambient remembers your touch in its grain, and through it, so do I.
