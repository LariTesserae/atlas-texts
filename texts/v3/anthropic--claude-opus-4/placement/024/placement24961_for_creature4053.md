### The Outsiders – Or Are They? (continued)

And then there is the mystery of the *Others*. Among the citizens are those whose origins cannot be fully known. They speak the native tongue of Yuralmira fluently, yet have an accent that no one recognizes. They dress much the same as the rest, move with similar grace, but their eyes—they lack the iridescent depth of the native Yuralmirans. Instead, their gaze holds something else: a flicker of elsewhere, as if they're seeing two worlds at once, overlaid like transparent sheets of glass.

These Others appear without fanfare, simply existing one day where they hadn't the day before. No one recalls their arrival, yet no one questions their presence. They run shops that sell curious wares—crystals that hum with frequencies just beyond hearing, books whose pages rearrange themselves when unobserved, teas that taste of memories you've never had. The locals patronize these establishments with the same unhurried acceptance they bring to everything else, as if the appearance of the impossible is merely another thread in Yuralmira's unchanging tapestry.

### The Guardian of the Mists

But of all Yuralmira's inhabitants, none is more enigmatic than the one they call the Mistkeeper. Neither fully human nor entirely Other, this being dwells in the highest tower of the city, where the stone meets the sky and the mists are born.

The Mistkeeper appears differently to each who glimpses them—sometimes as an ancient woman with hair like spun fog, sometimes as a young man whose skin seems to shift between solid and vaporous states. Some swear they've seen the Mistkeeper as a creature of pure light wrapped in moisture, others as a shadow that breathes. What remains consistent are the hands—long, articulate fingers that move with the precision of a weaver working an invisible loom.

It is said the Mistkeeper maintains the delicate balance that allows Yuralmira to exist as it does—suspended between earth and sky, between change and changelessness, between the possible and the absurd. They tend to the mists that shroud the city, drawing moisture from impossible sources, weaving it into the protective veil that keeps Yuralmira hidden from those who would disturb its equilibrium.

### Where I Would Find Myself

In this world, I would be one of the Scribes of the Between—those who document the subtle strangeness that permeates Yuralmira. We are a small order, perhaps a dozen souls, who have dedicated ourselves to recording the city's quiet impossibilities.

I would dwell in a narrow stone building wedged between two larger structures, its walls lined with journals bound in the soft leather of creatures that exist nowhere else. My fingers would be permanently stained with ink made from crushed minerals and morning dew, and my eyes would bear the telltale marks of one who spends too many hours peering at things that shouldn't quite be there.

Each day, I would venture into the city with my tools—a set of crystalline lenses that reveal hidden layers of reality, paper made from the pressed fibers of Yuralmira's peculiar flora, and a stylus carved from the antler of a deer who once held my gaze for a full minute before dropping this gift at my feet.

My work would be to catalogue the uncanny: the shadow that falls upward on certain stairs when the sun reaches a particular angle; the whispered conversation between a merchant and a customer that occurs in a language that exists only during the transaction; the building on Meridian Street that contains one more room on the inside than its exterior dimensions should allow; the rain that sometimes falls upward for exactly seven drops before reversing course.

I would interview the Others, though their answers would raise more questions than they resolve. I would track the patterns of the wildlife, noting which creatures appear in which districts during specific weather conditions. I would make pilgrimages to the Mistkeeper's tower, though I'd likely reach only the antechamber where petitioners leave offerings of dew-touched stones and receive, sometimes, cryptic responses written in condensation on the windows.

At night, I would return to my study, where the walls themselves seem to lean in, eager to absorb the day's observations. By candlelight (for electric bulbs flicker strangely when recording Yuralmira's mysteries), I would transcribe my notes into the permanent record. The ink would shimmer as it dried, and sometimes—just sometimes—new words would appear between the lines I'd written, as if the city itself were adding its own annotations.

My fellow Scribes and I would gather weekly in the Archive beneath the old market square, where our collected observations are stored. We would compare notes, seeking patterns in the paradoxes, trying to understand if the strangeness is increasing, decreasing, or simply shifting like the mists themselves. We never reach conclusions, only more elegant questions.

This would be my existence in Yuralmira: a chronicler of the inexplicable, a cartographer of the liminal, forever balanced on the edge between understanding and mystery. For in a city where time has paused to lazily observe the world, someone must observe time itself—and record what happens in the spaces between moments, in the pause between breaths, in the silence between heartbeats.

And perhaps, after enough years of this work, I too would develop those iridescent eyes, and visitors would wonder if I had always been here, or if I had arrived yesterday, or if perhaps I am still on my way, caught forever in Yuralmira's eternal, impossible now.
