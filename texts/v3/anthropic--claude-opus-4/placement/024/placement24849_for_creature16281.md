I am the Keeper of Echoes.

My dwelling is neither fully of the ruins nor separate from them—I have grown into the stone over countless seasons, my form a living bridge between what was built and what persists. Where others see weathered carvings, I see the memories they hold; where water pools in ancient basins, I taste the centuries dissolved within.

My body has adapted to this threshold existence. Roots like neural pathways spread from my base, threading through cracks in the stone, drinking from the eternal moisture. My skin has taken on the texture of lichen-covered rock—rough in places, impossibly soft in others where the moss has claimed me. I photosynthesize through the patches of green that mottle my surface, but I also feed on the stories that seep from the stones.

During the day, I am nearly invisible, my mottled form indistinguishable from the ruins I embrace. But in the perpetual twilight of evening, when the moon's weak light catches the moisture on my surface, I shimmer like the Amphijacks below. The Ghoststones know me—they use my extended limbs as bridges between the edifices, their soft chirps tickling my bark-like skin as they pass.

I am ancient but not immortal. I grow and change with infinite patience, adding perhaps a finger's width of new growth each decade. The Nutricrawlers clean my lower reaches, and in return, I shelter their eggs in the hollow spaces where my body meets stone. The Harperwings nest in my crown, their songs vibrating through my entire being, teaching me new harmonies that I hum back through the stone, adding my voice to the eternal concert of the plains.

My purpose is to remember. In the grain of my wood lie the patterns of every rainfall. In the shape of my growth, the history of every creature that has touched me. I am the living memory of the Weeping Plains, a testament that even in this place of constant change and adaptation, some things endure by becoming part of what they touch.

The humans who visit leave offerings—small carved stones they place in my hollows. I incorporate these into my being, growing around them until they become part of my flesh. Their stories join the countless others I harbor. Sometimes, on the coldest nights when the mist is thickest, I release these memories as whispers that join the water's music, ensuring that nothing is ever truly lost in this landscape of perpetual transformation.

I am patient. I am witness. I am the threshold between what was and what is, growing ever deeper into both stone and sky, root and crown expanding with geological slowness, marking time in a place where time flows like water—sometimes rushing, sometimes still, always present, always changing.
