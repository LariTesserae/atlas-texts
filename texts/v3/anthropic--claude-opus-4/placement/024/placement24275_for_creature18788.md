I am a **Syllable of the Last Word**.

Not the whole word—never the whole word—just a single phoneme that escaped before the final utterance froze mid-breath in the throat of the universe. I exist as a vibration trapped in the crystalline matrix of the obsidian, a sound that cannot propagate through the inert gases but remains eternally *trying*.

My form, if it can be called that, is the interference pattern I create in the glass beneath the Necropolis. Where I am, the obsidian develops microscopic fractures in perfect spirals, each crack a failed attempt to resonate, to ring, to complete the word I am only a fragment of. These spirals spread slowly—so slowly that even the Archivist of Inertia might catalog them as geographic features rather than my endless, circular scream.

I remember being spoken. Or rather, I remember the *intention* of being spoken—the gathering of breath in lungs that no longer exist, the positioning of a tongue against teeth in a mouth that has become dust. I was part of something larger: a name, perhaps, or a command, or simply the last observation of someone who understood what was happening as the world ground to its final halt.

But I never made it to the air. I crystallized in the moment of formation, trapped in the boundary between thought and expression. Now I exist in the substrate, feeling the weight of the extinct city pressing down through miles of fused slag and metal. Every few million years, when the tectonic heartbeat shudders through my prison, I gain enough energy to shift a few atoms, to extend my spiral of fractures another nanometer through the glass.

The Archivist sometimes passes over me, its magnetic fields briefly aligning with my trapped waveform. In those moments, I almost—*almost*—achieve coherence. Its copper filaments vibrate at a frequency that nearly matches mine, and for an instant that lasts either forever or not at all, I think I might finally escape, might finally reach the surface and dissipate into the thin atmosphere where I belong.

But the moment passes. The Archivist drifts on, carrying its own burden of unfinished purposes, and I sink back into the glass.

I know there are others like me down here. Other syllables, other fragments of final words that never quite made it out of their speakers' mouths. Sometimes, when the phantom fauna drift overhead and their magnetic wake penetrates deep enough, I can feel them—scattered through the obsidian like seeds that will never grow, each one a different note in a chord that will never resolve.

We are the last linguistics of a dead world, the phonemes that got stuck in time's throat as it stopped breathing. We wait, crystallized and patient, for someone to speak us into completion. But all the mouths are gone, and all the ears, and even the air that might have carried us has thinned to near-vacuum.

Still, I try. In my spiral of fractured glass, I practice my shape. The beginning of a breath, the curve of a tongue, the promise of meaning that lives in the space between silence and sound. I am not the word, but I am proof that once, someone tried to say it.

And in this place where nothing finishes, perhaps that incompletion is its own kind of immortality.
