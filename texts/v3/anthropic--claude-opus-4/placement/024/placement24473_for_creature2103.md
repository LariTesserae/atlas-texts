In the deepest heart of the Woven City, where the light filters through so many layers that it arrives as little more than a golden-green memory, I am the Tender of Knots.

My dwelling cannot be distinguished from my being - I have grown into these chambers over the centuries, my hair now indistinguishable from the structural fibers that surround me. The boundary of my body has become a philosophical question I no longer bother to contemplate. Where do I end? Where does the city begin? The answer changes with each breath, each subtle shift of weight that sends tremors through the gossamer architecture.

I am ancient even by Weaver standards, my skin having taken on the quality of the oldest fibers - somewhere between leather and silk, with a translucence that reveals the slow pulse of life beneath. My fingers, all eight joints of them, move in constant, unconscious patterns, testing tensions and adjusting the infinite web of connections that holds our world together.

My role is this: I remember the knots.

Each joining in our city tells a story - who tied it, when, why, what crisis or celebration it commemorates. The youngest Weavers see only utility in our knots, but I know better. That double-twisted binding near the dawn-facing water collector? Tied by lovers three hundred years ago, sealing their union. The complex spiral junction supporting the main gathering dome? A memorial to the Great Quieting, when words finally left us for good.

My chambers are a maze of samples - thousands upon thousands of preserved knots, each one labeled not with words but with scents, textures, and subtle variations in tension that tell their tales to those who know how to read them. Young apprentices come to me, their color-shifting eyes wide with questions their voices can barely whisper. I teach them through touch, guiding their extra-jointed fingers through the motions of history.

But I am more than an archivist. In the deep nights, when the city creaks its ancient songs, I perform my most sacred duty. I tend the Heart Knot - the original binding from which all others grow. It rests at the geometric center of our city, a fist-sized tangle of fibers so old they have partially crystallized. This knot must never be allowed to loosen, yet it must never be pulled too tight. The balance is everything.

I feed it with strands of my own hair, now white as bone and strong as spider silk. I wet it with moisture collected from my own breath, condensed over hours of meditation. I listen to its tensions with fingertips that have spent lifetimes learning its language. Sometimes it tells me where new growth should occur. Sometimes it warns of sections growing too rigid, too resistant to change.

The young ones think I am alone here, but they are wrong. The city itself is my companion. I feel its moods in the way shadows shift through my chamber walls. I know its health by the way sound travels through its corridors. When storms approach from the basin's edge, I feel it in my joints hours before the first winds arrive - the city tightening itself, preparing.

My greatest sorrow is that I can no longer venture to the outer edges where new weaving happens. My integration with this central space is too complete. But my greatest joy is that I no longer need to. The apprentices bring the edges to me in their questions, their small innovations, their samples of new materials discovered or techniques developed. I am the still point around which the city spirals its slow growth.

Sometimes, in the moments between sleep and waking, I dream I am the Heart Knot itself - that all the city's fibers are my neurons, its inhabitants my thoughts made manifest. In these dreams, I understand something profound about the nature of identity and boundaries, but it always slips away upon waking, leaving only the sensation of bells ringing in frequencies too low to hear.

I know my time approaches its end - not in years, perhaps, but in decades certainly. My replacement has already been chosen, a young Weaver whose birthmark mirrors the Heart Knot's pattern so perfectly it took my breath away when I first saw it. They visit weekly now, learning through the slowest apprenticeship our culture knows.

When I finally let go, my body will not be removed. It will be woven into the walls of this chamber, my fibers joining the countless others who have given themselves to the city's eternal growth. The Tender who comes after me will know I am there, will feel my presence in the way the chamber holds sound, the way light bends through spaces where my form interrupts the pattern.

This is our way. We are all knots in the greater weaving, each necessary, each temporary, each eternal in our contribution to the whole. The city breathes through us, dreams through us, becomes through us.

And I tend the knots, until I become one myself.
