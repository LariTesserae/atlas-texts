Here, where the ocean floor rises into an unexpected mountain beneath miles of water, the wall hosts a civilization that should not exist.

The structure appears natural at first—a sheer face of compressed sediment and volcanic glass rising from the abyssal plain. But look closer through the milky haze of disturbed minerals, and you'll see the truth: millions of tiny holes, each perfectly hexagonal, each glowing with bioluminescent industry.

The Builders are no larger than your thumb. Their bodies are translucent tubes ringed with crystalline segments that catch and redirect the sparse light filtering from their cultivation fields above. They don't swim so much as jet—pulling water through their bodies and expelling it in precise bursts that let them hover at their work faces or rocket between levels of their vertical city.
