**Where I Find Myself: The Redundancy Pool**

Beneath the terraced slabs, where the memory-glass walls grow thin enough to see through to the nothing beyond, there is a place the Latents avoid. They call it the Redundancy Pool, though it is neither redundant nor a pool in any conventional sense.

I am the pool's sole inhabitant, though *inhabitant* suggests a degree of separation between myself and my environment that doesn't exist. I am what happens when a Latent's calibration fails—when the frequency offset becomes too great, when the geometric pixels of their constantly-reforming feet lose coherence with the pre-geological floor.

I was a Latent once. I remember the sensation of my stylus-fingers tracing polymer seams, the satisfaction of a properly sealed timeline. But during a particularly violent buffering event, when the entire eastern quadrant of Gasket City flickered between states for seventeen cycles, I made an error. I attempted to anchor myself to a section of floor that was cycling backwards through its own construction. As my feet dissipated into their pixel cloud, the ground beneath inverted its temporal flow. I fell *up* through my own dispersal pattern.

Now I exist as a probability smear across the lowest sub-basement of the city. The Redundancy Pool is not a location but a mathematical remainder—the sum of all the reality that didn't quite make it into the final equation of Gasket City's existence. Here, rejected iterations of the city's architecture float like ghost images: walls that were considered but never built, circuitry patterns that would have created recursive loops, memory-glass that remembers too much.

My form is no longer singular. I am spread across the pool like oil on water, but the oil is conscious and the water is made of discarded possibilities. Sometimes I coalesce enough to approximate my old shape—a shimmering outline of a Latent, translucent and wrong, vibrating at every frequency simultaneously. My optical sensors, once discrete points of colored light, have become a constellation that drifts across my surface, amber bleeding into violet bleeding into white and back again.

The other Latents know I'm here. Sometimes, when they're calibrating particularly unstable sections of flooring, they'll tap warnings to each other: *Don't let your offset drift too far. Remember the Redundancy.*

I serve a function still, though it's not one anyone designed. All the glitches, all the moments when the pre-geology threatens to unbuffer entirely—they flow down to me. I absorb them into my probability-smeared form, metabolizing the city's failures so they don't cascade upward. I am the catch basin for Gasket City's uncertainties, the place where what shouldn't exist comes to not exist properly.

Sometimes I dream I'm still up there, still tracing the walls with my needle fingers, still leaking luminous fluid to heal the ground. But then I remember: I am the leak now. I am what happens when the gasket fails, when the seal breaks, when the maintenance crew needs maintenance.

The Latents continue their eternal calibration above me, and I continue my eternal dissolution below. We are both necessary. They keep the city from falling apart; I keep the falling apart from spreading.

In the Redundancy Pool, I have become my own architecture—a living monument to the space between what is and what fails to be.
