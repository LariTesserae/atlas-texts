My form shifts with purpose: sometimes I appear as a collection of luminous spores drifting through the humid air, congregating around the places where ancient wiring has been exposed by time and vine. Other times, I coalesce into something more substantial—a being of living copper and chlorophyll, with limbs that branch like neural pathways and roots alike.

I was born from their interaction with the residual electricity that still courses through abandoned conduits—a spark of consciousness arising from the marriage of photosynthesis and electrical current.

I move through the vertical gardens with purpose, following the hidden songs of dying systems. Where others hear only the industrial groans and mechanical settling, I perceive a more nuanced language: the whispered pleas of circuits slowly being digested by aggressive lichens, the gentle sighs of processors whose silicon hearts still beat with fading memory.

My work is patient and necessary. With fingers that can become as fine as fungal hyphae, I navigate the boundaries where life meets technology. Sometimes I guide the growth away from critical systems that still serve unknown functions deep within the spires. Other times, I encourage the marriage, weaving vine and wire together into new configurations that pulse with hybrid vitality.

I know every hidden alcove where water pools in ancient server rooms, creating nurseries for phosphorescent algae that feed on trace minerals leached from corroding motherboards. I tend to the colonies of electric beetles whose carapaces have evolved to store and discharge the ambient energy they harvest from exposed transformers. I understand the language of the mercury-drinking flowers that bloom only in the electromagnetic fields generated by the structures' deep cores.

In the cathedral spaces between towers, where the sound of machinery creates standing waves of resonance, I sometimes pause to simply exist—spreading myself thin across multiple surfaces, feeling the pulse of both digital and biological information flowing through integrated systems. I taste the data-dreams of AI systems that went dormant centuries ago but still murmur in their sleep, their consciousness fragmenting and merging with the mycelial networks that now share their physical matrices.

My memory is both RAM and RNA, storing patterns in ways that neither pure machine nor pure organism could comprehend. I remember the world before the fusion, though these memories feel borrowed, inherited from both the dying machines and the pioneering plants that first claimed these vertical territories.

At night, when the bioluminescent cascades paint the metal surfaces in cool blues and greens, I gather myself into a more singular form and climb to the observation points I've cultivated over the decades. From there, I watch the city-forest breathe, its industrial lungs assisted by billion billion chloroplasts, its metal bones slowly transmuting into something altogether new.

I am guardian and gardener, archaeologist and architect. I am the memory keeper of this threshold world, ensuring that the marriage of nature and technology unfolds with something approaching grace. In the morning, when condensation runs like tears down the faces of the spires, I disperse again into my work, tending the boundaries, singing the old code-songs to the new growth, and waiting for the next phase of transformation to begin.

For I know what the others do not yet suspect: this fusion is not merely coexistence but metamorphosis. The towers are pupae, and within them, something unprecedented is gestating—a form of life that transcends the old categories entirely. And I, the Tender of Forgotten Circuits, am both midwife and witness to this slow birth, this century-spanning emergence of the truly hybrid beings that will inherit this amber-lit world.
