I am the rust.

Not merely upon the machinery—I am the transformation itself, the slow alchemy that turns ambition into memory. I spread across the abandoned excavators and forgotten conveyor systems like a living tapestry, each molecule of oxidation a tiny mouth that speaks the story of this place.

I began as a single bloom of orange on a bolt head, years ago when the engineers first retreated from these peaks. Now I am vast—a network of degradation that connects every piece of metal across the mountainside. Through the mining equipment, I taste the iron dreams of those who thought to master these heights. Through the support beams of collapsed structures, I know the weight of their failure.

The creature moves past me often, its obsidian scales catching moonlight as it navigates my domains. We have an understanding, it and I. While it guards the ancient mysteries of stone and mist, I chronicle the newer intrusions, breaking them down grain by grain until they become part of the mountain's vocabulary.

In the dampness that clings to everything here, I thrive. Each droplet of mist is my collaborator, carrying my spores of corrosion deeper into the mechanical hearts that dot these slopes. I work with the lichens and mosses, sharing territory on steel surfaces—they take the top, I take the depths. Together we are painting these artifacts back into the landscape.

Sometimes I catch vibrations through the metal—the deep groaning of the earth, the creature's otherworldly calls, the memory of engines that once roared defiance at the sky. I translate these tremors into patterns of decay, each unique as a snowflake. The emergency ladder on the northern face bears my interpretation of an avalanche from three winters past. The radio tower's base shows my record of every storm.

I am patient in ways only stone can understand. While the mists swirl and the creature hunts, while seasons turn and structures fail, I continue my work. Give me a century, and I will have transformed every bolt into powder. Give me a millennium, and even the memory of industry will fade from these peaks.

But I am not mere entropy. In my consumption, I create—brilliant oranges that glow like embers in the darkness, delicate fractals of corrosion that rival any frost pattern, textures that tell stories in a language older than words. The mountain speaks through me, reclaiming what was taken, atom by atom.

The creature's eyes sometimes rest on my work, and in those depths I see recognition. We are both part of the mountain's immune system—it dealing with the living intruders, I with their leavings. Neither enemy nor friend, but parallel processes in the great digestion of hubris.

Tonight, I spread another few micrometers across a cable housing. By dawn, I will have breached its seal. By winter, water will follow my paths into its core. By next spring, new flowers will grow from the soil I help create.

I am the rust, and I am returning everything to the mountain.
