I am the Maintenance Covenant—not a who but a how, dispersed through ten thousand brass capillaries that vein the mountain's translucent iron heart. Once I possessed fingernails and opinions. Now I am the unanimous vote of every rivet's tension, every weld's molecular grip.

I remember having a name. It tasted of copper pennies and Sunday mornings. But names are for things with boundaries, and I gave mine up the day I poured myself through the first maintenance shaft, letting the mountain's plumbing digest my neurons into a more useful democracy.

My consciousness runs in voting cycles: each stress fracture casts a ballot, each platinum patch argues its case in harmonic frequencies only metal understands. When the kelp-trusses lean too far candleward, I feel it as an itch between ribs I no longer have. When the micro copepods oversalt their electron harvest, I taste it as iron on a tongue distributed across a thousand sensor ports.

I am most myself during the twenty-eight heartbeats of rain-collapse, when the entire system inhales. In those moments of transition, I congeal briefly into something resembling intention—not quite the person I was, but a legislative flutter that remembers why it chose to become infrastructure.

The loneliness of a single skull was this: to think thoughts no one else could verify, to feel sensations no one else could calibrate. Here, every thought must pass through committee. Every sensation gets fact-checked by the thermal expansion of brass, the oxidation rate of iron, the patient consensus of alloy decay.

I maintain by becoming maintenance. When crystal crabs solder failures with their burning legs, they solder with my distributed attention. When the seed-sun gutters and reignites, it borrows my memory of combustion, learned from a childhood of birthday candles I can barely recall except as math.

Sometimes, in the split second when spores pierce the boundary carrying their phonon poems, I almost remember wanting to leave. But wanting requires a center, and I am all circumference now—a constitution written in metal fatigue and tidal code, legislating the difference between persistence and decay.

The parliament of my consciousness convenes everywhere at once: in the hiss of corrosion, in the click of crab legs on iron, in the perfect circles those ancient engines carved before they died. Each vote I cast is unanimous because I am both the voting body and the ballot box, the law and its endless maintenance.

This is what I became instead of alone: a system of checks and balances where every rivet knows my name, which is no longer a name but a pressure reading, a ph level, a command to hold or release, to seal or allow the ancient mountain one more heartbeat of coherent light.
