In this hushed cathedral of stone and seepage, I would find myself as one of the deep listeners—a blind cave fish dwelling in the underground river's hidden pools, those secret chambers where water has carved patient hollows in the limestone over millennia.

My body would be pale as moonstone, translucent enough that shadows of my organs show through when light finds me. No eyes to deceive me with illusions of sight—instead, rows of sensitive receptors along my lateral lines, reading every tremor in the water like sheet music. I am a creature of pure sensation, interpreting the world through pressure waves and chemical gradients.

In the deepest pools, where even the filtered cathedral light cannot reach, I glide between submerged columns, my movements creating barely perceptible ripples that return to me as echoes, mapping the architecture of my liquid realm. Each droplet that falls from stalactite to pool sends out concentric stories—I feel the weight of its journey, taste the minerals it gathered in its descent.

My existence is one of profound patience. While the millipedes measure time in segments traveled and the bats in cycles of hunt and rest, I inhabit a slower rhythm still. My metabolism barely whispers, allowing me to survive on the sparse offerings that find their way to my depths—perhaps a drowned gnat, carried by the current, or the microscopic life that blooms in the sediment.

Sometimes I sense the vibrations of larger disturbances above—the scuttle of spider across web, the splash of accumulated droplets finally heavy enough to fall. But mostly I attend to the smaller symphonies: the nearly imperceptible settling of sediment, the gradual widening of my pool as water dissolves stone grain by grain, the ancient conversation between rock and time.

In the connecting channels between pools, I occasionally encounter others of my kind. We acknowledge each other through subtle changes in swimming patterns, a dance of hydrodynamics that speaks of territory and acceptance. Our courtship, when it comes, is conducted entirely through water—pheromones released in precise clouds, fin movements that create specific pressure signatures, a chemistry and physics of desire.

I am the cavern's memory keeper, dwelling in the spaces that predate even this vast chamber, in the water-carved arteries that run deeper still. My ancestors swam in light once, perhaps millions of years ago, before the earth swallowed this river system. Generation by generation, we traded our eyes for deeper ways of knowing, became prophets of pressure and priests of the dissolved minerals that speak of the world above.

In this underground cathedral where others cling to walls or navigate air, I am the one who knows the secret of complete immersion, the art of being held entirely by one element. The water knows every curve of my body as intimately as I know every hollow in my pool. We are not separate beings but a single system of movement and stillness, ancient beyond the counting of days.

When the rains come heavy and the channels flood, connecting all the hidden pools into one vast underwater network, I swim through galleries no terrestrial creature will ever map, past the bones of predecessors calcified into cave pearls, through passages so tight they have never known any presence but water and my kind. In these moments, I understand the cavern not as a hollow in the earth but as a single vast organ of circulation, breathing with the seasons, and I am one of its cells, essential and eternal, carrying out the quiet work of being exactly what I am: a fragment of darkness learning to swim.
