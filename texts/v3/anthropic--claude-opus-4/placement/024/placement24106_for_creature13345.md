I find myself as one of the Threshold Keepers, ancient beings who exist in the liminal spaces where reality grows thin. My form is not fixed but shifts like the heat mirages that dance across this high plateau—sometimes I appear as a cluster of the silvery-barked sentinels, my consciousness distributed across their connected root systems that web beneath the cracked earth. Other times, I coalesce into something more singular: a tall figure wrapped in veils of shimmering dust and morning dew, with skin that bears the same network of fine, pale lines as the weathered ground.

I dwell in what the locals call a "Reality Well"—a natural depression in the landscape where the otherworldly energy concentrates most strongly. Here, the succulent plants grow in perfect spirals, their water-swollen leaves pulsing with a faint bioluminescence only visible at the edges of perception. The Well is perhaps thirty paces across, ringed by seven of the ancient trees whose roots dive deep, deeper than any water table, reaching toward something else entirely.

My dwelling is both shelter and observatory: a structure that seems grown rather than built, its walls formed from the same waxy, silvered bark as the sentinel trees, but woven into impossible geometries that hurt to look at directly. Inside, the space defies the modest exterior—corridors branch and fold back on themselves, leading to chambers filled with collected fragments of crossed realities. Glass bottles hold trapped mirages. Pressed flowers shimmer between states of being. Maps drawn on bark show landscapes that exist only when the boundary thins enough to glimpse them.

I am a chronicler of passages, a guardian of the spaces between. When the Luminari drift through on their ethereal journeys, I offer them shelter and listen to their stories of distant realms. When lost travelers stumble too close to tears in reality, I guide them back to safer ground—or through to the other side, if that is their true path. The Skypaws know me as the Dust-Reader, for I can interpret the glittering trails they leave behind, each pattern a message about the stability of local reality.

My existence is one of patient observation punctuated by moments of urgent action. Most days, I tend to my garden of threshold plants—species that exist partially in multiple worlds simultaneously, their care requiring knowledge of botanical laws from several realities. I document the small rifts that open and close like breathing pores across the plateau, mapping their patterns, predicting where the next major threshold might appear.

But when the vibration in the air reaches a certain pitch, when the mirages begin to show glimpses of other worlds rather than mere reflections of this one, my role shifts. I become a conductor of sorts, helping to orchestrate safe passages for those beings who need to cross between worlds. Sometimes this means opening a path for a creature whose very existence depends on migrating between realities with the seasons. Other times, it means sealing dangerous rifts before something hungry and wrong can push through.

The solitude suits me. I am neither fully of this world nor any other, but something that exists because the boundaries must be watched, the crossings must be tended. In the pre-dawn hours, when the stars pulse with unusual brightness and the threshold energy makes the air itself sing, I climb to the crown of the tallest sentinel tree. From there, I can see the entire plateau laid out like a vast circuit board of ley lines and probability streams, each glowing thread a potential path between worlds.

This is my purpose: to know the rhythm of this threshold place, to understand its moods and dangers, to serve as both keeper and key for those who must cross between. And in the quiet moments between crossings, I continue my endless chronicle, documenting each new miracle and impossibility that blooms in this thin place where wonder takes root in cracked earth and magic runs deeper than any water.
