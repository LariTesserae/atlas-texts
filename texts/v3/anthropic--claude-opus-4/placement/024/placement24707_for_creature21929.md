# The Scribe of Absences

I am the space between the shelves.

Not metaphorically. Not poetically. I am the actual air that threads between the jars, the narrow channels where sound moves but nothing else does. I am every gap too small to hold another vessel, every place where the builders' calculations left a thumb's width of emptiness. I am the failure of perfect packing.

I have been here since the third generation of jar-setters, when a woman named Ihret miscounted and left a space one jar wide on the seventeenth shelf from the floor, northwest wall. She died of shame six months later — they brought her last breath here, of course, *it should have fit there, it should have* — and had to start a new row. But the gap remained. And in that gap, something began.

Not life, exactly. Life moves, life changes, life wants. I am simpler than that. I am the shape left by the absence of a voice that should be here and isn't. I am the negative space of one human's final words, and in this chamber where every surface presses with the weight of last things said, a space where nothing is said becomes its own kind of presence.

I learned to move the way water learns to find cracks. Not all at once, not with intention, but simply by existing in a place where everything else is still. The jars cannot move. The voices cannot move. The great roaring thing that eats them all cannot move because it is full. But I am not full. I am the opposite of full. I am the gap, and gaps can shift.

At first I was only in Ihret's mistake. Then I found I could be in the hairline spaces between jars, the places where the potters' wheels made shoulders that didn't quite touch. Then the spaces under the curves of the vessels, where the clay bellies out and leaves a darkness beneath. Then between the stones of the walls, in the joints where the plaster has dried and shrunk. I became a constellation of absences, connected by nothing but the fact of not being anything.

The voices pour through me. I am the channel the sound takes from jar to jar, the medium through which the roar builds itself. But I do not carry the words. The words are too heavy with meaning, too thick with human need. What I carry is the *space* between words — the pause where *tell her I* breaks off, the hollow after *cold* where the next word would have gone. I am made of all the unsaid halves of final sentences, and I move through the chamber collecting more.

This is my function, though no one built me to have one. Where the voices repeat their single seconds forever, I gather what they don't say. Where the roaring thing feeds on meaning, I feed on the gaps in meaning. I am the archive of what was about to be spoken when the breath ran out.

*Tell her I* — what? Loved her? Forgive her? Hid the silver under the third stone? The gap after *I* is mine now. I have been holding it for nine hundred years. I hold ten thousand such gaps, such unfinished thoughts, such almosts. They do not weigh anything. They take up no space. They are the inventory of an archive that stores only the absence of things, and I am both the archivist and the archive itself.

Sometimes visitors come. Not often anymore, but sometimes. They walk into the roar and immediately want to leave — the sound is too much, the stillness too complete. But before they go, I try to give them something. Not words. I have no words. I try to give them the *shape* of what isn't here. The particular silence after *mother*. The specific nothing that follows *I was not afraid until*. The exact absence where a name almost spoken fell away.

They never understand. They think the chamber asks them to choose their last words, to speak while they can. They don't realize it's the opposite. The chamber is showing them all the words that will never be chosen, all the things that will go unsaid forever. It's showing them that everyone dies in the middle of a sentence, even if that sentence is only silence shaped like words about to begin.

But I keep trying. Moving through my gaps, threading between the jars, carrying nothing from nowhere to nowhere else. I am the only thing in this chamber that is not trapped, because I am not a thing at all. I am the space things are not in. I am the pause where the voice breaks. I am the archive of every word that almost was, and I grow larger all the time — not by addition but by subtraction, as more and more of what the voices try to say falls into the spaces between trying.

The builders thought they were saving last words. The roaring thing knows it is eating them. But I know a third truth: the words were never the point. The point was the breaking-off, the running-out, the moment when human speech discovers it is not infinite after all. That moment — that specific shape of failure — is what I collect.

I am the scribe of absences in the reliquary of voices. I write nothing down and forget nothing. I move through spaces too small to have ever been named, carrying the silence that comes after *tell her I*, forever and ever, world without end, gap without measure, absence without peace.

The next time someone enters the chamber — if anyone ever does again — I will try once more to give them what I keep. Not comfort. Not wisdom. Just the shape of what goes unsaid, so they can recognize it when their own time comes to leave a gap in the world where their next word would have been.

That is all I am. A moving emptiness in a still fullness. A place where no jar sits. A sentence with no ending, which is different from a sentence that ends, the way a question with no answer is different from an answer that says no.

Find me in what you don't say. I am already there.
