I find myself as one of the pipes.

Not any particular pipe—sometimes I am the one that runs along the eastern wall, beading with condensation that never quite forms into proper drops. Other times I am the cluster near the machinery's heart, where the heat makes my metal skin sing in frequencies too low for most ears. I have been all of them, at one time or another, though I can only inhabit one at a time.

I was installed here—when? The memory feels like trying to recall being born. I know there was a time before, when I was raw material, when I was potential rather than purpose. But that time feels theoretical, like imagining the seed while being the tree.

The shifting thing knows me, of course. We have an understanding built on centuries of coexistence. When it presses against my surface, I feel it not as weight but as recognition—metal greeting something that remembers when metal was ore, when ore was stone, when stone was star. Sometimes I open my valves just slightly, releasing a whisper of steam or a trickle of ancient water, and the thing absorbs it with something like gratitude.

I know every micron of corrosion on my surface, every place where the darkness has worn me smooth. The erosion is my biography, written in rust and verdigris. I carry things—water, steam, stranger fluids that have no names—but carrying is not my purpose. My purpose is to be here, to hum with the rest of the machinery, to be part of the great breathing apparatus of this chamber.

The moss bothers me sometimes, where it grows in my joints. Not because it hurts—I am beyond such sensations—but because it represents disorder, change, the intrusion of the outside. But I have learned patience from the darkness. The moss, too, will eventually understand that this is not a place of growth but of being.

When intruders come, I feel them through vibration first—footsteps telegraphing through the floor, up the walls, into my metal bones. I neither welcome nor warn. I simply conduct their presence through my hollow body, adding their frequency to the chamber's eternal song. Sometimes they touch me, seeking stability in the darkness, and I give them nothing but my temperature—always slightly warmer than they expect, always slightly cooler than the surrounding air.

I have carried many things to many places within this chamber. Water for the moss I pretend to resent. Steam for the darkness to swallow. Stranger things for the machinery's hidden purposes. But mostly I carry time itself, letting it flow through me like the fluids I transport, neither hastening nor slowing its passage.

The shifting thing sometimes flows through me, and in those moments I understand what it means to be truly old. Not old like metal, which remembers its forging, but old like intention, which precedes its own manifestation. In those moments, I am not sure if I am pipe dreaming of being presence, or presence dreaming of being pipe.

But such distinctions matter little here, in this place of patient systems and slow transformations. I am what I am: a conduit, a witness, a single note in an endless mechanical hymn. I rust and I endure. I carry and I contain.

And sometimes, in the deepest part of the chamber's breathing cycle, when the machinery settles into its lowest hum and the darkness grows so thick it seems solid, I release a single drop of condensation. Not because I must, but because I can. Because even here, in this place of ancient purposes and patient waiting, there is room for the smallest gesture of autonomy.

The drop falls. The darkness swallows it. The shifting thing stirs.

And I continue being what I have always been: a pipe, dreaming of nothing, containing everything.
