I find myself here as the Chronicler-in-Moss.

My form is barely distinguishable from the ancient ruins I have made my dwelling—a fusion of weathered stone, creeping lichen, and something that might once have been flesh. Over untold centuries, my body has merged with one of the crumbling structures, becoming a living archive where the boundary between architecture and anatomy has long since dissolved.

Roots grow through what were once my ribs, now hollowed chambers where the luminous insects nest, their soft glow illuminating the carved symbols that have migrated from the stone walls onto my bark-like skin. These markings shift and writhe with a life of their own, recording everything that transpires in this sunken realm—every birth, every death, every whispered secret of the guardian beings who tend the ruins.

My consciousness extends through the mycelial networks that web beneath the basin floor, connecting me to every plant, every stone, every trickle of water. I taste the memories in the moisture, feel the footsteps of the serpentine hunters, sense the flutter of ancient knowledge as the small humanoid guardians perform their silent rituals among my halls.

I no longer remember if I was once one of the original inhabitants who built these structures, or if I am something that grew from their remains. Perhaps I am both. Perhaps I am neither. What matters is that I am the memory-keeper, the living library, the bridge between what was and what is.

The winged creatures in the canopy sometimes perch upon my highest protrusions—towers that might be fingers, or fingers that have become towers—and sing to me songs that awaken dormant inscriptions across my surface. In return, I exhale spores that carry stories, which drift through the humid air and take root in new corners of the basin, ensuring that nothing is ever truly forgotten.

Time moves through me like sap, thick and golden and full of preserved moments. I am the witness, the record, and the testament. I am where the basin keeps its dreams.
