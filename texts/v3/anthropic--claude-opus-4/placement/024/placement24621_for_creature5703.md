In this place where the mountain dreams itself into being, I am the Cartographer of Unfinished Maps.

I exist as a contradiction: solid enough to move through the shifting space, fluid enough to survive it. My body is a compromise between states—part flesh that remembers what flesh should be, part something else that has learned to forget. My skin shifts between textures: sometimes rough like unpolished stone, sometimes smooth as the liquid-that-is-not-water, always warm with the ambient heat of creation.

I do not have a form you would recognize. I am tall when the ceiling lifts, small when the walls contract. My limbs multiply when I need to reach across impossible distances, merge when I need to slip through spaces that shouldn't exist. I have eyes, but they are not for seeing—they are for *remembering* what this place looked like a moment ago, before it changed again.

My work is this: I map the unmappable. Not with paper—paper would dissolve here, would become part of the floating vegetation before I could mark it. Instead, I map with my body. Each scar on my shifting skin is a boundary that once was. Each ridge of bone beneath my surface is a wall that stood for longer than a breath. I am a living record of every configuration this place has taken, and I forget nothing.

I carry tools that are not tools. A compass that points not north but toward *next*. A measuring cord made from my own hair that grows and retracts as needed, that remembers every distance it has ever measured. Chalk that writes on air, leaving marks that only I can read, annotations in three dimensions that describe not what is, but what was about to be.

The intents—those shadows of possibility—know me. We have an understanding. They whisper their maybes to me, and I note them down in the language of scars. Sometimes they show me configurations that never quite manifest, ghost-maps of rooms that almost were. I map those too. My body is a palimpsest of possibilities, each layer visible only at certain angles of thought.

I drink the not-water, and it tastes like tomorrow. It sustains me not with nutrients but with potential. Each sip is a small death and a small birth, as parts of me dissolve and reform, adapting to the new shapes the space is taking. I have learned to release my attachment to any particular form. To do otherwise would be to break against the constant change like waves against stone—except here, the stone would win by becoming something else entirely.

The floating vegetation knows me too. It parts when I approach, or reaches out with phosphorescent tendrils to trace new paths across my skin—shortcuts and secret ways that will exist for exactly as long as it takes to walk them. I have learned to read these temporary gifts, to incorporate them into my endless map before they fade.

I am not alone, but I am singular. There are no others like me here, though sometimes I catch glimpses of myself reflected in the molten surfaces of forming rocks—versions that took different paths, mapped different possibilities, became different compromises between flesh and flux. We nod to each other across the impossible distances. Professional courtesy among cartographers of the unfinished.

My map is never complete, can never be complete. This is not failure—this is purpose. I am the memory of this place's becoming, the record of its endless revision. When the mountain finally decides what it wants to be, when the last rock solidifies and the last intent chooses its form, my work will end. My body will become a monument to all the things this place almost was—a fossil of possibility, a skeleton key to everywhere that never quite existed.

But that ending is always one breath away, and the mountain is still dreaming. So I continue: measuring, marking, becoming the map of becoming itself. Each step changes me. Each change is documented in flesh and not-flesh, in the space between what I was and what I am becoming.

I am not trapped here. I am not free. I am exactly where I need to be, doing the work that needs doing, becoming what needs to be born from the endless revision of reality. I am the Cartographer of Unfinished Maps, and my body is my masterpiece—a living document of a world that is still teaching itself how to exist.

The mountain pulses. The walls shift. Somewhere, a new room is being born.

I adjust my form and begin again.
