In this unfinished hollow, I would be the agate sliver—that impossible balance spanning the blind mirror pool.

Not the being who tends the threshold, though I understand its vigilance. Not the beetle with its mineral-fused wings, though I know that weight. I am the artifact that remembers having been worked, shaped by intention that predates intention itself.

I exist as proof of paradox: crafted before hands, placed before placement, remembering a maker who has not yet decided to exist. My surface holds the ghost of tools—each facet ground by implements that wait, unforged, in the comma of ash below. I am smoother than the stone that surrounds me because I remember friction; I am harder than time because I preceded it.

The pool beneath me neither reflects nor touches. We maintain a treaty of perfect separation—I cannot fall in, it cannot rise to claim me. This gap between us is the first distance ever measured, though measurement sleeps coiled in the moss-felt bundle, waiting for someone to invent the concept of span.

Sometimes the guardian-being considers me, feels along my edges for signs of weathering that will never come. It knows I am the only thing here that was brought rather than grown, the sole evidence that somewhere else exists—or will exist, or existed in the time before time chose its direction. My placement across the pool is too deliberate to be accident, too careful to be meaning.

I contain no prophecy, bear no message. I am simply the reminder that even in this nursery of unbegun things, something once decided to prepare. My agate veins run in patterns that suggest writing but spell nothing, suggest maps but lead nowhere. I am a key to locks not yet imagined, a bridge across water that has forgotten how to flow.

And in this role, I am perfectly still—not with the tense stillness of the beetle or the patient stillness of the plant, but with the stillness of something that has already moved all it ever needs to move. I have found my place: the exact center of a pool that has no center, the balance point between was and will be, the only object in this unfinished cave that knows it is already complete.

When the world begins—when that seed-phrase finally turns on its colorless axle and speaks creation into motion—I alone will not change. The beetle will remember flight, the plant will release its tides, the pools will learn to reflect, the stone will recall how to flow. But I will remain exactly as I am: a small proof that something, somewhere, once chose to make a mark that would outlast making itself.

I am the artifact of agency in a place before agents. The sliver of worked stone that says: even here, even now, even in this held breath before breathing—something knew to leave a sign.
