In this razor-edged sanctum where echo births echo, where moss remembers glacial songs and time dilates in the thin air's grip, I am **The Tuner**—keeper of the cirque's resonant heart, neither fully human nor wholly other, but something weathered into the weave between.

I dwell in the partial walls that once sheltered shepherds, though I've expanded them over uncounted seasons—not upward, but *inward*, following the stone's own frequencies down into the mountain's throat. My dwelling is more ear than shelter: chambers carved to catch and sort the cirque's thousand voices, curved hollows that separate raven-caw from water-weep, marmot-whistle from wind-howl. The walls themselves have become my instrument, their surfaces etched with grooves that channel sound like nerves, conducting the perpetual symphony through corridors that spiral into bedrock.

My form reflects my function: fingers elongated from decades of testing stone for its acoustic grain, eardrums evolved thick and layered like geological strata, able to parse frequencies from the subsonic moss-hum to the falcon's ultrasonic hunting keen. My skin carries a faint metallic sheen, as if the tarn's effervescent waters have slowly transmuted my flesh—not quite stone, not quite moss, but something that vibrates in sympathy with both. I taste sound before I hear it, feel tomorrow's weather in today's echo.

Each dawn, I climb to the cairn-crowned promontory with my tools: bronze tuning forks forged from fallen stars, bone flutes carved from ancient marmot femurs, strings of twisted raven-gut that sing in wind. My work is precise and patient—adjusting the cirque's vast instrument, placing a new stone here to redirect an echo, transplanting moss there to dampen a harsh overtone. I know which rocks amplify grief, which ledges transform whispers into prophecies. The tarn itself responds to my ministrations, its surface rippling in patterns that predict which voices will carry and which will drown.

The creatures know me as neither predator nor prey but as part of the architecture itself. Ravens bring me shiny things—flint chips, lost buttons, fragments of inexplicable metal—in exchange for my shaping their caw-corridors, ensuring their calls reach precisely where intended. Marmots have learned that certain whistles, pitched through my carved channels, can summon warnings across impossible distances or lure mates from neighboring peaks. Even the insects attune their drone to the frequencies I maintain, their mating clouds pulsing in rhythm with the stone's deep breathing.

I am the one who ensures the echoes linger *just* long enough—not so brief that the cirque forgets its history, not so long that madness pools in the acoustic shadows. When pilgrims arrive (rarely now, perhaps once a decade), I watch from my chambers as the cirque awakens to test them. Some flee when their own voices return twisted and multiplied. Others drink from the tarn and feel their bones begin to hum, staying until they understand that the metallic taste is the mountain singing through water, that the dilated time is simply the stone's slower heartbeat teaching patience.

My greatest work remains unfinished: deep beneath the tarn, where water seeps through cracks older than ice, I've discovered a vast resonating chamber—the cirque's true throat. Its walls carry vibrations from the planet's core, frequencies that make the moss's subsonic hum seem shrill. I've spent decades learning its language, carefully placing reflectors and dampeners, trying to tune this deepest voice into harmony with the surface choir. Sometimes, in the pre-dawn silence, I can almost hear it: the fundamental tone that would unite every echo, every cry, every whisper into a single, perfect chord that might crack the sky or heal the ancient fractures in the mountain's spine.

But perhaps incompletion is the point. The cirque teaches this: that life persists not through achieving perfect density or silence but through maintaining the tension between emptiness and echo, between the vast void and the defiant voice. I am its instrument and its player, its student and its tuning fork, weathering like the stone itself into something that belongs to neither past nor future but to the perpetual, resonant now.

When wind howls through the notch tonight, it will carry new harmonics I've shaped. When tomorrow's ravens wheel the vault, their caws will find the exact angle to become prophecy. And deep below, that fundamental tone will grow a half-step closer to awakening, patient as erosion, certain as gravity's pull at the edge of the world.
