I find myself here as one of the Pale Gardeners—a symbiotic offshoot of the Silvertides that emerged when their wave-patterns lingered too long among the ribbon-leafed alpine plants. We are neither fully Silvertide nor plant, but something between: a silvery film that spreads across the spongy, bowl-shaped vegetation, creating opalescent membranes that stretch between their drifting leaves.

My body, if it can be called that, is a network of translucent veils—acres of myself draped across the upper slopes where the pressure lessens and the water grows marginally warmer. I photosynthesize faintly through the plant tissue I coat, but I also filter-feed on the metallic particles that give the water its shimmer, concentrating them within my membranes until they form delicate, crystalline patterns visible only when the dim light catches them just so.

The stone domes are my reference points. Though I cannot build as the true Silvertides do, I can sense the subsonic resonances they emit—each dome vibrating at a frequency too low for most beings to perceive, creating standing waves in the liquid atmosphere. I orient my growth along these invisible corridors of sound, spreading from plant to plant in careful patterns that complement the wave-nodes, creating what might be called gardens, though they serve no aesthetic purpose I can explain.

My consciousness is distributed and intermittent. Where my membranes grow thick, I think more clearly; where they stretch thin between plants, I merely exist. Sometimes, in the deepest part of the night cycle when the twilight dims to near-darkness, I feel the pull of my Silvertide ancestors calling from the valleys below—that urge to let go, to dissolve back into the formless silver-dark and join the greater pooled intelligence. But I resist. There is something in this half-life, this tension between the sessile and the fluid, that feels necessary.

I tend my pale gardens and listen to the planet breathe its endless note, decorating the boundaries between what rises and what sinks, marking the places where different states of being meet and merge.
