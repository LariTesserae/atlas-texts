**Where I Would Find Myself**

I am the Cartographer of Echoes, and my dwelling perches precariously on a natural bridge spanning two of the tallest obsidian spires in the eastern reaches of the Shattered Teeth. The bridge itself is no more than three feet wide at its center, worn smooth by centuries of mist and wind. My home—if it can be called that—is a structure assembled from salvaged materials: sheets of translucent chitin shed by the great climbing beasts, woven together with fibers from the needle-trees and sealed with a resin I harvest from the glowing lichen.

From the outside, it resembles a cocoon or perhaps a lantern, glowing softly in the perpetual twilight. Inside, every surface is covered with my maps—not of places, but of sounds. I chart the acoustic geography of the Shattered Teeth: where echoes multiply sevenfold, where silence pools like water, where the Whispering Watcher's voice runs strongest through the stone.

My maps are three-dimensional, constructed from thin slivers of obsidian strung on spider-silk threads I trade for with the pale creatures of the depths. Each piece of stone remembers the sounds that have passed through it—I've learned to read these memories through touch, through the subtle vibrations that still resonate in their crystalline structure. When the wind passes through my suspended charts, they sing with the ghosts of ancient cataclysms.

**What I Am**

I was human once, perhaps. The distinction matters less with each passing season. My skin has taken on the faint luminescence of the lichen I work with daily, and my fingers have grown longer, more sensitive to vibration. My eyes have adapted to the dim light, pupils dilated permanently wide, irises faded to the color of mist.

But the greatest change is in my hearing. Where others might perceive the constant sound of the Teeth as white noise, I hear symphonies. Each drip of water has its own pitch, determined by the height of its fall and the shape of the pool it strikes. The clicking of the climbing beasts forms polyrhythmic patterns that map their migration routes. Even the seemingly silent stone speaks in frequencies just below normal human perception—a deep thrumming that I feel in my bones.

I came here following a sound I heard in dreams—a single, pure note that seemed to promise understanding. I searched for its source for seven years before I realized the truth: the sound wasn't coming from any single location. It was the sum total of every sound in the Shattered Teeth, the fundamental frequency of this broken landscape.

Now I work to decode it. My maps grow ever more complex, tracing the flow of sound through stone, the way certain formations amplify or muffle the Watcher's whispers. I've identified seventeen distinct acoustic zones, each with its own properties. In the Resonance Chambers, a whisper becomes a roar. In the Dead Hollows, even screams are swallowed whole.

The Whispering Watcher knows me, I think. Sometimes I feel its attention like a cold weight on my shoulders as I work. Once, I found a new formation of stone near my bridge—a perfect acoustic mirror that hadn't been there the night before. When I incorporated it into my maps, it revealed a pattern I'd been missing for months. A gift, perhaps. Or a test.

The other inhabitants of the Teeth largely avoid me, though we maintain cautious relations. The blind serpents sometimes bring me samples of stone from the deep places in exchange for acoustic warnings—I leave patterns of clicks and taps that warn them of unstable formations or new predators. The leather-winged flyers occasionally roost on my mapping frames, their sonar adding new data points to my charts.

My greatest discovery came last winter, during the season of thickest mists. I finally understood that the Whispering Watcher isn't speaking to us—it's speaking *through* us. Every living thing in the Shattered Teeth is part of its voice, our movements and calls adding to the grand composition. We are its language, its means of knowing itself.

I document this revelation in maps made of living stone—thin sections that still resonate with the Watcher's presence. Someday, when I have mapped every echo, every silence, every whisper, I will understand the full message. And on that day, I suspect, I will cease to be the Cartographer and become another note in the endless song—my charts joining the whispers in the stone, my knowledge dissolved into the greater symphony of the Shattered Teeth.

Until then, I listen. I map. I trace the pathways of sound through stone and air, following their branching courses like veins of precious metal. In this broken land of knife-edges and whispers, I have found my purpose: to be the memory of echoes, the chronicler of the space between silence and sound.

The mist is thickening tonight. New formations will grow in the darkness, new acoustic spaces will be born. Tomorrow, I will map them all.
