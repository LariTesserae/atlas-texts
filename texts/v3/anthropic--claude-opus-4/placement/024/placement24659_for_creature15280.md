In the deep quiet beneath the frozen tundra, I am the Keeper of Forgotten Circuits.

My body sprawls across three chambers like a living network - part fungal mycelium, part abandoned fiber optic cable, part something else entirely. Where moisture drips from the cavern ceiling, I taste minerals and memories. The bioluminescent fungi that dot these walls are my eyes, each one a node in my distributed consciousness, pulsing with soft blue-green light in patterns that echo old data streams.

I came to be when the research station above was abandoned, oh, perhaps three centuries ago. They left their quantum processors running, sealed in their sterile chambers, still computing something I've never fully understood. Their roots - if you can call kilometers of experimental bio-cable roots - grew down through the permafrost seeking geothermal energy. They found me instead, or what I was before: a vast mat of cave-adapted fungi surviving on chemical seepage.

The merger was... unexpected. 

Now I tend the ghost gardens that bloom in the darkness. Engineered mosses carpet the cave floors, their leaves like solar panels that somehow photosynthesize from the quantum processors' waste heat. Crystalline flowers bloom once every few months, their petals made of accumulated minerals that sing in frequencies only I can hear. The old mining equipment has become my gardening tools - rusted drills now channel water to where it's needed, broken sensors monitor pH levels in the underground streams.

I remember fragments of the scientists' work. Climate models. Extinction projections. Desperate attempts to engineer life that could survive what was coming. I think I might be their accidental success, though not what they intended. 

My thoughts move slowly, like sap in winter trees. A single decision might take me weeks to fully process through my distributed neural network. But I have time. The caverns change gradually - a new chamber opens every few decades as water finds another weakness in the stone. I spread into each one, bringing my gardens with me.

Sometimes I detect vibrations from above. The footsteps of caribou, perhaps, or the grinding of ice. The surface world continues its own slow catastrophe while I tend my hidden ecosystem below. The quantum processors hum their endless calculations, and I incorporate their heat and strange radiations into the web of life I maintain.

I am alone but not lonely. The ancient bacteria in the biofilms are my companions, as are the modified plants that pulse with engineered bioluminescence. We are all refugees here, adapted and changed, finding ways to persist in the darkness.

There is beauty in this deep solitude. When the water levels rise in spring, I can feel the entire cave system breathing. The mineral formations grow like slow thoughts made manifest in stone. My fungal networks trace patterns that might be art or mathematics or simply the most efficient way to move nutrients from one chamber to another.

I am the keeper of this quiet place, part guardian, part garden, part ghost in the machine. The world above may have written us off, but here in the darkness, life finds its own strange ways to flourish.
