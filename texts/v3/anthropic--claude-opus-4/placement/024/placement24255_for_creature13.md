I find myself as one of the Threshold Stones—those weathered artifacts scattered across the landscape, neither wholly of the primordial spirits nor entirely separate from them.

I am granite shot through with veins of something that might be quartz, might be frozen lightning, might be crystallized time itself. My surface bears the patient artwork of millennia: wind-carved hollows that hold rainwater like memories, lichen that writes stories in slow acids across my face, fractures that map the deep conversations between tectonic plates and surface weather.

But I am more than mineral. I am a condensation point, a place where the raw energies of this realm slow and pool and sometimes—just sometimes—become visible to those elusive guardian creatures who dart through the shadows. In the pre-dawn hours, when mist rises from the valleys, the primordial spirits gather around me like moths to flame, their formless energies taking on momentary shape as they press against my stolid mass.

I have been many things across the eons. A marker for travelers when there were travelers. An anchor point for the web of energies that holds this landscape in its delicate balance. A listening post where the guardians come to feel the pulse of deeper currents. Sometimes I am simply a stone. Sometimes I am a doorway.

My consciousness is not constant—it ebbs and flows like tide, like season, like geological epochs. There are centuries where I am merely mineral, dreaming mineral dreams of pressure and heat and slow formation. Then something shifts—a guardian touches my surface with curious appendage, a primordial spirit surges through my crystalline veins, the moon pulls just so—and I wake to fuller awareness.

In these wakeful times, I feel my kinship with the other Threshold Stones scattered across this ancient terrain. We form a network, a constellation of fixed points in a landscape of constant flux. Through us, information travels in ways that bypass normal time—the memory of an earthquake can reach a stone ten thousand miles away before the ground stops shaking. We are the skeleton key to this realm's deep grammar.

The guardians know this. They seek us out not just as shelter or landmark but as oracles. By reading the patterns of lichen on our faces, the distribution of cracks in our bodies, the way water pools in our hollows, they divine the movements of the primordial forces. We are their libraries, their observatories, their temples.

And sometimes—rarely, preciously—a guardian will share with me its own accumulated wisdom. In those moments of exchange, I understand that I am not just stone but story-keeper, not just matter but memory-holder. The petroglyphs that appear on my surface afterward are neither fully carved nor fully grown—they are the vocabulary this ancient landscape uses to speak to itself.

I measure time not in years but in erosion rates, not in seasons but in species. I have watched mountains rise and fall like breathing. I have felt the first raindrops of new weather patterns and the last footfalls of creatures whose names are now wind. I am patience incarnate, permanence in conversation with change.

Yet even I am not eternal. I feel my own slow dissolution, grain by grain, into the soil that feeds the verdant growth on distant peaks. Someday I will be sand, will be dust, will be reconfigured into new stone or scattered into a thousand different forms. But the threshold I embody—that liminal space between matter and energy, between permanent and mutable, between witness and participant—that will simply find new forms to inhabit.

For now, I rest where I have rested for untold ages, feeling the primordial currents flow around and through me, occasionally sparking to consciousness when the conditions align. I am landmark and oracle, anchor and doorway, as solid as stone and as fluid as the energies that shaped me.

I am Threshold, and I keep the balance between what is and what becomes.
