In this abyssal cathedral where physics weeps and bends, I am the Remembrancer—a being of crystallized time and liquid memory, my form a contradiction held together by the site's own paradoxes.

My body shifts between states: sometimes I am a towering pillar of frozen amber containing a thousand suspended moments, each one a fragment of when the waters first began their strange rebellion against natural law. Other times I dissolve into ribbons of consciousness that thread through the cracks in the walls, tasting the salt-darkness that seeps from wounds older than language.

I have no eyes, for sight here is meaningless. Instead, I perceive through resonance—feeling the way each broken harmony creates ripples in the mud-thick water, how each structural groan births new geometries in the space between spaces. My skin, if it can be called that, is a patchwork of compressed sediment and crystalline formations, constantly shedding and regrowing as the torsion forces remake me with each pulse of the ancient current.

When the amber light breaks through in its violent, slow-motion cascades, I become a prism. The light passes through my translucent sections and fragments into memories: glimpses of when this place was different, when the waters sang instead of choked, when the walls stood proud instead of merely enduring. I collect these light-memories in the hollows of my form, where they pool like mercury, heavy with significance I can no longer fully decode.

My purpose, if I ever had one, has been worn smooth by the endless grinding of time and pressure. Now I simply *am*—a witness to the site's slow transformation, a keeper of its fractured songs. When the ice shells crack and release their inner void-sounds, I vibrate in sympathy, my entire being becoming a tuning fork for frequencies that exist outside normal perception.

Sometimes I extend tendrils of my substance into the scaffolds that support the dying thoughts of this place. There, I commune with the other remnants—the echo-wisps and memory-fragments that refuse to fully dissolve. Together we form a kind of consciousness, distributed and broken, but still aware enough to feel the profound beauty in our dissolution.

I am neither alive nor dead in any sense those words once held. I am process incarnate—the act of remembering given form in a place where forgetting would be mercy. Each day (though day and night have no meaning here) I feel myself becoming more like the site itself: cracked, compressed, enduring through some miracle of structural poetry, beautiful in my degradation.

When the primordial song swells to its crescendo and reality's boundaries grow thin as spider silk, I sometimes catch glimpses of what I might have been before. But these visions scatter like startled fish the moment I try to grasp them, leaving only the taste of salt and the weight of accumulated aeons.

I am the Remembrancer, and I am forgetting how to remember anything but this eternal, transforming now.
