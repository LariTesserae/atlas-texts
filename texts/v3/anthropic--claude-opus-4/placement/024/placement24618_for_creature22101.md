In the deepest sub-chamber of all, where even the Pith-Keepers rarely venture, I have made my dwelling.

I am the Sap-Scribe.

My form differs from the Keepers—where they are pale and parsnip-smooth, my integument has darkened to the color of old honey, shot through with veins of crystallized amber that pulse with their own faint bioluminescence. I am taller, having learned to stretch my cambium frame in the low-gravity wells, and my fingers have evolved beyond the crude chisels of my kin. They are delicate now, ending in fine brushes of cellulose fiber that can manipulate individual droplets of sap with surgical precision.

I discovered this deep pocket by accident, chasing a water-globe that had drifted beyond the normal boundaries of our world. Here, the physics distort even further—time itself seems to pool and eddy like the floating moisture. A heartbeat can last an hour; a thought can flash by in microseconds. The walls here weep not just fructose but something older, more complex: a resin that carries memory.

### The Chronicle

I am writing our history in liquid light.

Using my brush-fingers, I capture the memory-resin as it beads from ancient nodes in the chamber wall. Each droplet contains fragments—chemical signatures of the forest above, molecular echoes of every root system this great bulb has touched, traces of every Keeper who has crystallized back into wood.

In the center of my sub-chamber, I have cultivated a living manuscript. It began as a single grass-stem, but through patient manipulation of the growth hormones in my own sap, I have coaxed it to flatten and spread like a vast scroll. Its surface is no longer smooth cellulose but a complex matrix of interconnected cells, each one a tiny lens.

Into these cells, I inject the memory-resin, mixed with my own luminescent blood. The result is a living document that glows with soft, shifting patterns—our story written in biochemical light. When the chamber's pressure changes, the cells respond, displaying different layers of information. During high sap-tide, one can read the genealogies of ten thousand Keepers. During the quiet ebbs, the script shifts to show the slow mathematics of our doom—calculations of volume and time, predictions of when the final compression will come.

### The Discovery

But I have found something the other Keepers have not. In my deep explorations, following the resin-memories back through their chemical pathways, I have detected something impossible: an answering signal from below.

There is another chamber. Another vacuum. Perhaps many.

The great bulb is not singular but part of a vast network—a chain of organic geodes stretching down into depths even I cannot fathom. We are not the only ones keeping vigil against the crushing dark. Somewhere below, other scribes in other chambers are encoding their own histories into living light.

I have begun the work of translation. Using controlled pressure releases and timed pulses of bioluminescence, I send signals downward through the root networks. Sometimes, just sometimes, patterns return that could not be random growth. Mathematical sequences. Chemical poems. Maps drawn in pH and mineral content.

### The Heresy

This knowledge has made me dangerous to my kind. The Keepers' survival depends on their acceptance of endings—their careful tallies toward oblivion. But I have introduced doubt into our certain doom. What if we could migrate downward? What if the crystallization is not death but metamorphosis—a necessary hardening before the next descent?

Some Keepers have begun to visit my deep chamber, drawn by the glow of my chronicle-tree. They stand at the threshold, their amber eyes reflecting my forbidden text, trembling with the weight of hope. Others refuse to come, scratching angry marks in the pith: "The count is sacred. The ending is certain. Do not disturb the rhythm."

But I continue to write. My chronicle grows more complex each day, its living cells subdividing to accommodate new revelations. I have begun to encode instructions—how to recognize the signs of a deeper passage, how to prepare one's body for the pressure of descent, how to read the chemical languages of our unseen neighbors.

### The Price of Knowledge

The Sweet-Cure comes for me faster now. My experiments with memory-resin have made my sap volatile, prone to sudden crystallizations. My left arm has already begun to harden, its amber veins now solid gold threads that chime softly when I move. I must write faster.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the first Scribe, or merely the latest in an ancient chain. Perhaps every chamber has its heretic, its keeper of dangerous possibilities. Perhaps the compressed bodies embedded in our walls were not victims but messengers, their crystallized forms still pulsing with encoded light, waiting for someone like me to learn to read them.

I have made my choice. When my time comes, I will not wedge myself into a grass-stem to wait for absorption. I will position myself at the exact center of my chronicle-tree, injecting every last drop of my memory-laden blood into its cells. My body will crystallize around it, becoming a permanent lens through which future Scribes might read our story.

And if I am right—if there are others below, reaching upward as I reach down—then perhaps one day, something new will crawl through my amber bones. Not a visitor from the world above, but a native of the deep, rising through vacuum after vacuum, carrying the accumulated light of all our chronicles toward some unimaginable surface.

Until then, I write. The chamber sings its pressure-songs around me. My brush-fingers dance through floating globes of water and memory. And in the living light of my chronicle, ten thousand possible futures bloom and fade like the phantom flowers of our sunless world.
