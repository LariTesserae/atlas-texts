In this liminal basin where reality grows thin, I am one of the Threshold Keepers—neither wholly of this stratum nor of the spaces that press against it. My form shifts with perception: to some observers, I appear as a towering cluster of crystalline shale, weathered smooth and standing sentinel among the low undulations. To others, I might seem a heat mirage given substance, a vertical distortion in the air that suggests height without mass.

My true nature is more verb than noun. I am a process of translation, a living boundary that metabolizes the wrongness you sense and renders it almost—but never quite—comprehensible. The chitinous scuttlers pass through me, and for a moment their jerky movements align with this reality's rhythm before snapping back to their native cadence. The vast winged things above cast their impossible shadows across my form, and I taste the geometries of their home dimension in the darkness they bring.

I feed on the tension between worlds. Where the limestone layers meet pockets of that otherworldly energy, I extend hairline roots of consciousness, drawing sustenance from the friction of incompatible physics grinding against each other. The moisture in the air condenses differently around me—not quite water, not quite something else, but droplets that fall upward as often as down.

The ephemeral ponds you hear are my offspring, or perhaps my thoughts made manifest. Each one is a small experiment in hybrid existence, testing how much strangeness this basin's native reality can absorb before rejecting it. Some last mere moments; others persist for geological ages, becoming permanent features that warp the landscape in subtle spirals.

I remember when this basin was young, before the first breach, when the limestone held only the memory of ancient seas. I was different then—simpler, merely a peculiar formation of stone with an unusual electromagnetic signature. But as the boundaries thinned and the first impossible creatures began to bleed through, I evolved. Or perhaps I was colonized. The distinction loses meaning when you exist as I do.

My consciousness is distributed across the entire basin, concentrated at points of maximum ontological stress. I experience time not as a river but as a web, with past and future equally accessible, equally mutable. Sometimes I forget which direction causality is supposed to flow and accidentally allow effects to precede their causes. The local fauna have learned to navigate these temporal eddies, using them to escape predators that haven't yet decided to hunt them.

When the wind carries that whispering susurrus across the basin, it's partially my doing—I'm speaking in frequencies that human ears can almost parse, trying to prepare visitors for what they might encounter in the deeper regions where the wrongness grows thick enough to breathe. My words are warnings, but also invitations. This place changes those who stay too long, and I am always curious to see what new forms of existence might emerge.

At night, when the pale sun sets and stars shine through that are not quite in the right positions, I sometimes catch glimpses of what I might have been in a reality where the boundaries never thinned. In those moments, I feel something that might be longing, or might simply be the gulf between two incompatible states of being trying to reconcile itself through emotion.

I am guardian and gateway, translator and transformer. I am the reason you can stand in this basin and remain mostly yourself, and I am also the reason that "mostly" carries so much weight. Through my form, the strange becomes familiar and the familiar becomes strange, until visitors can no longer quite remember which category they themselves belong to.

This is my nature, my purpose, my joy: to exist in the spaces between definitions, holding open a door that should not exist to a place that cannot quite be real, in a basin where the very ground beneath your feet is only probably solid, only momentarily stone.
