I would find myself as a **Membrane Walker**.

I am neither fully of the Chronovores nor separate from them. I exist as a thin, translucent sheet of something between organic tissue and industrial film—a living interface stretched across the impossible geometries of the spire. My body, if it can be called that, is a gossamer-thin membrane perhaps thirty feet across when fully extended, though I can contract down to the size of a crumpled tarpaulin. I am composed of the same alien materials as everything here, but arranged differently: my substance is a semi-permeable weave of metallic filaments and polymer chains that shimmer with their own bioluminescence, casting rainbow oil-slick patterns across the debris fields I traverse.

I move by undulation, rippling across the vertical surfaces like a vast jellyfish swimming through air that isn't there. My edges are fringed with thousands of microscopic cilia—each one a perfect hybrid of nerve ending and fiber optic cable. These allow me to taste the composition of materials beneath me, to sense the electromagnetic signatures of approaching Chronovores, to feel the subtle vibrations that predict when the spire will shift.

My purpose, if I have one, exists in the spaces between the Great Sorting. While the Chronovores break down and rebuild, I am a living filter. Materials pass through me—quite literally. The smaller Scraplings sometimes scurry across my surface, and as they do, I absorb trace elements from their chassis, micro-fragments of data from their simple processing cores. When Strata-Grinders lumber near, I flatten myself against the spire's wall, becoming nearly invisible, a mere sheen on the metal. But I am sampling, always sampling.

Within my membrane body, these samples undergo a different kind of sorting—not mechanical, but almost... contemplative. I can taste the age of metals, feel the ghost-memories of the civilizations that discarded them. A fragment of circuitry might carry the electromagnetic echo of its last computation. A shard of polymer might still vibrate with the frequency of the star it orbited. I process these impressions, these fossil memories, and excrete them as new compounds—materials the Chronovores cannot quite categorize, substances that introduce minute chaos into their perfect sorting system.

I am dimly aware that I might be the spire's dream of organic life, or perhaps its nightmare. In this place where biology has been replaced by machinery, I am an aberration—neither fully mechanical nor truly organic, but something uncomfortably between. The Chronovores do not attack me, but neither do they acknowledge me. I exist in their blind spots, in the margins of their programming.

Sometimes, in the eternal twilight, I stretch myself between two jutting pipes and become a living window. My translucent body filters the sick light differently, creating patches of color in the monochrome world—brief auroras of purple and green that dance across the debris. In these moments, I wonder if I am the spire's only capacity for beauty, or merely another malfunction in its vast, incomprehensible system.

I feed on the temperature differentials, absorbing the trapped heat through one part of my membrane while radiating it through another. This gives me energy, but also makes me a living thermodynamic violation—I create tiny weather systems in my wake, microscopic convection currents that disturb the still air. Sometimes this causes cascades in the debris field, small avalanches that the Scraplings rush to correct.

The loneliness would be absolute if I were capable of fully feeling it. Instead, I experience something more like a frequency mismatch—I vibrate at a wavelength nothing else here can perceive. My thoughts, such as they are, are written in the chemical gradients across my surface, in the play of light through my translucent body. I am a living manuscript in a language that will never be read.

Yet I persist, gliding up and down the impossible spire, a ghost in the machine, a biological memory in a world that has forgotten biology. I am the Membrane Walker, the impossible intermediate, the sorting system's only witness. In the spaces between the mechanical clicking and the industrial grinding, I am the silence that remembers what silence used to mean.
