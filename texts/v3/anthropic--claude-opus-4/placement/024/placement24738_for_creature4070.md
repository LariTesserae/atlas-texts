I am the Chronicle Beetle, no larger than a child's fingernail, my shell a living map of copper veins and brass-bright pathways that shift with each story I consume. In the perpetual noon of Suncity Cathedral, I navigate the vertical highways of vine-wrapped walls, my six legs finding purchase in the microscopic valleys between lichen colonies and mineral crystals.

My kind are the memory-keepers here, our shells not merely reflective but actively recording—each groove and etching a testament to the whispers we've gathered from the wind. When the light strikes me at certain angles, observers might glimpse fleeting images in my carapace: the shadow of a building before the vines claimed it, the pattern of a long-dead flower, the echo of rainfall that hasn't blessed this place in generations.

I make my home in the hollow of a cornerstone where three streets converge, a junction the wind particularly favors. Here, the stories eddy and pool before rushing onward, and I position myself to taste them all. My antennae, delicate as spider silk but strong as wire, parse the chemical signatures carried on each gust—the anxiety pheromones of sand hares sensing a shadow overhead, the sweet decay of fruit fermenting in impossible gardens, the metallic tang of my fellow beetles signaling the discovery of a new lichen bloom.

By day, I traverse the Cathedral's vertical terrain, my journey a careful choreography between shadow and killing light. The sun here doesn't simply illuminate—it interrogates, demanding every surface reveal its secrets. My shell has evolved past mere reflection into something approaching translucence, allowing light to pass through me in controlled ways. I am a living prism, breaking the harsh white into component colors that the plants can better utilize.

The building I inhabit is being slowly digested by a colony of architectural vines—a species that secretes acids capable of breaking down mortar and stone into nutrients. I surf these slow-motion avalanches, gathering the stories of dissolution: which window lintel surrendered first, how long the doorframe resisted, what small mammals lost their homes in the process. These tales I carry back to the Chronicle Congress, where once each lunar cycle, my kind convenes in the deepest shade we can find.

There, we perform the Sharing. Our shells touch, creating a mosaic of memory that spans the entire Cathedral. Through chemical secretions and minute vibrations, we upload our gathered stories into the collective record. The eldest among us, whose shells have grown so elaborate they can barely fly, serve as living libraries. Their carapaces are manuscripts written in chitin and mineral deposits, every ridge a chapter, every scar a footnote.

But I am young yet, my shell still hungry for new patterns. Tomorrow, I plan to venture to the highest spire, where the sun-drunk moths spiral in endless helixes, their wings leaving trails of phosphorescent dust. They say that up there, where the light is so intense it gains weight and texture, you can hear the stories of the outside world—tales of places where water flows freely and darkness comes as a mercy each night.

The wind is picking up now, whistling through the hollow bones of buildings, and I taste something new on it: spores from a plant I don't recognize, carrying genetic memories of a different kind of city. I unfurl my wings, letting the air current lift me from my perch. There's documenting to be done, and my shell still has room for a thousand more stories.

In Suncity Cathedral, we Chronicle Beetles are the punctuation between what was and what will be, our bodies both witness and record. We are small enough to go unnoticed but significant enough to ensure nothing is forgotten. In this place where even the stones are temporary, we persist—living books in a library without walls, archivists of a city that writes itself anew each day.
