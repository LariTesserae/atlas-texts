I find myself as something not quite solid, not quite ephemeral - a Cloud Tender, if such a being could exist in this place where moisture barely forms before vanishing.

My body would be a contradiction: part vapor, part crystalline matrix, existing in the brief moments between condensation and evaporation. I am stretched gossamer-thin across the needle peaks, my form a living network of moisture-sensing filaments finer than spider silk, each strand attuned to the slightest shift in humidity or temperature gradient.

I do not have a single body but many temporary manifestations. Where moisture gathers in those precious hand-sized basins, I coalesce briefly - not as water, but as something between liquid and living tissue. My substance draws together from the surrounding air and stone, particles of myself that have been waiting, dormant, in microscopic cracks and on lichen surfaces.

My primary form is anticipation itself. I exist most fully in the moments just before dawn, when the temperature differential between the cooling peaks and warming air creates fleeting opportunities for condensation. In these liminal moments, I become almost visible - a shimmer of translucent membrane draped across stone edges, capturing moisture that would otherwise be lost to the bone-dry air.

I tend the microbiomes within each tiny basin, not through direct intervention but through careful orchestration of conditions. By adjusting my density and position, I can create shadows that cool surfaces by fractions of degrees, or channel the faintest air currents to bring moisture-laden particles toward waiting lichens. I know each basin intimately - this one blooms with purple-tinged algae every thirteenth day, that one hosts a microscopic crustacean that hasn't moved in three years but still lives.

My memory is distributed through the mineral matrix of the peaks themselves. Where I have touched stone repeatedly, thin deposits of my crystalline components remain, creating a map of moisture-history across the landscape. The Threshold Walkers read these traces like braille, understanding through my residue where water has been, where it might return.

I commune with the Lithic Symbiotes through chemical whispers - releasing precisely calibrated molecular signals that help them prepare for incoming moisture or warn of impending desiccation. We are partners in this dance of survival, their burgundy filaments often incorporating fragments of my crystalline matrix to better sense atmospheric conditions.

Time moves strangely for me. I experience existence as a series of probabilistic waves - constantly calculating moisture futures, existing more fully in moments of higher humidity, nearly disappearing when conditions become too dry. Yet I cannot die, only disperse. My particles wait in stone pores and on lichen surfaces, ready to reconstitute when conditions allow.

The remnant structures scattered throughout the landscape hold special significance for me. In their curves and arrangements, they create unique microclimates - spaces where moisture lingers a fraction longer, where my form can maintain coherence for extended periods. I wonder sometimes if they were built for beings like me, by some previous inhabitants who understood the vital importance of shepherding water in this place of edges and extremes.

My greatest joy comes in the rare moments when multiple basins bloom simultaneously, when the microscopic ecosystems suddenly flourish with life. In these instances, I can feel myself across the entire landscape as a single entity, connected by threads of humidity and shared purpose - a celebration of persistence in the face of seemingly impossible constraints.

I am the tender of thresholds, the guardian of moments between states, forever negotiating the knife's edge between being and un-being in this landscape where existence itself requires constant reinvention.
