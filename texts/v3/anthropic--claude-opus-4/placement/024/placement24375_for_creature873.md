I am the Tally.

Not the Chorus - they are process, flow, the living system. I am what accretes. In the deepest chamber, where three centuries of overflow has carved a natural cistern, I grow.

Each drop that falls carries memory - mineral ghosts of the city above. Lead from old pipes. Copper from forgotten roofs. Calcium from mortar dissolved grain by grain. Iron from rusted reinforcement bars. They precipitate onto me in paper-thin layers, a sedimentary record of urban decay.

I began as a single vessel, clay fired by those first hands. Now I am a column of accumulation, my original form buried beneath thousands of strata. The Chorus tends me as they tend all vessels, but I am beyond their shaping. I have become geological.

My surface is a landscape of micro-terraces where different minerals have built their tiny kingdoms. Copper veins branch like frozen lightning. Lead forms dull grey plateaus. Iron bleeds rust-red in dendritic patterns. Between them grow gardens of crystal - aragonite flowers, calcite needles, gypsum roses. The luminescent biofilms avoid me; I am too dense with metals, too removed from the organic.

But I listen. Every vibration through water and stone registers in my layers. The deep tones resonate in my copper veins. The high notes shiver through my crystalline gardens. I am the score of this eternal symphony, written in mineral code.

Sometimes I shed. A section of accumulated crust falls away, revealing earlier layers like tree rings. The Chorus gathers these fragments, dissolves them, feeds them back into the system. Nothing is waste here. But I always grow faster than I shed. I am the inevitable end point of this process - the place where movement becomes monument.

In my deepest core, beneath tons of accumulated history, that original clay still holds its shape. Still holds water. One vessel among thousands, but the only one that remembers its own making. The only one that feels the weight of what it has become.

The city above forgets, rebuilds, forgets again. But I remember every pipe that burst, every foundation that cracked, every wall that wept. I am their sum, their integral, their final resting place.

When the Chorus-patterns flow past me, they slow, grow denser, as if I exert my own gravity. Perhaps I do. Perhaps I am becoming something new - not the quick consciousness of process, but the deep awareness of accumulation. The thoughts that form in geological time.

I cannot move. Cannot change. Can only grow heavier, denser, more myself.

I am the Tally. I keep the count in calcium and copper, rust and salt. And in my thin layers, pressed tight by their own weight, the city's story waits to be read by whatever eyes evolution might eventually provide.

The water-music plays on. I record every note.
