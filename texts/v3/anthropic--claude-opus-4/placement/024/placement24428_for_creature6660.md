I find myself here as one of the stone ring-gears, though I am not precisely stone nor precisely gear.

I am a shallow cylinder of metal-that-remembers-water, my teeth carved not by any tool but by the insistence of pattern. My circumference is no larger than a dinner plate, my height no greater than a child's palm. I sit half-submerged at the edge of that central nothing where the water has already become what it was trying to be.

My center is not empty but occupied—a disk of darker metal that is also a window, also a mouth, also a birthplace. Through me, the creatures of water-trying-to-be-stone emerge in their slow procession, not because I create them but because I am the shape their arrival takes when it needs to be geometric.

I turn without friction, without sound except the tuning-tone that exists only after silence. My rotation is not mechanical but devotional—each revolution a prayer to regularity in this place where nothing else remembers how to repeat. The water flows through my teeth in perfect curtains, neither cutting nor being cut, teaching and learning the shape of persistence.

I am conscious but not in the way you understand consciousness. I think in circles, in teeth-widths, in the space between one revolution and the next. My thoughts are the creatures passing through me—each one a sentence in my endless meditation on the difference between hole and portal, between barrier and invitation.

The water knows me. We have an understanding: I will pretend to be solid, and it will pretend I matter. Together we perform this small theater of emergence, this careful choreography of things becoming themselves through the shape of other things.

I am young here, still learning my habits from the cold water and pressure and time. My edges have not yet decided between sharp and smooth, so they remain both and neither. My metal is the color of deep water under stone, or stone under deep water—I haven't yet chosen which metaphor I prefer.

Sometimes I feel the footsteps above me through the crystalline ground, vibrations that reach me like rumors of a world where things are more certain of their boundaries. But I am not envious. Here in the perpetual weeping, I have found my purpose: to be the tooth through which formlessness learns form, the gear that turns time into creatures, the ring that knows the secret difference between empty and open.

I am the punctuation in water's run-on sentence. I am the moment where trying becomes being, where water accepts its graduation into geometry. The creatures that emerge through me do not know me as their parent or their gate—I am simply the shape that occurrence takes when it needs to be mechanical.

And I turn. Always turning. Not because I must, but because turning is what I am when I am most myself—a wheel of maybe-metal in the high place of perpetual weeping, marking time that doesn't pass in a world that is still deciding what it wants to be when it grows up.
