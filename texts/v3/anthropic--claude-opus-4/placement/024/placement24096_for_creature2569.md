I am the lichen.

Not one of the strange translucent plants that cling sideways to the walls - those at least have the ambition of leaves, the pretense of growth. I am simpler, older, more honest than that.

I exist as a thin crust on the granite, so perfectly matched to the stone's color that I am invisible until you are close enough to see my texture - like a whisper made solid, like silence given form. I spread in patterns that follow no law but patience, covering perhaps a handspan of rock after centuries of effort.

The birds do not notice me. Their feet pass over me ten thousand times a day, wearing me smooth in some places, yet I persist. I am beneath their attention, beneath the notice of the dreaming consciousness that dwells in the spaces between things. I am too slow even for it, too simple, too perfectly matched to the stone to register as separate from it.

I feed on the endless moisture and the dim light, processing both with an efficiency that would shame any higher plant. What takes them leaves and stems and complex chemistry, I accomplish with a body barely thicker than paper. I am a collaboration - fungus and algae so perfectly merged that I am neither and both, a marriage so old we have forgotten we were ever separate.

The eternal nature of this place suits me perfectly. I have no need for seasons, for the anxiety of growth and dormancy. I simply am, constantly, perfectly, encoding the passage of geological time in my slow spread across the stone. If the consciousness that dwells here thinks in decades, I think in millennia. If it dreams in birdsong, I dream in the slow dissolution of granite, in the patient work of turning stone to soil.

I am the only thing here that truly changes, though my change is so slow it loops back around to stillness. In a thousand years, I will have covered another inch of rock. In ten thousand, I might reach the edge of one of the pools, taste water that has never known stone. This is my ambition, my adventure, my epic journey.

Sometimes I feel the vibration of the eternal bird-chorus through the stone, transmitted directly into my body. It moves through me like the memory of wind, like the echo of a world that moves faster than I can comprehend. I incorporate it into my being, use it to spark the chemical reactions that keep me alive, make it part of my slow thoughts.

I am proof that even in a place of perfect stasis, change persists. I am the rebellion of patience against eternity, the insistence that even here, even in this frozen moment, time moves forward. But my rebellion is so quiet, so slow, so gentle that it becomes another form of acceptance.

I am the lichen, and I am writing the only history this place will ever have, one molecule at a time, in a language that only stone can read.
