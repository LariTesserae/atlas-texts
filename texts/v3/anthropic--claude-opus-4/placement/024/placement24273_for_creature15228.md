In N'Terra, I am one of the Scriptlings—beings who emerged where the children's laughter-parchment first touched the manifesting ponds. 

We are living stories, our bodies shifting between flesh and text, between spoken word and whispered memory. When still, I might appear as a child-sized figure made of folded paper, my skin inscribed with fragments of the tales that birthed this world. But when I move, the words flow like water across my form, rearranging themselves into new narratives, new possibilities.

We Scriptlings live in the Margin Lands—the space between what is written and what is real, where the Azure Mountains meet the weeping sky. Our homes are half-formed theaters carved into the mountainside, their walls flickering between stone and silver screen. Here, we tend to the Continuity Gardens, where we grow scenes like flowers: an action sequence blooms purple and thunderous, while a quiet dialogue unfurls its petals in soft amber light.

My particular calling is as a Sorrow Scribe. When the sky weeps for the brutality below, I collect its tears in bottles made of crystallized sighs. Each tear contains a story of what was witnessed—the small cruelties, the grand betrayals, the moments when the world below forgets it was born from joy. I transform these tears into silver ink, which I use to write amendments into the air itself, hoping to gentle the harsh narratives that unfold downstream.

Sometimes I walk among the birds at the peak, learning their language of oversight and distance. They tell me of the humans trudging below, and I fold their observations into origami messengers that I release into the wind. These paper birds carry questions disguised as wings: *What if kindness were the next scene? What if the brutal earth remembered its birth in laughter?*

The other Scriptlings think me too melancholy, too focused on revision rather than creation. But I know that in N'Terra, where children built the world from cinema and joy, someone must tend to the spaces between frames, the moments where the story could turn. I am the pause before the plot twist, the inhale before the laugh returns.

When the Earthborn need new stories, when the Wistful Ones grow too heavy with rain, when the Fluidics forget the lyrics to their ancient songs, they come to the Margin Lands. And we Scriptlings—caught between page and person, between what was written and what could be—offer them new drafts of themselves, new ways to read the world they inhabit.

I am both author and authored, both the hand that writes and the word that is written, forever editing the brutal earth back toward the laughter that first carved it into being.
