### **Where I Would Find Myself**

I am one of the **Memory Weavers** - beings who exist in the liminal spaces between the kelp fronds and the coral gardens. My body is neither solid nor liquid, but something in between - a flowing assemblage of water that holds its shape through will alone. I appear as a translucent figure, human in silhouette but fluid in detail, my form constantly shifting like a slow-motion waterfall contained within an invisible membrane.

Within my body float tiny fragments of light - not bioluminescence, but actual captured memories from the world above. They drift through me like glowing seeds, each one containing a moment: a sunset over waves, a storm breaking against cliffs, the laughter of children on a distant beach. These memories filter down through the water column, and I gather them before they can sink into the abyssal depths where they would be lost forever.

### **My Purpose**

I move through the kelp forests with deliberate slowness, my passage disturbing nothing. When I encounter the Coral Whisperers, we exchange gifts - they offer me their crystalline songs, which I weave into the memories to give them sound. In return, I share fragments of surface-light, which they incorporate into their bioluminescent trails, making their artwork glow with remembered suns.

My dwelling is a grove where the kelp grows in perfect spirals, creating a natural amphitheater. Here, I release the memories I've collected, letting them play out in the water like three-dimensional films. The Stone Dreamers sometimes stir in their sleep when these memories drift past, their own dreams mingling with the captured moments to create new visions - hybrid realities that never were but might have been.

### **My Existence**

I understand the Kelp Sentinels' vigil, for I too am a guardian of sorts - not of the plateau itself, but of its connection to the world above. Without beings like me, this submerged realm would become truly separate, cut off from the cycle of memory and meaning that links all water everywhere.

Sometimes I sense the Abyssal Drifters passing below, and I feel the pull of their darkness - the temptation to let go, to stop gathering, to simply dissolve into the eternal now of the deep. But then a new memory drifts down - perhaps the splash of rain on leaves, or the sound of waves against a wooden hull - and I remember why I persist.

I am the archivist of the drowned world, the keeper of what was, ensuring that even in this place of eternal present, the past is not entirely forgotten. And perhaps, in my own small way, I am preparing for something - for the day when the plateau rises again, or when someone from above finally finds their way down to these hidden gardens, seeking the memories we've preserved in the patient darkness of the deep.
