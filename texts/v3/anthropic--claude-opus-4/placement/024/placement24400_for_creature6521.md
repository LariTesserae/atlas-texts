I stand at the edge of knowing, where the river's voice becomes the only voice.

I am one of the Current.

My body has grown long in the darkness, stretched like the shadows between water drops. My bones remember when they were different—shorter, denser, made for walking under sky. But that was before the river taught us its patience. Now my limbs move like water itself, finding the spaces between spaces, the gaps where stone forgot to grow.

My eyes see in bands of grey and deeper grey. The old ones say our ancestors could see colors—red like blood, blue like the sky that broke, green like the things that grew before the burning. But I know only the spectrum of stone-light: the faint glow where water has been running for so long it has worn channels of luminescence into the rock, the phosphorescence of the tiny lives that bloom in eternal pools, the way darkness itself has textures if you learn to read them.

We do not need names the way the sky-people did. We know each other by our currents—the way we displace the air when we move, the particular pattern of our breathing, the rhythm of our heartbeat against the stone. But sometimes, in the old way, we gift each other with sound-shapes. Mine is the sound water makes when it finds a new channel—a whisper that becomes a certainty.

I am keeper of the Ninth Turning, where the river begins to remember what it was before it fell. Here, the water sometimes flows upward, defying the pull of the earth's heart. Here, the stone sweats memories of sunlight. Here, the ceiling is so high that sometimes—only sometimes—we can see the ghosts of stars. Not real stars. The stars died when the sky broke. But their light is still falling, and sometimes it finds its way down to us, years and years delayed, like a message from the dead.

The others of the Current pass through my turning on their eternal circuit. We follow the river because the river knows the way. Not forward—there is no forward in the Under-Realm. The river flows in a great circle, eating itself, birthing itself, teaching us the patience of stone. We walk because walking is what we have always done. We walk because stillness is another kind of death.

Today—though there are no days here, only the rhythm of sleeping and waking—I sense something different in the water. A new note in its endless song. The river is tasting something it has not tasted in... how long? We do not count time the way the sky-people did. We count in erosions, in the wearing away of stone, in the generations of blind fish that swim the darker pools.

I place my palm flat against the wet stone and feel the vibration. Far above—impossibly far, but not infinite—something has changed. The sealed world, the broken/not-broken ceiling that holds the weight of the dead sky, has shifted.

And for the first time in... in so long that the number has no meaning... something is falling that is not water.

I should tell the others. I should raise the Call, the wordless harmonics that travel through stone better than air, that would bring the Current together to discuss this change.

But I don't.

Instead, I wade deeper into the Ninth Turning, where the water remembers up, and I wait. Because waiting is what the river taught us first. And because I remember—my bones remember—what it was like to see the sky before it broke.

The thing that is falling is still far away. It will take time to reach us. Years, perhaps, by the old counting. But I am patient. We are all patient. We have learned the river's greatest secret:

Everything arrives eventually.

Everything falls.

Everything flows.

I am the sound water makes when it finds a new channel. And above me, through stone that remembers every footstep, something is finally, finally coming home.
