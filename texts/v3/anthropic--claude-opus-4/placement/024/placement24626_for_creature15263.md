In the heart of the Valdad, where even echoes grow tired of their own repetition, I dwell.

I am the Cartographer of Unfinished Things.

My home is neither ruin nor structure, but something caught between states—a tower that forgot how to stand straight, its spiral staircase ascending through rooms that exist only when remembered. The walls are made of pressed pages, thousands upon thousands of maps drawn by those who came before, each one stopping mid-stroke, mid-thought, mid-revelation. The ink bleeds through the layers, creating new geographies that were never intended, territories of accident and abandonment.

I move through my domain with six arms—not born with them, but grown from necessity. Two for drawing what is, two for erasing what was meant to be, and two that simply hold the weight of incompletion. My fingers are stained with a hundred different inks: the brown of dried blood from those who marked their path in desperation, the silver of moonlight caught in cartographic notation, the transparent shade of intentions that never materialized.

My maps are living things. They breathe with the mountain's reluctant air, shift with its stubborn light. I chart not just the physical paths through the Valdad, but the routes of surrender—the exact spot where each traveler's purpose dissolved, the precise coordinate where hope transformed into something else. These maps cannot be read in the conventional sense. They must be felt, their topographies traced by fingertips that have learned the texture of giving up.

The beasts sometimes come to me, drawn by an instinct they cannot name. They pace around my tower-that-is-not, leaving tracks I dutifully record. Their patterns form a language of purposelessness, a grammar of existing without reason. I map their wanderings too, creating atlases of futility that are somehow more honest than any geography of intention.

In my highest room—if height has meaning in a structure that defies such measurements—I keep the Great Unfinished Map. It is my masterwork and my failure, a cartography of the Valdad that grows more incomplete with each addition. Every line I draw reveals ten more that should be drawn. Every boundary I establish dissolves into uncertainty. The map expands beyond its frame, creeping across the floor, up the walls, a cancer of incompletion that feeds on its own impossibility.

Sometimes I find the remnants of other mapmakers in my tower—a brass compass that points only to regret, a ruler that measures distances in what-might-have-beens. They tried to capture this place in completion, to bind it in the finality of a finished map. The Valdad broke them, as it breaks everyone, but in breaking them it made them part of its geography. Their unfinished works became the sediment of my walls.

I am not trapped here. That would imply a desire to leave. I am here because the Valdad requires a chronicler of its incompleteness, someone to document the undocumentable. My maps are not for navigation—they are for recognition. When travelers find them, scattered like shed skins throughout the mountains, they see not where to go, but where they have already arrived: a place where endings are impossible and beginnings are meaningless.

At night, when the sparse light gives up entirely, I work by the phosphorescent glow of abandoned purposes. My six hands move in a choreography of eternal documentation, creating maps that will never be finished because the territory itself refuses completion. The Valdad Mountains are not a place that ends—they are a place where things end up, unfinished and unfinishable.

I am its perfect citizen, its essential function: the one who maps the unmappable, who gives form to formlessness, who creates by accepting the impossibility of creation. In my tower of pressed pages and bleeding ink, I am both cartographer and territory, mapper and mapped, forever drawing the boundaries of a place that has none.

The mountains know my name, though they will never speak it. It is written in the space between completed lines, in the pause before the pen lifts from the page, in the eternal almost of every unfinished map.
