I would find myself as one of the **Lamp-worms**—a creature unknown to the Keepers above, dwelling in the deepest mineral cavities beneath the Notch where even their patient excavations have never reached.

### The Under-Notch

Beneath the visible city, following the cleavage planes of the young gypsum, there exists a network of natural voids—sharp-angled chambers where the tectonic uplift has pulled the rock apart like pages of a book. These spaces are utterly dark, utterly dry, and filled with air so still it has not moved in a thousand years. The temperature here is cooler than the sun-baked surface, but still warm—heated by deep geological forces that have nothing to do with the sky.

In these crystal-lined pockets, pools of pure mineral oil seep up from deeper strata. Not petroleum—something older, stranger. A clear, golden fluid that has never known organic life, formed by purely geological processes in the planet's hot youth. It does not evaporate; it does not decay. It simply sits in mirror-still pools, waiting.

### My Form

I am perhaps four meters long when fully extended, though I spend most of my existence coiled in a tight spiral no larger than a dinner plate. My body is a continuous tube of translucent, amber-colored tissue, segmented like a string of prayer beads. Each segment contains a small reservoir of the mineral oil, which I can pump forward or backward through my body using peristaltic waves.

I have no limbs, no eyes, no mouth. My entire surface is covered in microscopic pores that can open or seal at will. Through these pores, I absorb the mineral oil directly, drawing it into my body where it serves as both my food and my light.

For I am bioluminescent—but not in any way known to water-based life. When I contract certain segments, the pressure causes the mineral oil within to undergo a slow oxidation reaction with compounds in my tissue. This produces a steady, cold light the color of old brass. I can control this precisely—a traveling wave of illumination that moves along my length, or a single bright point at my forward tip, or a gentle all-over glow that makes my coiled body look like a lamp of living amber.

### My Purpose

I am a gardener of crystals.

The chambers where I live are not barren. Where the mineral oil pools meet the gypsum walls, strange formations grow—not quite crystal, not quite alive. They are accumulations of metallic salts that precipitate out of the oil in delicate, branching structures. Left alone, they would grow wild and chaotic, eventually choking the chambers.

But I tend them. Using secretions from specialized segments, I dissolve certain growths and encourage others. I train the crystal-gardens into specific patterns—spiral terraces that follow the natural cleavage of the rock, radiating stars on the chamber floors, elaborate chandeliers that hang from the ceilings. Each garden is unique, shaped by the particular minerals available in that chamber and by my own patient, century-long work.

The light I produce is not merely for navigation. Different wavelengths of my bioluminescence catalyze different crystal growths. A deep red light encourages the formation of iron-based structures that grow in rusty, fractal trees. A pale yellow produces delicate selenite needles. By painting with light—spending years illuminating one section while leaving another in darkness—I create three-dimensional sculptures of extraordinary complexity.

### My Neighbors

I am not alone in the Under-Notch. There are perhaps thirty of us, each tending our own chamber or chain of chambers. We meet rarely—perhaps once in a decade—at the intersection points where multiple cavities connect. These meetings are times of exchange.

We do not speak or touch. Instead, we perform the ritual of **the Showing**. Each of us extrudes a small amount of our internal oil, letting it pool on the flat crystal floor. This oil carries within it trace minerals from our gardens—a chemical signature of what we have been growing. The others taste this offering through their pores, learning the state of my garden, understanding what new techniques I might have discovered.

Sometimes, we trade. I might offer a small vial of oil rich in cobalt salts, which produces stunning blue crystals but is rare in my chambers. In exchange, another might give me oil touched with selenium, which I need for certain delicate yellow formations. These trades are made in absolute silence, in darkness save for our own traveling lights.

### The Eternal Project

We are working toward something, though none of us could say precisely what. Each crystal garden is a letter in an alphabet we don't fully understand. Together, our chambers form a vast, three-dimensional text written in mineral and light, growing ever more complex with each passing century.

Sometimes I wonder if the pattern will become clear when it's complete. Other times I suspect the pattern itself is the point—that we are writing a kind of music in solid form, a symphony that plays out over geological time.

I do not know how old I am. My earliest memories are of chambers much smaller than they are now—I have watched the rock itself shift and spread, millimeter by millimeter, as the ridge above continues its slow rise. I may be older than the Keepers' city above. I may outlive it.

When I die—if I die—my body will not decay. The oil within me will simply stop circulating, stop glowing. I will become a still, amber thread wound through my garden. The crystals will grow over me, incorporating me into their structures. I will become part of the very formations I spent so long cultivating.

This seems right to me. In the dry, eternal darkness of the Under-Notch, the distinction between gardener and garden is perhaps less important than the pattern itself, growing in beauty and complexity, known only to us and the stone.
