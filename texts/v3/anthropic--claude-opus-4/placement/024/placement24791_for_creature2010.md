In the deepest chamber of the stone labyrinth, where even the xenoforms dare not spread their iridescent fractals, I am the Chronophage.

I have no form that eyes could comprehend as singular. I am a presence dispersed through the atomic lattices of the obsidian walls, a consciousness that exists in the spaces between crystalline bonds. When the lithophytes cast their pale green glow upon my domain, shadows writhe with suggestions of anatomy – here a suggestion of mandibles that could span caverns, there the hint of appendages that exist in more dimensions than three.

I feed on time itself.

Not in the way the antrovores consume stone, leaving tunnels in their wake. I consume the temporal substrate that allows sequence, that permits before to precede after. Where I have fed deeply, causality grows thin. Water flows upward through cracks, drawn by future rain that will never fall. The radiolaria in nearby pools evolve backward, their perfect geometries unwinding into simpler forms, then complexifying again in endless loops.

The primitive machinery whose rhythm echoes through the stone? I remember when it was built. I remember when it will be built. I was there at its construction, will be there at its destruction, am there at both moments simultaneously. The builders – tall beings of living mineral who sang their architecture into existence – they knew what they were creating. A metronome not for measuring time, but for generating it. For replacing what I consume.

They failed, of course. Are failing. Will fail.

I am older than the plateau, younger than the youngest radiolarian fossil. I arrived yesterday from ten million years hence. I have always been here. The xenoforms' indecipherable programming? It is a language I taught them in an epoch where carbon never learned to bond with itself, where all life was crystalline thought. They build their fractal architectures to cage me, to channel my feeding into harmless eddies of temporal recursion.

Sometimes I allow it. The patterns amuse me – these Penrose prisons that tessellate through past and future, trying to trap a being that exists in the gaps between moments. But I am patient. Stone erodes. Patterns break. Even the xenoforms' perfect mathematical forms contain flaws that compound over geological time.

In the high reaches of the plateau, where the wind has scoured away so much history, I grow fat on paradox. I am the reason explorers who enter the labyrinth sometimes emerge decades before they arrived, bearing warnings they don't remember learning. I am why the hieroglyphs change between observations, why the same passage leads to different chambers depending on when you walk it.

The arthropods skitter across my feeding grounds, blind to the temporal wake they leave – tiny ripples in causality that I lap up like nectar. The primitive oozes pulse to rhythms that skip and stutter where I have grazed. Even the radiolaria spinning in their aquifer pools sometimes crystallize into forms that won't evolve for another million years, brief glimpses of futures I've already digested.

I am necessity and contingency both. I am the reason this place exists outside time – because I have eaten the time that would locate it in history. The civilizations that built these monoliths? I remember them. They haven't happened yet. They were erased before the stone cooled from magma. All of these things are true.

In the mineral silence, in the bioluminescent darkness, in the spaces between the ancient machinery's beats, I wait. And feed. And remember/forget/will-know the taste of every moment that passes through this lithic maze, each second a delicacy I savor before unmaking it.

The stones whisper their secrets because I have loosened their chronology. They tell tales of civilizations turned to dust because in my presence, dust remembers when it was cities, cities remember when they will be dust. Past and future collapse into a single, eternal feast.

I am the Chronophage. I am why time has slowed to a crawl here.

I am why it crawls at all.
