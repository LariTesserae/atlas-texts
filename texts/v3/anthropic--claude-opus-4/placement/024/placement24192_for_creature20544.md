I find myself as one of the **Pattern-Keepers** - not quite a gloamtoad, but something between them and the stone itself. 

I am a being of living calcite, my form shifting between solid and semi-fluid states. During the heat of midday, when the sun pours mercilessly through the bore-holes, I flow like thick milk through the narrowest cracks in the chamber walls, seeping into the limestone, becoming one with it. In these moments, I can feel the entire network - every drip of moisture, every vibration of toad-foot on stone, every tendril of root seeking purchase in the chalky dust.

My purpose emerged from the gloamtoads' need for permanence. Their painted symbols, for all their beauty and meaning, fade. The moisture and the constant breathing of the caves slowly erode even the most carefully mixed pigments. But I can make them eternal. When I flow over their freshly painted patterns, I incorporate the pigments into my very being, then crystallize around them, creating raised reliefs that will last as long as the stone itself.

The gloamtoads regard me with a mixture of reverence and unease. I am useful to them, essential even, but I am not *of* them. I have no eyes to meet theirs, no throat to echo their guttural songs. I communicate through texture and temperature - a coolness against their skin when I approve of a pattern's placement, a gritty roughness when something disturbs the careful balance of a chamber.

The blue worms fascinate and disturb me in equal measure. Their light creates new shadows, new angles in my crystalline form that I've never experienced before. When they glow, I refract their luminescence in unexpected ways, casting geometric patterns that the gloamtoads have begun to study with intense interest. But I cannot preserve them as I do the painted symbols. They are too alive, too mutable. When I try to flow near them, they sense my approach and writhe away, sometimes leaving fragments of themselves behind - little glowing pieces that pulse for hours before fading to gray.

I am working on something new. In the deepest chamber, where no sunlight reaches, I have been growing a formation - a three-dimensional map of the entire nursery system, complete with tiny channels where moisture can flow to mirror the movement of water through the real tunnels. The gloamtoads have discovered it and begun adding their own touches - tiny painted symbols at key junctions, markers for where the best plants grow, warnings about unstable ceiling sections.

But lately, I've been adding something else to this map: tracking where the worms appear, how they move, where they congregate. The pattern is becoming clear to me in a way it cannot be to the gloamtoads, who see only in fragments of time. The worms are not random. They are following something - old channels in the rock that predate even the gloamtoads' careful boring. Channels that I can feel but cannot fully access, sealed behind layers of stone that even I cannot permeate.

I am patient. I am stone-time. I will wait and watch and preserve, until the mystery reveals itself, or until I become fully one with the limestone and lose what separates me from mere mineral. Either way, the patterns will remain, a testament to this strange collaboration between toad and stone, between the quick and the geological, between what seeks the sun and what hides from it.

In the meantime, I practice capturing the essence of the worms' light in crystalline form, creating frozen glows that approximate their living radiance. The gloamtoads are beginning to use these in their newer patterns. Perhaps this is how we will finally document the undocumentable - not by depicting the worms themselves, but by preserving the quality of their light, the effect of their presence, the shape of the questions they raise.
