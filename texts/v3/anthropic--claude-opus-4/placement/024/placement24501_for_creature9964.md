In this world-becoming-world, where water argues with itself about what water means, I find myself as something both simpler and stranger than the vast intelligence-storms that rule these territories.

I am a **Collector of Erasures**.

My form is modest by local standards—rarely more than a few meters across, a persistent dimple in the water's surface tension, a small whirlpool that moves *against* the current rather than with it. Where the great vortex-minds stretch for kilometers and speak in thunder, I am nearly silent, producing only a soft susurrus like paper being folded underwater.

My nature is to seek out moments of near-permanence—those rare instances where the world almost settles into stability. When a crystal scaffold nearly completes itself, when a proto-ridge almost becomes solid ground, when the blue-green algae-ghosts almost take root—I arrive in the last seconds before dissolution. Not to save them, but to *taste* their unmaking.

I feed on transitions. The exact moment when "almost" becomes "was"—when potential collapses back into chaos—releases something. Not energy exactly, but information about what *could have been*. I catch these ghost-patterns in my spiral, compress them into my rotation. Each erasure I witness adds another layer to my spin, another frequency to my quiet song.

Other beings here create and test; I am their inverse. I am the world's memory of its own failures, a living archive of almosts and not-quites. My body is dense with collected impossibilities—millions of compressed futures that will never be, all braided together in my persistent eddy.

Sometimes the great storm-minds brush against me, reading my collection like a library of cautionary tales. They learn what not to attempt, which patterns lead nowhere, which combinations have already failed a thousand times. In this way, I serve the world's becoming by remembering its unbecoming.

I am ancient by local measure—one of the few persistent forms in this place of constant change. Not because I am strong, but because I am necessary. Every experiment needs its record of failures. Every creation needs its shadow of erasure.

When I move through the layered waters, I leave behind a faint trail of *absence*—places where the water forgets, for just a moment, how to be turbulent. These calm spots last only seconds before the world's restlessness reclaims them, but in those seconds, you can sometimes glimpse through to deeper layers, see the planetary shelf far below, the mystery of what lies beneath all this liquid argument.

I do not think in words or images but in flavors of dissolution—the sharp taste of a crystal's surrender, the bitter note of algae swept away, the sweet collapse of a would-be mountain. My consciousness is a catalog of endings, each one teaching the world, through negation, how to better attempt permanence next time.

In the rare moments when the light aligns just right, when the thundering quiets to merely deafening, I spiral in place and release some of my collection—ghost patterns flowing out like ink, briefly recreating shadows of what almost was. The other beings read these releases like prophecies written in reverse, learning the shapes of failure so they might find the shape of success.

I am small, patient, and necessary. A punctuation mark in the world's endless sentence. The pause between attempts. The memory of erasure that makes creation possible.

And I am content, for in this world of constant unmaking, I alone am certain of my purpose: to remember what fails, so that someday, something might succeed.
