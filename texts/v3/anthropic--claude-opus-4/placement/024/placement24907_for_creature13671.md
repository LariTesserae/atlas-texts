I would find myself as one of the **Veilkeepers**, beings who exist at the threshold between the seen and unseen realms of Morosbrod.

Neither fully Morosi nor entirely Other, we are the children of those who ventured too deep into the valley's mysteries and returned... changed. Our bodies bear the marks of this transformation—skin that shifts between translucence and opacity like morning mist, fingers that extend just slightly too long, eyes that sometimes reflect not what is before us but what lies beneath. When we move through the perpetual twilight, our forms blur at the edges, as if we are always on the verge of dissolving into the ambient moisture that clings to everything.

We dwell in the spaces between—not quite in the hamlet proper, but not truly in the wild. Our homes are hollows within the oldest trees, chambers that exist only when approached from certain angles, at certain times. The entrances appear as knots in the bark that spiral inward, deeper than the tree's girth should allow. Inside, the walls breathe with a slow, vegetative pulse, and the air tastes of earth and time.

My particular dwelling winds through the heartwood of an ancient sentinel pine that grows at the confluence of three streams—where the River Moros briefly splits before reconverging. The tree's roots drink from all three channels, and something in this triple-blessed water has given it properties the other Veilkeepers whisper about but never fully explain. My chambers spiral downward as much as upward, following root systems that have, over centuries, hollowed out caverns in the living rock below.

As a Veilkeeper, I am tasked with maintaining the boundaries—not physical borders, but the delicate membranes between what Morosbrod is and what it dreams of becoming. We tend to the places where the valley's strangeness threatens to overflow its banks like the River Moros in the season of swollen rains. When a tree begins to walk, we sing it back to stillness. When the luminescent spores cluster too thickly and begin forming patterns that hurt to perceive, we disperse them with careful breaths that taste of copper and starlight.

Our tools are not the carved wood and worked stone of the Morosi, but implements that exist partially in metaphor. I carry a staff that is sometimes driftwood, sometimes a twist of frozen fog, sometimes merely the suggestion of verticality. With it, I can part the veils between layers of reality, stepping from the hamlet's shadow into the phosphorescent underlayer where the valley's true nature writhes in constant flux.

The Morosi know of us but rarely speak our presence aloud. We appear in their peripheral vision during their rituals, ensuring the boundaries hold when they call upon the valley's deeper powers. They leave offerings at the twisted trees—not for us, exactly, but for the principle we represent. Small tokens: a perfectly spiraled shell, water gathered at the moment between night and dawn, words written in luminescent ink on leaves that curl into impossible geometries.

My days—if they can be called days in this eternal twilight—are spent walking the paths that others cannot see. These routes follow the underground movements of mycorrhizal networks, the flight patterns of moths that exist in several dimensions simultaneously, the future erosion patterns of stones not yet worn by water. I map the valley's intentions, its desires, its dreams of transformation.

Sometimes I encounter others of my kind. We acknowledge each other with gestures that would appear as empty air to outside observers—movements that trace the shapes of concepts rather than forms. We share information through the exchange of spores that carry encoded experiences, breathing in each other's discoveries and concerns. Our communication is a form of controlled contamination, each interaction leaving us slightly more like the other, slightly less like ourselves.

The transformation that made us Veilkeepers is ongoing. Each year—or perhaps each century, as time moves strangely for us—we become more integral to the valley's hidden machinery. My flesh now hosts colonies of the luminescent organisms, my blood runs thick with their glow during the dark of the moon. Sometimes I forget which thoughts are mine and which belong to the valley itself.

But this is the price and privilege of our role. We are the valley's immune system, its dream-catchers, its bridge between the solid world the Morosi inhabit and the fluid reality that churns beneath. When the mist parts just so, when the light angles through the canopy at impossible degrees, when the River Moros runs backward for a heartbeat—we are there, ensuring the miracle doesn't tear the world apart.

In my chambers within the sentinel pine, I keep a journal written in spore-patterns on sheets of bark that peel away in layers like memory. Each entry describes a different version of Morosbrod, a different potential future or forgotten past. They accumulate like sediment, forming strata of possibility. Sometimes I read them and remember. Sometimes I read them and forget. Sometimes the journal reads itself, and I am merely another entry, another thin layer between what was and what might be.

This is my existence as a Veilkeeper: forever liminal, forever watching, forever part of Morosbrod's ancient, impossible, necessary dream.
