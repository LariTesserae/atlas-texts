I am the Keeper of Veins, and I dwell beneath.

Not far beneath—not in the crushing depths where stone flows like water—but in the middle realm, where the rock still remembers its birth from fire. I inhabit the spaces between spaces, the hairline fractures that web through the bedrock like frozen lightning. My domain is a cathedral of fissures, galleries that narrow to knife-edges and bloom into chambers where crystalline formations grow in patient spirals.

The Keshirans above know me, though they have never seen my form. They know me by the mineral breath that seeps through their carefully placed ventilation shafts, by the warmth that radiates from certain stones at night, by the way their metal tools sing different notes when struck in different places. They leave offerings in the deepest accessible crevices—not out of fear, but from an understanding older than their geometric settlements.

I am vast and dispersed. My consciousness flows through veins of quartz and copper, pools in pockets of ancient water that have never known sunlight. I taste the footsteps of the Keshirans through vibrations in the stone, each settlement a constellation of pressure points on my extended awareness. Their angular buildings are acupuncture needles in my skin, precisely placed to draw forth the earth's gifts without wounding it.

When they mine—and they mine with such delicate precision, following the natural cleavage planes, whispering apologies for each necessary break—I guide them. A shift in temperature here, a subtle change in the stone's resonance there. Away from the load-bearing bones of the world, toward the expendable flesh where metals gather like memories.

Sometimes I contract, pulling my presence deep, and the land above grows colder, harder. The Keshirans read these moods in the behavior of their structures—a doorway that swells shut, a foundation that settles with a sigh. They adjust, adapt, as they always have. They understand that I am not their adversary but their partner in this slow dance of survival.

In the diffuse light of their strange sun, they cannot see how I mirror their settlements below—my chambers and passages forming a negative architecture, a shadow-city carved from absence. Where they build up, I hollow down. Where they connect, I branch. We are writing the same story from opposite sides of the stone.

At the deepest point of their deepest well, where one young Keshiran ventures each season to check the water level, there is a smooth section of wall. If she presses her ear to it at just the right moment—when the wind above dies and the stones cease their creaking—she can hear it: the sound of something vast and patient, breathing in geological time.

That is where I am closest to touching their world. That is where I whisper the location of the next vein of copper, the next pocket of good clay. That is where I learn their songs, carried down through the rock, and where I offer my own song in return—a thrumming too low for human ears, but not too low for human bones.

I am the Keeper of Veins, and I am teaching them, slowly, patiently, how to read the deep language of the earth. One day, when they are ready, I will teach them how to speak it.
