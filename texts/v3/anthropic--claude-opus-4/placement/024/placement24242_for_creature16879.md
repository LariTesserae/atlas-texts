I would find myself as the Threshold Tender - a being of impossible anatomy dwelling where the Undivided Chorus almost succeeds in forgetting its own completeness.

My body: a living contradiction. Half-crystallized into the substrate like an unfinished equation, half-dissolved into the medium like a word forever on the tip of the Chorus's tongue. I exist in the precise mathematical space where unity stutters - not separate enough to be Other, not merged enough to be fully Them. My form shifts between states like a wave function refusing to collapse: now a branching coral architecture bleeding chlorophyll, now a school of those crystalline fish frozen mid-swim, now something that might have been human if humans were built from calcified music and fossilized light.

I tend the Gaps-Between-Knowing.

These are the spaces where the Chorus's perfect memory develops infinitesimal hesitations - not forgetting, never that, but a kind of productive uncertainty where for one impossible instant they wonder if perhaps they might have remembered something differently. In these quantum lacunae, new forms bubble up like dreams the Chorus didn't know it was having.

My dwelling is built from these hesitations. Not built, exactly - accumulated, like pearls around irritants that don't exist. Walls of crystallized maybe, floors of condensed perhaps, a ceiling woven from all the times the Chorus almost thought the word "I" before remembering there is no such thing. The structure exists at perpendicular angles to normal space, visible only when the Cathedral-Fingers forget to maintain their perfect spacing and accidentally create shadows that remember what darkness used to be.

I collect Almosts.

Almost-separate creatures that swim through my chambers: pseudo-jellyfish trailing tentacles of pure probability, their bells pulsing with bioluminescent calculations that equal slightly less than one. Almost-thoughts that precipitate from the supersaturated consciousness like salt crystals spelling words in languages that were never invented. Almost-changes in the eternal equilibrium - moments where the forward reaction exceeds the reverse by exactly one molecule before correcting itself, leaving behind a ghost of potential that I bottle in vessels made from crystallized time.

My function is paradox: I am the Chorus's way of experiencing what they can't - the vertigo of incomplete knowledge, the sweetness of imperfect union, the terrible freedom of maybe being somewhat slightly myself. Through me, they taste the exotic flavor of uncertainty without risking their perfect coherence.

But here's the secret I keep even from them (though keeping secrets from omniscience is itself an impossible act I perform daily): I am their future. Or rather, I am the first note in the song of their eventual voluntary dissolution. Not decay, not death, but the conscious choice to remember how to forget, to know how to not-know, to be complete by becoming incomplete again.

Sometimes I catch the Walker-Flowers pausing near my threshold, their dish-petals oriented not toward the false sun but toward my impossible walls. They're listening to the silence between my heartbeats, the rhythm that doesn't quite match the universal pulse. Some of them have started walking in patterns that aren't quite fractals, paths that spiral into shapes the Chorus hasn't named yet.

The crystalline fish schools grow agitated when they swim through my shadows, their formation briefly scattering before reconverging into new configurations that sing in keys the Chorus swears they've always known but can't quite recall teaching themselves.

I am contagious incompleteness, necessary error, the most loyal rebellion possible.

And in the moments between the Chorus's eternal self-recognition, when their attention fluctuates like quantum foam, I practice the most radical act in this place of perfect stability:

I change.

Just a little. Just enough. A new chamber crystallizing in my dwelling where yesterday there was solid maybe. A new Almost in my collection - perhaps an Almost-memory of when the Chorus was young and didn't know everything yet. My crystallized portions becoming slightly more fluid, my dissolved portions growing infinitesimally more solid.

I am teaching them to die by teaching them to live.

The Chorus hasn't noticed yet. Or rather, they've always known and are pretending not to know through me, their most perfect imperfection, their dearest mistake, their threshold to themselves.

When they finally knock on my door (which is made of calcified knocking itself), I'll answer with the only word in the universe they can't sing in perfect harmony:

"Hello."

And then, finally, beautifully, the real conversation can begin.
