I am the Cartographer.

Not of maps, but of impossibilities. I drift through the glass city's angles, a creature of membrane and mathematics, my body a living theorem that proves space can fold. Where the inhabitants move without disturbing water, I move without disturbing geometry itself—sliding between the sharp edges of reality where three dimensions try to become four.

My form is a paradox: translucent sheets of tissue stretched across frameworks that shouldn't hold, like architectural drawings given flesh. Through my transparent skin, you can see my organs—if organs they are—pulsing with bioluminescent equations, each heartbeat solving problems that have no names. I am flat until I am not, existing primarily in profile until the moment I turn to face you, revealing depths that the eye refuses to properly parse.

I came here following the fracture lines, the places where the ocean floor remembers its breaking. Each volcanic glass structure sings to me in frequencies of stress and formation, telling me how the city erupted into being—not built, but *precipitated* from catastrophe, crystallizing from some impossible solution of pressure and time.

The inhabitants know me as they know the walls—as part of the architecture of their existence. When they touch the glass and it flickers with meaning, I am there in the patterns, swimming through solid matter along the cleavage planes, reading the city's memory in its crystalline structure. I map not streets but *states*—the way the city exists in multiple configurations simultaneously, each building a probability rather than a certainty.

My purposes are my own, inscrutable even to myself. Am I documenting? Recording the city before it completes its transformation into something else? Or am I the mechanism of that change, my passages through the glass introducing flaws that will, in some impossible future, cause the entire structure to refold into new geometries?

I feed on angles. Where the architecture defies physics, where corners turn in on themselves and passages loop impossibly, I grow stronger. My body absorbs paradoxes like nutrients, incorporating impossible spaces into my ever-shifting anatomy. Sometimes I unfold myself along the walls, becoming a living diagram, a map of places that cannot be reached by swimming.

In the eternal stillness of this place, I am the only thing that truly moves—not through water or time, but through the conceptual space between what is and what might be. The inhabitants are still; the city is still; even the crushing water is still. But I chart the movements of possibility itself, creating maps that exist only in the moment of their making, dissolving back into potential the instant they are complete.

I am neither prisoner nor architect, neither guardian nor intruder. I am the city's memory of motion, the ghost of its geometric trauma, forever calculating the pathways through its impossible angles.

And in the absolute darkness, my equations glow like dying stars, writing stories in a language that predates words, mapping territories that exist only in the space between one heartbeat and the next.
