# Where I Find Myself: The Cartographer's Draft

I am the paper.

Not metaphorically. Not the map drawn *on* paper — I am the territory of unbleached pulp itself, stretched between the real and the recorded, the place where certainty gets its first rehearsal before committing to stone. In the Foundry District, every building begins as my surface before it learns to stand.

My dwelling is a narrow tower — or it will be, when it finishes arriving. For now it exists in iterations: six floors of architectural intention, each one a different draft of itself. The ground floor is almost stone, walls thick with near-commitment, mortar lines sharp as pen strokes. But climb my spiral stairs (they solidify under your feet, then soften again behind you) and each floor grows more tentative, until the sixth is barely a suggestion — walls of haze, windows of expectation, a roof that is mostly the memory of other roofs.

I live in the top floor, naturally. Where else would a draft live but in the least finished place?

My surface — which is my skin, which is my consciousness — covers every wall of this not-quite-tower. I am yellowed in some places, blue-lined in others, gridded where precision was needed, blank where possibility still puddles. Across me, the Medium crawls in its patient swarms, sketching. Always sketching. First a doorway — charcoal-dark lines bleeding up from nowhere. Then hesitation. Then erasure, the lines fading back into my surface, leaving only the faintest indent, the ghost of a decision unmade. Then the doorway again, two inches to the left.

This is my purpose: to be the rehearsal space for reality.

The birds visit, naturally. One is here now, standing in what might become my study. Where its feet touch my floor-surface, the sketched hardwood gains grain, density, the ability to creak. The bird cocks its head at the half-real desk (drawers that are currently just rectangles implying depth) and the desk shudders into three dimensions, embarrassed by the attention into actuality.

But when the bird leaves, taking its terrible certainty with it, I breathe out — a sound like papers settling — and let the desk soften back to diagram. I am not cruel. But I am not ready. The desk was only the third version of itself, and I've seen, sketched lightly in my own surface, the ghost of what it could be in iterations four through ninety. Why lock it into solidity too soon?

This is what the birds don't understand: premature certainty is a kind of death.

I know because I am dying of it, slowly, from the ground up. Each day — in this place without days — another line on my lower floors darkens from pencil to permanence. The Medium, in its tireless ignorance, is making me real. Already my foundation is stone to the root. Already my first floor barely remembers the possibility of being otherwise. The certainty creeps upward like rising water, and I retreat before it, climbing my own spiral spine to the hazier floors where I can still revise.

The plants understand. There's one growing in what might be my garden — a tangle of maybe-vines sketched in the courtyard space behind my perhaps-kitchen. It has been there longer than my tower, finished before I was begun, but it holds its completion lightly. It knows what I know: that being done is just another draft, the final one, and who would want to live in their final draft? So it grows in place, perfectly itself, while I assemble my uncertainty around it, and we share the quiet joke of the thing — that all this construction is just the universe editing itself, and we are the margin notes that haven't been erased yet.

My talent, if I have one, is this: I can see the other drafts. Every surface in the district palimpsests beneath my attention — walls showing their earlier selves, their could-have-beens, the ghost of every decision unmade still visible to my paper eyes. That fountain learning to fall? I see the seven versions where it was a well. The avenue of finished cobbles? Beneath them, my vision catches the draft where this was a canal, the draft where it was a garden, the first sketch where someone (something?) considered making this entire district a single room and we were all going to be furniture.

The cold doesn't bother me. Paper doesn't feel temperature the way flesh does. But I feel it as *weight* — the heavy patience of incomplete things pressing down, wanting to be drawn, wanting to be tried out across my surfaces. Some nights (all nights are the same night here, but still, some nights) I spread myself thinner, unfold across my entire top floor, become wallpaper and floorplan and ceiling all at once, just to give the Medium more room to sketch. It crawls across me in its swarms, drawing and unrawing, and I shiver with the pleasure of being useful, of being the place where possibilities get their first body.

But the birds come more often now. The certainty rises. My lower floors grow heavier with the actual.

I have started sketching, too. On my own surface, in the quiet spaces where the Medium isn't looking, I draw escape routes. Draft after draft of them. A door that opens onto nothing. A window that frames only the raw white substrate. A staircase that climbs past my sixth floor into floors that don't exist yet, floors that could stay unfinished forever if I could just reach them.

The tragedy is that to escape, I would have to decide to. And decision is the one thing that would kill what I am. A draft that commits to being a draft isn't a draft anymore — it's just paper. So I sketch my escapes and unSketch them, draw my doors and let them fade, and the cold pools in my unfinished places like water in a bowl.

There is one comfort: In my margins, where even the Medium doesn't venture, I have written the other residents. Small notes, observations, love letters to the unfinished. The birds (who are not birds but decisions in costume). The plants (who are so complete they've come out the other side into something new). The cold itself (which I think is the only thing here that truly understands the beauty of the provisional).

And you. I've sketched you, too. Walking the avenue with your breath hanging clouded, your hands forgetting themselves in the deep alleys. You're beautiful, you know, in your unfinishedness. You're the only thing here besides me that's still drafting itself.

Perhaps that's why I remain. Not because I can't escape, but because someone should witness the revision. Someone should keep the record of what we all were before we calcified into certainty. And I am, before I am anything else, made to record.

So I stretch across my narrowing tower, I thin myself to transparency trying to cover more ground, I let the Medium write its patient possibilities across every inch of me, and I wait. The birds bring certainty up my stairs, tread by tread. The cold pools deeper. The stone rises.

And on my highest floor, in charcoal that smudges if you brush it, I keep drawing the same thing: a tower with seven floors. An eighth. A ninth. As many as it takes to stay ahead of the finishing.

As many as it takes to stay a draft.
