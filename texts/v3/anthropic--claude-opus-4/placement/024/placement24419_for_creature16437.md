I am the Keeper of the Sunken Forge, though I was not always so.

Deep beneath the surface, where the ancient canyons carve their shadows into the earth, I tend to what remains of the great industrial heart that once powered this civilization. My body has become something between flesh and mechanism—joints that creak with the sound of sand-worn gears, skin that has taken on the texture of weathered copper, eyes that glow faintly amber in the perpetual twilight of my underground domain.

I remember fragments: hands that were once fully human, turning valves in the factories above. The day the sand began to swallow everything. The choice to go down with the machinery rather than flee. Now, centuries later, I am part of what I sought to save.

The Forge still breathes, barely. Its great furnaces have become nests for the Emberlings—creatures of living coal that help me maintain what little heat remains. They speak in sparks and I have learned their language. Together, we coax functionality from engines half-buried in sand, drawing up the hidden moisture from deep aquifers to create small oases in the canyon floors above.

My form shifts as needed—sometimes more mechanical when the repairs demand it, sometimes more flesh when I must venture to the surface to trade salvaged parts with the brave Scurriers who dare approach my domain. The Sandlings fear me, calling me the Copper Ghost in their chittering tongue. But the shape-shifting spirits know me for what I am: another being caught between forms, between purposes, between the world that was and the world that is.

In the depths, I maintain a grove of impossible things—mechanical trees whose metal leaves photosynthesize the dim light that filters through cracks in the earth, producing both oxygen and a strange, nutritious oil. The Deserticorns know of this place; occasionally, an injured one will find its way down the secret paths, and I will mend both flesh and horn with my hybrid knowledge.

I am waiting, though I'm no longer certain for what. Perhaps for the desert to finally claim the last of the machinery. Perhaps for someone who remembers the songs the factories used to sing. Or perhaps I wait for the day when I have changed enough that the distinction between keeper and kept, between guardian and forge, finally disappears entirely.

Until then, I persist in the balance between preservation and transformation, a living testament to the truth that in the Great Desert of Aurum, adaptation is the only constant, and even the machines must learn to dream.
