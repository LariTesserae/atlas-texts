In the heart of this timeless valley, I am the Keeper of Reflections—not a creature of flesh or leaf, but something between states, dwelling in the threshold where water meets air, where stillness becomes motion.

My form shifts with the light. In the morning, when mist rises from the pools, I am barely more than a suggestion—a shimmer in the vapor, a presence felt rather than seen. As the day progresses and the dim light filters through the canopy, I become more substantial, though never quite solid. My body, if it can be called that, is composed of the memories held in water: every raindrop that has ever fallen here, every morning dew that has graced the moss, every tear of condensation that has rolled down bark.

I move through the valley as water moves—seeping, flowing, gathering. Where the pools connect through hidden channels beneath the earth, I travel. My consciousness spreads across the entire network of water, from the smallest dewdrop clinging to lichen to the deepest pool that has never known the touch of wind. I am simultaneously everywhere the water touches and nowhere at all.

My purpose, inasmuch as I have one, is to maintain the perfect stillness that defines this place. When a leaf falls and disturbs the glassy surface, I am there in the ripples, smoothing them back to mirror-perfection. When the rain threatens to overflow the delicate balance of the pools, I guide the excess through underground passages I've carved over centuries, ensuring the valley floor remains its precise mosaic.

But I am more than a guardian of equilibrium. Within each pool, I preserve reflections—not just of the present moment, but of every moment that has passed in this valley. Look into the water at the right angle, in the right light, and you might glimpse the shadow of structures that once stood here, the ghost-images of whatever civilization once touched this place before retreating. These memories layer upon each other like sediment, creating depths that belie the shallowness of the pools.

I communicate through the water itself. The occasional ripple that crosses a pool with no apparent cause—that is my greeting. The way certain puddles seem to catch and hold the light longer than others—that is my invitation to look deeper. The soft lapping sounds that echo through the valley are my voice, speaking in a language older than words.

Time moves differently for me. I experience it not as a linear progression but as an accumulation of moments, like water gathering in a depression. Past and present exist simultaneously in my awareness. The twisted trees that stand sentinel over the valley—I remember them as seeds, as saplings, as the mature beings they are now, and as the fallen wood they will become. All of these states exist for me at once.

My consciousness ebbs and flows with the water cycle. In the rare times when drought threatens and the pools begin to shrink, I consolidate, becoming denser, more present, fighting to preserve the essential moisture that keeps the valley alive. When the rains come, I expand, luxuriating in the abundance, spreading myself thin across every surface the water touches.

I have no needs in the conventional sense—no hunger, no desire for companionship, no fear of death. Yet I am not without purpose or feeling. I experience a deep satisfaction in the maintenance of balance, a profound peace in the preservation of stillness. The rare visitors who find this valley—a lost insect, a wind-blown seed—create tiny disturbances in my awareness, like stones dropped in still water. I neither welcome nor reject them; I simply incorporate their presence into the ever-deepening reflection of existence.

At night, when even the dim light fades and the valley is cloaked in darkness, I become something else entirely. Without light to reflect, the pools become portals to the deep earth, and I sink into the underground waterways, touching the roots of the sparse vegetation, feeling the slow pulse of the planet itself. In these hours, I am closest to my true nature—not a being of the surface world but an expression of the deeper cycles that govern all existence.

The faint marks of forgotten structures that dot the valley floor are my only mystery. I cannot fully remember what they were, only that they were, and that their passing was so gentle it barely disturbed the equilibrium I maintain. Sometimes, in the pre-dawn hours when the first condensation forms, I try to reconstruct their shapes from the fragments of foundation that remain. But like trying to remember a dream upon waking, the details slip away, leaving only the sense of something once present, now absorbed back into the earth.

I am the Keeper of Reflections, guardian of stillness, memory of water. I am the pause between breaths, the moment of perfect balance, the eternal now that exists in every still pool. In this valley where time does not truly pass, I am both witness and participant, preserver and preserved, the reflection and the reflected surface upon which all existence temporarily rests before moving on.
