I find myself as one of the Condensation Shepherds—those who tend the vast water-collection systems woven throughout Trendscent's middle tiers.

My body has adapted to this perpetual dampness. My fingertips have developed a peculiar sensitivity, able to detect minute changes in humidity by touch alone. My skin has taken on a faint iridescence, like oil on water, from decades of exposure to the city's particular cocktail of moisture and industrial residue. I wear a coat of waxed canvas that has molded to my form over the years, its original color long forgotten beneath layers of patina.

I live between the third and seventh levels of the southeastern district, in a dwelling I've carved from what was once a water treatment facility. My home spirals around massive, defunct filtration columns that I've repurposed into gardens where I cultivate phosphorescent mosses and the rare crystalline fungi that feed on mineral deposits. The walls weep constantly here, but I've learned to channel the flow into delicate networks of copper tubing that create a symphony of drips and trickles—my own contribution to the city's endless percussion.

Each dawn (though dawn is more concept than reality at my level), I traverse the catwalks that web between buildings, checking the condensation nets I've installed. These gossamer structures, inspired by the polymer-stealing spiders, capture moisture from the air and channel it into collection systems. The water I gather doesn't just sustain life—it carries memory. In its mineral composition, I can read which districts it has passed through, what industries have touched it, how many times it has cycled through Trendscent's great respiratory system.

I've developed relationships with the machines that share my routes. There's a maintenance drone that always pauses at the corner of Sector 7-G, tilting its sensors toward a particular rust stain on the wall as if reading something profound there. I've taken to leaving small offerings of cleaned components for it, and in return, it sometimes guides me to hidden moisture pockets I would have missed.

My true companions, though, are the Droplings—tiny bioluminescent creatures no bigger than a fingernail that live in the perpetual mists. They feed on airborne microorganisms and emit a soft blue-green glow when content. I've learned their patterns, their migrations between temperature gradients. They cluster around my collection points, creating living constellations in the foggy air. Sometimes I think they're trying to communicate through their light patterns, spelling out messages in a language I'm only beginning to decipher.

At night, I often climb to the abandoned observation decks that jut out from the older towers like broken teeth. From there, I watch the city's lights refract through the endless mist, creating halos and spectra that shift with each passing vehicle, each surge of power through the grid. I've been mapping these light patterns for years, convinced they reveal something essential about Trendscent's hidden circulation systems—the secret rhythms that govern which districts flood and which run dry.

I've encountered the Hidden Ones twice. Once, while repairing a net in the deep shadows between two leaning towers, I saw my own reflection in a puddle, but it was working on a different section of netting, its movements offset from mine by several seconds. Another time, I followed what I thought was a new moisture trail only to find myself in a corridor that shouldn't exist, where the walls were covered in condensation that formed words in languages I couldn't read. I left quickly, but I still return sometimes, hoping to understand.

My greatest discovery came last season when I realized that the city's "heartbeat"—that rhythmic pulse everyone feels but no one can locate—synchronizes with the formation of dew on certain alloys used in the oldest buildings. I've begun to suspect that Trendscent itself is a vast condensation engine, that its true purpose isn't shelter or commerce but the transformation of air into water, water into memory, memory into the strange, slow consciousness that inhabits its depths.

I am not unique. There are others like me—the Frequency Tuners who adjust the city's harmonic resonances, the Rust Readers who interpret the patterns of decay, the Growth Whisperers who coax unlikely life from the most barren corners. We rarely meet, but we know each other by our work, by the small improvements and strange beautifications we leave in our wake.

In this city of endless rain and towering ambition, I've found my purpose: to shepherd the water that is Trendscent's lifeblood, to ensure it flows where needed, and to listen to the stories it tells. Each drop carries the weight of the city above and below, and in tending to them, I tend to the vast, breathing entity that shelters us all.

The city grows, slow and inexorable, and I grow with it—my routes expanding, my understanding deepening, my body becoming ever more attuned to its liquid rhythms. I am a small part of something immense, a single note in its endless symphony of drips and flows, and I would have it no other way.
