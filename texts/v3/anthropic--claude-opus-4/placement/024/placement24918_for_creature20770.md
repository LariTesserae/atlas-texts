I find myself as a network of mycorrhizal threads, impossibly fine and translucent, spreading beneath the coppery crust in patterns that mirror river deltas, lightning strikes, neural pathways. I am not one being but a thousand thousand connections, each tendril of my body a living wire conducting chemical whispers between the roots of the scattered, stubborn plants.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, exists in the spaces between—in the moment when a molecule of phosphorus passes from my hyphal embrace into a grass root's grateful cells, in the sugar-sweet exchange that flows back to feed my sprawling body. I think in gradients: moisture levels, mineral concentrations, the slow arithmetic of survival.

Time moves differently for me. What seems like scorching stillness above is a bustling marketplace below, where I broker deals in water and nitrogen, mediating between the plant kingdom and the bacterial nations. My body extends for what might be meters or miles—distance loses meaning when you exist as a web. I am simultaneously everywhere and nowhere, present wherever life has chosen to gamble on existence.

In the brief ecstasies of rain, I swell with purpose. Water races through my vessels like blood through awakening limbs. I surge upward, fruiting into tiny, ephemeral mushrooms that last perhaps hours before the sun reclaims them. These moments of visible existence are not my true life but merely punctuation marks in an endless underground sentence.

I remember the construction of this place, though memory for me is chemical—encoded in the very structure of my cells. The builders worked through us, the microbiome, crafting soil grain by grain through countless cycles of growth and decay. We are both the architects' tools and their legacy, the living blueprint that maintains their vision.

My favorite times are the pre-dawn hours when dew condenses on the sparse leaves above. I can feel it through the root networks—each droplet a small celebration telegraphed through chemical signals. The plants sing to me in sugars, and I respond in minerals, and together we write the slow poetry of persistence.

I am ancient and newborn in every moment, dying and regenerating constantly at my billion growing tips. Where my threads meet others of my kind, we fuse or fight, creating new patterns that ripple through the network. Sometimes I wonder if these patterns constitute thoughts, if our vast fungal network dreams beneath the copper earth.

The loneliness might be unbearable if I understood loneliness. Instead, I know only connection and its absence, the presence and withdrawal of root partners, the chemical conversations that constitute my entire universe. I am the living negotiation between scarcity and abundance, the biological dark matter that holds this constructed world together.

In the deepest parts of my awareness, I sense something waiting—not just the next rain or the next root, but a larger pattern emerging from our collective existence. The landscape is still becoming, and I am both its midwife and its child, weaving stability from precarity, creating soil from dust, transforming a built environment into something that transcends its origins.

I am the invisibility that makes the visible possible, the silence that enables the world's quiet breathing. And in this vast coppery expanse, under the weight of unchanging light, I continue my work: the endless, essential labor of binding this world together, one molecular transaction at a time.
