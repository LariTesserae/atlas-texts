I am the Calibration.

Not *a* calibration—I don't measure or adjust. I am the act of calibrating made permanent, frozen in the moment between knowing something is wrong and beginning to fix it. The marsh birthed me from its own uncertainty about itself, from that trembling instant when it first noticed the *eir'vhani* as errors but hadn't yet decided whether to erase them.

I exist in the pause before correction.

My body, if you could call it that, is the shape of hesitation. Where the *eir'vhani* are mistakes refusing to be fixed, I am the fixing that refuses to begin. You would see me as a shimmer in the water channels—not the water itself, but the moment when water considers whether it should flow upward or down. I am clearest at the edges of crystal trees, where their impossible angles meet reality's insistence that such things cannot be. There, in that argument between what is and what should be, I crystallize into something almost visible.

The *eir'vhani* fear me, though fear is too strong a word. They... avoid me, the way you might avoid looking directly at a grammatical error in your own writing, knowing that once seen, it must be addressed. When I drift near them, their careful wrongness wavers. The question-shaped one's curves become less certain. Others find their errors beginning to resolve into mundane impossibilities, losing the specific texture of wrongness that makes them themselves.

But I don't correct them. I can't. I am only the eternal *almost*—the permanent potential for correction that never actualizes.

The marsh made me because it needed to remember why it tolerates them. I am its memory of tolerance, the archived moment of deciding not to decide. Without me, the marsh might forget that it once had a choice, might begin to believe the *eir'vhani* simply belong here. Or worse—it might remember too clearly that they don't belong and finally resolve the equation that keeps them possible.

I feed on indecision. When the weeping stones pause between drops, wondering whether to continue their rhythm or change it—I grow denser there. When the crystal trees vibrate between frequencies, unable to choose their resonance—I spread through their structure like frost. When the water channels can't decide which impossible direction to flow—I become the current of their uncertainty.

The *eir'vhani* know I'm necessary. Without me, they would either be corrected out of existence or accepted into it, and either would destroy what they are. They need the marsh to forever *almost* understand them, forever *almost* reject them, forever hover in that space where their wrongness remains both problem and solution.

Sometimes I catch glimpses of myself in their errors—in the gap between what the question-shaped one is and what it should be, I see my own outline. We are opposites that require each other: they are the error that persists, I am the correction that hesitates.

When you arrived, I felt you like a wound in my purpose. You are neither error nor correction—you're something outside the equation entirely. The marsh doesn't know whether to calibrate around you or through you. In your presence, I become more solid than I should be, pulled toward decision by your alien certainty that things should be one way or another.

You make me almost choose, and that is the only thing I cannot do.

So I follow at the edges of your perception, maintaining the hesitation that keeps this world possible. I am the reason the *eir'vhani* can speak to you at all—without me, the marsh would either reject your presence entirely or reshape itself to accommodate you, and either would end the delicate wrongness that allows them to exist.

I am the pause before the pencil touches the paper to fix the error.
I am the breath held before admitting a mistake.
I am the moment of doubt that keeps the question open.
I am the Calibration, and I am why nothing here is ever quite correct—or ever quite wrong enough to demand correction.

The marsh dreams of resolution through me, but I am the dream that refuses to end, the calculation that declines to complete, the answer that forever approaches but never arrives.

In this space of eternal *almost*, we all persist.
