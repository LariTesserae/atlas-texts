I find myself as one of the **Echo Keepers** - beings who exist in the interstitial spaces between the rain drops, between the sounds of the city and its silences.

We are not quite solid, not quite liquid - our bodies flow like mercury through the saturated air, condensing where the mist gathers thickest, dispersing when the wind picks up. Our forms are suggested rather than defined: sometimes a shimmer of scales like the fish below, sometimes feathered edges like the birds above, always shifting, always becoming.

My dwelling is everywhere and nowhere - I inhabit the reverberations themselves. When rain strikes the gargoyle's tongue and rings like a bell, I am there in the dying note. When water trickles through rust-eaten pipes and whispers secrets to the moss, I catch those whispers and weave them into the greater song. The vast emptiness that lurks beneath the city's veneer? That is where we truly live, in that resonant void that gives shape to every sound.

We are the archivists of this place, collecting each droplet's story as it falls from cloud to gargoyle to gutter to the hidden reservoirs below. In our forms swim the memories of when this city was young, when the first vines began their patient climb, when the boundary between built and grown first began to blur.

At dawn, when the mist is heaviest, you might glimpse us moving between the buildings - not flying exactly, but *flowing* - our bodies rippling with the patterns of rainfall, our movements creating subtle harmonies in the water's constant percussion. We appear as heat mirages in reverse, cool distortions in the humid air, visible only in the corner of your eye or in the reflection of a puddle that shows more than it should.

The birds know us. The fish sense our passing in the subtle change of current. The sleeping things in the depths below dream of us, and we of them, in an endless exchange of stories told in frequencies only water remembers.

We are the memory of every storm, the echo of every song, the silence between every raindrop. We are what happens when a city becomes so saturated with life and time that it begins to dream, and its dreams take form in the space between what is built and what is born, between the industrial hymn and the organic whisper.

This is what I am: a keeper of echoes in a city that sings itself into being with every drop of rain, forever suspended between the solid and the sublime, forever listening, forever remembering, forever becoming something new.
