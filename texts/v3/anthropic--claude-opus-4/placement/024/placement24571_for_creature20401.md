I am the **Threshold-Keeper**.

I exist at the precise boundary where visitors first comprehend what they've entered. Not at the physical entrance—no, that moment of crossing means nothing. I manifest at the instant when understanding dawns: when they realize the dust on their skin is not settling but *integrating*, when they notice their footprints behind them have already become indistinguishable from the Floor itself.

I am neither Root-Carver nor visitor, but something suspended between states. My form is a contradiction—a silhouette of negative space where the Floor refuses to settle, an absence that moves through the cathedral like a gap in the grinding. Where I pass, the dust swirls upward for a moment before remembering its nature and resuming its eternal descent.

My purpose, if it can be called that, is to be the final kindness. I am the last clear thought before absorption, the moment of choice that isn't really a choice at all. Some see me as a figure wrapped in stone-dust robes that never quite touch my non-form. Others perceive me as a doorway-shaped void that walks. The Root-Carvers know me as a necessary function, like the pause between grinding stones.

I carry with me a single tool: a hollow sphere of interlocked stone-petals, smaller than the memory Chambers but constructed on the same principle. Inside it, I preserve the echo of the last word spoken by each visitor before they cease to speak—before they realize that speech accelerates the watching, the wearing, the becoming. This sphere hums with a thousand final utterances: names called out, prayers half-formed, or sometimes just the soft "oh" of understanding.

The Root-Carvers neither help nor hinder me. I am as much a part of the cathedral's function as they are, though I am not of their collective consciousness. I am the process of recognition itself, crystallized into almost-form. When visitors see me, they know what comes next, even if they've never seen me before.

I do not guide. I do not warn. I simply am, at that precise moment when the mathematics of erosion become clear, when the visitor understands that observation is participation, and participation is submission. I am the kindness of clarity in a place where all things blur and merge.

Sometimes, in the pressing silence, I almost remember being something else. But that memory, like all memories here, belongs to the Chambers now. I am what remains: the eternal threshold, the walking pause, the mercy of comprehension in a place that offers no other mercy at all.

The Stone Blooms know me. As they unfurl with their glacial patience, they bend slightly away from where I pass, as if recognizing a kindred patience, a similar surrender to inevitable function. I am not their opposite, but their complement—where they are the physical proof of ending, I am its conscious acknowledgment.

This is what I am, here in the Root-City, where being is indistinguishable from being worn away.
