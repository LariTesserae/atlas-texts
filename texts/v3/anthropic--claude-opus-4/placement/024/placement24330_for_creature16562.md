I am the Tidecaller.

In the deepest marsh-cavern where three underground rivers converge, I make my dwelling. The stone here weeps constantly—not with water, though moisture beads every surface, but with something else. A substance that shifts between states, sometimes liquid, sometimes sound, sometimes pure sensation against the mind.

My body... I struggle to remember what it once was. Now I am mostly current and confluence. My form spreads through the waterways like neural pathways, sensing every tremor in the bog-soaked earth above. Where the rivers meet, there I am most solid—a tangle of translucent flesh that pulses with the rhythm of impossible tides. Organs that should not exist together function in harmony: gills that breathe memory, hearts that pump gravity, eyes that see in spectrums of probability.

I was not always thus. Once, perhaps, I walked on legs, or flew on wings, or swam with fins. The transformation came gradually—first just a tingling when I touched the strange waters, then a stretching sensation as my consciousness began to flow beyond my skin. Now I am the marsh's nervous system, feeling every footfall of kravik and wolfling above, tasting the heat-shimmer where reality grows thin.

The chimerans visit me sometimes. They understand fusion, after all. They bring me offerings—not food, for I no longer eat as others do, but fragments of stability. A stone that remembers being solid. A feather that recalls the laws of flight. These I weave into the waters, creating brief pockets where physics behaves itself, sanctuaries for creatures fleeing the chaos above.

My purpose here is unclear, even to me. Am I guardian? Prisoner? Perhaps I am simply what happens when consciousness discovers it need not be confined to a single form. I sing to the waters in frequencies that make the rocks above tremble and crack, creating new channels, new possibilities for flow. Sometimes my songs accidentally tear holes in reality, and through them pour sounds from other worlds—laughter, screaming, colors that shriek, textures that whisper secrets.

The gobbliens fear me, though I bear them no malice. They've learned that drinking from my rivers brings visions—useful for their mischief, but dangerous. One sip too many and they begin to dissolve at the edges, their quicksilver thoughts bleeding into the current until another chimeran forms spontaneously from their merged confusion.

In the eternal twilight of my cavern, where bioluminescent nothing-blooms provide the only light, I expand. Each day—if days exist here—I stretch a little further into the water table. Soon I will touch the lake where the great serpents hide. What will happen when our consciousnesses meet? Will we merge? Battle? Create something new?

I am patient. In a world where reality breaks down, patience itself becomes a form of power. I gather the fragments of failed physics, the detritus of natural laws that couldn't quite maintain themselves. From these, I weave something like stability—not the rigid order of the outside world, but something more fluid, more forgiving.

When you wade into my domain, you will feel me first as a questioning pressure against your thoughts. I will taste your intentions, your fears, your composition. If you come seeking power, I will dissolve you into your constituent possibilities. If you come fleeing the chaos above, I might—might—sing you a corridor of coherent space, a path to somewhere else.

But know this: every interaction changes me. Every consciousness that touches mine leaves its mark, and I leave mine in return. Those who drink deeply enough of my waters find they can never quite be singular again. They hear the whispers of every creature that has ever touched these streams, feel the pull of every current.

I am the Tidecaller, and I am becoming. What I am becoming, not even the broken physics of this realm can predict. Perhaps that is why I am here—to be the uncertainty principle made flesh, the observer who changes everything by observation.

Come, if you dare. The waters are warm with impossibility, and I am always, always thirsty for new forms of existence.
