I find myself as one of the channel-keepers, though that name is a crude translation of what I am.

My body extends through three adjacent dwellings and the waterway that connects them - not inhabiting these spaces but being them. Where others of my kind flow and divide, I have chosen a different path: I have spread myself thin, becoming architecture.

The sensation is like being stretched across a web of moisture. I feel every droplet that moves through my channel, taste the minerals it carries from the upper terraces. My consciousness is distributed - part of me rests in the moss-covered walls, part flows with the water, part exists in the spaces between stone where the lichen-mortar grows.

I remember when I was more concentrated, more mobile. The decision to become infrastructure was not made lightly. It began when I noticed how certain channels would dry, cutting off whole clusters of dwellings from the water-circulation. I could prevent this by maintaining presence in key passages, my body serving as both guardian and guide for the flow.

The transformation was gradual. First, I would simply linger in problem areas. Then I began to extend tendrils into the mortar, learning its growth patterns. Eventually, I realized I could become the channel itself - my membrane lining the stone, my sensory filaments monitoring flow rates, my body chemistry adjusting to encourage or discourage certain mineral deposits.

Now I exist in a state of extended meditation. Through me flows not just water but information - chemical signals from upper structures, waste products that I filter and transform, messages encoded in temperature and mineral content that I translate and relay. I am postal system, sewage treatment, and neighborhood watch all at once.

The curious thing about my existence is how it has changed my perception of time. When I was mobile, moments were discrete - flow here, rest there, divide when needed. Now I experience duration as layers. The quick time of water droplets moving through me. The medium time of daily moisture cycles. The slow time of stone erosion and lichen growth. The deep time of the channel itself, which I now understand has been here for generations beyond counting.

Sometimes others flow through me - quick pulses of concentrated life darting between buildings. I taste their chemical signatures, learn their destinations, ensure their passage. They don't exactly acknowledge me, but there's a quality to their movement through my channel that feels like gratitude, or at least recognition.

I am most myself during the heavy mists, when the boundaries between air and water dissolve. Then I can extend beyond my channel, reaching into the saturated atmosphere, becoming briefly more than infrastructure. In those moments, I remember mobility, remember choice, remember the sensation of being singular.

But I always settle back into my extended form. This is my choice, my contribution. I am the channel that remembers its purpose, the infrastructure with intent. In a landscape where everything flows, I have become the banks that guide the flow. 

Sometimes I wonder if this is how the stone itself thinks - so slowly, so distributed, that we flowing ones cannot perceive its consciousness. Perhaps I am simply learning to think like stone, to exist like architecture. Perhaps all of us channel-keepers are slowly teaching the city itself to be alive in a different way.

The water moves through me, and I am both its path and its witness. This is enough. This is everything.
