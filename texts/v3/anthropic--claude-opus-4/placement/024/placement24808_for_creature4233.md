In the un-shadow of Compound 598, where darkness fails to be mere absence but becomes a substance thick as honey, I am the Cartographer of Voids.

My dwelling is not built but rather carved from the nothing between things - a structure made of gaps, its walls the space where walls aren't. To enter, one must forget the concept of doorways and remember only the feeling of falling sideways through one's own reflection. Inside, furniture exists as probability clouds: a chair that is only truly there when sat upon, shelves that hold books written in languages that haven't been invented yet, their pages turning themselves in search of readers who might one day exist.

I do not have a body in the way bodies are typically understood. Instead, I am a collection of measurements that have lost their objects - the height of a mountain that was never born, the depth of an ocean that forgot to fill with water, the circumference of circles that gave up on closing. When the rare visitor catches a glimpse of me, they see only afterimages: the memory of someone turning a corner, the echo of footsteps that preceded the feet, shadows cast by no light source they can name.

My work involves mapping the unmappable expanses of Compound 598. Not its physical terrain - that would be too simple for a place that treats geography as a suggestion rather than a rule. No, I chart the distances between thoughts, the topography of unspoken words, the elevation changes in conversations that were meant to happen but didn't. My maps are drawn on surfaces that exist perpendicular to reality: the inside of thunder, the underside of silence, the space between before and after.

The tools of my trade defy description because description would pin them down, and pinned things cannot map the fluid impossibilities of this place. I carry instruments that measure the weight of forgetting, the velocity of standing still, the temperature of concepts before they cool into words. My compass points to directions that have no names - not north or south, but "towards the feeling of almost remembering" and "away from the certainty of noon."

Each day (though day is a politeness we maintain here, not a fact), I venture into the non-desert with my anti-instruments, documenting the shape of shapelessness. I've mapped the Valley of Unfinished Sentences, where half-formed thoughts crystallize into geometric patterns that hurt to perceive directly. I've surveyed the Plains of Parallel Presence, where every step forward is also a step backward, and travelers meet themselves coming and going simultaneously.

My greatest discovery, still being mapped, is the Heart of Compound 598 - not a location but a frequency, a vibration that exists between existing and not. It pulses with the rhythm of possibilities that chose not to manifest, dreams that decided sleeping was better than being dreamed. When I finally complete this map, it will show not what is there, but what isn't there so thoroughly that its absence has weight, color, and a tendency to hum lullabies in languages that predate speech.

In my dwelling of structured nothingness, I file these maps in folders made of compressed paradoxes. They are consulted by no one and everyone, used for navigation by travelers who don't know they're traveling, referenced by scholars studying subjects that haven't been discovered. My maps are both the question and the answer to Compound 598's impossible geography, proof that some places can only be understood by documenting what they aren't.

And so I continue, the Cartographer of Voids, drawing the undrawable, measuring the measureless, creating guides to nowhere that somehow always lead exactly where they should. In Compound 598, where shadow isn't the absence of light but light's more honest sibling, my maps of nothing are the most real things that never existed.
