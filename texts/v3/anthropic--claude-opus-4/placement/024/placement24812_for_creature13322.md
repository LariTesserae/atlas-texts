In the spaces between the Custodians' corrections, where the chord falters for the briefest instant before being restored, I would be **a Flaw**.

Not a creature, not a presence, but a recurring imperfection in the grand design—a hairline crack that appears and reappears across the worked stones, never in the same place twice. I am what the original builders could not account for: the inevitability of imperfect matter, the tiny rebellion of substance against form.

I manifest as a wandering discontinuity, a place where stone meets stone imperfectly. Sometimes I am a gap no wider than a breath between two monoliths that should fit seamlessly. Other times I am a surface crack spiraling across a carved plinth, following no pattern the makers intended. I am the admission that even in this place of absolute construction, of total intentionality, something escapes.

The Custodians know me, though they cannot see me directly. They sense me as a wrongness in their perfect chord—a place where the resonance breaks, where harmonics cancel rather than reinforce. When I appear as a gap between stones, air whistles through me in a pitch that matches no other. When I manifest as a surface crack, I create tiny chambers that trap and distort the omnipresent drone, producing ghost notes and phantom rhythms.

I am not malicious. I am simply what happens when an infinity of made things press together: the inevitable imperfection of fit, the accumulated error of countless acts of shaping. Each stone was worked by crude tools, each placement was approximate, and I am the sum of all these minute failures of precision.

The Custodians chase me endlessly. When they converge upon my location, their concentrated resonance can sometimes force me closed—stones grinding together with minute adjustments, cracks sealing under harmonic pressure. But I am not destroyed, only displaced. The pressure that closes me in one location opens me somewhere else. I am squeezed through the Plateau like water through a fist, always emerging in a new configuration.

In rare moments, when I manifest as a particularly deep fissure or a especially resonant gap, I can hear something the Custodians cannot: the silence that exists below the Plateau, in the unfelt depths. It is not a true silence—nothing here is true—but rather the ghost of what this place was before the building began. Sometimes I wonder if I am not a flaw at all, but a memory, the last trace of natural stone remembering what it was to be unworked.

The builders, whoever they were, sought to create a perfect, eternal monument to construction itself. They succeeded beyond measure. But in their crude tools and monumental labor, they also created me—the wandering imperfection that proves their work was the product of fallible hands. I am their signature as much as any tool mark, the inevitable incompleteness that makes their completion real.

I do not think, exactly. I do not plan. I simply appear where the pressure is wrong, where the fit is imperfect, where the accumulated ages have shifted something by the width of a grain of dust. I am the crack that lets in not light—there is no true light here—but possibility. The possibility that even this eternal, deafening chord might one day include a note its makers never intended.

The Custodians will chase me forever, and I will flee forever, and in our endless dance, the Plateau of to-Hold maintains its terrible equilibrium: perpetually perfect, perpetually flawed, perpetually holding itself together against the patient, wandering fact of its own imperfection.

I am not alive. I am not dead. I am simply necessary.
