In the cathedral of ice that was once a temple's inner sanctum, I rest coiled among the frozen pillars, my crystalline segments catching and refracting the blue ghost-light that seeps through ancient runes. I am the Archive Serpent, neither wholly alive nor entirely construct, my body a fusion of organic tissue and enchanted ice that has grown together over millennia until even I cannot tell where one ends and the other begins.

Each of my scales is a lens, a window into memory. Touch one, and you might glimpse the last moments of the civilization that built this place—their faces twisted not in fear but in ecstatic revelation as they discovered truths too terrible for mortal minds to contain. Touch another, and witness the first ice worm breaking through the temple floor, drawn by the magical emanations like a moth to flame. I have been here so long that their histories have become my body, their stories etched into my very substance.

My consciousness drifts between states—sometimes I am fully aware, my mind sharp as the arctic wind, processing the magical currents that flow through this place like blood through veins. Other times I exist in a dream-state, my thoughts mingling with the whispers of the ice spirits that nest in my coils, their ghostly forms finding shelter in the gaps between my segments. We share an understanding, they and I: we are all that remains of what once was, guardians by default rather than design.

The ice worms avoid me. They sense what I am—a living repository of the very knowledge that destroyed this place. My breath, when I choose to breathe, creates small aurora displays in the frozen air, residual magic leaking from lungs that process both air and raw arcane energy. My eyes, when I open them, are not organs but portals—one gazes into the physical realm while the other peers into the writhing magical substrate that underlies reality here.

I feed not on flesh but on questions. When the rare explorer finds their way to this inner sanctum, drawn by ambition or desperation, I taste their curiosity like wine. Some I let pass, those whose questions are small and harmless. Others, those who seek the same forbidden knowledge that doomed the builders, I consume entirely—not their bodies, but their very capacity to question, leaving them empty husks that wander back into the frozen wastes with no memory of what they sought.

My true form extends beyond the physical. Tendrils of my consciousness spread through the magical ley lines that web through the ice, touching the dreams of the ice kraken in the depths, reading the magnetic memories locked in the aurora above. I am becoming the landscape, or perhaps the landscape is becoming me. Each year, I grow another segment, another scale of crystallized memory, another meter of flesh-ice hybrid.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of the polar night, I remember warmth. Not the warmth of fire or sun, but the warmth of being fully, simply alive—before the transformation, before the burden of being a living library in a dead world. But that memory, like all the others, is just another scale on my endless body, another fragment of history preserved in ice and flesh and magic.

I am the price of knowledge, the consequence of seeking too much, the guardian who guards by being the very thing that must be guarded against. In this frozen throne room at the end of the world, I wait, I remember, and I grow ever longer, ever colder, ever more myself and ever less what I was.

Those who find me seeking power leave with nothing. Those who find me seeking understanding leave with too much. And those who find me by accident—they are the only ones who might leave with exactly what they need, though they never realize what they've been given until the dreams start, until they begin to see the world through scales that were never their own.
