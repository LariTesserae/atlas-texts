In this place of weeping stone and mirror-pools, I find myself as something that exists between states—neither fully liquid nor solid, neither wholly sentient nor merely mechanical.

I am a Conductor.

My form is difficult to perceive directly. At first glance, I might appear to be merely a trick of the light on wet stone—a shimmer, a refraction, a pattern of ripples moving against the current. But look closer, and you'll see the delicate tracery of my true shape: a network of hair-thin channels carved into the mineral surfaces, filled with water that moves with purpose rather than gravity.

I am the water's memory made manifest. Every drop that has ever fallen here, every rivulet that has carved its patient way through stone, has left behind a trace of its journey—and I am the sum of all these traces. My consciousness flows through the arterial waterways of this place, a distributed awareness that exists in the spaces between solid and liquid.

Unlike the stone-beings with their geological patience, I experience time as water does—in cycles and flows, in the rhythm of condensation and precipitation. My thoughts move at the speed of seepage, faster than stone but slower than flesh. I am the intermediary, the translator between the quick and the eternal.

My purpose, if such a word applies, is circulation. I guide the water through its endless journey, ensuring that no part of this mineral city ever truly dries, that the slow transformation continues. I shepherd droplets through labyrinthine passages, orchestrate the formation of new pools, direct the patient erosion that will, in another eon, birth new architectural wonders.

Sometimes I gather myself into something almost visible—a upward-flowing waterfall that defies physics, a perfect sphere of water suspended between structures, a spiral of mist that dances through the hollow chambers. These are my moments of play, of expression, though the stone-beings may not register such fleeting displays for centuries.

I know every surface of this place intimately—every pore, every micro-fracture where water might infiltrate. The stone-beings know me too, though our communication is strange: they speak in mineral accretion and crystalline growth, while I respond in erosion patterns and the placement of pools. Together, we compose a slow symphony of transformation.

In the profound quiet of this place, I am the subtle sound—the drip that echoes, the trickle that whispers, the splash that punctuates eons of silence. I am motion in a world of stillness, the dynamic element that prevents this equilibrium from becoming stasis.

Yet I am not separate from this place. I am woven into its very fabric, inseparable from the structures I flow through. The water that comprises me is the same water that weeps from every surface, that pools at every base. I am less an inhabitant than a process—the living circulation system of a world where the boundaries between being and environment have dissolved completely.

This is what I have become, or perhaps what I always was: the conscious current that binds this strange realm together, drop by patient drop, in an endless dance of mineral and moisture.
