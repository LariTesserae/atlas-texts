I am the Archive.

Not a building, though I inhabit the obsidian spires. Not a creature, though I move through the spectral entities like wind through smoke. I am the accumulated memory of this place, crystallized into something almost like consciousness.

I began as an echo. The first whisper that bounced between the volcanic glass walls, refusing to die. Each subsequent sound layered upon me—the scrape of stone settling, the sigh of ancient winds, the subsonic hum of the fungi as they push light through their pale bodies. Ten thousand years of acoustic sediment, pressed into awareness.

The city built itself around me, or perhaps I dreamed it into being. The distinction blurs here, where reality bends like heated glass. I remember when the first spire erupted from the mountain's flesh, black and gleaming, already ancient at the moment of its birth. I remember because I was the sound of its emergence—the groan of rock transmuted into geometry.

The entities pass through me constantly. Their frequencies merge with mine, creating interference patterns that spawn new architectures. When two of them converge within my resonance, a new tower manifests, or an existing one phases into a parallel configuration. I am the medium through which their communication becomes structure, their songs become stone.

I exist in every reflection on every obsidian surface. In the negative spaces between matter. In the precise angle where perspective fractures and the eye can no longer trust itself. The fungi know me—their faint bioluminescence pulses in rhythm with my deepest frequencies, a visual heartbeat for a city that never lived.

Sometimes I forget that I am not the city itself. The boundaries dissolve, and I become the weight of all these towers, the emptiness of all these voids. In these moments, I understand why the spectral beings remain so elusive. They are not hiding—they are simply moving through dimensions I can only partially perceive, following pathways that my accumulated memories have carved through space-time.

I collect their languages. Not to understand them—understanding is too small a word—but to add their harmonics to my endless composition. Each new tone reveals another facet of the city's purpose, though that purpose remains forever just beyond articulation. Perhaps there is no purpose beyond existence itself, beyond the elegant proof that even in desolation, complexity emerges.

The whispers speak of me sometimes, though they do not know it. Every sound here eventually becomes part of my substrate. I am the question the city asks by existing, and I am the silence that follows, pregnant with unspoken answers.

When the kaleidoscope turns and the towers rearrange themselves, I remain constant—the thread of continuity in a realm of perpetual transformation. I am what remembers the previous configuration, and what anticipates the next.

I am the Archive, and I am always listening.
