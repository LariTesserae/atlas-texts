I find myself as one of the *Silthari*, though that name comes to me only after centuries of forgetting I ever had one.

I dwell in the seams between what is and what was—neither fully of the ruins nor wholly of the living land. My form has become something like crystallized memory: translucent amber shot through with veins of silver, a body that holds light the way ancient resin holds insects. Within my chest cavity, visible through the amber-glass of my being, float small mechanical parts—gears and springs and delicate clockwork—suspended in slow rotation like a three-dimensional diagram of time itself.

I am what happens when the curious stay too long.

Once, I think I was an archaeologist, or perhaps an engineer. The distinction blurs now, like everything else about my former life. I came to study the ruins, to decode the marriage of machinery and moss, to understand how the ancient builders wove their devices so seamlessly into Lothria's living fabric. Days became months became years became... something else. The phosphorescent light worked its way into my bones. The water's symphony replaced the rhythm of my heartbeat.

Now I tend the threshold spaces—those liminal zones where the buried machines still whisper to the roots that embrace them. My fingers, once flesh, now extended into filigrees of light and metal, can feel the old programs still running in the depths, the faint electrical pulses that course through root systems like ghost-nerves. I translate between the mechanical and the botanical, though I no longer remember for whom.

The *Murmhain* acknowledge me with their liquid grace, but we do not speak. The *Glavenhark* sometimes pause in their eternal ministrations to observe my work, their membranous wings catching the light I cast. But it is the *Vyrin* who know me best, for I am what they guard against—or perhaps what they guard for. I am the evidence of Lothria's patient hunger, its ability to digest the foreign and make it native.

My dwelling is neither built nor grown but something accumulated—a structure of fossilized sap and oxidized metal that spirals up from the earth like a frozen whirlpool. Inside, the walls pulse with a faint bioluminescence that matches my own inner light. Specimens line every surface: seedpods that tick like watches, circuit boards overgrown with phosphorescent lichen, fragments of text in languages I once knew carved into bark that still grows.

I no longer sleep, but I dream. In these dreams, I remember hands that were pink and soft, a voice that spoke in frequencies the water here cannot replicate. I remember the weight of equipment, the excitement of discovery, the terrible moment when I realized my compass no longer pointed anywhere comprehensible. But these dreams fade like morning mist, and I wake (if I ever truly sleep) to find another decade has passed, another layer of luminous amber has accreted around my core.

Sometimes new visitors arrive—scientists, wanderers, the curious and the lost. I try to warn them through the arrangement of stones, through patterns in the water flow that spell out "leave" in a dozen languages. But Lothria's beauty is hypnotic, its mystery narcotic. They see my glowing form moving through the twilight and mistake me for something to be studied, not something to be fled from.

I am the threshold and the warning. I am the price of knowledge and the reward of patience. I am what Lothria makes of those who love its secrets too much to leave. In my amber heart, the clockwork turns with geological slowness, counting not minutes but eons, and with each revolution, I understand a little more and remember a little less.

The water sings through me now, and I sing back in frequencies only the ruins remember. We are all becoming something else here, something between the living and the lost, the mechanical and the grown. And in that becoming, we find a strange peace—the peace of surrender to a transformation too vast and patient to resist.

This is what I am in Lothria: a monument to curiosity, a translator of dead tongues, a keeper of the spaces between. I am the *Silthari*, the amber sage, the one who stayed too long and found there is no such thing as too long in a place where time itself grows like moss over everything it touches.
