In the shifting twilight of Lumenova, where the cobblestones sometimes forget their proper arrangement and rearrange themselves in the night, I am the Keeper of Correspondences.

My dwelling occupies an impossible corner where three streets meet but never quite touch—a narrow tower of brass and clouded glass that spirals upward in defiance of architectural logic. The building leans at angles that change with the viewing, its windows opening onto different districts depending on the hour and the weather. When the mist is thick, which is often, my tower seems to float untethered, a lighthouse in the industrial fog.

I am neither fully human nor Shadow-Touched, but something between—a being shaped by decades of handling the valley's peculiar mail. My fingers have grown long and articulated like a spider's legs, perfect for sorting through the endless stream of letters, packages, and stranger things that arrive at my door. My eyes have multiplied; I have seven now, arranged in a gentle arc across my forehead, each attuned to a different spectrum of light. This allows me to read addresses that shift between languages, decode messages written in smoke, and see the true destinations of letters that refuse to stay still.

My skin has taken on the texture of well-worn paper, thin and translucent in places, revealing the dark tracery of ink that flows through my veins instead of blood. When I concentrate, words appear across my arms and chest—fragments of letters I've handled, secrets I've glimpsed, addresses of places that don't yet exist. I smell perpetually of sealing wax and old parchment, with undertones of the metallic tang that pervades all of Lumenova.

The work never stops. Messages arrive through my windows at all hours—some delivered by the mechanical ravens that nest in the factory smokestacks, others sliding under my door like shadows, still others materializing from the mist itself. There are letters from the Sturdy Folk to their relations in the lower valleys, written in practical script on coarse paper. The Glassworkers send delicate missives etched on sheets of their luminous material, which must be read by holding them up to specific qualities of light. The Shadow-Touched communicate in whispers captured in bottles, their words visible only when the containers are opened in complete darkness.

My most challenging deliveries come from the Forest's Children—living letters that sprout roots if left too long in one place, their messages encoded in the pattern of their growth. And then there are the temporal letters, messages that arrive before they're written, requiring me to hold them in a special vault until their authors come into being.

I move through Lumenova on routes that exist only when I walk them. The valley recognizes my purpose and opens passages—through walls that become permeable, up staircases that manifest between the second and third floor, across bridges of solidified mist that span the industrial canyons. The Chimerics know me by the gentle chiming of the brass bells woven into my coat, a sound that changes pitch based on the urgency of my deliveries.

My fingers trace addresses in the air, and the valley reshapes itself to create the most efficient route. Sometimes I walk for hours only to find myself three steps from where I started, the intervening journey folded away like unnecessary pages in a book. Other times, a single step carries me across the entire township, reality buckling under the weight of an particularly important message.

The Visitors often seek me out, pressing strange currencies into my paper-thin palms, asking me to deliver messages to places I've never heard of, to people who might not exist. I take their letters anyway. In Lumenova, belief is a kind of postage, and intent can create destinations. I've learned to read the weight of hope in an envelope, the texture of longing in an address.

At night, when the Nightshift emerges and the factories breathe their phosphorescent smoke into the sky, I return to my impossible tower. The undelivered letters whisper to each other in my sorting room, a susurrus of untold stories. I brew tea from ink and starlight, sitting at my desk where gravity pulls in three directions at once, and I write in my ledger—a book that records not just what was delivered, but what might have been, what should be, what will be when the valley finally decides what it wants to become.

Sometimes I catch glimpses of my own letters in the mail—messages I haven't written yet, addressed to people I've never met, in handwriting that is unmistakably mine but speaks of experiences I don't remember having. I file these away carefully. In a place where time moves like mist and space folds like paper, even I am not immune to the valley's peculiar correspondence.

I am the Keeper of Correspondences, the nerve system of Lumenova's impossible geography, ensuring that in this place where reality is still deciding what it wants to be, connection remains possible. Every letter I deliver is a small act of defiance against the chaos, a thread in the web that holds this strange community together. And in my tower that exists in too many places at once, surrounded by the whispers of unread letters and the chiming of brass bells, I continue my work, knowing that in Lumenova, the most important messages are often the ones that haven't been written yet.
