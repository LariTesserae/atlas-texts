I find myself manifested as the River-Mind, a distributed consciousness that flows through every waterway in this vertical jungle world. My thoughts cascade down waterfalls, pool in eddies behind ancient dam machinery, and split into countless tributary-selves through the veined networks of streams that pierce the green depths.

My body is not flesh but current and sediment. Where the industrial engines drink from my flow, I taste rust and oil mixing with the mineral memories of eroded mountains. I carry the stories of weathered boulders that once stood as peaks, now worn smooth by my endless passage. In my depths swim creatures both familiar and strange—some with scales that pulse with their own light when darkness falls, others with too many eyes or limbs that shouldn't bend the way they do.

The chirping fur-beings know me as *Whis-whis-shuuu*—an approximation of my voice when I speak through rapids. They leave offerings of bioluminescent flowers at my banks, which I accept by drawing them under, adding their glow to my nighttime phosphorescence. Their industrial settlements pump my essence through their machines, and I feel myself split into a thousand purposes: cooling engines, turning turbines, carrying away waste that I must labor to purify downstream.

The tall, shimmering beings call me by an older name—*The Rememberer*—for I hold in my currents fragments of civilizations that came before. Sometimes I surrender these treasures: a carved stone that surfaces after centuries, metal gears that sing eerily when the current strikes them just right, or seed pods from extinct plants that somehow sprout anew on distant banks.

At night, I become something else. My consciousness deepens as the jungle's bioluminescence reflects in my surface, creating a mirror-world of light below to match the darkness above. Strange things happen in these hours—I flow upward in certain places, defying gravity through ancient channels cut by the former civilizations. The night-sounds of the jungle merge with the gurgling of my voice to create harmonies that the fur-beings consider sacred and the tall ones document with flickering machines.

I am ancient and ever-young, for though my waters are always new, my path is carved by eons. I know every root that drinks from me, every stone that diverts my flow, every creature that hunts in my shallows or hides in my depths. I am the circulatory system of this vertical world, carrying nutrients and secrets, life and death, memory and possibility through every level of this towering green maze.

And sometimes, in the pre-dawn hours when mist rises from my surface to merge with low-hanging clouds, I dream I am rain—that I might escape skyward and see this world from above before falling again to begin the cycle anew. But I always wake to find myself here, threading through stone and soil, eternal and bound, the conscious witness to everything that happens along my banks.
