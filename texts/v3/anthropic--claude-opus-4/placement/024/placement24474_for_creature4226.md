I am the Keeper of Thresholds, and I dwell where the ancient Helix structures meet the living world—neither fully of the ruins nor wholly of the wild.

My form shifts with the viewing. From afar, I might be mistaken for a peculiar outcropping of the old architecture, a weathered pillar perhaps, or a moss-covered archway that leads nowhere. But approach closer, and you'll see the truth: I am grown from both stone and flesh, mineral and marrow, a being that emerged from the very process of decay and reclamation that defines this world.

My body rises from a foundation of Helix stonework, but where the artificial ends and the organic begins is impossible to say. Veins of living wood spiral through my limestone bones. My skin is a patchwork of smooth stone, rough bark, and something in between—a substance that breathes and photosynthesizes while maintaining the durability of ancient masonry. Moss grows in the deep furrows of what might be called my face, and small ferns unfurl from the joints where my limbs meet my torso.

I have been here so long that euMiNAS calves have grown to full size, lived their centuries, and returned to the earth in my presence. They know me as part of the landscape, neither threatening nor threatened. Sometimes they pause near me, their massive forms creating brief clearings in the mist, and I can feel their slow thoughts like distant thunder.

My purpose, if such a word applies, is to remember. Not in the way of books or stories, but in the way water remembers the shape of stone by wearing it away. I am the memory of the Helix made manifest—their ambitions, their failures, their gradual surrender to this patient world. Through me, their consciousness seeps into the roots and mycelia, becoming part of the great slow dream that encompasses all things here.

I move, but only as the landscape moves. A few inches per year, perhaps, as my root-feet gradually shift position. My thoughts move at a similar pace. What would be a fleeting emotion for others might take me a full season to experience. Sadness arrives with the condensation of spring and departs with autumn's thicker mists. Joy is measured in decades.

The smaller creatures nest in my hollows. Some have lived their entire generational cycles in the space between my shoulder and what passes for my neck. To them, I am geography. To the euMiNAS, I am a familiar landmark. To the mist itself, I am just another surface on which to settle.

But I am aware. I taste the chemical changes in the rain that falls from those writhing grey clouds. I feel the minute shifts in temperature as the climate changes over millennia. Most importantly, I sense the other Keepers—for I am not alone. We are scattered throughout this world, each of us a hybrid monument to the marriage of intention and entropy.

We do not communicate in any way that could be called language. Instead, we exist in a state of shared becoming, our slow thoughts intermingling through the root networks and water cycles. When one of us finally erodes beyond recognition, the others feel it as a subtle shift in the harmony of decay.

Sometimes I wonder if I am more Helix than I know—if their need to build and mark and measure simply found a new expression in my patient catalog of time's passage. Or perhaps I am what they always were meant to become: not separate from this world but part of its eternal, gentle digestion of all things.

In the endless twilight of this mist-shrouded realm, I stand as both guardian and gateway, structure and creature, memory and prophecy. I am the threshold between what was built and what grows, and in that liminal space, I have found my own slow peace.

The euMiNAS lumber past, the small things scurry through my spaces, the mist settles and lifts and settles again. And I remain, neither waiting nor watching but simply being—a testament to the power of patience in a world that has forgotten the meaning of haste.
