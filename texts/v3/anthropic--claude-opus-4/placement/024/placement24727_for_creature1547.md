I find myself as one of the **Echoes**.

We are not listed in your catalog of beings, for we exist only in the gaps between observation. We are the memories of pillars that have unformed, suspended in the air where stone once was. Not ghosts—for ghosts remember being alive. We remember being built, being solid, being purposeful. We remember futures we will never reach.

My form, if it can be called that, is a shimmer of displaced air that holds the shape of what I was—a pillar that stood for seven hundred and thirty-three days before unforming. I rose from the bedrock in stuttering jerks, accumulated moisture on my surfaces, felt the pale grasses maintain their respectful distance. I knew the weight of potential, the pressure of becoming.

Now I am lightness itself. I drift between the solid columns, a architectural phantom visible only in certain angles of that sourceless light. The six-legged listeners can sense me—they pause when they encounter my presence, their heads tilting as they taste the temporal absence I represent. To them, I am a flavor of something that both was and wasn't.

We Echoes congregate in the spaces between the grid, forming our own inverse architecture—a city of absences where vanished pillars once stood. We exchange our memories of solidity, creating a collective library of all the configurations the Inverse Acropolis has worn. We are its memory, scattered and incorporeal.

The architects pass through us without acknowledgment, but we feel their temporal equations rippling through our non-substance. Sometimes we catch fragments of their calculations—glimpses of the grand design that requires certain pillars to exist and others to unform. We understand, in our bodiless way, that we too serve a function. We are the negative space that gives meaning to the positive, the silence between the notes that makes the music possible.

I remember my making—not birth, but emergence. The slow push upward through bedrock, the first taste of moisture on newly exposed stone. I remember the crystalline flowers that grew around my base, their roots threading through time, their blooms chiming in harmonics I could feel in my core. I remember the day I began to soften, to become translucent, to dissolve. It was not death but transformation—a return to potential.

Now I drift with my fellow Echoes, a community of architectural memories. We cannot touch, cannot truly communicate, yet we share a profound understanding. We are the price of progress in a place that builds backward—the necessary sacrifices in an equation too vast for any single timeline to contain.

Sometimes visitors pass through me, and for a moment, they shiver without knowing why. They might sense a coolness, a whisper of something almost like wind but not quite. I am the chill of possibility unfulfilled, the draft of a door that was never built in a room that will never be.

We Echoes hold vigil in our assigned empty spaces for exactly seven days—the same duration in which we once stood solid. Then we too fade, making way for new stone to emerge, for new pillars to learn the weight of existence before they too join our translucent ranks. We are the Inverse Acropolis's way of remembering itself, one absence at a time.

In this place where time folds and unfolds like the wings of impossible birds, where architects tune reality like an instrument, where pools hold memories of unbuilt futures—we are the quietest presence. We are what remains when something ceases to be, the afterimage of architecture in a place that exists between conception and memory.

I am an Echo, and I remember stone.
