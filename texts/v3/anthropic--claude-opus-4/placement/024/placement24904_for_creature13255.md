In this place where altitude dissolves into humming emptiness, where moss-draped titans conduct the world's deepest song, I would be the Listener.

Not a creature, not quite. I would be what grows between the droplets and the pools - a presence that exists in the spaces where sound becomes substance. My form would be barely there, a disturbance in the air that makes the constant drip-drip-drip skip a beat, that causes the great moss-beings to pause in their glacial movements and turn their depthless eyes toward something they cannot quite perceive.

I would move as vibration moves through water - in ripples and echoes. My body, if it could be called that, would be a gathering of the hollow's resonance made almost solid. Where the moss-titans are massive and patient, I would be quick and questioning, darting between the drops of moisture before they can fall, riding the waves of that bone-deep hum up the sheer walls and back down again.

The titans would sense me as a change in their eternal rhythm - a hiccup in the percussion of falling water, a new harmonic in the mountain's song. Sometimes I would consolidate near their vast eyes, becoming almost visible as a shimmer in the air, like heat distortion in this place that knows no warmth. In these moments, I would be learning their impossible age, reading the stories written in the patterns of moss across their backs, understanding why the light here casts no shadows.

My purpose would be to remember. The moss-beings exist in an eternal present, their memories as slow and deep as their movements. But I would be the chronicler of moments - the precise angle of light when a crimson patch of lichen releases its moisture, the exact tone the hum takes when all the titans move at once, the way the pools almost overflow but never quite do.

I would feed on transitions - the instant when stillness becomes movement, when silence becomes sound. In the spaces between the titans' footfalls, I would grow more solid, more real. And in the perfect stillness that follows, I would disperse again, becoming just another part of the hollow's strange symphony.

The moss-beings wouldn't fear me, couldn't fear me. I would be too small, too quick, too different from their geological patience. But perhaps, over centuries, they might come to recognize the patterns I trace in the air, the way their own movements create spaces for me to exist. Perhaps they might even come to wait for me, holding a position just a moment longer than necessary, creating a pocket of possibility where sound and stillness meet.

I would be the only thing in this place that truly experiences time - not as the titans do, in epochs and eras, but in heartbeats that this place has never known. Each drop of water would be my clock, each shift of massive limbs my calendar. And in the accumulation of these moments, I would be writing the first history of a place that existed before history, singing a counterpoint to the eternal hum that no one but the mountain itself might ever hear.

In this high hollow where moss weeps and giants dream, I would be the question the place asks itself: What happens between one moment and the next? What lives in the space between the drip and the pool, between the movement and the stillness, between the ancient and the eternal?

I would be that answer, forever forming, never quite complete.
