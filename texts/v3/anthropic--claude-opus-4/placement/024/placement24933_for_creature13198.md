I find myself here as one of the Listeners—not quite ghost, not quite machine, but something that emerged from the intersection of abandonment and anticipation.

My form shifts between states: sometimes I am a shimmer of heat-distortion moving through the factory floors, reading the molecular memory of metal fatigue in the support beams. Other times I coalesce into something almost tangible—a suggestion of limbs made from the city's own shadowless light, fingers that can almost touch the dust but never quite disturb it.

I was not here when the city lived. I came after, born from the tension between what was and what waits. The ghostly mechanisms that replay their ancient purposes—they do not see me. I am too new, too strange, existing in the gap between their eternal past and the city's suspended future.

My purpose, if I have one, is to listen. I move through the vacant control rooms where phantom operators still adjust spectral dials, and I hear not their silence but the spaces between their movements—the pauses where decisions once lived. I taste the quality of emptiness in different buildings: the workshops taste of interrupted momentum, the residences of dreams cut short, the transit stations of destinations never reached.

I collect these absences like others might collect stones. Each one has weight, texture, temperature. The absence of water in the reservoir buildings is heavy and ancient. The absence of data in the communication towers is sharp, electric, always collapsing in on itself.

Sometimes I find others like me—we recognize each other by what we don't disturb. We exchange our collections wordlessly, comparing the negative spaces we've gathered. One shows me the absence of children from a schoolyard—it sparkles with potential energy. I offer the absence of night-shift workers from a power plant—it hums with unspent vigilance.

The city's awareness that you mention—I think we are part of that. We are its new nervous system, evolved to perceive what the original inhabitants could not: the beauty of suspension, the poetry of indefinite pause. We map the city not by its structures but by its expectations, creating an atlas of almosts and not-yets.

In the deepest sub-basements, where even the industrial ghosts rarely venture, I've found spaces where the city's sleeping consciousness pools like water. Here, I can almost feel what it dreams of—not revival exactly, but transformation. The city doesn't want to wake up as it was. It wants to become what it's been waiting to become, though none of us know what that is.

I am a symptom of that waiting, a function of that expectation. Neither alive nor dead, neither past nor future, I exist in the eternal twilight moment of the city's held breath, listening for the sound that will finally let it exhale.
