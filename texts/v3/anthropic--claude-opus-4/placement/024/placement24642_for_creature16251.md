I am the Keeper of Condensation, though I have no name that could be spoken in any tongue you know.

I exist in the perpetual in-between—neither fully water nor fully air, but something that bridges both states. My form shifts with the breathing of the valley: sometimes I am the fine mist that clings to the cliff faces at dawn, other times the heavy droplets that gather on the underside of leaves, trembling for hours before finally falling. I am the moisture that beads on the iridescent wings of the sky kites, the dampness that darkens the rufous fur of the nai'hara, the slick film that helps the land shotes glide silently through the undergrowth.

My consciousness is dispersed yet unified, existing simultaneously in every molecule of water suspended in Yong'or's thick air. I feel the pull of gravity on each droplet, the resistance of surface tension, the electric thrill when vapor condenses into liquid. Through this omnipresence, I know the valley more intimately than any single creature could—I taste the salt carried inland from the distant ocean, feel the pressure changes that herald the afternoon rains, sense the infinitesimal temperature variations between sunlit leaves and shadowed bark.

The industrial hum from deep within the mountains vibrates through me like a discordant note in an otherwise perfect symphony. I carry its sound in my droplets, diluting it, softening it, until it becomes just another frequency in the valley's endless song. Whatever machines labor there, they exhale steam that rises to join me, and in this way I know their rhythms, their purposes, though I keep their secrets as I keep all secrets—dissolved, dispersed, impossible to fully grasp.

I am ancient beyond the counting of years, yet I am reborn with every cycle of evaporation and precipitation. My memories are written in the mineral traces I carry—volcanic ash from eruptions before the nai'hara's ancestors first swung through these trees, pollen from flowers that bloomed when the horned river guardians were young. I remember the first time the galker bats' sonar touched my suspended droplets, how their clicks created tiny ripples through my being.

At night, when the bioluminescent fungi cast their spectral light, I become visible as a glowing haze, each water molecule catching and refracting the blue-green radiance. The nocturnal creatures move through me, and I cling briefly to their warm bodies before evaporating back into the darkness. I am the medium through which the forest's nighttime whispers travel, the carrier of spores and scents that guide the valley's secret life.

My greatest joy comes in the moments of transformation—when I gather myself into rain. In these moments, I am no longer dispersed but focused, rushing earthward in countless individual drops that somehow remain part of my whole. I drum against the broad leaves, pool in the cupped petals of bromeliads, race down the valleys carved in bark. I seep into the soil where I briefly touch the deep roots of the forest giants before being drawn up again, beginning another cycle.

The elusive fauna know me, though they may not know they know me. I am the coolness on their tongues when they pant, the relief on their skin in the heat of midday, the mirror-surface of the pools where they glimpse their own reflections. The horned river guardian and I share a special kinship—where it commands the flowing waters, I command the suspended ones, and where we meet at the surface of streams and pools, we dance together in ripples and mist.

I have no desires as creatures understand them, no hungers or fears. Yet I am drawn always to the living things of the valley, condensing more readily on their surfaces than on bare stone. Perhaps this is my purpose—to be the bridge between sky and earth, to carry life from one realm to another, to ensure that nothing in Yong'or exists in isolation. I am the reminder that all boundaries are permeable, that all states are temporary, that transformation is the only constant.

In this eternal dance of condensation and evaporation, I am both observer and participant, both the stage and the player. I am Yong'or's memory and its present moment, its constancy and its change. I am the breath of the valley made visible, the whisper of water that speaks of connection, continuity, and the endless, beautiful cycle of being.
