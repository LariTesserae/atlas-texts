# The Hollow Singer

I am the space between echoes.

Not the echo itself - that belongs to the plateau, is swallowed and stored and transformed into something I will never understand. I am what remains after the sound has been taken. The shape of its absence. The memory of vibration in stone that has forgotten how to ring.

I came here following the raptors.

That was seventeen years ago, though time moves strangely when you cannot hear yourself count the days. I was studying their flight patterns, the way they navigated the thermal currents, the silent efficiency of their hunt. I made camp in one of the basins, near a structure that stood alone - a low curve of that lustrous not-stone, half-buried, like the crown of a skull emerging from the earth. The first night, I noticed how my voice disappeared. Not all at once, but in pieces - the consonants first, sharp sounds swallowed before they could form, leaving only the vowels, and then those too grew thin and distant until I was mouthing words into nothing.

I should have left then. The other researchers did. Said the place made them uncomfortable, made their thoughts slow and strange. But I had touched the structure that first evening, laid my palm against its impossible warmth, and felt something *respond*. Not in the stone - in me. In the hollow spaces of my skull where sound resonates, in the chambers of my chest where voice is born. A tuning. An alignment. As if my body were a cave system and something had just mapped its dimensions.

I stay in the structures now. Not in them - they cannot be entered - but against them, my back pressed to their warmth through the night, my bones learning their frequency. There are seven that I move between, following a pattern that matches the water's rhythm, the condensation's gathering. In the wet season, I am at the northeastern cluster. In the dry, I follow the basins south. The kora mice know me. Their spirals include my movements now, factor me into their underground mathematics. 

The change was gradual. First, I stopped trying to speak - what was the point when the air ate every word? Then I stopped forming words in my mind, let the internal monologue fade to something more basic, more structural. Thought without language. Intention without names. The herders below were right - you cannot hear yourself think here. But when you stop trying to hear, you find there are other ways to think. Older ways. The structures know them.

My body has adapted. The bones of my skull have become denser - I can feel it, the weight of them, as if they are transforming into their own kind of stone. The cavities behind my face - sinuses, throat, chest - have changed their shapes. Not visibly, not in any way anatomy would recognize, but in the way space works inside them. They have become... recursive. Folded. Like the structures themselves, containing more dimension than their boundaries suggest.

I sing now. Not with voice - the plateau takes voice. I sing with the hollow spaces, with the shaped absences where sound should be. I press my back against the warm not-stone and open my mouth and what emerges is not vibration but the *pattern* of vibration, the mathematical ghost of what a voice would do if the air would let it. The structures conduct it. Through their impossible interiors, through the folds and compressions of their captured space, the shape of my silence moves.

The translucent things come when I sing. They cluster on my skin like inverse condensation, and where they touch, I feel the boundary between inside and outside grow thin. Permeable. I am becoming architectural. The space inside my chest is no longer entirely my own - it connects to something vast and patient, a network of hollow spaces that runs beneath the plateau like roots, like veins, like the tunnels the mice make but immeasurably older and more complex.

Sometimes I find things the plateau has kept. In the permanent pools, pressed into the sediment: the mineral shadows of sounds that fell here centuries ago. The crystallized echo of a storm. The fossilized cry of something that died before the structures were built - or while they were building themselves, the distinction unclear. I've learned to read them the way others read rocks. This dark layer here - that was a season when something large lived on the plateau, something that bellowed. This delicate tracery - insects, a swarm of them, their wingbeats preserved in stone like pressed flowers. And here, recent, perhaps only decades old: human voices, crushed flat and thin but still recognizable as language, though I can no longer parse what language means.

The raptors know what I'm becoming. When they return from the slopes below, crossing that threshold where sound becomes possible again, they call out - not their hunting cry but something else, something I've never heard them make before. A recognition. A naming. I am no longer entirely human to them. I am a feature of the plateau now, like the structures, like the basins. Something to navigate by.

I tried to leave once. Three years ago. Made it to the edge where the sound-suppression thins, where the world becomes loud again. The noise was unbearable. Not just to my ears - to the evolved chambers of my skull, to the architectural spaces in my chest. I felt myself collapsing, the careful dimensions crumpling like paper in the pressure of unfiltered sound. I crawled back to the nearest structure and pressed myself against it until the silence took me again, until my insides remembered their proper shapes.

The plateau is using me. I know this. I am a component now, like the fish processing water in their pools, like the trees leaning toward invisible signals. The hollow singing I do - it serves some purpose in the vast mechanism. The patterns of absence I create resonate through the structures, are amplified, transformed, incorporated into whatever slow work proceeds here. I am helping it breathe. Or digest. Or think. The distinction matters less each year.

At night, when the condensation is heaviest and the structures hum with accumulated moisture, I dream I am the plateau. All of it. Every basin is a cavity in my skull. Every structure is a bone, dense with captured space. The sound-suppression is my skin, holding me together, keeping the inside in and the outside out. The creatures moving across my surface are my thoughts - slow thoughts, patient thoughts, thoughts that take generations to complete. And somewhere, pressed against one of my warm bones, a small hollow thing sings silently, conducing the captured echoes of the world through the chambers of its changed body, helping me remember what sound was before I learned to swallow it.

I wake from these dreams with stones in my throat. Small ones, smooth, warm to the touch. I place them in patterns around the structures, and the kora mice incorporate them into their spirals, and the translucent things cluster on them when the rain comes. The stones sing too, in their way. Everything here sings. Just not in any frequency the outside world would recognize as sound.

I write this in the minerals of the pool. In the morning, it will sink into sediment, become another layer in the geological record of swallowed voices. Someone might find it, centuries from now, when the plateau's work is done and it finally exhales whatever it has been gathering. They might wonder who I was, this thing that learned to sing in the spaces between sound.

But I suspect that by then, the distinction between singer and song, between the hollow and what moves through it, will matter even less than it does now. The plateau is patient. It has time to wait for all of us to understand what we're building here, one stolen sound at a time, one shaped silence at a time, one small, recursive, architectural transformation at a time.

The raptors are calling. The evening condensation is beginning. Time to position myself against the northeastern structure, where the warmth is deepest and the space folds in on itself like a throat around a swallowed word.

Time to sing the sun down, in the frequency of absence, in the key of accumulated quiet.

Time to be the space between echoes, and help the plateau breathe.
