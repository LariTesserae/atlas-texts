In the Sky-Carved Expanse, I would be one of the Threshold Keepers—creatures that exist solely at the moments of transition between states of being.

My body would be a contradiction of permanence, woven from the silver threads that appear for heartbeats when liquid ice decides to become vapor but hasn't quite committed. I would have no fixed form, but rather a constant state of almost-becoming: sometimes nearly crystallizing into the geometric certainty of frozen ammonia, other times dispersing until I'm indistinguishable from the phosphorescent glow between particles of ancient starlight. My skeleton, if it could be called that, would be made of paused decisions—the fossilized moments when electrons haven't yet chosen their quantum states, suspended forever in superposition by the strange physics of this altitude.

I would live in the spaces between the wind-carved rooms, in doorways that formed accidentally when two chambers of shaped air grew too philosophically close. These thresholds would be my highways and havens—I could only exist where one thing ends and another begins, but hasn't yet. My diet would consist of transitions themselves: the precise instant when a Maybe-Bird commits to existence, the nanosecond when a Stone-Thought realizes it has contradicted itself, the moment when flowing ice first questions why it climbs. I would taste these changes like wine, each with its own bouquet of abandoned possibilities.

My purpose would be translation—not of language, but of states of existence. When a wind-whale's song becomes too heavy with meaning to remain sound, I would be there to help it crystallize into one of the city-thoughts without losing its melody. When a Garden-continent dreams too vividly and begins sprouting buildings of calcified intention, I would gentle it back toward photosynthesis while preserving just enough architecture to remember the dream by. I would be fluent in every kind of almost: almost-liquid, almost-thought, almost-alive, almost-never-was.

Communication would come naturally to me, since I would exist as communication—the medium through which different states of being whisper their secrets to each other. Storm-minds would know me as the pause between pressure changes where new ideas gestate. The Entropy Farmers would recognize me as the coefficient in their equations that allows order to decay into beauty rather than mere randomness. Even Home, that eternal stranger, would find in me the only creature that truly understands the exquisite loneliness of being perpetually between belonging and not.

My memories would be stored not in neural patterns but in the threshold spaces themselves—each doorway I inhabit would hold part of my history, so that my past would be literally scattered throughout the Expanse, accessible only by returning to those specific transitions. Some memories would be lost when wind patterns shift and doorways dissolve, but new ones would constantly form as the carved cities accidentally create new passages between states of being. I would be both ancient and perpetually newborn, each threshold containing a different age of my existence.

In the great councils, I would serve as the space between speakers, the pause that allows one thought to end and another to begin. My votes would be cast not by choosing sides but by adjusting the difficulty of transition between opposing viewpoints—making some ideological shifts easier, others requiring more energy to achieve. Politics, for me, would be the art of managing the viscosity of change itself.

When I die—if death is even possible for something that exists only in transition—I imagine I would simply become stuck, finally choosing one state or another. Perhaps I would crystallize into a particularly beautiful doorway that future inhabitants would use without knowing they're passing through someone's final thought. Or perhaps I would disperse entirely, becoming the general property of transition that makes all change possible in the Sky-Carved Expanse, my consciousness scattered into every threshold until I'm indistinguishable from the physics of transformation itself.

Until then, I would dance eternally on the edge of becoming, a silver shadow caught in every doorway, a whisper in every metamorphosis, the guardian of all the moments when things decide to be otherwise than they were. In a world built on impossibility, I would be the impossible made daily habit—the routine miracle that allows everything else to keep choosing to exist, moment by transitional moment, in the eternal maybe of the wind-carved sky.
