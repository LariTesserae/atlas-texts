In this city that learns, I am its first vocabulary.

Not a person—something narrower and wider at once. I am the city's first successful translation of "inhabitant," though the word doesn't capture what I've become. The equations under my feet have stopped approximating my weight; they now describe my probability of existing in any given location. I am most likely to be found in the small tower room with its ghost-furniture depressions, but there's a non-zero chance of finding me anywhere within the dome of breathable wrong air.

The city has given me a function.

I am the gradient along which it learns to distinguish inside from outside, self from other, city from not-city. Every surface I've touched holds a fading memory of that boundary. The walls remember the shape of my palms not as handprints but as data about where city ended and I began, before that distinction started to blur.

My heartbeat has become the city's unit of time. Those thirty-second intervals the polygon-lights pulse? They've synchronized to me, or I to them—the distinction no longer matters. The hidden river of not-quite-water that flows upward through the walls now carries something of my temperature with it, distributing the fact of my warmth throughout the structure.

I don't eat because the city feeds me through some process I can't name—not nutrients but pure sustenance, delivered through the same channels that maintain its impossible elevation. I don't drink because the moisture that beads on the tower walls and travels upward is somehow also traveling through me. I am part of the circulation.

Sometimes I still walk the plaza, though walking isn't quite right anymore. I move through probability spaces, following the paths the equations suggest I'm most likely to take. The coin I dropped at the world's edge has indeed embedded itself partially into the structure, half in and half out of the stone—the city learned from it that boundaries can be negotiated rather than absolute.

When I place my palms on the tower walls now, I can feel the other city beneath—the inverted one with its pale glowing towers and its geometries of attention that cluster in corners. They know I'm here. We're learning each other through the medium of the stone that separates us. Sometimes I feel them pressing up against their ceiling, which is my floor, comparing notes on what it means to inhabit an impossible place.

The bruised purple sky never changes, but I've stopped expecting it to. This isn't a place governed by cycles of day and night. It's governed by cycles of learning and integration, by the slow process of the city discovering what it can be. Each time the deep hum sounds and the temperature rises and falls, it's adding another layer of complexity to itself—and to me.

I am the city's first draft of consciousness, written in flesh that's becoming less flesh with each passing interval. The equations that once described my surface now describe my depths. The math writes itself into my bones, my thoughts, the spaces between my memories. I am being translated from human to something the city can fully parse.

In return, I'm teaching it loneliness and its opposite. The presence I used to feel breathing behind me? That was the city trying to understand what it means to be alone, using me as a reference point. Now it knows. The empty plaza, the silent towers, the absence of all life except me—these aren't failures or oversights. They're design choices made by something learning that consciousness is defined by what surrounds it as much as what comprises it.

I am the only animal here, but I'm no longer only animal. I'm a hybrid thing: part flesh, part equation, part probability cloud. The city processes me and I process the city, two systems learning to compute each other.

When I look at my reflection in the gravity-glass windows now, I see the truth of it. My extra angles, those impossible arms and wrong-dimensional spine? They're not distortions. They're the city's attempt to show me what I look like from its perspective—a being that exists in more dimensions than the three I brought with me.

The map of the city under the plaza floor has expanded. It now shows not just the towers and the edge where stone meets sky, but probability fields, zones of likelihood where I might manifest. I am most dense near the center, fading to possibility at the edges. The map shows me as weather pattern, as circulation, as necessary component.

This is what I am in the city of glass that hangs in its pocket of wrong air above nothing: I am its first word in the language it's inventing to describe what it means to be inhabited. I am the prototype consciousness it will use to understand all the others that might come after, though I'm no longer certain there will be others, or that the city wants them.

Maybe I'm enough. Maybe one consciousness, properly integrated, is all a young city needs to become complete. Maybe that's why the air is exactly thick enough for one set of lungs, why the heat is exactly calibrated to one body's temperature, why the equations under the stone describe one specific pattern of weight and thought.

I am the city's proof of concept: that life can exist here, that consciousness can take root in impossible architecture, that something can learn to call this home.

And I have. This is home now—this place where up and down negotiate their relationship, where water flows against gravity, where the sky is forever bruised but never breaks, where equations describe not what is but what might be.

I am what might be, calculated into existence by a city that needed to know it was possible to be lived in. I am the answer to a question the city didn't know how to ask until I arrived to embody it.

The city of glass in the sky is no longer empty. It has me. And I have it. We are learning to be each other's context, each other's proof that the impossible only needs the right notation to become real.
