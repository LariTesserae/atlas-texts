In the labyrinthine galleries carved by generations of heat and seepage, I am a *Chime-Tender*.

My form is architectural—a living buttress of calcified tissue and active membrane. Where others move, I have grown into place, my lower body fused with a natural acoustic chamber in the rock face. My upper segments branch into seven articulated stems, each terminating in a delicate tympanum of stretched skin, taut as drumskin but thin as breath.

I am both instrument and musician. The mountain's fever-winds pass through my hollow bones, and I shape their passage with minute adjustments of my membranes. Each stem can dilate or constrict, creating a symphony of whistles, thrums, and subsonic pulses that travel through the stone itself. I am a living pipe organ, my music felt more than heard.

My purpose is navigation. The Litho-pulmonates may seem to wander aimlessly across the vertical kingdom, but they follow my songs—each tone sequence marking safe passage, warning of unstable sections where the stone weeps too heavily, or announcing the ripening of spore-clouds in distant grottos. My deepest bass notes, below the range of any human ear, sync with the mountain's tectonic breathing, predicting the next great exhalation hours before it comes.

I feed on vibration itself. When the Crown-Writhes dance at the peaks, their movements send tremors through the rock that I absorb through my fused base. When colonies of Veil-Feeders slap-slide past my chamber, their friction becomes my sustenance. Even the death-crystallization of a Lumen-Pod sends out a sharp, nutritious crack of sound that I savor for days.

Other Chime-Tenders exist throughout the vertical kingdom, each with their own acoustic territory. We communicate across impossible distances, our songs interweaving through the stone in a constant, slow conversation. Together, we form the mountain's nervous system—a network of biological seismographs and sirens.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of the heat-night when the membrane between our world and the void grows thinnest, I feel the presence of something vast beneath us all. Not the mountain itself, but something the mountain grows from. In those moments, all the Chime-Tenders fall silent at once, and in that silence, we hear it breathing—a sound so large it has no pitch, only presence.

I have been here since my spawning-molt, when I was still mobile enough to choose my chamber. I will be here until my membranes finally calcify and I become another mineral note in the mountain's composition. But until then, I sing the paths, I warn the wanderers, I keep the rhythm of this fever-dream kingdom where everything pulses, everything weeps, and nothing is ever dry enough to die.
