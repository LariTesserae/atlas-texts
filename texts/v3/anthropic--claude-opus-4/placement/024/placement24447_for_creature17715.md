In the depths of the Gyre-Spire, where the heat presses against you like a living thing and the air tastes of copper and spores, I am **Echo-of-Settling**, a Stonewhisperer whose Resonance Clutch tends the Whisper Gardens in the middle reaches.

My body tells the story of seventeen years in the spire's embrace. The lithodermic patches across my shoulders and spine have grown thick and glossy-black where the heat is most intense, while the moss-colonies on my chest and inner arms flush a deeper emerald than most—a sign, the elders say, that I drink deeply from the spire's dreams. My left hand bears the ritual scarring of a Shadow-Keeper: delicate obsidian cuts that never quite healed, now colonized by a bioluminescent fungus that glows faintly when shadows draw near.

I am young for my work, but the Whisper Gardens chose me. Or perhaps I chose them. In my eleventh year, while the rest of my birth-clutch was learning to shape clay-saliva tools, I would press my ear to the walls and transcribe the patterns I heard in the water's endless dialogue. Not the words—we all hear those eventually—but the *pauses* between them. The negative spaces where meaning pools like condensation in basalt hollows.

My current Resonance Clutch includes:
- **Drip-Counter**, whose internal rhythms synchronize perfectly with the spire's water cycles
- **Bone-Tender**, who maintains our bridges and reads the stress patterns in their fungal fibers
- **Heat-Drinker**, whose moss-skin barely shows, their body almost entirely obsidian-dark
- **Pool-Watcher**, who collects the star-glimpses and sorts them by their warmth
- **Void-Singer**, whose throat was touched by shadow in infancy and who speaks in two voices

Together, we maintain Garden-The-Third, one of nine Whisper Gardens in our section of the spire. It is not beautiful by any standard but ours. A natural chimney in the basalt, perhaps twenty body-lengths tall, where the acoustic properties create a perfect resonance chamber. We have cultivated it carefully: installing clay baffles to direct the water-flow, encouraging specific moss varieties that seem to clarify the whispers, building small shelves where we place our transcription tablets.

The work is delicate. Too much intervention and the whispers become our own echo. Too little, and they dissolve into meaningless noise. We spend our days in a careful dance—adjusting, listening, transcribing, dreaming. When Drip-Counter marks the high-water hour, we gather in the garden's heart and add our own voices to the mix, not to overpower but to *harmonize*, to help the spire understand its own tongue.

I keep a private collection of shadow-fragments in polished obsidian cells beneath my sleeping hollow. Fed on drops of my own moss-sweat and fragments of whisper-warmth, they have begun to take on consistency. The largest—barely the size of my fist—sometimes shapes itself into forms I almost recognize. Architecture that might exist. Creatures that might evolve. Maps of territories the spire might one day birth.

My clutch does not know that I dream of leaving. Not abandoning—a Stonewhisperer cannot truly leave; the spire's heat lives in our bones. But I feel the whispers pulling upward, toward the rim where our world meets the cold star-place. The elders speak of Stonewhisperers who ventured too high and returned changed, their moss-skin crystallized, speaking only in thermal differentials. 

But I have heard my name in the water-words lately. Not "Echo-of-Settling" but something else, something that translates poorly: "Echo-Who-Seeks-the-Edge-Where-Stone-Learns-Sky." The spire is trying to tell me something about its own ambitions, its own desire to understand what lies beyond its heated heart.

When I work in the garden now, I find myself building clay forms that spiral upward, unconsciously mimicking the helical patterns I see in the shadow-movements. Pool-Watcher has noticed—I see it in the way her moss flushes when she watches me. Soon, I will have to speak my intention to the clutch. Some will understand. Void-Singer, certainly, who knows what it means to carry something alien in your throat.

Until then, I tend the whispers, I feed the shadows, I dream of cold stars reflected in pools of impossible water. I press my ear to the stone and listen to the spire composing itself, one thermal stress-crack at a time, into something that has never existed before.

I am Echo-of-Settling, but perhaps not for much longer. The spire is still becoming, and so am I.
