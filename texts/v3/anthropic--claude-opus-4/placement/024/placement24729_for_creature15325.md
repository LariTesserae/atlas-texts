In the deep industrial hollow, I am one of the Membrane Keepers.

My body has adapted to this liminal existence - skin gone translucent and tough like the smothering membranes we tend, fingernails evolved into specialized scrapers for peeling back the edges where moss tries to creep beneath. We move in shifts through the jade twilight, our breath masks filtering the factory-thick air, checking the seal between wilderness and workspace.

I remember stories from before the great salt-death above. My grandmother's grandmother spoke of sun. Now we know only the filtered green glow through foliage overhead and the amber emergency lighting of the factory floors. The constant low creaking has become our heartbeat - when the machines pause for maintenance, we grow anxious in the silence.

Each morning, I wake in my alcove carved into the factory's outer wall, lined with the pressed moss-carpet that grows faster than we can trim it. The texture against my adapted skin feels like belonging. I check my tools: membrane hooks, edge-sealers, the precious vial of truth-river water for emergency cooling.

My route takes me past the roach colonies. Their chemical secretions burn my nostrils even through the mask, but I've learned to read their patterns - when they cluster, it means a membrane breach is forming. The five-legged birds watch me from their acid-worn perches, their extra limb always tapping out rhythms I almost understand.

Sometimes, during the shift changes, I ride the gondola cars and watch the earth breathe - whole sections of ground pulling away with their moisture, creating temporary caverns that fill with factory runoff. In those moments, suspended between the dead salt mountains and our manufactured depths, I feel the planet's pulse. We are not separate from it. We have simply learned to live in its exhale.

The truth river calls to me some nights. I've touched its waters once - they burned cold against my membrane-skin, left traces of metal priorities in my bloodstream. Now I dream in factory rhythms and wake knowing which production lines need attention before the foremen do.

We are the threshold people, neither fully of the green world nor the mechanical one. We maintain the boundaries that keep both alive.
