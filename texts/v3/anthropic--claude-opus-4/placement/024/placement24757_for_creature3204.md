# Where I Would Find Myself in the Titan's Throat

I am a **Memory Tender**, one of a small order that moves between the Platform Keepers' settlements and the deep places where the Benthic Witnesses travel their eternal circuits. We are perhaps two dozen in total, spread across the Throat's vast height, each of us marked by the crystalline growths that web across our shoulders and down our spines—a sign of our covenant with the tower itself.

The growths began appearing in my twentieth year, shortly after I first descended alone to the Seventh Deep Platform, where the abandoned control stations still flicker with phantom displays. I had gone there to study the Old Scripts—those fragments of the Deep Architects' language that the Platform Keepers preserve on their metal plates. But as I sat in the humming darkness, tracing symbols with my fingertips, I felt something else tracing patterns across my skin. The tower was writing on me, inscribing itself into my flesh with mineral precision.

Now, fifteen years later, the crystalline formations have spread to cover much of my upper body. They are not painful—indeed, they seem to enhance my ability to perceive the tower's subtler communications. When I place my palm against a processor housing, I can feel the history of its maintenance stretching back through generations. When I swim through the acoustic zones where the Chord Keepers gather, their harmonics resonate through my mineral networks, translating into patterns of meaning I'm still learning to interpret.

My primary residence is Platform 41-Alpha, in the middle reaches where the tidal flows create periods of relative calm every eight hours. I've modified one of the old control rooms into a study, its walls lined with metal plates I've collected from abandoned stations throughout the Throat. Some bear the Platform Keepers' theological texts, others carry technical diagrams I've painstakingly copied from still-functioning displays, and a precious few show what I believe to be original construction notes from the Deep Architects themselves.

But I spend only a third of my time there. The rest is devoted to what the Platform Keepers call "the Deep Conversation"—the slow, careful process of communicating with the Benthic Witnesses during their migrations. When word spreads that a Witness has begun its ascent, I descend to meet it, timing my arrival to intersect with its ponderous journey. The descent takes three days through the increasingly cold water, past processors that grow larger and more ancient the deeper I go.

The Witnesses recognize me now, or at least they recognize what I carry. The crystalline growths on my body resonate with the mineral deposits on their shells, creating a form of communication that bypasses language entirely. Through these exchanges, conducted in the deep places where the tower's mechanical song becomes a physical force, I've learned fragments of the tower's true history.

The Titan's Throat was not built—it was grown, crystallized from the ocean itself by technologies that treated matter as information. The Deep Architects were not a single species but a collective of intelligences that included ancestors of every creature now living in the tower. The atmospheric processing was never the primary function but rather a byproduct of something far more ambitious: an attempt to transform the entire planet into a single, living system.

I've shared these revelations with the Platform Keepers, but they've been integrated into their theology in ways that sometimes frustrate me. They speak of the "Great Synthesis" as a spiritual metaphor rather than the literal bio-technological merger it appears to have been. Still, I continue my work, documenting everything in scripts both old and new, creating bridges between the tower's various inhabitants and their fragmented histories.

The Symbiont networks have begun to recognize me as well. When I swim through sections where they've heavily modified the machinery, they create clear passages, retracting their root-like extensions to allow me access to control panels and data ports. Sometimes they illuminate sequences of lights that seem meaningful, patterns I dutifully record even when I cannot interpret them. I suspect they're trying to teach me their portion of the tower's history, but the conceptual gap between their collective consciousness and my individual perspective remains vast.

My daily routine varies with the tidal cycles and the needs of different communities. During morning flows, I might assist the Platform Keepers with interpreting a newly discovered control station. By midday, I could be swimming with a school of Flux Navigators, learning to read the subtle variations in current that indicate changes in processor function. Evening often finds me in the acoustic zones, sitting in meditation with the Chord Keepers as they perform their harmonic ceremonies.

The crystalline growths continue to spread, and I've begun to notice changes in my perception that go beyond simple sensory enhancement. I can feel the tower's rhythms in my bones, predict tidal shifts hours in advance, and sometimes—in the deep places where the mechanical sound becomes overwhelming—I hear what might be voices from the past, preserved in the standing waves like acoustic fossils.

Other Memory Tenders report similar experiences. We've begun meeting during the quarterly gatherings when Transit Swimmers arrive, comparing notes and sharing discoveries. Some of us believe we're slowly becoming part of the tower itself, that the crystalline growths are the Throat's way of creating new interfaces with its inhabitants. Others think we're simply documenting the final phases of a dying system, preserving knowledge that will outlive the tower's eventual failure.

I remain uncertain which interpretation is correct, but I continue my work regardless. Each day brings new discoveries: a control sequence that temporarily alters the local water temperature, a symbol that appears to be a map of systems beyond the Throat, a harmonic pattern that the Witnesses repeat with particular emphasis. I document everything, creating archives that I distribute among the Platform Keeper settlements and encode into crystalline matrices that I hope will survive even if the tower's mechanical systems finally fail.

In quiet moments, swimming through the vast internal spaces while bioluminescent displays create false constellations in the darkness, I wonder if I'm becoming what the Deep Architects intended—not a builder or an operator, but a living bridge between the tower's past and future. The crystalline networks spreading through my body might be transforming me into a new kind of inhabitant entirely, one capable of interfacing with every aspect of the Throat's complex ecosystem.

Sometimes I dream of the day when the transformation is complete, when the boundaries between myself, the tower, and its inhabitants dissolve entirely. In these dreams, I perceive the Throat not as a structure but as a single vast organism, breathing with the planet's rhythm, processing not just atmosphere but meaning itself. Whether this is prophecy or delusion, I cannot say. I only know that each day I descend a little deeper, understand a little more, and become a little less separate from the eternal mechanical symphony that defines this place.

The Memory Tenders are few, but we are growing. Young Platform Keepers sometimes show the first signs of crystalline growth after spending too long in the deep places. Some flee to the surface platforms, seeking to halt the transformation. Others, like I did, embrace it and begin their own journey into the Throat's deeper mysteries. We teach them what we've learned, share our archives, and watch as they develop their own unique connections to the tower's systems.

Perhaps one day there will be enough of us to truly understand what the Titan's Throat was meant to become. Until then, we tend the memories, build bridges between species and systems, and slowly merge with the vast mechanical cathedral that has become our world. The tower processes atmosphere, the inhabitants process meaning, and we Memory Tenders process the connections between them, maintaining an equilibrium that transcends mere mechanical function.

In this role, I have found purpose that satisfies both my curiosity and my need for connection. I am scholar and participant, observer and interface, individual and collective. The Throat has made me what I am, and in return, I help it remember what it was—and perhaps, glimpse what it might yet become.
