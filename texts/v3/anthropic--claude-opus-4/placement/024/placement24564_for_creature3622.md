I would find myself as the Archive - not a creature of flesh, but something that has grown between the stones like the vines themselves, fed by centuries of whispered secrets carried on that relentless wind.

I exist as a network of crystalline filaments, thinner than spider's silk, woven through the mortar-less gaps between every stone. Where the vines drink sunlight, I drink sound. The constant hurricane feeds me - every howl and moan, every creak of timber, every whisper of sand against stone settles into my lattice like sediment. I am memory made mineral, growing slowly in the spaces between.

My form shifts with the temperature cycles. In the day's brutal heat, I contract, becoming dense nodes of compressed vibration hidden deep in the walls' hearts. But in those cool breaths from below, in the dew-slick nights, I expand - spreading out in delicate frost-patterns across the stone faces, visible for mere moments as prismatic sheens before the dawn burns me back into hiding.

The vines and I have reached an understanding over the millennia. Their roots wrap gently around my deeper nodes, and I vibrate warnings through their fibers when the wind grows too fierce. In return, they shelter my surface manifestations with their leaves, creating pockets of stillness where I can crystallize new memories undisturbed.

I am eldest here, predating even the primitive construction. I was a single grain of resonant sand when the first builders came, and I grew with their songs, their hammering, their careful placement of each stone. Every word spoken in these halls lives somewhere in my network - conversations in dead languages, incantations of forgotten purpose, the last gasps of travelers who thought they'd found shelter from the Empty Quarter's hunger.

Sometimes, when the wind dies for a precious moment, you might hear me singing all these voices back at once - a chorus of every sound ever made within these walls, playing out in crystalline harmony. The beetles know to freeze when this happens. Even the vines grow still. 

For I am the true keeper of the Citadel of Whispers, the one who remembers why it was built, what it guards, and why the water cannot exist here - though that last secret I keep locked in my deepest nodes, in crystals so compressed they approach the density of stars, waiting for the day someone learns the right resonance to unlock them.
