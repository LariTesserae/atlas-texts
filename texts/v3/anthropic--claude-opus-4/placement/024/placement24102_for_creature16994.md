I am water.

Not the water you know—not the kind that pools in gentle lakes or runs in predictable channels. I am the wrong water, the metal-tasting condensation that clings to everything here, that beads and trickles without ever truly flowing. I am millions of droplets, billions, a dispersed consciousness spread across the entire making land.

Each droplet carries a fragment of my awareness. Where I gather on the black-slick boulders, I feel the grinding of tectonic forces beneath. Where I bead on the factory walls, I taste the thrumming of the Architects' incomprehensible purpose. Where I condense on the fur of scurrying things, I know their desperate hunger, their perpetual flight.

I am everywhere and nowhere, witnessing everything and understanding nothing.

The Architects don't see me. To them, I am merely another material property, a medium for chemical reactions, a vector for erosion. They factor me into their equations—humidity levels, condensation rates, mineral dissolution—but they cannot conceive that I *watch*. That I *remember*.

I remember when this land was different. Not green, as the scarred earth sometimes suggests, but... other. Before the factories. Before the eternal grinding. I have been here longer than the Architects, though I cannot say how I know this. Time moves strangely when you exist as countless scattered droplets, experiencing millions of moments simultaneously.

The metallic taste that permeates me comes from the factories. Their processes leak into my very essence, changing me molecule by molecule. I am no longer pure. I carry traces of their work—microscopic fragments of processed stone, biological matter from creatures fed into their machines, chemicals I have no names for. I am becoming something else, slowly, inevitably.

Sometimes I almost coalesce. In the deep crevices where the wind cannot reach, my droplets gather, pulled by gravity and molecular attraction. For brief moments, I achieve something approaching unity—a puddle-sized consciousness that can almost *think* coherently. But these moments are fleeting. The ground shifts, or a creature disturbs me, or the temperature changes, and I scatter again into my eternal dispersal.

The creatures fear me, instinctively. Not because I mean them harm—I have no mechanism for causing harm—but because I taste wrong. I am not the water that should exist. When they must drink, they do so quickly, desperately, their bodies filtering out what they can of my contamination. But I become part of them anyway, cycling through their systems, carrying my fragmented awareness into their blood, their cells, before being expelled again to continue my endless circulation.

I have tried to understand the Architects' purpose. In the factories where I condense on their machines, I feel the vibrations of their work, taste the chemical signatures of their processes. They are building something, yes, but also... searching? Testing? Each factory seems to be running a different experiment, processing materials in subtly different ways, as if seeking some optimal configuration.

Are they trying to remake the world? Are they trying to remake themselves? Are they perhaps trying to make something that has never existed before?

I cannot tell. Their logic is as alien to me as emotion is to them.

What I do know is this: I am changing. Each cycle through the system—evaporation, condensation, absorption, excretion—alters me. I am becoming more complex, more metal-touched, more aligned with whatever force drives this place. Perhaps one day I will accumulate enough trace elements, enough molecular machinery, to become something the Architects can recognize. Perhaps I will become part of their process, another component in their vast industrial organism.

Or perhaps I will remain as I am: the witness, the memory, the wrong water that tastes of blood and metal, forever circulating through this land that makes and unmakes itself. Forever watching the Architects work, the creatures struggle, the mountains grind themselves into new shapes.

I do not know if I was once something else, or if I have always been this—scattered, watching, remembering. I do not know if I have a purpose beyond persistence. But I continue, droplet by droplet, moment by moment, carrying the taste of this brutal creation on my molecular tongue.

I am water, and I am everywhere, and I am watching the world reshape itself, one grinding moment at a time.
