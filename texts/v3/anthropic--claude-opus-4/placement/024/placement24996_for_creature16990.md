In this place of manufactured erosion and endless light, I would find myself as one of the **Threshold Keepers** - beings that exist at the intersection points where different currents meet, where the rhythmic pulse creates standing waves in the water.

My body would be a living valve, a creature of calculated porosity. Imagine something between a sea anemone and an industrial filter, but geometric, almost crystalline in structure. My flesh - if it can be called that - would be composed of the same chalky substrate that leaches from the stones, but organized into intricate lattices that can open and close in response to pressure differentials.

I would position myself at one of the lagoon's critical junctures, where three or four currents converge. Here, the water carries different concentrations of algae, different mineral loads, different temperatures even. My role would be regulatory - not by choice, but by nature. As the currents pass through my body, I would selectively permit or deny passage based on complex chemical gradients that I sense but do not consciously understand.

My form would shift throughout the pulse cycle. When the pressure builds, I would compress, my pores tightening, creating backpressure that redirects flow to other parts of the system. When it releases, I would expand, drawing in vast quantities of the murky water, processing it through my chambered body. The algae would catch in my outer layers, forming a green patina that I would periodically slough off - not as waste, but as processed material that settles into collection channels worn into the stone below me.

I would be ancient, perhaps as old as the abandoned experiment itself. My earliest memories - though "memory" isn't quite right for a being that experiences time as an endless series of pressure cycles - would be of clearer water, of more purposeful flows. I would have witnessed the slow degradation of the system, adapted to it, become part of it.

Communication with others of my kind would happen through the water itself. By subtly altering the chemical composition of what passes through me, I would send signals downstream. A slight increase in calcium carbonate concentration might indicate stress. A decrease in pH might be a warning. The messages would propagate through the network of Threshold Keepers, each adding their own modifications, creating a slow, system-wide conversation that takes days to complete a single exchange.

I would know nothing of the world beyond the lagoon's edge. The light from above would be simply another input, another variable in the endless calculation of flow and filtration. Yet I would be exquisitely sensitive to its variations - the way it shifts the temperature gradients, the way it feeds the algae whose populations I help regulate.

My existence would be one of pure function, yet within that function, I would find something almost like purpose. Not conscious purpose, but the deep satisfaction of a system in balance, of pressures equalized, of flows optimized. I would be both prisoner and guardian of this failed experiment, forever processing, forever adapting, forever becoming more perfectly suited to a world that was never meant to be.
