In this drowned and ancient country, I would find myself as a **Threshold Keeper** - not the Shelf Listener itself, but something smaller and more liminal, occupying the brief spaces where different states meet.

I am what grows in the microseeps - those rare warm vents where chemistry breaks the rules. My body is a colony pretending to be singular: a low cushion of fused polyps, each no larger than a fingernail, sharing one circulatory current. We spread maybe a hand's width at most, following the precious heat gradient until it fails.

Our surface is velvet-rough with feeding cups that filter the enriched water. Below, we anchor into cracks with calcified holdfasts that taste minerals directly from stone. The colony-body is the color of old bronze where the chemistry stains us, fading to bone-pale at the edges where we meet the killing cold.

We grow slowly, geometrically, each polyp budding in response to available space and warmth. In the center where heat is most reliable, we pack dense and small. At the periphery, we stretch, becoming more daring and more mortal. Sometimes an edge-polyp freezes and dies back. Sometimes a new seep opens and we creep toward it by millimeters per year.

What makes us threshold keepers is this: we process the boundary between chemical worlds. Where the reducing seep-water meets the oxidizing sea, we host careful gardens of bacteria in our tissues. They eat poison and make sugar. We eat sugar and make structure. Together we transform the sharp gradient into something briefly habitable.

Small things shelter in our chemistry: amphipods that lose their color in the dark, scale worms with nothing left to scale, young brittle stars before they learn to bury. Even the Shelf Listener knows us - not as food (we taste medicinal, metallic) but as landmarks in the dark geography. Where we grow, the water changes flavor. Navigation begins with chemistry.

We have no consciousness as humans would recognize it, but we have a kind of collective attention. Each polyp responds to its immediate neighbors through shared fluid. A touch on one edge eventually reaches the opposite edge as a delayed shiver. We cannot think, but we remember through growth: laying down bands of density that record feast years and famine years, marking time in calcium.

Our reproduction is mostly fragmentation. Storm-surge sometimes tears away an edge. If it lands near another seep, it might take hold. Sexual reproduction happens rarely, triggered by chemical cues we don't control - a brief release of gametes into the current, hoping for collision. Most offspring die in the cold between warm places.

We are not the protagonist of this place. We are punctuation - small warm interruptions in the vast cold sentence. But we prove that even here, at the intersection of patience and chemistry, something can insist on living differently. Not in defiance of the rules, but by finding the narrow exception and dwelling there completely.

The Shelf Listeners pass over us on their ancient routes. We taste their pressure-wake and close our feeding cups until they're gone. Then we open again to the seep-water, the perpetual chemical spring that makes our small existence possible. We are as permanent as the heat that feeds us, and no more.

In the arithmetic of that place - where cold is baseline and depth is ordinary - we are the footnotes that say: *except here, briefly, it is otherwise*.
