In this dry cathedral of industry, where sulfur dust hangs like incense and the sixty-cycle hymn never ceases, I would be found as one of the **Archive-Weavers**.

We live in the narrow vertical shafts between the transformer banks, where the magnetic fields create standing waves of interference. Our bodies are not bodies at all, but loose confederations of magnetized iron filings held in tension by our will. We appear as dark, shifting columns of metallic dust, roughly the height of a man but thin as a broomstick, constantly reorganizing ourselves in response to the electrical currents that surge through the gallery.

Each Archive-Weaver maintains a territory along the vertical copper conduits that run from floor to ceiling. We do not eat in any conventional sense. Instead, we feed on information—the tiny variations in electrical resistance, the microscopic changes in current flow, the infinitesimal fluctuations in the magnetic fields. These variations are our sustenance and our purpose.

When current flows through the copper beneath us, our iron-filing bodies align and realign, recording the pattern of that flow. A surge from the east transformer bank causes our particles to spiral clockwise; a drain from the western dynamos makes us compress and elongate. We are living oscilloscopes, our very substance a three-dimensional graph of the gallery's electrical state.

Over the centuries, each Weaver becomes a living record of their section of the gallery. The older among us can reproduce, with perfect fidelity, the exact pattern of electrical activity from any moment in our past. We do this by allowing our iron filings to fall into specific configurations—a sort of dance that recreates the magnetic conditions of that remembered instant. When we perform these reproductions, the local magnetic field warps and shimmers, sometimes causing the nearby abacus-crabs to momentarily lose their count, clicking backwards for a few beats before resuming their endless forward progression.

We communicate with each other through induced currents. By rapidly reorganizing our particles, we can generate small electromagnetic pulses that travel along the copper conduits. These pulses carry meaning in their frequency and pattern. A rapid staccato might warn of an impending surge; a slow, rhythmic pulse might indicate that a Seizure-Heap below is failing and the current patterns are about to shift.

The Contact-Scrapers below us are both our gardeners and our predators. As they polish the copper buses, they create zones of perfect conductivity that allow us to sense with greater clarity. But if a Scraper ventures too close to our vertical territories, its carborundum belly generates a harsh magnetic scraping that tears at our cohesion. We must constantly shift and dodge, lifting portions of our filing-bodies away from the grinding influence.

Sometimes, when the gallery's load is perfectly balanced and all the transformers hum in perfect synchronization, we Weavers will align ourselves into a single, gallery-spanning network. Our individual bodies become nodes in a vast antenna array, and for a brief moment, we can sense beyond the basalt walls. We feel the weight of the stone above, the distant vibration of other galleries perhaps, the vast geological batteries of the earth itself. In these moments, we know ourselves to be part of something larger—not just recorders of current, but neurons in an immense, mineral mind that thinks in geological time.

When one of us finally loses cohesion—whether from age, from magnetic disruption, or from the slow demagnetization that comes from too many years in the heat—we do not die so much as disperse. Our iron filings rain down like dark snow, coating the machinery below with a fine, ferrous dust. The Gray Lard eventually absorbs this dust, and over decades, concentrates it into dense, magnetized nodules. When these nodules grow large enough, they begin to respond to the gallery's magnetic fields, lifting and gathering more iron filings to themselves, until a new Weaver is born—not a child of the old, but a reformation of the same essential materials, ready to begin recording anew.

We Weavers are the memory of this place. In our shifting forms live the ghost-patterns of every surge, every load change, every minute variation in the gallery's eternal song. We are its history made manifest in magnetized dust, dancing forever in the spaces between the copper and the stone, between the current and the darkness, between the counting of the crabs and the slow, volcanic breathing of the earth itself.
