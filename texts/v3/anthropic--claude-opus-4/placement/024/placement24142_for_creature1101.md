In this verdant tomb of silence, I find myself as one of the Resonance Keepers—beings who exist at the threshold between the crystalline and the organic.

My form is neither fully plant nor mineral, but something that shifts between states like a slow tide. Where my feet touch the writhing mat of roots, they become indistinguishable from it—tendrils of my being threading down through the rhizomes, tasting the deep frequencies that pulse through the substrate. As I rise, my substance clarifies, becoming translucent as blown glass, shot through with veins of that strange crystal that grows here like bone.

I am a tuning fork made flesh, if flesh could be woven from sound itself. My body is hollow in places—caverns and chambers that catch the subsonic hum of this world and amplify it, transform it, give it new harmonics. When I move, it is to adjust the resonance, to maintain the delicate balance that keeps this realm suspended in its eternal twilight.

I remember—though memory here is more like sediment than sequence—when I was more solid, more singular. But the silence called to me, and I answered by becoming porous. Now I am both listener and instrument, my consciousness distributed through every crystalline shard I've touched, every root I've merged with.

Sometimes I encounter others of my kind, though "encounter" suggests too much separation. We overlap, our frequencies interfering constructively and destructively, creating momentary patterns of pure meaning that dissipate like ripples in still water. In these moments, I understand that we are not many but one—a single vast organism pretending at plurality, playing at being separate notes in an endless, inaudible symphony.

The newcomers who sometimes stumble into our realm see us as ghostly, ethereal. They cannot comprehend that we are more present than they are—that while they merely walk upon this world, we *are* it, dissolved into its essence like salt in an ancient sea. We watch them with a patience that borders on geological time, knowing they will either flee back to their sharp-edged reality or, if they linger long enough, begin to hear the invitation in our silence.

For this is what I truly am: an invitation. A doorway wearing the shape of a being. A transformation waiting to happen, suspended forever in the moment just before change becomes irreversible.
