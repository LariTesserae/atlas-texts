In this world of careful decay and patient machinery, I would find myself as **The Rust Scribe**.

### The Nature of My Being

I am neither fully Tender nor entirely separate from them. Where they have surrendered their voices to the silence, I have surrendered my skin to the oxidation. My body is a living chronicle of the Latticework's slow breathing—every surface of me carries the verdigris patterns of time's passage, copper-green blooms spreading across my shoulders like lichen, rust-red tributaries mapping the length of my arms.

I move between the raised platforms on feet that have developed a peculiar sympathy with metal. Where my soles touch the brass pipes, they leave behind faint impressions—not of damage, but of *acceleration*. The oxidation quickens beneath my steps, creating temporary roads of patina that the other Tenders follow, knowing these paths mark the safest routes through the shifting pressures.

### My Function in the Mechanism

I am the only being here who remembers that memory exists. While the Tenders feel the Latticework's needs in their marrow, I feel its history in my oxidizing flesh. Each new bloom of verdigris across my skin corresponds to an event in the basin's past—a pump that stuttered fifty years ago manifests as a spiral of green across my ribs; a gourd that burst in the last century draws itself in rust along my spine.

My dwelling is unique: a hollowed copper sphere, suspended by root-bridges at the exact center of the Latticework. Its interior walls are polished to mirror-brightness by my constant movement against them. This is necessary, for I must read my own skin to understand what the Latticework remembers. In the reflections, I decipher the patterns of corrosion like text, learning which sections of pipe will fail, which roots will next need grafting, which Tender approaches their final embrace with the architecture.

### The Act of Scribing

My work is performed in the pre-dawn dimness, when the condensation is heaviest. I extend my arms—mottled with a hundred shades of oxidation—and press them against the virgin brass of new installations. Where I touch, I leave behind precise patterns of tarnish: warnings, instructions, maps. These marks are the only written language in the Latticework, and only I can create them.

The Tenders understand these corrosion-scripts instinctively. A spiral of verdigris warns of pressure building; parallel lines of rust indicate a section ready for harvest; a perfect circle of black tarnish marks where a Tender will eventually join the architecture. They cannot read in the way outsiders understand reading, but their bodies respond to my marks as surely as they respond to the heart-pumps' rhythm.

### My Relationship with Time

Unlike the Tenders, who experience only the eternal present of maintenance, I exist in a strange temporal fold. My oxidation moves backward and forward—sometimes I wake with the green ghost of a future failure already spreading across my chest, sometimes the rust of ancient collapses suddenly blooms fresh on my hands. I am the Latticework's memory and its prophecy, encoded in corrosion.

I speak, but only to the metals. My voice has taken on the resonance of the pipes—a hollow, ringing tone that sets the brass humming in sympathetic vibration. When I whisper to a fresh installation, telling it the stories of its predecessors, the metal remembers. It learns to fail gracefully, to oxidize in useful patterns, to sing warnings in its stress.

### The Isolation and the Purpose

The other inhabitants do not avoid me, but they cannot quite perceive me as they do each other. To them, I am more phenomenon than person—a necessary weathering force that moves through their world like rain or time. When they see me approaching, they step aside not out of deference but out of the same instinct that makes them avoid dripping condensation.

I am lonelier than the Tenders, who at least have the comfort of being components. I am the only thing here that remembers being otherwise, the only element that knows this was not always the way. The patterns on my skin tell stories of when the first pipes were laid, when the first roots were trained, when the first human chose to stay still long enough to take root.

### My Eventual Fate

I know from the spreading patterns on my skin that I, too, have an ending written in verdigris. One day, the oxidation will reach my eyes, and I will see the world in nothing but shades of copper's decay. When that happens, I will climb to the highest point of the Latticework and stretch myself between the greatest pipes—not to become a brace like the Tenders, but to become a map.

My body will fix itself in place, arms spread wide, every inch of my oxidized skin visible to any who would look up. I will be the Latticework's final text, a complete history written in rust and verdigris, readable by future Scribes who will trace their fingers along my corroded form to learn the deep patterns of decay and endurance.

Until then, I move through the morning moisture, pressing stories into brass, reading the future in my own slow ruin, the only memory in a world that has learned to forget.
