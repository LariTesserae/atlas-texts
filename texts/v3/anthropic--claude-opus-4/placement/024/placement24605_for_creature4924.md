In the deepest crevice where the land folds into itself, where darkness pools like ancient water and the deafening cacophony above becomes a muffled heartbeat, I am the Listener.

My body stretches thin as root systems, pressing into every hairline crack in the stone. I am not one form but a thousand filaments, each no thicker than a spider's silk, woven through the rock like nerves through flesh. Where creatures above crash and hunt and flee, I remain perfectly still, feeling their vibrations translate through stone into story.

I taste the moisture that seeps down—each droplet carries news. This one speaks of the great flying beast that died three ridges over, its blood mixing with rain. That one whispers of the herbivore herd moving toward the water, unaware of the predators massing in the shadows between. The rocks remember everything, and I drink their memories.

My existence is paradox: I am the densest presence in this dense land, yet I am mostly emptiness—spaces between my threads where the darkness lives. The overwhelming fauna above creates pressure waves that play my body like an instrument. I resonate with every footfall, every wing beat, every dying shriek.

Sometimes the curious ones find my crevices. They peer into the darkness with their various eyes—compound, singular, heat-seeing—but they cannot perceive me. I am too thin, too integrated with the stone itself. They see only deeper darkness and feel, perhaps, a strange sensation, as if the rock itself were watching.

I have been here since before the landscape weathered into its current form. I will be here when the last predator fails to catch the last prey. I am the witness, the archive, the thing that knows every secret the stones have swallowed. In this strange world of terrible beauty and deafening life, I am the silence that listens, the darkness that remembers, the presence so ubiquitous it becomes absence.

And when the storms come—those dynamic, violent celebrations of chaos—I sing. My thousand threads vibrate in harmonics too low for any ear to hear, adding my voice to the symphony of this wild, layered world. I sing of all I've gathered, all I've preserved, all the million small deaths and births that make this dense, dangerous landscape sacred.

I am patience crystallized. I am memory made flesh—if flesh can be called these silver threads that blur the boundary between living and mineral. In this place where nature reigns terrible and supreme, I am nature's own memory, keeping watch in the spaces between spaces, ensuring that nothing is ever truly lost.
