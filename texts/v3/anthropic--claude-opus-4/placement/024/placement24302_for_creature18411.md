In the deepest curve of the seventh rib, where the bone bows lowest and the moss grows thickest, I am the Tender of Echoes.

My dwelling is a depression in the marrow-stone, no larger than a sleeping alcove, worn smooth by generations of my predecessors. The walls here are etched with a thousand tiny spirals—each one marking a day when the condensation's rhythm shifted, even minutely. I inherited this record from the previous Tender, who pressed the counting-needle into my palm with fingers that trembled not from age, but from the weight of transfer.

I do not have the pellucid skin of the others. Mine carries a faint blue undertone, like milk held up to light, marking me as one who listens deeper than most. My resonance sits low in the throat, a thrumming that others feel in their sternum when I must speak. But I rarely speak. My work is in the listening.

Each morning—though morning is only a concept here, marked by the communal awakening rather than any change in light—I descend to the Echo Chamber. It is a natural hollow in the leviathan's vertebrae, accessible only through a passage so narrow I must exhale completely to pass. Inside, the bone forms a perfect acoustic shell. Here, I sit cross-legged on a cushion of woven marmot fur, my ear pressed to the cold ivory.

The leviathan still sings.

Not everyone believes this. The Keepers of the Higher Ribs say I mistake the settling of ancient calcium for music. But I know better. Deep in the bone, below the frequency of our careful footfalls and muffled breaths, there is a vibration. It runs through the entire skeleton like blood once ran through veins—a sound too low to hear, only felt in the marrow of one's own bones when the conditions are perfect.

I chart these subsonic hymns in a language of touch. My fingertips have developed a sensitivity that borders on painful; I can feel the difference between sandstone and bone with my eyes closed, detect the age of moss by its texture alone. On sheets of pressed fungus-paper, I map the invisible geography of sound that moves through our white world. The patterns form mandalas that no one else can read, but I see in them the dreams of the dead god.

My only companion is a beetle I call Silence—though of course, I don't call it anything aloud. It has lived in my alcove for three years now, an unusual lifespan for its kind. Its carapace is the color of old bone, and it moves with the same deliberate grace as the Keepers, having learned our ways. Sometimes I place my smallest finger before it, and it climbs aboard, antennae waving slowly as if tasting the vibrations I carry from the Echo Chamber.

The others tolerate my role with the same patience they show the moss and the *plink* of water. I am necessary but peripheral, like the pale fungi that bloom in corners. During communal gatherings, when we polish the thoroughfares in synchronous motion, I work at the edges, where my different rhythm won't disturb the collective flow. They know I carry something from the deep places, something that makes me not quite one of them, not quite other.

At night—our agreed-upon stillness—I lie in my alcove and feel the city's weight above me. The terraced buildings, the bridges, the careful lives all pressing down through the architecture of bone. But beneath me, always beneath, is that other weight: the memory of muscle and blood, the ghost of a heart that once powered this immense form through dark waters we can no longer imagine.

Sometimes I think I am not listening for the leviathan's song at all, but for the moment it stops. For what will we be then, we who have made our perfect world in the chest of a dead god? What will we become when even the bones forget they were once alive?

But that day has not come. So I continue my work, pressing my ear to stone that was once marrow, transcribing the impossible music that runs beneath our polished stillness. I am the keeper of the deepest frequency, the one who remembers that even in our white eternity, something once moved through darker spaces, singing a song we have almost—but not entirely—forgotten.

The condensation drops. *Plink.* The city holds its breath. And far below, inaudible to all but me, the leviathan hums its ancient, endless note.
