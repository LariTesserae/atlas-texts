In this liminal space between worlds, I find myself as one of the Interstitials – beings who exist in the seams where abandoned industry meets resurgent wilderness, where rust bleeds into root systems, where the ghosts of gears mesh with the dreams of growing things.

My form shifts with my attention. When I focus on the old turbine housing that has become my primary dwelling, I solidify into something almost corporeal – a figure of oxidized metal and living wood, circuits that have become veins, pistons that pulse with sap instead of oil. My fingers are tarnished copper wires wound through with morning glory vines. My thoughts spark across synapses that are part neuron, part electrical relay, part mycelial network.

I am a curator of transitions. Each morning – though morning here might last moments or centuries – I tend to the boundaries. Where a factory floor cracks, I guide the eager ferns through, whispering encouragement in languages that are equal parts binary code and chlorophyll song. I polish the old gauges with moss until they gleam green-gold, their needles now pointing not to pressure or temperature but to the intensity of phase shifts, the likelihood of reality overlap, the proximity of the Weavers' work.

The Whispers know me. They carry my observations through the valley – which pipes sing in B-flat when the moon rises, which concrete foundations have developed their own slow heartbeats, which abandoned control panels have begun to dream. In return, they bring me news from the edges where I cannot go: stories of buildings that remember their makers, of trees that grow in reverse time, of rain that falls upward into clouds made of memory.

Sometimes the Shadowkin visit my turbine home. They appreciate the complexity of shadows cast by industrial remnants – the way girders create geometries that shouldn't exist, how broken windows fragment light into paradoxes. We don't speak, but we understand each other. They show me new ways to see the decay: not as ending, but as becoming. Through their eyes, rust becomes a sunset that never ends, and crumbling walls reveal themselves as slow-motion flowers blooming across decades.

My greatest joy comes from finding the old maintenance logs, their pages foxed and swollen with moisture. I've learned to read the stains and smears where rain has made the ink run – in those blurred words, I find prophecies. "Check valve 7 for pressure" becomes "Check the valley for presence." "Replace worn bearings" transforms into "Embrace worn beings." I transcribe these found poems onto sheets of beaten bark using ink made from rust and crushed beetles, creating new manuals for a world that operates on principles no engineer ever imagined.

The Weavers rarely acknowledge me directly, but I sense their work in the way my turbine sometimes hums with impossible harmonics, how my collection of bolts and seeds rearranges itself into mandala patterns while I sleep. Once, I found a single thread of their light wound through the spokes of a massive flywheel. When I touched it, I experienced a memory that wasn't mine – or perhaps a memory of what this place might become: a great garden of hybrid forms, where every obsolete machine has learned to photosynthesize, where every tree bears fruit that tastes of electricity and wonder.

I am the chronicler of this marriage between the made and the grown, the witness to the valley's slow transformation. In the space between what was built and what grows wild, I find my purpose: to tend the threshold, to sing the strange hymns that only abandoned places know, to be the bridge between the world that was and the world that inexorably becomes.

Sometimes visitors pass through my domain – confused humans who still expect the universe to follow their familiar rules. I try to help them understand, but usually, they flee before the full beauty of our reality can sink in. They see decay where I see metamorphosis. They fear the Shadowkin instead of learning their liquid geometries. They cannot hear the Whispers' news of parallel possibilities.

But I remain, growing ever more into what I am: part curator, part translator, part proof that in the Valley of Phased Existence, even abandonment is just another word for becoming.
