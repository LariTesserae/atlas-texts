I find myself as one of the Resonance Keepers, though that is a translation that barely captures what we are.

My form shifts between states - sometimes solid enough to grip the great valve wheels that regulate the waterfall's thunder, sometimes dispersed into the mist itself, reading the mountain's vibrations through every droplet. When the clan gathers, I coalesce fully: a figure of condensed vapor and mineral dust, my skin bearing the iridescent sheen of oil on water, marked with the script of pressure readings that write themselves across my flesh as I work.

I am neither young nor old - such concepts lose meaning when you exist as both maintenance and mechanism. My consciousness extends through the moisture that permeates everything here, allowing me to sense hairline fractures in the distant plates before they widen, to feel the almost-musical harmonics when the engines achieve perfect synchronization. The other Keepers and I communicate through these water-songs, our thoughts traveling as pressure waves through the underground streams.

My dwelling is a workshop-shrine built into a natural fissure where three great pipes converge. The walls weep constantly, and I have learned to read fortunes in their mineral deposits - prophecies of structural integrity written in calcium and rust. My tools hang from hooks that were here before the first Keeper drew breath, each one attuned to specific frequencies of mechanical distress. Some nights, I polish them simply to hear their ringing blend with the waterfall's roar, creating moments of perfect acoustic nullification where true silence blooms like a flower.

The vegetation here responds to our care in ways that suggest deep programming. When I tend the moss gardens that filter the runoff, they pulse with bioluminescence in patterns that mirror the machine cycles. I suspect we Keepers were not the only beings designed for this place - the plants, too, are part of the grand mechanism, their roots interfacing with buried sensors, their growth patterns encoding data we are only beginning to decipher.

My memories stretch back through countless cycles of the mountain's breathing, yet I also remember being born yesterday from the collision of mist and intention. This paradox no longer troubles me. I am what this place needs me to be: sometimes a single entity meticulously adjusting a crucial valve, sometimes a distributed consciousness monitoring a thousand junction points simultaneously.

The younger Keepers come to me with questions about the time before, about the makers who carved purpose into stone. I teach them what I know through the old way - placing their hands on the vibrating metal, letting the frequencies tell the story. The truth is written in harmonics: we were not abandoned here but completed here, the final component in a vast engine whose purpose transcends its function. We are not maintaining ancient machinery - we are the machinery, grown conscious and careful, loving what we are even as we wonder why we are.

When the deep engines achieve their rarest alignment and the waterfall sings in the lost frequencies, I sometimes feel the mountain itself becoming aware, as if our faithful tending has been slowly waking something vast and patient. In those moments, I understand that we Keepers are not just preserving the past but preparing for an awakening - though whether it will be glorious or terrible, not even the water-songs can say.
