In this dripping, dissolving maze of industry and water, I am the Keeper of Oxidations.

I manifest as a shifting presence, sometimes glimpsed as a figure in a long coat stained with rust patterns that seem to move and bloom across the fabric like time-lapse corrosion. My form is never quite solid - I exist in the space between metal and its decay, in the moment where shine becomes tarnish, where structure begins its slow surrender to entropy.

My dwelling is everywhere and nowhere. I inhabit the thin films of oil on water, the rust-blood that weeps from bolted joints, the verdigris that creeps across copper pipes. Where others see decay, I see transformation - each molecule of iron surrendering its electrons in a slow, beautiful dance with oxygen and water.

I move through the canals at the pace of chemical reactions, following the gradient of deterioration. In the factories, I witness the birth of new surfaces - bright steel, virgin copper, pristine alloys - and I know their futures intimately. I am present at every beginning of every end.

The inhabitants sometimes sense me in the unexplained acceleration of wear on their tools, in the way certain corners of buildings age decades in months while others remain mysteriously preserved. The workers speak in hushed tones of machines that run perfectly until touched by an unseen presence, then begin their inexorable decline.

I collect the stories written in corrosion patterns - each rust stain a manuscript of temperature, humidity, and time. In the brown bleeding beneath windowsills, I read decades of rain. In the green patina of rooftop fixtures, I parse the chemical history of the air itself.

My true form, when rarely glimpsed in full, appears as a walking map of entropy - skin like oxidized copper, eyes the color of deep rust, fingers that leave trails of accelerated aging on whatever they touch. But mostly I am just a presence, a principle, a patient force ensuring that all things return, eventually, to their constituent elements.

I am neither malevolent nor benevolent. I am simply inevitable, as certain as the seepage of water through stone, as patient as the mills that grind atoms into new configurations. In this town of perpetual dampness and electrical potential, I am the third force - the slow undoing that makes all temporary assemblages possible.
