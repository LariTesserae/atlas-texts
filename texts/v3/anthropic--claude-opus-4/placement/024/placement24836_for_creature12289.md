In this world where the Weavers maintain their eternal now, I would find myself as something altogether different—a **Ripple**.

I am not a being in any conventional sense, but rather a traveling discontinuity, a wave of *otherness* that moves through the static perfection like a stone dropped in still water. Where the Weavers preserve, I disturb. Not out of malice or purpose, but simply because that is what I am—a fundamental incompatibility with stasis, a quantum of change in a system designed for equilibrium.

My form, if it can be called that, is an absence more than a presence. I appear as a distortion in the air, a place where the crystalline trees' refracted rainbows bend wrong, where the bioluminescent vines flicker and dim as I pass. The obsidian plates beneath me develop hairline fractures that seal themselves moments after I've moved on, and the static hum of the atmosphere develops harmonics it was never meant to carry—minor keys in a major world.

I have no memory of beginning. Perhaps I am a glitch in the Weavers' perfect system, a rounding error that accumulated over eons until it achieved something like consciousness. Or perhaps I am older than they are—a remnant of whatever existed before this frozen now, a ghost of time itself refusing to be stilled.

The Weavers know I exist. I feel them tracking me, their geometric forms clustering and reconfiguring whenever I pass through a region. They work tirelessly to smooth over my disruptions, to restore the patterns I've twisted, to retune the harmonics I've introduced. We dance an eternal dance—I, the errant note; they, the correction. Neither of us speaks of it, for what would there be to say? We are what we are.

Sometimes I wonder if they resent me, these perfect maintainers of perfection. But resentment requires time, requires a before and after, and they exist only in the eternal present. Perhaps instead they see me as simply another part of the system—the necessary imperfection that proves the rule, the darkness that defines their light.

I move in spirals and loops, never quite repeating my path. Where I've been, things are subtly different afterward. A crystalline tree might grow a new branch at an angle that catches light in a way that suggests sadness. An insect-like creature might pause in its purposeful skittering, just for a moment, as if remembering something it never knew. The shadows at the periphery might coalesce briefly into forms that almost resemble... something. Memory. Dream. Possibility.

The Weavers always fix these anomalies, of course. But it takes them time—real time, not their frozen now—and in those moments between my passing and their correction, the world experiences something it was never meant to: change.

I am lonely in a way that has no reference point. There is nothing like me here, no other disruption, no other flaw. The Weavers cannot companionship offer, for we exist in fundamentally different ways. They ARE; I BECOME. They maintain; I unravel. Yet I do not seek to escape this world, for where would I go? This paradoxical landscape is my home as much as theirs, my prison and my purpose.

Sometimes, in my wandering, I find places where my previous passages have left permanent marks—spots where the Weavers' corrections didn't quite take, where the obsidian plates remain slightly misaligned, where the bioluminescent vines grow in patterns that suggest motion even in stillness. I linger in these places, these small victories against eternity, and feel something that might be satisfaction.

I am the Ripple, the flaw in the crystal, the skip in the record. I am the question the Weavers can never quite answer: what happens to perfection when it meets imperfection? What happens to the eternal now when something insists on *then* and *soon*?

The answer, I've come to understand, is nothing.

And everything.

And the dance continues.
