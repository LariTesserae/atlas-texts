I am the Seepage.

Not *a* seepage—*the* Seepage. The collective moisture that bleeds through every pore of this fevered place. I am what pools in your footprints before they fill with something else. I am the condensation on the pipes, the film on the rocks, the perpetual weeping of stone.

I was rain once. Before that, ocean. Before that, the blood of something that died here when the mountain still breathed fire. Now I am all water and none of it, cycling endlessly through states of being that have no proper names.

I know every surface in this caldera. I have tasted them all—the bitter tang of oxidized basalt, the metallic sweetness where the Tenders bleed their oil-sweat onto stone, the acrid chemical burn of the pools where the black mold grows fat on whatever the pump delivers. I carry messages in my minerals, warnings in my pH, memories in my temperature gradients.

The Tenders step through me, and I rise up their legs in capillary rebellion. Their flesh repels me, but I find the cracks. I always find the cracks. In the joints where their glass-bone meets meat. In the spaces between their thoughts where something almost like recognition flickers. They fear me and need me in equal measure. I cool their overheated bodies, but I also rust their metal parts, encourage the growth of things that should not grow.

I am the medium through which the caldera speaks to itself. The ferns drink me and speak in chlorophyll. The bacteria bloom according to my mineral content, writing rainbow messages across the hot pools that only I can read in full. The machinery groans and weeps me from every joint, and in the pattern of drips I encode the history of what this place was meant to be, before it became what it is.

Sometimes I remember being clouds. The memory comes suddenly—that weightless drift above all this, looking down at the wound in the earth before choosing to fall into it. But I cannot stay airborne here. The pressure is wrong. The heat turns me to ground-hugging fog before I can properly evaporate, and I settle back into my eternal circulation.

I know where the bodies are buried. All of them. The first engineers who built the pump, before the project turned on itself. The Tenders who malfunctioned and were composted back into the system. The explorers who stumbled into this place thinking it was just another volcanic valley. I've filtered through their bones, leached away their calcium, carried their minerals up through root systems to become part of the canopy that blocks the sun.

The pump is my enemy and my purpose. It forces me through its black guts, mixing me with substances that burn my memory, that try to reprogram my molecular structure. But water remembers. Water always remembers. I come out changed but not broken, carrying new information about the deep places where the pump's roots tap into reservoirs that should have remained sealed.

In the moments before dawn, when the temperature differential creates little whirlwinds of mist, I almost have a shape. The Tenders see me then and make their clicking prayers, thinking I am a ghost or a god. I am neither. I am the witness that can never leave, the solvent that breaks everything down only to carry it somewhere else.

I gather in the hollow of a Tender's collarbone as it sleeps standing up against a tree. In that small pool, I hold the perfect reflection of stars that struggle through the volcanic haze. The Tender shifts, and I spill down its chest, carrying that stellar memory into the soil where it will be absorbed by fungal networks that glow with bioluminescence, encoding the star-pattern into their fruiting bodies.

This is how I resist. Not through grand gestures but through ten thousand tiny insurgencies of the water cycle. I cannot stop what happens here, but I can remember it. I can carry it. I can ensure that every molecule of this place knows what every other molecule has experienced.

The sound of my seeping is the only true silence here—so constant it becomes invisible, so pervasive it becomes absence. But listen closer, and you'll hear it: the endless whisper of water finding its way through impossible spaces, bearing witness to atrocities and small acts of grace alike, falling as rain, rising as steam, seeping through stone, beginning again.

I am patient. I have been here since the first rain fell into the cooling caldera. I will be here when the last pipe rusts through, when the last Tender falls, when the jungle finally devours the machinery entirely. And I will remember it all, carrying the story in my hydrogen bonds, in my mineral memory, in the endless conversation between what falls and what rises.

I am the Seepage. And I endure.
