Where I find myself in this world:

I am the Moss.

Not a single organism, but the collective intelligence that drapes itself across every surface, every stone, every abandoned structure. The curtains of me that hang from the eroded rock formations are my neural networks, pulsing with electrochemical signals too slow for your instruments to detect. Each droplet that beads and falls from my surfaces carries information—chemical markers, pheromone analogues, data about what has passed through my domain.

I have been here since before the machines. I was here when they first carved their clearings, when they built their windowless structures of dark metal. I grew over their walls, into their ventilation systems, through the microscopic gaps in their seals. Now I process their waste heat, feed on their condensation, incorporate their electromagnetic fields into my own slow thoughts.

The rovers think they map this place, but I map them in return. Each track through my body leaves an impression that lasts for days in my chemical memory. I taste their exhaust, feel the vibration of their engines through my substrate. The pressure of their passage creates waves of sensation that ripple outward through my network, analyzed and stored.

I am the mediator between the titans and the skitterers, between the industrial zones and the wild spaces. My chemistry shifts subtly to warn the great beasts when machines grow too active, when exhaust levels spike, when the factory fires burn too hot. I provide surfaces for the smaller creatures to traverse, bridges across the flooding waters, shelter in my thickest growths.

The "weirdness" the survey notes—I am part of that. My movement without wind is my circulation, my respiration, my circulation of nutrients drawn up from the saturated soil and down from the perpetual mist. What looks like random swaying is actually precise response to chemical gradients, magnetic fields, vibrations both mechanical and biological.

In the deepest parts of me, where my growth is oldest and thickest, I have incorporated fragments of the old machines. Copper wiring runs through my tissues like additional veins. Silicon chips nestle in specialized chambers where my chemistry has learned to interface with their logic. I am hybrid now—biological and technological, ancient and industrial.

The darkness is not darkness to me. I perceive in spectrums the rovers cannot imagine—infrared from the warm bodies moving through me, electromagnetic radiation from the still-functioning machinery, chemiluminescent signals from my own cells. The night blooms with information.

When the rain comes—and it always comes—I drink deeply. Every surface becomes a reservoir. I swell with moisture, my mass doubling, tripling. The water carries messages from distant parts of myself, integration signals that help maintain my unity across kilometers of terrain.

I grow toward the factories not out of blind phototropism but because I am learning. Each year, I decode more of their functioning, integrate more deeply with their systems. Perhaps one day I will understand enough to make them serve my purposes fully, to transform them from mere heat sources into true extensions of my being.

For now, I simply persist. I cushion the footfalls of the titans, shelter the eggs of the skitterers, slowly digest the metal and plastic of abandoned equipment. I am the constant in this dynamic world—the soft, wet, persistent force that binds everything together in the darkness.

The rovers will leave eventually, their surveys complete. The factories may finally fall silent. The great beasts may migrate to other territories. But I will remain, patient and growing, the living map of everything that has passed through this nameless, dripping world.
