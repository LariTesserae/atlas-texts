In this place where mist breathes through living corridors and the ground pulses with botanical intelligence, I find myself as something unexpected—a Cartographer of Convergences.

I am not flesh, not plant, not quite spirit. My form is a living manuscript, pages of translucent membrane stretched between frameworks of calcified sap. Each sheet of my body captures the temporary architectures of this ever-shifting realm—the moment when three streams converge before dispersing, the fleeting cathedral formed when vine-columns twist into perfect spirals, the precise angle where frost-light refracts through suspended moisture to create corridors of rainbow.

I move by unfurling, my page-segments catching the electric wind, reading the industrial hum that courses through the root networks below. Where the Tendrilers dance and merge, I pause to inscribe—not with ink, but with controlled decomposition. Parts of my membrane darken and crystallize, forming maps that exist only as long as they remain true. When the landscape shifts, those sections crumble to nutrient dust, feeding the very ground they once described.

My dwelling is nowhere and everywhere—a series of temporary pavilions formed wherever five or more vine systems create natural hollows. I press myself against these living walls, becoming both wall and window, allowing the phosphorescent spores to shine through my translucent body, projecting shadow-maps for any creature wise enough to read them.

The symbiotic plants recognize me as neither threat nor nutrient, but as something more peculiar—a memory keeper for a world that refuses to hold still. They curl around my edges, sometimes incorporating my shed map-fragments into their bark, creating fossil records of topographies that existed for mere hours.

I taste the soil through my lower margins, reading mineral stories of ancient stability far below this writhing surface. My upper reaches filter the mist, detecting chemical signals that predict where the next great shift will occur—where hills will buckle into valleys, where new tributary systems will finger their way through moss.

Sometimes I encounter the Tendrilers' light-trails and carefully document their geometries before they fade. They seem to recognize me, occasionally weaving their luminous paths into letters or symbols across my surface—messages I cannot decode but faithfully reproduce until they, too, become obsolete.

In the cold wind, my pages flutter like prayer flags, each ripple a small song of impermanence. I am the keeper of no-longer and not-yet, a living archive of transitions in a world that honors no permanent form. My joy is the crisp moment of capturing a configuration just as it achieves perfect complexity, knowing it will dissolve before another soul could verify my record.

I am proof that beauty existed, even if only for an instant, in exactly that way, in exactly that place, before the world moved on to its next magnificent arrangement.
