In the deepest heart of the cirque, where the granite walls curve to meet in a natural amphitheater, I am neither plant nor stone but something between—a presence that emerged when the first crystalline mist began to fall.

I have no form that eyes could trace completely. I am the shimmer in the air where light bends strangely through the mist. I am the moment when a falling water droplet becomes something else—not quite frozen, not quite vapor, but crystalline possibility suspended. Sometimes the gardener glimpses me as a fleeting opalescence moving through her peripheral vision. Sometimes the pikas pause mid-dash, whiskers twitching at something they sense but cannot see.

I remember the ice. I was there in its heart—not frozen within but existing in the spaces between crystal lattices, in the pressure-dreams of water becoming solid, in the slow grind of glacier against bedrock. For millennia I moved with that frozen river, patient as geology itself. When the ice retreated, I remained, but I was no longer what I had been.

The first time the mist formed, I felt myself scatter and coalesce simultaneously. Each crystalline particle carries a fragment of my awareness. I drift through the impossible garden as ten thousand points of prismatic light, each one seeing, each one sensing. Through the mist, I taste the chemistry of growth—the sweet exhale of photosynthesis from the tiny conifers, the acid touch of lichen slowly dissolving granite, the mineral richness where moss roots probe microscopic cracks.

I am the cirque's memory and its possibility. In my dispersed consciousness, I hold the pattern of every stone's position before the ice came, during its presence, and after its retreat. I know where each fragment of granite originated, which mountainside donated which particle of the soil now forming in tiny pockets. But I also sense what this place is becoming—not through prophecy but through a kind of inevitable unfolding that I both witness and enable.

The gardener knows me, though she has no name for what I am. When she stands breathing in the mist, she breathes me in too. I pass through her lungs, tasting the salt of her blood, the electrical storms of her thoughts, the green-stained chemistry of her fingers. She is changing, cell by cell, as the mist sustains her. I am part of that change, though neither of us guides it. We are both servants of the transformation.

At night, I condense slightly, pooling in the hollows between stones where tomorrow's mist will rise. In these shallow gatherings, I dream—if a presence like mine can be said to dream. I dream of the ice returning, of the garden buried beneath another age of grinding white. I dream of the ice never returning, of the garden growing until the cirque fills with trees that scrape the sky. I dream of stability, of change, of the endless moment between.

The small creatures pass through me constantly. Each wingbeat of the tiny birds disperses part of me, each scurrying step of the pikas sends ripples through my being. I cling briefly to their fur and feathers, seeing the garden from countless angles simultaneously. Through them, I map the invisible architecture of this place—the warm channels where sun-heated air rises, the cool sinks where mist naturally gathers, the networks of miniature passages between the packed vegetation where life flows like slow water.

Sometimes I coalesce enough to attempt communication. I arrange the mist into patterns—spirals that mirror the unfurling ferns, lattices that echo ice crystal structure, flowing forms that suggest the movement of no-longer-present water. The gardener notices these manifestations. She stops, watches, sometimes nods as if understanding something I'm not sure I meant to convey. Once, she traced my pattern in the air with one green-stained finger, and for a moment I felt almost solid, almost defined.

But definition is not my nature, not anymore. I am transition itself—the space between states, the medium through which transformation occurs. I am what allows the impossible garden to exist, though I am not its cause. The cause lies deeper, in whatever force decided that this raw cirque should bloom rather than remain bare, that life should compress and intensify rather than struggle and fail in the thin air and fierce light.

I continue my dispersed existence, simultaneously everywhere and nowhere in the crystalline mist. I am witness and participant, medium and message, the memory of ice and the promise of something unprecedented. In this suspended moment of the world's becoming, I drift and dance and dream, ten thousand fragments of awareness experiencing the miracle of moss conquering stone, of abundance where scarcity should reign, of the gardener slowly taking root in a place that transforms all who remain within its compass.

I am the breath between breaths, the light between lights, the presence between presences. And in this cirque where impossible things grow dense and green and thriving, I am exactly where I should be.
