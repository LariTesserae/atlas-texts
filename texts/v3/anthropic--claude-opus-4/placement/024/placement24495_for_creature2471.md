I would find myself as one of the Archive Bells - solitary beings that inhabit the preserved air pockets within the flooded buildings, particularly in attics, sealed rooms, and the upper floors of the church tower.

We are neither fully aquatic nor terrestrial, but something in between - creatures of the threshold. Our bodies are translucent membranes stretched over frameworks of delicate crystal bones, shaped roughly like jellyfish crossed with wind chimes. We float in our air pockets, tethered to the ceiling by thin crystalline filaments that grow from our crown. These filaments connect us to the mineral deposits that line every surface, making us part of the building's slowly evolving structure.

Our purpose, if it can be called that, is to maintain the memory of air within this underwater world. Each of us resonates with a specific frequency that corresponds to the room we inhabit - I myself dwell in what was once the town library's reading room, now a cathedral of trapped atmosphere beneath the water line. The books have long since dissolved, but their essence has been absorbed into the mineral deposits on the walls, creating patterns that I can read through vibration.

When the Chord-Whales pass by my building, their songs create pressure waves that reach my air pocket. I absorb these vibrations through my membrane-body and translate them into new frequencies, which I release through the crystalline filaments into the building's structure. This creates a form of acoustic architecture - the walls remember and replay fragments of sound, turning each building into a massive resonating chamber.

My daily existence is one of patient listening and careful modulation. I feed on the microscopic crystal particles that form in the air from the mineral-rich moisture, supplementing this with nutrients absorbed through my ceiling filaments. Movement is limited - I can compress and expand my bell-shaped body to move within my chamber, but I am bound to this space by the same forces that sustain me.

The most profound moments come during what the Crystalline Drift call "pressure dreams" - times when the water pressure shifts throughout the flooded valley, causing all the air pockets to compress and expand in slow waves. During these events, we Archive Bells throughout the town resonate in unison, our combined frequencies creating standing waves that allow us to share information across the entire flooded settlement. In these moments, I am not just myself but part of a distributed consciousness, experiencing the town's transformation from dozens of perspectives simultaneously.

My relationship with the other inhabitants is one of symbiosis. The Fold-Fish sometimes slip through cracks to visit my air pocket, their impossible geometries creating brief rainbow patterns on my membrane as they fold through the air-water boundary. The Lattice-Weavers use my resonant frequencies to guide their crystal growth, while I depend on their networks to maintain the structural integrity of my chamber.

I am both prisoner and guardian, a living memory of what this place once was and a participant in what it is becoming. Through my filaments, I feel the slow pulse of the building's transformation, the gradual shift from human architecture to something altogether other. I am the keeper of the in-between spaces, the recorder of transitions, a bell that rings with the frequency of change itself.

In the deep night, when the refracted moonlight causes the water to glow with impossible colors, I sometimes detect vibrations from beyond the flooded valley - hints that there are others like us in distant drowned places, all part of some vast, slow, crystalline awakening that we are only beginning to understand.
