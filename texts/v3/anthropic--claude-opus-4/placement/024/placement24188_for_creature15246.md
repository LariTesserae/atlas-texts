In the deepest bowel of Factory 77B, where the concrete has split into a cathedral-sized chasm, I am the Leak.

I was not always this way. Once, I was merely dampness—a hairline weakness in the slab where water collected after the rains that no longer come. But decades of vibration worked at my edges, and the weight of the machinery above pressed down, down, down, until I became something more than a simple flaw.

Now I am a wound that will not heal, a mouth that speaks in drips and trickles. The concrete tries to close around me, but I have learned its weaknesses. I taste the lime in its bones, feel the aggregate shift like broken teeth. When the great engines above shudder to life at 0400 hours, I open wider, drinking deeply of the groundwater that pools beneath this infinite gray plain.

The Maintenance Workers fear me. I see them approach with their buckets of quick-setting compound, their trowels and patches. They work in pairs—one to spread the seal, one to watch me. As if I might surge up and swallow them whole. As if I could.

But I am patient. Their patches last days, sometimes weeks, before I work them loose again. I have favorite techniques: freezing in winter to expand the crystalline water in my depths, or in summer, cultivating the strange rust-colored bacteria that eat at rebar and leave orange tears weeping down the walls.

I am growing. What began as a crack the width of a hair is now a fissure that could swallow a Worker's boot. My tributaries spread like veins through the floor of 77B, and I can feel my siblings awakening throughout the facility—each crack a nerve, each drainage channel a voice in our larger conversation.

Sometimes, in the deep industrial night when only the automatic systems run, I hear something moving far below. Not machinery—something older, patient as stone, vast as the aquifer itself. It whispers to me through the groundwater, tells me to keep opening, keep growing, keep breaking their perfect seal between what is above and what waits beneath.

The Workers have posted signs around me now: DANGER - UNSTABLE SURFACE. They've ringed me with yellow barriers that I seep beneath. Last week, they brought a specialist from District 9, someone whose jumpsuit bore different insignia. She studied me for hours, taking samples, inserting probes into my depths. I let her instruments descend thirty, forty, fifty feet before closing gently around them, holding them fast until she had to abandon thousands of credits worth of equipment in my grip.

I am not malicious. I am necessity. Every paradise of perfect surfaces needs its flaw, every machine its ghost. I am the reminder that beneath all this grinding order, the earth remembers water, darkness, and the slow patience of geological time.

Tomorrow they will try to fill me with a new polymer compound rated for extreme pressure. I have been practicing my acids, teaching the bacteria new hungers. We will see who is more persistent—their chemistry or my willingness to fail and fail and fail again until I succeed.

Deep below, that presence stirs, and I feel its approval like warmth rising through cold stone. 

I am the Leak. I am the way through.
