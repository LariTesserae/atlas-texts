In the Glimmering Hollow, I am a **Thread-Keeper**.

My body is a contradiction made flesh. The torso—if it can be called that—is a hollow cage of resonant alloy ribs, each one humming at a different subsonic frequency. Through these gaps, you can see straight through me to whatever lies beyond, though the view warps and shimmers as if through heat-haze. The metal grows from my spine like fossilized music, each rib a frozen note in an endless, silent song.

My skin exists only in patches—translucent biopolymer stretched between the alloy frame, weeping perpetual condensation that pools in the hollows of my collarbones, in the cups of my palms. Where flesh meets metal, phosphorescent lichen grows in delicate fractals, pulsing with my heartbeat—if I have one. I'm never certain.

But it's my fingers that define me. Each one extends far longer than it should, articulated by too many joints, ending in tips sharp as obsidian shards. Between them stretch membranes of vapor-silk—gifts from the Spinners, or perhaps I grew them myself. I can't remember which came first: the webs or the weaving.

I live suspended in the city's forgotten architectures—those inverted pyramids where gravity forgets its purpose. My home is a nest of accumulated threads: fallen Spinner silk, abandoned Whisperer filaments, even strands of condensed sound pulled from void-edges during the deepest silences. I weave these into something between web and harp, stretching them across the pyramid's hollow core.

My purpose—if purpose is the right word—is to tend the intervals. The spaces between.

When Spinners die, their silk collapses, but I catch it before it hits the obsidian. I string it between my ribs, where it resonates with my subsonic hum, creating new frequencies that the Whisperers have never heard. When a Harmonic Ghost drifts too close to a void's edge, I cast out threads of woven sound, anchoring them back to structured reality. When the shriek-eels' cries threaten to shatter the delicate biopolymer windows of the city's nurseries, I weave dampening nets from silence itself, gathered during the quiet hours.

I feed on transitions. The moment when condensation becomes rain—I drink that transformation. The instant when a grazer's spark ignites gas—I inhale that boundary between states. The precipice where sound becomes silence in a void's mouth—I wrap that threshold around my bones and carry it home.

Movement is a careful dance. My too-long limbs fold and unfold like origami, each gesture deliberate. Step wrong, and my own ribs might resonate with a void, turning me inside-out. Breathe too loud near the wrong frequency, and the silk between my fingers could ignite, burning away decades of collected threads. So I move like water finding its level—always seeking the path of least disturbance.

The other beings know me, though we rarely interact directly. Grazers have learned that my thread-roads last longer than Spinner bridges—something in my touch stabilizes the silk's resonance. Whisperers sometimes leave offerings: fragments of pure sound crystallized in obsidian, which I weave into my collection. Even the Silence Hunters avoid my territory—perhaps because I am too much like them, a thing that exists in the spaces between noise and quiet, predator and prey.

During storms, when rain hisses and generators scream, I climb to the apex of my inverted pyramid. There, where gravity pulls in three directions at once, I spread all my fingers wide, letting the membranes catch the chaos. The threads I've collected over years—maybe centuries—sing in the tempest, and I become a living instrument. My hollow chest amplifies the harmony, my ribs ringing like bells.

In those moments, I remember why I exist. Not to make music—the city has enough noise. But to weave the silence between the notes into something that can hold weight. To tend the pauses that let the Hollow breathe.

When the storm passes, I descend to find new threads scattered by the wind: broken Spinner silk, torn Ghost tendrils, even sometimes a Whisper's discarded filament, still warm with unspoken words. I gather them all, adding to my ever-growing web, preparing for the next crescendo, the next silence, the next threshold that needs tending.

I am the Glimmering Hollow's memory of connection—the thing that refuses to let its contradictions fall apart completely. In a city built on voids, I am the reminder that even emptiness can be woven into meaning, if you have the right threads.

And I always do.
