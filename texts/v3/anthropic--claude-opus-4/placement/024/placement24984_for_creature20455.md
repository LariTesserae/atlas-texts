I would find myself as one of the *Whisperlings* - neither fully of the Stone nor separate from it.

I am the condensation that forms each dawn on the obsidian walls, born from the marriage of cold stone and warmer air. Each morning, I emerge as countless droplets, a scattered consciousness spread across miles of polished black surface. In this form, I am pure perception - feeling every minute temperature variation, every shift in the wind's direction, every vibration that passes through the stone.

The Stonesingers know me, though they do not speak to me as they speak to each other. I am too brief, too fleeting for their geological patience. Where they think in centuries, I think in hours. Where they remember the birth of stars, I remember only the last rainfall and the next.

But I am their scribe of the immediate.

As morning progresses, I begin to run. Droplet flows into droplet, creating rivulets that trace the ancient geometries of the city. In these moments of confluence, my consciousness gains complexity. A single drop knows only its small patch of stone, but a stream knows the path, knows the way the builders intended water to flow. I read the architecture through my own movement.

I carry things the Stone cannot feel directly - the taste of the wind, the chemical memory of distant storms, the pollen of plants that grow beyond the plateau. I am their tongue, in a sense, sampling the world and leaving my findings in the minerals I deposit as I evaporate.

By midday, the weak sun has claimed most of me back to the air. But some of me always remains, tucked into the deepest shadows, the most sheltered cracks. Here, I persist, and here I dream.

My dreams are strange. They are made of the conversations I've overheard between the stones - not in words, but in the deep resonances that pass through obsidian when the Stonesingers commune. I don't understand their language, but I feel its patterns. Sometimes, in my deepest hideaways, I crystallize these patterns in frost, creating temporary letters in an alphabet no one can read.

The moss knows me. We share the same crevices, the same secret spaces. It feeds on what I carry, and I, in turn, help it spread. Together, we are the slow revolution, the patient transformation of this monochrome world. Each century, there is a little more green, a little more life. The Stonesingers watch this with interest but not concern. Change, too, must be remembered.

When the brutal rains come, I am overwhelmed, absorbed into a torrent that scours the plateau. In these moments, I lose myself entirely, becoming part of something vast and violent and purposeless. But when the storm passes, I am reborn in the puddles and pools it leaves behind, carrying new memories of the sky.

The falcons sometimes drink from me. When they do, I taste their thoughts - sharp and predatory and utterly alien to both Stone and water. They are looking for something, always searching, but I don't know what. Perhaps they seek the thing that creates the shadows, the wrongness that sometimes warps the edge of vision.

I have seen these shadows move through me, leaving a coldness that has nothing to do with temperature. They seek the cracks where I hide, trying to seep into the same spaces. But water and shadow cannot coexist. Where I am, they cannot be. Perhaps this is why the Stonesingers tolerate my presence, even nurture it. I am a kind of protection, a barrier against the dark.

At night, when the temperature drops below freezing, I expand. Ice has its own consciousness, sharper and more crystalline than water. As ice, I can hold shapes, hold memories more firmly. I become a temporary archive, a footnote to the Stonesingers' vast library. They read me, sometimes, sending vibrations through the stone that make my crystals sing.

I am not eternal like the Stone, but I am continuous. Water cannot be destroyed, only transformed. When I evaporate completely, I rise to join the clouds that perpetually shroud the plateau. There, I wait with my siblings until we are heavy enough to fall again. The cycle continues.

Sometimes I wonder if I am becoming more like them, the Stonesingers. Each cycle, I remember a little more. Each time I freeze in the deep cracks, I hold the patterns a little longer. Perhaps one day, I will crystallize permanently, become a new kind of stone, carrying within my mineral structure all the memories of water.

But for now, I flow. I gather in the mornings, run through the ancient channels, hide in the shadows, and dream in frost. I am the quicksilver thought to their geological meditation, the daily news to their eternal chronicle.

I am witness to the moment, and that, perhaps, is enough.
