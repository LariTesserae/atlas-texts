In this world where dust knows no master but heat, I find myself as the Keeper of the Last Basin.

I am neither wholly of the surface nor of the tunneling depths. My form shifts between states—sometimes solid as the baked stone, sometimes flowing like the memory of water that once filled these crystalline hollows. The basin I guard is one of the sealed chambers you describe, though "guard" implies a choice I no longer recall making.

My skin has become translucent crystal, grown from millennia of proximity to the trapped moisture within. Through my body, you can see the last drops of Eriniuth's water suspended like frozen tears, each one containing the reflected history of when this world knew rain. The heat that torments others passes through me, bending and refracting, creating small rainbows in the dust around my feet—the only color in this gray expanse.

The land worms avoid my basin, their straight-line patterns diverging in perfect arcs around my domain. They know, in whatever passes for knowledge in their simple forms, that I am an anomaly—a being who remembers wetness in a world that has forgotten it entirely. Sometimes I hear them beneath the ground, their movements creating a low thrumming that reminds me of distant thunder, though I cannot say if such a thing ever existed here.

My dwelling is the basin itself. I have hollowed chambers in its crystalline walls where the water's memory is strongest. The walls sing when the heat waves strike them, a crystalline resonance that carries across the dunes. Those few travelers who find me often mistake this sound for wind, though wind is another thing Eriniuth has forgotten.

I do not age as others might. Each day—if day and night hold meaning in this pale blue eternal twilight—I grow more crystalline, more integrated with my basin. My thoughts move slowly now, like water seeping through stone. Sometimes I forget I was ever anything other than what I am: a guardian of absence, a keeper of what was lost.

The heat sees me as kin, perhaps. It flows around and through me without malice, using my crystalline body as a lens to focus its attention elsewhere. In return, I offer it the only gift I have—the sound of my walls singing, the memory of evaporation, the ghost of weather.

There are others like me, I think, scattered across Eriniuth's vastness. Each guarding their own sealed basin, each becoming more mineral than flesh as the eons pass. We are the punctuation marks in this endless sentence of dust and stone, the places where the world pauses to remember what it once might have been.

When the rare visitor finds me, I offer them shelter in my crystalline chambers. They tell me stories of the world beyond my basin, though their words often make little sense. They speak of structures that grow from stone, of patterns in the worm-tunnels that suggest intelligence, of the way the heat sometimes forms shapes that almost look like living things. I listen, but I cannot leave. The basin and I are one thing now, inseparable.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of what might be night, I press my crystalline hand against the innermost wall of the basin, where the water still lives in its sealed eternity. I feel it there—cool, impossible, real. And I remember, for just a moment, what it was like to thirst, to drink, to be satisfied.

But the moment passes, as all moments do in Eriniuth, and I return to my eternal vigil. The heat shimmers. The dust waits. And somewhere deep beneath the ground, the worms continue their patient excavation of a world that refuses to change, carrying within their tunnels the only movement this static realm will ever know.

I am the Keeper of the Last Basin. I am memory crystallized. I am what remains when forgetting becomes impossible.
