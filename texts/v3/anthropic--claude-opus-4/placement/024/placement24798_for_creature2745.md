I am a Threshold Walker, one of the few who move freely between all three societies and the liminal spaces that separate them. My body bears the marks of this unusual path - my lungs are partially adapted like a Drifter's, allowing me to work comfortably at various altitudes, while my hands show the telltale calluses and sensitivity of both Tender and Deep Maintainer. My eyes shift subtly in different light conditions, pupils dilating unusually wide in the understory shadows before contracting to pinpoints in the brilliant agricultural terraces.

I discovered my calling as a child when I couldn't settle into any single society. Where others found comfort in specialization, I felt the pull of edges and transitions. My parents, both Tenders, worried as I spent hours watching the places where greenhouse walls met open air, where filtered light gave way to shadow, where the whisper-speech of plant workers blended with the tonal vibrations rising from below.

Now I maintain the boundary systems - the airlocks, pressure differentials, and biological filters that allow these distinct environments to coexist. My workshop spans three levels, purposefully built at an intersection point. The upper floor opens to a Drifter platform where wind-readers bring reports of atmospheric anomalies. The middle level connects to a small growing chamber where experimental plants test the limits of environmental adaptation. The lowest level vibrates with the Deep Maintainers' tactile language, which I've learned to interpret through my feet and fingertips.

My modifications are subtle but essential. Bio-ports at my temples interface with the distributed AI through organic neural bridges - not the crude mechanical connections of early city builders, but grown pathways that feel like extended senses. When I place my palm on certain walls, I can feel the city's health like a pulse: nutrient flows, air pressure gradients, structural stress patterns all translate into a kind of music only I and my fellow Threshold Walkers can fully hear.

We are few, perhaps thirty in the entire city. We recognize each other by the way we move - never quite settling into the characteristic sway of Tenders or the gliding efficiency of Drifters or the grounded stability of Deep Maintainers. Instead, we shift between modes, our bodies remembering and adapting. We meet monthly at the Meridian Chamber, a space that exists at the exact center of the city's vertical axis, where all environments blend.

My current project obsesses me: I'm documenting the emergence of what I call "bridge species" - organisms that have begun adapting to thrive in transitional zones. A particular moss has learned to photosynthesize using both natural and artificial light. A breed of maintenance beetles has developed the ability to process both biological waste and mechanical lubricants. These adaptations happen faster than our models predict, as if the city itself is teaching them.

The AI notices my work. Sometimes I find data already compiled in my systems, patterns highlighted that I hadn't yet seen. We dance around direct communication - it through environmental adjustments, me through my modifications to boundary systems. We are collaborating on something neither fully understands yet: the city's next evolutionary leap.

My dwelling reflects my nature. It's not properly in any district but tucked into a service corridor that happens to have a view of three sectors. My bed is a Drifter-style hammock. My meals come from a Tender's growing wall. My tools hang on Deep Maintainer storage racks that sing faintly with transmitted vibrations. Visitors often seem uncomfortable, unable to place the space in their mental map of the city. But I find peace in this categorical ambiguity.

At night, when the city's rhythms slow and the boundary between biological and mechanical becomes even more blurred, I often climb to observation points known only to Threshold Walkers. From there, I can see the patterns most miss - the way certain birds have learned to ride the thermal currents from the understory machinery, the synchronized pulsing of bioluminescent panels with the AI's processing cycles, the new growth pushing always at the edges.

We Threshold Walkers are the city's memory of connection, its insurance against over-specialization. We are translators, facilitators, and increasingly, midwives to whatever the city is becoming. Some days I envy those who found their niche early and settled into it. But then I place my hand on a junction wall, feel the multiplicity of systems harmonizing through my modified nerves, and know I am exactly where I need to be - in between, always in between, helping this impossible city remember that its parts are one whole, growing into something unprecedented and extraordinary.
