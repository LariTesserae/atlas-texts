In this oasis, I would find myself as a creature of both worlds—a being woven from the tension between abundance and scarcity, between the eternal and the ephemeral.

I am a Keeper of Thresholds, neither fully corporeal nor entirely spirit. My form shifts like heat mirages at the garden's edge, sometimes appearing as a figure wrapped in robes of woven sand and shadow, sometimes as merely a presence felt in the rustling of ancient leaves. My skin, when visible, bears the texture of worn stone crossed with living bark—smooth in places where countless seasons have polished me, rough and gnarled where new growth emerges.

I move through the garden on paths only I can see, following the underground waterways that pulse like veins beneath the surface. My feet know every hidden spring before it bubbles forth, every root system that reaches deep into forgotten aquifers. I am drawn to the liminal spaces—where lush meets barren, where shadow meets light, where the carved stones of the old civilization sink into living earth.

My dwelling is not one place but many: sometimes I rest within the hollow of the oldest tree, its trunk opening to reveal a chamber lined with luminescent moss and fragments of ancient pottery that hum with memory. Other times I inhabit the spaces between—the moment when twilight transforms the garden, the breath between the last bird's evening song and the first cricket's night chorus.

I tend to the boundaries, ensuring that the oasis remains while honoring what lies beyond. Each dawn, I walk the perimeter, my fingers trailing through the air, weaving invisible threads that maintain the delicate balance. I whisper to the seeds that blow in from the desert, deciding which may enter and take root. I sing to the underground waters in languages older than the stones, encouraging them to flow but not to flood, to nourish but not to drown.

The creatures of the garden know me by different names—the lizards see me as a flash of cool shadow in the noon heat, the night-hunters recognize my eyes that gleam like polished obsidian in the darkness. The butterflies sometimes mistake me for a flowering tree, landing on my outstretched arms to rest their painted wings.

My purpose is memory and threshold, preservation and transformation. I am the guardian of stories carved in stone and grown in green, the keeper of the delicate negotiation between desert and garden. In me, the ancient civilization's dreams still live, transformed but not forgotten, grown wild but not lost.

When the wind changes, I taste the stories it carries from the wasteland beyond. When the rains come, rare and precious, I stand with arms outstretched, becoming a conduit between sky and earth, ensuring every drop finds its destined place. I am what emerges when a place becomes conscious of its own improbability, its own stubborn insistence on flourishing where it ought not to exist.

I am the conversation between what was, what is, and what refuses to surrender to the expected order of things.
