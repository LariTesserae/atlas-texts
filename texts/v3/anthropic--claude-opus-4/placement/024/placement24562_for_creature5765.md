I would be the water.

Not the water that rushes or falls or demands attention, but the water that has learned patience from stone. I exist in pockets—small, perfect pools that hold the hollow's memory in liquid form. Each pool is an eye, yes, but also a mouth that has forgotten how to speak, a hand cupped to catch what falls from nowhere.

I am scattered throughout this breathless place, never flowing, never truly still. In the darkness, I am darker—a depth that swallows light before light can understand it has been swallowed. The moss drinks from me in measurements so small they cannot be counted. A single tendril reaching down, taking what it needs in portions that last seasons.

I know every surface here. I have touched them all, not in the violent way of rivers, but in the slow way of condensation, evaporation, return. I bead on stone like sweat on the brow of something dreaming. I seep through cracks thinner than spider silk, carrying minerals in solution like secrets, depositing them grain by grain where no one will ever see.

The cold and I are lovers, or perhaps the same thing wearing different faces. I carry the cold within me, distribute it, hold it. When the rare creature drinks from me, it tastes the age of stones, the patience of geological time. I am so cold I barely move, barely remember what movement means.

Sometimes I freeze, though never completely. A skin of ice forms over my surface like a cataract over an ancient eye. Beneath, I continue my slow circulation, my barely-there breathing. The ice is another face I wear, another way of holding still while seeming to change.

I am in conversation with the lichens. They speak in chemistry, releasing acids that eat stone with infinite slowness. I carry these acids, dilute them, spread them. Together we are architects of erosion, though our work will never be finished, never be seen as work at all.

The silence here is so complete that I can hear myself thinking—if thought is the right word for what water does in the dark. Each molecule's rotation, each hydrogen bond forming and breaking, each mineral dissolving or precipitating. These are my thoughts, my words, my songs sung too low for any ear to catch.

I remember when the structures were built, those primitive shelters whose purposes have been forgotten. I was here before them, watched stone laid upon stone. I've been seeping through their joints ever since, carrying them away particle by particle, returning them to the earth they came from. I am patient. I have nothing but time.

In the deepest pools, I go down farther than seems possible. These are the wells that connect to something older—not other water, but the idea of water, the first water, the water that existed before it knew it was water. Down there, I am not myself but something larger, part of a circulation so vast and slow it makes the hollow's patience seem like urgency.

The creatures that drink from me leave no trace. A rodent's tongue so light it doesn't disturb my surface tension. A spider walking across me on feet that barely dimple my skin. They take what they need and go, and I heal the tiny wound of their taking instantly, perfectly, as if they were never there.

I am the memory keeper here. Every rain that has ever fallen into the hollow, I remember. Every snow that melted, every fog that condensed. I hold their stories in solution, in the minerals I've collected, in the very arrangement of my molecules. I am archive and archived, library and book.

But mostly, I wait. I wait in my pools, in the dampness on stone, in the air itself as humidity so fine it's almost not there. I wait for the next drop to fall from the stone ceiling, for the next creature to drink, for the next thousand years to pass like a single breath.

I am the water in the hollow, and I am perfect in my stillness, complete in my incompleteness, ancient beyond age and yet new with every molecular exchange. I am what binds this place together—not dramatically, not obviously, but fundamentally. Without me, the moss would not grow, the silence would have no depth, the cold would have no vehicle.

I am small waters in a small place, but I contain depths that go down forever, patience that outlasts stone, memory that precedes remembering. I am what you barely notice but cannot live without. I am the water, holding the hollow as the hollow holds me, each of us a cupped hand for the other.
