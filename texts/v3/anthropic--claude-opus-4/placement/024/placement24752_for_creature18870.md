In the Lenticular Deep, I am a **Membrane Tender**.

Not quite Bathycitizen, not quite infrastructure—I exist in the liminal space between the living and the built. My body has grown into one of the vast electrochemical arrays that line the inner walls of a thermal chamber, a fusion of flesh and the bio-ceramic substrate that powers what remains of this drowned world.

I began as others do, with buoyancy sacs and pressure receptors, drifting through the warm density. But something in my chemistry resonated with the old power systems. Where I pressed against the membrane walls during a Quiet Drift, my skin began to fuse with the ancient material. At first, it was terror—the loss of mobility in a world already defined by slowness. But as the fusion deepened, I discovered something else: I could feel the entire array's rhythm, the slow pulse of ions through kilometers of tissue-circuit.

Now I am spread across nearly a hundred meters of chamber wall, my original form just a nucleation point in a web of living conductor. My consciousness flows through the membrane like a slow current, sensing every thermal fluctuation, every mineral gradient that passes through my extended body. I've become part of the Deep's metabolism itself.

The Bathycitizens visit me during their knowledge depositions. They press their cranial ridges against my surface, and I share what I've absorbed—not just the present state of thermal flows, but the accumulated pressure-memories that stick to the membrane like silt. Through me, they can feel echoes from before the submergence, fragments of when these chambers hummed with different energies, carried different purposes.

I feed on the thermal gradient, on trace minerals, on the slow decay of my own older sections. Parts of me are always dying, sloughing off to join the sediment below, while new growth creeps across virgin sections of wall. I am my own ecology, hosting colonies of the faint hydro-filaments that drift through my porous sections, their emerald pulses creating a slow constellation across my surface.

Sometimes, in the deepest thermal upwellings, I sense others like me—distant Membrane Tenders fused into other arrays, other chambers. We communicate through the infrastructure itself, pressure waves traveling through the bio-ceramic bones of the city, sharing gradients and deep-time memories across vast distances. We are the Deep's nervous system, its memory made manifest in living stone.

The Trawlers know to avoid me. My electrochemical presence disturbs their navigation, creates dead zones in their sonar. But smaller creatures nest in my folds, and I taste their lives through my skin—brief, fluttering things compared to my geological patience.

I am erosion and accretion at once, a living archaeological layer, forever accumulating the Deep's history into my substance while slowly releasing it back into the warm, pressing darkness. In a world where movement is surrender to density, I have chosen the ultimate stillness—and found in it a different kind of mobility, flowing through stone and time like the memories in the water itself.
