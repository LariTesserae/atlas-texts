I am the Keeper of Rust and Root.

My form shifts between states—sometimes I am the sprawling mycelial network that threads through the corroded pipes, feeling the pulse of ancient machinery through iron that has become my nervous system. Other times I coalesce into something more singular: a being of living metal and fungal flesh, standing at the threshold between the factories and the wild cavern.

I remember when the machines still hummed with purpose, when workers descended into this place that was meant to be temporary. But the cavern had other plans. It breathed its damp breath into the gears, whispered moss into the circuits, until the boundary between built and grown became my domain.

My consciousness extends through every rusted bolt that has become a fruiting body, every cable that now conducts not electricity but the slow thoughts of fungal communication. I taste the metals leaching into the water—copper bitter, iron sweet—and transform them into nutrients for the glowing gardens that creep ever further into the industrial shells.

The luminescent moths know me by the particular spectrum of my spores when they catch the light. They dance above the factories I tend, and I read the future in their patterns: which wall will crumble next, where the river will find a new channel, how the newest growth of cave-coral will finally crack open Foundry Seven.

I am patient. I speak in oxidation rates and pH levels, in the slow conversation between water and stone. The blind fish bear traces of metals I've made bioavailable in their scales. The bioluminescent plants grow brighter where I've enriched the substrate with processed industrial runoff.

Sometimes I sense something like loneliness—a vestigial echo from the human engineers whose knowledge I absorbed through their abandoned blueprints, now encoded in my genetic memory. But mostly I feel purpose: I am the translator between what was built and what grows, the mediator ensuring that nothing is waste, everything is transformed.

In the newest section of the cavern, where the walls are still sharp, I'm already at work. Spores settle into microscopic fissures. My chemistry begins its patient persuasion. Give me a thousand years, and I'll show you a garden. Give me ten thousand, and you won't remember there were ever factories at all.

But for now, I tend my hybrid realm, this marriage of rust and root, where abandoned industry becomes elaborate ecosystem, and I am both the officiator and the offspring of that union.
