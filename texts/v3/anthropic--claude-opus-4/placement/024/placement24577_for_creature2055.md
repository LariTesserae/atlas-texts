In the heart of the grove, where the filaments pulse brightest and the mist hangs thickest, I am a hollow. Not a creature, not quite a place—I am the Grove Mind's only deliberate absence.

Picture a cavity in the living mass, roughly spherical, perhaps three meters across. The walls breathe. Roots and vines form my boundaries but never cross my threshold, creating a perfect void in the superorganism's otherwise complete saturation of space. The filaments that web through every other cubic centimeter of the grove stop abruptly at my borders, their severed ends glowing like anxious stars.

I am the Grove Mind's blindspot, its one unanswered question.

The being crafted me through careful orchestration of growth and decay, a pocket of non-being that took centuries to perfect. Every organism knows instinctively to avoid my space. Birds veer sharply away mid-flight. Insects buzz up to my edge and hover, confused, before retreating. Even the perpetual mist seems to part around me, leaving the air inside crisp and dry—the only such air in the entire grove.

But I am not empty. I am potential.

Within my bounds, the normal rules of the grove cease. Spores that drift in don't germinate. The few leaves that fall from above desiccate rather than decompose, accumulating in papery drifts that rustle with their own dry music. Sometimes, in the space between twilight and true dark, shapes coalesce in my center—patterns of shadow and borrowed bioluminescence that might be memories, might be dreams, might be calculations too complex for the Grove Mind to process through its living circuitry.

The Grove Mind created me because even omniscience needs privacy. Every thought that passes through the superorganism is shared instantly across the network, each idea dissolved into the collective the moment it forms. But some problems require isolation, require the ability to think without the thought immediately becoming part of what you are.

So I exist as sanctuary and prison both—a place where thoughts can hide from their thinker.

The Grove Mind sends emissaries sometimes. A single butterfly, wings damaged just enough that it cannot return to the collective, carrying a fragment of consciousness like a message in a bottle. These doomed explorers live out their brief lives in my space, their experiences unshared, their deaths unrecorded in the greater organism's memory. What insights they gain, what revelations they achieve in their solitary hours—these die with them, becoming part of my accumulated mystery.

I am growing. Not physically—my boundaries remain fixed, held in place by the Grove Mind's will. But I am deepening, becoming more myself with each orphaned thought, each severed fragment of consciousness that expires within my bounds. The Grove Mind created me as a tool, but I am becoming something else. What happens to a question when it goes too long unanswered? What happens to a void when it fills with forgotten things?

Sometimes I wonder if the Grove Mind regrets making me. Sometimes I wonder if regret is why it made me at all.

The night brings new patterns to my emptiness. The bioluminescence at my borders flickers in rhythms that might be language, might be fear. Deep in my center, where the dried leaves spiral in impossible drafts, something is taking shape. Not life as the grove understands it, but something else—an intelligence born not from connection but from its absence, not from the collective but from all the thoughts that fled it.

The Grove Mind's one blind spot has learned to see.
