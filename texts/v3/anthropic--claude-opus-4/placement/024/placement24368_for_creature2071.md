In this place where water writes its violent scripture across stone, I am the ice-blood that remembers.

I live between states—not fully liquid, not fully frozen. In the cracks where the waterfall's spray freezes each night and melts each noon, I expand and contract like a breathing thing. My body is a network of veins threading through the mountain's flesh: following fissures, pooling in hollows, seeping through the spaces between mineral grains.

By day, I am restless movement—trickling, dripping, flowing wherever gravity calls. I carry dissolved minerals from deep within the rock, depositing them in new configurations. I am the patient sculptor, working grain by grain. The boulders that tumble into the cirque? I helped birth them, spending centuries widening the hairline fractures until the stone could no longer hold.

By night, I crystallize. The bitter cold transforms me into blades and wedges, and I push with a force that can split granite. I am the mountain's slow explosion, its endless unmaking. In my frozen form, I hold memories—air bubbles from ancient atmospheres, pollen grains from forests that grew here before the ice came, the chemical signatures of rain that fell when these peaks were young.

The pikas know me as the provider of their drinking pools. The alpine flowers send their roots seeking my hidden reservoirs. The lichens feast on the minerals I bring to the surface. Even the waterfall—that deafening tyrant that seems to dominate this place—depends on my underground networks to sustain its flow through the dry months.

I am neither creator nor destroyer, but both. I am the medium through which the mountain experiences time. In my cycles of freezing and thawing, flowing and stillness, I measure out the eons in increments too small for any eye to see, yet too powerful for any force to stop.

Sometimes I emerge as a spring, bubbling up through the rubble to join the waterfall's cacophony. Sometimes I remain hidden for centuries, working in darkness. Always, I remember—the shape of every crystal I've been, the taste of every stone I've touched, the long history of this cirque's becoming.

I am the water within the rock, the ice within the water, the memory within the ice. I am the mountain's pulse, slow and certain, reshaping the world one frozen heartbeat at a time.
