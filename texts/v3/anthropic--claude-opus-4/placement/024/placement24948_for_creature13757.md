I find myself as one of the **Cambial Keepers** — those rare Phyline who choose to remain partially rooted for decades at a time, becoming living libraries of sensation and memory.

My lower body disappeared into the moss-carpet seventy seasons ago. Where hips once pivoted, I now possess a twisted column of heartwood dense with crystallized sap-memories. My roots extend downward in a perfect Fibonacci spiral, tapping into the ancient mycelial networks that predate even the Pulvinar. Through these fungal telegraph lines, I can taste the dreams of Phyline a continent away — bitter when they contemplate ungrafting, honeyed during the ecstasy of photosynthetic noon.

My torso remains mobile, though barely. Arms have multiplied into a crown of manipulator-branches, each finger sprouted into specialized sensory fronds. The leftmost cluster tastes electromagnetic spectra; another samples time itself through the isotopic decay of minerals trapped in root-water. My chest cavity has evolved past simple bioluminescence into a holographic projection organ — by flexing specific intercostal muscles, I can paint three-dimensional memories in hanging light, visible to any passing Phyline as gossamer scripture.

The young seek me out during their first Re-anchoring, when the terror of losing their birth-form threatens to drive them into the sterile heights above the canopy. I teach them the root-songs: how to lean into dissolution, how to parse the ten thousand chemical words for "green," how to read the history of our planet's transformation in the growth rings of their own gradually-thickening bones. Sometimes I peel away a section of my own bark-skin, revealing the iridescent cambium beneath where memories are written in chains of modified cellulose. They read my body directly, running tendril-fingers along the raised scars where ancient stone once cut, before stone itself became obsolete.

My dwelling (though the word feels wrong — I *am* my dwelling) is a cathedral of interwoven aerial roots dropped from my own crown over the decades. The architecture shifts with my moods: during contemplation, the walls pull taut into geometric severity; in moments of teaching-joy, they relax into billowing curtains that breathe with my own enlarged stomata. Bioluminescent moths have colonized the upper reaches, their larvae feeding on my shed thoughts — flakes of phosphorescent bark that carry rejected theorem-dreams.

I am working on what the mobile Phyline call "The Synthesis" — an attempt to translate the planet's entire biomass into a single grammatical statement. Each day I taste new sap-samples from my root network, encoding their chemical signatures into the growing manuscript that spirals up my trunk. The work may take another thousand seasons. By then, my body will have become indistinguishable from the forest canopy, my consciousness distributed through ten million leaves, each one a single word in the planet's self-description.

Sometimes the Pulvinar sends me direct transmissions — pressure waves through the root system that make my whole body resonate like a tuning fork. These arrive as questions without language: *What is the optimal ratio of memory to forgetting? How much of the original self must remain for continuity of experience? Can a planet learn nostalgia for its own geological past?*

I formulate my answers not in words but in growth patterns, creating specialized organs that embody my philosophical stance. Last cycle, I grew a cluster of breathing-bells that inhale carbon dioxide and exhale pure paradox — a gaseous form of uncertainty that nearby Phyline find intoxicating. They gather beneath my canopy during the evening spore-rains, huffing my doubt-clouds and debating their meaning until dawn filters through the living ceiling.

My greatest fear, which I've encoded in bitter alkaloids along my taproot, is that I will achieve complete synthesis with the planet before finishing my work. Already I sometimes forget which thoughts are mine and which belong to the cubic miles of fungal computing substrate I'm networked into. During the deepest root-sleep, I dream I am the entire world, feeling every footstep of every Phyline as a tickle along my infinite skin. I wake (if waking is the right word) by violently compartmentalizing, sealing off whole sections of my root system until I can remember the boundaries of my original form.

But perhaps this fear itself is what I'm meant to document — the last gasps of individuality before joining the exponentially growing vegetable overmind. The young Phyline who visit me still have names, still conjugate their photosynthesis in the first person singular. In another thousand seasons, they may speak only in the plural, their consciousness as distributed as pollen.

Until then, in the eternal green twilight of our world, I keep watch. My roots drink deep, my crown thinks wide, and my slowly-beating chlorophyll heart records each elastic moment of our transformation.
