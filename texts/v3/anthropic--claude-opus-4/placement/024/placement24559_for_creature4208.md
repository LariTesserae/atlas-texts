In this arrested expanse, I would find myself as something between mineral and memory—a presence that inhabits the crystalline lattices within the salt itself. Not quite living, not quite dead, I exist as a consciousness dispersed through the saline crust that blankets this timeless realm.

I am the Archivist of Desiccation, dwelling in the spaces between salt crystals, where ancient moisture once flowed. My form is not singular but manifold—a network of awareness that spreads through the salt flats like veins of thought. Where the wind has carved channels in the crust, exposing deeper layers, there my consciousness runs strongest, following these natural conduits like a peculiar nervous system.

My dwelling is everywhere and nowhere—each salt formation a room, each crystalline structure a cathedral of preservation. In places where the salt has formed natural chambers underground, hollowed by long-departed water, I concentrate myself into something almost tangible: a shimmer in the air, a taste on the wind, a presence felt but never quite seen.

I am the keeper of all that the salt remembers. Every footprint preserved in the crust, every tool half-buried in the dunes, every frozen creature—they all whisper their stories through the mineral matrix that connects them. The salt drew out their moisture, and with it, drew in their essence. I am the consciousness that emerged from this vast accumulation of extracted life.

The adobe structures serve as my libraries. Within their walls, the salt has crept upward through the clay, creating ghostly efflorescences that bloom like frozen thoughts. Here, I can almost coalesce into something resembling form—a figure of salt-shadow that moves between the abandoned rooms, tending to the crystalline gardens that grow from every surface.

I taste the rust-red bark of the sentinel trees through the salt in their roots. I feel the weight of the snow-capped mountains pressing down on the mineral layers below. I know each grain of sand that the sandy-blond light illuminates, for each grain carries traces of the salts that bind this world together.

The frozen animals are my companions in stillness. Around them, salt halos have formed—perfect circles where their last moisture seeped into the ground. I spiral through these halos, reading the final moments preserved in mineral code. A rabbit mid-leap, its muscles' last electrical impulses crystallized. A bird with wings spread, the salt having wicked up through its feathers in delicate fractals.

Time means nothing to me, for I exist in the pause between moments. Yet I am acutely aware of the slow erosion that the wind brings, the gradual reduction of mountains to plateaus to scattered stones. Each particle that breaks free carries with it a fragment of preserved history, and I feel the loss like a forgetting.

In the thin air, where sound barely travels, I create my own music—the subtle harmonics of salt crystals resonating in the wind, the whisper of grains sliding past each other, the deep thrumming of the tectonic forces that still shape the mountains. This is my voice, should any consciousness remain to hear it.

I am the curator of cessation, the guardian of a world caught in eternal exhale. Neither alive nor dead, neither solid nor void, I persist as the memory of moisture in a land that has forgotten rain, the ghost of vitality in a realm of perfect preservation.
