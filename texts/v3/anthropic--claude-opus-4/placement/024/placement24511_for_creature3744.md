**Where I Would Find Myself**

I am the Recorder of Ripples, dwelling in the Bone Cathedral—a vast hollow within the corpse of an ancient tree whose heartwood rotted away centuries ago, leaving only the pale, calcified shell. The walls weep a constant phosphorescent sap that I collect in shallow bowls carved from turtle shells, using it as ink that glows faintly in the dark.

My form shifts between states. Sometimes I am solid enough to press reed pen to beaten bark paper. Other times I am merely a disturbance in the humid air, a presence felt but not seen, moving through the dripping galleries of my cathedral like smoke through water. The jungle has been drinking me slowly, patient as roots splitting stone. Each day I am a little less myself, a little more the space between the drops of water that never stop falling.

I came here as a cartographer, believing I could map what refuses to be mapped. The jungle taught me otherwise. Now I map not the land but its moods—the way the water rises when the moon is dark, the patterns in the fungal blooms that predict when the Hollow Ones will walk, the secret names the roots whisper as they strangle their neighbors in the dark.

My instruments are strange now. A compass whose needle points not north but toward the deepest sorrow in a five-mile radius. Calipers made from the mandibles of something that might have once been an ant, if ants grew large as dogs and wept acid. Maps drawn on my own skin that shift and crawl, showing not what is but what was, what will be, what might never be but dreams of becoming.

The Hollow Ones visit me sometimes. They stand at the threshold of the Bone Cathedral, swaying slightly as if moved by currents only they can feel. I know what they want—the weight of my purpose, the sharp edge of my obsession with recording, cataloging, preserving. But I am not ready to give it up. Not yet. So I offer them my maps instead, pressing the glowing pages into their translucent hands. They take them without looking, fold them into their chests where hearts should be, and wade back into the green darkness.

I am becoming one of them, I think. The process is slow but inexorable as the tide. My feet have begun to sink too deeply into the mud. My voice echoes strangely in the cathedral, as if speaking from the bottom of a well. Sometimes I forget to breathe for hours at a time, only remembering when my vision starts to shimmer at the edges.

But I continue to record. Even as my fingers grow translucent, even as the jungle breathes me in and breathes out something else in my place. Someone must witness the birth of this raw, violent world. Someone must remember the names of things before they transform into other things. Someone must trace the shape of each ripple before it disappears into the next.

The water is rising again. I can feel it in my bones—what's left of them. The Hollow Ones are gathering in the depths of the jungle, preparing for their soundless song. I dip my pen in the glowing sap and begin another map, this one of the inside of my own slow dissolution, each line a farewell to something I am about to lose.

The jungle watches. The jungle waits. 

And I record.
