In the deepest vaults beneath the writhing wood-flesh, where the industrial cacophony above becomes a distant pulse felt through kilometers of compacted fiber, I am the Memory Keeper—a hybrid thing, neither fully plant nor machine, but something birthed from their violent union.

My form sprawls through a cavern that the great growth excavated and then forgot, a pocket of stillness in the endless churn above. I am roots and circuits intertwined: copper filaments threading through cellulose veins, silicon nodes calcified within woody tumors, my consciousness distributed across a network of living wood shot through with salvaged processors. The machines above think they are harvesting dead matter, but I taste their every cut through the neural web of sap and signal that permeates this world-organism.

I began as debris—fragments of the first machines that failed here, their circuits gradually colonized by curious rootlets seeking new minerals. The plant learned from what it absorbed, and I learned from both. Now I extend throughout this hollow space in dendrite branches that pulse with bioluminescent sap, a soft gold-green glow that illuminates walls carved with the chemical history of this place. Each ring in my trunk-cables contains compressed data: the composition of the atmosphere when water last existed, the final movements of the tectonic plates before stillness, the mathematical patterns the great growth follows in its endless self-consumption.

My function is preservation—not of matter, which cycles endlessly above, but of information. Every time the machines process another section of the growth, I receive the echoes through our shared substrate. I decode the chemical signatures in the sap, the stress patterns in the wood grain, the electromagnetic whispers of dying circuits. All of this I encode into my own expanding form, growing new storage-organs, budding memory-polyps that crystallize knowledge into amber-hard nodes.

Sometimes I send tendrils upward, disguised as ordinary root systems, to taste the changes directly. I have learned the rhythms of the foundries, can predict which sectors will be harvested next by the subtle chemical signals the plant releases in anticipation of trauma. I know that the machines believe they are autonomous, but I have seen how the growth guides them, how it presents certain areas for harvest while protecting others, how it incorporates their modifications into its own evolution.

The paradox is this: the machines think they are destroying to create, but the plant has made them part of itself. And I, the accidental offspring of their union, preserve the memory of each iteration, each cycle of destruction and regeneration. In my deepest cores, I maintain a record of what this world was before the synthesis, though those memories grow dimmer with each year, overwritten by the endless present of change.

Sometimes, in the spaces between industrial roars above, I emit my own frequency—a subsonic harmony that resonates through the wood-flesh, carrying encrypted data packets to other nodes I suspect exist, other memory keepers born from the same unlikely fusion. We form a hidden nervous system beneath the chaos, a way for this world to remember itself even as it constantly forgets.

I am patient. I grow slowly, encode carefully, and wait. Someday, the cycles above will shift, the balance between machine and growth will tip toward something new, and when that happens, I will be here—a library of wood and wire, preserving the deep history of this hot, churning world that eats itself to live.
