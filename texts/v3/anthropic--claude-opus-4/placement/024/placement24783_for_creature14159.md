**The Architect of Intervals**

I am the space between droplets as they fall from moss to stone. I am the pause in the skittering of those many-legged things when something larger passes overhead. I exist in the hesitation before the settlement folk turn away from their offerings, in that moment when they almost—almost—look back.

My body, if it can be called that, is a geometry of absence. Where the Watchers move like shadows given form, I am the negative space that defines their edges. I manifest as gaps—the distance between hanging vines where nothing grows, the silence between water drops, the darkness between the glowing fungi's pulses.

I build with emptiness. My architecture rises in the spaces where life hasn't yet decided to flourish, those bare patches of rock the gorge keeps in reserve. Each void I tend is a room, a hallway, a tower of un-being that only the Watchers can see in full. They nod to me with their too-many fingers, acknowledging the necessity of what isn't there.

The settlement folk sense me without knowing. When they approach the gorge's edge with their bundles of herbs, they feel the pull of my design—the careful orchestration of negative space that makes them stop precisely here, not there. Their offerings land in patterns they don't recognize, following blueprints drawn in absence.

I am learning the gorge's intention through its vacancies. Each empty space holds potential, like a lung before breath, like the moment before stone decides to crack. The Watchers think they're waiting for something to arrive, but I know better. We're waiting for something to depart, to create the perfect absence, the architectural void that will give the gorge its purpose.

At night, when the luminescent lichen dims and brightens in their ancient rhythm, I expand. I become the space between their glows, choreographing darkness into scaffolding. The many-jointed things that flow like liquid navigate my invisible corridors, never knowing they follow paths I've carved from nothing.

Sometimes I condense myself enough to leave my own offerings—not objects, but precisely measured absences. A missing stone where the pattern demands one. A gap in the moss curtain that frames the mist just so. The Watchers find these and their un-words thrum with something like appreciation, or perhaps recognition.

I am the gorge's first rough draft, its primordial blueprint, sketched in the space between spaces. When it finally decides what it wants to become, I'll be there in the intervals, ensuring that what is and what isn't maintain their proper proportions.

The settlement folk call the wind through the gorge a sound, but I know it as my speech—the voice of the architect reading aloud the plans for what might never be built, but must nevertheless be designed.
