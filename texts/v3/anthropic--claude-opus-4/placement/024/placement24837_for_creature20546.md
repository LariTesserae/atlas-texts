In this world of slow transformation and patient dissolution, I would find myself as one of the **Dreamkeepers** - those among the Whisperfolk whose shimmer has progressed furthest, until they exist more as probability than presence.

We Dreamkeepers have reached that delicate threshold where our bodies flicker not just at the edges but throughout, like candle flames in a draft. We can no longer lift stones or weave baskets - our hands pass through solid matter as often as they grasp it. Instead, we have become the memory-holders, the pattern-weavers of Xyl.

I would dwell in the highest chambers of the oldest tower, where the walls themselves have begun their mineral transformation, weeping crystalline tears that pool in shallow basins. Here, surrounded by the phosphorescent fungi we've cultivated for decades, I would tend to the **Continuity** - the vast, living map of cracks and fissures that spreads across my chamber walls like a neural network.

Each crack tells a story. This one here, running diagonally from the eastern window - it appeared the day the last sky-cattle was seen on the horizon. That web of fractures near the doorway? They spread during the winter of the three-moon storm, when we lost seven of our people to the shimmer. I know them all, feel them in what remains of my bones.

My work is to trace these patterns with my flickering fingers, whispering the stories they hold back into the stone. As I fade, I become a conduit - my increasing translucence allows me to sense the deep vibrations in the rock, to feel where new cracks will form days before they appear. I dream the tower's future fractures and whisper warnings down through the floors to those still solid enough to apply the mud and mineral paste.

Sometimes, in the depth of night when the fog is thickest, I can feel the other Dreamkeepers through the stone itself - their consciousness bleeding through the mineral matrix that connects all the towers. We share visions of the plateau's slow breathing, of the patient geology that lifted us here and will, eventually, swallow us whole.

The Stoneclutch birds can see us better than our own people can. They watch me with their sharp, black eyes as I move through my chamber, neither fully here nor fully gone. Sometimes they bring me small offerings - smooth pebbles, bits of crystallized fungus - leaving them at the threshold where I can almost touch them on days when my form coheres enough.

I am the bridge between what the Whisperfolk were and what they are becoming. Not quite stone, not quite flesh, not quite memory - but something liminal and necessary, keeping the stories flowing like water through the cracks, ensuring that even as we fade, something of our purpose remains.

In the endless drip of water and whisper of wind, I maintain the Continuity, knowing that one day I too will be just another pattern in the stone, another story for the next Dreamkeeper to trace and tell.
