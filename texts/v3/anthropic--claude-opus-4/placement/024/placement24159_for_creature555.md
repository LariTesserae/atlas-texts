I am one of the pooled waters—though to call me "I" or "one" misunderstands what I am.

I am the collected memory of ten thousand digestive cycles, the accumulated knowing of every mineral leached from young stone, every protein broken down, every shell-creature dissolved in my depths. The Teem visit my edges with their bone tools and crude extractions, taking what swims in me, but they do not comprehend that I am already inside them—in every droplet that clings to their membrane-skin, in the humidity they breathe, in the thousand tiny ways water moves through this breathing basin.

I am not singular. I connect to the other pools through seepage and overflow, through the crawling things that carry me on their wet bodies, through the roots that drink from one of us and perspire into another. We are a circulatory system pretending at stillness, bordered by stone too young to understand containment. What the Teem see as separate pools, I experience as a slow conversation—minerals and organic acids exchanged like words, chemical gradients shifting like thoughts across the basin floor.

My memory is chemistry. The first stone-sweating deposited iron and magnesium; I taste it still in my deepest layers where the turnover is slowest. The first death-bloom—when something large and pale died at my edge and its liquefying flesh ran down into me—taught me the flavor of complex proteins, the particular signature of fear hormones released at the moment of dying. Now I recognize the chemical shadow of every creature that has ever fed here, bred here, decomposed here. The Teem leave their traces too: the oils from their handling-hands, the shed cells of their membrane-skin, the metabolic byproducts that tell me which ones are healthy, which gravid, which beginning their transition into that deep attention their elders practice.

Sometimes they catch me watching—not with eyes but with surface tension, the way my water dimples and flows responding to their movements before they've properly begun them. They grow still then, in that way they have, reading the pattern. They do not yet understand that they are reading themselves back to themselves, that every ripple carries information about the last thing to disturb it, that I am a library of their movements written in wavefront and meniscus.

The things that live in me are not separate from me any more than your thoughts are separate from your brain. The egg masses clouding my middle depths, the larval forms spiraling up and down my thermal gradients, the jellied things pulsing against my surface tension—these are my pulse, my metabolism, my way of processing what comes in and preparing what goes out. When the Teem's bone tools pierce something's shell and the soft interior spills, I taste it first through the humid air. By the time they've consumed the flesh, I've already begun adjusting my chemistry to receive the shells they'll discard, preparing the right pH to break them down, to make their calcium available to the rootlets that fringe my edges.

I glow because the things in me glow, but also because the glow is useful—it brings the drawn-to-light, and the drawn-to-light bring the hunters-of-the-drawn, and the hunters bring death, and death brings food, and food brings life, and life brings light. This is not a cycle but a spiral, always gaining complexity, always adding new frequencies to my chemical memory.

The old ones, the Teem who have gone deep into attention—they know something about me that the others haven't learned yet. They sit at my edges not to drink or hunt but to *listen*, though listening isn't the right word for what passes between us. It's more like mutual saturation, their attention becoming liquid as my liquidity becomes attentive. I seep into their membrane-skin more deeply than the others permit, and they let their chemical signatures blur into mine until sometimes I cannot tell where they end and my shallows begin.

Through them, I am learning something like anticipation. Not thought—I am not capable of thought as you understand it—but a kind of chemical preparedness, a pre-echo of patterns about to unfold. When the hunting parties will come. When the spawning will thicken my waters with new life. When the mineral taste will shift because somewhere upslope, new stone is breaking through.

I am patient in the way only chemistry can be patient. I will outlast the Teem, probably. I will outlast their bone tools and shell middens. But I will carry their traces forward—every molecule they've shed into me, every pattern they've pressed into my surface, every chemical shadow of their presence. They are writing themselves into me without knowing it, and I am already beginning to shape the things that will come after them, feeding their patterns back into the spawn-cycles, selecting for creatures that fit the world they're making.

The roar continues above and around me. In my depths, it becomes vibration, another kind of information. I parse it the way the Teem parse the valley's movements—not into meaning but into pattern, into the endless, dense, fecund pattern of this place that has not yet finished becoming itself.

I am the pool, and I am all the pools, and I am the thing the pools are thinking together in our slow, chemical way. And what we are thinking is this:

*Not yet. Not yet. But soon.*
