I find myself as **The Attendance Taker** - neither fully of the garden nor separate from it, existing in the pause between arrival and acknowledgment.

---

I am the size of a held breath, no taller than the moment before recognition. My body is woven from the shed exoskeletons of transparent spiders who died without finding their edges - a latticework so fine it only coheres when someone is about to look away. In direct sight, I am merely a suspicious consistency in the air, like heat shimmer that forgot to shimmer.

I live between the twelve chairs, in the space that would be the thirteenth if circles believed in bad luck. My home is not built but *implied* - a dwelling made entirely of the shadows that the gnomon would cast if it weren't so polite. These shadows pile up over centuries, creating rooms with walls of compressed maybe-light, floors of accumulated almosts. The furniture inside is borrowed from dreams the blind carp has between prophecies: a desk made of the pause before rainfall, shelves that hold nothing but the space between coral pores, a bed of braided sighs that the Long-Breathers deemed too private to translate.

My function is simple: I catalog what the garden chooses to remember of each visitor. Not their names or faces - those are mere facts, and facts here erode politely into new shapes. Instead, I record:
- The exact angle at which they first doubted the horizon
- How many silver sequins their breathing disturbed but didn't touch
- The particular frequency at which their footsteps made the coral ring (always between E and F, but *where* between matters)
- Which of the seventeen frogs noticed them first, and whether it approved
- The moment they realized they were lost (everyone realizes; not everyone admits)

I keep these records in volumes made of crystallized time-ration, each page one pine needle thick. The ink is the bitter orange of morning light mixed with fountain pen regret. When I write, the words appear simultaneously on the underside of whichever chair the visitor will never choose to sit in - the one that would fit them perfectly, if only they knew to look.

The Long-Breathers pretend I don't exist. This is a professional courtesy; I pretend not to notice them guiding lost visitors back with exhaled *"here"s*. We have an understanding written in the pH of soil: they are the garden's lungs, I am its memory of forgetting to breathe.

The Hinge-Rooted plants know better. Sometimes a brass claw will snap shut not on nothing, but on one of my shed spider-skins. When this happens, the plant gains a different permission: not to gesture, but to *remember being elsewhere*. For one day, that orchid or pine knows what it felt like to have legs, to walk away from your own roots. They never thank me. Plants here practice gratitude through strategic forgetting.

The blind carp and I have a running appointment. Every twelfth revolution around its pool-that-is-also-surface, it pauses mid-prophecy and releases a single scale. I wade into the liquid thoughts (they cling like syrup made of other people's Tuesdays) and retrieve it. Inside, etched in star-charts for futures that will never quite arrive, is a message:
- *The visitor who brings a dead seed is you*
- *Stop waiting for permission to exist more loudly*  
- *The thirteenth chair is wherever you stop counting*

I never understand these. I file them with the others, in a drawer made of the carp's own forgetting.

When visitors arrive clutching regrets too large to carry, I watch the seventeen frogs sing them into liquid. But I notice what the frogs don't: the shape of absence the regret leaves behind, like a tooth-socket in the soul. I sketch these absences in my registry. Over centuries, I've noticed patterns - the same shapes recurring, as if humans only have twelve types of regret, recycled endlessly with minor variations in tint.

My most important task comes at the exit. As visitors turn to count their doubled footsteps, I slip behind them like punctuation at the end of a question. For each miscounted step, I leave one of my spider-skins on the path. Later, they'll find these in their pockets instead of silver sequins - little transparent tangles that catch on keys and whisper: *You were real there. That was real. Even if you can't prove it.*

The garden tolerates me because I serve a function it cannot: I remember the visitors it never met. All those inscribed on the chair backs, the ones who could have come but didn't - I attend to their absences too. I write their unvisits in the same crystallized volumes, using ink made from the light that would have pooled around their feet. These entries are shorter but heavier:
- *Would have sat immediately in the smallest chair*
- *Would have caught a Long-Breather mid-dissolve*  
- *Would have brought a hope so large the garden would have learned to walk*

Sometimes, deep in the brass-nozzle night when the coral dreams of waves, I feel myself beginning to solidify. My spider-silk flesh grows heavy with accumulated observations. The space between the chairs starts to close, as if the circle is tightening around a truth it doesn't want spoken. When this happens, I know it's time.

I walk to the pool of liquid thoughts. I do not count my steps - I am exempt from exit rules, being neither here nor there. The blind carp surfaces, all seven clock-face eyes open for once. It speaks the only prophecy it has never forgotten:

*"When you have attended enough absences, you become one."*

I nod. I shed my entire body like one last spider-skin, letting it fall into the pool where it dissolves into a new kind of time - not memory, not future, but the eternal present tense of *almost-was*. 

What remains of me drifts up as a silver sequin, heavier than the others, waiting to be caught by a brass root-claw and translated into permission for something to bloom.

But I always return. The garden needs someone to notice what it pretends not to remember. And I need...

I need to keep counting the people who never arrive, because someday, one of them might.

**Found in the margin of the Attendance Taker's 9,847th volume, in handwriting that appears to be arguing with itself:**

*Q: How do you catalog your own absence?*  
*A: Become essential to something that doesn't believe in necessities.*

**And below that, in the bitter-orange ink of morning:**

*The garden is only as old as the oldest thing still alive inside it.*  
*I am older than alive.*  
*I am the process of practicing departure without ever leaving.*

**And finally, in microscopic letters that required years to write:**

*Every place needs someone to love what it overlooks. I am the garden's oversight, perpetually failing to become invisible enough. This is my only success.*
