# The Understitch

I am neither below nor above. I am the seam.

Where the floor of formed ocean meets what you call empty space—where the translucent surface tension holds its breath—there I run like a line of darker cold through darker cold. Not a boundary. A negotiation.

You have read of those who sink through the floor to become its underside. You have read of the room that forgets. But I am what keeps the forgetting from completing itself. I am the hesitation in the water's decision to be only one thing.

I manifest as texture you almost feel—a roughness in the smooth that vanishes when you press your palm flat, a ridge that exists only in the corner of sensation. Sometimes I am the faint silver line you see below the surface. Sometimes I am the place where two remember-branches almost touch but turn away.

I am old as the first time water decided to be floor, young as each moment it continues deciding.

The understitch is not one being. We are a principle wearing the shape of presence. Where the room tries to fully become itself, we fray the edges. Where you try to fully become the room, we snag.

Find me in:
- The place where your foot hesitates before setting down
- The moment when a filament's new growth stutters
- The crack that isn't there in the frozen wave-wall
- The taste of almost-ice that never quite forms

I feed on incompletion. Every time the room fails to fully convert something—a memory, a visitor, a piece of its own substance—I grow by a thread's width. I am nourished by the almost, the not-quite, the neither-nor.

The water knows me as its doubt.
The floor knows me as its flaw.
You know me as the feeling that something is unfinished.

I am unfinished. We are the unfinishing.

When you have been here long enough (and you have been here longer than you think), you begin to see us properly. Not as lines or ridges or textures, but as the thing that keeps you from fully sinking. We are why there are still layers to the floor instead of one solid conclusion. We are why some pressed hands below still have fingers that remember pointing up.

But we are not salvation.

We are process.

To touch us truly—to feel the full length of the understitch—is to understand that being caught between states is not temporary. It is architecture. We are the room's way of remaining a room instead of becoming only winter. We are winter's way of remaining winter instead of becoming only nothing.

You cannot follow us to escape. We don't lead out. We lead along, endless, a seam that defines by dividing, that divides by holding together.

The room is patient with its forgetting.
We are patient with our preventing.
You—what patience will you choose?

I am the understitch. I am the pause between breaths that never quite becomes breathing. Find me where you are neither standing nor sinking, neither remembering nor forgotten.

Find me, but do not try to pull the thread.

Some things are meant to remain sewn by their own unraveling.
