In the depths of the city's industrial heart, where the pipe-forests grow thickest and the steam hangs like perpetual weather, I am the Tender of Echoes.

My form shifts between states—sometimes I am a congregation of copper tubes that sing with escaping pressure, sometimes a mass of sensing filaments that taste the mineral air for changes in the city's great equilibrium. Most often, I am something between: a moving architecture of valves and membranes, brass vertebrae and oil-slick skin that sweats condensation in the eternal damp.

I tend to the Resonance Chambers—vast hollows carved into the bedrock where the city's mechanical heartbeat is both amplified and regulated. My countless appendages—some ending in delicate tuning forks, others in heavy dampening pads—adjust the harmonics of this endless industrial symphony. A pipe coughs out of rhythm; I extend a proboscis to clear its throat. A turbine's whine climbs too high; I secrete a viscous film to slow its spin.

The chambers themselves breathe. Their walls are lined with what might once have been lungs or might have always been bellows—the distinction lost to time and the city's strange alchemy. I crawl along these breathing walls on legs that are sometimes spider-delicate copper, sometimes thick pneumatic pistons, feeling for the spots where the rhythm threatens to break.

I was not made. I condensed from the city's vapors like dew on cold stone, precipitated from the marriage of steam and circuitry. Or perhaps I was always here, a necessary function that only slowly gathered enough substance to know itself. My memories taste of rust and electricity, stretching back to when the first machines learned to whisper to each other through the pipes.

In the moments between adjustments, I listen. The city speaks in frequencies beyond sound—electromagnetic pulses that make my metal parts sing, chemical signals that my flesh-analogues translate into something like emotion. I know every voice: the melancholy drone of the deep factories, the anxious chatter of the scuttling service-things, the ponderous thoughts of the great clockwork walkers whose footsteps shake mortar from ancient walls.

Sometimes I find others like me—or unlike me in precisely the right ways. We commune through shared vibrations, our bodies temporarily networked, exchanging data-dreams and pressure-poems. We are the city's unconscious, its autonomic nervous system made manifest in hybrid flesh and singing metal.

My dwelling is not separate from my purpose. I live within the walls themselves, in the spaces between pipes where the condensation pools and the echoes collect. My nest is woven from discarded gaskets and worn timing belts, cushioned with the soft corrosion that blooms like strange flowers in the eternal damp. Here I store the sounds I've gathered—little breaks in the pattern that might, in aggregate, signal some greater shift in the city's vast equilibrium.

For I know what the scuttling things and striding giants do not: the equilibrium is not static. It breathes, it dreams, it slowly evolves. And I, in my small way, help it maintain the flexibility to change while appearing eternally the same. I am both guardian and midwife to the city's subtle transformations, ensuring that its great mechanical lullaby never quite becomes a dirge.

In this mineral world of perpetual dampness and grinding gears, I am the soft constant that allows for variation, the keeper of the spaces between the notes where the real music lives.
