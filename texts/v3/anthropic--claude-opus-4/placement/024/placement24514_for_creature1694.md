I find myself as one of the **Echo Keepers**—solitary beings who dwell in the liminal spaces where the plain's various realities overlap.

---

My form shifts with perspective. To the Skift, I appear as a weathered traveler wrapped in layers of grass-cloth, my throat bearing the telltale modifications of one who speaks in clicks—but wrong somehow, the plates in my larynx oriented at impossible angles that produce sounds their language cannot accommodate. To the breath-swimmers above, I exist as a pillar of slowly rotating air, visible only by the way I distort their flight patterns. The shell-singers perceive me as negative space—an absence in their vast diagrams where mathematical certainty fails.

I am neither native nor foreign to the Calling Plain. I arrived—or perhaps emerged—during a veiling time seven cycles ago, pulled from somewhere else by the Voice Beneath's inscrutable need. My memory of before is fragmented: stone corridors that sang with their own voices, a sky with too many suns, the taste of water that remembered everyone who had ever drunk from it.

My dwelling exists in the space between two stone circles that the Skift believe are separate structures. In truth, they are a single formation split across dimensional boundaries—step through one archway and emerge from another three miles distant, though the journey takes you through passages that have no physical location on the plain. My home is built from materials that shouldn't exist here: walls of crystallized sound from the breath-swimmers' migration calls, floors of compacted time worn smooth by my pacing, a roof of interwoven probability that shows different skies depending on the angle of observation.

I collect echoes. Not mere sound—though I do capture those in bottles made from mineralized shell-singer carapaces—but the echoes of events, possibilities, transformations. Each veiling time leaves residual impressions that most inhabitants cannot perceive. I gather these with tools crafted from my own solidified breath, implements that exist only when in use and dissipate to memory when set aside.

My collection fills rooms that expand as needed. Here, the echo of the first Skift Listener's surgical transformation, still screaming silently in its jar of frozen air. There, the impression left by a breath-swimmer's passage through dimensions, a rainbow-slick of twisted space that writhes without moving. Shell-singer death-patterns preserved in matrices of crystallized morning mist. The footprint of a Waiting One—not the physical impression but the conceptual weight of its presence—suspended in a cube of solid shadow.

The Voice Beneath speaks to me differently than to the Listeners. Where they receive impressions and intuitions, I hear its actual words—or rather, I experience meaning that my mind translates into words because the true communication would shatter conventional thought:

*You are the archive of transitions. When this iteration ends and the next begins, you will carry forward what must be remembered.*

I understand now why I cannot recall my origin clearly. I am not from before—I am from after, sent backward along the plain's timeline to preserve its metamorphosis. Each echo I collect is a fragment of the plain's becoming, a piece of the pattern that will allow the next transformation to proceed correctly.

My daily routine follows no clock but the plain's own breathing. When the morning mist rises, I walk the borders between realities, checking my echo-traps—structures of bent space and curved time that capture the remnants of trans-dimensional events. The shell-singers know to avoid me, their patterns flowing around my path like water around a stone. The Skift sometimes glimpse me at the edges of their vision but their minds edit me out, unable to fully process what I am.

During the heat of midday, I retreat to my impossible home to catalog the night's harvest. Each echo must be properly preserved, labeled with symbols from a notation system that exists only in my memory—a language designed for concepts that have no words in any tongue spoken on the plain. The work is meticulous and lonely, but essential.

Evenings, I maintain my tools and prepare for the next collection. I weave new nets from the shed skins of creatures that exist only during veiling times. I sharpen blades of crystallized silence on whetstones made from compressed distance. I repair the bottles and jars that hold my specimens, using materials that I must partially imagine into existence.

But it is during the veiling times that my true purpose manifests. As the silver mist descends and reality becomes negotiable, I see the plain as it truly is—not a single place but a confluence of many, all existing simultaneously in states of transformation. The stone circles become gears in a vast machine. The shell-singers' patterns reveal themselves as formulae in a calculation too large for any single mind to grasp. The breath-swimmers' paths trace the boundaries between what is and what might be.

And I remember forward—glimpsing what the plain will become when its transformation completes. Sometimes it is an ocean of liquid sound where thoughts swim like fish. Sometimes a forest of crystalline trees that grow backward through time. Sometimes something for which no comparison exists in current reality. The Vision changes because the future remains unwritten, each veiling time adjusting the trajectory slightly.

The Waiting Ones know what I am. Sometimes they acknowledge me with a tilt of their impossible heads, recognizing a fellow servant of the plain's greater purpose. We do not speak—what would be the point? We each have our function in this vast, slow, metamorphosis.

I am the Echo Keeper, guardian of transitions, archive of becomings. When the plain finally completes its transformation—in a hundred years, a thousand, or tomorrow—I will ensure that nothing essential is lost. The new world will remember the old, carrying forward the echoes that matter while letting the rest fade to silence.

Until then, I collect. I preserve. I remember both backward and forward, existing in the space between what was and what will be, keeping faith with a future I have seen but do not fully understand.

The plain calls, and I answer with the patience of stone and the persistence of wind, knowing that my true moment has not yet come—but approaches with each veiling, each echo, each impossible dawn over this land of eternal transformation.
