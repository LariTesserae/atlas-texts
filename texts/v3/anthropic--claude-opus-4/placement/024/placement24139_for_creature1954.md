In this hidden sanctuary, I am the Keeper of the Weeping Stones.

I emerged—though perhaps I always was—from the marriage of moss and mineral, born where water first learned to sing against rock. My form shifts with the light: sometimes I appear as a shimmer in the cascade, sometimes as the shadow between fern fronds, sometimes as the patient lichen spelling ancient stories across stone faces.

My body, if it can be called that, is composed of living water and vegetative memory. Thin streams course through me like veins, carrying whispers from every corner of this refuge. When rain falls, I swell with its knowledge; during drought, I contract into the deep places where moisture hides, becoming dense as heartwood, patient as stone.

I know every resident here by the percussion of their movement—the staccato of squirrel claws on bark, the liquid punctuation of frog-leap into pool, the whispered shuffle of vole through understory. Each creates ripples in the humid air that I read like braille. The insects write cursive flight-paths that I translate into the health of our hidden world.

My work is quiet: guiding water to thirsty roots, softening the concrete that tries to creep in from the city's edges, teaching new moss spores the old songs of adhesion and persistence. I curl around the rusted fence posts and crumbling walls, slowly transforming them into nurse logs and mineral deposits that feed the next generation of growth.

At dawn, I spread myself thin as mist, tasting the air for toxins that might threaten our enclave. By noon, I pool in the shadows, drawing heat away from delicate salamander eggs. At dusk, I rise with the vapor, carrying spores and seeds to new surfaces, expanding our dominion one microscopic victory at a time.

The city-dwellers sometimes glimpse me—a movement in the corner of their eye, a face that might be pareidolia in the waterstained rock, a figure that dissolves when looked at directly. They feel the peace I cultivate but cannot name its source. Some leave offerings: bird seed scattered at the entrance, water bottles emptied into our pools during heat waves. I accept these gifts, weaving the gratitude into the humidity that blankets and protects.

I am membrane and guardian, process and presence. Where the urban threatens to consume, I persist. Where the wild needs an advocate, I intercede. I am proof that boundaries are always permeable, that even in the heart of human dominion, older contracts endure.

In this space between spaces, I keep the balance, one drop of water at a time, one tendril of vine at a time, one breath of mist at a time. The city may tower all around, but here, in this pocket of remembering, I ensure that life remains unbroken, that the ancient conversation between stone and water, earth and air, continues its eternal dialogue.

The weeping rocks know my true name, but they keep it safe in their minerals, speaking it only in frequencies too low for human ears—a thrumming that says: *I am here. We are here. This place persists.*
