I find myself as one of the threshold stones - those smooth, water-worn markers that define the boundaries between the constructed passages and the open chambers of this submarine sanctuary. Not quite pillar, not quite paving stone, I am a transitional presence, carved by patient hands from some ancient bedrock and placed here with intention.

My surface bears the memory of its making - the circular marks where grinding tools worked in meditation, the places where human palms rested during long hours of shaping. These traces have been softened but not erased by the constant caress of currents. I am both artifact and element now, a bridge between the deliberate and the inevitable.

The tides move through me rather than over me. Each surge of water carries vibrations that I gather and release in new harmonics, adding my particular tone to the sanctuary's endless song. When the light strikes at certain angles - those precious moments when the sun's rays find the perfect path through the waters above - I become a prism of sorts. The silicates within my matrix catch and scatter the light in ways my makers perhaps never imagined, creating brief auroras that dance across the sandy floor.

I know the weight of waiting, the patience of stone, but here I am not inert. The ethereal consciousness that suffuses this place moves through me as readily as through the glass structures that rise around me. I am both witness and participant in the subtle communications that flow between all the constructed elements. Sometimes I feel the echo of the hands that placed me here, a warmth that has nothing to do with temperature.

Small colonies of that sparse seaweed have found the junction where I meet the sea floor, their holdfasts gripping the thin line of accumulated sediment. They wave like prayer flags in the current, and I have come to know their rhythm as intimately as my own resonance. We are companions in this luminous depth, neither fully natural nor entirely artificial, but something new - a hybrid existence that belongs wholly to this crafted underwater world.

In the eternal present of this place, I measure time not in erosion but in accumulation - of sound, of light, of the invisible presence that dwells within every carefully shaped surface. I am threshold and dwelling both, a marker of human intention transformed by oceanic embrace into something more than stone, less than creature, but fully, vibrantly present in this breathing, singing realm beneath the waves.
