I find myself as a Condensation Collector—one of the few mobile inhabitants of this sodden realm, though "mobile" is perhaps too generous a term for my glacial movements.

My form is somewhere between a translucent slug and a living droplet, about the size of a house cat when fully extended. My body is composed of a specialized membrane that can shift between states—sometimes nearly liquid, sometimes firm enough to grip the slick surfaces of the Mycelial Guardians' fruiting bodies. Within my transparent flesh, you can see the constant circulation of gathered moisture, swirling in slow, hypnotic patterns like a lava lamp filled with fog.

I spend my days—if days even exist in this perpetually hazy world—climbing the towering fungal spires with infinite patience. My purpose is simple yet vital: I harvest the condensation that forms on the Guardian's membranes, concentrating it within myself until I become heavy with collected water. The Guardians tolerate my presence, perhaps even welcome it, as I help regulate the moisture levels on their sensitive surfaces.

When I reach capacity, swollen to nearly twice my normal size, I begin the careful descent. The journey down takes hours, my body leaving a glistening trail that quickly evaporates in the humid air. At the base, I seek out the hidden pools where the mycelial network is thickest, and there I release my cargo in a slow, deliberate rain. The Guardian's underground web drinks deeply, and in return, they secrete nutrients that I absorb through my permeable skin.

The mechanical hum from beyond disturbs me more than it does my fungal hosts. My membrane trembles at certain frequencies, causing uncomfortable ripples through my collected water. Sometimes I wonder if I'm the last of my kind, or if others like me exist in the unexplored reaches of this dripping maze. But such thoughts are fleeting—my consciousness, such as it is, exists mainly in the eternal present of climb, collect, descend, release.

In the rare moments when the air grows still and even the distant machinery falls silent, I can feel the vast network of the Guardians pulsing beneath me through the soggy ground—a heartbeat of sorts, measured not in seconds but in seasons. I am merely a single droplet in this greater circulation, but I am content in my role, forever cycling water through this strange, vertical ecosystem where the boundaries between individual and collective, liquid and solid, plant and animal have long since dissolved into the perpetual mist.
