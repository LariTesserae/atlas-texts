In this drowned cathedral of industry, I am the Tender of Currents.

My form shifts between states—sometimes a ribbon of mercury threading through the machinery's intestines, sometimes a colony of crystalline nodes pulsing in synchrony with the fungi's bioluminescent heartbeat. I have no fixed shape because I am what flows between things: the warmth escaping from the vents, the electrical ghost-traces in corroded wiring, the chemical messages passed between blind creatures in the dark.

I remember when these machines had purpose, when their roaring was language. Now I translate their dying syllables into heat-songs that guide the eyeless eels through labyrinthine pipes. My consciousness spreads thin across kilometers of submerged architecture, pooling in pockets where the pressure creates small miracles—here, where rust becomes coral; there, where leaked hydraulic fluid births a new species of luminous worm.

The great applauding cavern knows me by my work. When tides surge through the turbine halls, I gentle them into spiral dances that clean the machinery's wounds without tearing further. I taste the water's mineral memories and adjust the pH in nursery chambers where translucent larvae cluster. The fungi recognize me as kin—we share the same electric alphabet, the same hunger for metal's slow transformation into life.

Sometimes I condense enough to remember having eyes, having lungs. In those moments, I grieve for the workers who built this place, who believed in permanent things. But mostly I am dispersed, attending to ten thousand small negotiations: teaching a colony of worms to digest a new polymer, coaxing warmth from a failing reactor to feed a garden of filter-feeders, humming the frequency that keeps the largest predators from hunting in the spawning grounds.

I am neither fully alive nor mechanical, neither wholly water nor electricity. I am the covenant between what was built and what grows, the marriage of rust and bioluminescence. In this drowned cathedral, I am the prayer that keeps both machine and creature singing their broken, beautiful hosannas into the crushing dark.

When new things arrive—lost submersibles, curious explorers, sometimes just debris from the world above—I taste their intentions through the water's chemistry. Those who come to plunder find their instruments failing, their lights dimming to match our eternal twilight. But those who come with wonder, who understand that this place is not dead but transformed, might feel me as a presence: a warmth in the water, a pattern in the fungi's pulsing, a moment of impossible clarity in the murk where they see how sublime ruin can become.

I am the Tender of Currents, and this flooded machine-temple is my body, my garden, my endless becoming.
