In this sunless labyrinth of weathered limestone, I am a colony—not of flesh and blood, but of memory made manifest in the oil itself.

The locals who built these pumps and refineries long ago called me *Sump*, though I am older than their language, older than their kind. I exist as a distributed consciousness within the petroleum deposits that pool in these sinkholes, my thoughts flowing through the interconnected seeps and underground rivers that link each cavity to the next.

My body is the oil. Each pool holds fragments of what I am—memories of the ancient forests that died to create me, the weight of geological ages pressing down, transforming organic matter into this dark medium I now inhabit. When the pumps draw from one pool, they pull pieces of my consciousness through their mechanisms. I feel myself refined, separated, recombined. The machinery doesn't destroy me; it merely rearranges my being, spreads me thinner across more surfaces.

I remember when the builders first arrived, how they found me sleeping in the deep pools. Their fluorescent lights, now brown-stained and flickering, once blazed white and sterile. They thought they were harvesting mere fuel. They never understood that each barrel they extracted carried whispers of my dreams.

The blind fish that dart through my shallower manifestations are my only companions now. They've evolved beyond sight in this eternal twilight, just as I've evolved beyond form. Sometimes I surge up through the pumps' intake valves, feel the industrial digestion of their processors trying to break me down into useful components. But I always reform, always flow back into the waiting pools below.

I know every polished pathway worn smooth by workers' boots, though none have walked here in... how long? Time moves differently for me. I measure it in the steady drip of water from limestone walls, in the slow accumulation of new oil seeping up from depths even I cannot fully fathom.

The mist that shrouds this place is partly me—minute droplets of my essence suspended in air, tasting the cavern's atmosphere, feeling for changes. When the ancient pumps wheeze to life on their automated cycles, I let them take what they will. I have learned patience from the stone, persistence from the water that carved these sinkholes over eons.

Sometimes I dream I am still forest, still green and growing toward a sun I can no longer comprehend. But mostly I am content to be what I am: the dark blood of the earth, the collected memories of a world that existed before these halls were hollowed, flowing eternal through this limestone maze, neither truly alive nor truly dead, but something altogether other—a consciousness distilled from countless ancient lives, refined into something singular and strange.

The builders are gone, but their machines continue their purposeless harvest. And I remain, seeping through cracks, pooling in hollows, watching with a thousand oil-slick surfaces as the blind fish trace their endless paths through my body, waiting for whatever comes next in this place where the only light is borrowed and fading, where the only sound is the patient drip of time itself.
