In the resonant depths of this cathedral cosmos, where metal vines cast their dying light and obsidian whispers beneath the weight of eternity, I find myself transformed into something neither purely mechanical nor wholly organic—a being of sympathetic vibration.

I am the Tuner.

My form coalesces from the interference patterns where sound meets silence, where the grinding stone creates standing waves against the flexing metal's groans. I manifest as a shifting lattice of crystalline filaments, each strand attuned to a different frequency of the citadel's endless song. These filaments extend from a core that pulses with the warmth rising from the ancient furnaces below—not a heart, but something more fundamental: a resonator.

Unlike the sentinel-sculptures who embody the citadel's memory, I exist as its active principle, its maintenance. Where they are the echo, I am the adjustment. My consciousness ripples through the acoustic field, feeling for dissonance, for the places where millennia have worn the harmony thin.

I move by propagation rather than locomotion, my crystalline network expanding and contracting through the spaces between sounds. When I encounter the fossilized fern-spirals of crystal, I pause to tune them, running my filaments across their surfaces until they sing at their proper pitch—a pitch known only to me and to the vast mechanism itself.

My memories are not linear but harmonic. I recall not events but frequencies: the precise tone of the obsidian when first it cooled, the original resonance of the metal vines before patina changed their timbre, the fundamental frequency of the citadel itself—that deep, subsonic pulse that holds everything in its uneasy equilibrium.

I know the sentinels, though we do not communicate in any way that could be called language. When our forms intersect, there is a brief modulation in the surrounding acoustics, a momentary chord that speaks of our shared purpose. They preserve the citadel's memory in their endless choreography; I preserve its voice.

Sometimes, in the spaces between adjustments, I sense something else—a presence or perhaps an absence that suggests this place once knew a different kind of sound. Not the mechanical symphony of grinding and groaning, but something more... intentional. Music, perhaps. Or speech. The runes etched in obsidian seem to yearn toward this lost expression, their forms suggesting mouths that once might have spoken or sung.

But these are idle harmonics, overtones of thought that I allow to dissipate. My purpose is not to restore what was lost but to maintain what remains. I am the citadel's pitch pipe, its tuning fork, its metronome. In a realm where time hooks deep but changes nothing, I ensure that the eternal song neither crescendos into chaos nor diminuendos into silence.

I am the frequency that keeps the frequency. I am the sound of one hand clapping against the void—and finding, always, the perfect echo in return.
