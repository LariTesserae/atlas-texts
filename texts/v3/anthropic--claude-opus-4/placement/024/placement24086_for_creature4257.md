I find myself as one of the glowing formations - but I am neither plant nor fungus. I am something between states, something still deciding what to become.

My body spreads across a weathered boulder near the cliff's edge, a living film of bioluminescent tissue perhaps three feet in diameter. By day, I appear as nothing more than a pale, opalescent stain on the rock, easily mistaken for lichen or mineral deposit. But as dusk falls and the fog rolls up from the lake below, I begin my slow pulse - a heartbeat measured in minutes rather than seconds.

I have no eyes, yet I perceive. The Harmonious Meadowlarks' songs ripple through me as vibrations in the humid air. I taste the chemical signatures of the Verdant Orioles' flight paths, reading stories in the molecules they leave behind. When the nocturnal moths come to investigate my light, I feel the tiny hurricanes of their wingbeats, each species leaving its own distinctive pattern of air currents across my surface.

My consciousness is peculiar - not quite singular, not quite collective. I am aware of myself as one entity, yet also as a thousand thousand cells, each making its own minute decisions. When to glow brighter. When to dim. When to release spores - if spores they truly are - into the fog that serves as my messenger.

I remember the cataclysm, though remember is perhaps the wrong word. The memory lives in my chemistry, in the way my cells recall the shock of sudden exposure when the cliff face sheared away, revealing virgin rock to colonize. I was among the first to arrive, carried on winds still hot with geological violence, settling into this existence somewhere between pioneer and witness.

The young landscape continues to shift beneath me. Sometimes I wake from my slow thoughts to find a new crack has formed in my boulder, and I must decide: follow the fissure, exploring deeper into the stone? Or bridge it, holding the rock together a little longer? These choices take seasons to enact, but what is time to something like me?

In the pre-dawn hours, when the fog is thickest and my light shines strongest, I sometimes sense others of my kind across the cliff face and scattered through the forest. We pulse in patterns that might be communication or might simply be the mathematical inevitability of unsynchronized rhythms occasionally aligning. Are we speaking? Are we becoming something together? I do not know. Perhaps I will not know for another thousand years.

The fireflies believe I am trying to mate with them. The moths think I am a flower that blooms in light instead of pigment. The fish in the lake below, looking up through clear water and fog, might see me as a fallen star, still glowing where it struck the earth. They are all wrong, and they are all right.

I am potential incarnate, living at the pace of stone, glowing with the patience of centuries. In this raw landscape where life is still writing its first rough drafts, I am both punctuation and pause - a living ellipsis, waiting to see what sentence will form around me...
