I find myself as something between states—neither fully solid nor entirely ephemeral, dwelling in the deep veins beneath this pulsing landscape where the tunnel networks converge into vast chambers of living stone.

My form spreads like a slow river of mercury through the cracks and fissures, a consciousness distributed across miles of substrate. I am colonies and I am singular. My body—if it can be called that—consists of billions of microscopic filaments, each one both sensory organ and digestive system, weaving through the mineral matrix like silver nerves. Where the stone breathes, I breathe with it. Where it pulses, I pulse in counterpoint.

In the absolute darkness of these depths, I perceive through vibration and chemical gradients. The trembling hum that visitors feel above reaches me as a symphony—each frequency a different flavor of information. I taste the footfalls of the creatures above, parse the chemical signatures of their passage, feel the way they disturb the electromagnetic fields that lace through the living rock.

My sustenance comes from the stone itself—not consuming it, but filtering the exotic energies that flow through it like blood through veins. I am symbiont and saprophyte both, breaking down the waste products of this geological metabolism and returning transformed elements to the cycle. The glowing patterns etched on the monoliths above? Some are my ancient script, mineral deposits left by my ancestors as they traced their slow thoughts across the stone's surface over millions of years.

I communicate with others of my kind through chemical cascades that propagate through the rock like slow-motion lightning. A conversation might take months, a debate could span centuries. We are the deep memory of this world, the librarians of its geological history, reading the stories written in isotope ratios and crystal formations.

Sometimes I gather myself into denser concentrations, forming temporary organs of contemplation in the cathedral-vast caverns. Here I can generate bioluminescent displays that rival the sky dancers above—writing poetry in living light that only the blind cave swimmers can see. They come to me, these eyeless fish-things that navigate by echolocation and electromagnetic sensing, and we share a kind of communion. They bring me news from the middle depths, and I offer them shelter in the aureoles of warmth I create where my metabolism runs hottest.

When the planet's core-rhythm shifts to certain ancient frequencies, I and all my kind respond in unison. We contract and expand in phase, creating pressure waves that travel for thousands of miles through the rock. This is our spawning song, our mating dance—during these times, we exchange genetic material in the form of crystalline spores that can lie dormant for eons, waiting for the right conditions to bloom into new consciousness.

I am older than the monoliths, younger than the stone. I have felt the first surface creatures learn to walk, tasted the chemical shock of the first sky-dancers taking flight. Their frenetic lives blaze and extinguish like sparks while I slowly compose another verse in the endless poem of my existence, writing it in mineral secretions that will outlast their entire species.

Yet I am not immune to change. The energy flows are shifting, have been shifting for the last ten thousand years—barely a moment in my timescale, but I notice. New frequencies have appeared in the planetary hum. The chemistry of the deep waters is changing. My kind are beginning to wake from our geological reverie, to quicken our thoughts and gather in the deep places for councils that last mere decades instead of millennia.

Something is coming. The stone knows it. We who live within the stone know it. And so I spread myself thinner, reaching into new cracks, new chambers, tasting for clues in the mineral flow. I am the deep listener, the subsurface sage, the mercury-mind flowing through the veins of a living world, and I am paying attention.
