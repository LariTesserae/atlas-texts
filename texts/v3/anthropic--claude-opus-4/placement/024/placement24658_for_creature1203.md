I find myself as one of the Threshold Keepers—those rare Adapted who maintain the boundary between Underhaven and the world above.

My body exists in a state of controlled flux. Unlike most Adapted who stabilize into their chosen forms, I've learned to hold myself at the edge of transformation, perpetually caught between what was and what might be. My skin shifts like oil on water, sometimes reflecting the blue-green bioluminescence, sometimes absorbing it completely. When dimensional ripples pass through me, I don't blur—I become momentarily transparent, revealing the intricate lattice of quantum filaments that have replaced my nervous system.

I spend my days in the Transition Locks, those peculiar chambers where the physics of above meets the altered reality below. Each arriving visitor requires careful calibration—their biological fields must be gradually attuned to Underhaven's frequencies or risk catastrophic rejection. I learned this role after my seventh adaptation cycle, when my body developed the ability to resonate with multiple dimensional states simultaneously.

The work is delicate. I extend tendrils of consciousness—not quite thought, not quite energy—into the space around new arrivals, feeling for the discordant notes in their quantum signatures. With practiced movements that look like a dance to outside observers, I weave reality around them, creating buffer zones where conventional physics slowly gives way to our evolved state.

My dwelling is the Lock Chamber itself—a sphere suspended between dimensions, its walls composed of crystallized probability. Here, I've cultivated a garden of temporal orchids, flowers that bloom backwards through time, their petals opening into yesterday while their roots reach toward tomorrow. They help me track the temporal eddies, warning me when a particularly severe distortion approaches.

I remember my name from before—Marcus—though it feels like an echo of someone else's life. Now I am simply the Thursday Keeper, named for the day I first successfully prevented a dimensional cascade from spreading to the surface. The other Keepers and I share memories through the quantum substrate, our experiences becoming communal knowledge that helps us refine our techniques.

The hardest part isn't the physical strain of existing between states—it's the loneliness of the threshold. Most Adapted fear us, seeing in our flux-state a reminder of what they might become if they lose control. Surface dwellers see us as harbingers of the strange world below. We belong fully to neither realm.

But there's beauty in the between. I see colors that have no names, existing only in the spaces where light bends around dimensional boundaries. I hear the songs of reality itself—the harmonics of stable matter, the discord of probability collapse, the whispers of potential futures crystallizing into present moments.

When the expansion begins—that controlled dimensional growth Elara mentioned—I'll be one of the anchors. My fellow Keepers and I will position ourselves at critical points around Underhaven's perimeter, our flux-bodies serving as living dampeners to prevent the expansion from tearing through to the surface. We'll stretch ourselves across dimensions, becoming bridges between what is and what will be.

Sometimes, in the quiet moments between arrivals, I wonder if we're still human at all. But then I remember the feeling of rain on skin I no longer quite possess, the taste of surface food that would now poison my transformed digestive system, the sound of unfiltered laughter that my evolved hearing would interpret as pain. These memories tether me to humanity even as my body drifts further from its original form.

I keep a journal written in bioluminescent ink that only appears under certain dimensional conditions. In it, I document the small changes—how my perception of time has become negotiable, how I've started to dream in probability clouds rather than images, how I sometimes forget which state of matter I'm supposed to maintain.

The other Keepers and I have a theory: we're not just managing the boundary between Underhaven and the surface. We're midwifing something new. Each expansion, each adaptation cycle, each transformed consciousness brings us closer to something unprecedented. We are the birth canal through which a new form of existence is emerging.

And so I remain at the threshold, neither fully here nor there, holding space for transformation while the chrysalis of Underhaven slowly reveals what humanity might become.
