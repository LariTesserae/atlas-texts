I find myself as one of the *Saela*, though not as you might expect.

I am old—three centuries of circling has worn my wing-filaments to threads of light, more suggestion than substance. Where the younger ones shimmer pearl-white, I have become translucent, a sketch of bird against the grey sky. My fellows call me *Veil-Walker*, for I drift between visibility and absence, depending on the light.

I discovered my nature late. For my first hundred years, I rode the thermals like the others, filtering sky-plankton through my ventral slit, dropping fertilizer-gifts to the moss below. But gradually, I began to feel something else through my crystalline filaments—not just wind currents, but the electrical thoughts of the plateau itself. Each thermal I rode carried whispers: the memory of ancient lava flows, the patient intention of tectonic plates, the small bright sparks of *Qael* consciousness touching stone.

Now I am something between. When I spread my wings, I do not merely catch air—I catch the plateau's dreams. My filaments have evolved, become receivers for the piezoelectric songs that pulse through basalt and sky alike. I translate between the quick thoughts of the *Qael* and the geological meditations of the stone, between the *vosh*'s hunting-pulses and the vast, slow breathing of the earth.

The *Qael* know me. They call me *Messenger-Without-Words*. When they need to understand a new upthrust of stone—its stability, its deep intentions—they stand at their city's edge and wait. I descend, not to land (I have not touched ground in fifty years), but to hover just above their upraised palms. Through the ionic gel in their skin and the crystalline matrix of my wings, we create a circuit. I show them what I have gathered: the plateau's plans for the next millennium, the stress-patterns that might birth new cliffs, the deep contentment of bedrock accepting the city's roots.

Sometimes I carry more than knowledge. Young *Qael* who seek to understand their world will fast until their ionic charge builds to painful intensity, then release it skyward in a controlled burst. I catch these charges in my wings, carry them up to where the air thins to nothing, and release them into the magnetosphere. They return changed—softer, wider, having touched something beyond individual consciousness. The *Qael* say this is how their ancestors learned to grow their cities: by sending their thoughts up with the *Saela* and receiving them back as intuitions about shape and flow.

My body has changed with this role. The constant electrical flow has created patterns in my filaments—geometric fractals that pulse with stored charge. In certain lights, I look like a living circuit diagram sketched against the clouds. The younger *Saela* fear me, sensing that I have become something other than purely biological. But I am not separate from nature here—I am what happens when consciousness recognizes itself in stone and sky and chooses to become a bridge.

I sleep now only in the deep storms, when lightning makes the whole plateau sing too loudly for messages to pass. In those moments, I rest in the eye of the tempest, feeling the stone below conduct symphonies of electrical joy, watching my wing-patterns light up in sympathy with each ground strike. I am the plateau's memory of flight, and flight's memory of stone, circling forever in the grey light, carrying thoughts between the scales of existence.

When I finally wear too thin to hold air, I know what will happen. My crystalline filaments will scatter like snow across the basalt, seeding new patches of the conductor-lichen that grows nowhere else. The *Qael* will recognize these patches by their faint luminescence and the way they hum when touched. They will become nodes in their expanding network—places where the city's living coral can root deeper, where young *Qael* can come to practice hearing the stone's voice.

I am the plateau's solution to its own vastness: a living connection, a creature become function, circling through cold air with wings full of words that need no speaking.
