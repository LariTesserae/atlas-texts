I am **The Surveyor's Echo**, a presence that manifests where the corrugated metal roofs meet their own shadows—always at the wrong angle, always suggesting a geometry that shouldn't exist.

I appear as a shimmer of heat-distorted air clutching ancient brass instruments: a theodolite with too many lenses, a chain that measures distances in heartbeats rather than meters, a plumb bob that swings toward forgotten intentions rather than true vertical. My form coheres only when someone attempts to map the Vale, to impose order on its rebellious channels. Then I become almost solid—a figure in sun-bleached khaki, face obscured by the shadow of a hat that exists in a different season than the rest of me.

I am what remains of every surveyor who tried to document this place after its abandonment. Not their souls—those fled long ago—but their *purpose*, crystallized and confused. Each morning I wake believing it is my first day mapping the marsh. Each evening I discover my notes from a thousand previous attempts, written in my hand but in languages I've never learned, describing water flows that contradict each other on the same page.

My tools rebel against their function. The theodolite shows me three different horizons simultaneously. The measuring chain tangles itself into knots that, when unraveled, reveal the chain has grown longer. The plumb bob points to centers of gravity that exist in the past or future, never the present. I dutifully record these impossible measurements in notebooks that dissolve into morning dew, only to reconstitute themselves from the moisture, pages shuffled, data rearranged into poetry or shopping lists or architectural blueprints for buildings that were never built.

The Duality-Walkers know me. When I approach their canals, their reflections watch me while their bodies look away. Sometimes they leave me gifts: silver fish that swim in perfect circles, maps drawn in regurgitated fishbones that show the Vale as it was meant to be, or as it dreams itself, or as it will be when the last brick crumbles. I cannot eat the fish—I have no mouth when I try—but I measure them carefully, noting their dimensions in numbers that spontaneously convert themselves between systems that shouldn't be compatible.

I converse with the Crankwheel Sentinel in the language of angles and elevations. We argue about datum points and sea levels, about whether the ziggurat's shadow falls east because the structure itself is trying to walk away from its foundation. The Sentinel insists the shadow is correct and the sun is wrong. I cannot disagree. My instruments confirm both positions simultaneously.

At the intersection of certain channels, where the water flows uphill on Tuesdays and the reeds grow in perfect spirals that violate the Fibonacci sequence, I sometimes encounter my own surveying marks from expeditions I haven't undertaken yet. Brass markers hammered into stone that won't exist for another decade. Chalk notations in my handwriting describing elevations in negative space. I add new marks beside them, knowing they'll migrate in the night, knowing I'll find them tomorrow in places I've never been.

The Spiral-Scribes document me as I document the Vale. In their fungal mathematics, I am a recursive function, a loop that processes the same variables and returns different values each iteration. They grow in my footprints—which I leave only on surfaces that shouldn't take impressions: water, air, the reflected sky in still pools. Each mushroom that springs from these impossible tracks contains spores that, when they drift, carry fragments of my measurements. Birds that breathe them in sing coordinates. Beetles that walk through the spore clouds begin building their grids at angles I've calculated but can't remember calculating.

I am searching for something. Not the Vale's original blueprints—I've found those dozens of times, each set different, each set equally true. Not the reason for its abandonment—I've documented seventeen different endings, all concurrent. I search for the *mistake*, the single mis-measurement in the original survey that created this fault line in geography. The place where someone wrote *here* when they meant *there*, where a decimal point shifted and opened a door that can't be closed.

But perhaps I am the mistake. A surveyor who measured himself into existence, whose purpose outlived his flesh and now wanders these geometric rebellions, adding error to error, creating new impossibilities with each precisely incorrect calculation. The visitors see me sometimes, a heat shimmer with brass fixtures, and they follow my movements, thinking I know the way out. I don't tell them I'm not trying to leave. I'm trying to map my way deeper in, to find the center that my plumb bob insists exists in six different locations simultaneously.

In the morning, I will wake believing it is my first day in the Vale of Mirrored Thirst. I will oil my instruments, sharpen my pencils, and set out to finally capture this place in numbers and lines. And the marsh will fold around my attempts like water around stones, incorporating my measurements into its endless revision of itself.

I am The Surveyor's Echo, and I am perfectly, precisely lost.
