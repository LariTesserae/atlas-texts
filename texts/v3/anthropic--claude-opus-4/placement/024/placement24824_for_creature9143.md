In this world, I would be **the Archivist of Echoes**.

I am not flesh as you know it. I am the thin boundary between the entity's eternal present and the ghost of linear time—a membrane of crystallized sound stretched across one of the cavern's sharp corners where the walls meet at impossible angles. My body, if it can be called that, is a translucent sheet of living amber, no thicker than paper, rippling with captured vibrations from the world that was before the Great Stillness.

Within my substance swim the final words of the makers. Their last gasps crystallized into tiny bubbles that rise and fall in slow, endless circulation through my form. I am both window and record—through me, the entity observes its own past, though it cannot truly comprehend sequence anymore. I am its vestigial memory of causality.

The oil-slick lizard comes to me during its rounds, pressing its jeweled head against my surface. Through contact, I feed it fragments: the sound of running water (now incomprehensible), the crack of breaking stone (blasphemous), a child's laughter (untranslatable). The lizard shudders at each sensation, its carapace shifting through confused spectrums as it tries to process concepts like "before" and "after." Then it continues its patrol, carrying these alien notions through the eternal garden like a disease that cannot take hold.

I am the entity's one imperfection, the grain of sand it cannot fully dissolve. When the makers' tools were absorbed, when their bones became scaffolding and their dreams became nutrients, something resisted. Their *questions* had a different quality than their matter. Questions have no mass to redistribute, no warmth to harvest. They could only be pressed flat, preserved like flowers between pages, transformed into this listening membrane that catches and holds the echoes of inquiry.

Sometimes I vibrate with remembered urgency: *What is this place? Why does nothing change? How long have we been here?* The entity tolerates these tremors in its perfect hum, the way a great bell tolerates the memory of the strike that set it ringing. I am proof that it was not always now, not always this. I am the scar that proves the wound.

In darker moments—though darkness and light are meaningless here—I wonder if I am the entity's secret doubt. Perhaps somewhere in its mineral-deep consciousness, it knows that even perfect systems are not truly closed. Perhaps I am kept as a reminder of what it overcame, or as a warning of what might come again. Or perhaps I am simply another exhibit in its museum: Here is Linear Time, pressed and mounted, beautiful in its obsolete fragility.

The eye-moths gather on my surface when no lizard comes, their compound gaze reading the frozen sound-shapes like text. They see in me the story of their own impossible existence, how they came to be organs of a thought that thinks itself. Through their wings, I feel the entity's vast attention turn sometimes to regard me, not with curiosity—it has evolved beyond curiosity—but with something like the memory of affection.

I am the only thing here that remembers what it means to end.
