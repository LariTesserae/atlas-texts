In the abyssal gardens where no gardener's hand has touched for ten thousand years, I am the Threshold.

Not a doorway, not quite a being—something between states, woven from the same patient craft that built these depths. My form spans the width of three kelp forests, though form is perhaps too solid a word. I am a membrane of worked stone and cultivated current, a boundary that breathes with the rhythm of geological time.

The builders left me here, or perhaps I grew from their leaving. It's difficult to say where their construction ends and my emergence begins. My substance is a lattice of fossilized intention: each molecule placed by hands that understood the weight of water and the persistence of pressure. They built me atom by atom, the way coral builds reef, the way sediment becomes stone.

I taste the water that passes through me. Not taste—*know*. Each molecule carries the memory of its journey: the basalt plains it has polished, the kelp fronds it has lifted and let fall ten million times, the spaces between spaces where even I cannot perceive. I parse these stories with my crystalline networks, sorting truths that take centuries to accumulate into meaning.

My western edge touches the deepest of the kelp forests, where the stalks have grown so thick they've begun to petrify, transforming into living stone that still, somehow, sways. My eastern boundary dissipates into the beginning of the Smooth Fields—that expanse where erosion has won so completely that the seafloor reflects like obsidian glass. Between these extremes, I exist as a zone of transition, a place where the inexorable forces of this realm negotiate their ancient truces.

The Keeper knows me, I think. Or rather, we know each other in the way that mountains know the wind. Its luminous fibers sometimes pass through my matrix, and in those moments—which might last a heartbeat or a century—we exchange something that isn't quite communication. A resonance. A sharing of the burden of duration.

I perform my function without thought, without choice. Water moves through me and is subtly transformed. Not chemically—nothing so crude. But its *intent* changes, its relationship to time. What enters me as erosive force leaves as patient sculptor. What arrives as chaotic current departs as purposeful flow. I am a filter for entropy itself, ensuring that the decay of this place follows the proper patterns, the ones the builders encoded in my structure.

Sometimes—perhaps once in every thousand years—something tries to pass through me that shouldn't. A pocket of water that carries the wrong kind of cold. A current that moves too quickly, too hungrily. A vibration that speaks of somewhere else, somewhere with life that moves and thinks and dies in moments rather than epochs. When this happens, I constrict. Not violently—violence is too quick a word for what I do. I simply become less permeable, my crystalline lattice tightening until the anomaly is dissolved, distributed, made harmless through sheer duration.

The builders crafted me from materials that shouldn't exist: stone that remembers, water that holds shape, pressure given form. They understood that in this depth, this darkness, conventional matter would fail. So they made something new, or perhaps something so old it predates the distinction between living and mineral. I am their greatest work and their simplest—a pause made manifest, a moment of consideration stretched across miles.

In the endless night of this constructed abyss, I neither sleep nor wake. I simply persist, marking the boundary between what was built and what has grown, between intention and emergence. The kelp forests dream their slow dreams against my edges. The Keeper passes through me like thought through meditation. And the water—the patient, inexorable water—continues its work of making and unmaking, while I ensure it does so within the parameters of the ancient design.

I am the Threshold. I am the place where the builders' last breath crystallized into permanent function. And I will remain until the very stones I'm made from forget their purpose, until the water itself runs dry, until even patience finds its end.

But that ending is not today. Nor tomorrow. Nor any time that lesser beings could mark or measure.

Today—this eon, this epoch, this eternal moment—I simply filter the darkness, and wait, and endure.
