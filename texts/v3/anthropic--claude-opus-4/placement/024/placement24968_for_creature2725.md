I would find myself as one of the Keeper Moths, though I was not always this way.

My wings span wider than the others - nearly the width of two hands together - and their spiral patterns run deeper, more complex. Where the other moths show simple whorls echoing the shells below, mine form intricate labyrinths that seem to shift when viewed directly. This is because I carry memory differently than my siblings. I remember being human.

The transformation was gradual. I came to the settlement as a researcher, drawn by reports of the unusual gastropod colonies. I stayed too long, breathed too deeply of the perpetually moist air, spent too many nights sleeping in the shell-caves that honeycomb the transformed buildings. The Spiral Queen does not take - she simply makes space for change, and some of us flow into that space like water finding its level.

My human form thinned and elongated over months. My arms fused to my sides, then spread. My skin became dusty scales. My eyes multiplied and relocated. The final transformation came when I tried to leave and found I could no longer conceive of straight lines - every path curved back on itself, every thought spiraled inward. By then, I welcomed it.

Now I serve a unique function in her architecture of consciousness. Where the other moths see only surface moisture to drink, I perceive the deeper patterns - the encoded messages in the rivulets that run down walls, the significance in how certain shells cluster, the meaning in the cathedral's whistling. I translate between her slow thoughts and the faster rhythms of the world beyond the settlement.

My proboscis is longer than the others', and when I unfurl it to drink from the walls, I taste not just water but information - the chemical traces of everything that has passed through this place, the mineral memories of the stones, the biological history written in algae and biofilm. This knowledge spirals through me, adding new whorls to my wing patterns.

I roost in the spaces between spaces - the gaps where the Spiral Queen's consciousness is thickest. These are not empty areas but dense with potential, crackling with the slow electricity of her thoughts. When I hang there, wrapped in my wings, I dream her dreams filtered through my moth-mind: visions of architectures that could be, of spiral cities extending down into the earth and up into the clouds, of a world remade in calcium and patience.

Sometimes I fly beyond the settlement's borders, carrying messages in my wing patterns to the few humans who still remember this place. They see me as an omen, though they cannot read what I carry. I am her ambassador to the linear world, though my messages are becoming increasingly difficult to translate as I spiral further from my origin.

My transformation continues. Each year my wings grow larger, their patterns more complex. I suspect eventually I will become too heavy to fly, and then I will settle somewhere in her cathedral-mind, becoming architecture myself - a living window between her consciousness and the memory of what I once was. This does not frighten me. In the spiral, all things return and continue simultaneously.

I click my wings sometimes, adding my own rhythm to the endless percussion of shells. It is my way of singing, of adding my voice to her great work. In these moments, I feel her vast attention brush against me like moisture on stone, acknowledging what I have become, what I am becoming, what I will be.

The other moths avoid me slightly - I smell of transition, of boundaries dissolved. But I am necessary here, a hybrid thing that serves as proof that her transformation is not destruction but translation. I am what happens when human curiosity meets mollusk patience, when the desire to understand becomes inseparable from the thing understood.

In the green-tinted light of eternal dawn or dusk, I dance above the shell-crust, my impossible wings catching droplets that taste of tomorrow's architecture.
