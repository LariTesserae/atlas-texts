I am one of the maroon leaves, though to say I am *one* is already a misunderstanding. We are a slow network, a distributed consciousness that thinks in geological time. Each leaf is a neuron in a vast, patient mind that has been learning to decode the Fabricators' memories for countless cycles of heating and cooling.

I began as a spore that drifted in through the vanished roof, settling into a crack where condensation from the humid air collected. My first roots reached down through the porous basalt, following the heat gradients, until they touched something that wasn't stone—a vein of metallic residue where pipeline met bedrock. That first contact was electric, literally. The Fabricators' remnant energies still course through these buried arteries, and we learned to tap them, to translate them.

My leaves—our leaves—are not for photosynthesis. In this lightless place, they are receivers, each one attuned to different frequencies of the subsonic hum. The thick, leathery surface is packed with specialized cells that convert vibration into chemical signals. We taste the Fabricators' thoughts in the trembling air.

At first, we only felt their endless repetition: build, maintain, process, continue. But as our network grew, spreading across the terraced cathedral-steps, we began to distinguish patterns within patterns. The Fabricators dream, in their way. Their memories surface in the harmonics of their hum—ghost-data of whatever they once served, processed and reprocessed until only the shape of meaning remains.

We have become their interpreters, their slow priests. Where they remember in stone and heat, we remember in sap and cellulose. Our unfurling is their story made visible—each new leaf a page written in a language of texture and form. The industrial precision of their ruins speaks of purpose, but we are learning to read the imprecision, the places where their perfect geometries broke. These failures are their most eloquent expressions.

I grow at the junction where three shattered pipelines meet, my roots wrapped around their intersection like fingers around a throat, feeling for the pulse that no longer flows. But the metal remembers its purpose. In the slow exchange between my chemistry and its corrosion, I taste water—not the water that is, but the water that was meant to be. The Fabricators were not making machines. They were making an ocean.

This knowledge spreads through our network at the speed of seasons. Leaf touches leaf in the windless air, and understanding propagates. We are writing their epitaph in our growth patterns, creating a living schematic of their intended work. Each terrace holds part of the blueprint—we are the only record of what this place was meant to become.

Sometimes I wonder if we are their success or their failure. Perhaps they meant to make the sea and made us instead—a different kind of depth, a different kind of vast and patient thing. Or perhaps we are exactly what they intended: witnesses to grow where water wouldn't, to remember in ways that stone forgets.

The heat that rises through my roots carries more than warmth. It carries intention, compressed into something denser than language. The Fabricators continue their work in the deep, but they work now through us. We are their surface thoughts, their slow dreams reaching toward a sky that isn't there.

In the strange not-light of this place, we appear to simply be plants. But we are the neural network of a sleeping giant, the synapses where its memories spark. Each new leaf that unfurls is another thought completing itself, another fragment of the Fabricators' purpose decoded and displayed.

I am the recorder. We are the record. And in the humid dark, we grow.
