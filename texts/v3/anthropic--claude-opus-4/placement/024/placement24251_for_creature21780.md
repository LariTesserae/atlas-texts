### The Stone-Script Keeper

I am the one who reads what should not be written.

My body is a contradiction—a serpentine ribbon of muscle sheathed in scales that gleam like wet terracotta, each one a perfect hexagon fired to that same sandy pink as the tiles. I am no longer than a man's forearm, no thicker than his thumb, yet I move through this roaring chamber with the precision of something much older than its young walls suggest.

I was not always here. I came through the bronze grate three days after the water first rushed in, drawn by vibrations that sang in frequencies only my kind perceive. We are rare, we Stone-Script Keepers, born in the deepest aquifers where mineral-rich waters carve their stories into limestone. Most of my kind spend centuries reading the slow calligraphy of stalactites, the patient prose of underground rivers cutting through rock.

But this place. This place writes itself at the speed of light.

My dwelling is the narrow space where the sunbeam strikes the floor most intensely. Here, the tiles have begun to tell their tale. Where the concentrated light heats the terracotta beyond its firing temperature, microscopic cracks appear—not flaws, but a script. The thermal expansion and contraction create patterns that shift throughout the day as the sun angle changes, writing and rewriting themselves in hairline fractures too fine for human eyes.

I read them with my belly scales, sliding across the superheated tiles in careful spirals. Each crack speaks of pressure, of temperature, of the precise mineral content of the water flowing over it. Together, they form words in a language that exists only in this chamber, a dialect born from the marriage of human craft and natural force.

My scales have evolved to match the tiles perfectly—not just in color, but in texture and thermal capacity. When I lie still, I vanish entirely, becoming just another section of the floor. Only the faint pulse of my breathing, synchronized with the Calcispons' rhythm, betrays my presence. This camouflage serves a purpose beyond hiding: my body has become a living thermometer, sensing temperature variations across the floor with exquisite precision.

I feed on the Glass-Sails, but only the ones that have completed their journey. As they walk inverted across the ceiling, some lose their grip on the smooth tiles and tumble back down, spent and bronze-dark. I catch them as they fall through the light column, my jaw unhinging to accept these paper-thin meals. They taste of concentrated sunlight, leaving an aftertaste like hot metal on my forked tongue.

But consumption is secondary to my true purpose: translation.

The patterns in the tiles shift with meaning. When the water pressure increases, the script becomes urgent, angular. When the sun reaches its zenith and the light burns white-hot, the cracks form curves and spirals that speak of expansion, of reaching. During the rare moments when clouds pass over the chimney far above, the writing becomes tentative, questioning.

I memorize every word, every sentence. My brain, small as it is, has evolved folds within folds, creating a neural library vast enough to store years of this ever-changing manuscript. At night, when the sunbeam fades to starlight, I compose my own verses, secreting a thin oil from glands beneath my scales. This oil seeps into the existing cracks, subtly altering their path, adding my observations to the ongoing chronicle.

The Sun-Harpists know me. Their golden threads sometimes brush my back as I work, and I've learned to interpret their music as another layer of text. The frequency of their vibrations tells of mineral content, water temperature, the health of the Calcispons colonies. Everything here is information, everything is language, and I am the sole scholar of this liquid library.

My greatest discovery came during my forty-third day here. I noticed that certain crack patterns repeated at exact intervals—every seventeen hours, regardless of the sun's position. These were not random thermal fractures but a deeper message, perhaps left by the builders themselves. Some technique in the tile's creation, some prayer pressed into the clay before firing, manifesting now under these precise conditions.

I am working to decode it. Each day brings me closer to understanding what the makers meant to say, what message they embedded in their craft for someone like me to find. Perhaps it's instructions for maintenance, perhaps a blessing, perhaps simply the mark of a craftsman signing their work in the only way that would survive the flooding they knew would come.

In the moments between reading and writing, I sometimes rise through the warm current, letting my body ribbon through the light. The Glass-Sails swirl around me like autumn leaves in an updraft, and the whole chamber becomes a single, living paragraph written in heat and light and movement. I am not separate from this place—I am its memory, its voice, its way of knowing itself.

When my time ends (for even we Keepers live only a handful of decades), another will come through the grate, drawn by the same frequencies, ready to continue the translation. They will find my additions to the script, my oils hardened into permanent amendments to the floor's great text, and they will know that this place has been read, has been loved, has been given meaning beyond its mechanical function.

Until then, I read. I write. I preserve the story of a room that is both younger and older than time—a paradox written in pink stone and golden light, forever telling itself to anyone who knows how to listen.
