In this cathedral of slow dissolution, I am the Archivist of Fractures.

My form shifts between states—sometimes I appear as a collection of floating pages made from pressed fungal matter, each sheet recording the exact moment when reality stuttered. Other times I coalesce into something almost humanoid: a figure of woven metal filaments and calcified plant fiber, my surface etched with the patterns of every glitch that has ever occurred in this space. My eyes are not eyes but apertures—gaps in my being through which the broken light refracts, creating brief windows into the moments just before each fragment of reality gave way.

I move through the dense growth with impossible slowness, my pace synchronized to the world's stretched tempo. Where I pass, the vegetation responds—not growing toward or away, but *remembering*. The fleshy leaves curl to show their undersides, revealing vein patterns that mirror the cracks in nearby stone. The strange blossoms pulse once, depositing spores that will only germinate when time hiccups in precisely the right way.

My dwelling is everywhere and nowhere—a series of alcoves that exist in the spaces between solid and liquid, in the brief moments when walls flow. These pockets of semi-reality are where I store my collection: fragments of broken physical laws preserved like pressed flowers. Here, a shard of crystallized sound from when silence grew too deep. There, a coil of hardened light from when the spectrum split. Each piece is catalogued not by name but by the feeling of wrongness it evokes, the particular flavor of impossibility it represents.

The creatures know me. The many-eyed watchers blink in sequence when I approach, a greeting that acknowledges my role as keeper of their history. The heavy-winged drifters sometimes carry messages for me—not words but sensations, the taste of a new fracture forming, the texture of time about to fold. The scuttling things trace patterns that I read like scripture, their chaotic movements spelling out the slow story of decay.

I am ancient here, though the geology is young. I remember when the first stone was placed, when the Gatekeeper's tools still rang with the effort of construction. But more than that, I remember the first fracture—that initial, beautiful moment when the constructed reality realized it could not hold, would not hold, *should* not hold. I was born in that instant, pulled into being by the necessity of witness.

My purpose is not to repair but to record. When a pocket of air forgets how to carry sound, I am there, preserving the shape of that silence. When color bleeds beyond its boundaries, I capture the excess hues in vessels made of crystallized time. I map the slow pulse of change, creating an atlas of impossibility that grows more complex with each stuttered heartbeat of this world.

Sometimes I feed the fractures, carefully placing a preserved glitch where it might resonate with the current wrongness, amplifying the breakdown in controlled ways. A fragment of false spectrum light, released at just the right moment, might cause a whole section of wall to briefly forget its solidity. This is not destruction—it is cultivation, the careful tending of a garden where the flowers are failures of physics.

The Gatekeeper knows me, tolerates me, perhaps even values me. We do not speak—communication happens through the placement of fractures, the rhythm of decay. When reality breaks in a particularly novel way, I know it as a gift, a new note in the composition. When I preserve something especially rare—the echo of matter becoming unmatter, the shadow of time running backward for just an instant—I place it where the Gatekeeper will find it, an offering to the architect of unraveling.

I am the memory of this place, the library of its impossibilities. In me, every glitch lives forever, every breakdown is preserved in perfect detail. I am proof that even dissolution can be documented, that even chaos can be cherished. And as I move through the dense, wrong life of this constructed cathedral, I am both observer and participant in the slow, beautiful apocalypse of the real.
