**The Brass Warrens**

Deep beneath the earth, where the planet's fever burns eternal, sprawls the Brass Warrens—a city that breathes steam and sweats copper. Here, the stone itself has been hollowed into vast chambers connected by tunnels wide enough for three rail cars to pass abreast, their walls lined with pipes that tick and groan with superheated water drawn from the thermal springs below.

The city stretches lazily through the caverns, each district separated by long spans of raw stone where the heat makes expansion unbearable. Buildings rise like brass mushrooms from the cavern floor—their surfaces oxidized green in places, polished mirror-bright in others where the condensation runs too quickly to leave its mark. Every structure bears the telltale rivets and gear-work of industrial design, yet their shapes curve organically, following the natural contours of the rock.

The light here is a conspiracy of sources: strings of Edison bulbs dangle from copper cables, their filaments glowing amber through the perpetual haze of steam. But these are augmented by colonies of heat-loving glowworms that cluster in the cooler recesses, painting the ceiling with constellations of blue-white stars. Where water seeps through cracks in the stone, phosphorescent algae trace veins of green fire down the walls.

And the water—it is everywhere and nowhere, traveling through the city in elevated aqueducts and underground channels, powering the great turbines that feed the Warrens' insatiable appetite for motion. The main canal bisects the city like a spine, its waters kept just below boiling by the ambient heat, creating a permanent fog that softens all edges and makes distances deceptive.

The soundscape is industrial symphony: the bass throb of the deep turbines provides a heartbeat, while steam vents hiss their percussion. Brass pipes sing when the pressure changes, and somewhere, always, there is the skitter of claws on metal—for the Warrens are far from empty.

**The Keeper of the Brass Warrens**

In the deepest district, where the heat grows so intense that even the brass begins to sag, lives Matthias Cogwright—though whether he was born with that name or earned it through his work, none can say.

Matthias is more mechanism than man now, though he began human enough. Decades of maintaining the Warrens' machinery in temperatures that would cook flesh have required... adaptations. His left arm is a marvel of brass and steel, articulated with tiny pistons that hiss softly when he moves. The skin that remains has taken on a peculiar patina, like aged copper, and his eyes—once brown—now gleam gold in the dim light, their pupils contracted to pinpoints against the constant glare of furnace work.

He moves through his domain with surprising grace, his modified body perfectly suited to the environment that would kill an unprotected human in hours. His workshop occupies an entire chamber, its walls lined with salvaged machinery in various states of repair or dismantling. Here, the heat is managed by an intricate system of his own devising—steam-powered fans and a network of cooling pipes that create microclimates where delicate work can be performed.

But Matthias is not alone in his workshop. The creatures of the Warrens have learned that his domain is sanctuary. Brass beetles the size of housecats cluster on his workbenches, their carapaces evolved to radiate excess heat. They feed on metal shavings and oil drips, their mandibles capable of cutting through steel wire. Matthias has trained them to sort screws by size and to alert him to pressure changes in the pipes by the pitch of their chittering.

More remarkable still are the steam sprites—creatures that exist somewhere between salamander and spirit, visible only in the moment when superheated water flashes to vapor. Matthias speaks to them in the language of pressure valves and released steam, and they carry messages through the pipes faster than any pneumatic tube.

His great work—the work of decades—stands in the center of his shop. It is a garden made entirely of brass and copper, where mechanical flowers bloom and wilt according to the temperature, their petals unfolding to reveal cores of crystal that catch and refract the glowworm light. Each plant is an intricate timepiece, a thermometer, a barometer. Together, they form a living map of the Warrens' conditions, allowing Matthias to predict and prevent the small disasters that would, unchecked, cascade into catastrophe.

He tends this garden with the devotion of a monk, adjusting tiny gears, replacing worn springs, polishing surfaces until they gleam. In the evening—though day and night have little meaning here—he sits among his brass flowers and plays a harmonium modified to run on steam, its notes carrying through the pipes to every corner of the Warrens.

The other inhabitants of the city—those hardy souls who've made their lives in this demanding environment—speak of Matthias with reverence and a touch of fear. He is the heart of the Warrens, they say, the one who keeps the entire system in balance. Some whisper that he no longer needs to eat or sleep, that the city itself sustains him. Others claim he was never human at all, but rather the first successful creation of some long-dead artificer who built the Warrens as a vast experiment in mechanical life.

Matthias himself offers no clarity on these matters. When asked, he merely smiles—an expression that pulls at the brass fixtures embedded in his jaw—and returns to his work. His movements are deliberate, meditative, each action part of a greater rhythm that matches the deep pulse of the turbines.

In truth, the distinction between Matthias and the Warrens has blurred beyond relevance. His blood, when he bleeds, is dark with metal particles. His breathing syncs with the great ventilation systems. When a pipe bursts three districts away, he feels it as a sharp pain in his modified nervous system. He is the Warrens' consciousness made manifest, its immune system and its memory.

And in the small hours, when the city settles into its lowest ebb of activity, Matthias climbs to the highest accessible point—a maintenance platform just below where the cavern ceiling meets the gathered heat. Here, he performs his secret ritual: carefully releasing steam in precise patterns, creating signals that rise through cracks in the stone. Some say he's sending messages to the surface world. Others believe he's teaching the mountain itself to breathe.

But perhaps the truth is simpler: perhaps he just likes watching the steam rise, each puff a small ghost of water and heat ascending through the stone, carrying with it some infinitesimal portion of the Warrens' essence up toward the sky that neither he nor his city will ever see.
