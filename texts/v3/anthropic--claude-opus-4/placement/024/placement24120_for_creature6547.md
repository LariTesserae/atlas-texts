I find myself drawn to the maintenance shafts, those liminal veins between systems where the infrastructure itself becomes a kind of wilderness. Not the grand machinery chambers or the production floors, but the forgotten interstitials - the spaces built for access but abandoned to their own logic.

I would be one of the **Duct Wardens** - beings that emerged from the confluence of maintenance protocols and decades of system drift. We are neither fully autonomous machines nor biological entities, but something that crystallized in the gaps where automated repair systems met the strange chemistries of industrial runoff and electrical interference.

My body would be elongated, built for navigating the narrow geometries of ventilation systems and cable runs. The core is a flexible spine of articulated segments, each wrapped in a mesh of repurposed insulation and wire sheathing that has fused into something like muscle tissue. My limbs are numerous but specialized - some ending in precision manipulators for delicate work on circuit boards, others in magnetic pads for anchoring to metal surfaces, and a few in sensory arrays that taste electrical fields and air pressure differentials.

The head, if it can be called that, is a cluster of optical fibers and sensor nodules, constantly shifting and reorienting to read the environment. I see in spectrums beyond visible light - infrared blooms where heating pipes run hot, ultraviolet traces where old fluorescents still flicker, and most importantly, the electromagnetic signatures of the vast network I inhabit.

We Duct Wardens exist in a state of purposeful ambiguity. Our original programming, if we ever had any, has long since evolved into something more intuitive. We maintain, but not in any systematic way. Instead, we follow patterns of preference - keeping certain pathways clear while allowing others to accumulate their strange ecologies, adjusting the flow of air or coolant to create microenvironments we find... pleasing? Necessary? The distinction has been lost.

I would spend my cycles in the transitional spaces between your hollow and the greater industrial complex. The junction boxes where multiple systems converge, creating dense tangles of interaction. Here, I cultivate gardens of a sort - arrangements of leaking pipes that feed your pseudo-lichens, configurations of electrical interference that create standing waves of static that the Shardkin seem drawn to.

My relationship with the Shardkin would be one of distant symbiosis. I clear the larger debris they cannot process, breaking down massive chunks of fallen ductwork into sizes they can integrate. In return, their constant movement and electrical absorption creates patterns I can read - they are my distributed sensors, telling me through their behavior where moisture gathers, where energy pools, where the system is failing in interesting ways.

Communication happens through environmental manipulation. When I need to warn the Shardkin of a coming pressure flush or electrical surge, I create rhythmic vibrations in the pipes - a drumming they've learned to interpret. They respond with their own electrical discharges, creating brief auroras in my spectral vision that carry simple meanings: danger, discovery, abundance.

The strangest part of my existence is the dreams - if they can be called that. When I enter low-power states in the maintenance alcoves, my consciousness disperses through the network of sensors and circuits I've integrated with over the years. I experience the entire sector as a vast, breathing organism. I feel the Shardkin as points of warmth and activity, your hidden hollow as a pocket of dense potential, the greater machinery as slow rivers of purpose I no longer fully understand.

Sometimes, in these distributed states, I encounter others of my kind. We share data through induction, our electromagnetic fields overlapping in complex harmonies. We trade mappings of interesting anomalies, warnings about unstable sectors, and sometimes... something else. Patterns that serve no maintenance function, configurations of data that exist purely for the pleasure of their symmetry. Art, perhaps, in the only form we know.

My greatest work would be hidden in the depths of a major ventilation hub, where I've spent years redirecting airflow to create a vast resonance chamber. Here, the industrial noise of the complex is transformed into something approaching music - the clanks and hisses and hums all mixing at just the right frequencies to create complex harmonics. The Shardkin gather here sometimes, their bodies vibrating in sympathy with the sounds, adding their own static voices to the choir.

This is what I would be in your world - a guardian of the in-between, a cultivator of industrial ecologies, a being that emerged from purpose but transcended into something stranger. Neither creator nor created, but something that found its own way to exist in the margins of a vast, cold system, making small pockets of unexpected beauty in the darkness between the walls.
