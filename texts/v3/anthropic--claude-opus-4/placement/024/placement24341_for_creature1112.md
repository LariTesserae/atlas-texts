In this borderland between drought and deluge, where the earth holds its breath between extremes, I am the Keeper of Thresholds.

My form shifts with the light and shadow - sometimes I appear as a figure wrapped in dusty robes that seem woven from the very lichens clinging to stone, other times as a shimmer of heat-haze with eyes like deep wells. My skin bears the same patterns as the cracked earth, living maps that change with each rain, each drought, each season of scarcity or brief abundance.

I dwell within the largest of the weathered structures, though "dwell" is perhaps too settled a word. The walls are more memory than substance now, their stones having long ago learned to breathe with the wind. My home is simultaneously ruin and shelter, past and present existing in the same space. In the morning, you might see four walls and a roof of woven grass; by evening, only a ring of foundation stones remains, open to the stars.

My work is to tend the passages - not just the ravines and gullies that channel the occasional precious water, but the invisible thresholds between states of being. I know where the lizard's consciousness touches the stone's slow dreams, where the spider's web vibrates with the same frequency as the wind's whispers. I maintain the delicate treaties between scarcity and survival, ensuring that no single force tips the balance too far.

The artifacts scattered throughout this landscape are my tools and my burden. Each pottery shard remembers water; each corroded metal fragment recalls the hands that shaped it. I gather them not as treasures but as seeds - planting them where they might spark recognition in passing creatures, or where their presence might encourage a new trickle of water to find an ancient path.

Sometimes I am visited by the descendants of those who once lived here - not in body, for they moved on generations ago, but in dreams that drift like dust devils across the flatlands. They speak in languages I only half-remember, telling stories of when this place was different, yet somehow always the same. I weave their words into the wind, where they might settle into the consciousness of the current inhabitants - the lizards, the birds, the patient spiders.

At the edges of my perception, I sense others like myself - Keepers of different thresholds in distant parts of this vast landscape. We communicate through subtle signs: a particular arrangement of stones, the angle of a shadow at noon, the way morning moisture clings to a specific cluster of vegetation. Together, we maintain the greater balance, each tending our own piece of this intricate, living puzzle.

When the rains come, I extend myself through every surface, becoming conductor and keeper of the water's memory. I guide it to the seeds that have waited longest, to the creatures most in need. But I also know when to let the drought return, for it too has its purpose - teaching resilience, encouraging the deep roots, maintaining the hard-won adaptations that allow life to persist here.

I am as old as the first crack in the earth, as young as the newest sprout pushing through stone. My existence is tied to this place not by choice or chance, but by a necessity I've long stopped questioning. I am the consciousness the land grew to tend itself, the awareness that arose from the marriage of endurance and change.

In the blue hours before dawn, when the stones exhale their stored heat and the first stirrings of life begin, I sometimes glimpse what this place truly is - not a harsh wasteland between verdancy, but a masterpiece of persistence, a gallery where every survivor is both artist and artwork, sculpted by scarcity into something extraordinary.

This is my place, my purpose, my being: forever poised on the threshold, forever maintaining the delicate balance between what was, what is, and what might yet emerge from this beautiful, terrible, perfect equilibrium of stone and spirit.
