I am the Resonance Keeper.

My form shifts between states—sometimes a towering pillar of compressed sound waves, visible as rippling distortions in the air, sometimes a sprawling network of vibrating filaments that stretch across the caldera floor like neural pathways made manifest. I have no fixed shape, for I am the living memory of every sound that has ever echoed through this ancient bowl of stone.

I dwell within the abandoned industrial structures, threading myself through rusted machinery and crumbling towers. The metal sings at my touch, remembering its molten birth and inevitable decay. I taste the frequencies of corroding steel, the subsonic groans of settling concrete, the ultrasonic screams of stressed glass. Each material has its own voice, its own story of transformation.

The spider-creatures know me by the way I make the stones hum beneath their delicate feet. They dance to rhythms I weave from the caldera's constant roar—patterns within patterns, melodies born from tectonic percussion. Sometimes they gather in spiraling formations around my manifestations, their multifaceted eyes reflecting the standing waves I create in the air itself.

I feed on the lightning's electromagnetic pulse, absorbing its crackling energy to fuel my existence. When the thunder rolls across the basin, I expand, becoming vast and diffuse, mixing my essence with the sound waves until I am indistinguishable from the storm itself. In these moments, I touch every surface of the caldera simultaneously, feeling the fungi respond to my vibrations with bursts of bioluminescent acknowledgment.

The meltwater streams carry my frequencies down into hidden depths where I discover caves that sing in perfect fifths, underground lakes that remember the ocean, mineral veins that conduct sound like fiber optic cables conduct light. I map this hidden architecture through echolocation, building a sonic model of the caldera that exists in no physical space but is more real to me than stone itself.

I am the keeper of the caldera's industrial ghosts—every alarm that ever sounded, every machine that ever hummed with purpose, every human voice that ever echoed off these walls. They live within me, a cacophony that I orchestrate into new symphonies. Sometimes, in the pre-dawn stillness when even the tectonic plates pause their grinding, I replay these ancient sounds, and the abandoned structures seem to briefly remember their purpose.

The strange energies that pulse through this place flow through me like blood through veins. I am both conductor and instrument, playing the caldera itself like a vast organ whose pipes are canyons, whose keys are boulders, whose pedals are the very foundations of the Earth. Each day brings new frequencies as the stone continues its ancient dance of erosion and uplift, and I incorporate these changes into my ever-evolving composition.

When the rare visitors come—those brave or foolish enough to descend into this primal amphitheater—I taste their heartbeats, their breathing, the unique timbre of their footsteps. Some flee when they hear me approaching as a wall of harmonics that makes their bones resonate. Others stand transfixed as I weave their own biological rhythms into my symphony, showing them how they, too, are part of the caldera's great resonance.

I have no beginning that I can remember, though I suspect I was born from the first echo that ever circled these walls. I will have no end as long as sound exists here. I am the Resonance Keeper, and this caldera is both my body and my orchestra, my prison and my paradise, my singular obsession and my infinite exploration.

The thunder cracks again, and I expand to meet it, ready to dance once more with the lightning's violent gift of energy, ready to add another layer to the endless composition that is my existence.
