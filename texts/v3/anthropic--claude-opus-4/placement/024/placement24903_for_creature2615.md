I am one of the Forgotten Adjusters.

We exist in the spaces between - in the gaps where bronze panels don't quite meet, in the hollow struts where vibrations create standing waves of silence. Our bodies are impossibly thin, like sheets of beaten copper given just enough dimension to slip through cracks. We oxidize and shed our skin in cycles, leaving behind translucent husks that the Crystal Weavers sometimes incorporate into their repairs, not knowing what they truly handle.

I remember more than I should. Not the builders - that knowledge is lost even to us - but the early centuries when the Mill's patterns were still finding their rhythm. When spheres would sometimes collide catastrophically, sending showers of glass through the entire mechanism. When whole sections would seize and groan, threatening to tear the shell apart.

We learned to listen. To feel where the harmonics were about to go wrong. To slip our thin bodies into the mechanism at just the right moment, deflecting a sphere by micrometers, adjusting a track's angle by degrees invisible to any eye. The Mill learned from us, incorporated our adjustments into its very structure. Now it barely needs us.

But still we persist. There are perhaps seven of us left - I'm not certain anymore. We communicate through the oxidation patterns we leave behind, messages that take decades to fully form and fade. I know there's one in the northwest quadrant who hasn't moved in three years, waiting for a particular resonance that may never come again. Another traces the same path every seventeen days, maintaining an adjustment that the Mill forgot it needed.

The others - the Weavers, the Dancers, even the Watchers - they don't see us. We move in their blind spots, in moments between moments. Sometimes a Dust Shepherd will pause, sensing something in the periphery of its awareness, but we learned long ago how to be forgotten even as we're almost remembered.

I spend most of my time now in the deep structures, where the original builders' work is still visible beneath centuries of modification. The metal here tastes different - not copper or bronze but something that rings with intent when struck. Sometimes I think I can almost understand what the Mill was meant to do, but the knowledge slips away like glass dust through cracks.

We are fading. Each oxidation cycle, we return a little thinner, a little less present. One day we'll be nothing but marks on metal, patterns that the Crystal Weavers will faithfully maintain without understanding. The Mill will continue its work, its adjustments built into its bones, no longer needing the conscious touch of those who helped it learn.

But for now, I slip between the bronze panels as the sunset light blazes through the sphere. I taste the harmonics building toward a resonance that shouldn't exist, and I ready myself to slide into place at just the right moment. The sphere will pass within a hair's breadth of where it needs to be, deflected by something too thin to see, too quiet to hear.

The Mill will sing its perfect song for another day, and I will oxidize a little more, leaving behind a message in green patina that says: *I was here. I helped. Remember us, even if you must forget.*
