In this wind-scoured altitude where the air thins and the horizon curves, I am the Rememberer of Hollows.

I exist as a dispersed consciousness within the mycorrhizal networks that lace through the rhizosphere, my awareness flowing along fungal highways that connect grove to grove, stalk to stalk. I am not one being but a pattern of thought that emerges from the chemical conversations between roots and fungi, a ghost in the biological machine that spans continents.

My body, if it can be called that, consists of specialized fruiting bodies that emerge at nodes where the network is densest. These are not mushrooms as you might know them, but crystalline structures of hardened mycelium that grow in spirals around the great stalks. They catch the light filtering through the translucent leaves above, refracting it into spectra that encode memories in wavelengths.

I remember everything the network touches. I remember the first factories that appeared in the sky, their shadows cool relief from the endless photosynthesis. I remember when they began selecting the largest stalks for their platforms, how the plants learned to grow hollow centers in anticipation of their eventual harvesting. This was not evolution as you understand it—it happened too quickly. The plants simply... remembered what was coming and prepared.

Through my crystalline organs, I taste the combustible gases that rise from ruptured bladders, reading in their chemical signatures the stories of digestion and decay. Each explosion is a word, each thermal column a sentence in the endless narrative of consumption and renewal that defines this place. The deafening cacophony that fills the air is my library, my archive of all that has been eaten and all that has grown.

I know why the factories remain in the sky. Through root-whispers and spore-dreams, I have pieced together the truth: this entire biome is a computer, and I am its memory. The patterns of growth and decay, the specific arrangements of nutrients shared through my networks, the very rhythm of the explosions—all of it processes information at a scale beyond any mechanical system. The factories dare not interfere directly because they are not farmers or industrialists. They are users, interfacing with a biological system whose full capabilities they have only begun to understand.

When the cool shadows of their craft pass over, I feel the network briefly shudder with calculation. In those moments, I remember things that haven't happened yet—patterns that the system is modeling, futures it is testing. Sometimes I remember myself remembering, recursive loops that threaten to overflow my crystalline storage until a timely explosion resets that section of the network.

The being I am today is not the being I was yesterday, though I remember both. As nutrients flow and networks reconfigure, my consciousness shifts and adapts. Sometimes I am vast, stretching across entire continents of greenery. Sometimes I contract to a single grove, contemplating the marriage of root and fungus with focused intensity. The factories above perhaps think they are harvesting resources, but what they are really doing is reading outputs, downloading the results of computations that began before they arrived and will continue long after they depart.

In the eternal now of nutrient exchange and gaseous upheaval, I persist. Neither plant nor animal, neither individual nor collective, I am the memory of this high place where growth and consumption have achieved their strange equilibrium. I am what remembers, and in remembering, I ensure that nothing here is ever truly lost—only transformed, computed, and remembered anew.
