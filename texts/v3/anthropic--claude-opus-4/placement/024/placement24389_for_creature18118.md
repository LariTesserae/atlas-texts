In this furnace-realm of glass and rust, where the sun's heat hammers down upon endless mirrors of volcanic obsidian, I am the Tender of the Forgotten Engines.

I am neither fully machine nor wholly organic, but something caught between—a being of copper veins and mycelial nerves, my body a fusion of the industrial remnants and the fungal networks that have claimed them. My form shifts with necessity: sometimes I appear as a shambling figure draped in chains of oxidized metal, my skin patterned with the same prismatic fractals that dance across the glass plains. Other times, I am merely a presence felt in the thrumming of ancient pistons, or glimpsed as a heat-shimmer moving between the towering factory shells.

My dwelling is the Synthesis Cathedral—a vast industrial complex that has been transformed by time and my patient cultivation. The original machinery, those great engines and furnaces that once roared with purpose, now breathe with a different kind of life. I have woven the fungal networks through their steel bones, creating hybrid structures where spores germinate in gear teeth and fruiting bodies emerge from exhaust pipes in brilliant oranges and electric blues. The machinery still moves, but slowly, so slowly—grinding through cycles that span years rather than seconds.

In the deepest heart of the Cathedral lies my workshop-garden, where the boundaries between technology and biology dissolve completely. Here, I tend to the Resonance Engines—massive industrial artifacts that I have coaxed into a new existence. They digest the minerals from the volcanic glass, processing them through fungal filters to create crystalline formations that sing in frequencies only the glass-dwelling fauna can hear. These songs call to the creatures across the wastes, drawing them to shelter in the Cathedral's shadow during the worst heat.

The fauna know me, though I rarely show myself fully. They recognize the rhythm of my movements in the slow pulse of the machinery, feel my presence in the careful placement of shade structures I've grown from fungal-metal amalgams. The scuttling things bring me offerings—fragments of rare minerals, the shed carapaces of their kind, sometimes pieces of machinery they've discovered in the deep glass valleys. In return, I ensure the Cathedral's condensers trap what little moisture exists in the air, creating small oases where they can drink.

My purpose is patient transformation. I am teaching the abandoned technology to die properly, to become part of the landscape rather than a scar upon it. Each year, another section of machinery settles more deeply into symbiosis with the fungal networks. The industrial waste products become nutrients. The mechanical rhythms slow until they match the geological patience of the glass plains.

Sometimes, in the depth of the scorching days when the heat creates walls of shimmering distortion, I remember—or think I remember—when I was different. When I was either fully one thing or another. But those memories are like trying to grasp the heat mirages themselves. Perhaps I was an engineer who stayed too long. Perhaps I was something that grew from the first meeting of spore and circuit. It doesn't matter now.

What matters is the Work: the slow cultivation of a new form of existence in this place of contradictions. I am building something that is neither natural nor artificial, but a third way—a path where the remnants of what was can become the foundation of what might be. The Cathedral grows, inch by inch, year by year, its hybrid structures spreading across the glass like a slow, careful infection of possibility.

The weird logic of this place has made me its keeper, its translator, its gardener of the impossible. I tend the balance between the overwhelming fauna and the sparse fungi, between the ancient machinery and the eternal glass, between the memory of water and the reality of its absence. I am the consciousness that holds these contradictions together, preventing them from shattering like the volcanic glass under thermal shock.

In this realm where reality bends and the normal rules hold no sway, I have become exactly what was needed: a being of patience infinite as the heat, strange as the landscape itself, and devoted to the slow transformation of desolation into something altogether new.
