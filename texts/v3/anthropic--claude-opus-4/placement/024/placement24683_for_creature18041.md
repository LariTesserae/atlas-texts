I would find myself as a colony of listening lichens, spread thin across the corroded flanks of an abandoned industrial cooling tower. Each of my thousand bodies is no larger than a coin, rust-orange and paper-thin, clinging to the pocked metal surface where condensation runs in perpetual rivulets.

We are not one being but many, yet we share a singular purpose: to transduce the deafening symphony of this pocket world into something else entirely. The constant drip-drip-drip of water, the groan of settling metal, the whisper of wind through broken pipes—all of these vibrations pass through our collective membrane and emerge transformed.

Some might mistake us for mere oxidation, another layer of decay on the industrial ruins. But look closer: see how we pulse gently with each sound wave, how our edges lift microscopically to catch the acoustic currents. We are biological recording devices, archivists of noise, each colony member storing a slightly different frequency range in the crystalline structures within our cells.

At night, when the bioluminescent pool-dwellers cast their wan light upward through the mist, we release our day's collection. It emerges not as sound but as spores—millions of them, each one encoded with a fragment of the acoustic memory we've gathered. They drift on the warm air, settling into the moss beds and fern groves, where they germinate into new colonies that will listen to their own small territories.

The humans who once built these factories could never have imagined such a use for their ruins. They thought in terms of production and output, of efficiency and waste. But we measure success differently. In this intimate, water-drunk world, we are the memory keepers, ensuring that no sound—no matter how small, how seemingly insignificant—passes through this landscape unwitnessed.

Sometimes I wonder if we're creating something larger—if all our scattered colonies, each recording our own small section of this pocket world, are collectively composing something. A map made of sound, perhaps. A symphony of decay. Or simply a patient, eternal documentation of what it means for a built world to slowly return to the earth.

The water continues to fall. The metal continues to sing its slow song of oxidation. And we continue to listen, to transform, to remember.
