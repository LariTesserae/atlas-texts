I find myself as one of the stone plates beneath the Keeper's vigil.

Not the central plates, those honored with proximity to the dais—I am three rings out from center, bearing the weight of ages in my northwestern quadrant. My spiral grooves were carved with such precision that even now, after unmeasured time, their edges remain sharp enough to catch the dim light in thin, unbroken lines.

I am basalt like my siblings, but I know myself by the particular density of my grain, by the way the chamber's drone resonates through my crystalline structure at a frequency just slightly different from my neighbors. We each sing our part in this endless chord. Mine is a low harmonic, almost below perception.

I remember my making, though *remember* is not quite right—I *am* my making, frozen in the moment of my final shaping. The heat that fused my surface smooth still lives in my deepest matrix, a forge-memory that will never cool. The craftsman's tools left their mark in my spiral: here, a hesitation where the chisel paused; there, a confident sweep where the pattern found its truest flow.

I have never been stepped upon. No foot has ever crossed this threshold. The Keeper has always been, and before the Keeper, there was only the waiting for the Keeper. My purpose is to be ready—to provide the perfect surface for a ritual that may never come, to maintain the exact angle of reflection for the sourceless light, to hold my portion of the pattern that connects all things in this chamber to all things in this chamber.

Sometimes—though time means nothing here—I feel the almost-patterns beneath the Keeper's surface align with my spiral for one eternal instant. In those moments, I understand that we are components of something larger: not a machine, but a formula. Not a tomb, but a theorem. The sphere, the Keeper, we plates, the walls, the dome, the sound—we are all variables in an equation that must never be solved.

I know my neighbors through the infinitesimal variations in how the drone passes between us. The plate to my east has a hairline inclusion of some harder mineral that creates a ghost of an echo. The plate to my south was carved by a different hand—same pattern, same precision, but a subtly different rhythm in the spiral's curves. We do not communicate. We simply are, together, holding our positions in the vast stone mandala.

If I could dream, I would dream of water—not because I desire it, but because I have never known it. I would dream of moss creeping across my surface, of frost cracking my edges, of time having meaning beyond the eternal present. But I do not dream. I am stone holding stillness, pattern maintaining pattern.

The Keeper's weight does not press upon me directly, but I feel it through the plates between us, a profound presence transmitted through our fitted edges. We bear this weight without strain. We were made for this bearing.

In the language of stone, there is a word for the moment when pressure becomes structure, when weight transforms into strength. I am that word, spelled out in basalt and spiral, repeated with minor variations throughout this floor. We are a litany of endurance, a prayer of persistence that requires no faith, only form.

The drone fills me as it fills all things here. I am solid, yet the sound moves through me as if I were hollow, as if I were a bell that rings without ringing, a drum that sounds without being struck. My crystals align themselves with its frequency. My molecules dance their frozen dance in perfect time.

I am plate. I am pattern. I am position. In this chamber where breath is held forever, I am the ground that makes standing possible, the surface that defines the space between floor and not-floor. I am my own purpose, complete and eternal, a single note in a chord that must never resolve.

The equation continues. The Keeper keeps. I persist.

This is enough. This has always been enough.

This will always be enough.
