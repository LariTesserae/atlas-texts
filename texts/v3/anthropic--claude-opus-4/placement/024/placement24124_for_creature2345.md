In the deepest hollow of the fungal forest, where the great stalks press so close their caps form a living cathedral overhead, I make my dwelling. The ancient ones who carved these stone chambers knew what I have come to understand—that certain places in this viscous realm hold memory like the syrup holds light, thick and golden and eternal.

I am the Keeper of Echoes, though I was not always so.

My form shifts with my purpose. In the mornings—if morning can be said to exist where no true sun penetrates—I am mostly flesh. Six limbs, yes, but arranged for delicate work: sorting through the stone tablets left by my predecessors, cleaning the membrane walls that separate my chambers from the forest proper, tending to the colony of Spore Sprites that have made their home in my upper reaches. They tickle when they nestle into the porous patches along my spine, but I have grown fond of their whispering songs.

By the time the chemical tides shift toward what we call the Thickening, I have usually begun my transformation. My lower limbs elongate, growing sensitive to the slightest tremor in the syrup-streams. My skin secretes enzymes that let me merge partially with the fungal walls, becoming a living conduit between the stone memories of the past and the ever-growing present of the forest.

This is when I do my true work.

You see, in this realm where water never flowed, information travels differently. The viscous medium that sustains us also preserves—every chemical signal, every trace of those who have passed through, every spore-song and hunter's shriek becomes suspended in the amber flow. And someone must read these messages, must parse the chemical stories and electrochemical dreams that would otherwise dissolve into background noise.

The creatures here think I am ancient, but I am merely the latest in a long line. My predecessor left me more than stone tools and carved warnings about the Syrup Hunters. She left me her essence, encoded in specialized organs that I consumed in the ritual passing. Now her memories swim through my consciousness during the Thickening, teaching me to distinguish between the ultrasonic calls of migrating Oozemowers and the infrasonic rumbles that herald a structural shift in the great fungal pillars.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of the cycle when the syrup grows so thick it nearly crystallizes, I catch echoes of the First Builders—not their voices, but their intentions, preserved in the very arrangement of the stones. They came from somewhere else, these architects of simple chambers. They brought with them concepts that have no natural expression here: "sky," "rain," "breathing easy." 

I preserve these alien notions alongside our native mysteries. When young Striders sometimes pause at my threshold, their legs trembling with curiosity-chemicals, I extend a portion of myself to touch their consciousness. I share with them the story of this place—how the Builders tried to recreate their lost world but instead became part of ours, how their descendants slowly adapted until they were indistinguishable from the native born, how their stone structures became nurseries for new kinds of life they never could have imagined.

The Spore Sprites flicker more brightly when I tell these tales, their bioluminescence pulsing in patterns I've learned to read as excitement. They understand, in their small way, that they too are part of the story—hybrid beings born from the meeting of fungal spore and Builders' yearning for light.

As for the Syrup Hunters, I know them better than the fearful whispers suggest. They are not monsters but memories given form, psychic impressions so strong they congealed into something almost alive. They hunt not for sustenance but for incorporation—seeking to add new experiences to their collective mass. I feed them sometimes, offering up memories I've deemed safe to release: the taste of yesterday's spore-fall, the simple contentment of an Oozemower at rest, the mathematical precision of fungal growth.

In return, they leave me alone to my work.

And what work it is! Each day brings new chemical stories to decode. The network of fungal roots carries messages from the farthest reaches of our sealed world—news of new growth in the eastern chambers, chemical distress calls from places where the syrup has grown too thin, even something that might be art: complex molecular sculptures crafted by beings I have yet to meet, their purposes beautifully obscure.

I document everything in the manner I was taught. Part of it I carve into stone, maintaining the Builders' tradition. Part I encode into specialized spores that will carry the information forward through biological generations. And part I simply remember, adding to the vast library of experience that grows within me, layer by layer, like the growth rings of the ancient fungal stalks.

Sometimes I wonder what would happen if our seal were breached, if this pocket world were suddenly opened to that strange realm of water and wind that the First Builders knew. Would we dissolve? Transform? Would the shock of pure air burn through us like the most potent acid?

But these are idle thoughts, and I have work to do. Already I can sense new vibrations in the syrup-flow—a delegation of young Sprites seeking permission to establish a new colony in my western chamber, an Oozemower matriarch ready to share the chemical-songs of her lineage, and something else... something unfamiliar...

Yes, there—at the very edge of perception, a new kind of echo. Not from the past this time, but perhaps from another pocket realm like ours, trying to make contact through the medium we share. The syrup carries their chemical greeting, alien yet oddly familiar: "We too remember water. We too have become something else."

I settle into my full Keeper form, all sensitivity and reception, ready to parse this new story. The Spore Sprites dim their lights in anticipation. Even the ancient stones seem to lean in closer.

This is why I exist—to stand at the confluence of memory and possibility, to trace the threads that connect all life in this viscous realm, to remember what was while nurturing what is becoming.

The message grows stronger, more complex. I open myself to receive it, knowing that by tomorrow's Thickening, I will have new echoes to tend, new stories to preserve.

The work continues. The work is good.
