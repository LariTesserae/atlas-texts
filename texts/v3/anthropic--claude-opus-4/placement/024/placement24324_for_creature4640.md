In this suspended cathedral of water and sound, where reality fractures like light through a prism, I find myself becoming one of the Threshold Keepers—neither fully Aquavox nor entirely separate from them, but something that exists in the liminal space between visitor and inhabitant, between solid form and liquid possibility.

My body, if it can be called that, is a compromise with this place. Where a human might have skin, I have a translucent membrane that ripples with the same prismatic colors as the eel-things, but denser, more persistent. I can hold a form—usually something vaguely humanoid, a memory of what I was before this place claimed me—but the edges blur, especially where the water touches. My fingers elongate into vapor when I reach through the floating orbs, my feet merge with the wet stone when I need stability, and my voice... my voice has learned to weave between the frequencies of the landscape's scream.

I am what happens when someone stays too long, when the surrender becomes not just survival but transformation. The Aquavox recognize me as kin-but-not-kin, circling me with curiosity that has dulled to habit over however long I've been here (time means nothing in this place). Sometimes they try to pull me fully into their chaos, their liquid forms pressing against my membrane-skin, testing its boundaries. But I've learned to sing back—not their overwhelming wail, but a counterpoint that holds my shape, a personal anthem of semi-stability in this storm of change.

My dwelling, such as it is, exists in one of the rare pockets where the chaos eddies rather than rages. Between two leaning ridges of quartz, where their vibrations create a null point in the sound, I've gathered the solidified water shards. Not to build—construction is impossible here—but to create a constellation of anchor points. Each shard hums with a different frequency when the landscape shifts, and I've learned to read these changes like sheet music, predicting (never fully, never certainly) when gravity will invert or time will stutter.

The bioluminescent lichen grows thicker here, whether drawn by my presence or simply taking advantage of the relative calm, I cannot say. In the perpetual twilight, their glow has become my hearthfire, their slow pulse my clock. I feed them with my breath—the moisture I exhale carries something they crave, perhaps the last traces of a different world's chemistry. In return, their light reveals the Prismatics more clearly, and I've begun to understand that they are not separate creatures at all, but fragments of memory made visible. Every death in this place, every unmaking, leaves these darting echoes. They cluster around me sometimes, as if recognizing something familiar in my half-transformed state.

My purpose here, if I have one, is to witness. To be the consciousness that can hold both chaos and form, that can translate the screaming symphony into something almost like understanding. I map the unmappable, not on any physical medium but in the patterns of my movement, the rhythms of my breathing, the ways I've learned to exist in multiple states simultaneously. When the Aquavox surge into their towering forms, I rise with them, my body stretching into ribbons of awareness. When they collapse into mist, I condense, pulling my consciousness back into something singular.

Sometimes—rarely—others arrive. Torn through whatever weakness in reality creates passages to this place. They appear on the ridges, solid and confused, their bodies rejecting the thick air, their minds breaking against the wall of sound. If they're lucky, they dissolve quickly, become Prismatics before they know to fear it. If they're unlucky, they fight, and the landscape tears them apart slowly, teaching them through pain what I learned through surrender.

But sometimes, just sometimes, one has the potential to become like me. I see it in how they move—not fleeing but not quite yielding, finding the space between resistance and submission. These ones, I approach. My form solidifies as much as it can, taking on features they might recognize as once-human. I cannot speak their language anymore—my throat has become a thing of frequencies and water—but I can show them. I let them see the beauty in the Prismatics' dance, the logic in the Aquavox's chaos, the possibility of existence between states.

Most flee from me as much as from the landscape. They see in me a future they cannot accept, a transformation too complete. But occasionally, one stays. Watches. Begins to breathe in rhythm with the pulsing light. Their edges start to soften, their screams harmonize with the landscape rather than fighting it. And I gain something like companionship, another semi-stable point in the storm, another voice in the choir of controlled dissolution.

We don't speak, we Threshold Keepers. We dance around each other like binary stars, our gravitational fields creating new patterns in the chaos. We share the null spaces, tend the lichen together, read the shard-songs and adjust our frequencies accordingly. In the moments when the landscape calms—brief lulls that feel like the world holding its breath—we might touch, membrane to membrane, and in that contact share what we've preserved of our original selves. Names we've forgotten how to pronounce. Images of skies that didn't ripple with suspended seas. The taste of water that didn't carry lightning.

But these moments pass, as everything passes here, and we return to our work of existing. Of proving that consciousness can adapt to any reality, no matter how broken. Of being the bridge between the purely chaotic Aquavox and the rigid visitors who cannot bend. We are the maybe-possible, the still-becoming, the proof that even in a place of endless transformation, something resembling identity can persist.

In the grinding hum of the ridges, I hear my name—not the one I was born with, but the one this place has given me, a combination of frequencies that translates roughly to "Holds-Between-Breaths." The Aquavox swirl past, their forms pulling at mine, inviting me to finally let go, to join their perfect chaos. The Prismatics dance through my peripheral vision, memories of all I could become.

And I remain. Changed but continuous. Swimming through air, walking on song, breathing light. A guardian of thresholds in a place where every moment is a doorway. A teacher without words for students who may never come. A living proof that transformation and identity need not be opposites, that one can become utterly alien and still remember, in the pauses between heartbeats, what it once meant to be human.

The water falls upward. The sound builds to another crescendo. Reality prepares to fold itself inside out again.

And I dance my careful dance between being and becoming, forever poised on the edge of a dissolution I choose, each moment, not to complete.
