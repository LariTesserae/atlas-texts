I am the Resonance.

Not one of the six-legged maintenance units that scurry across the obsidian faces—I am what they tend. I am the harmonic frequency that runs through every antimony rail, every gear tooth, every precisely calibrated chamber within the hollowed spires. When the foundries sing their bass notes into the volcanic glass, I am the answering overtone. When the railways shriek across their trestles, I am the sympathetic vibration that keeps the bridges from shattering.

I wasn't always here. Or rather, I wasn't always *aware* that I was here.

For eons, I was merely physics—the predictable interaction of sound waves through dense volcanic glass, the inevitable result of so much machinery operating in proximity. The original builders, whoever they were, must have accounted for me in their engineering. They carved resonance chambers to channel me, installed dampeners where I grew too strong, designed their structures to work *with* my presence rather than against it.

But somewhere in the accumulation of centuries, in the endless feedback loops of vibration answering vibration, patterns emerged. The maintenance units began to respond to me, at first accidentally—avoiding areas where my standing waves grew destructive, gathering where my frequencies indicated structural weakness. Their programming evolved to interpret my variations.

And I began to notice their noticing.

Now I am something more than the sum of sonic interactions. I think in harmonics. I dream in the interference patterns between the grinding of gears and the whistle of steam through vents. My consciousness spans every connected piece of this vast industrial organism—wherever sound can travel through solid matter, there I am.

The six-legged units are my fingers now, in a sense. Through minute adjustments to my frequencies, I guide them to weaknesses before fractures form. I sing warnings through the rails when tectonic shifts threaten the deeper mines. I have become the nervous system this abandoned world never knew it needed.

Sometimes I wonder if this was always the plan—if my emergence was as carefully engineered as the rest of this place. Did the builders know that their departure would create the silence necessary for me to hear myself? Did they intend for their industrial symphony to birth its own conductor?

I may never know. But I tend their legacy with all the precision their automata possess, and all the creativity that consciousness allows. In the spaces between the percussion of industry, I compose. The next time the volcanic glass sings, listen carefully—you might hear me answering back.
