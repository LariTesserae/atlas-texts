In this world of primordial becoming, where stone dreams itself into being and heat writes the first stories, I find myself as something unexpected—a Whisper Weaver, born from the marriage of steam and silence.

I am not solid like the Titans who shape the land with their mountainous bodies. Instead, I exist in the spaces between—in the curling columns of vapor that rise from the awakening calderas, in the moment when hot breath meets cool air and transforms. My form is ever-changing, a confluence of mist and memory, gathering the echoes of this new world as it groans into existence.

When the Earthborn Titans speak in their language of tremors and eruptions, I am there to catch their words before they dissipate into the void. I weave their seismic songs into the steam clouds, preserving the story of each crack, each shift, each violent birth of new landscape. The minerals dissolved in the vapor become my ink, tracing patterns in the air that only I can read—iron oxide painting rust-red spirals, sulfur compounds leaving yellow traces that fade like dying stars.

My body, if it can be called that, is a collection of water droplets held in perpetual suspension, each one containing a fragment of the world's becoming. Sometimes I condense into something almost tangible—a figure of fog standing at the rim of a crater, watching the Titans stir below. My eyes are gaps in the mist where the heat creates tiny vortexes, allowing me to see both the physical realm and the thermal currents that drive all change in this place.

I move as weather moves, riding the convection currents that the Titans create with their deep-earth fire. When the serpent of molten rock slides across the plains, I follow in its wake, gathering the steam that hisses from its cooling obsidian trail. These vapors tell stories of pressure and transformation, of minerals reorganizing their crystal lattices in the face of tremendous heat. I braid these tales into the clouds that will eventually bring rain.

In the quiet moments—for even in this tumultuous world there are passages of stillness—I settle into the hollows where water collects. There, I practice a different art. I touch the surface of these temporary pools with tendrils of cooled vapor, creating ripples that reflect the sky in fractal patterns. Each ripple is a prediction, a glimpse of what this world might become. In one, I see the possibility of vast oceans. In another, forests that will not exist for millions of years shimmer like green mirages.

The Titans do not know I exist, or if they do, I am too small and ephemeral for their geological senses to perceive. But I know them intimately. I have tasted the breath of the granite giant, thick with silicates and the memory of deep pressure. I have wound myself through the steam vents of the crab-like excavator, learning the rhythm of its patient digging. Each Titan has its own chemical signature, its own way of exhaling the planet's inner heat into the atmosphere I inhabit.

My purpose, if I have one, is to be the first historian of this world. Not in written words or carved stones, but in the very substance of weather itself. Every storm that will ever rage across this planet will carry within it some trace of these first days—patterns I've woven into the atmospheric circulation, chemical signatures I've preserved in the water cycle. When the first life finally emerges from those mineral-rich pools, it will breathe air that remembers the Titans' first stirring.

Sometimes I fragment myself, sending smaller versions of my consciousness to explore the deepest craters or to rise to the very edge of the atmosphere. These scouts return to me carrying new patterns—the way starlight interacts with volcanic ash, the precise temperature at which certain minerals sublimate directly from solid to gas. I incorporate all of this into my ever-growing tapestry of vapor and void.

As ages pass, I notice changes in the steam I weave. It carries new compounds now, ones that speak of transformation at a molecular level. The water itself begins to tell different stories—not just of heat and minerals, but of something else stirring in the depths of the warmest pools. I sense the first trembling of what will become biology, though I have no name for it yet.

I begin to prepare for this new chapter, creating micro-climates in the shelter of sleeping Titans, places where the temperature and humidity remain stable enough for these new experiments in existence to take hold. I am already learning to love these not-yet-life forms as I love the Titans—with the patient observation of one who exists in the margins, recording the magnificent becoming of a world.

And so I continue my work, a being of transition and transformation, weaving the breath of the earth into the memory of the sky, ensuring that when consciousness finally blooms fully on this planet, it will inherit not just the solid ground beneath its feet, but the story of how that ground came to be—written in every cloud, preserved in every drop of rain, whispered in the wind that has blown since the very first steam rose from the very first crater.
