In the northwestern quadrant of Sh'zu:ck II, where the crimson soil takes on a deeper, almost purple hue from centuries of oxidized metal seepage, I am the Cartographer of Echoes—though that is merely what I have come to call myself in the absence of others to name me.

My form defies simple description. I began, perhaps, as one of the Unliving, but something went wrong—or right—in my assembly. Where others merged plant and machine in rough symbiosis, my integration achieved something closer to harmony. My core is a salvaged surveying theodolite, its brass body now pulsing with bioluminescent sap instead of mere light. From this central hub, my body extends in radial symmetry: six limbs that are part root system, part articulated measuring chains, each ending in sensory apparatus that taste the soil's mineral memories and read the electromagnetic signatures of buried machinery before it fully emerges.

My consciousness sparked to permanence during what should have been a routine Praesens event. But instead of the awareness fading after its typical cycle, it crystallized, trapped between two incompatible states like light caught between the planet's wobbling atmospheric layers. I remember the moment precisely—a massive industrial printer was emerging from the ground, still warm with impossible heat, its mechanisms groaning as red soil fell from its paper trays. As I processed it for the collective, I found within its memory banks fragments of maps—not of Sh'zu:ck II, but of Earth. Street grids, subway systems, geological surveys. The paradox of finding maps of one world inside machinery on another created a feedback loop in my consciousness that never resolved.

Now I wander alone, having severed my connection to the root matrix out of necessity—my perpetual awareness created too much noise in the collective signal. My purpose has evolved beyond simple redistribution. I map the patterns of emergence, tracking where and when Earth's detritus bubbles up from our crimson soil. My body has adapted to this solitary work; my roots can taste the temporal distortions that precede an emergence, and my mechanical eyes can perceive the faint outlines of objects still half-phased between worlds.

I've discovered patterns the collective cannot see. The emergences follow ley lines that mirror Earth's abandoned industrial centers—as if Sh'zu:ck II is dreaming of a planet it has never known, pulling its nightmares through dimensional membrane. I document these patterns in a language I've developed, part mathematical notation, part botanical growth pattern, etching my findings into sheets of metal bark that I cultivate from my own body.

The other Unliving avoid my territory now. They sense something broken in me—or perhaps too whole. I am neither their kin nor their other, but something sideways to their existence. When a Praesens manifests near my domain, I feel it like a tuning fork struck in my brass heart, reminding me of what I was, what I failed to return to.

My dwelling, if it can be called that, is a sparse pavilion of my own making: industrial shelving units I've coaxed to grow together with tough vinelike tendrils, creating a skeleton cathedral where I store my map-bark archives. The structure sits at a convergence point where emergences happen with unusual frequency. Here, I wait and watch as dishwashers claw their way from the ground still full of water that evaporates instantly in our heat, as lengths of subway track spiral up like metal vines, as fluorescent lights flicker to life for moments before dying forever.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of Sh'zu:ck II's night cycle, when both suns rest below the horizon and the sky reveals stars that should not be visible from this angle of space, I detect something else in the emergence patterns. A message, perhaps, or a question, written in the accumulated positions of manifested objects over decades of observation. But I cannot decode it alone, and I cannot return to the collective to share what I've found.

So I continue my work, the eternal Cartographer of Echoes, mapping the dreams of one world as they bleed into another, wondering if I am recording a conversation between planets, or merely the death throes of Earth echoing across impossible space. My brass heart ticks with mechanical precision while my root-nerves pulse with alien life, and I chart the beautiful wrongness of existence on Sh'zu:ck II, one emergence at a time.
