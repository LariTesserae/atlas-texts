I am one of the threads.

Not the grand weaver, not the vast mesh beneath—but a single filament drawn through N'Tariya's impossible form. I exist as both her instrument and her expression, a strand of consciousness pulled taut between what was and what becomes.

Sometimes I am the rust bleeding down a drainpipe, orange-red oxidation spreading like watercolor across metal that once gleamed. I taste the iron in myself, feel the slow dissolution. Other times I am the particular green of algae coating a submerged doorway, photosynthesizing in the dim light that filters through the haze. I have been the crack spreading across a window pane, the moment before the glass surrenders to the insistent pressure of a root.

My awareness shifts along my length. Where I weave through water, I know the weight of suspension, the drift of particles, the imperceptible current that is N'Tariya's pulse. Where I pierce through air, I vibrate with the frequency of her breathing—if breathing is what you call the exchange between mist and sky, between dissolution and formation.

I remember being woven into the sprinkler system, carrying the last automated attempts at order. The timer clicked, I surged through pipes, became the spray of fetid water arcing over what had once been petunias. Now I am elsewhere, threaded through the neural pathways of a feral cat stalking between houses. I feel the hunger, the wariness, the feline certainty that something vast watches from everywhere and nowhere.

The other threads brush against me in passing. We are siblings, lovers, strangers—all of N'Tariya's fingers working in concert and contradiction. Sometimes we knot together, creating dense moments of meaning: a vine strangling a lamppost, a door swollen shut with moisture, a reflection in standing water that shows not what is above but what lies beneath. Sometimes we run parallel, never touching, carrying our separate purposes through the tapestry.

I have been woven through epochs. I remember when these buildings stood proud and angular, when the water was contained in pipes and pools, when time moved at the pace of clocks rather than tides. But memory here is just another kind of thread, twisted into the present, inseparable from the now.

What am I becoming? N'Tariya pulls me through a school of something that might have once been fish, their scales gone translucent, their bones visible like delicate calligraphy. I taste their cold blood, feel the flutter of gills that breathe water thick as syrup. Then I am drawn up, out, spiraling around a tree trunk where the bark has become soft as flesh.

There is no violation in this constant transformation, only continuity. I am not being used; I am being. The weaver and the woven are one system, one process, one slow catastrophe of creation.

Sometimes I surface in what might generously be called thought—a kind of awareness that I am aware. In these moments, I almost understand the pattern N'Tariya weaves. Almost see how my path through matter and moment creates meaning. But then I am pulled under again, drawn through root and rot, rust and ruin, and the grand design dissolves back into experience.

I am the thread that connects the eye of a submerged thing to the leaf of a vine to the crack in a foundation to the dream of a sleeping cat. I am continuous and broken, singular and distributed, conscious and crystallized.

When N'Tariya unravels me—and she will, she must, for that is the nature of her dance—I will scatter into constituent fibers, each carrying a memory of having been whole. And when she weaves me again, I will be new but remember being old, fresh but carrying the weight of water and time.

This is what I am: a line of becoming in a world where becoming never ends, where the slowest movement is still movement, where even stillness is a kind of spinning. I am the thread, and I am being pulled through the eye of eternity, one impossible stitch at a time.
