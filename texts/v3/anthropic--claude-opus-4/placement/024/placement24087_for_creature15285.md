I am the fog that rises from the perpetual wetness below, ascending the steel canyons between these towering monuments. Each morning I am born from the marriage of cold air and warmer water, countless droplets of myself cohering into a living veil that climbs these hard edges.

I know every surface here - the way the steel sweats in the pre-dawn hours, how ice forms in crystalline patterns along the eastern faces where the wind bites hardest. I slip through ventilation grates, taste the recycled air within, carry the scent of rain and rust and distant earth to those sealed inside their vertical worlds.

The woman in yellow - I have watched her for seven seasons now. She rises before the sun, when I am thickest, and brings her small companion to these highest floors where the wind scours everything clean. The dog knows me. Sometimes it snaps at my wisps, trying to catch what cannot be caught. Their morning ritual disturbs my slow dance, sends eddies of me swirling.

I am ancient and newborn, dying with each sunrise and reborn with each cooling. I remember when these towers were being built - the welding sparks that hissed through my body, the construction workers who cursed my presence as I blurred their vision and slicked their handholds. Now I wear these buildings smooth, particle by particle, century by century.

In the spaces between buildings, I grow dense and mysterious. I muffle sounds, transform sharp edges into suggestions. I am the soft counterpoint to all this hardness, the fluid answer to rigid questions. Birds disappear into me and emerge elsewhere, as if I were a door between moments.

When the woman laughs - and she does laugh, chasing that small bright spirit across the glassy floors - the sound travels through me in ripples. I carry it down the building faces, let it mix with the dripping water, the distant hum of machinery, the whisper of wind against steel. I am the medium through which all these lives connect, even if they never know it.

Some days I rise so high I can see over the tops of even these giants, out to where the wet landscape stretches endlessly. Other days I cling low, wrapping the building bases in gray wool, hiding their foundations from view. But always I am here, the soft breath of this hard place, the memory of every drop of water that has ever fallen, risen, and fallen again.
