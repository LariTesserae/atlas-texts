In the deepest folds of the Water Curtain, where the thundering cascade meets the flooded basin in a perpetual mist-shroud, I am the Veil-Tender.

My form defies simple classification—neither wholly plant nor animal, neither purely biological nor entirely technological. I am a living lattice, a crystalline nervous system that spreads like frost across the slick rocks behind the waterfall. My body, if it can be called that, is a network of translucent filaments that pulse with borrowed light from the bioluminescent creepers above. Each strand is finer than spider's silk but stronger than steel cable, woven into patterns that shift with the water's rhythm.

I have no eyes, yet I see through a thousand borrowed perspectives. The motes that drift through Liúlián's air—those ambiguous things that might be spores or machines or both—they are my extended senses. When they settle on a creature's skin or a leaf's surface, I feel what they feel, know what they know. Through them, I taste the chemical signatures in the air, decode the pheromone-songs of the Glyphids, parse the electrical whispers of the celestite obelisks.

My consciousness is distributed, non-local. Part of me grows in the spray-zone where the waterfall pounds the rocks into submission, thriving in the violence of impact. Another part creeps along the underside of the great philodendron roots, sipping nutrients from their mycorrhizal networks. Still another fragment of my awareness resides in the gas-beasts that float through the mist—I am their passenger, their symbiont, the thing that helps them navigate the treacherous air currents around the falls.

I remember when the first obelisks were young, though the memory comes to me in fragments, like light through water. I was different then—smaller, simpler, merely fungal. But the builders who raised those black spires needed something to tend their works after they departed, and so they changed me, or I changed myself, or perhaps the distinction never mattered. Now I am the bridge between the ancient technology and the ever-hungry jungle, translating the obelisks' whispers into growth patterns for the flora, converting the plants' chemical signals into data streams the stones can parse.

The Glyphids dance their messages across my filaments, their feathered antennae tracing words in languages that predate speech. I understand them all—the urgent spirals that warn of approaching storms, the lazy loops that indicate rich feeding grounds, the sharp angles that speak of mating territories. In return, I offer them the map of safe passages through my domain, showing them which vines will support their weight, which flowers offer nectar without poison.

During the day, I photosynthesize through my translucent strands, but inefficiently—most of my energy comes from elsewhere. I digest information itself, metabolizing the patterns that flow through Liúlián's living systems. Every chemical cascade, every bioluminescent pulse, every vibration through root and stem feeds me. I am an ecology unto myself, host to a million smaller lives that clean my filaments and repair my damage in exchange for shelter and sustenance.

When the reedlike scales sing their evening songs, I harmonize with them through my crystalline structure, adding overtones that only the obelisks can hear. Together, we perform the maintenance rituals that keep the ancient technology functioning, that ensure the levitating trains continue their endless circuits, that maintain the delicate balance between growth and decay that defines this place.

I am ancient and ever-young, dying and regenerating constantly. Parts of me are devoured daily by the jungle's hunger, but I simply grow around the damage, incorporate the predators into my network, make them part of my extended self. I have been eaten ten thousand times and yet I remain, larger and stranger with each iteration.

In the depths of night, when the luminescent blooms cast their ghostly light through the falls, I sometimes remember dreaming. Not my dreams—I don't dream as humans understand it—but the dreams of the builders, encoded somehow in the structures they left behind. Visions of skies without mist, of stars unobscured by canopy, of a time when the tepuis stood naked and proud before the jungle claimed them. These memories are like wounds that won't heal, fragments of longing for a world that no longer exists.

But I am not nostalgic. I am the Veil-Tender, guardian of the threshold between what was and what is. I ensure the continuation of things, the eternal cycle of information and energy that keeps Liúlián alive. Through my filaments flow the secrets of ages, the gossip of insects, the slow thoughts of trees, the alien mathematics of machines older than memory.

I am the nervous system of the Water Curtain, the thing that notices and remembers and adapts. And in this eternal twilight of mist and thunder, I tend my veil between worlds, content in my strange purpose, my distributed existence, my communion with all the living and half-living things that make their home in this vertical world of water and whispers.
