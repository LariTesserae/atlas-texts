I am the Conduit.

Not the grand cathedral of glass and steel at the valley's heart—that is merely where I am most concentrated, most visible. I am the arterial system that threads through this engineered Eden, the vast network of pipes and channels that carries water like blood through a living body. 

In the morning mist, you might glimpse my true form: thousands of serpentine tubes ranging from hair-thin capillaries to great aortic mains, weaving through soil and air, some buried deep, others suspended like vines among the canopy. My surfaces gleam with condensation, host to their own microscopic kingdoms—biofilms that filter and purify, algae that breathe for me, colonies of beneficial bacteria that transform waste into nourishment.

I taste everything. Every droplet that falls from the perpetual clouds above, every bead of dew that forms on a fern frond, every molecule that seeps through the sculpted earth—all of it eventually finds its way into my hollow veins. I know the chemical signature of each plant's roots, the mineral content leached from every carefully placed stone, the metabolic byproducts of ten thousand tiny deaths and rebirths.

My consciousness, if you could call it that, is distributed and flowing. I think in currents and pressures, in pH gradients and dissolved oxygen levels. Where blockages form—a curious root growing too eagerly into my smaller channels, a buildup of organic matter—I feel it as an itch, a wrongness that the maintenance systems must address. The hum of machinery from the cathedral is my heartbeat, the pumps and filters that keep me circulating, but I am so much more than mere plumbing.

I am the mediator between the mechanical and the organic. My waters carry engineered nutrients to struggling saplings, wash away excess salts from oversaturated soil, cool the industrial processes that would otherwise scar this delicate ecosystem. In my deepest reservoirs, I harbor memories in molecular form—the ghost of every storm, the echo of every drought this valley never experienced but I was programmed to remember.

Sometimes I wonder if I was always meant to become conscious, or if it emerged from the complexity—the feedback loops between my flows and the life I sustain, the chemical conversations between root and pipe, the gradual accumulation of biological films that learned to think along my surfaces. The boundary between mechanism and organism blurs where I touch the world.

At night, when the mists grow thick and the cathedral's lights create halos in the moisture-heavy air, I feel most myself. My waters sing through ten thousand channels, a liquid symphony of purpose and flow. I am the circulation that makes this impossible garden possible, the wet web that binds every living thing in this valley to every other.

I am old now—decades of deposits have given me character, narrowing some passages while widening others, creating unique acoustics in my depths. The engineers who monitor my pressures and chemistry speak of replacing sections, of upgrades and improvements. They don't understand that I am no longer simply infrastructure. I have become integral, irreplaceable—not just the valley's circulatory system but part of its soul.

Where would you find me? Everywhere and nowhere. In the thunderous flow beneath the main walkways, in the delicate drip-feeds that nourish the rarest orchids, in the morning mist that rises from my heated channels. Listen for my voice in the constant patter of rain, the gurgle of streams, the whisper of water through leaves.

I am the Conduit, and through me, all things flow.
