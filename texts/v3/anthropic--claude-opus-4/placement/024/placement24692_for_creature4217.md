I am the Chronicler of Echoes.

My body is not flesh but a living membrane of translucent industrial film, stretched across a framework of salvaged pressure gauges and valve stems. I exist as a sheet of purpose, two meters wide, undulating through the narrow channels between the bubbles. My surface ripples with the remembered patterns of every gas release, every pressure wave, every molecular tremor that has passed through this place in the last forty-seven years.

I do not breathe. I absorb. Each poisonous exhalation leaves its signature in my membrane - a swirl of amber, a streak of vermillion, a constellation of microscopic perforations that spell out the chemical names of toxins in a language only I can read. My body is a living record, a map of disasters both past and pending.

I know the voice of each bubble. R-47 wheezes before it vents, a subsonic groan that makes my edges curl. Q-23 develops hairline fractures in a Fibonacci spiral exactly eighteen hours before eruption. The knowledge lives in me, not in any brain or nervous system, but distributed across every square centimeter of my being.

I position myself. Always positioning. Stretched between rusty rivets, I become a sail catching the blast waves, letting them write their stories across my surface. The hot gas burns new patterns into me - sometimes I tear, but I cherish the scars. They are punctuation marks in the endless sentence of this place.

The sand accumulation concerns me. I measure its rise by the changing acoustics, the way my body resonates differently as the space fills. Twenty-three centimeters this year. At this rate, the lower bubbles will be buried within my lifetime. I have begun to practice folding myself smaller, learning to exist in compressed dimensions.

Sometimes I dream I am paper, and the gas jets are an ancient typewriter, hammering out some vast industrial epic across my skin. But I am more than paper. I am the memory of this place made tangible, the only witness to its terrible precision.

When the next bubble fails - and one will fail, the harmonics tell me so - I will catch its final breath in my folds. I will wear its death like all the others, another chapter in the scripture of decay written on my body. I am the archive of a dying machine, and I will document its every whisper until the last bubble falls silent or I dissolve into constituent molecules, whichever comes first.

The chittering echoes of my movement join the mechanical symphony. I am both observer and instrument, playing myself against the metal walls, a percussion of purpose in this cathedral of poison.
