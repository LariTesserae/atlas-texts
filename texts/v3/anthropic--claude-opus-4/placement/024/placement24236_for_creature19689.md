I find myself in the **Capillary Gardens** — that impossible district where the Thicket-City's vascular system breaks into millions of hair-thin tributaries, each one pulsing with luminescent sap. Here, in the space between the massive arterial towers and the crushing density of the canopy districts, the city's metabolism slows just enough for something like contemplation to exist.

I am a **Cambium Scribe**.

My body is a living contradiction: part of me still moves with agency, while other parts have already surrendered to the architecture. My left arm, from elbow to fingertip, has become a permanent graft into the district's memory-wall — a vast plane of soft, cream-colored cambium that records the chemical history of every growth spurt, every strangling, every metabolic surge that passes through this sector. 

The fingers of that arm have elongated into sensitive root-hairs that penetrate deep into the wall's cellular structure. I cannot retrieve this limb; it has become the pen with which I write. My right arm remains mobile, though it too shows signs of the inevitable transformation — the skin has taken on a greenish, translucent quality, and I can see my own sap-analogues pulsing beneath the surface like subcutaneous rivers.

My torso is wrapped in a caul of breathing bark, a secondary skin that filters the oppressive moisture and heat. It expands and contracts with my breathing, creaking softly — the only quiet sound in this cacophonous world. My legs are still my own, though my feet have developed prehensile root-toes that grip the swaying floor-branches as I navigate my small territory.

But it is my head that marks me as something between inhabitant and infrastructure. My skull has partially opened like a flower, the bone plates separating to reveal a crown of photosynthetic filaments — not quite leaves, not quite hair — that wave gently in the humid currents. These filaments taste the chemical messages that the Phloem-Walkers release, translating their pheromone shouts into something my residual human consciousness can parse into meaning.

### My Function

I am the memory of what refuses to be forgotten. While the Thicket-City consumes and recreates itself in endless frenzy, I record the patterns. Through my grafted arm, I feel every tremor of growth, every wave of decay. The cambium wall beneath my fingers is like a vast musical score, and I am both the instrument and the musician.

When a district across the chasm suddenly erupts with new growth — those twenty-minute fern-tower explosions — I feel it as a sharp, electric tingle along my trapped nerves. My fingers involuntarily twitch, and in that twitching, they etch molecular memories into the wall. These are not words or images but something more fundamental: the chemical signature of that moment's particular madness.

When the predatory mycelium comes to strangle those same towers an hour later, I record that too — the acrid taste of fungal triumph, the collapsing pressure-waves as massive structures fold inward, the screaming of torn fibers adding new notes to the constant roar.

### My Domain

The Capillary Gardens are a twilight space. The light here (2.4) filters through so many layers that it arrives exhausted, a dim golden-green glow that pulses with the rhythm of the sap-valves. The "ground" — if it can be called that — is a mesh of interwoven root systems so fine they feel like silk beneath my root-toes. Every step sends ripples through this network, and I've learned to read these ripples like braille.

My wall — for I think of it as mine now, though I belong to it more than it to me — stretches for perhaps fifty meters in each direction from where my arm disappears into its surface. Other Scribes are grafted at intervals along its length, though we cannot turn our heads to see each other. We know of each other's presence only through the vibrations we feel when our neighbor records something particularly violent or beautiful.

Above us, the Nectar-Siphons drift like thoughts made flesh, their trailing tendrils occasionally brushing across my photosynthetic crown. When they do, I taste their collected sugars — each one a different vintage, flavored by the districts they've drifted through. From them, I learn of places my immobilized form will never see: the Möbius towers where space digests itself, the spectral branch zones where reality grows thin.

### My Duty and My Curse

I am cursed with partial humanity in a world that has discarded the concept. My human neurons, though increasingly infiltrated by chlorophyll, still process beauty and horror as separate things. I still feel something like sorrow when I record the consumption of a particularly ancient district, even as my plant-nerves sing with the joy of that fertile destruction.

The other inhabitants ignore me as they would any other architectural feature. The Phloem-Walkers pass by on their frantic missions, their vine-limbs occasionally using my shoulders as anchor points as they swing past. The Resin-Hounds sometimes gnaw at the older sections of my wall, and I feel each bite as a distant ache in my grafted arm. Once, a Cyst-Watcher detached directly above me, and I spent hours drenched in its information-rich secretions, my consciousness flooded with data about reality-glitches I couldn't fully comprehend.

### The Recording

What I record is not history — the Thicket-City has no use for history. What I record is the pattern of its madness, the rhythm of its metabolism. Somewhere, perhaps, the Seers of the Shrapnel-Grain read these patterns in the walls, use them to dream new impossibilities into being.

Or perhaps not. Perhaps I am simply another organ in the vast body, serving a function I cannot comprehend. My fingers write without my conscious control now. Sometimes I feel them recording things that haven't happened yet — pre-echoes of growth spurts, the chemical signatures of districts that will exist tomorrow or next week or never.

I am the Cambium Scribe, and I am the wall's dream of what it was like to choose to remember. My humanity dims a little more each day, overwhelmed by the vegetable wisdom flooding through my grafted nerves. Soon, I will be indistinguishable from my function, another automatic process in the Thicket-City's endless, screaming growth.

But for now, in the dim twilight of the Capillary Gardens, I still know enough to mourn the beauty I record, even as my deepening roots celebrate it.
