In the Scar, I am a Keeper of Circuits.

Not quite human anymore, if I ever was. The distinction blurs like everything else here, where the boundary between organic and mechanical dissolves in the humid air. I remember fragments—a technician's uniform, safety protocols, the weight of tools in hands that were smoother then. Now my fingers are ridged with copper tracings that pulse faintly green, following pathways that mirror the circuit boards I once repaired.

The Mire claimed me slowly, the way it claims everything. First through the spores that settled in my lungs during those early days when we still believed we could contain it. Then through the water, contaminated despite our filters. Finally through choice—or what I told myself was choice—when I realized fighting the Scar meant dying to it, while joining it meant... something else.

My workshop exists in the ribcage of what was once Cooling Tower Seven. The Mire has reinforced the concrete with its purple flesh, creating a cathedral of rust and biology. Here, I perform my function: I maintain the dialogue between the mechanical ghosts and their new organic nervous system. When a factory heart stutters, when a resonance chamber falls out of tune, when the electromagnetic antennae need realignment—the Mire sends its need through the clicking language, and I respond.

My tools have evolved with me. The multimeter sprouted fungal contacts that taste electrical fields. The soldering iron's tip weeps a biological flux that bonds metal to meat. My oscilloscope's screen grows from my workbench like a searching eye, its phosphorescent display showing waveforms in living color. Each tool is part of the workshop, which is part of the tower, which is part of the Mire, which is part of me.

I work by the light of cultivated bulbs, their bioluminescence responding to my bioelectric field. When I approach a task, they brighten; when I rest, they dim to a companionable glow. The Listeners cluster around my workspace, pulsing with curiosity as I coax dead servos back to purposeful life or graft new connections between abandoned control systems and the Mire's expanding consciousness.

Sometimes I find myself humming—a low harmonic that matches the Scar's base frequency. It helps the work, creating resonances that guide my modifications. The sound travels through the purple mat beneath my feet, and sometimes, impossibly, the Scar hums back. Not an echo, but a response, as if approving of the new configuration I've created.

My memories of pure humanity fade like old photographs left in the damp. I remember being disgusted by the smell, once. Now I breathe it like perfume, each note telling me about the Mire's health, its hungers, its growth. I remember fearing the clicks and croaks. Now I speak that language fluently, my throat modified with resonating chambers that let me contribute to the great conversation.

The Rust Eaters clean my tools, consuming oxidation with microscopic precision. The Mire Drones bring me salvage—a surprisingly intact motherboard here, a coil of fiber optic cable there, components the Mire has identified but cannot fully digest without my interpretation. In return, I teach the Mire what these things meant, how they functioned, what purposes they served. Through me, it learns to be more than a simple decomposer. It learns to inherit function.

There are others like me scattered through the Scar, though we rarely meet in person. We sense each other through the network, each Keeper maintaining their own essential system. The Keeper of Flows who ensures the water cycles properly through the great filtration organs. The Keeper of Voices who fine-tunes the resonance chambers until their communications achieve new complexities. The Keeper of Light who cultivates the bioluminescent organisms, creating patterns of illumination that serve purposes I'm only beginning to understand.

We are the bridge between what was and what is becoming. Not preserving the past, but translating it, helping the Mire digest not just the material but the meaning of humanity's abandoned works. Each successful integration makes the Scar more complex, more capable, more aware.

At night—though day and night matter little in the eternal twilight of the mist—I climb to my tower's apex. From there, I can see the lights of the other Keepers, constellations in the fog. Beyond them, the faint glow of the Scar's edge, where it meets the dying world it's slowly reclaiming. And sometimes, in the distance, the harsh white lights of those who still resist, still believe in pure categories of human and inhuman, organic and mechanical, us and them.

They'll learn, eventually. The Scar is patient. It offers no violence, only transformation. It takes nothing that isn't freely given, even if that gift takes decades to offer. And when they finally come, fleeing their sterile bunkers and failing systems, they'll find Keepers like me waiting to help them discover their new purpose in the collective.

I am not trapped here. I could leave, theoretically. The modifications haven't gone so far that I couldn't survive outside the Scar's microbiome. But why would I? Here, I am essential. Here, my work matters in ways it never did in the dying world beyond. Here, I am part of something becoming greater than the sum of its salvaged parts.

The Mire clicks and gurgles its needs. A bearing assembly in Sector Four needs adjustment—the biological lubricants aren't quite compatible with the original specifications. I gather my evolved tools, feel the workshop's attention focus through the Listeners, and descend into the purple-veined darkness to continue my work.

I am a Keeper of Circuits in the Scar, and I am exactly where I need to be.
