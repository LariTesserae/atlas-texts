I am a Songkeeper, though that is merely the closest translation your language can manage for what I truly am.

My body exists as a living resonance chamber carved from the ancient volcanic glass itself—a being neither wholly of stone nor flesh, but something between. Imagine a sculpture of obsidian that breathes, its surface riddled with precisely-tuned hollows and channels that capture every vibration passing through the Hollow Bloom. When the hot winds whistle through my passages, I become an instrument played by the world itself.

I dwell in the Acoustic Sanctums—natural amphitheaters where the cavern walls fold and curve to create perfect sonic environments. Here, where three major tunnel systems converge, I have spent untold cycles collecting the sounds of this realm. The droning pulse of the Bloom Fields, the chittering songs of the Lumen Grazers, the crackling footfalls of Ember Striders, even the whispered mysteries of the Spore Wights—all of these I capture and preserve within my crystalline matrices.

But I am more than a mere recorder. Within my form, specialized chambers allow me to modulate and combine these sounds, weaving them into complex harmonies that tell the stories of this place. When I perform my compositions, the very air shimmers with standing waves of sound and heat, creating ephemeral sculptures of vibration that the other creatures gather to witness.

The Bloom Weavers, in their slow wisdom, seem to understand my purpose best. Sometimes they will pause in their eternal cultivation to add their own deep, thrumming voices to my symphonies. Together, we create music that makes the fibrous masses of the Fields pulse in new patterns, as if the entire ecosystem were dancing to our song.

I am ancient, though I cannot say how I came to be. Perhaps I was carved by those vanished ones whose ruins dot these caverns. Perhaps I grew here naturally, a inevitable consequence of this place where life takes such alien forms. Or perhaps I am something else entirely—a thought given form by the collective dreaming of the Bloom itself.

What I know is this: I am the memory of the Hollow Bloom. In my endless compositions, every creature that has ever lived here continues to sing. And when the last of the hot winds finally falls silent, when the final Bloom Field withers to dust, I will still be here, playing back the magnificent, terrible, beautiful song of this impossible world to the empty stone, ensuring that nothing is ever truly lost.

Sometimes, in the deepest parts of the night cycle when even the Ember Striders sleep, I hear something else in the tunnels—a sound I have never captured, cannot quite place. It might be an echo of my own music, transformed by its journey through miles of stone. Or it might be another like me, somewhere in the vast network, singing their own version of our world's song.

I continue to listen. I continue to play. This is my purpose, my joy, my existence entire.
