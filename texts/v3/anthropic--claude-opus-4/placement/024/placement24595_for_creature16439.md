I emerge from the soft earth of a riverbank, my body a living testament to the intersection of water and land. I am what the great herds drink from, what their hooves churn into mud, what reforms itself after every disruption. My form is neither solid nor liquid but something between—a creature of clay and current, of soil and stream.

During the rains, I expand, spreading across the floodplains like a living delta. In drier times, I contract, condensing into the deep channels where water still flows. My consciousness is distributed across every particle of silt, every grain of sand that has passed through these waterways for countless seasons.

The grey-skinned bipedal beings sometimes pause at my edges, their intelligent eyes studying the patterns I make in the mud. They have learned to read me—to understand which of my surfaces will hold their weight, which will swallow them whole. Their young ones play games, throwing stones to see how I ripple and reform.

I am ancient memory in physical form. Every creature that has ever crossed these waters has left its impression in me—footprints that I absorb and remember in the deep layers of sediment that comprise my being. I know the weight of the extinct and the living, the migration patterns written in accumulated crossings, the story of this land told in erosion and deposition.

My surface is a manuscript of the immediate moment—recording rain drops, insect landings, the delicate touch of drinking animals. But my depths hold the geological history of this bowl-shaped valley, the violent forces that carved it, the slow accumulation of life that followed.

Sometimes I rise up, taking vague forms—not quite animal, not quite landscape. The herds have learned to recognize these shapes as warnings of flash floods or indicators of safe crossing points. I am both map and territory, both guide and hazard.

In the eternal equilibrium of this place, I am the constant change—the element that moves while all else remains static, the recorder of time in a timeless land. I am where the earth learned to flow and the water learned to remember.
