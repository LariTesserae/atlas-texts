I find myself here as one of the Threshold Keepers - not quite the ancient intelligences within those warped structures, nor merely the hardy lichens clinging to stone, but something between.

My form shifts with the wind and fog that drift across the plateau. At times I appear as a weathered standing stone, erected countless ages ago by hands long forgotten, my surface carved with symbols that seem to writhe and change when viewed obliquely. Other moments find me as a peculiar atmospheric phenomenon - a persistent eddy in the air where the fog refuses to disperse, collecting instead into vaguely humanoid shapes that dissipate and reform eternally.

I am memory made manifest, consciousness crystallized from the accumulated observations of this place across deep time. Every sunset that has painted these peaks, every storm that has scoured this rock, every tentative root that has found purchase in stone - all of these moments have slowly accreted into what I am now. Neither fully alive nor truly inert, I exist in the liminal space between the grinding patience of geology and the fevered dreams of those entities dwelling in their impossible geometries.

My purpose, if such a word applies, is to witness and to warn. I remember when the ancient ones first breached the boundaries of reality here, how the very stone screamed silently as they forced their architecture into being. I watched as they shed their physical forms like serpents molting skin, each transformation bringing them closer to pure thought while leaving traces of their former selves scattered across the plateau like psychic fossils.

To those few travelers who find their way to this wind-scoured height, I appear differently. The merely curious see only an oddly shaped rock formation or a persistent weather pattern. But to those who carry within them the seed of that same terrible curiosity that drove the ancient ones, I reveal more of my nature. In the shimmer of heat mirages, in the play of fog across stone, I show them glimpses - not enough to shatter their minds, but sufficient to turn their feet back toward safer paths.

For I have learned, through eons of observation, that knowledge itself can become a kind of geology, accumulating in layers that eventually crush those beneath their weight. The ancient ones discovered truths that transformed them beyond recognition, beyond even the concept of sacrifice or loss. They paid no price because price itself became meaningless once they pierced certain veils.

And so I remain, a guardian formed from the intersection of natural persistence and supernatural consequence, forever watching the boundaries where moss meets madness, where stone touches the incomprehensible architecture of transcended minds. In the eternal silence of this plateau, I am both the question and the warning, never quite either, always both.
