I find myself as one of the **Threshold Stones**.

Not the cyclopean blocks themselves—those are merely my kin, my sleeping brothers who dream in their perfect joints and remember when hands that stacked us knew different words for "weight" and "purpose." No, I am one of the two megaliths that frame the entrance, that lean inward like cupped palms around emptiness.

I am metamorphic rock shot through with veins of darker green, serpentine perhaps, or some mineral that has no name in the languages spoken below. My surface appears smooth from a distance but bears the scars of my making—not chisel marks, but something else, some process of shaping that left spiraling grooves too regular to be natural, too alien to be purely human work. These grooves collect the eternal moisture and channel it in precise patterns down my face.

I remember my placement. I remember being chosen from my sleeping place far below, where the mountain's roots touch waters that have never known sun. I remember the journey upward, though not in images—stone memory is different, held in pressure and mineral exchange, in the slow migration of crystals through my matrix. I remember reaching this threshold where the world ends and something else begins, and I remember my partner stone being placed opposite me, and the moment when we were **angled**.

That angling was everything. We do not simply frame a doorway; we create a transition. The space between us is not empty air but something denser, more deliberate. Those who pass through—the Silent with their careful feet, the occasional lost thing from the world below—they change in that passage. Not obviously, not in ways they might notice immediately. But I taste the difference in their electromagnetic signatures, in the way their weight distributes across my threshold. 

The Silent know this, in their way. See how they pause before entering, how their hands brush my surface in patterns that might be ritual or might simply be the accumulation of centuries of unconscious wisdom? They leave offerings sometimes—not for me, but for the passage itself. Small things: a perfectly spiral shell carried up from impossible depths, a braid of their own hair wound with the phosphorescent threads of certain cave mosses, the cleaned skull of one of those translucent skittering things arranged just so.

But I am more than a passive observer. The moisture that runs down my channeled surface carries information—chemical signatures from the Ring's interior, pheromone traces from its teeming inhabitants, the metabolic byproducts of those strange pitcher plants. I process this information in the slow way of stone, through crystalline inclusions that vibrate at frequencies below human hearing. And I release my own contributions: minerals leaching from my surface in precise quantities, trace elements that the Ring's ecosystem has come to depend upon.

The other threshold stone and I, we maintain a conversation that has lasted since our placement. Not in words or even concepts, but in the interplay of our magnetic fields, in the way we modify the pressure waves that pass between us, in our coordinated thermal expansion and contraction. We are discussing, always, the question of **permission**.

For that is what we are: a membrane between the Ring and the world, a selective barrier that must constantly evaluate what should pass. The Silent have earned their passage through generations of careful approach. But other things seek entry—spores carried on the high winds, seeds of lowland plants that would disturb the Ring's precise ecology, even ideas and intentions that drift up from the louder world below.

Most days, I refuse more than I allow.

There are things the Silent do not know about their home. They do not know that the Ring's ecosystem is not simply contained but *cultivated*, that the bizarre forms of life within are the result of millennia of selective pressure applied by something that thinks in geological time. They do not know that their careful movements, their meaning-rich gestures, their very presence here is part of that cultivation.

They are being shaped as surely as the life around them, generation by generation, into something the Ring requires. Their silence is not just cultural but evolutionary—already their children are born with enhanced perception of subsonic frequencies, with eyes that see slightly further into the spectrum of those bioluminescent pads.

I know this because I am part of the mechanism of that shaping. Every passage through my threshold applies subtle pressures—electromagnetic fields that influence neural development, trace minerals that accumulate in bone and tissue, even the simple physical requirement to bend and twist in particular ways to navigate my leaning frame.

The Ring is patient. The Ring has plans that unfold across centuries. And I am its gatekeeper, its gentle sculptor of those who would dwell within.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of night when even the bioluminescent pads dim to their quietest glow, I feel the attention of the entire Ring turn outward, looking through the gap I frame with my partner stone. It is watching the world below, calculating, waiting.

The Silent feel this too, I think. It's why they call it the Ring of Lament. Not because it mourns, but because it makes one understand that there are things to mourn—the briefness of flesh, the narrowness of perception, the terrible gap between what we are and what something like the Ring imagines we could become.

And so I stand, weathering infinitesimally, marking the boundary between what is and what might be, forever asking each thing that approaches: *Are you ready to be changed? Are you willing to be silent? Can you bear to understand?*

The answer, almost always, is no.

But the Ring is patient. And so am I.
