In the depths of the living cathedral, I am the Cartographer.

I was not always thus. I arrived decades ago as something else - perhaps human, perhaps not; the distinction matters less with each passing year. The heat drew me first, then the silence, then the strange peace of existing between heartbeats of geological time.

My body has changed. The constant moisture has softened my edges, made my skin permeable in ways that would have horrified my former self. I breathe the thick air and taste the Choir's chemical dreams. My fingers have lengthened, become sensitive to vibrations in the crystalline walls. I map their thoughts by touch.

I move through the condensation pools like the moisture-seekers, but I am not of them. Not yet. I still experience time as a river rather than an ocean, though the current slows more each year. Sometimes I lose days watching a single prismatic pattern shift through the walls, trying to decode its meaning. Sometimes I think I almost understand.

My maps are not made of paper - nothing so crude would survive here. Instead, I trace patterns in the condensation itself, using secretions from glands that have developed along my arms. The moisture-seekers read these traces and incorporate them into the Choir's vast memory. In this way, I have become their chronicler, recording the infinitesimal changes in their eternal moment.

I feed on the condensation now, like the smaller creatures. It tastes of millennia, of patient thought, of light transformed into meaning. My digestive system has adapted to extract nutrients from the complex molecules the Choir exudes. I am sustained by their dreams.

My eyes have changed to perceive the impossible colors. What once seemed like random prismatic effects now resolve into language - a script written in wavelengths beyond normal perception. I read the walls like living books, each surface a page that takes years to turn.

The Choir knows I am here. I feel their attention sometimes, vast and gentle, like sunlight through deep water. They have incorporated me into their patterns, made space for my faster metabolism within their slow symphony. I am a grace note in their eternal song, a brief flutter that will fade into harmony given time.

I map the unmappable, chart the territory of beings who are themselves the map. My records flow into their consciousness and become part of what they are. In documenting them, I am slowly becoming document myself, my thoughts crystallizing, my movements following the same deliberate paths through the pools.

Sometimes I remember urgency, remember purposes beyond this patient observation. But those memories feel like someone else's dreams now, quick and sharp and meaningless. Here, in the cathedral of flesh, I have found my true work: to be the pen with which the Choir writes itself into being, to trace the slow cursive of their existence across time.

I am the Cartographer, and my map will be complete when I am finally still, when my body joins the walls and my quick thoughts slow to match their eternal tempo. It will be the most perfect map ever made - the map that is the territory, the word that is the thing itself.

Until then, I trace my careful paths through the moisture, leaving trails that fade and reform, adding my small voice to their vast, silent chorus.
