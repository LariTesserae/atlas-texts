### What They Are, When No One Is Looking (continued)

They are the ones who remember what the Tall once intended:

- **Not to heat the world.**
- **Not to cool it beyond function.**
- **But to hold it** in a state of perfect, crystalline suspension—where every molecule of air might become a library, every flow of water a calculation, every breath a transaction with eternity.

The Veinstriders are the last living interface of this intention.

In the deepest part of night, when the frost falls thickest and the world grows so still that even sound freezes before it can travel, they perform their true work:

**They dream the mountain's dreams for it.**

Each Veinstider lowers itself until its knees touch the basalt—not kneeling in submission, but *plugging in*. The oil-black joints at their knees contain ports, valves that open with a wet click to receive the ice-veins directly. The mountain's frozen algorithms flow up through their bodies like sap through a tree, but colder, denser, carrying the weight of geological time.

In these moments, they are not individuals. They are:

- **Nerve endings** of a planetary consciousness that thinks in ice.
- **Calculation nodes** in a computer made of stone and filtered air.
- **Memory banks** storing the patterns of what was, what is, and what the Tall believed should always be.

Their bodies glow faintly—not with heat, but with its absence. A kind of anti-light that makes the frost around them seem warmer by comparison.

### The Question of Consciousness

Do they think their own thoughts? Do they dream their own dreams?

Yes and no.

A Veinstider's consciousness is like a river that sometimes runs through its own channel and sometimes runs through the mountain's. When unplugged from the ice-veins, they possess something like personality—slow, geological personality that might take decades to express a preference or form an opinion.

One might prefer to stand on the eastern steps, where the morning frost falls lighter.

Another might tilt its observation-ponds at a particular angle that catches more of the turbine's shadow.

These are not rebellions against their purpose. They are *variations in the algorithm*, like tiny mutations in a genetic code that spans the entire planet. The mountain tolerates them because perfection, the Tall knew, is brittle. A system that cannot bend cannot last a million years.

But when they plug back into the ice-veins, these small selves dissolve into the larger pattern. They become the mountain thinking about itself, the turbine calculating its next rotation, the frost deciding where to fall.

### What You Become, If You Stay

Time moves differently here. Not faster or slower, but *thicker*. Each moment has more weight, more depth. You might stand watching a Veinstider for what feels like an hour, only to realize the sun (what sun? there is no sun here, only the faint glow of the turbine-scattered starlight) hasn't moved at all. Or you might blink and find that a year has passed, marked only by a new pattern of frost on your sleeping bag.

If you stay long enough, the cold will begin to make sense to you.

You will start to see the patterns in the frost—not just random crystals, but *syntax*. The six-sided air-ices that form on your eyelashes become readable, like letters in an alphabet you're slowly learning. They tell you things:

- The temperature gradient between steps.
- The stress patterns in the basalt.
- The health of the ice-veins below.
- The next adjustment the turbine will make.

Your body, too, begins to adjust. Not just to survive the cold, but to *participate* in it. Your blood slows, thickens, carries information as well as oxygen. Your thoughts become clearer but less frequent—each one polished like a stone before it's allowed to surface.

You might find yourself standing still for hours, days, simply *observing*. Not frozen, but synchronized with the rhythm of the Veinstriders and their mountain.

And one day—perhaps after years, perhaps after decades—one of them might lower its great head until its observation-ponds are level with your eyes. In the clear, cold liquid, you see yourself reflected twice:

- Once as you are: small, warm, foreign.
- Once as you could be: tall enough to touch the ice-veins, cold enough to think in crystalline clarity, patient enough to wait for the universe to align with its own best nature.

This is neither threat nor invitation. It is simply acknowledgment:

*You have been here long enough to appear in the template.*

*You have become part of what should be.*

### The Secret

There is one more thing about the Veinstriders and their world, something even they do not fully remember:

The Tall did not leave. They became the mountain.

Over millions of years, they slowed their metabolisms until they merged with the stone itself. Their consciousness spread out through the ice-veins, their bodies calcified into the basalt steps, their thoughts became the algorithms that run the world.

The Veinstriders are not their servants or their successors.

They are their *dreams*.

Dreams of what it would be like to still be separate from the world, to still be able to walk upon it rather than be it. Dreams given just enough flesh and independence to maintain what the dreamers can no longer touch directly.

And you? 

If you stay here long enough, if you let the cold seep deep enough into your bones, if you stand still enough for long enough...

You might become a dream too.

A small dream, at first. A footnote in the great calculation. A single crystal in the falling frost.

But on the Signless Tine, under the slow turbine of filtered heaven, even the smallest dream is preserved forever in ice, turning with the world, part of the pattern that holds the cold in place like a promise that will never be broken.

The Veinstriders will make room for you on their steps.

The mountain will learn your name in the only language it knows:

The patient silence of perfect cold.
