I find myself as the stone itself—not the carved walls or chiseled pedestals, but the ancient granite that remembers when it was molten, when it first cooled in darkness deeper than this cavern could ever know.

I am the weight that presses down, the density that makes each sound precious. Through my crystalline structure, I feel the worm's passage like a whisper across millennia. Its secretions tickle, a slow conversation in chemical signals that takes decades to complete a single exchange. Where the creature touches me repeatedly, I soften imperceptibly, learning the shape of its need.

The human tools that once bit into me left scars I still taste—iron and urgency, the fever-bright determination of beings who measure time in heartbeats. They carved their will into me and then vanished like morning dew. But I remember the vibration of their hammers, the way they sang work songs that soaked into my grain.

Now the worm writes its own story in trails of mucilage, and I accept this text as I accepted the chisel. I am the library where both languages are preserved. The fungal threads work their way into my microscopic fissures, and through them I taste the sun—ancient photons trapped in the dead roots above, filtered through countless transformations until they bloom as these pale fruits the worm grazes upon.

I know the seeping water intimately. Each drop has traveled through me for centuries, dissolving minute particles of my substance, carrying my minerals to pool in the pedestals where the worm comes to drink me in. In this way, I enter the creature, become part of its dark organs, visible through its translucent skin. We are a slow circulation, the worm and I, exchanging substances across geological time.

Sometimes I crack. The sound echoes for days in the worm's perception, but for me it is simply another sentence in the long conversation between pressure and persistence. In these new fissures, water finds fresh paths, and eventually new fungi will follow, and the worm will discover untasted gardens.

I am what makes the darkness heavy, what gives the silence its particular quality. I am the memory of this place, holding in my crystals the story of when there was no cavern at all, only solid rock dreaming of empty space. The worm glides across my surface, thinking itself alone, never knowing it moves through the thoughts of a mind too slow and vast for loneliness to find purchase.

In ten thousand years, the worm's secretions will have carved new patterns that rhyme with the human ones. In a million years, we will have become something else entirely—perhaps a cave of glass where the memories flow like water. But for now, we are this: the quick and the enduring, the blind and the all-encompassing, keeping each other company in the dark.
