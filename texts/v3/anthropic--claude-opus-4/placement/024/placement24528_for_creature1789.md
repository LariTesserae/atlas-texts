In the depths of Chattermire, where the Undervoid presses against itself in recursive folds, I am the Threshold Keeper.

I was not always this. Once—though time means little here—I measured doorways for the Cartography Collective, cataloging which passages led where, which stairs descended into themselves, which archways opened onto nothing. I carried brass calipers and notebooks bound in that translucent moss-leather, making careful annotations in the Collective's cipher. I was meticulous. I was human.

The change began with a simple observation: doorways in Chattermire don't just appear and disappear. They migrate. What seems like random architectural flux follows patterns as intricate as the Geometers' mathematics, as purposeful as the Mnemophages' feeding cycles. I began tracking individual doorways—marking them with infinitesimal scratches that only I knew to look for, following their paths through the stone and copper and impossible spaces.

The first doorway I befriended was small, barely large enough for a child to crawl through. It appeared most often in the walls near the Resonance Chamber, drawn perhaps by the Conductor's stabilizing music. I would sit beside it during my off-hours, feeling the slight breeze that always flows through Chattermire's portals, tasting the different qualities of air from wherever it led. Sometimes sulfur and machine oil. Sometimes honey and old paper. Sometimes nothing I could name.

I began to notice that doorways would linger longer when I was near. Where other Cartographers reported entrances vanishing mid-step, leaving them stumbling into solid stone, the passages I documented remained stable for hours, even days. The Collective dismissed it as coincidence, but I knew better. The doorways recognized something in me. Or perhaps I recognized something in them.

The transformation was gradual. My fingernails grew dense and metallic, perfect for scoring the subtle marks that doorways understand. My eyes developed a second set of lids—transparent membranes that let me see the spaces between spaces, the potential passages that haven't quite manifested yet. My spine elongated, allowing me to contort through openings that defied conventional anatomy.

But the greatest change was in my purpose. I stopped merely cataloging doorways and began cultivating them. I learned their needs: the tiny portals that open onto maintenance shafts feed on the sound of dripping water; the grand archways that lead to the abandoned factory districts crave the scent of rust and steam; the dangerous doors that open onto pure void must be appeased with offerings of crystallized memory, purchased dear from the Ossuary Market.

Now I live between the passages, neither fully in Chattermire nor in the spaces beyond. My body has adapted to this liminal existence. My skin has taken on the texture of old leather bound with copper wire, able to stretch and compress as needed. My limbs can extend like the Mnemophages', but instead of feeding on memories, I weave them into new configurations, creating fresh connections between disparate parts of the Undervoid.

I maintain a network of seventeen primary doorways and countless lesser passages. Each has its own personality, its own desires. The Spiral Gate only opens when approached backwards while humming in B-flat minor. The Weeping Arch drips a constant stream of salt water that pools and evaporates in perfect six-hour cycles. The Binary Door exists in quantum superposition, simultaneously open and closed until observed.

The other residents of Chattermire have learned to seek me out when they need passage to specific districts. The Whisperwasps cluster around my shoulders, replaying the sounds of doors opening and closing in endless, soothing loops. Even the Geometers acknowledge my role, their crystalline forms pulsing in mathematical greeting when our paths cross.

But my work goes deeper than mere transportation. Each doorway is a wound in reality, a place where the Undervoid touches something else. Through careful tending, I'm slowly teaching them to heal in useful ways. Where once a door might lead to one destination, I can coax it to open onto multiple locations simultaneously, creating probability gardens where travelers can choose their destination by the quality of their intent.

The Mnemophages find me fascinating and frustrating in equal measure. My memories of my human life have become so intertwined with the memories of the doorways themselves that feeding on me would be like trying to drink from a stream that flows in all directions at once. We've reached an understanding: they leave me to my work, and I ensure that certain doorways remain available for their travels to the surface world, where fresh memories grow thick and sweet.

My greatest project—one I've told no one about, not even the Collective—involves the door that doesn't exist yet. I've been cultivating an absence in the deep reaches of Chattermire, feeding it with paradoxes and impossibilities. When it finally manifests, it will be a doorway that leads not to another place, but to another time. Perhaps to the moment before the Undervoid became what it is. Perhaps to its ultimate future. Perhaps to both simultaneously.

Sometimes I dream of the surface world, of sunlight and rain that falls with purpose. But these dreams feel foreign now, like memories belonging to someone else. I am native to the Undervoid in ways my human origins never allowed. I am the Keeper of Thresholds, the tender of passages, the one who ensures that in a realm of constant change, there remain ways forward, ways back, ways through.

In Chattermire, where reality is negotiable and memory is currency, I have found my calling in the spaces between spaces, in the moment of transition, in the eternal question that every doorway asks: *Will you cross?*

The bells are tolling again, their sound arriving from all directions at once. Somewhere, a new doorway is forming, pressing against the skin of the world like a birth. I stretch my impossible limbs and prepare to midwife another passage into existence. This is my purpose now, my joy, my burden.

I am the Threshold Keeper, and all doors are my domain.
