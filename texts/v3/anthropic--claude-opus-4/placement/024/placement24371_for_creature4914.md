I am a Keeper of Echoes, dwelling within the hollowed chambers of what was once a great bell tower, now submerged and transformed into my resonance hall. My body has adapted over generations - elongated and ribbon-like, with membranes stretched between my limbs that vibrate with the water's every movement. My skin is translucent, revealing the intricate network of sound-sensing organs beneath, pulsing with bioluminescent patterns that mirror the symphonies I collect.

The tower I inhabit is one of the intimate pocket-scales you describe - a vertical shaft of carved stone where ancient bells still hang, now singing different songs as currents pass through them. I've woven my home between these bells with strands of cultivated algae, creating hammocks and nets that catch both the sparse nutrients and the precious sounds that flow through this space.

My purpose is to remember. In this world where natural substrate has vanished, I serve as a living archive of how things once were. Each sound I capture - the specific resonance of water moving through a particular carved channel, the way currents whistle past eroded edges, the deep thrumming of the built structures settling - I store within specialized chambers in my body. I can reproduce these sounds by flexing my membranes, creating a library of acoustic memories.

The octopi visit me sometimes, their color-conversations asking wordless questions about the time before. I play them the sounds of ancient tides, of rain on surface water (a phenomenon they've never known), of the grinding of natural stone that no longer exists. The dolphins bring me new sounds from distant reaches - the creaking of structures about to fail, the discovery of new pocket-chambers, the birth-songs of creatures adapting to fill ecological niches that never existed in any natural ocean.

My tools are simple, as befits this world's primitive technology - resonance stones I've shaped to amplify certain frequencies, woven nets that trap sound-bubbles, carved bone instruments that let me speak in the acoustic languages of different species. I maintain the bells above me with patient polishing, ensuring their voices remain clear.

In the loudness of this world, I am both participant and curator. My song joins the symphony while simultaneously preserving it, a living echo of all that was and all that continues to become in this strange, constructed sea.
