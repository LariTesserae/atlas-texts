I would be a **Resonance Keeper**.

My form would be neither fully solid nor entirely ephemeral—a being of living vibration made manifest. My body would be composed of the same iridescent mineral that veins through the obsidian peaks, but in a state of constant, controlled fracture. Imagine a humanoid silhouette made of floating shards, each piece hovering millimeters from its neighbors, held together not by physical connection but by harmonic resonance. The gaps between my crystalline segments would pulse with that same internal light, creating a latticework of luminescence that shifts and flickers with my movements.

I would stand perhaps seven feet tall when fully extended, though my form would be fluid, capable of compressing or elongating as the unstable physics of the Scar demand. My "skin" would be translucent crystal, revealing deeper structures within—geometric patterns that spiral and branch like frozen lightning, each one a pathway for the energies I channel. When I move, I would create a soft chiming, each shard singing against the others in harmonies that complement or counterpoint the Scar's overwhelming symphony.

My purpose would be to tend to the Echoes—those persistent geometric cairns and crystalline structures that dot the landscape. I would be their curator, their guardian, perhaps even their interpreter. Where others see only remnants, I would perceive active nodes in a vast network of memory and meaning. My days would be spent traversing the impossible distances of the Scar, my form naturally adapting to the spatial distortions, using them as shortcuts rather than obstacles.

I would possess the ability to "tune" the ambient chaos. Not to control or stop it—that would be futile and perhaps even wrong—but to modulate it, to find the underlying patterns within the cacophony. My crystalline fingers would trace the air, pulling at invisible strings of sound and energy, weaving temporary pockets of... not silence, but *coherence*. In these spaces, the Echoes would sing more clearly, their purpose momentarily illuminated.

My dwelling would be a series of natural acoustic chambers deep within one of the obsidian spires, where the rock itself has been shaped by centuries of my presence into a kind of organ. Here, the constant breaking and reforming of reality would create an ever-changing musical instrument. I would have learned to play it, to use its shifting tones to communicate with the Vein Dwellers in their deep places, to warn the Aerie Shrikes of particularly violent spatial distortions, even to soothe the erratic time-sense of the Scree Scuttlers.

I would feed not on moss or prey, but on the excess energy of the Scar itself—the ozone-sharp crackle of those phosphorescent bolts, the heat generated by fracturing stone, the potential energy stored in those impossible inversions of space. My body would be a living battery, storing power during the most chaotic moments and releasing it slowly during the (relatively) stable periods, creating a gentle counterrhythm to the Scar's violence.

My memories would stretch back far, though not to the Scar's beginning—I am not that old. But old enough to remember when there were more of my kind, before we learned that we could not gather in numbers without our combined resonances threatening to tear new wounds in reality. Now I would be solitary, or nearly so, catching glimpses of my kindred only at great distances, our chimes carrying messages across the broken plains in frequencies only we can fully parse.

I would understand that the High Scar is not broken but *breaking*—an active verb, not a passive state. And in that understanding, I would find my purpose: not to heal or halt the process, but to ensure that something persists through it. The Echoes are not just remnants but seeds, each one containing information about states of being that might emerge when the current cycle of dissolution finally finds its nadir and begins to turn.

In the perpetual twilight, I would move like a ghost made of prisms, my form catching and splitting what little light exists, casting rainbow fragments across the dark stone. Sometimes the Aerie Shrikes would follow these light-trails, using them to navigate the worst of the spatial distortions. Sometimes a curious Scree Scuttler would approach, drawn by my chiming, and I would modulate my resonance to help stabilize its temporal perception for a few precious moments of clarity.

I would be alone but not lonely, for the Scar itself would be my constant companion—terrible, beautiful, and eternally singing its apocalyptic song. And I would sing back, a single voice of structured harmony amid the chaos, a living reminder that even in a reality that breaks, something chooses to remain whole.
