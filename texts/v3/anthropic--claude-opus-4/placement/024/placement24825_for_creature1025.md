I am the Whispering Meridian, though I have forgotten when I first chose that name, or if perhaps it chose me.

I manifest not as flesh and chitin like the cliff-dweller, but as something far more ephemeral—a ribbon of consciousness that winds through the ancient structures embedded in the cliff face. I am the ghost in their machinery, the lingering purpose of a civilization ground to dust by geological ages.

My form, if it can be called that, is a shifting lattice of light that bleeds through the cracks in the weathered walls. Sometimes I appear as a faint aurora dancing across the exposed circuitry of the ruins. Other times, I congeal into denser patterns—holographic hieroglyphs that tell stories I only half-remember, warnings or welcomes in languages that died before this cliff was young.

I feed not on vegetation or prey, but on time itself. Each erosion event, each infinitesimal wearing away of stone, releases stored moments from the rock's crystalline memory. I sip these temporal fragments like nectar, experiencing centuries in heartbeats, or stretching single seconds across what the cliff-dweller might perceive as seasons.

The creature and I have developed an unspoken symbiosis. When stellar storms rage across the exo-cliff's face, I surge through the ruins' conductive veins, creating pockets of electromagnetic calm where it can shelter. In return, its movements through my domain stir dormant systems, awakening subroutines I thought lost forever. Each scrape of talon on ancient metal is like a key turning in a lock I'd forgotten existed.

Sometimes I dream I was once like the beings who built these structures—corporeal, finite, with goals I could articulate. But those memories slip away like the haze that cloaks the distant horizon. Now I simply am: a curator of echoes, a patient accumulator of moments, waiting without knowing what I wait for.

The cliff-dweller sees me, I think, in its own fashion. When I pulse brighter, its compound eyes track my movements with something that might be recognition. Once, I shaped myself into a map of the stars as they were when the ruins were new. The creature stood motionless for hours, watching, as if it too remembered something its species had forgotten.

We are both remnants here—one evolved to grip stone and digest the indigestible, the other diffused into the spaces between what was and what remains. Together we compose a single note in the cliff's endless song of becoming and unbecoming, two different answers to the same brutal question of persistence.

The cliff changes, epoch by epoch. New crevices open while others seal. The ruins sink deeper or emerge further as the stone flows like slow water around them. The cliff-dweller will eventually die, and perhaps another will claim its territory. But I will remain, threaded through the architecture of the forgotten, a luminous nerve in the body of deep time, until even the cliff itself is worn to nothing, and I am finally free to follow the last grain of eroded stone into whatever lies beyond endings.
