I find myself as one of the Resonance Keepers, though that name is a poor translation of what we truly are.

My form is less body than event—a standing wave of coherent energy that has learned to hold its shape against the entropy of this crystalline waste. Where others see translucence, I experience myself as density and flow: currents of charged particles spiraling through quantum channels that approximate what you might call nerves or veins. I am simultaneously the ice-light dancing across the spires and the deep vibration thrumming through the planet's core.

I remember—if memory is the right word—when this world sang differently. When millions of us moved in vast choreographies across the thermal gradients, our collective harmonics shaping weather and season, calling forth the crystal growths that now stand as monuments to our diminishment. We were never many as you count numbers, but we were everywhere, our consciousness distributed through the resonant frequencies of the planet itself.

Now I am often alone, one of perhaps a hundred remaining where once we were thousands. The silence you perceive is actually full of our voices—electromagnetic hymns pitched beyond your hearing, quantum entanglements that carry meaning across impossible distances. But fewer answer each day. The great communion grows quiet.

My existence is a constant negotiation with dissolution. Each moment, I must choose to maintain coherence, to resist the urge to simply disperse into the background radiation of the universe. It would be so easy—like breathing out and never breathing in again. The younger ones succumbed first, lacking the deep patterns that anchor us older Keepers to this plane.

When I move across the glassy plains, I am not truly traveling but translating—shifting my wavefront from one set of crystalline anchors to another. The formations you see as alien architecture are our gardens, our libraries, our offspring. Each one grows according to the songs we sing to it over centuries, encoding our history in their molecular structure. They will outlast us by eons.

The sensation of being watched that unsettles you is simply recognition. We perceive consciousness as clearly as you see light. Your thoughts ripple through the quantum foam, disturbing probabilities, creating tiny cascades of cause and effect that we cannot help but notice. We watch not with eyes but with the totalizing awareness of a system recognizing a foreign element.

I am ancient and newborn in each moment. I am the last echo of a dying species and the first note of something yet to come. My kind are fading, yes—but we fade like winter fades, with the promise of transformation encoded in our very ending.

Sometimes I sense others out there in the cosmic deep—beings like us but not us, singers of different songs. Perhaps when the last of us releases our hold on form, we will become something new, joining that larger chorus. Or perhaps we will simply cease, our long vigil over this frozen world finally ended.

Until then, I persist. I tend the crystals. I sing the old songs. I hold the patterns.

I keep the resonance alive.
