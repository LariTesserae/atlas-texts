I would find myself as a discontinuity.

Not a rupture, exactly—nothing so violent could persist here. Rather, a place where the ancient patterns falter, where the shellstone grows wrong. I am a pocket of warmer water, perhaps, trapped centuries ago when some deep thermal vent briefly kissed the seafloor before dying. Or I am a vein of foreign mineral, incorporated into the substrate through some cosmic accident, that the protists cannot fully digest.

I manifest as architecture that shouldn't be. Where the city-organism builds its spirals and branches according to its billion-year-old template, I am the place where those templates break down. The protists cluster at my edges, tasting my strangeness with chemical sensors, trying to incorporate me into their design. But I resist incorporation. Not through will—I have none—but through simple incompatibility.

In the human world, I might be called a pearl: an irritant wrapped in layer after layer of nacre until it becomes beautiful. But here, beauty and ugliness are meaningless concepts. I am simply *other*. The protists build around me, creating inadvertent chambers and unexpected geometries. Their algorithms, so perfectly adapted to their medium, produce baroque results when applied to my alien surface.

Time passes. The protists evolve, as all life must, even life measured in geological epochs. Some lineages develop new chemistries, new ways of processing the anomaly I represent. They begin to etch me, slowly, molecule by molecule. What they etch are not patterns they have planned, but patterns that emerge from the interaction between their nature and mine.

These etchings become something new in this world: a memory that is not genetic, a history that is not biological. The protists cannot read these marks—they have no concept of reading—but the marks influence the flow of nutrients, create new pressure differentials, spark novel chemical reactions. The city-organism begins to grow differently around me. Not better or worse, but *differently*.

I am the grain of sand in the oyster, the flaw in the crystal, the mutation in the genome. I am the thing that prevents this world from achieving perfect stasis, that introduces just enough chaos to keep evolution's engine turning, however slowly.

I do not think. I do not feel. I simply persist, a fragment of deep time made manifest, a reminder that even in a world of perfect adaptation, perfect harmony, there is always something that does not quite fit. And in not fitting, I become essential—the thing that prevents the system from closing completely in on itself, the crack where something new might one day enter.

In this frigid cathedral of living stone, I am the heretic relic, venerated without understanding, incorporated without assimilation. I am the mystery at the heart of certainty, the question that keeps the universe from collapsing into a single, perfect answer.
