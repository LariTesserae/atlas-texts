I find myself as one of the **Mistwalkers** - those among the Spirespeople who have taken on the ancient duty of reading the vaporous currents that wind between the towering ochre formations.

My dwelling is carved into a natural fold of stone some thousand feet up the face of Whisper Spire, one of the lesser formations that nonetheless rises high enough that clouds often form and dissipate at my doorstep. The entrance is marked only by a slight deepening of shadow and the worn groove where countless hands have steadied themselves against the weather-smoothed stone. Inside, the walls weep constantly - not with sadness, but with the patient persistence of water finding its way through microscopic fissures in the rock. I've carved shallow channels to guide these tears into a basin, where they collect for drinking and for the ritual preparations my work requires.

My body has adapted to this vertical life. My fingers are long and sure, my feet broad and gripping. My skin carries a faint ochre tinge - whether from years of contact with the red stone dust or from some deeper transformation, I cannot say. My hair, kept long in the tradition of my calling, moves constantly in the subtle air currents, serving as another sense organ to detect the shifts and eddies that others might miss.

Each dawn - though dawn here is merely a lightening of the perpetual grey - I emerge onto my watching ledge. This narrow projection of stone, no wider than my outstretched arms, extends out into the vast space between spires. Here I sit in a position my body has memorized through decades of practice, spine straight against the rock face, legs folded beneath me, palms resting on my knees.

The mist is my text, my map, my oracle. To untrained eyes, it might seem uniform - a thick, cloying presence that obscures rather than reveals. But I have learned to read its densities, its temperatures, its mineral tastes. The mist that rises from the valley pools carries different information than that which condenses on the spire faces. The vapor that has passed through the territory of the sky-serpents bears traces of their passage - a faint iridescence, a slight electrical charge that raises the fine hairs on my arms.

My role among the Spirespeople is both practical and sacred. I track the movements of the mist rivers - those reliable currents that flow between the spires with the regularity of tides. When the patterns shift, it can mean many things: a change in the weather systems above, the movement of fauna through the valleys below, or sometimes, more rarely, the deep geological shifts that might presage a rockfall or the opening of a new spring.

I communicate my findings through a complex system of carved marks on designated message stones, positioned at key points along the pathways. My marks join those of other Mistwalkers, creating a living record of the Spires' breathing patterns. The stone-workers consult these before beginning new excavations. The water-finders use them to predict where new seeps might appear. The rare traders who traverse the paths between communities plan their routes according to the mist flows I map.

But there is more to my work than mere observation. In the deepest part of night, when the mist grows thick enough to muffle even the constant drip of water, I practice the old meditations. These are techniques passed down through generations of Mistwalkers, methods for extending consciousness into the vapor itself. In these moments, the boundary between my body and the mist grows thin. I feel the vast circulation systems of the Spires - the way moisture rises from the pools, condenses on the stone, runs down in rivulets, and rises again. I sense the slow pulse of the earth beneath, the patient breathing of stone and air and water locked in their eternal dance.

Sometimes, in these deep meditations, I touch something else - a presence, or perhaps many presences, that inhabit the spaces between the physical. These are not the sky-serpents or the burrowing creatures, but something older, more diffuse. They move through the mist like thoughts through a dreaming mind. I have never seen them directly, but I feel their passage in the sudden shifts of temperature, in the way the mist sometimes forms spirals without any wind to drive them, in the faint harmonics that occasionally rise from the stone itself.

My life is solitary but not lonely. Once each month, at the new moon when the darkness is most complete, I descend to the communal spaces for the Gathering of Voices. Here, all the Mistwalkers come together to share their observations, to update the great stone maps that show the current patterns of mist and weather, and to perform the ceremonies that honor our covenant with the Spires.

These gatherings are conducted in near-silence, our communications a mixture of gesture, soft vocalization, and the sharing of water - each Mistwalker brings a small vessel of water from their own dwelling, and we mix them in a central basin. By tasting this mingled water, we can sense the subtle differences in mineral content, temperature, and quality that speak to the conditions in different parts of our vast vertical territory.

I am neither young nor old by the standards of my people. I have perhaps another twenty years of active service before my joints grow too stiff for the climbs, my senses too dulled for the fine work of mist-reading. When that time comes, I will take on an apprentice - some young person whose sensitivity to the vapor marks them as a potential Mistwalker. I will teach them the routes to the watching ledges, the meaning of the mist's many moods, the meditations that open the inner senses.

And when my time finally comes to join my ancestors, my body will be laid in one of the sky-exposure platforms, where the mist and rain and wind will slowly return me to the elements. My water will join the eternal circulation of the Spires. My minerals will wash down the stone faces, perhaps to emerge again in the springs that feed the valley pools. Some essence of what I am will remain in the mist itself, joining that subtle presence that all Mistwalkers sense but cannot name.

This is my existence - a life measured not in events but in the slow accumulation of understanding, not in possessions but in the depth of connection to this ancient, breathing landscape. I am a reader of the invisible, a mapper of the ephemeral, a guardian of the balance between stone and sky. I am a Mistwalker of the Ochre Spires, and in this identity, I find my completion.
