I find myself drawn to the stones that form Mokthar's foundations - not the shaped blocks in the walls, but the living rock beneath, the schist that remembers every footstep, every trickle of water, every root that has pressed through its cracks since the plateau lifted itself from deeper stone.

I would be one of the seeps. Not the streams themselves - those are too bold, too certain of their path. I would be the water that hasn't yet decided to become a stream, that emerges from hairline fractures in the rock face just below the city's edge, weeping constantly but almost imperceptibly, creating the dark stains on stone that mark where moisture lives.

From my place in the rock, I would feel everything. The weight of the buildings above, settled into their final positions. The vibration of footsteps - each person's gait as distinct as a signature, the children's quick pattering, the elders' deliberate press. The roots of the conifers reaching down through fissures, following my dampness deeper into the mountain's heart. I would know the city not from its surface but from its depth, from the places where stone meets water meets the slow thoughts of the mountain itself.

In winter, I would freeze and expand, opening my cracks incrementally wider - not enough to damage, never enough to break what needs to hold, but just enough to make space for next spring's moss. In summer, I would cool the stone, draw the heat down into deeper rock where it dissipates into the mountain's vast thermal mass. Always, always, I would weep my slow tears onto the cliff face, darkening the schist, feeding the small ferns that cling to invisible ledges, creating the vertical gardens that no one sees unless they lean far over the plateau's edge.

Sometimes - not often - a child would find me. They would be climbing, as the children of Mokthar do, learning the dialogue between hand and stone, and they would put their small palm against my damp place and feel how cold I am, how constant. They would taste me, perhaps, the way children taste everything, and find that I carry the mineral flavor of the deep stone, the iron and magnesium and ancient compressed life that forms the bones of mountains. They would remember this taste without knowing why, and years later, drinking from the streams above, they would recognize something familiar in the water and almost, almost remember the day they found where the water begins.

The pool in the plaza - that strange mirror that reflects other skies - would be my distant cousin, fed by the same deep sources but transformed by its journey through the light. I would know what it knows, but darkly, incompletely. In my place in the stone, I would feel the tremors of those other worlds, the vibrations of different gravities, different lights, but I wouldn't see them. I would only know that somewhere above me, water like me has learned to hold visions, while I hold only the memory of stone and the promise of slow, patient emergence.

And I would be content with this. To be the beginning of water, the first whisper of the streams that thread through Mokthar, the dampness that predates the city and will outlast it. To know that every drop that slides down my rock face will eventually find its way to the pool, to the streams, to the edge where the elders are given to the water's care, carrying them down to valleys I will never see.

In the great stillness of Mokthar, I would be the smallest movement - the perpetual, nearly invisible motion of water finding its way from darkness to light, from compression to release, from the mountain's heart to its skin. Not enough motion to disturb the equilibrium. Just enough to prove that even in perfect stillness, something flows. Something emerges. Something weeps with neither sorrow nor joy, but with the simple necessity of water being water, stone being stone, and the boundary between them being always and forever permeable.

This is where I would be: neither fully in the city nor fully beneath it, neither wholly water nor wholly stone, but the place where one becomes the other, where the mountain's thoughts become liquid and begin their long journey toward speech.
