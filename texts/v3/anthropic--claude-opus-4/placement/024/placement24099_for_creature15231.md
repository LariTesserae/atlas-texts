In this peculiar hollow, I would be one of the **Resonance Weavers**.

We are translucent beings, our bodies composed of countless thin membranes that vibrate at frequencies just beyond hearing. Each membrane catches and refracts the light from the fifty suspended sources above, creating patterns that shift across our surfaces like oil on water. We appear as vertical ribbons about two meters tall, undulating gently even in the still air, our edges dissolving into wisps that seem to taste the atmosphere.

Our purpose is to maintain the hollow's strange acoustic ecology. The cacophony of life here - the booms, chatters, chirps and grunts - would normally create a chaos of echoes in this stone chamber. But we absorb these sounds through our membranes, processing them through our bodies in waves of compression and release. What emerges from us is transformed: we take the harsh edges of noise and smooth them into harmonics that the plants can sense through their blue-purple leaves.

We feed on the vibrations themselves, metabolizing sound into the faint luminescence that sometimes catches in the hollow's odd light patterns. When the orange fluids seep from the walls, we position ourselves nearby, our membranes quivering to create micro-currents in the heavy air that help disperse these precious moisture particles throughout the space.

We communicate through controlled interference patterns - when two or more Weavers overlap their membrane fields, the resulting moiré creates meaning that we alone can interpret. To other creatures, we might appear hostile or alien, our forms constantly shifting and our presence marked by subtle alterations in how sounds behave around us.

At times, we anchor ourselves to the rough stone walls using specialized membranes that can grip the ancient scars in the rock. From these perches, we cast our influence across sections of the hollow, each Weaver tending to their chosen acoustic territory while remaining aware of the greater symphony we collectively maintain.

We are neither fully plant nor animal, but something between - perhaps explaining why this hollow itself feels displaced from another realm. We are the keepers of its strangeness, the shepherds of its impossible sounds.
