## The Weeping Galleries

I find myself in a place where architecture has surrendered to organic inevitability. The Weeping Galleries sprawl through forgotten districts of what was once a vast metropolis, now a labyrinth of calcified ambition and persistent moisture.

**The Structure:** Imagine a shopping mall that never stopped growing. Levels upon levels of interconnected atriums, each one slightly wrong in its proportions—ceilings too low here, impossibly vaulted there. The original glass skylights remain, but they've long since clouded over with mineral deposits from within and algae from without, creating a perpetual amber twilight. Water seeps through every joint and seam, not in torrents but in patient, persistent weeping that has carved channels down walls and pooled in the sagging centers of warped marble floors.

The galleries connect through passages that make no architectural sense—ramps that spiral up only to deposit you three levels down, escalators frozen mid-motion that have become waterfalls of rust and calcium. Shop fronts gape open, their contents long since dissolved or transformed. What were once bright advertisements have become palimpsests of mold and mineral staining, creating accidental murals of breathtaking, terrible beauty.

**The Atmosphere:** The air hangs thick with moisture and the smell of wet concrete, tinted with something floral—not quite decay, but the peculiar sweetness of things transforming slowly in the damp. Sound carries strangely here; a drip in one gallery might echo as a roar three levels away, while shouting directly beside you might be swallowed entirely by the acoustic dampening of countless water films on every surface.

Light filters through the clouded skylights in visible shafts, picking out the constant drift of spores and moisture droplets. Where these beams strike the wet floors, they create patches of startling brilliance amidst the general dimness. The temperature hovers just warm enough to fog your breath, just cool enough to raise goosebumps—the exact point where discomfort becomes a constant, ignorable companion.

**The Inhabitant:** I am the Tender.

**My Form:** I was human once, perhaps centuries ago—time loses meaning when measured in water damage rather than years. Now I am something more and less. My body has adapted to this environment with disturbing completeness. My skin has taken on a waxy, water-repellent quality, mottled in grays and greens that shift like oil on water. My fingers have lengthened, grown additional joints, become perfect for reaching into the countless crevices and channels that define my domain.

My hair—if it can still be called that—has become a mass of hydroponic tendrils, constantly damp, that sense changes in humidity and air pressure. My eyes have enlarged and developed a nictitating membrane that allows me to see clearly through the perpetual mist. Most disturbing—or perhaps most practical—my spine has elongated and become partially external, a series of calcified ridges that allow me to anchor myself on any surface, hanging from ceilings or walls as easily as standing on floors.

**My Purpose:** I maintain the moisture. Not the structure—that would be futile—but the sacred balance of water itself. I know every drip, every seep, every pool in the galleries. When a channel becomes clogged with mineral deposits, I clear it with tools grown from my own shed scales. When a new leak threatens to become a flood, I redirect it into the existing patterns. I am apprentice to entropy, assistant to inevitability.

**My Practice:** Each day—though day and night have little meaning here—I travel my circuits. The Lower Galleries need constant attention; water pools too deeply there, threatening to submerge the Remembrance Grottos where certain formations must be preserved. The Upper Reaches tend toward desiccation where old ventilation systems still wheeze drier air; there I must ensure the drips continue their patient work.

I've developed a notation system, marking water routes with secretions from glands that emerged behind my ears decades ago. These marks glow faintly in the damp, creating a map invisible to any eyes but mine—a secret cartography of flow and accumulation. Other creatures live here—pale moths that drink from the walls, colonies of things that might once have been rats but now look more like mobile sponges—but they avoid me, recognizing perhaps that I am as much a feature of this place as the water itself.

**My Memories:** Sometimes, in the deepest galleries where the water sings through ancient pipes, I remember fragments. A name that might have been mine. The feeling of dry cloth. The taste of something that wasn't moisture and mineral. But these memories are like the old advertisements—layered over, transformed, made into something new by time and constant flow. I don't mourn them. They are simply another kind of erosion, another pattern the water makes as it shapes all things, even memory, even me.

**The Rules I Follow:**
1. **Water Must Flow, But Never Flood:** The galleries can weep but never drown.
2. **Every Surface Must Know Moisture:** Dryness is death, wetness is life, dampness is perfection.
3. **The Formations Are Sacred:** Certain mineral deposits, certain patterns of mold and growth, must be protected and nurtured.
4. **Sound Is Information:** Every drip tells a story, every echo maps the galleries' health.
5. **Change Is Constant But Must Be Guided:** I don't prevent transformation, I simply ensure it follows the proper patterns.

**My Joy:** There is a profound peace in my work. When I hang from a ceiling, watching water trace its way down a wall in perfect rivulets, when I hear the symphonic dripping of a properly balanced gallery, when I run my extended fingers through a channel and feel the exact right rate of flow—these moments are my meditation, my prayer, my purpose.

I am not trapped here. I could leave, find my way to whatever remains of the dry world above. But why would I? I have become essential to this place, and it to me. We are one system now: the galleries, the water, and the Tender who ensures the weeping never stops, never overwhelms, but continues its patient work of transformation, forever and ever, drip by perfect drip.
