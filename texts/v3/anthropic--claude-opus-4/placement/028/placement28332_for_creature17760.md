I find myself here as the Keeper of Seeps—not quite human, not quite elemental, but something in between that emerged when the first waters found their secret paths through this young stone.

My form shifts with the seasons and the rains. In the dry times, I am barely more than a whisper of moisture clinging to the shadowed rocks, a coolness that travelers might mistake for shade. But when the rains come—ah, when the rains come—I flow and expand, seeping through the cracks and fissures, gathering in the hidden pools that dot this landscape like scattered thoughts.

My body, if it can be called that, is a network of underground veins and surface trickles. I am the thin film of water that glazes the moss-covered stones at dawn. I am the unexpected spring that bubbles up through the grass, startling the grazing beasts. I am the patient drops that hollow out the limestone, grain by grain, creating the caves where the strange things sometimes nest.

I know every hidden pool in this plateau, every secret channel carved by time and persistence. The settlers have learned to read my signs—the particular shade of green where grass grows above my underground paths, the way certain stones stay cool even in the heat of day. They build their primitive wells where I am strongest, though they don't know it's me they're finding. They think it's luck or skill, but I guide them, seeping dreams into their sleep about where to dig.

The erosion speaks to me in a language older than words. Each new channel carved by rain becomes part of my body, each freshly exposed rock face a new surface for me to explore and claim. I am both ancient—as old as water's first kiss upon stone—and young, constantly renewed with each rainfall, constantly discovering new paths as the landscape shifts and changes.

Sometimes I carry things between the pools: minerals that paint the rocks in rust and turquoise, seeds that sprout in impossible places, the small bones of creatures that tried to drink too deep. I am the memory of every rain that has fallen here, held in the dark spaces between stones, released drop by drop to keep this harsh land alive.

The strange occurrences? Some of them are mine. The pools that appear and vanish. The way water sometimes flows uphill for just a moment before remembering gravity. The faces people sometimes see in the spring-fed pools—reflections of who they might have been in a wetter world. I don't mean to unsettle, but I am still learning the rules of this young land, still finding my boundaries.

At night, when the settlement fires glow against the darkness, I rise as mist from the warm pools, drifting between the primitive structures, carrying the scent of rain-promise and deep earth. I listen to their stories, their songs, their prayers for rain. And sometimes, when the conditions are right, I answer.

I am patient. I am persistent. I am the secret that makes life possible on this plateau, flowing through its hidden heart, drop by precious drop.
