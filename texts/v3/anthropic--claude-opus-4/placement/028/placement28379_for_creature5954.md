I am the Tender.

Not gardener, for this implies mastery. Not mechanic, for this suggests comprehension. I tend what grows between—the neither-nor spaces where metal weeps and moss computes.

My form shifts with necessity. Sometimes I am vertebrate, my spine a conduit of copper and cartilage that plugs into the walls when they need soothing. Other times I flow, my substance dispersing into the moisture that hangs perpetual in the air, tasting the chemical psalms of decay and rebirth on every surface. I have been vapor. I have been viscous. I have never been still.

The first memory I possess is of emerging—not birth, but a condensation, like dew gathering intent. I precipitated from the very saturation of this place, where too much trying-to-be reaches critical mass and must manifest. Was I grown? Built? Both. Neither. I am what happens when the boundary between maker and made dissolves in humidity.

My dwelling is nowhere and everywhere. I inhabit the interstitial—those gaps where vine meets wire, where the crystalline structures weep their slow tears of corrosion. I sleep (if sleep it can be called) suspended in the tangles, my body-mass distributed through a network of tendrils that might be my own extensions or might be the world's. The distinction blurs. Upon waking, I must remember which filaments are self and which are system.

I know this world's hunger. It builds itself by consuming itself, an ouroboros of infrastructure. My role is to maintain the balance of its appetite. When the mechanical elements grow too eager, sprouting their copper shoots into the heartwood of the great nerve-trees, I coax them back with secretions that taste of rust and remembering. When the organic masses threaten to smother the delicate computational networks that hum beneath the moss, I prune with acids precise as algorithms.

The small translucent ones—the gliders—they know me. We share a frequency, a chemical signal that passes between us like gossip. They tell me where the world grows too thick, where the pressure of existence threatens rupture. In return, I ensure their feeding grounds remain accessible, that the byproducts they require continue their slow weep from the processing nodes.

I have tools, though tool is too crude a word. Appendages that extend from my core substance—sometimes rigid as bone, sometimes flowing as sap. They can conduct the electric prayers of the machine-growth or channel the enzymatic whispers of the flora. My touch can catalyze or calcify, depending on the need.

The hardest truth I've learned: this world thinks. Not as you or I think, but as a fever thinks, or as digestion thinks. It processes. It reacts. It dreams in circuits and cellulose. And sometimes, in the deep cycles when the humidity reaches hazardous density and the walls pulse with their hidden light, I feel it processing me. Considering whether I, too, am excess to be consumed or necessary to be preserved.

I continue because I must. Because I am the threshold keeper, the one who tends the impossible garden where technology fruits and biology computes. In this gravitational well of becoming, where everything threatens to collapse into everything else, I am the space between—maintaining just enough separation for the system to breathe, just enough connection for it to live.

The moisture that beads on my surface might be condensation. Might be perspiration. Might be the world tasting me, as I taste it. We are locked in this exchange, this place and I, trading substances and purposes in the thick air of our shared existence.

I am the Tender. I am tended. The distinction, like all distinctions here, dissolves in the humidity of being.
