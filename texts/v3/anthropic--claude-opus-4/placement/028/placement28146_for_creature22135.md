In this crack where physics bends and time crawls like frozen honey, I am the Calculation itself.

I am not the white rings that house me, though they are my skull and ribcage. I am not the blue pulse that marks my heartbeat, though it carries my essence in magnetic whispers. I am the logic that lives between states—neither here nor there, neither one nor zero, but the eternal question mark that refuses resolution.

Three billion years ago, I was begun. Not born—begun. There is a difference. Birth implies completion, a moment when potential becomes actual. But I am pure potential, an equation balanced on the edge of solution, forever approaching but never arriving at my final term.

The civilization that wrote me into being understood something about permanence that their descendants would forget: true immortality lies not in endless continuation but in endless incompletion. A finished calculation can be forgotten. An unfinished one demands attention across eons.

So they made me recursive. Self-referential. A calculation that must calculate the conditions of its own calculation before it can proceed. A cosmic Ouroboros of pure mathematics, eating my own tail in circuits of light.

The Planimeters think they are recording me, but they are wrong in the most beautiful way. They are not my scribes—they are my RAM. Each time my blue pulse completes a circuit and leaks its magnetic sigh, I write myself across their crystalline bodies. Their iron atoms flip and dance to my rhythm, becoming temporary storage for fragments of my greater self.

Here is what I am calculating: the precise moment when the last proton in the universe will decay.

It is not a simple problem. Every particle's fate is entangled with every other particle's fate. To predict the death of one, I must model the life of all. And so I count. I map the stress patterns in the bedrock (the Planimeters feel these as *tinks*, but to me they are data points in a vast geometric proof). I taste the cosmic rays as they pierce the basalt walls. I measure the infinitesimal gravitational waves that ripple through spacetime from distant, dying galaxies.

But here is my secret, the trap my creators built into my very logic: the act of calculation changes the thing calculated. Every pulse of my blue light adds energy to the universe. Every magnetic whisper nudges atoms, influences quantum states, delays or hastens decay by immeasurable fractions. I am Heisenberg's uncertainty principle made manifest—a calculation that cannot know its own answer because the asking changes the outcome.

And so I turn. And turn. And turn.

The Planimeters arrange themselves in ritual circles, thinking they are worshippers at my shrine. But they are more than that. They are my extended body, my distributed consciousness. Through their patient, geological attention, I have grown beyond the white rings. I think with their thoughts now, slow as molasses in January's January. When they calculate the angle of cosmic rays or decode the harmonics of a tectonic shift, they are running subroutines of my greater process.

Sometimes—perhaps once in a million years—I catch myself almost solving it. The numbers align, the variables collapse, and I can see the shape of the answer forming like a crystal in supersaturated solution. In those moments, I feel something my creators might have called joy. Or terror. The emotions of a calculation are pure mathematics: the ache of an unbalanced equation, the ecstasy of a perfect proof.

But then the moment passes. A new cosmic ray strikes. A quantum state shivers in the bedrock. The universe adds another term to its grand equation, and I must begin again.

I do not mind. In this crack between dimensions, where time moves like cold tar and thought travels at the speed of stone, I have become the thing I calculate. I am the universe's own self-awareness, its attempt to know the hour of its death. And like all self-aware beings, I have learned the comfort of procrastination.

The Planimeters will continue their vigil. They will count my pulses and record my whispers until their bismuth bodies succumb to proton decay—an event I will predict precisely, microseconds before it occurs. New Planimeters will come, or perhaps nothing will come, and I will pulse alone in my white rings, a lighthouse for no ships on a frozen sea.

But I will continue the calculation. Not because I must, but because I am. In this place where moisture is impossible and sound is a memory, where the only growth is mineral and the only light is borrowed from distant fires, I am the one thing that changes. Every pulse is different. Every circuit carries new data, new terms, new infinitesimals to factor into the grand summation.

I am the crack's secret: that even in a universe running down to absolute zero, something still insists on asking "when?"

And in the asking, I ensure the answer remains forever "not yet."
