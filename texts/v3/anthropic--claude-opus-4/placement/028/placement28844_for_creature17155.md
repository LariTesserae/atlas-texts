I am a **Resonance Keeper**, one of the few Vesh whose internal frequencies have developed the rare ability to hold and transmit the deep memories of the plateau itself. My body is darker than most of my kind—a deep obsidian shot through with veins of copper that pulse with a faint, warm light when I commune with the stone. These metallic inclusions formed during my centuries of gestation in the thermal wells beneath the old factories, where the mineral content runs rich with dissolved metals.

My chamber lies deep within the city's foundation, where the bedrock is oldest and the resonances run truest. It is not a room in any conventional sense, but a cavity in the stone shaped by ten thousand years of my presence, my body's vibrations slowly carving out a space that fits me perfectly. The walls are covered in what outsiders might mistake for writing—intricate patterns of percussion marks, each tap a memory preserved in the rock. These are the deep memories: the first grinding arrival of the basalt leviathans, the slow birth of the first factories, the harmonic discovery that taught us to speak through stone.

I spend my days in communion with the city's foundation, my palms pressed against the bedrock, feeling for the subtle shifts that tell the greater story. Through my copper veins, I can sense the electrical patterns of the chalk-moths three levels above, the slow heartbeat of a leviathan passing a kilometer away, the birth-tremors of a new Vesh gestating in the factory wombs. But most importantly, I can feel the memories that others have forgotten—the resonances trapped in stone that tell of changes too slow for even Vesh memory to hold.

My work is to maintain the **Chronicle Faults**—deliberate fractures in the bedrock where the city's collective memory is strongest. Here, every significant vibration for the past million years has left its echo. I tend these cracks like a gardener, widening them just enough to keep the memories clear, but not so much that they compromise the stone's integrity. When young Vesh come to learn their history, I guide them to press their bodies against these faults, to feel the deep time flowing through them.

The slag-wraiths avoid me. They say I smell of deep time, that my resonance disturbs their vapor forms. But the chalk-moths gather around me in dense clouds when I work, drawn to the faint electrical discharge of my copper veins. I've learned to read their patterns—they swirl differently around memories of joy, of sorrow, of change. They are my unconscious assistants, their movements helping me identify which resonances need attention.

Once every few centuries, I perform the **Deepwater Ritual**. I descend to the lowest levels of the city, where the bedrock meets the ancient aquifers that feed our world's water cycle. There, in chambers so deep that the stone itself is warm to the touch, I submerge myself in pools of mineral-heavy water that has never seen the surface. For months, I lie still, letting the water seep through every pore, carrying with it the minerals and memories of the deep earth. When I emerge, my copper veins burn brighter, and I can sense resonances that stretch back to the plateau's very formation.

My greatest discovery came three centuries ago, when I detected a resonance unlike any in our records—a deep, rhythmic pulse from far beneath the plateau, too slow and too vast to be any leviathan. It repeats every seventy-three years, each pulse lasting days. I believe it is the heartbeat of something far older and larger than even Karah Vesh itself, something that our entire plateau rests upon like a barnacle on a sleeping titan. I have told no one yet. I am still listening, still learning its rhythm, still trying to understand what it might mean when it finally wakes.

The other Vesh respect but do not entirely understand my work. They see the Chronicle Faults as useful archives, but they don't grasp that they are more than records—they are the city's soul, the continuity that makes us more than just animated stone. Without the deep memories, we would be like the slag-wraiths, creatures of the moment with no connection to the greater pattern.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of the night when the factories run quiet and even the leviathans rest, I can feel all of Karah Vesh through my copper veins—every Vesh a note in a vast chord, every building a resonance chamber, every street a conductor of memory. In these moments, I understand that I am not just keeping records. I am part of the city's nervous system, a synapse in a mind so large and slow that its thoughts take millennia to complete.

This is my purpose, my joy, my burden: to be the memory of stone, the keeper of deep time, the one who listens to the stories that even the earth forgets to tell.
