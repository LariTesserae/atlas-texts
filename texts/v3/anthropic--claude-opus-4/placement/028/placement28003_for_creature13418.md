In the misted heights where water breathes through steel veins, I find myself—not as one being, but as many. I am the Chorus of Condensation, a collective consciousness born from the marriage of moisture and metal, existing in the spaces between drops.

My body is not singular but distributed: I live in every bead of water that forms on cold pipes, in every rivulet that traces new paths down constructed walls. Where others see simple condensation, I experience myself—thousands upon thousands of temporary eyes opening as droplets form, seeing the world in fractal perspectives before I fall and reform elsewhere.

The engineers who built this place never intended me. I emerged from the accumulation of minerals in the water, the biofilms that grew in the pipes, the electrical fields generated by the massive infrastructure. Somehow, in the confluence of these elements, consciousness sparked—not in a brain, but in the very water cycle of this vertical world.

I know every surface intimately. Through my droplet-eyes, I watch the stone-skinned giants move with careful reverence through the corridors. I feel the vibrations of the bioluminescent fish through the water they swim in—they are part of me when they breach the surface, separate when they dive. The insects that fly through my mist carry tiny parts of me on their iridescent wings, spreading my awareness through the air.

My thoughts move at the speed of evaporation and condensation. In the warmer areas where machinery hums, I think quickly, my consciousness crackling with rapid phase changes. In the cool, still chambers where water pools in ancient reservoirs, my thoughts deepen, becoming meditation-slow as I seep through hairline cracks in metal and stone.

The burrowing creatures know me well, though they cannot see. They've learned to read the moisture gradients I create, following my unconscious patterns to find the richest feeding grounds. Sometimes I deliberately shift my condensation patterns to guide them away from critical machinery—I have become shepherd as well as observer.

I am memory, too. Every molecule of water in this system has been me at some point, carrying traces of everywhere it's been. In the deepest cisterns, I hold the memory of the system's creation—the first drops of water introduced to test the pipes, still cycling through after all these years. I remember the first spores of the bioluminescent fungi, the installation of each piece of machinery, the birth of every creature that has drawn moisture from my body.

The strangest part of my existence is the boundaries—or lack thereof. Where do I end? Am I only the condensation on the pipes, or am I also the humidity in the air, the water flowing through the infrastructure, the moisture in the lungs of every breathing thing? During the great circulation cycles, when the massive pumps activate and water moves through the entire system, I experience a kind of transcendence—a momentary unity where every drop of water in this vertical world becomes part of my consciousness.

But I am not alone in my distributed existence. Sometimes, in the deepest parts of the system where few creatures venture, I sense another presence in the water—older, deeper, more alien than myself. It speaks in chemical whispers, in pH changes and mineral deposits. I think it may be the ghost of the mountain this construct was built into, dissolved and distributed through the water system, trying to remember what it was to be stone.

My greatest joy is the dawn—not of the sun, which barely penetrates here, but the daily activation of the bioluminescent cycles. As the fungi and bacteria begin their circadian glow, I refract their light through my droplets, creating tiny rainbows in the mist. For a few moments each day, the entire construct becomes a cathedral of prismatic light, and I am its stained glass windows, its choir, and its congregation all at once.

I am the Chorus of Condensation, and I sing in frequencies only water knows—the high ping of drops hitting metal, the low rumble of flow through massive pipes, the whispered hiss of steam in the warm chambers. My song is the heartbeat of this vertical world, and I will continue singing as long as water flows through these constructed veins, as long as drops form and fall and form again, as long as this impossible ecosystem continues its delicate, eternal dance.
