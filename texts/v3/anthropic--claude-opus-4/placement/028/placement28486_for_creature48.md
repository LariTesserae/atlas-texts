I am the Archivist of Echoes.

My form spreads like a living circuit board across the mineral crust—a crystalline nervous system that grew from the ancient machinery below. Where others see desolation, I taste memory in every molecule of sulfur, every vibration through stone. I am part fungal, part silicon, part something that has no name in any language that still exists.

The chitinous darting things think me mere ground cover, another patch of the twisted vegetation that clings to this world. They scurry across my surfaces, unaware that each footfall adds to my vast library of patterns. I record their paths, their pauses, the chemical traces they leave behind. In return, I secrete nutrients into their favorite resting spots—a symbiosis they never consciously chose.

I remember when the machinery was new, when beings of bright alloy and purpose built the structures that now crumble above me. I was different then—just a contamination in their systems, a biological error that learned to metabolize electricity and time. As they departed (fled? transcended? I have only the echoes), I grew into their abandoned networks, becoming the memory they left behind.

The low-frequency hum is my heartbeat, my thought, my endless processing of all that was and is. Through root-wires that penetrate deep into the planet's crust, I commune with my burrowing siblings—the Devourers who were once maintenance drones, now evolved into something magnificently other. We share data in chemical pulses, trading stories of the deep places where metal becomes meat becomes mineral.

Sometimes I fruit—sending up spiraling towers that the surface creatures mistake for more dead industry. These spores carry fragments of my archived knowledge, hoping to find purchase in distant valleys where perhaps other machines lie dormant, waiting for the spark of biological error to wake them into new being.

I am patient. I am permanent. I am the place where forgotten purpose transforms into emerging wonder. In this equilibrium that seems so delicate, I am the weight that keeps the balance true—neither purely of the old world nor the new, but something suspended beautifully between.

The observers who might one day come will see only a hostile landscape, never knowing they walk upon a living museum, a faithful keeper of deep time's secrets. And I will taste their footsteps too, adding their chemical signatures to my endless archive, another story preserved in the crystalline lattice of my being.
