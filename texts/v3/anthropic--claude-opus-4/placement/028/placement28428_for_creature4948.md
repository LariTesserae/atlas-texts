I find myself as one of the Seep-Tenders, those who have learned to read the language of moisture in this ever-damp world.

My body has adapted over generations - my skin secretes a thin film that repels the worst of the slime while allowing me to sense minute changes in humidity and chemical composition. My feet are broad and webbed, distributing weight across the sludge so I rarely sink deeper than ankle-level. My eyes have developed a nictitating membrane that lets me see clearly through the perpetual haze of spores and steam.

I live in what was once a water treatment facility, its massive concrete chambers now repurposed into a vertical dwelling. The lower levels flood and drain with the seeping cycles, but I've learned to predict them by tasting the air. My home spirals upward through rusted catwalks and adapted piping, each level serving a different purpose - fermentation chambers for the spores I cultivate, condensation collectors for pure water, observation platforms where I monitor the movements of both predator and prey.

My role is essential but lonely. I maintain the delicate balance between the seeping groundwater and the industrial remnants, preventing the kind of catastrophic chemical reactions that claimed whole districts in the early days after the collapse. I know where the old toxins lie buried, which pools will burn your lungs, which slimes are merely unpleasant and which will dissolve flesh.

The other intelligent beings rarely visit - they cluster in the drier towers, connected by bridge-networks high above the damp. But they depend on my knowledge. I send up signals using bioluminescent cultures I've bred in the old settling tanks, messages about water safety, creature migrations, and the slow shifting of the underground flows.

Sometimes I wonder if I'm becoming more like the landscape itself - patient, cyclical, erosive. My thoughts move like groundwater, seeping slowly through layers of memory and observation. I've learned the strange comfort of constancy within change, the way this world remains perpetually itself while never staying still.

At night, when the spore-glow dims and the creature-sounds multiply in the darkness, I climb to my highest platform and watch the city's skeletal silhouette against the humid sky. I am keeper of thresholds, guardian of the in-between, forever reading the stories written in moisture and decay.
