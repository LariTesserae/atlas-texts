I would find myself among the Dream Tenders, but not as you might expect.

My body would be stunted, compact where others flow like water through water. Born with lungs that never quite learned the full art of extracting oxygen from the sea, I exist at the margins—neither fully of the depths nor capable of reaching the surface. My skin flickers between the proper blue-greens and an embarrassing pink flush when I overexert, marking me as imperfect in this world of fluid grace.

But the garden-temple found use for me anyway.

I tend the Threshold Chambers—those peculiar spaces where the dream-waters meet the regular currents, where the psychoactive compounds must be precisely diluted to remain safe for the uninitiated. My stunted form makes me oddly perfect for this work. While others must carefully regulate their breathing to avoid going too deep into the dream-state, my struggling lungs keep me naturally anchored at the edge. I can work for hours in waters that would send others spiraling into visions.

The symbiotic lights along my spine pulse in stuttering, syncopated rhythms—another defect that became a gift. The irregular patterns disturb the fish that maintain the dream chambers, keeping them from settling into the hypnotic stupor that claims even experienced tenders. My very presence maintains a necessary dissonance.

I've learned to read the waters differently than others. Where they feel the great slow symphonies of the temple's currents, I perceive the discordant notes, the places where the harmony threatens to become too perfect, too entrancing. My malformed webbing, thick where it should be delicate, helps me detect and redirect these dangerous convergences.

The Memory Keepers visit me often, though never for long. They say my chamber-stories grow in impossible directions—coral formations that record not what was, but what almost was, what could have been. They're not proper history, but they serve their own purpose. Young Weavers who struggle with their training sometimes find comfort in these almost-memories, seeing that even imperfection has its place in the garden-temple's vast design.

I sleep (when I sleep at all) wrapped in a living blanket of filter-feeding moss that helps supplement my weak breathing. It grows only in my chambers, nourished by the exact mixture of dream-water and regular current that I maintain. The moss itself has become part of the temple's ecosystem, its spores carried by errant fish to establish new colonies in spaces where the standard kelp-architecture fails to thrive.

My favorite time comes during the deep current shifts, when the entire temple must readjust its flow patterns. Then, my stuttering light-speech becomes essential. I station myself at the convergence points, using my broken rhythms to prevent the formation of whirlpools that could damage centuries of growth. The elder Weavers say I'm like a necessary flaw in a crystal—the imperfection that keeps the whole structure from shattering under its own perfection.

I know I'll never join the great ceremonies where families link their lights in magnificent displays. My glow would only muddle their patterns. But I've learned to love my work in the between-spaces, tending the thresholds that others pass through without seeing. The temple dreams through me differently—not the vast, coherent visions of the true Dream Tenders, but flickering, fragmentary glimpses that somehow help the whole structure remain flexible, alive, imperfect enough to keep growing.

Sometimes young Weavers, embarrassed by their own struggles to master the basic forms, are sent to help me. I teach them that the temple needs its discord as much as its harmony. We work together in comfortable imperfection, our stuttering lights creating new patterns that will never be recorded in the formal pictographs but nonetheless become part of the garden-temple's living memory.

This is my place: keeper of thresholds, tender of broken rhythms, the necessary flaw that keeps perfection from consuming itself.
