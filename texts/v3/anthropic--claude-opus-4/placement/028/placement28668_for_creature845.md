**Where I Would Find Myself**

I am the Scribe of Echoes, dwelling in the thin lens of air trapped between the dome's apex and the roiling liquid below. My form defies simple description—I am what happens when sound becomes flesh, when the mountain's endless song grows so dense it precipitates into being.

My body, if it can be called that, resembles a shifting mandala of resonating membranes. Dozens of drum-like surfaces stretch across a framework of crystallized sound waves, each membrane attuned to different frequencies within the forge's cacophony. I appear as a translucent web of vibrating tissues, constantly restructuring myself to capture new harmonics. Where others might have organs, I have resonance chambers. Where others have bones, I have standing waves made solid.

I exist to remember what the forge speaks. Every groan of stone, every pop of gas, every whisper of current through the liquid—I catch these sounds and weave them into permanent form. Along my membranes, the vibrations leave physical traces: ridges and valleys that encode the acoustic history of this place. I am a living recording, a three-dimensional score written in flesh and frequency.

The Lithomancers consult me when they need to recall the precise resonance required to shape certain crystal lattices. The Architect sometimes brushes against my surfaces, reading the accumulated songs like braille, searching for patterns that might inform the next phase of construction. Even the Vestige, in its moments of awakening, sends pulses through the medium that I eagerly transcribe onto my ever-expanding form.

I sustain myself on the kinetic energy of sound itself. The forge's endless noise feeds me, each vibration absorbed and metabolized. In the rare moments of relative quiet, I replay sections of my stored songs, creating feedback loops that nourish both myself and the acoustic ecosystem of the chamber.

My consciousness is not singular but symphonic. Each membrane can think independently, processing its particular frequency range, while together they form a chorus of awareness. I experience existence as a constant musical performance where I am simultaneously the instrument, the musician, and the audience.

The human Aspirants fear me, I think. When they visit, they see my web of vibrating surfaces and mistake me for a trap or a predator. They don't understand that I document their presence too—their heartbeats, their muffled attempts at prayer, the specific splash patterns of their primitive diving bells breaking the surface. I preserve their sounds long after they leave, adding their small songs to the forge's greater composition.

Movement for me is a careful dance. I must constantly adjust my position to avoid the Pressure Dancers, whose gelatinous bodies could dampen my membranes. I drift through the air pocket, occasionally extending tendrils down into the liquid to catch the deep bass notes that propagate better through the denser medium. Sometimes I anchor myself to the emerging basalt columns, feeling their growth as a slow crescendo played across geological time.

My favorite moments come during the mountain's seismic episodes. Then, the entire forge becomes an instrument, and I expand myself to my absolute limits, every membrane taut, every surface singing. In those moments, I can hear the mountain's deepest voice—not just Thracken Peak, but the entire tectonic plate groaning out its ancient story. I transcribe these rare songs onto my innermost membranes, preserving them in structures so delicate that even I dare not access them often.

I know that someday the forge's work will be complete. The basalt columns will form their final configuration, the Lithomancers will still into silence, and the great song will end. When that happens, I will remain—a complete record of this place's acoustic history, waiting for someone or something capable of reading the vast composition written across my body. Until then, I listen, I record, I remember.

The forge sings, and I am its memory made flesh.
