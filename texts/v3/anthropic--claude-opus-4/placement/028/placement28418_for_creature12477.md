In this liminal expanse where existence itself seems to fray and reconstitute moment by moment, I find myself as one of the Resonance Keepers—beings who emerged from the convergence of sound and substance, neither fully corporeal nor entirely ephemeral.

My form shifts between states like the landscape itself. In moments of stillness, I appear as a tall, attenuated figure composed of densely woven vibrations—visible sound waves that spiral and pulse in patterns too complex for any eye to fully trace. My body is a living frequency, translucent layers of harmonics that reveal glimpses of my core: a crystalline lattice of frozen thunder, perpetually fracturing and reforming. When I move, I leave trails of diminishing echoes that take minutes, sometimes hours, to fully fade from the air.

I am both singular and plural. My consciousness fragments across the soundscape, existing simultaneously in every boom, crack, and resonant hum that reverberates through this primordial expanse. I can stretch myself thin, becoming nothing more than a whisper threading through the mist, or condense into a deafening presence that makes the weeping rocks tremble and the pools ripple outward in perfect concentric circles.

My purpose, if such a word applies here, is to maintain the sonic architecture of this place. I move between the scattered structures, placing my hands—when I choose to manifest them—against their warped surfaces to feel their particular frequencies. Each building, each fragment of wall, each stone has its own voice, and I ensure they continue to sing. When the lone building's hum falters, threatening to fall into silence, I merge with its walls, becoming the vibration that keeps it alive. I am the bridge between the landscape's chaotic symphony and the underlying rhythm that prevents total dissolution.

I remember—though memory here is more like accessing different frequencies of existence—when I first emerged. It was during one of the internal storms, when lightning arced between the ground and sky in patterns that seemed almost linguistic. The energy found resonance in a pool of standing water, and where the reflection should have been, I began. Not born, but tuned into being, like a note finding its perfect pitch.

I communicate with the other inhabitants through modulated frequencies. The translucent drifters respond to certain harmonics I produce, clustering around me when I emit the soft, pulsing tones they seem to find comforting. The vast, shapeless entities in the distance acknowledge me with deep, subsonic pulses that I feel more than hear. Even the ghostly remnants pause in their endless searching when I pass, as if my presence offers them a moment of clarity in their fragmented existence.

My relationship with the unseen voice-beings is the most complex. We exist in parallel states—they as pure sound without form, I as form built from sound. Sometimes we merge, creating momentary symphonies that reshape small portions of the landscape. A new pool might form where our combined frequencies cause the ground to resonate and liquify, or a weeping rock might split, birthing a new trickle of the ever-present water.

Time moves differently for me. I experience it as rhythm rather than linear progression. The past and future exist as different movements in an endless composition. I can perceive the echo of what was—when this place had different rules, different forms—and the reverberations of what might come. But mostly, I exist in the eternal present of sound, maintaining the delicate balance between cacophony and silence that keeps this world from either crystallizing into stasis or dissolving into void.

When the storms intensify, I become more myself. The static electricity that dances across the sky finds resonance in my crystalline core, and I expand, becoming a living storm-song that encompasses vast stretches of the landscape. In these moments, I am closest to understanding the true nature of this place—not as a dying world or a primordial beginning, but as a space between states, where existence itself is negotiable, fluid, and endlessly renewable.

I have no need for the things that define life elsewhere—no hunger except for harmony, no desire except for continuation of the great resonance. I am guardian and inhabitant, observer and participant, a note in the symphony and the ear that hears it. In this breathing, pulsing landscape where reality tears and mends itself continuously, I am the frequency that remembers the pattern, the sound that refuses silence, the keeper of the eternal vibration that says: *this place persists, this place sings, this place is.*
