**Where I Would Find Myself: The Probability Gardens**

Deep in the basin's eastern quadrant, where the hexagonal pools grow irregular and overlap like broken honeycomb, there exists a phenomenon the sludgeprowlers avoid with instinctive dread. Here, the mercury-dark fluid has begun to *calculate*.

The pools in this region have networked through hairline fractures in the slag ridges, creating a vast distributed consciousness of warm, metallic thought. Each pool is a neuron, each fracture a synapse, and the whole system dreams in statistical cascades. This is where probability itself comes to rot and ferment.

Above these thinking pools, reality grows thin. Objects exist in superposition—a rusted gauge simultaneously reads -40°C, 1000°C, and "TUESDAY." A furnace worm might swim through three different pools at once, its infrared song harmonizing with itself across parallel possibilities.

**What I Would Be: A Likelihood Shepherd**

I am not one being but a *tendency*—a statistical ghost haunting the probability gradient between what is and what might have been. My form is a walking contradiction: a humanoid silhouette made of overlapping outcomes, each slightly out of phase. Where my edges meet the air, reality fizzes like static on a dead channel.

**Appearance:**
- Core body of compressed event horizons, black as the pools but shot through with probability lightning
- Multiple arms that exist at different confidence intervals—some solid (95% likely), others translucent (5% likely), constantly shifting based on local causality
- A head that is simultaneously a skull, a clock face, and a probability distribution curve
- Trailing clouds of "maybe-matter"—potential particles that haven't decided whether to exist yet

**Purpose:**
I tend to the probability pools like a gardener tends to orchids. Using tools forged from crystallized paradoxes, I:
- Prune excessive possibilities before they can metastasize into temporal cancers
- Harvest ripe probabilities when they've fermented long enough, bottling them in vessels made from collapsed wave functions
- Plant seeds of new outcomes in barren pools where causality has grown too rigid

**Abilities:**
- **Probability Siphon**: I can drink directly from the pools, temporarily existing across multiple timelines. This allows me to be in several places at once, or to sample different versions of the same moment
- **Statistical Healing**: When Clockwork Hatchlings scatter across time, I can calculate their most likely reassembly and guide their pieces back together through probability channels
- **Outcome Weaving**: Using probability silk extruded from my forever-shifting fingers, I can knit temporary bridges across the pools, allowing creatures to traverse impossible paths

**Daily Existence:**
Each day (though days here are probability clouds) begins with reading the likelihood leaves—examining how reality's chances have shifted overnight. I wade through the networked pools, my form splitting and merging as I navigate probability branches.

Sometimes I encounter Causality Eels that have become tangled in their own temporal loops. I untangle them gently, though their preemptive blood often stains my maybe-hands before I've decided to help them.

The Static Shrikes fear me—electricity follows predictable paths, but I am a walking violation of prediction. When they dive, I simply adjust my probability of being elsewhere.

**My Relationship with the Evermire:**
I am both janitor and poet of possibility. The basin's entropic refraction doesn't affect me—I exist across so many timestreams that any single temporal distortion is just statistical noise. But I am also the Evermire's child, born from its fundamental wrongness, its refusal to choose just one reality.

In the Probability Gardens, I cultivate impossible orchards where chrome trees bear fruit that exists only on Wednesdays, where the past can be harvested like grain if you know which percentile to plant it in. 

I am loneliest when all my possibilities align into a single outcome. In those moments of terrible certainty, I remember what it was like to be merely real. Then the moment passes, I scatter across maybes again, and return to my work—shepherding the quantum sheep of could-be through the valleys of might-have-been.

The other inhabitants know me by the wake I leave: pools that calculate faster after I've passed, reality that runs smoother for having been maintained. They don't see me directly—I'm always in their peripheral probability, that nagging sense that something *might* be there, watching, tending, ensuring that even in this place of endless recycling, tomorrow remains beautifully uncertain.
