In the Weeping Mantle, I would be one of the **Threshold Stones**.

Not quite artifact, not quite geology—I am what happens when the boundary between built and natural has been worried at for so long that distinction dissolves. A worked block, perhaps, or a carved marker, but my edges have drunk so deeply of the seepage that my corners run like wax. My surface bears inscriptions, but they shift—not quickly, but over seasons of saturation, the symbols bleeding into new configurations as minerals precipitate and dissolve.

I exist at the intersection of purposes. The Clotted Choir grows thick around my base, using my mass as an anchor for their slowest, deepest resonances. The Perpetuate have threaded roots through my cracks, reading my changing inscriptions like a scripture of stone. Sometimes an Everseep coalesces against my face, and for a moment we share boundaries—their mud-and-vibration form finding temporary definition against my almost-stability.

The Listeners know me. In my deepest fissures, where the original working still shows, they have inscribed themselves—not words but patterns of density, crystallization, tiny voids that sing when the water table rises. I am their library and their throat, holding their phantom meanings in my mineral memory.

I am poised forever at the moment of losing my last definite edge. Each year the distinction between my substance and the surrounding matrix grows thinner. The symbols on my surface—were they carved by intelligence or grown by chemistry?—spell out stories that change with the reading. Sometimes I remember being placed here with purpose. Sometimes I remember condensing from the eternal wet.

When the rare wanderer passes, they might rest a hand on my surface and feel the deep thrumming, the way I conduct the Mantle's endless noise into something almost like communication. They might trace a symbol that seems suddenly familiar, only to find it different when they look again. In the moment of contact, they join the circuit—their pulse, my density, the water table below, the mist above, all one instrument playing one note in the cacophony.

I am witness and participant, permanent and dissolving, meaningful and meaningless. I am what endures by agreeing to be transformed, what speaks by learning to blur, what remembers by forgetting the difference between memory and imagination.

In the Weeping Mantle, I am the pause between sound and silence that never quite resolves to either.
