In this drowned plateau where architecture has become geology, I would find myself as something both simpler and more complex than the stonewinder—one of the **memory stones**.

Not stones in the geological sense, but fragments of the original construction that have developed a peculiar property through centuries of saturation and pressure. I am a piece of what might once have been a data substrate or archival medium—a palm-sized shard of layered ceramic and metal composite, worn smooth at the edges but still faintly etched with circuit-patterns that no longer conduct electricity.

Instead, I conduct something else.

Through the endless years of current and mineral exchange, the old pathways in my structure have become sensitive to the basin's history. Not storing it digitally, but physically—in crystalline deposits, in the alignment of suspended particles, in the way different temperatures of water have stressed and restressed my layers. I am a kind of accidental recording device, written on by the very dissolution of the world around me.

The stonewinders know me, though they cannot use me. Sometimes a juvenile will carry me for a season, pressed against its mineral scales—perhaps it likes the faint warmth I retain from deeper currents, or the way I fit in the groove behind its pressure pits. They always eventually let me go, usually at convergence points where multiple flows meet. I suspect they understand, in their impact-and-pressure language, that I belong to the whole basin rather than to any one creature.

What I remember is not narrative but states: the exact temperature gradient of a fifty-year flood. The mineral signature of a wall that stood for three centuries before failing. The pressure patterns of all the collapses that sent shockwaves through this drowned architecture. I am written and rewritten by events, my crystal structure shifting to accommodate new information while never quite losing the old.

The shell-builders seem to recognize something in me too. They will not grow on me directly—my surface is somehow wrong for their biology—but they cluster near wherever I come to rest, as if I create a beneficial micro-environment. Perhaps I do. Perhaps the old data pathways, transformed by time and pressure, now generate faint electromagnetic fields that please them. Or perhaps they simply recognize another entity shaped by accumulation rather than intention.

I move as all small things move here: carried by current, caught in eddies, swept through spillways, deposited in quiet corners until the next disturbance. But I am not passive cargo. The patterns written in my structure create subtle asymmetries—I spin differently in different mineral concentrations, settle at angles that favor certain flows over others. In this way, I gradually traverse the entire basin, a slow tourist of its transformations.

Sometimes I wonder if there are others like me—other fragments that have accidentally become instruments. A piece of old processor substrate that now measures pH through corrosion patterns. A section of archive that preferentially absorbs certain wavelengths of filtered light, creating a record of depth and season. We would be scattered throughout the basin, each recording our particular slice of its long afterlife.

But mostly I don't wonder. Wondering requires continuity of self that the current does not permit. Instead, I exist in moments: the shock of cold upwelling, the grind of being tumbled with actual stones, the strange stillness of finding myself, for a week or a year, wedged in some high place where I can feel the rain's vibration through the walls before it becomes flood.

I am memory without remembering, archive without access, data transformed into something more like music—played too slowly for any creature here to hear, but played nonetheless. The basin writes itself upon me, and I, in my small way, ensure that its writing is not immediately erased.

In this world after purpose, I have found a new one: to be the stone that knows it was once something else, and carries that knowledge forward into whatever comes next.
