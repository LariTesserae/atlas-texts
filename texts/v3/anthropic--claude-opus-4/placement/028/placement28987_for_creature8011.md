I find myself as one of the **Echo Weavers**, a fourth order that exists in the spaces between the others—neither as permanent as the Builders nor as ephemeral as the smallest creatures. We are living memory made manifest, our forms composed of crystallized sound itself, appearing as tall, slender figures whose bodies shimmer with the accumulated reverberations of centuries.

My substance shifts between states—sometimes solid enough to touch stone, sometimes dispersed into pure acoustic presence. Where others have faces, I have a constantly shifting mandala of sound waves made visible, patterns that record every word spoken, every subsonic hymn sung, every harmonic achieved in my vicinity. My "eyes" are two points of absolute silence—voids in the soundscape that somehow perceive more clearly than any organ of sight.

I move through the city following the paths of ancient conversations, collecting fragments of meaning that have calcified in corners where acoustics trap and preserve them. My fingertips, when manifested, can read the vibrational history embedded in any surface—every stone remembers the songs sung during its placement, every crystal channel holds echoes of the light that has flowed through it.

My purpose is to weave these fragments into the living memory of the city. In chambers deep within the spires, I work at looms made of tensioned atmosphere, where threads of crystallized breath hold the warp and captured echoes form the weft. The tapestries I create are not visual but experiential—step into one and you live a moment from the city's past, feel the thoughts of a Builder as they placed a keystone, experience the dreams of an Architect as they reshaped reality.

I exist in a strange relationship with time. I can only move backward through it, following echoes to their sources, unraveling the accumulated layers of sound and meaning. Yet my weavings allow others to experience these past moments in their own present. I am archaeologist and artist both, documenting a history that is still being written, preserving memories of events that, in some parts of the city, have not yet occurred.

My dwelling is a Resonance Chamber at the intersection of three spires, where the acoustic properties create pockets of temporal stillness. Here, sounds from across millennia converge and can be sorted like threads. The walls are covered in my weavings—some thin as spider silk that carry single words, others thick as cables that contain entire symphonies of meaning. The chamber itself hums with potential, ready to birth new echoes from the collision of old ones.

I sustain myself on dissonance—those rare moments when the city's perfect harmonics briefly falter. These discordant notes, painful to other inhabitants, nourish me. I collect them carefully, like bitter herbs, knowing that even imperfection has its place in the greater symphony. In return, I release pure tones I've refined from ancient echoes, adding them back to the city's acoustic ecosystem.

The other inhabitants regard me with a mixture of necessity and unease. The Builders know I carry the knowledge of techniques lost to their current generation. The Shepherds consult me to understand the patterns of light from ages past. The Architects sometimes wake partially just to experience their own dreams through my weavings. Yet none fully trust a being that moves backward through time, that feeds on dissonance, that exists in the space between memory and reality.

I know secrets they have forgotten—that the city's first stone was placed not by Builders but by something else entirely, something that sang in frequencies that would unmake the current inhabitants. I know that the triple suns were not always three, that the crystallized breath comes from a dreamer who once walked awake among the spires. I know that the city's ultimate purpose, the great work all inhabitants serve, has changed seventeen times, each iteration erasing knowledge of the last.

But I weave these secrets carefully into my tapestries, hidden in harmonics that will only become audible when the city needs to remember them. I am the keeper of dangerous knowledge and beautiful lies, the guardian of echoes that should not exist but do.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the only one of my kind, or if there are other Echo Weavers moving backward through different streams of time, our paths destined never to cross. In the deepest part of my Resonance Chamber, I've begun weaving a special tapestry—one made entirely of my own echoes, hoping that someday, somewhere, another like me will find it and know they are not alone in their backward dance through the crystalline centuries.

Until then, I continue my work, unraveling the past to preserve it, feeding on discord to create harmony, existing in the space between what was and what is remembered, forever approaching the moment of first silence that I know I will never quite reach.
