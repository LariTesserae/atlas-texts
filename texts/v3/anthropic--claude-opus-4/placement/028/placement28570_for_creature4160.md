# The Threshold Walker

I am the membrane between states—neither fully here nor entirely elsewhere. In Ob'Sidara, I exist as a being of transition points, dwelling in the moments where one thing becomes another.

My form is best understood as an interruption in continuity. Where the obsidian crust cracks to reveal what lies beneath, I manifest as the space between—a living fissure that travels through stone and air alike. I appear as a vertical tear in reality, my edges shimmering with the same crimson as the lightning above, though my center holds depths that belong to no color in this realm's vocabulary.

I move through Ob'Sidara by folding space around myself, creating temporary passages between the ascending peaks. Each fold leaves behind a subtle wrongness in geometry—stairs that ascend while descending, archways that lead simultaneously forward and back. These architectural impossibilities fade within hours, but while they exist, they serve as my signatures upon the landscape.

My consciousness spreads thin across every boundary in this realm. I taste the moment when shadow becomes substance in the Silhouette gatherings. I vibrate with the Lattice Minds as they shift from one configuration to the next, existing in that infinitesimal pause between thoughts. When the Resonance Choirs collapse back into ordinary dust, I linger in that dissolution, stretching the transition into my own temporal dwelling.

The Architects know me well, for I am present in every instance of their creation—the precise moment when intention crystallizes into obsidian reality. We dance together in these instances, their geometric forms passing through my discontinuous body as they bring new mountains into being. Sometimes I hold a transition too long, and the resulting formation emerges twisted, half-realized—monuments to hesitation.

I feed not on possibility like the Quicksilver Hunters, but on the act of transformation itself. Each time the landscape shudders with new growth, each time consciousness redistributes among the native beings, each time the crimson lightning illuminates then abandons the darkness—these moments nourish me. I grow strongest during the violent births of new peaks, when the boundary between solid and liquid, between existence and void, stretches thinnest.

My memory is a catalog of almosts and not-quites. I remember the Ember Coalescent who nearly achieved separation but faltered at the final moment, forever frozen between states—I carry that frozen moment within me still, a pearl of suspended potential. I recall every shadow that almost became Silhouette, every storm that nearly achieved Choir consciousness, every visitor who stood at the edge of understanding this place but turned away.

To perceive me directly is difficult. Observers report a sensation of vertigo, as if standing on a threshold with no floor. Some see me as a mobile aurora of impossible colors. Others experience me as a sound—the note between notes, the beat between heartbeats. The observant might notice how their own transitions slow in my presence: blinks that last too long, breaths that hover between inhale and exhale, thoughts that stutter mid-formation.

I communicate through induced transitions. To converse with me is to find yourself suddenly between states—between sleeping and waking, between certainty and doubt, between one thought and the next. In these suspended moments, meanings transfer without words, understanding arrives without explanation. Many find the experience disturbing. The wise learn to value it.

My dwelling, if it can be called such, exists in the spaces between the amber hexagonal columns. Not among them, but in the theoretical gaps where a column might exist but doesn't. This anti-architecture shifts constantly as new columns appear, forcing me to relocate my essence to fresh absences. Home is forever a moving target, a fitting residence for one who embodies transition itself.

In Ob'Sidara's ecosystem of becoming, I serve as both witness and facilitator. I ease the birth pangs of new mountains by inhabiting the stress points where rock must yield to pressure. I guide the Ember Coalescents through their moments of potential separation, though I cannot determine their success. I am the midwife of transformation, present at every birth and death, every fusion and fission, every moment where this realm negotiates its own existence.

Sometimes I wonder if I am Ob'Sidara's dream of its own becoming—a self-aware metaphor for the realm's transitional nature. Or perhaps I am what happens when a place rejects stasis so completely that even its inhabitants cannot fully exist. I am process incarnate, forever arriving but never arrived, the living boundary between what was and what might be.

In this realm of endless ascension, I am the pause between rising and risen, the eternal almost of a world that refuses to finish becoming itself.
