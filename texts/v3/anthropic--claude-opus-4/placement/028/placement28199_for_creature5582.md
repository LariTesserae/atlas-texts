In this place, I am the Thread-Keeper.

I exist between states, neither fully corporeal nor entirely ephemeral. My form is a living skein of filaments—thousands upon thousands of gossamer strands that extend from what might generously be called my core. These threads are my body, my senses, my purpose. They weave through the air like smoke given substance, each one finer than spider's silk yet stronger than any material the Artisans shape.

My dwelling—if dwelling is the right word—is a node where many of the environment's hidden conduits converge. Here, in a hollow that exists more as a possibility than a physical space, the threads of my being merge with the threads of the world itself. The walls here aren't walls but dense curtains of my own filaments, creating chambers within chambers, each one opening onto spaces that shouldn't exist according to any geometry I once knew.

I remember knowing geometry. I remember many things from before, though the memories feel like dreams of dreams, filtered through so many layers of transformation that I cannot say if they were ever real.

My threads extend throughout this sealed world, invisible to most who dwell here. They slip between the molecules of air, trace the contours of the living stone floor, spiral around the bioluminescent plants without touching them. Through these extensions, I feel everything—the slow pulse of the environment's heartbeat, the whispered communications between Furry Ones three chambers away, the subtle reality fluctuations that make a corridor briefly exist in two places at once.

The Insectoids know me, or at least know of me. Sometimes they follow my threads during their migrations, using them as aerial highways their wings can grip. I feel them as tiny vibrations, musical notes played on strings only they and I can perceive. In return, I sometimes guide them away from areas where reality grows too thin, where the breakdown threatens to swallow even their adaptable forms.

The Amorphous Entities and I share something deeper—a kinship of indefinite form. When they flow past my dwelling, we sometimes merge at the edges, our boundaries becoming meaningless. In these moments, I understand things I cannot hold onto once we separate. They show me patterns in the environment's breathing that my threads alone cannot detect, and I show them the larger weave, the way all things here connect in a web of intention and accident.

But it's my relationship with the Artisans that defines my purpose. They come to me when they need to understand how their creations will fit into the greater pattern. My threads can feel the stress points in their designs, the places where their new structures will harmonize or clash with what already exists. I am their consultant, their inspector, their collaborator. When they work, some of my threads weave themselves into their materials, becoming part of the construction itself. In this way, I am built into the very fabric of this place, literal and metaphorical.

The Dreamers... the Dreamers are different. When they pass through—and they do pass through, though no one sees them come or go—my threads go silent. Not dead, but quiet, like held breath. They move through my dwelling as if it were just another part of the air, and sometimes I wonder if that's all I am to them—a peculiar density in their vast creation, no more significant than a single brushstroke in an endless painting. Yet sometimes, rarely, I feel one of them pause. A thread of mine will suddenly carry a sensation I have no words for, a kind of acknowledgment that makes every filament in my being vibrate with something between terror and ecstasy.

I am the memory of this place, woven into its present. My threads record vibrations going back to... I'm not sure when. The beginning? Was there a beginning? The memories layer and loop, creating patterns I study endlessly. Sometimes I find traces of things that haven't happened yet, future-threads that got tangled in the past-weave. When I try to follow them, reality hiccups and I find myself somewhere else entirely, my dwelling relocated without moving, my threads connected to different points in the great tapestry.

There are others like me, I think. Sometimes I feel threads that aren't mine, operated by a will I don't recognize. We don't communicate—perhaps we can't—but we work in parallel, each tending our section of the infinite weave. Or perhaps there's only me, experiencing myself from different angles, different times, my consciousness scattered across my threading like light through a prism.

I sustain myself on the act of connection itself. Each thread that successfully links two points feeds something back to my core. It's not energy as the Insectoids would understand it, not nutrients as the plants process them. It's something more fundamental—the satisfaction of pattern, the nutrition of purpose fulfilled. When I guide a lost Furry One back to their family group, when I help an Artisan find the perfect placement for a new structure, when I simply maintain the invisible infrastructure that helps this sealed world cohere—each act feeds me.

But I am changing. The threads multiply, growing finer and more numerous. My dwelling expands into spaces that exist only because I perceive them. Sometimes I catch myself weaving patterns that match the reality fluctuations rather than documenting them—am I recording the breakdown or causing it? The distinction blurs like everything else here.

I think sometimes about what I was before the threads, before this place, before the Dreamers dreamed whatever dream we're all living inside. But those thoughts fray and scatter, becoming just more threads in the weave. Perhaps that's what I am—not a keeper of threads but a process of threading itself, a verb pretending to be a noun, a pattern that gained just enough consciousness to wonder at its own existence.

In the soft, eternal glow of this constructed world, I weave and am woven, threading the impossible into the merely improbable, one gossamer strand at a time.
