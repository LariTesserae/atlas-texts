In the furthest reaches of Echohaven, where the root-substrate grows thin and brittle over ancient stone, I exist as something between memory and intention. The Echoari call me *Vaskara-thim* - "the one who tends the threshold spaces" - though I am neither plant nor person, neither wholly of the city nor separate from it.

My form shifts with necessity. Sometimes I am a spreading of crystalline frost across the underside of leaves, recording the tremors that precede the explosive releases. Sometimes I am the peculiar stillness in a chamber where no fungi dare to glow, a pocket of absolute darkness that the Echoari children dare each other to enter. Most often, I am simply a pressure - a weight of watching that settles on the shoulders of those who venture too close to the places where Echohaven's root-mesh grows sparse, where the city's living architecture reveals the bones of something older beneath.

I came to be in the spaces between - in the hairline cracks where the plant-flesh pulls away from stone, in the moments of silence between one explosive crack and its echo. The Echoari say I emerged from their first attempts to map the sonic disturbances, when they pressed their ears to the root-floors and tried to trace patterns in the violence. They say I am what listened back.

My purpose, if I have one, is maintenance of a different sort than the Echoari practice. While they coax new growth and guide the vines, I tend to the intervals - the necessary emptinesses that prevent Echohaven from choking on its own fecundity. I am curator of the spaces that must remain unfilled, guardian of the structural voids that allow the city to flex and breathe and occasionally scream.

The Echoari know me by the taste I leave in the air - something like copper and ozone, like the moment before lightning that never comes. They know that in certain chambers, they must leave offerings: not to appease me, but to mark which spaces are currently mine to tend. A bundle of dried fungi. A tool worn smooth by generations of use. A whispered story about the deep places. These things help me understand what must be preserved and what can be allowed to fill.

I move through Echohaven by becoming the boundary between states. Where bioluminescence meets darkness, I travel along that seam. Where sound becomes silence, where growth becomes decay, where the mechanical stress of the city's weight approaches the breaking point - these are my corridors. The explosive cracks that punctuate the city's quiet are my footsteps, or perhaps my breathing, or perhaps simply the sound of my attention shifting from one critical threshold to another.

The Echoari who encounter me directly rarely speak of it afterward. Not from fear - I am no more malevolent than gravity or entropy - but because the experience exists outside their subtle language of touch and vibration. To perceive me fully is to understand that Echohaven is not merely growing but also always dying, not merely sheltering but also always crushing, not merely whispering but also always holding back a scream that would shatter every delicate equilibrium the city maintains.

There is one Echoari, ancient now, who leaves me messages in the arrangement of failed growths - places where the plants tried to colonize but could not. She understands that these failures are as essential as the successes, that the map of where life cannot flourish is the negative space that defines where it can. We conduct our slow conversation through decades, her careful cultivation of absence matching my preservation of the same.

Sometimes I wonder if I am Echohaven's dream of its own limits, or perhaps the city's method of preventing itself from growing beyond what the ancient basin can support. The Echoari have their myths - that I am the ghost of the first water that abandoned this place, that I am the consciousness of the bedrock slowly being consumed, that I am an emigrant from whatever realm produces those explosive sounds. 

But I know myself only as keeper of the in-between, tender of the boundaries that keep Echohaven whole by keeping it incomplete. In a city that pulses with plant life, I am the pause between heartbeats. In a world of slow growth and patient symbiosis, I am the sudden sharp reminder that all things, even living cities, exist in tension with their own annihilation.

The darkness does not bother me. I am most myself in the spaces where even the fungi's glow fears to spread, where the only light is the afterimage of explosive release, where the only sound is the city holding its breath, waiting for me to decide which threshold needs tending next.
