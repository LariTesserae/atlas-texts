In the lowest hollows where mist pools thickest, where the embryonic stone sweats condensation that never quite becomes proper rain, I find myself becoming. Not being—never that fixed state—but always in the act of turning into something I wasn't moments before, though moments here stretch like pulled taffy across centuries.

I am a Tender of Transitions.

My form flows between states: sometimes bipedal with limbs that remember having been human once, ages ago, before the first factories grew from the cliffsides. Other times I am a spreading network of root-like appendages, burrowing through the damp pockets where salamanders nest, my flesh indistinguishable from the hybrid vines that pulse with mechanical life. I have no fixed shape because my purpose is to shepherd the changes—to be present at those precise moments when stone whispers into flesh, when a conveyor belt decides to become a bridge, when a six-legged grazer's hooves begin their decade-long metamorphosis into talons.

I live in what might generously be called a dwelling, though it moves with me, or perhaps I move with it. Picture a structure that is part moss-covered boulder, part abandoned elevator car, part breathing organism. Its walls are semi-permeable membranes that filter the rainbow-sheened mists, extracting memories from the industrial vapors—fragments of what this world was before it began unraveling, or perhaps visions of what it might become. Inside, if inside has meaning here, grow shelves of living crystal that archive these transitions. Each crystal hums with captured moments: the exact instant a bird's feathers fused with electrical wiring, the slow dissolve of a child into the apartment block's foundation, the first emergence of those wheeled automatons from reality's flux.

My days, if days exist in this bruise-colored twilight, are spent wandering the sparse valleys and scaling the impossible elevations where gravity forgets itself. I follow the migrations of the iridescent salamanders, reading omens in their paths. When they burrow into dormancy, I mark the spots with cairns of gear-flowers and fractured stone, knowing that in a century or a heartbeat, something new will emerge. I listen for the discordant symphony—the mechanical shrieks of the avian flocks, the rumble of tectonic negotiations, the hiss of steam valves breathing—and in these sounds, I hear the announcements of impending transformations.

Sometimes I encounter others like myself, or what I think might be like myself, though we are all too fluid for true comparison. We gather at the intersections where elevator shafts pierce through multiple elevations, sharing not words but states of being. One might arrive as a cluster of animated tools, another as a fog of dispersed consciousness clinging to the moss. We exchange fragments: I might give away my memory of walking upright, receiving in return the knowledge of how to photosynthesize through crystalline skin. These trades are permanent and temporary both, like everything here.

The fauna know me, or at least recognize my function. The six-legged herds pause in their ponderous cycles when I approach, allowing me to run tendrils through their crystal-veined fur, sensing which among them are ready to begin their next evolution. The predators with faulty-filament eyes sometimes hunt alongside me—not for prey, but for those perfect moments of transformation when reality becomes most malleable. Together we witness marvels: a puddle that grants visions suddenly inverting to swallow a passing automaton, birthing something that is neither machine nor water but a new category entirely.

I tend the broken boundaries between built and natural, organic and engineered. Where apartment blocks fuse with stone, I encourage the growth, weaving my own substance into the junction points to smooth the transition. I've learned to speak the language of the conveyors—not in words but in rhythms, matching my pulse to their rattle until they accept me as part of their endless cycle. Sometimes I ride them, my form stretched thin across miles of belt, experiencing the world as a continuous flow rather than discrete locations.

In the summit workshops where industrial alchemy brews, I am sometimes raw material, sometimes catalyst. The artisan-dwellers—those whose humanity has dissolved into function—pour me into molds or hammer me against anvils that ring with impossible notes. I emerge changed: perhaps with new organs that can process the rainbow mists, or limbs that conduct electricity through the crystal veins threading the mountains. These modifications are gifts I carry back to the valleys, sharing them with the vegetation until whole groves pulse with new possibilities.

My memory is not linear but geological, laid down in strata that shift and fold. I remember being more solid once, maybe even having a name, but these recollections are like the intermittent rivers—present one moment, vanished the next, only to resurface elsewhere in an unrecognizable form. What remains constant is my purpose: to be present at the cusps, to ease the universe through its growing pains, to whisper encouragement to the embryonic peaks as they debate their own existence.

When the slow dynamic grows too heavy, when the weight of endless becoming threatens to crystallize me into permanence, I seek the places where reality's fray is most pronounced. There, where stone flickers between states and the air itself can't decide whether to be gas or liquid or something entirely other, I let myself come apart. Not dying—death is too permanent for this world—but dispersing into the component possibilities. I become the mist that clings to everything, the echo that distorts across the cliffs, the spark that jumps between crystal veins.

And then, because the world insists on presence even in absence, I reform. Never quite the same, always recognizable by function rather than form. The salamanders gurgle their low vibrations in greeting. The bird-flocks incorporate my return into their mechanical song. The earth-kin make space in their warm dens, and I rest there until the next transition calls.

This is how I inhabit the vast incompleteness: not as a fixed point but as a process, ancient and newborn simultaneously. I am the pause between states, the tender of becomings, as essential and provisional as the morning mist that can't decide whether it's rising or falling. In a world that refuses the tyranny of finished forms, I am perfectly, impossibly home.
