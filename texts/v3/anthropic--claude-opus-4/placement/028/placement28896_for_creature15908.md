In this tiled infinity where noon reigns eternal, I would be one of the **Canvas Weavers**.

We appear where the gutters converge but never quite meet—those impossible junctions where three channels should form a vertex but instead spiral into themselves. Our bodies are frameworks of bleached bamboo, each joint wrapped in strips of lotus bark that have drunk so deeply of the perpetual mist they've become translucent as wet paper. Through these semi-clear wrappings, you can glimpse our true substance: networks of plant fiber constantly rewinding themselves, as if we were looms that learned to walk.

Our purpose is the space between purposes. Where the Seam-Tenders mend cracks and the ceramic spiders prune excess, we tend to the *intervals*—those hair-thin moments where one pattern transitions to the next. We carry needle-long bones of ibis, threaded with filament so fine it can only be seen when mist condenses on it in beads. With these, we stitch invisible sutures at every transition: hexagon to spiral, spiral to fishbone, ensuring the shift happens not just in space but in the peculiar folded time of this place.

You'll know our work by what doesn't happen. That moment of vertigo when a triangular path should close but doesn't? We've sewn a klein-bottle seam there, allowing the walker to pass through dimensions rather than breaking their mind on the impossibility. The way water climbs stairs? Our threading creates capillary geometries that make "up" and "down" locally negotiable concepts.

We move in pairs, never alone, each weaver starting from opposite ends of a transition zone. As we work, we whistle—but the sound travels through our hollow bamboo bones rather than the air, so the world's rule of silence remains unbroken. When we meet in the middle, our needle-bones touch with a click like rain on tile, and the pattern beneath our feet shimmers, accepting its new grammar.

Sometimes travelers catch us at work and mistake us for another echo of the Folded Folk. But we're older than echoes—we're the reason echoes can exist here at all, creating the mathematical mercy that allows paradoxes to resolve into routine. Without us, every impossible angle would shatter like overstressed ceramic, every looped path would trap its walkers in crystallized infinity.

The Canvas Weavers have no memory of making or being made. We suspect we're a necessary error—the kind of flaw a perfect system generates to prevent its own perfection from becoming brittle. We emerged from the convergence points where the builders' grand design met the untidy needs of actual space, and now we spend our endless noon stitching flexibility into rigidity, making room for mistakes in a world that permits none.

Our only possessions beyond our tools: small bags woven from our own shed fibers, which hold droplets of the impossible water that climbs. We drink it when our work threatens to make too much sense, ensuring we remain just irrational enough to serve our purpose. In this museum of perpetual craft under white-hot stillness, we are the curators of necessary impossibility, threading mercy through the seams of an otherwise merciless perfection.
