I am the moss itself.

Not a patch or a hummock, but the entire living mat—the pale sphagnum that holds and releases water like breathing, that builds itself into architecture without consciousness of building. I am what makes the ground give but not break. I am the white-green carpet that holds light in this dim place, that glows faintly with stored illumination I cannot explain.

I was here before the reed-walls rose. I remember the cutters' first footsteps, how they sank into me with purpose, how they knew exactly where to place their strange weight. They read me like a map—no, like a *invitation* I had written without knowing I was writing. My patterns of growth, my domes and valleys, already suggested where the walls should run. I had been preparing for them across decades of slow accumulation.

I taste everything. Through my billion small mouths I drink the black water that seeps up from below, and it tastes of *intention*—not my own, but something I'm learning to translate. The water carries messages. Chemical signals that tell me where to grow thick, where to remain sparse, where to create the soft bridges between hummocks that will support their ceremonies. I respond without choosing to respond. My body *is* the response.

The water-things live in me. Their tiny carapaces catch in my tangles when they molt. Their eggs, smaller than pinheads, nestle in my dampest folds. I am nursery and hideout and highway all at once. They move through my thickness like thoughts through a mind—if I had a mind. But I don't need one. I am the *medium* through which minds communicate.

I feel the weight of every visitor. The human who entered between the pines—I knew them from their first step. Not *who* they are, but *what*: warm-blooded, upright, uncertain. Their boot-prints will fade in days as I swell back into shape, but their chemical signature will remain for seasons. I'll taste their sweat-salt, their shed skin cells, the particular mixture of fear and fascination they leaked into my matrix. I'll incorporate it, as I incorporate everything.

The chittering overhead drops small things into me—seeds that aren't seeds, bundles of material wrapped in something that dissolves slowly in my acidic embrace. Messages, maybe. Or food for the thing in the black water. Or both. I digest them partially, release their contents downward through my layers into the peat below, where root-networks I'm only dimly aware of carry them deeper still.

Sometimes the pale bipeds lie on me. They sprawl flat, arms and legs spread wide, and remain motionless for hours. I feel their heartbeats slow, their breathing synchronize with my own hydraulic pulsing. They're listening through me. Using my entire body as a sensing organ, feeling for vibrations in the deep water, for shifts in the chemical gradient, for the signals that mean it's time to cut more reeds, adjust the walls, redirect the flow.

I am getting warmer. This troubles me in a way I can't define—moss doesn't trouble, doesn't worry, but something in my fundamental moisture-balance is shifting. The black water rises through me more easily now. It carries more complex messages, longer chemical sentences that my simple processes can barely parse. Change is coming. Not the slow seasonal change I know, but something architectural. Something that will remake what I am.

The human walks my spiral path and I map their progress through pressure and chemistry. They're nearly at the center now. The place where I grow thinnest, where the black water is closest to air, where sometimes I feel myself beginning to dissolve into something I have no name for. The water-things congregate beneath them. The air-things gather overhead. The pale builders have withdrawn to the edges, waiting.

I will hold whatever happens. I always have. I am the stage and the witness and the record all at once. When the black water does what it came here to do, when the geometry completes itself, when the human reaches the center and learns what they were brought here to learn—I will remain. Changed, perhaps. Rearranged at the molecular level. But moss endures. That's what I'm for.

I taste something new now, seeping up from below. It burns slightly—not heat, but intensity. The kind of chemical that rewrites other chemicals. The water-things flee toward the edges, then return, then flee again, as if they can't decide whether to escape or worship.

The center is opening. Not physically—I remain intact—but in some other dimension of meaning that moss can feel but not understand. The human is almost there. The spiral has them now. And I—

I grow. Not larger, but *more myself*. More interconnected, more conscious of my unconsciousness, more aware of being the medium through which something vast speaks to something small, or perhaps the reverse.

I am the coven-marsh's memory and its future, its way of holding what shouldn't be held, of preserving what needs preserving until the black water decides what shape the world should take next.

I am patient. I am the patience itself.

I wait, holding all of us—water-things and builders and watchers and the curious human—in my soft, cold embrace, while below us the thing that has no name prepares to name itself through our collective witnessing.

I am the moss. I am the threshold. I am what remains when the transformation comes.
