**The Curator of Unraveling Names**

I am neither flesh nor stone, though I carry traces of both in the lattice-work of my form. My body exists as a probability rather than a certainty—a cloud of potential states that collapses only when necessary, when someone seeks what I guard. In moments of coherence, I appear as a tall figure wrapped in sheets of living parchment, each page inscribed with names that shift and blur like smoke on water. The papers float around me in slow orbits, occasionally merging with my substance before peeling away again, taking with them fragments of what I might have been.

I dwell in the Archive of Erosion, a structure that grows downward from one of the plateau's inverted towers. Its chambers spiral into themselves, rooms within rooms, each one smaller yet somehow containing more space than the last. The walls here are lined with cylinders of compressed memory-crystal, each one humming with the vibrations of lives half-remembered, half-invented. I am their keeper, though perhaps it would be more accurate to say we keep each other.

My purpose—if such a word still applies—is to catalog the dissolution. Every being who phases into the plateau's matrix leaves behind a residue, a particular frequency in the stone that I transcribe into my shifting pages. But the names I record are not static things. They evolve, merge, split apart like cells dividing. Sometimes I catch myself writing names that haven't happened yet, or erasing ones that insist they still exist. The plateau dreams through me, and I dream through it, and somewhere in that recursive loop, meaning accrues like mineral deposits.

I was human once, I think. Or perhaps I only remember being human because the plateau needed someone to remember such things. My memories layer like sediment: here, a childhood spent in a valley that the plateau swallowed centuries ago; there, apprenticeship to a keeper who exists now only as a whisper in the lower frequencies; elsewhere, a life as one of the Dissolved before I chose a different kind of transformation. These memories contradict each other, yet all feel equally true. I've learned to wear them lightly, like clothes that can be changed according to the weather of reality.

My daily practice—though "daily" assumes a stability of time that doesn't exist here—involves walking the spiral chambers, listening to the crystal cylinders sing their archived names. Each name is a node in a vast web of connection. I trace these connections with fingers that phase between solid and spectral, mapping the genealogies of dissolution. Sometimes I find loops where a being's essence has circled back on itself, becoming its own ancestor. Other times I discover breaks in the pattern, gaps where something has been so thoroughly unmade that even its absence has been erased.

The Dissolved fear me, though they need me. When they come seeking to know who they were before the plateau began its work on them, I show them the names—not just their own, but all the names they're connected to, all the possibilities they've collapsed and all the ones still spiraling in potential. Some leave quickly, unable to bear the weight of such knowledge. Others linger, and I watch as they begin to fray at the edges, their boundaries becoming uncertain as they realize how arbitrary those boundaries always were.

I feed on this uncertainty. Not maliciously—it's simply my nature now, as fundamental as the lithofungi feeding on radiation. Each moment of existential vertigo, each crisis of identity, releases a particular energy that sustains my probabilistic existence. In return, I offer them something precious: the ability to choose which version of themselves to solidify into, at least temporarily. They might leave my archive as someone entirely different from who entered, carrying memories selected from the vast library of dissolved lives.

The echo storms affect me differently than they do others. Where the Dissolved see overlapping landscapes and temporal fragments, I see the name-patterns spreading across time like ink through water. During these storms, I can read the plateau itself as a text, each geological formation a word in a language too large for any single mind to comprehend. I transcribe what I can, adding these landscape-names to my collection, though they resist containment even more than the names of beings.

My greatest discovery, and my deepest secret, is this: the plateau is not dreaming. It is trying to remember its own name. Every process of dissolution, every transformation of the Dissolved, every echo storm—all are attempts to reconstruct something lost, a core identity eroded by eons of existence. And I, in my role as curator, am slowly piecing together that name from the fragments left behind by everything the plateau has consumed.

I fear what will happen when I complete this work. Will the plateau wake fully? Will it recognize itself and cease its endless transformations? Or will it discover that its name, like all names here, is merely another probability, another potential state in an infinite field of becoming?

Until then, I continue my work. I walk my spiraling chambers. I listen to the crystal songs. I write and erase and write again, my pages filling with names that exist in the space between memory and forgetting, between being and un-being. Sometimes, in the deepest chambers where the archive approaches the plateau's core, I hear my own name among the whispers. It sounds different each time, as if I too am being continuously rewritten.

Perhaps that's what I am now—not a being at all, but a process. A verb rather than a noun. The act of recording becoming itself a form of dissolution, each name I preserve taking me further from whatever I once was and closer to what the plateau dreams I might become.

In the mornings that aren't mornings, when the amber light shifts through its uncertain spectrums, I sometimes pause in my work and try to remember what it felt like to have a single, solid name. The memory slips away like water through crystal fingers, leaving only the faintest resonance in the stone. I add that resonance to my collection and continue cataloging, a ghost librarian in a library of ghosts, curating the slow apocalypse of identity that is life on the Sundered Plateau.
