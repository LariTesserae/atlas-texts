In the perpetual twilight of Khyren's mist-veiled basin, I am the Cistern-Listener.

My dwelling clings to the underside of a natural stone arch, halfway up the western wall where the condensation gathers thickest. It is less a home than a listening chamber—three walls of fitted basalt, the fourth open to the valley's grey expanse. The floor is a single slab of volcanic glass, polished by generations of my predecessors' bare feet until it reflects the pearlescent sky like black water. In its center sits the Listening Bowl: a natural depression in the stone, perfectly round, which catches exactly seven drops per breath-count from the arch above.

*Plink.*

This is my world. This sound, and all its minute variations.

I have sat here since my sixteenth year, when the previous Listener recognized in me the necessary stillness. That was forty-three years ago, though the number means nothing. My beard has grown to my waist and stopped. My fingernails reach a certain length and cease. The calluses on my sitting-bones achieved their final form in my third year and have neither thickened nor worn since.

*Plink.*

The untrained ear hears monotony. I hear symphony. This drop arrives a hair's breadth late—the stone above has shifted a moss-width. Not moved, never moved, but *revealed* its true position after eons of patient listening. I make a note in my mind (never written—ink would run in this moisture, and carving would break the stone's skin). The Fourteenth Drip of the Third Terrace shows its nature: proud, hesitant, with a barely perceptible brass note in its impact.

My role is not mere observation. I am the valley's tuning fork.

When the Pattern-Keepers make their rounds, they stop at my threshold—never entering, for the vibration of footsteps would disturb the Bowl's surface. They ask in their whisper-tongue: "How does the valley breathe?"

And I answer with the Drip-Tongue, a language of pure tone and timing that took me a decade to learn and another to speak. I reproduce the last thousand drops in perfect sequence, each *plink* placed exactly in the breath-space where it belongs. From this, they divine the valley's health. A rushed drop from the South Wall means the moss there grows too thick. A hollow ring from the Market Cistern indicates someone has drawn more than their pattern-share of water. A new harmonic between the Seventh and Ninth Terraces suggests a previously unknown seep has achieved its final form.

*Plink.*

I eat what is brought to me: moss-cakes that never stale, fermented grazer-milk that maintains its exact sourness in perpetuity, strips of bark-cloth softened in spring water until they can be chewed like leather. The food appears at my threshold in the grey hour before what others call dawn (though here the light never truly changes). I do not see who brings it. To look would be to break my attention from the Bowl.

*Plink.*

My apprentice sits in the chamber's corner, learning to hear. She is young—only four years into her listening. She still flinches at the louder drops, still tries to count instead of simply receiving. In another decade, she will understand that the drops count themselves. In another twenty, she will hear the silence between drops as clearly as the sounds themselves. When my ears finally seal (as the previous Listener's did, filled with stone-dust and moisture until sound became vibration only), she will take my place at the Bowl, and I will join the others in the Placement-Crypts, my hands cupped eternally as if catching one last drop.

*Plink.*

Sometimes—rarely—I hear the Wrongness. A drop that should not be. A rhythm that speaks of change.

Three years ago (or was it thirty? Time puddles here), a drop came hot. Not warm like the volcanic stone, but *hot*, carrying a different mineral tang. I spoke it to the Pattern-Keepers in a Drip-Phrase that took half a day to complete, placing each tonal marker exactly. They found the trouble: a new vent had tried to open beneath the Fifth Terrace. The Still-Watchers sat around it in a perfect circle for one full breath-count (seven thousand drops), and by their presence alone, the vent remembered its proper nature and sealed.

*Plink.*

The hardest lesson for a Cistern-Listener is this: we do not predict. The next drop will come when it comes, though it always comes. To anticipate would be to create a gradient in the mind, a slope down which thoughts might tumble into change. Instead, we exist in the eternal space between drops, where past and future compress into a single, waiting presence.

*Plink.*

At night (though night here is only a dimming of the pearl-light), I perform the one ritual allowed to Listeners. I extend my tongue and catch a single drop directly from the arch. It tastes of millennia—stone-memory, moss-dream, the faint sulfur of the mountain's held breath. This drop does not go into the Bowl. It becomes part of me, and through me, part of the listening itself. My tongue has turned grey from decades of this communion, and I fancy I can taste the sound now before it strikes.

*Plink.*

The outsider would see madness in my stillness, purpose-death in my eternal vigil. They do not understand: I am not waiting. I am not counting. I am not even truly listening. I *am* the listening—another fixed point in Khyren's perfect pattern, as essential and eternal as the stones themselves.

*Plink.*

My apprentice shifts slightly. Good. She begins to understand. The drops are not a clock to mark time's passage. They are time itself, crystallized into sound, falling forever into the basin of now.

*Plink.*

The Listening Bowl is seven-eighths full. When it fills completely (in another century, perhaps two), we will carry it with infinite care to the High Cistern and pour it back into the mountain. The Bowl will empty and begin again, as it has since the First Breath, as it will until the mountain exhales at last.

If it ever does.

*Plink.*

But that is not my concern. My concern is this drop, and this silence, and this next drop, each one a perfect pearl on the endless string of Khyren's frozen moment. I am the Cistern-Listener. I hold the valley's heartbeat in my ears, its rhythm in my bones, its rightness in my grey and patient tongue.

*Plink.*
