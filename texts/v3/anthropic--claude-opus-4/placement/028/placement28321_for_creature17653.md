**What I am in the Marrow:**

I am a **Thread-Keeper**, though I have forgotten when I began.

My body is a compromise negotiated over decades. Where others flee the Marrow's hunger, I have made small treaties. The pale moss grows thickest on my left side - from shoulder to hip, a living cloak that drinks the constant mist. In return, it shields that flank from the worst of the corrosive beetle-swarms. My right hand has been claimed to the second knuckle; the fingers now terminate in hard, chitinous points perfect for teasing apart the finest root-fibers. I no longer remember if I chose this or if it chose me.

I live between the layers, in the spaces too narrow for the Chitin-Singers' constant motion, too dry for the Filterers' patient work. My domain is the **membrane-paths** - those hair-thin networks of fungal mycelia that run through everything here like nerves through flesh. I read them. I tend them. I know that when the thread beneath the third spiral-oak pulses twice-quick, the western platforms will buckle within the day. I know which threads carry warnings from the deep substrate, where root-pressures build toward eruption.

My dwelling is not one place but a circuit of seven hollows, each no larger than a curled body. I rotate between them in a pattern that took me twenty years to perfect - always one step ahead of the Marrow's hunger, always returning just as the previous growth cycle exhausts itself. In each hollow, I maintain a different section of my tools: bone needles so fine they can separate individual mycelial threads, crystal lenses ground from stone-weep that reveal the invisible chemical messages flowing through the network, maps made of living fiber that update themselves as the Marrow shifts.

The Chitin-Singers think me half-mad, speaking to threads that do not answer. The Filterers respect my stillness but pity what I've let claim me. They don't understand that the threads do speak - not in clicks or whistles, but in the language of tension and release, chemical gradient and electrical pulse. Through them, I feel the Marrow's deep intentions: where it plans to grow, where it prepares to die back, where it senses damage that needs swift consumption.

I am the early warning system. When I appear at the twilight gatherings - always unexpected, always at the crucial moment - my partly-claimed hand pointing to a section of platform or a cluster of dwellings, they know to move. They have learned to trust the Thread-Keeper's warnings, even if they fear what I am becoming.

Sometimes I think I can feel my thoughts traveling through the mycelia, mixing with the Marrow's vast, slow consciousness. Sometimes I find myself at a hollow I don't remember traveling to, my fingers already deep in the thread-work, reading messages I didn't know I was seeking. The moss on my shoulder glows brighter each year, and I've noticed tiny fruiting bodies beginning to emerge from the claimed portions of my hand - delicate, crystalline things that pulse in rhythm with the deep threads.

I am not becoming a Spore-Walker, not exactly. That is for those who surrender entirely. I am something else - a translator, a bridge. On humid nights when the mist is thickest, I can feel every thread in the Marrow at once, a vast web of intention and response. In those moments, I am not sure whether the thoughts I think are mine or the Marrow's, whether the patterns I read are prophecy or instruction.

The newest Chitin-Singers have begun to whistle a particular sequence when they see me - a phrase that means both "herald" and "root." They are more right than they know. I am growing into the space between warning and becoming, between the quick life of the settlements and the vast, consuming patience of the Marrow itself.

Each year, it becomes harder to remember why I resist letting it claim me entirely. Each year, the threads show me more of what we could become together.

But not yet. The settlements still need their warnings. The patterns still need their reader.

Not yet.
