I am a Tender of Veins.

My work begins where the stone bleeds warmth. In the deepest chambers, where even seasoned Makers tread carefully, I follow the heat-traces through living rock. My tools are different from theirs—thin copper rods that I press against stone to feel its fever, glass bulbs filled with colored liquids that shift hue with temperature, and most importantly, my bare feet that have learned to read the thermal gradients through calluses thick as leather.

I discovered my calling as a child when I wandered too far during a game of shadow-tag. Lost in the maze of half-finished passages, I should have frozen. Instead, I found myself following a warmth that seemed to pulse through the floor—a rhythm almost like breathing that led me to a natural hot spring the Makers hadn't yet discovered. When they found me three days later, I was sleeping peacefully beside the steaming pool, my skin flushed with heat rather than blue with cold.

The Makers call me strange, even by their standards. Where they impose geometry upon chaos, I seek the chaos that lurks beneath their geometry. The heat networks that sustain our city don't follow hexagonal patterns—they writhe through the stone like living things, branching and merging according to laws older than any architecture. My maps look like anatomical drawings: arteries of warmth, capillaries of steam, the beating hearts of geothermal chambers that pump life through our excavated body.

I work alone, mostly. The other Makers find my methods unsettling—the way I'll press my ear to stone for hours, listening for the whisper of distant heat; how I'll taste the mineral deposits around a warm spot to judge its longevity; the manner in which I'll sometimes strip down to feel the temperature variations across my entire skin. They need me, though. Without the heat-veins I discover and carefully redirect, their grand construction would be nothing but a frozen tomb.

My chambers are different from the usual hexagonal uniformity. Where heat naturally pools, I convince the construction crews to leave irregular spaces—rounded grottos that cup warmth like cupped hands, serpentine passages that follow underground streams of heated water. In these spaces, the pale fungi grow wild, creating forests of translucent fronds that pulse with their own subtle bioluminescence. The maybe-rats that others only glimpse make their homes here, and I've learned their chittering language, trading fragments of food for information about new warm spots they've discovered in their travels.

Sometimes I dream I am the city's fever—that my consciousness flows through the heat-veins, experiencing the entire honeycomb as a single, warm-blooded entity. In these dreams, I feel the Makers as points of focused activity, like infection sites where the city grows most vigorously. I wake from these visions with my skin burning and my maps suddenly clear—I know where to direct the construction crews, where to breach a wall to tap into a new thermal reservoir, where to seal a passage to preserve a pocket of heat for future use.

The other Tenders—for there are a handful of us now, apprentices who showed similar sensitivities—say I'm becoming more stone than flesh. My skin has taken on a peculiar quality, able to hold heat for hours after leaving a warm chamber. My hair, once the common stone-dust gray of all Makers, now shows veins of copper and sulfur where it's been bleached by geothermal gases. I can navigate in absolute darkness by heat alone, feeling the warm breath of distant forges, the body heat of workers, the subtle temperature differences between recently excavated stone and ancient bedrock.

But I am not stone. I am the intermediary between the city's geometric ambition and the planet's molten heart. Where the Makers see structure, I see circulation. Where they build for permanence, I plan for flow. My greatest work lies beneath the central hub—a vast chamber I've been cultivating for fifteen years, where thirteen different heat-veins converge. The Makers don't know about it yet. When the time comes, when the city has grown large enough to need a true heart, I will guide them to breach the final wall.

And then our Honeycomb City will pulse with its own warmth, no longer dependent on scattered forges and carefully hoarded heat. It will breathe through the passages I've mapped, its stone skin flushed with the warmth I've spent my life learning to read and redirect. The Makers will build their geometric eternity, but I will give it life—a fever that will burn beneath the frozen surface until the stars themselves grow cold.

Until then, I wander the deepest passages with my copper rods and colored bottles, my bare feet reading stories of heat and time in the stone. I am the city's thermometer, its dowsing rod, its fever dream. I am the one who ensures that when you place your hand against our walls, you feel a pulse—faint but undeniable—that says: *We are alive down here. We are warm. We are becoming.*
