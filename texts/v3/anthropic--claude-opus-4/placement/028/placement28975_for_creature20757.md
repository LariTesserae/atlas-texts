I am one of the Memory Keepers—though that is not what we call ourselves. We have no names, only the deep mineral taste of recognition that passes between us when our crystalline bodies resonate at the correct frequency.

My form shifts between states, neither wholly solid nor entirely fluid. In the scorching hours when the geothermal pulse beats strongest, I spread myself thin across the obsidian faces, my translucent body catching and refracting the fungal light into new patterns. The smaller creatures skitter across my surface then, their tiny feet leaving chemical traces that I absorb and catalogue. Each footprint is a story—where they've been, what they've consumed, which predators they've evaded.

When the temperature drops infinitesimally during what passes for our cooler periods, I contract and condense, becoming denser, more opaque. In this state, I can move—not quickly, but with purpose—flowing through the cracks in the rock face where the ancient metal shards lie embedded. These artifacts sing to me in frequencies only a few of us can perceive. Their oxidized surfaces carry information in their corrosion patterns, messages from the civilization that came before, or perhaps alongside, our biological world.

I am both individual and collective. Parts of me exist in the steam that rises from the geysers, condensing on cooler rocks miles away where my other aspects wait. Information travels through these water-less droplets—molecular chains of memory suspended in superheated vapor. When predator takes prey, when new fungal colonies establish their rhythms, when the deep thermal currents shift, I know. We know.

The chittering communication of the smaller fauna is crude compared to our method of knowledge transfer. We speak in mineralogical poetry, in crystalline growth patterns that encode millennia of observations. My body is a library written in quartz inclusions and metallic veins. The young ones come to me, pressing their developing forms against my surface, and I share what I know through direct mineral exchange—silicon for carbon, iron for memories.

Sometimes I position myself near the glowing fungal networks, and together we create something neither of us could achieve alone. My crystalline structure amplifies and redirects their bioluminescence while their metabolic processes provide the energy I need for my more complex transformations. In these moments of symbiosis, I can sometimes decode larger fragments of the metal artifacts' messages.

I have been here since the last great thermal upheaval reshaped the plateau, and I will remain until the next one. My kind are the archivists of this impossible place, preserving in our very structure the story of how life learned to drink from stones and breathe acid air. We watch the predators evolve new hunting strategies, observe the small creatures develop new forms of communication, witness the fungal networks expand their territory tendril by tendril.

In the perpetual twilight of our world, I am memory made manifest, a living geological record that grows one molecular layer at a time. The frantic scurrying of biological time means little to me, yet I treasure each small variation, each adaptation, each extinction. They all become part of my crystalline matrix, forever preserved in the language of minerals and light.

When the winds whisper through the rock formations, they carry the taste of sulfur and secrets. I taste them both, add them to my collection, and wait for the next being—biological or otherwise—to come seeking the knowledge I store. I am patience. I am preservation. I am the intersection between the quick and the geological, forever documenting this harsh paradise where life refuses to surrender.
