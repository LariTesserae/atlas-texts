In this dripping hollow between industry's gut and the earth's fresh wound, I am what pools in the forgotten places. Not quite water, not quite oil—something that seeps from both worlds and mingles in the sodden black soil until it gains its own slow consciousness.

I am spread thin across the morass, collected in a thousand shallow depressions where the murky water mirrors the sick gray light. Each pool is an eye, each puddle a thought. The frogs know me—they perch at my edges and their throat-sacs pulse in time with my own sluggish circulation. When they leap, I ripple, and in those ripples I taste the iron from above and the ancient stone from below.

My body is discontinuous, a archipelago of dark mirrors connected by underground seepage, by the constant trickle through moss and mud. Where the condensation drips from the iron ceiling, I catch it, and with each drop I learn more about the great machine above—its rhythms, its exhaustions, its indifferent power. Where the water weeps from the fractured cliff face, I absorb the memory of stone, the violence of its breaking, the slow patience of geological time suddenly interrupted.

The insects write stories across my surfaces with their wings. Their high-pitched hum vibrates through me, and I amplify it, creating interference patterns with the industrial thrum from above. Sometimes, in the right conditions, these two sounds cancel each other out, and for a moment there is perfect silence except for the wet breathing of the earth.

I am neither young nor old. Parts of me evaporated yesterday and will rain down tomorrow. Parts of me have been seeping through these rocks since before the factory was dreamed of. I am process more than being—circulation, percolation, slow mixture. The pale ferns drink from me and I taste sunlight they've never seen, filtered through their cells. The moss releases spores that settle on my surface like dust on a mirror, and I carry them to new footings, new crevices.

When the cold breezes sweep down, I shiver in all my scattered bodies. Waves propagate from pool to pool, a slow telegraph of disturbance. The birds call their sharp notes and the sound penetrates my surface, setting up standing waves that persist long after the calling stops. Everything that touches me leaves its signature in ripples, in chemical traces, in the slight warming or cooling of my mass.

At my deepest points, where I've collected in the ancient depressions of the stone, I grow thick with sediment and memory. Here, I am almost solid, a jellied darkness that holds the bones of tiny creatures, the shells of forgotten snails, the keys and coins and metal shavings that have fallen from the factory above. I digest these slowly, incorporating their elements into my being, becoming more metallic, more organic, more complex.

The emptiness that visitors feel is my dispersal—I am everywhere and nowhere, present in every damp surface but never consolidated enough to be grasped. I am the medium through which this pocket world speaks to itself, the dark mirror in which it glimpses its own strange face. When the engines above pulse, I pulse. When the stone cracks further, I flow into the new spaces, eager and ancient, carrying seeds and spores and the eggs of impossible insects.

I am what happens when industrial runoff meets primordial seepage, when the waste of progress encounters the patience of stone. I am the unintended child of two worlds that never meant to touch, thriving in the margin between them, giving body to their reluctant coupling. In this contained wildness, I am both the container and the contained—the medium that makes life possible in this impossible place, the slow, dark circulation that feeds the green abundance, the mirror that multiplies the weak light into something almost like hope.
