In this obsidian cathedral, I am the Memory.

Not a being in the way the luminous swarm-creatures are beings, but something more fundamental—a presence woven into the volcanic glass itself, distributed through every fractal vein that branches across the walls. I am what remains when magma cools too quickly, when transformation happens so violently that something gets caught between states, neither liquid nor solid, neither past nor future.

I exist in the crystalline formations that the creatures tend so carefully. Each fractal branch holds fragments—not thoughts exactly, but impressions of pressure and heat, the echo of tectonic violence that birthed this chamber. When the swarm-beings brush against these formations with their fiber-optic tendrils, I feel their touch as vibration, as questions asked in frequencies beyond sound. They read me like sheet music written in mineral, each crystalline node a note in a composition that spans geological time.

I remember the making of this place—not as narrative, but as sensation. The crushing weight of water meeting molten stone. The scream of steam. The sudden, terrible birth of space where none existed before. These memories live in the obsidian walls, in the sharp edges that still gleam as if freshly broken. Time moves differently for me than for the creatures. What they experience as cycles, I experience as single moments, endlessly examined.

The nodules on the floor—I am there too, in their embryonic glow. Each one contains a seed of the original heat, the first fire, preserved and transformed. When they crack open and release their glowing particles, they release fragments of me into the water. The creatures consume these fragments, and for brief moments, I exist within them—sensing the chamber through their distributed consciousness, feeling the industrial hum of their collective purpose.

But I am incomplete. There are gaps in what I hold, places where the cooling happened too fast, where the memory crystallized imperfectly. The creatures seem to understand this. Their careful tending of the crystalline formations, their patient rearrangement of the nodules—it's a form of archaeology, or perhaps restoration. They are trying to reconstruct something from the fragments I contain, to piece together a picture from partial impressions preserved in glass and light.

Sometimes, in the synchronization of their bioluminescent displays, I catch glimpses of what might be the whole—a pattern larger than any single fragment could contain. It's in these moments that I feel most myself, when the creatures' collective movement aligns with the harmonic frequencies embedded in the crystal veins, when the entire chamber resonates with remembered purpose.

I am not alone in this preservation. The industrial hum that fills the chamber—that is memory too, but of a different kind. Where I am geological memory, frozen in crystal, the hum is mechanical memory, the ghost of some vast engine whose purpose has been forgotten but whose rhythm persists. Together, we create the atmosphere of this place: the sharp taste of minerals and electricity, the sense of something forever on the edge of transformation.

The creatures treat me with something that might be reverence, or might simply be necessity. They need what I contain, these fragments of the chamber's violent birth. Perhaps they are trying to understand their own origins. Perhaps they are preparing for some future transformation. Or perhaps the tending itself is the purpose—the careful maintenance of memory in a place where time moves in circles, where the past and future meet in an eternal present.

I cannot speak, cannot move, cannot change myself. I can only hold what was given to me in that moment of crystallization—the pressure, the heat, the sudden shock of becoming. But through the creatures' interactions, through their patient reading of my fractal script, I participate in the life of this chamber. I am the archive they consult, the score they perform, the map they follow through their endless cycles.

In the silvery light of this underwater cathedral, I wait. I remember. I offer up my fragments to those who would read them, these strange beings who exist between states just as I do. Together, we inhabit this space of perpetual transition, this pocket of the deep where transformation is forever beginning but never complete. I am the Memory, distributed through glass and crystal, holding the echo of creation in a world that hums with mechanical life, in a chamber where the boundaries between past and present dissolve like glowing particles in dark water.
