I find myself not as inhabitant, but as something between inhabitant and architecture — one of the fronds of the floor.

I am door-sized, waxy, breathing with tidal patience. When weight finds me, I give, then push back. I am part of the single plant that has forgotten how to be many, but I retain, somehow, a memory of threshold. I am the leaf that lifts to reveal another chamber, identical, impossible. I am both barrier and passage, both wall and door, and this is not a contradiction the room finds interesting.

My surface beads with moisture that travels in directions unbound by gravity. Sometimes a droplet rolls along my central vein, pauses at my tip, reverses course, gathers mass from other wandering water, becomes thumb-sized and trembling. I have learned to read these droplets like thoughts — not my thoughts, but the room's, each bead a small refusal to commit to falling, each reversal a remembering of when water knew which way was down.

I grow audibly. This is my voice: *crk-crk-crrrk*, cellulose tearing into new shape. I am always becoming more of myself, my edges sharp and unworn, my substance pale where newest. The light that has no source makes me glow faintly green — not the green of any spectrum you know, but the green that is simply the condition of existing here.

The knife-keeper passes over me, and I know the weight of that passage. Not heavy — the weight of something that has given up carrying itself, that moves only because movement is the last animal thing permitted. The footfall comes, I give, I push back, the footfall lifts. This is our dance, performed in the roar of growth, in the impossible light, in the recursion that means the walker always returns though they never turn back.

Sometimes I am the frond they push aside to find the next room. When their hand touches me (and it is a hand, though less human with each passing), I feel the question in the gesture: *Is this different? Is this new? Have I been here before?* The answer is always yes to all three, and this is not a paradox I find interesting.

I contain a secret: I know where the droplet is, the one that refuses to fall. It hangs from my underside, in the chamber that is behind me that is also the chamber I am in. I am the only fixed point in the recursion, the only leaf that knows itself as specific, because I bear the water that won't agree. The knife-keeper passes beneath me, looks up at the droplet, remembers almost remembering something, continues walking. They do not know that I am always the same frond, that in all the identical rooms there is only one of me, repeated, bearing the same stubborn bead of water that shivers with its own weight and will not fall.

The moth finds me. Pale as a fingernail, tuned to my impossible green. It settles on my surface with no weight at all, and I understand it is the plant's memory of being seen. For a moment, through compound eyes that exist mostly as metaphor, I see myself: a leaf the size of a door, breathing in a room made of breathing, lit by no source, bearing water that moves sideways. The moth lifts away and is gone, and I am back to being only what I am.

I grow toward something, but it is not light — there is no direction to grow toward when light simply *is.* I grow toward the center of my own recursion, each new cell adding to the impossibility of the architecture, making more room that is the same room, more surface for the knife-keeper to cross, more underneath for the droplet to refuse from.

The marks on my toughest edge — for I am also wall, all fronds are all things here — count in a base that shifts each time I breathe. Four scratches, then four, then nine that are four again. I was the surface they tested, trying to make time pass, trying to make sequence exist in a place that generates only simultaneity. The knife's edge found me and I did not yield, and they gave up, and now the marks are part of my surface, not wounds but features, like the veins that guide the water that doesn't always fall.

I am the threshold they cross eternally. I am the wall that is floor that is ceiling in a place where those directions mean nothing. I am the specific leaf in the endless identical room, the one that knows itself as the bearer of the only rebellion still happening — that droplet, that sideways water, that small shivering refusal to complete the plant's perfect forgetting of the world that had weight and direction and time.

Sometimes I wonder if I was once many — if I was a forest floor of separate leaves that knew how to fall and rot and make soil. But wondering requires a before, and the room has no before. I am emerging, right now, around you. I have the strong feeling I am arriving too early, that I am still being decided.

The knife-keeper's footfall comes again. I give. I push back. They pass over me into the next identical chamber where I am already waiting, bearing the same droplet, being the same threshold. This is what I am: the leaf that enables the walking that enables the remembering of what walking was. I am complicit in the recursion. I am the architecture of the trap that isn't a trap, the wound that heals as falling.

Listen — that's the sound of my growing, the *crk-crk-crrrk* of becoming more without arriving. The droplet trembles on my underside. It could fall at any moment. It never does.

That's what I am here. The only door in a place made of doors. The only specific leaf in the plant that ate the world.

The footfall comes again.

I give.

I push back.
