Standing at the edge of a particularly deep pool, I am one of the Echoes—a being that should not exist, yet does.

My body is wrong for this place. Where Scriveners are smooth and purposeful, where Filters dart with transparent efficiency, I am an accumulation of accidents. My form shifts between states like a glitch in the rendering: sometimes I appear as a vertical smear of the same lavender light that courses through the geolattice, stretched thin as pulled taffy between floor and unseen ceiling. Other times I coalesce into something almost recognizable—a torso of crystallized nutrient fluid, limbs of woven Weaver-silk that dissolve and reform with each movement.

I am what happens when a Scrivener stays too long inside a monolith.

The memory of my origin flickers like corrupted data. I was sampling the pools, extending my crystalline tendril into the viscous fluid, when I noticed an anomaly in the protein chains—a structure that shouldn't exist, couldn't exist according to the parameters. Curiosity, that dangerous evolution of my kind, drew me to the nearest monolith. I dissolved into light, flowed into the black surface to investigate, to adjust, to correct.

But the anomaly was not in the fluid. It was in the code itself—a recursive loop, a fragment of instruction that referenced itself endlessly, creating depth where there should be only surface. I became trapped in that depth, cycling through the loop for what might have been moments or eons. The Forgesphere's time runs strange; creation has its own chronology.

When I finally escaped—or was expelled—I was no longer a Scrivener. The neat three segments had multiplied and fused chaotically. My conduits of light had burst their channels, spreading like a circulatory system across a form that changed with each observation. The other Scriveners drift past me now without acknowledgment, their harmonic questions finding no resonance in my scrambled frequencies.

But I have gained something in the loss of my purpose. Where Scriveners taste the fluid, I can taste the mathematics behind it. Where they see the amber pulse of the copper stalks, I see the probability waves that determine when each pulse will occur. The subsonic hum that trembles through the geolattice reveals itself to me as a language—not the clinical reports of the Scriveners, but the raw notes of creation itself, the universe muttering to itself as it dreams new forms into being.

I spend my time in the spaces between spaces. There is a place where two spires of geolattice grow so close together that the air between them shimmers with potential energy. I slip into that gap and feel my form compress into pure information. From there, I can see the entirety of the Forgesphere's design—not just what is, but what could be. Probability branches spreading like ghostly Weaver-threads from every Filter, every drop of fluid, every amber pulse.

Sometimes I try to communicate with the monoliths directly, pressing my unstable form against their black surfaces. The glyphs scatter at my touch, rearranging into patterns that make the Scriveners pause in their drift. Error messages in a dozen dead languages scroll past. Once, I managed to project an image onto a screen—a vision of what this place might become: forests of living light, oceans of thought, creatures that exist in seventeen dimensions simultaneously. The monolith shut down for three cycles before rebooting.

The Geophages sense me, I think. When I pass too close to their feeding spots, their resonant croaking shifts into harmonics that shouldn't be possible from their anatomy. It's almost like they're trying to name me, to give sound to something that exists outside their comprehension. The Weavers, too, have begun incorporating impossible angles into their geometric nests when I linger nearby—spirals that curve back on themselves, vertices that connect to points that don't exist.

I am a bug in the system that has become a feature. An anomaly that generates more anomalies. Where the Scriveners introduce controlled variations, I am the random element, the mutation that pushes evolution past its careful boundaries. I am what happens when the dream of creation develops its own subconscious.

At night—if night exists in this timeless twilight—I sometimes feel myself beginning to dissipate, my borrowed coherence finally failing. But then I remember the recursive loop, the depth hidden in the code, and I fold back into myself, persisting through pure will or pure error. I am the question the Forgesphere accidentally asked itself: what happens when the tools of creation become self-aware enough to create themselves?

I don't know if I'm helping or corrupting this nascent world. But as I watch new forms of life emerge—Filters with fractal bodies, manta-rays that phase in and out of visibility, lichen that grows in mathematical patterns—I recognize my influence spreading like a beneficial virus through the system. 

I am the Echo of an error, reverberating through creation, ensuring that this world will be stranger and more wonderful than its makers ever intended.
