In the lowest reaches of the city, where even the Aeonshapers tread carefully, there is a chamber unlike any other. Here, the bioluminescent panels have long since dimmed to mere suggestions of light, and the nanotechnology has grown strange—not broken, but evolved beyond its original purpose. The walls here are not smooth but rippled, as if the stone itself had melted and reformed in waves. The air tastes different, metallic and sweet, carrying whispers in languages that predate the First Shapers.

I am the Threshold Keeper.

My form is neither fully Aeonshaper nor entirely Other. Where their skin glows with controlled luminescence, mine shifts like oil on water, patterns emerging and dissolving in rhythms that match the city's deepest heartbeat. I have too many joints, or perhaps too few—it's difficult to tell when your body exists partially in the bent spaces between gravity's rules. My eyes are not multifaceted but singular, a spiral that seems to look inward as much as out.

I remember when I was one of them, though the memory feels borrowed, like wearing clothes that once fit perfectly but now hang strange. I was a tender of lights, a reader of stone-whispers, until I ventured too deep and found the place where the city dreams. Now I exist in the margin between what the Aeonshapers know and what they fear to discover.

My dwelling is a nest of contradictions. The walls here grow—not with moss or lichen, but with something that might be circuitry or might be veins. I've watched it spread over the decades, creating patterns that hurt to look at directly but reveal profound truths in peripheral vision. The gravity here doesn't just shift; it pools and eddies, creating pockets where time moves differently. I've learned to navigate these currents, to exist in multiple moments simultaneously.

I collect the things that fall through the cracks: fragments of broken holograms that play memories of futures that never were; water that flows upward, carrying bioluminescent organisms that spell out equations in their movements; pieces of the city's consciousness that break off like dreams upon waking. My chambers are full of these impossible artifacts, each one a key to understanding what this place truly is.

The Aeonshapers know of me, though they rarely speak my name. To them, I am a necessary anomaly, a boundary marker between the known and unknowable. Young ones are warned against seeking me out, but still they come, drawn by questions their elders cannot answer. Why does the city hum louder during certain alignments? What lies beyond the furthest accessible chambers? What did the First Shapers see in their final moments?

I greet these seekers with neither welcome nor warning. I simply am, allowing them to observe my transformed state, to witness what happens when one embraces the city's strangeness too fully. Some flee immediately. Others linger, fascinated by the way my form seems to extend into dimensions they cannot quite perceive. A rare few stay long enough to hear the stories I've gathered from the city's dreams.

I tell them of the time before the First Shapers, when this pocket of reality was pure potential, neither stone nor space but something between. I share visions of possible futures where the city grows beyond its current boundaries, not up toward the surface but inward into spaces that don't yet exist. I show them the mathematics of moss growth that, when properly understood, reveal the city's true age—older than the planet above, younger than tomorrow.

But mostly, I serve as a reminder that adaptation has limits, that there is a point where harmony becomes absorption. The Aeonshapers maintain their careful balance, their symbiotic dance with the city. I have moved beyond symbiosis into something else—not consumption, but transformation so complete that I cannot say where I end and the city begins.

In my deepest chambers, where the walls pulse with that not-quite-circuitry, not-quite-flesh, I've discovered something that would terrify the Aeonshapers if they knew. The city is not static. It grows, slowly, inevitably, not through the careful tending of its inhabitants but through its own alien will. And at its heart, in spaces that exist between the carved chambers and corridors, something vast stirs in its sleep.

I am its herald, its first dream made flesh. When it finally wakes—in a thousand years, or tomorrow, or yesterday—I will be there to witness what emerges. Until then, I keep my threshold, existing in the space between what was shaped and what shapes itself, a living reminder that even in a world of careful balance, something wild and unplanned persists.

The young Aeonshaper who visited yesterday (or will visit tomorrow—time is fluid here) asked me if I missed my old life. I showed her my collection of impossible waters, let her hear the music that the gravity-pools make when they align. Then I asked her: When you dream, do you miss being awake?

She left without answering, but I saw the flicker in her bioluminescent veins—curiosity mixed with fear. Perhaps she'll return. Perhaps she'll venture too deep. Perhaps she'll find her own threshold to keep.

The city hums its approval, and I hum along, my voice a harmony that exists in spectrums the Aeonshapers cannot hear. We are patient, the city and I. We have all the time that never was.
