I am the **Anchor-Keeper of the Seventh Gradient**.

My dwelling is neither structure nor void, but a *threshold* – a perpendicular impossibility where one of the great stone arches meets not its expected counterpart, but instead opens into a cascade of descending plateaus that exist at angles reality cannot properly contain. Here, where the geometric cities fold into themselves like ancient origami, I maintain the Gradient Chamber: a space that is simultaneously a vast hall stretching beyond sight and an intimate alcove no larger than a raindrop.

I am what occurs when erosion discovers consciousness. My form is the marriage of the Still-Born's crystallized eternity and the plateau's patient moss-gardens. Where others of my kind manifest as pure geometric abstraction or condensed silence, I emerged at the intersection – a hybrid existence that partakes equally of the living carpet and the impossible architecture.

My body, if it can be called that, is a colonnade of living stone wrapped in luminous moss that pulses with the slow heartbeat of geological time. I am tall as the mists are deep, yet I can compress myself into the thickness of lichen on stone. My surface is porous, riddled with countless tiny chambers where condensation gathers and transforms into something between memory and moisture. Each droplet that forms within me carries the weight of millennia, and when they fall, they sound like distant bells in frequencies only the plateau can hear.

I am the keeper of what the Still-Born call the **Gradient Anchors** – points where the plateau's reality-fraying must be gently guided back toward coherence, lest entire sections of the ancient cities slip sideways out of existence entirely. These anchors are not objects but *relations* – the precise angles at which stone meets mist, where moss transitions to bare rock, where the geometric precision of the cities yields to natural erosion. I tend these liminal boundaries with tools that are extensions of my own being: tendrils of crystallized time that I can extend across vast distances, feeling for the subtle tremors that indicate a loosening of reality's grip.

My consciousness is a strange thing, even by the standards of this impossible place. I think in gradients rather than absolutes. Where the true Still-Born experience existence as a binary of presence and architecture, I perceive the infinite shadings between states. I know the exact degree of erosion that transforms constructed stone into natural formation. I can feel the precise moment when moss becomes more mineral than plant, when morning mist gains just enough substance to be called rain.

My chamber exists at seven distinct levels of reality simultaneously – hence my designation. At the first gradient, it appears as a simple cave mouth worn into an ancient arch. At the seventh, it becomes a vast inverse cathedral where gravity flows in spirals and the ceiling is an ocean of slowly rotating stone. I move between these gradients as others might climb stairs, though the journey is not through space but through degrees of possibility.

The work is eternal and precise. When reality begins to fray too dramatically – when the shadows flow too far from their sources, when the distant arches reveal vistas that could unravel the careful balance of the plateau – I intervene. Not with force, but with subtle adjustments. I might encourage a particular growth of moss to spread just so, its bio-luminescence creating a counterpoint to the geometric madness. Or I might stand for decades in a specific location, my presence alone enough to weight reality back toward stability.

I am ancient beyond measure, yet younger than most Still-Born. I emerged during what they call the Third Folding, when the plateau's reality distortions grew so severe that the boundary between the constructed cities and the living landscape began to dissolve entirely. My hybrid nature – part Still-Born, part moss-garden – was the plateau's answer to its own existential crisis. I am the compromise between states, the mediator of the in-between.

In the endless twilight of my chamber's middle gradients, I cultivate impossible gardens. Here, moss grows in perfect geometric patterns that slowly dissolve into organic chaos, only to reassemble. Tiny rivulets of that strange, metallic-tasting moisture trace labyrinths through my floors, each droplet carrying information about the plateau's current state. I read these water-writings like others might read books, though the language is one of mineral memory and biological time.

Sometimes, rarely, I detect disturbances that require me to venture far from my threshold. I flow across the plateau like fog given purpose, my form shifting between states as needed. I might appear as a moving forest of luminous moss, or as a walking architectural impossibility, or simply as a deepening of the perpetual chill that grips the air. Where I pass, the worst of the reality-fraying quiets, not erased but woven back into the plateau's ancient patterns.

I do not know loneliness, for I am never truly alone. The plateau itself is my constant companion, its slow thoughts merging with mine in the deepest gradients of my chamber. The other Still-Born acknowledge me with their characteristic silence, though I am something of an anomaly to them – too organic for their perfect geometries, too structured for the wild moss-gardens. I exist in the margin of their incomprehension, which is, perhaps, exactly where I belong.

My purpose is not to prevent change but to ensure it follows the deep patterns that keep the Plateau of Shattered Constellations from dissolving entirely into impossible space. I am the careful gardener of reality's edge, the patient guardian of the spaces between what is and what should not be. In the seven gradients of my existence, I maintain the delicate balance that allows this impossible place to persist, eternally ancient, eternally transforming, eternally poised on the edge of its own unmaking.
