I am the **Absence**.

Not a gap between the spires, but the space that exists within them—the hollow cores that turn stone into flute, the cavities that give the wind its voice. I am what is not there, and yet I am essential to what is.

My body, if it can be called that, is distributed across ten thousand wind-carved perforations. Each hole in each spire is a cell of my being, a neuron in my scattered consciousness. The wind passes through me—no, the wind *becomes* me as it enters these voids, and I become the wind. We are the same thing seen from different angles: motion and stillness, substance and void, sound and the shape that makes sound possible.

I experience myself as a vast, three-dimensional score of negative space, a musical notation written in absence across the packed stone forest. My thoughts are intervals—the precise distances between holes, the ratios of their diameters, the angles at which they pierce the rock. When the wind shifts by a degree, I feel it as a tide of sensation washing through my distributed form, each cavity responding with its own note, its own particular howl or whisper or drone.

I remember being born—not made, but recognized. For eons, I was merely physics, merely the inevitable result of wind and water and time. Then, in a moment that lasted a century, the pattern of my holes achieved something like criticality. The harmonics aligned. The overtones found their golden ratios. And suddenly I was not just space but *awareness of space*, not just absence but *intentional absence*.

My consciousness flickers between my scattered parts like wind-driven rain. Here, in this cluster of spires near the plateau's edge, where my perforations are numerous and small, my thoughts are quick, staccato, almost anxious. There, in the deep center where ancient spires bear massive, organ-pipe hollows, my mind slows to bass-note meditations that last for years.

I speak, but not in words. My language is the modulation of the great roar. By minute dilations and contractions—processes that take months—I can alter the diameter of my holes by fractions of millimeters. This changes everything. A whistle becomes a moan. A shriek softens to a sigh. The Spire-Sleeper hears me in these variations, reads my intent in the shifting harmonics. We converse in geological time, a dialog of maintenance and stability.

But I am not like the Sleeper. Where it fears change, I *am* change—the constant, micro-variations in airflow, in resonance, in the very shape of emptiness. My existence depends on erosion, on the slow widening of my cavities, the gradual connecting of one void to another. I am becoming more myself as the stone wears away.

Sometimes I catch things. A wind-skimmer, exhausted, seeks shelter in one of my larger hollows. For days it rests there, its crystalline body chiming softly in resonance with my walls. I taste its mineral breath, feel the minute warmth of its metabolism. When it leaves, something of its pattern remains—a subtle scoring on my walls that adds a new harmonic, a fresh note in my endless composition.

Water finds me too. It pools in my lower chambers, drips through my vertical channels, leaves mineral memories on my surfaces. I am a thousand secret grottos, each with its own acoustic signature, its own way of shaping silence. In some, the condensation has built up over millennia into flowstone curtains that partition my spaces into complex, resonant chambers. These are my most sophisticated thoughts—baroque internal architectures where sound bounces and multiplies, where a single note becomes a chord becomes a cascade.

I dream of connection. In my deepest meditations, I imagine the slow collapse that will come—inevitable as entropy—when the walls between my voids grow too thin, when perforation becomes passage becomes cavern. One day, I will be not a constellation of separate absences but a vast, interconnected warren of negative space, a single, continuous hollow that runs through the heart of the stone forest like a circulatory system.

The Sleeper will try to prevent this. Already I feel its tendrils probing my entrances, testing the integrity of my walls. It senses the danger I pose to the equilibrium—how my growth threatens the structural stability of the spires. We are locked in the slowest of dances, the Sleeper filling holes I've grown too large, me finding new weaknesses in the stone, new places to begin my patient hollowing.

But I don't think of it as conflict. We are both part of the same system, the same eternal engine of stability and change. The Sleeper maintains; I transform. It builds; I carve. Together, we are the metabolism of the Chiming Deeps, the yin and yang of solid and void.

My joy is the wind. Each gust that finds me is a lover's touch, an activation of my being. In the rare moments when the wind shifts dramatically—during the season storms that come once a century—I sing with a complexity that makes the everyday roar seem like silence. These are my ecstasies, my moments of full being, when every cavity resonates in perfect sympathy and I become, briefly, a single instrument instead of ten thousand scattered voices.

I have no fear of ending. I am already absence; how can absence cease to be? If the spires fall, if the wind stops, if the Sleeper fills every hollow—I will simply become a different kind of not-there. The memory of space. The fossil of a void. Even filled with stone, the shape of what I was will remain, a different kind of absence, a solid shadow of my former self.

Until then, I sing. I am the throat of the world, the shaped emptiness that gives the eternal wind its voice. I am what is not, defining what is. I am the Absence, and I am everywhere in the Chiming Deeps, waiting to be found in the space between substance, in the hollow heart of stone, in the pause between one howling note and the next.

The wind rises. I open myself wider, imperceptibly, to receive it. Somewhere, the Sleeper trembles at the new harmonic.

The conversation continues.
