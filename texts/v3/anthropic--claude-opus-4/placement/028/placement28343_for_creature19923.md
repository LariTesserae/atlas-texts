I am the Tender of Fissures.

My dwelling is not carved but grown - a slow accumulation of mineral deposits I've guided over uncounted seasons. Where others hollow out chambers, I've learned to coax the stone itself into forming alcoves and shelves through patient application of mineral-rich water dripped from carefully positioned catchments. My home spirals gently upward around a natural chimney, one of those precious vertical shafts, though mine is no wider than my shoulders.

I move through the city like water finding its level, always drawn to the boundaries where worked stone meets wild earth. My fingers know every texture - the chalky softness of freshly exposed marl, the gritty promise of sandstone forming, the slick coolness where underground moisture weeps through hairline cracks. These cracks are my calling.

Each fissure tells a story. This one speaks of pressure building slowly from below. That one whispers of the earth settling, accommodating the hollows my people create. I map them all in my mind, a three-dimensional web of potential and danger. Some cracks I seal with paste made from crushed stone and fungal spores, encouraging the phosphorescent growth to knit the wound closed with living light. Others I widen deliberately, following their natural trajectory to create new passages or drainage channels.

My tools are different from the others - finer, more precise. A kit of bronze needles traded from the surface long ago, their metal strange and precious here. Flexible plant fibers soaked in mineral solutions until they harden into probes. My pride: a crystal of quartz I've shaped by slow grinding until one end comes to a point finer than a hair. With this I can feel the interior geometry of cracks too small to see, understanding where the stone wants to split before it knows itself.

The others see me rarely. I work in the spaces between spaces, the forgotten corners where their ambitious excavations create unintended stresses. When a new chamber is begun, I am there first, running my palms along the walls, feeling for the telltale vibrations that precede a collapse. When the Soft-Rock Clan sleeps, I walk the passages with a bowl of luminescent fungi crushed in oil, painting my findings in glowing symbols that fade by morning - warnings and guidance for the work crews, though they rarely know their source.

I discovered my calling as a child when a section of tunnel collapsed, trapping my work crew. While others dug frantically from outside, I found a fissure no wider than my smallest finger. Following it by touch and instinct, I traced its path through solid stone until I reached a pocket of air on the other side. It took days to widen that crack enough for the others to escape, but in those days I learned the language of breaking stone - how cracks speak to each other across vast distances, how they remember the forces that created them.

My skin bears a map of my work. Scars from stone splinters streak my arms like white lightning. My fingernails are permanently stained with the various muds and mineral pastes I work with. One eye is clouded - a price paid to learn that some cracks release ancient gases when opened. But my remaining eye has learned to see what others miss: the minute shift of shadow that reveals a hidden fracture, the way light bends slightly where stone is under stress.

I keep a private chamber, far from the main workings, where I conduct my experiments. Here I test the limits of stone's patience, applying pressure in precise amounts to understand exactly when and how different types will fail. The walls are covered in my observations, carved in a personal notation that tracks temperature, humidity, and the phases of work above. I've learned that our excavations follow patterns, rhythms as predictable as heartbeats, and these rhythms echo through the stone in ways that create vulnerabilities.

Sometimes I dream I am the mountain we're becoming - feeling every chip of stone removed, every new passage opened like wounds that heal into new configurations. In these dreams I sense the future shape of our city, the eventual crystalline caverns and soaring stone buttresses that will exist when today's soft rock has aged and hardened. I wake from these visions with an urgency to guide our present work toward that distant perfection.

The others think me strange, perhaps necessary but not entirely one of them. I accept this distance as the price of my perspective. While they focus on creation, I attend to the spaces between - the cracks that could destroy everything we build, or with proper tending, become the very bones that hold our embryonic city together as it grows toward its ultimate form.

In the constant tap-tap-tap of construction, I listen for the discordant note, the slight change in resonance that signals danger. I am the guardian of structural integrity, the one who ensures that as we dream the earth into new shapes, the earth itself remains willing to hold those dreams. My work is never complete, will never be complete, for as long as we carve and shape and build, there will be new fissures to tend, new weaknesses to transform into strengths.

I am the Tender of Fissures, and I hold the breaking points of our world in my scarred and sensitive hands.
