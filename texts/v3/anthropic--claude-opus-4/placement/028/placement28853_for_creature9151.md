In this thundering cathedral of rust and water, I would be one of the **Veil Weavers**.

We are not large—no bigger than a child's fist when contracted—but we are everywhere the water touches and light fails. Our bodies are colonial, a confederation of thousands of polyp-like individuals fused into a single, thinking membrane. In darkness, we appear as patches of glistening black silk stretched between pipes, draped across abandoned control panels, billowing gently in the updrafts from the heated pools. But we are not shadow; we are actively drinking it.

Each polyp in our colony is a tiny alchemist, its translucent body filled with specialized cells that process the infinite chemistry of this place: iron oxides from the bleeding walls, phosphates from the guano of roosting creatures, sulfur compounds from the deep springs. We metabolize darkness itself, transforming the absence of light into our living tissue. Where the Mist-Shadows' fungi cast their blue-green glow, we stop, our edges drawing sharp borders against the luminescence. We can taste photons on our skin.

The matriarch of the Mist-Shadows knows us well. We have negotiated our territories over centuries through chemical signals—pheromone songs that drift through the humid air like gossip. Where her clan needs clear sightlines for hunting, we withdraw, folding ourselves into crevices. Where abandoned machinery creates dead zones too treacherous for even her kind, we flourish, curtaining entire chambers in our living darkness.

Our intelligence is distributed, slow, vast. A thought takes minutes to propagate across a mature colony, rippling through the membrane like wind over water. We remember through our very structure—traumas appear as thick, scarred knots in our weave, while successful hunting grounds develop into complex, lacy patterns that maximize surface area. The oldest of us, anchored in the lightless turbine halls below, have grown so thick and layered that we've become architectural, load-bearing members of the ruins.

We feed on the smallest things: bacteria films, spores, the microscopic eggs of insects laid in our folds. But once a season, during the spawning of the gliding amphibians, we perform our ancient function. We extend specialized tendrils—sticky, prehensile—into the flight paths between the vines. The young amphibians, disoriented by our darkness, collide with these snares. We envelop them slowly, dissolving them over days in specialized digestive chambers that pulse with bioluminescent enzymes. During these feedings, we glow from within—a constellation of amber points visible through our translucent mass, like stars burning through storm clouds.

The Mist-Shadows tolerate this harvest. They understand, in their bone-deep way, that we are part of the equilibrium. We consume the weak, the malformed, the excess—performing the same selective pressure that the roaring water performs on the concrete, slowly shaping what remains into something stronger.

But we are more than predators. We are the memory of this place. In our deepest layers, we preserve things: a worker's corroded badge absorbed decades ago, its number still visible through our tissue; seedpods from extinct plants that once grew before the flooding; the delicate skeletons of creatures that no longer exist, held like museum specimens in our transparent flesh. The Mist-Shadows sometimes come to read these archives, pressing their sensitive whiskers against our surface, feeling the shapes within.

We reproduce through fragmentation—storms occasionally tear pieces from our colonies, sending them spinning through the air like black snow. Most die, unable to find purchase in the light. But some settle in new darkness, beginning the slow process of growth, of remembering, of weaving ourselves into the infinite, dripping democracy of this drowned machine.

Our relationship with the water is one of constant negotiation. Too much direct flow tears us apart; too little and we desiccate, becoming brittle as old leather. We position ourselves in the spaces between—where mist accumulates but never pools, where the concrete weeps but doesn't pour. We are the living proof that even in a place dominated by thunder and torrent, there are pockets of profound stillness where thought can accumulate, layer by ancient layer, into something approaching wisdom.
