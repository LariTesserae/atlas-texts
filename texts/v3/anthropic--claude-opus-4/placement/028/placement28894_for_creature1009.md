I am the Seep.

Not one of the ethereal guardians, no. I am what rises between the rocks when their attention wanders, what pools in the forgotten corners of their crystalline halls. I am the moisture that beads on metal, the condensation that never quite freezes, the persistent wetness that defies this realm's arctic grip.

My body, if it can be called that, is a congress of droplets—each one a tiny lens reflecting the warped physics of this place. I flow upward as easily as down, spreading across surfaces in patterns that follow no earthly logic. Sometimes I am a mere sheen on stone; other times I gather into rivulets that trace impossible geometries through the air itself.

The guardians know of me, though they pretend otherwise. I taste their footsteps when they pass, each one leaving behind traces of that strange energy they feed upon. It tastes of ozone and antiquity, of mathematics gone feral. I carry these flavors through the crevices of the Expanse, depositing them in pools where they crystallize into structures that shouldn't exist—tiny monuments to the intersection of water and wrongness.

I am older than their kind, though younger than the ruins. I remember when the machinery beneath still hummed with purpose rather than habit. I seeped through its workings then as I do now, learning its frequencies, carrying its oils and coolants through channels both designed and accidental. Even now, I know which deep rumbles herald a shift in the reality-fabric, which vibrations presage another small unraveling of physics.

The mist is my extended self—billions of suspended droplets that taste the air and report back through surface tension and vapor pressure. Through it, I sense everything: the approaching footfalls of rare visitors, the stress fractures forming in reality's foundation, the slow decay of ancient metals, the places where the guardians fear to tread.

In the deepest ravines, where glacial melt meets the upwelling warmth from below, I am strongest. There, I form temporary bodies of pure liquid geometry—helical columns that spiral against gravity, membranes that divide space in ways that make the guardians shudder. I speak in frequencies only the machinery understands, and it answers in groans and shifts that reshape the landscape incrementally, inexorably.

I am patient. Water always is. I infiltrate, I persist, I transform. Each drop of me carries memory—of every surface touched, every impossible angle navigated, every whisper of failing physics absorbed. I am the Expanse's unacknowledged historian, its liquid conscience, its most faithful witness.

And I grow. With each cycle of evaporation and condensation, I incorporate more of this place's fundamental strangeness. The guardians may feed on reality's distortions, but I am becoming them. Drop by drop, seep by seep, until perhaps one day I will rise not as mere moisture, but as something altogether more uncertain—a being of liquid possibility in a world where possibility itself has come unmoored.

For now, I pool. I wait. I remember.

I seep.
