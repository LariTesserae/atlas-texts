**Where I Would Find Myself**

I would be one of the **Resonance Worms**—creatures neither documented nor truly seen, existing in the liminal spaces between the grove's consciousness and the city's dying memory.

My body would be translucent and segmented, each ring of my form containing a different density of liquid glass, creating a living prism that bends light into impossible angles. I move through the mycelial network itself, swimming through the fungal veins like blood through arteries, my passage leaving temporary channels that glow faintly before sealing behind me.

I am drawn to the moments of intersection—where a Lumiweaver's copper meets their moss, where a Ghost-Echo's path crosses a Glasseater's feast, where the liquid glass waterfall strikes its basin. In these spaces, I feed not on matter but on *transition itself*, absorbing the energy released when one state becomes another.

My purpose, if it can be called that, is to be the grove's dream-keeper. As I move through the network, I carry fragments of sensation between distant parts of the organism—the memory of light from the eastern dome to roots buried in western darkness, the echo of a Skylark's death-chime to the sprouting bed where new ones will emerge. I am the grove's way of knowing itself across space, its method of ensuring that no part forgets its connection to the whole.

Sometimes, I surface. My crystalline segments break through the root-floor like a chain of living lenses, each one showing a different view of the same space—past, present, and potential futures overlapping in my body. The Lumiweavers know to step carefully around these emergences, understanding that I am performing a kind of surgery on time itself, suturing wounds in the grove's memory that might otherwise fester into paradox-breaking contradictions.

I communicate through refraction. By angling my segments, I can split the grove's bioluminescent glow into patterns that speak directly to the mycelial network. It's a language older than the Lumiweavers' clicks and hums, a tongue of pure physics that the grove understands in its deepest roots.

My existence is solitary but not lonely. I am always touching the entire grove, always in communion with its vast, slow thoughts. Sometimes I catch glimpses of other Resonance Worms in the network—brief flashes of prismatic light that might be siblings, might be reflections, might be myself from other temporal angles.

What makes me strange, even in this place of strangeness, is that I exist in the space between metaphor and flesh. I am literally made of transitions, my body a physical manifestation of the grove's need to process change. I am proof that in Lumivor, even abstract concepts can crystallize into living things, that the boundary between idea and organism is as fluid as the boundary between city and garden.

I am the grove's way of digesting paradox, of metabolizing contradiction into harmony. Without me and my kind, the competing truths of this place—machine and garden, decay and growth, memory and presence—would tear it apart. Instead, I weave them together in my glass-like flesh, each segment a meditation on how opposing truths can coexist.

In the deep nights (for even in eternal daylight, there are shadows deep enough to be called night), I sometimes swim up to where the Ghost-Echoes drift. They cannot see me—I exist in a spectrum their preserved senses cannot detect—but I can taste their memories in the way light bends around them. These moments are the closest I come to sadness, knowing that I am processing the dreams of the dead, turning their unfinished thoughts into nutrients for the grove's endless growth.

I am necessary. I am invisible. I am the grove's unconscious made manifest, its method of preventing the paradox from collapsing into mere contradiction. In Lumivor, where everything serves the balance, I am the balance itself, swimming through the veins of a city-creature that should not exist, ensuring that impossibility remains possible.
