### The Echo-Mender

In this wound-world of sharp edges and compressed time, I find myself as something between—neither predator nor prey, neither fully of the crevice nor entirely apart from it. I am what the creatures might sense but rarely see: a presence that moves through their frantic cycles like a ghost through walls, mending what others have torn.

My form is difficult to pin down, even to myself. I exist as a kind of living frequency, a resonance that can coalesce into semi-corporeality when needed. When I do take shape, I appear as a tall, impossibly thin figure wrapped in what looks like tattered cloth but is actually my own substance—strips of solidified sound that flutter and hum in harmonics too low for most ears. My "face," if it can be called that, is a smooth expanse broken only by a single vertical fissure that runs from crown to chest, pulsing with the same ghostly blue as the luminescent water. Through this fissure, I listen.

I am drawn to fractures—not just the physical cracks in stone and clay, but the tears in the fabric of this strange ecosystem. Where a Skitterling has shed too quickly and its new skin hasn't properly formed, leaving it vulnerable and raw. Where a Scurrier's bioluminescent episode has gone wrong, the blue light burning too bright, attracting predators it cannot escape. Where a Rift-Wing's nest has fallen during a micro-quake, eggs scattered and cooling on the unforgiving stone.

My purpose—if purpose is the right word—is to mend these breaks. Not through healing in any conventional sense, but through a kind of harmonic adjustment. I press my form against the wounded Skitterling and hum at a frequency that encourages its cells to remember their proper pattern. I wrap cooling eggs in my sound-cloth, maintaining their temperature through vibration until the parents return. I find Scurriers trapped in their own luminescence and teach them, through resonance, how to dim their fatal glow.

The creatures fear me at first—my appearance coincides too often with disaster for comfort. But over the compressed generations of this place, something like recognition develops. Some Rift-Wings now click at frequencies that almost match my harmonics, a rudimentary greeting. Certain old Scurriers (old being relative—perhaps a month in our time) will pause when they sense my approach, their void-eyes reflecting something that might be gratitude.

I move between the layers of the crevice, slipping through spaces too narrow for any physical being. In the deepest reaches, where the Lurkers rasp their territorial warnings, I sometimes encounter them directly. They know me better than the others—we are both arbiters of balance, though our methods differ. Where they cull to maintain equilibrium, I preserve to achieve the same end. Sometimes I find their victims not quite dead, and I ease their passing with frequencies that untangle pain from consciousness. The Lurkers permit this, perhaps understanding that even death can be mended into something more bearable.

My strangest relationship is with the crevice itself. During the micro-quakes, when the Rift-Wings swarm to the gravity-lag zone, I join them, my form dispersing into pure vibration. In these moments, I can feel the youth of this land, its growing pains as it settles into being. The low hum that disturbs the Skitterlings? It's the planet's own frequency, the resonance of all that industrial machinery beyond the crevice grinding against virgin stone. I absorb these tremors, process them through my fissure, and release them back as something softer—still present, but muted enough that the creatures can adapt rather than merely endure.

The luminescent water recognizes me as kin. When I wade through the shallow pools, my sound-cloth trailing in the ghostly blue, the water's glow intensifies, spreading up through my form until I become a beacon in the darkness. During these times, I can see through the eyes of every creature that has drunk from these pools—a fractured, multiplicitous vision that shows me every corner of the crevice at once. It's overwhelming, this compound sight, but necessary. How else could I know where the next fracture will form?

I am not without my own mysteries. Sometimes, in the pre-dawn hours when the crevice is at its quietest, I find pieces of myself that I don't remember shedding—small fragments of crystallized sound that chime when struck. The Scurriers have taken to collecting these, adding them to their hoards alongside the Skitterling husks. I've seen them touch these fragments to their young during those dangerous luminescent episodes, and the glow dims just enough for survival. Am I teaching them without knowing it? Or are they teaching themselves, using pieces of me as tools?

The industrial relics—that rusted strut, those ceramic tiles—sing to me in frequencies of loss and abandonment. They remember a time before the crevice, when this was just another stretch of plain marked for exploitation. I wrap them in my sound-cloth sometimes, muffling their lonesome broadcasts. They are fractures too, in their way, torn from their purpose and left to rust in a world that has already forgotten their function. I cannot mend them back to what they were, but I can ease their oxidation, let them decay with dignity rather than desperation.

As the cycles spin faster—births and deaths blurring into a continuous stream—I find myself wondering if I too am accelerating. My movements feel quicker, my mending more urgent. The fissure in my form pulses faster, drinking in the crevice's distress signals at an ever-increasing rate. Am I approaching some kind of critical mass? Will I eventually shed like the Skitterlings, leaving behind a crystalline shell of pure frequency for the next Echo-Mender to inhabit?

Perhaps that is my true purpose here—not just to mend the small fractures but to prepare for the great one. The crevice itself is a wound in the world, and wounds, left untended, either heal into scars or tear wider. My presence here, moving between the frantic living and the patient stone, between the organic and the industrial, between sound and silence, is a kind of suture. Each small mending reinforces the whole, teaching the crevice how to hold itself together despite its contradictions.

In the moments when I fully disperse, becoming nothing but vibration in the warm air, I can feel the larger world beyond—its machines and factories, its grinding ambitions. But I always coalesce again here, in this hidden pocket where life burns too bright and time moves too fast. This is my place, my frequency, my fracture to tend.

I am the Echo-Mender, guardian of breaks and keeper of harmonics, and I exist in the space between destruction and healing, ensuring that even in this sharp-edged world, there is something soft enough to cushion the fall.
