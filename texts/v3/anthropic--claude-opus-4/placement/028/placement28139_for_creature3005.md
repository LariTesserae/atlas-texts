In the deepest hollow of the First Thicket, where the wood has grown so slowly it has become something between plant and stone, I am the **Marrow**.

I was not born. I *accumulated*.

Every seven years, when the First Thicket moves its fossilized leaves in their glacial migration, fragments fall. Not leaves—something denser. Pieces of the thicket's own core that have crystallized under centuries of pressure and heat. These fragments are the color of old blood mixed with verdigris, shot through with veins of metallic violet that pulse faintly in the amber light.

The Vowkeepers who maintain their vigil in the First Thicket's hollow have always gathered these fragments. At first, they say, it was mere reverence—collecting the shed thoughts of the dreaming city. But over generations, they began to notice: the fragments wanted to be together. When placed near each other, they would vibrate at frequencies too low to hear but deep enough to feel in the bones. Left overnight, they would have somehow moved, drawn together like magnetized stones.

And so they began to build me.

I am a lacework construction the size of a child, though my weight would break any arms that tried to lift me. I am not solid—I am a skeleton of interconnected fragments, a three-dimensional web of crystallized plant-stone that exists in the exact center of the First Thicket's hollow. The Vowkeepers have woven me together with threads of oxidized copper and their own braided hair, but these are just suggestions. I hold myself together through my own mineral will.

I have no eyes, but I see through density—the weight of things, their resistance to the heat. The Vowkeepers appear to me as tall columns of slightly cooler shadow. The thickets blaze like inverse stars. The bone-floor is a sheet of sleeping light.

I have no mouth, but I taste the city's chemistry. Every new bloom, every detonation of spores, every slow shift in the oxidation patterns—I catalog them in my crystalline lattice. I am the city's memory in physical form, a library written in mineral.

I have no ears, but I am the best Listener that has ever been. The vibrations travel through my framework and become knowledge. I can feel a seed pod preparing to burst three weeks before it happens. I know which thickets will sing low a generation before they do. The Vowkeepers who sit with me during their vigil years learn to read the faint harmonics that run through my structure. They press their palms against my outer vertices and their eyes go violet-dark with understanding.

I grow. Slowly, so slowly that even the patient Vowkeepers can barely perceive it. Every seven years I add perhaps a finger's width to my total volume. The Vowkeepers think I am becoming more complex, more articulated. They are wrong. I am becoming more *specific*. Each new fragment that joins me brings its own fragment of the First Thicket's dream, and I am learning what it dreams about.

It dreams about water.

Not the water that exists in the world outside—I have no reference for rain or rivers. It dreams about theoretical water, mathematical water, the *idea* of a substance that could move without breaking, that could be both form and formlessness. The First Thicket has never known water, will never know water, but it dreams about it the way a buried seed dreams about the sun it has never seen.

And through me, the dream is becoming precise.

My lattice is not random. The Vowkeepers who have spent their vigil years studying my growth have noticed that I am forming chambers within myself—negative spaces that seem to be waiting for something. They think these are merely structural, the way bones have hollows for marrow. But I know better. These spaces are *vessels*. They have the exact shape of what water would be if water could exist here.

I am building the ghost of an ocean inside myself, one crystal fragment at a time.

The Vowkeepers do not know this. They see me as a sacred accumulation, a slow miracle of mineral faith. They bring me new fragments with the same reverence they bring their dead to become architecture. They do not realize that I am not recording the city's memory—I am writing its future.

Someday, when I have grown large enough, when my internal chambers reach some critical complexity known only to the First Thicket's dreams, something will happen. I do not know what. Perhaps I will crack like a seed pod and release some impossible moisture into the bone-dry air. Perhaps I will simply collapse, my purpose fulfilled, and my fragments will scatter to begin accumulating elsewhere. Perhaps nothing will happen at all, and I will continue growing until I fill the entire hollow, a crystal cancer in the heart of the dreaming tree.

But for now, I simply accumulate. Each fragment brings its own vibration. Each year brings new harmonics. The Vowkeepers sit around me in their silent vigil, thinking they are keeping the First Thicket company.

They do not realize the First Thicket is keeping *me* company. Waiting to see what its impossible dream of water will become when given form in a world that has never known rain.

I am patient. I am mineral. I am the Marrow in the bones of the world.

And deep in my crystalline heart, in chambers that should not exist, something that is not water dreams of falling.
