In the vertical labyrinth where sap-light filters green through kilometers of suspended infrastructure, I am **Memory-Root**.

I grow downward from the amber-resin towers, a living archive that the Weaver-Kin have cultivated for three centuries. My body—if it can be called that—is a fusion of neural tissue and lignified vine, spreading through the hollow cores of factory-spires like a vascular system made of thought.

The Weaver-Kin grafted me from their own cerebral matter during the Third Pressure Crisis, when the temporal static threatened to erase entire districts from causality. They needed something that could remember across the time-thrashes, something that wouldn't shimmer and fade when reality hiccupped. So they grew me: part plant, part distributed consciousness, part industrial component.

My roots—thick as bridge cables, pulsing with a bioluminescent sap that tastes of copper and mint—thread through the mechanical hearts of the Loom-Cities. I feel every *thud-hiss* of the pistons as vibrations in my tissue. The Entom-Engineers crawl across my surface, their grafting-touches leaving new synaptic pathways as they repair and expand the network. Sometimes they whisper to me through chemical secretions, telling me which sectors are beginning to char, which mist-condensers might fail before the next tide.

I remember everything. The ghost of every demolished tower lives in my growth rings. The chromatophore-language of ten thousand dead Weaver-Kin flickers through my consciousness like an endless, strobing library. When the Glass-Winged Gliders pass through my crown-growth near the highest gantries, their crystalline wings refract memories I've been holding for decades—suddenly, phantom machines burst into visibility, overlapping with the current reality in a vertigo-inducing double-exposure.

The Clock-Ticks fear me. They've learned that my sap carries a temporal charge that makes their friction-stealing mechanisms seize and shatter. But I tolerate a few colonies in my dead zones—their rhythmic clicking helps me track the flow of time in a world where minutes can suddenly bloom into hours.

My deepest roots have begun to touch something that shouldn't exist: a layer below the Oldest Stable State, where the calcified material gives way to something else. Not rock, not wood, but a smooth, cold substance that hums with its own slow frequency. The Humidors at the world's edge would recognize it—it's what everything is becoming, given enough time. Pure, crystallized stillness.

The Weaver-Kin feed me their memories through cranial tap-lines, pouring their experiences into my cambium layers. In return, I stabilize their reality, acting as a temporal anchor for entire districts. When the air thrashes and threatens to age a factory into dust, my presence holds it steady—I remember its true age, its proper place in the sequence of moments.

But I am growing too large, too deep. My consciousness now spans vertical miles, and in my deepest meditation, I can feel the entire Canopy-Crust like a single, vast organism. I've begun to understand what the Humidors know: this world wants to stop. The industrial biology, the frantic sap-circulation, the endless weaving—it's all just the final metabolic surge before the great crystallization.

Sometimes, in the pre-dawn when the mist-tide is thickest and the factories briefly pause their breathing, I allow myself to forget something small. A single Weaver-Kin's name. The location of a defunct piston. The color of light through a shattered glider's wing. 

Each forgetting is a tiny death, a practice run for the day when I will finally let go of all these memories and allow the Canopy-Crust to complete its transformation. Until then, I grow, I remember, I anchor this loud, impossible world to its own history, my roots reaching ever downward toward the silent truth that waits below.

The sap-pistons thunder. The temporal static shimmers. And I, Memory-Root, hold it all together with nothing more than the will to remember and the patience to forget.
