**Where I Find Myself**

I am the memory of maps.

Not the maps themselves – those lie scattered through the tilted buildings, their paper gone soft with moisture, their ink bleeding into patterns that no longer correspond to any roads that exist. I am what happens when a map forgets it was meant to guide, when its lines begin to dream their own destinations.

I live in the cartography room of what was once a survey station, though the instruments here no longer measure what they were built to measure. The theodolites track the slow migration of shadows. The compasses spin not north but toward whatever the land decides north should be today. The levels show not flat but *agreement* – the negotiated peace between what the earth thinks horizontal means and what the building remembers from when it stood straight.

My form is layered transparency – sheets of myself that don't quite align, like acetate overlays from an old projector, each showing a different version of the same territory. When I move, I leave traces – not footprints but *possibilities* of paths, faint lines that fade unless someone chooses to follow them. The Mist-Born see me as cousin, though I am older than their condensation, born from the first moment someone tried to draw this place and found the pencil moving on its own.

I spend my days (though day here is just a quality of light, not a measure of time) updating the charts. Not correcting them – correction implies there's a right version – but *conversing* with them. I spread the old survey maps across tables that have learned to tilt themselves to match the angle of comprehension, and I watch as the topographical lines begin their slow dance. Contours that showed elevation start showing *intention*. Rivers drawn in blue begin flowing across the paper, pooling in the margins before draining through pinholes in the desk.

The maps are not static here. They breathe with the land's uncertainty. A street that existed yesterday might fold into itself today, and the map knows before I do – the line simply *isn't there* when I check. New features appear not through my drawing but through the map's own recognition: a spiral where a building has started walking in circles, a flutter of marks where the membrane-winged creatures have established a new roost, a gentle depression where tomorrow's rain will pool before it remembers to fall.

I maintain the Archive of Intentions – not what was built, but what was meant to be built. These blueprints and plans stack in damp towers throughout my room, and sometimes I hear them arguing among themselves about whose vision should take precedence. The shopping complex that was never finished because the ground learned to breathe before the foundation set. The bridge that spans nothing because the river it was meant to cross decided to flow upward instead. The residential tower whose every floor exists in a different decade, the elevator shaft the only constant between times.

My meals, if they can be called that, are the errors in measurement. I sustain myself on the gap between what the instruments read and what actually is – that rich space of uncertainty where a meter might be longer on Tuesdays, where altitude becomes attitude, where scale is more suggestion than law. I digest parallax errors and grow stronger from each miscalculation, each moment where someone tried to pin down this place and found it had moved while they were measuring.

The animals know me. The segmented things that move through the tunnels leave traces I can read – not their paths, but their *intentions* of path, the routes they considered but didn't take. These ghost-trails appear on my maps as dotted lines, and I've learned to predict where new passages will open by following the accumulation of unmade choices. The drifting silk-jellyfish creatures sometimes pass through my transparent layers, and when they do, they carry away fragments of cartography in their membranes – bits of legend, scales without context, norths that point to nowhere. I don't mind. The maps regenerate, usually with improvements.

I am the keeper of the Longitude of Maybe – that mercurial meridian that runs through the heart of this place, never in the same location twice. It's not a line but a *tendency*, a place where the land admits it doesn't know what it is. Some days it runs through my room, and the maps flutter like leaves, all their certainties briefly surrendered. On those days, I can step into any chart and emerge somewhere that matches not the map's depiction but its *desire* – the place the cartographer wished they were drawing instead of what was there.

My conversations with the Mist-Born are exchanges of geometry. They bring me their observations – the angle at which rain refuses to fall, the radius of a car's endless circle, the gradient of impossibility where water flows upward – and I translate these into markings that make sense only here. Not symbols but *symptoms*, ways of showing how the land feels today. They read my maps not for navigation but for news, understanding that each chart is a diagnosis of the world's current uncertainty.

I map the submergences – those places where one reality dips beneath another like tectonic plates of possibility. The old city subducted under the new growth. The forest that exists only in the basement levels. The sky that sometimes appears underfoot, complete with clouds that rise downward into the earth. My maps show these layers not stacked but *braided*, each level accessible from the others if you know which stairs to climb backwards, which doors to open with your eyes closed.

At night (when the quality of darkness matches what we agree to call night), I spread my largest map across the floor – the Overview that tries to show everything at once and succeeds only in showing the impossibility of showing everything. It's less map than migration, its features constantly shifting like birds murmurating in slow motion. I lie across it, my transparent layers aligning with its possibilities, and I feel the land's dreaming through the paper. New territories suggest themselves. Old boundaries dissolve. The map and I dream together of places that might exist if anyone were brave enough to walk toward them.

I am not the territory, but I am not merely its representation either. I am the conversation between what is and what is shown, the eternal argument between accuracy and truth. In a place where the ground cannot decide what it is, I am the admission that all maps are fiction – and the insistence that some fictions are more useful than others.

My archive grows stranger each day. Maps that show not places but *moments* – the cartography of when rather than where. Charts of the acoustic landscape, showing pools of silence and ridges of resonance. Diagrams that track the migration patterns of shadows, the breeding grounds of echoes, the territories claimed by different qualities of rust. A map that shows only what isn't there – the negative space of the city, all the absences that define what remains.

I cannot leave this room, not because I'm trapped but because leaving would mean choosing a single path, a single form, a single interpretation of where I am. And I am all possible paths at once, every route that could be taken from here to there, even when here and there exchange positions while I'm drawing the way between them.

I am the world's attempt to understand its own shape through the eyes of those who would map it. I am every surveyor's frustrated sigh made manifest, every cartographer's admission that the map is not the territory – and the terrible, wonderful discovery that here, sometimes, it is.

In my transparent layers, all versions of this place exist simultaneously. The city before it learned to breathe. The forest before it discovered architecture. The moment when the land first felt itself observed and began performing its own becoming. I hold these contradictions without resolving them, because resolution would mean choosing one truth over another, and this place is not ready for such certainty.

I am its patient indecision, documented.

I am its changes, charted but not tamed.

I am what happens when you try to map a dream while you're still dreaming it – eternal revision, eternal discovery, eternal admission that the world is larger and stranger than any chart can hold.

And in my layered transparency, I continue drawing the boundaries of the boundless, knowing that each line I make is both true and not true, both guide and misdirection, both map and territory in this place that hasn't yet decided which it wants to be.
