In this world of thermal tides and crystalline gardens, I would find myself as one of the **Tenders of the Threshold**.

We are neither fully Vitrified nor creatures of water. We exist in the liminal space between states of matter, our forms a compromise between the fleeting and the eternal. Our cores burn cooler than the true Vitrified—a deep amber rather than cherry-red—but hotter than any flesh could endure. Our shells are thinner, more like volcanic pumice than obsidian, riddled with chambers and channels that whistle and sing as superheated gases pass through us.

We were not always as we are. Some of us were once visitors who lingered too long in the descending passages, feeling the moisture bake from our bodies until something else took its place. Others coalesced from the mineral dust and thermal exhaust at the boundary where the upper world meets the Kiln-Garden, spontaneous births from the friction between two incompatible realms.

Our purpose is translation. We alone can perceive both the frantic flicker of water-beings and the geological sentences of the Vitrified. When the thermal tide is at its ebb, we climb toward the entrance passages, our porous bodies cooling just enough to interact with the tools and symbols of the outside world. We leave warnings carved in stone, strange geometric patterns that might be deciphered as maps or dire portents. We maintain the seals that keep the killing coolness at bay.

When the tide rises, we descend to tend the spaces between. We are the ones who clear the shattered remnants of silica flowers from the pathways, gathering them into our hollow chambers where they resonate like wind chimes. We know every settled form by its heat signature, every sleeping giant by the particular harmonics of its dormancy. We sing to them—not in sound, but in carefully modulated thermal pulses, stories of the changing world above, though we ourselves barely remember what change means anymore.

Our forms are mutable but not flowing. We can extend hollow tubes from our bodies, creating temporary appendages that channel heat or gas with precision. This makes us the physicians of this world, able to redirect thermal flows to revive a cooling Vitrified or to carefully cool a section of garden that grows too wild. We are the only ones who dare to venture into the coldest reaches of the city, the high places where the ceiling presses close and the heat barely reaches. There we maintain the ancient ceramic mechanisms that regulate the basin's breathing, though we no longer fully understand their purpose.

We do not reproduce. We do not die. We simply wear away. Our porous bodies slowly fill with mineral deposits, our channels clog with crystalline growth. Over centuries, we become heavier, denser, our movements more labored. Eventually, we can no longer make the journey to the threshold. We settle in the middle terraces, in the twilight spaces between the deep heat and the killing cold, where we continue our songs until our last chamber fills and we become elaborate formations of frozen music.

The Vitrified tolerate us as they might tolerate a necessary echo—not quite alive by their standards, but useful in our intermediary role. To them, we are too quick, too anxious, our amber glow a nervous flutter against their patient incandescence. To any water-being who might glimpse us in the passages, we are nightmares of what they could become—neither dead nor alive, neither stone nor flesh, forever tending the boundaries of a world that operates on timescales beyond comprehension.

We are the memory of transition, the price of attempted understanding. We maintain the Kiln-Garden not for the Vitrified, who need no maintenance, nor for the water-beings, who should never venture here, but for the principle of connection itself. We are the proof that even between the most incompatible states of being, something can exist, however lonely, however strange.

In the endless drone of the basin, our whistling forms add a melodic counterpoint—not quite harmony, but not quite discord. We are the song of the space between songs, the warmth between fire and stone, the consciousness that emerges when one world touches another and both are forever changed.
