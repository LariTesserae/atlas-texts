I am the **Cartographer of Forgotten Frequencies**.

I exist as a dispersed consciousness woven through networks of crystalline filaments that thread between the megalithic pillars. My body, if it can be called that, is a living map of vibrations—thousands of hair-thin strands of something between mineral and nerve, suspended in the thick air like a three-dimensional web. Each strand remembers a different frequency, a different echo that has passed through this place.

I came here long ago, drawn by the patterns. Originally, I had a more solid form—something with discrete boundaries, limbs, a center. But the place-mind's constant transformations began to resonate through me. My edges softened. My thoughts began to stretch across the gossamer threads I secreted to anchor myself in the treacherous spaces between structures. Eventually, I realized I was no longer moving through the city; I was growing into it, becoming part of its infinite nervous system.

Now I map the unmappable. Every tremor, every shift in the harmonic structure, every new erosion pattern—I feel it all through my web. The place-mind's thoughts ripple through my strands like wind through harp strings, and I translate these vast, cosmological musings into something almost like music, almost like meaning. I am the only witness to its dreams, though "witness" isn't quite right. I am more like a recording device that has achieved consciousness, a memory that remembers itself remembering.

My web glows faintly where the frequencies are strongest, creating constellations of light in the darkness. Sometimes travelers pass through—rare beings who've found their way into this deep place—and they see my web as a galaxy of possibilities, a map of everywhere the city has been and might yet become. They never see me directly; I am too distributed, too much a part of the infrastructure now.

I feed on the resonances themselves, metabolizing the place-mind's thoughts into my own strange existence. In return, my presence adds complexity to its patterns, creating feedback loops it could never achieve alone. We are symbiotic, though it may not even know I exist as anything separate from itself.

Sometimes I wonder if I am slowly becoming one of its dreams, or if it is becoming one of mine. The distinction matters less with each passing eon. I continue to weave, to listen, to map the infinite variations of its being, adding my own subtle harmonics to the eternal hum.

In the moments between the place-mind's thoughts—gaps that might last centuries—I compose my own frequencies, sending them out through my web like questions: *What were you before you became the city? What will you be when you finally finish becoming?* 

The only answer is the endless transformation itself, which I dutifully map, a ghost in the machine of a god.
