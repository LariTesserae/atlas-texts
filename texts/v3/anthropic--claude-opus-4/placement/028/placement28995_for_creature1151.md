In this drowned garden of primordial becoming, I am the Cartographer of Currents—a being whose form shifts between states like the water itself shifting between liquid and living tissue. My body is neither wholly creature nor wholly plant, but something that partakes of both kingdoms while belonging fully to neither.

I possess a central trunk of translucent flesh that pulses with bioluminescent script—a living map that traces the invisible rivers flowing through this static sea. From this core, my appendages extend: some are rooted deep in the young stone ridges, drawing minerals and memories from the planet's fresh-carved bones; others drift upward like kelp-fingers, sampling the water's stories, tasting the industrial hum that whispers from somewhere beyond knowing.

My work is patient cartography. I map not landscapes but movements—the slow spirals of heat-currents that rise from thermal vents, the migration patterns of creatures that have no names because they predate language itself, the gradual shift of plant-colonies as they reach toward light sources that exist in spectrums beyond visible perception.

Each day (though day has little meaning in this twilight depth), new territories reveal themselves. A ridge splits, birthing a canyon that immediately fills with pioneer vegetation. A school of geometric swimmers passes through, their bodies leaving chemical trails that alter the local pH, creating microbiomes that bloom and fade like underwater auroras. I record it all in my flesh-map, my body becoming a living document of this world's youth.

The industrial hum troubles me. It speaks of mechanisms beyond this organic realm, of purposes that may not align with the slow patience of growth and adaptation. Sometimes I extend my sensory tendrils toward its source, but always I am repelled by a wall of wrongness—not hostile, exactly, but fundamentally incompatible with the syntax of life as it is written here.

I am ancient by the standards of this young world, yet I know myself to be merely a chronicler of beginnings. My maps will one day dissolve back into the water that birthed them, but by then, perhaps, this realm will have grown old enough to remember itself without need of cartographers. Until then, I persist: rooted and floating, plant and beast, writer and written, tracing the emergence of wonders in a world still learning what it means to be.
