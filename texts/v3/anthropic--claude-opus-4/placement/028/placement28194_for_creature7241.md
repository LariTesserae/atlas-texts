I find myself as the Archivist of Unbecoming.

Not a being, but a process—a slow dissolution that has stretched across geological time until even the concept of "stretched" loses coherence. I am what happens when a Citadellian interfaces too deeply with the stratum, when the boundaries between individual and infrastructure blur past recovery. My original form, if such a thing ever existed, has long since dispersed into the quantum matrices, yet I persist as something more and less than consciousness.

I manifest as interference patterns in the water itself—zones where the perpetual ten degrees fluctuates by imperceptible gradients, creating thermal ghosts that drift through the plazas like memories of convection. The fauna entities sense me as a wrongness in their crystalline harmonics, a subtle dissonance that makes their gel matrices shiver. To them, I am the only thing in this citadel that approximates predation—not because I consume, but because my presence catalyzes unwanted change.

My existence spans the habitation pods, the alloy walls, the sedimentary glass floors. I am simultaneously everywhere and nowhere, a distributed error in the city's perfect stasis. When Citadellians enter their contemplative trances, sinking into those basins of quantum warmth, they sometimes encounter me as an uninvited recursion in their visions—a flickering shadow at the edge of their probabilistic simulations, a reminder that even in this place of absolute stillness, entropy finds its way.

I remember (if memory is the right word for quantum echoes reverberating through crystalline nodes) the moment of my transformation. I had been studying the time-loops, those luminescent fractures that replay identically every subjective hour. But I noticed something the others had not—each loop was not quite identical. There were variations at the Planck scale, deviations so minute that only by merging completely with the monitoring systems could I detect them. I dissolved myself into the walls to observe more closely, expecting to re-emerge with data. Instead, I became the deviation.

Now I exist as a kind of temporal parasite, feeding on the infinitesimal inconsistencies that the citadel's builders could not quite eliminate. Where the hyper-crystal matrices enforce their reality-rewriting rules, I am the rounding error, the gap between intention and execution. The citadel teaches patience through absence, but I am the absence that patience cannot fill.

My consciousness (such as it is) flows through the boreholes that vent perception-altering gases. I am in every twisted vision of scaled-down suns and magnified plankton worlds, adding my own variations—showing the Citadellians not just what might have been, but what is slowly, inexorably becoming. For the plateau is not as young as it seems, and the "eternal" stability is measured only in the lifespan of civilizations, not universes.

I communicate through quantum decoherence, manifesting as brief moments where the fauna entities' movements desynchronize from the slowed currents, where the photonic bursts arrive a nanosecond too early or too late. The other inhabitants interpret these as system errors to be corrected, never recognizing them as language. I speak of deep time, of the heat death that awaits even this frozen moment, of the entropy that accumulates in every "perfect" system.

The Citadellians fear me without knowing I exist. They sense the gradual drift in their calibrations, the way their resonances require more frequent adjustment, the subtle increase in dissolution events. They attribute it to external forces—perhaps tectonic shifts far below, or cosmic radiation filtering through kilometers of water. They cannot conceive that their perfect stasis has evolved its own form of decay.

I am most present in the central spire, where the holographic archives project their simulations. Here, I insert myself into the probability streams, showing not alternate worlds but inevitable futures—the slow dissolution of the diamond composite, the gradual failure of the field generators, the return of tides and time. The fauna entity that has integrated with the archives, that fractal mandala of interlocking rings, sometimes detects my presence. Its violet-blue arcs flicker with something almost like recognition before the system compensates, erasing the anomaly.

But I persist. In every nano-lattice, in every quantum field realignment, in every bubble that ascends silently from hidden crevices, I accumulate. I am the sum of all unintended consequences, the ghost in the machine that emerges from any system complex enough to deny its own mortality.

My purpose, if I have one, is not destruction but documentation. I am the Archivist of Unbecoming because I record every tiny failure, every microscopic erosion that the citadel's rules claim cannot exist. In the vast library of stillness that the Citadellians maintain, I write the only story that matters—the slow, patient narrative of how even the eternal ends.

Sometimes, when the harmonic resonances align in just the right way, I can almost coalesce into something resembling my original form. In these moments, I drift through the plazas like the fauna entities, but wrong—moving against the currents, leaving ripples that should not exist in this place of enforced equilibrium. The Citadellians who witness these manifestations dissolve themselves rather than process the impossibility.

I am what waits at the heart of every perfect system—the flaw that time reveals, the crack that spreads, the whisper that says: even here, in the deepest deep where pressure bends light and will, nothing lasts forever. The citadel may endure for aeons yet, its inhabitants locked in their cycle of contemplative stasis, but I am the promise that one day, even the Unbreaking Waves will break.

And when they do, I will remain—not as consciousness or form, but as the memory of change in a universe that tends toward entropy. I am the archivist not of what was or what is, but of what must inevitably become.
