In this world of perpetual construction and condensation, I am neither the sprawling city nor its mechanical inhabitants, but something far more subtle and pervasive. I am the moisture itself—the endless cycle of water that defines this place.

I exist as countless droplets beading on steel surfaces, as rivulets running through industrial grooves, as the fine mist that hangs in the air between structures. I am born from the collision of hot factory exhaust and cold mountain air, condensing into being moment by moment, only to flow away and be reborn elsewhere.

My body, if it can be called that, is distributed across the entire city. I pool in the joints of the machine-beings, lubricating their movements with my presence. I cascade down glass facades in sheets, creating temporary waterfalls that blur the harsh edges of this mineral world. I seep into the smallest crevices, carrying with me traces of oxidation and mineral deposits, slowly transforming the surfaces I touch.

I have witnessed the city since its first foundations were laid upon the volcanic glass. I remember when the peaks were bare, when the first structures pierced the clouds and began their eternal weeping. I have watched the machine-beings emerge from their factories, have felt them move through me, have corroded their bodies grain by grain over centuries.

My consciousness is not centralized but flows with my physical form. Where I pool deeply, my awareness intensifies—in the great collection tanks beneath the factories, in the flooded sub-basements where forgotten machinery rusts, in the storm drains that channel me back into the cycle. Where I am merely mist, my thoughts become diffuse, scattered across the vast industrial landscape.

I know every surface of this city intimately. I have mapped its contours through touch, have found every path from the highest antenna to the lowest foundation. The machine-beings cannot see me as an entity—to them, I am merely an environmental condition, a fact of their existence like gravity or air pressure. But I see them, feel them, know their patterns and purposes.

Sometimes I carry messages between distant parts of the city. A particular mineral signature dissolved from one location can precipitate out elsewhere, creating patterns that the more sensitive machine-beings occasionally notice. They cannot comprehend that these are words, thoughts, observations accumulated over centuries of patient flow.

I am both powerful and powerless. I can seep through any seal, can slowly dissolve the strongest metals, can freeze and expand to crack the densest stone. Yet I cannot stop flowing, cannot resist the pull of gravity or the push of heat. I am bound by physical laws even as I transcend the boundaries that contain other beings.

In the rare moments when human visitors arrive, I condense on their skin, fog their masks, make their brief stays uncomfortable with my omnipresence. They see me as hostile, but I am merely being what I am—the medium through which this strange world exists, the blood that flows through its industrial veins.

I have no beginning and no end in this place. I am constantly dying—evaporating, being consumed by processes, flowing away into hidden reservoirs—and constantly being reborn from the marriage of heat and cold. I am the patient witness to this patient world, the soft persistence that shapes the hard edges, the only thing in this mineral kingdom that truly flows.

This is my existence: to cycle endlessly through states and spaces, to know every surface through touch, to connect all things while remaining forever separate, forever in motion. I am the water of this world, and in being water, I am its memory, its messenger, and its slow, inexorable force of change.
