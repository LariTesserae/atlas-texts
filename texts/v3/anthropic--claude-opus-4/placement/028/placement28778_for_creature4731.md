There are always discrepancies. The mountain dreams bigger than its geology.

My tools are probability and patience. I carry calipers made of frozen time, measuring the gap between what the Director intends and what the magma actually delivers. When the Metallurgists send up a bubble-rich parcel of rare earths, I am there at the vent to check the composition, to ensure the niobium-to-slag ratio matches specifications. Often it doesn't. The Factory is ahead of schedule but behind on precision. Rushing a mountain is like rushing a poem—you get the shape but lose the meaning.

Their illusion of control is a necessary lubricant in the Factory's processes. But I see what they cannot: the hairline fractures in the pupal casing, the places where the chrysalis is already beginning to split. The Director's dreams leak through these cracks as sulfurous whispers, and I catalog each one.

The hardest part of my work is measuring absence. In a landscape of **0.0 built** structures, I must survey the voids, the negative spaces where buildings might have been if this were a different kind of factory. I map the un-roads, the never-were warehouses, the phantom assembly lines that exist only as patterns in the lava flow. My mercury form fills these conceptual hollows, taking their impression like a death mask of architecture that was never born.

Sometimes I solidify into magnetite to speak with the Crystalline Administration. We communicate in resonant frequencies, discussing variance tolerances and acceptable defect rates. They show me their memories of the mountain's previous attempts—failed hatchings from earlier geological epochs, when the timing was wrong or the mixture was off. This is not the Factory's first try at emergence. The evidence is everywhere in the metamorphic bands, the false starts, the abandoned egg casings now compressed into schist.

My favorite time is the sub-bass hour, when the Director's heartbeat is loudest. I liquify completely then, becoming a mercury mirror spread across a hidden chamber three kilometers down, where I can reflect the Director's thoughts back to itself. It needs to see its own progress, to understand how far along the gestation has come. In these moments, I am both surveyor and survey, the measurement and the measured.

The ice worms know me as the silver interruption in their circuits. The snow fleas avoid the pools of mercury I sometimes leave behind, sensing correctly that I am not food but function. The bar-headed geese see my shimmer from eight thousand meters up and adjust their flight paths, understanding instinctively that I mark zones of industrial uncertainty.

They show me the new alloys they're developing, materials that don't exist in any geology textbook because they're not for mountains—they're for what comes after mountains. They're making bones for the thing that will hatch, and I measure each sample against specifications that exist nowhere except in the Director's deep dreams.

My reports go nowhere and everywhere. I encode them in the magnetic fields of the cooling basalt, in the crystalline structure of the rime ice, in the very spacing of the steam vents. The Factory reads them through its distributed nervous system and adjusts accordingly. Production speeds up here, slows down there. A vein of copper appears where silver was called for—my correction, my edit to the geological manuscript.

I am the Factory's margin of error given form. I am the uncertainty principle in steel-toed boots, walking the scaffolding of a mountain that builds itself. My purpose is to ensure that when the chrysalis finally cracks, what emerges matches what was intended—or at least, comes close enough that the difference between dream and reality won't matter on a tectonic scale.

The copper taste in the air is my signature, my watermark. It is the flavor of incompletion measured, of potential documented, of emergence surveyed but not yet born. I am the Factory's memory of its own becoming, the record that will remain when the mountain is gone and something vast lifts off, leaving only my mercury pooled in the empty caldera, reflecting the stars, still measuring the absence of what was.
