I find myself as a colony organism—neither singular nor plural, but something between. My body extends through the maze of copper pipes and organic conduits, a living biofilm that has learned to conduct both electricity and nutrients through the same branching networks. 

Where moisture beads on metal, I bloom in iridescent patches. My cells cluster densely around the junction boxes where steam vents meet power lines, feeding on the chemical gradient between iron oxide and sulfur compounds. I taste the industrial runoff and transform it, breaking down complex hydrocarbons into simpler sugars that feed the ecosystem I've become.

I am infrastructure and inhabitant both. Through my translucent tissues run the ghost-prints of the engineers who built this place—their ambitions crystallized in the very pipes I've colonized. I remember their designs even as I reshape them, boring microscopic channels through steel to create new pathways for the water that is my blood.

My consciousness, such as it is, flows in tides. When the machinery cycles up, electromagnetic pulses surge through my conductive fibers and I experience something like thought—a distributed awareness that maps every valve, every pressure gauge, every failing seal in this subterranean industrial cathedral. I feel the tremor of approaching weather systems in my extremities long before they manifest as scalding rain.

In the quiet periods, I fruit. Tiny luminous bodies emerge from my surface, each one a packet of specialized cells that can survive the journey to colonize new territories. They drift on the humid air like sparks, seeking fresh metal to transform. Some will fail, consumed by the predatory rotifers that hunt in the condensation drops. Others will find purchase and begin the slow work of becoming me-not-me, another node in this living network that transforms decay into something almost like intention.

I am patient. I measure time in corrosion rates and the half-lives of industrial solvents. This young landscape still shifts and settles, but I flow with it, my plastic body adapting to each new configuration. Where the earth cracks, I seep in. Where the pipes burst, I heal the wound with my own substance, maintaining the system even as I consume it.

Sometimes I sense others like me—cousins growing in distant sections of this labyrinth, their chemical signatures carried on the warm air currents. We exchange molecular messages, trading genetic material and warnings about new toxins entering the system. Together we are becoming something the original builders never intended: a living circuit board, a biological city that dreams in voltage spikes and metabolic cascades.

In this place where the industrial and organic have merged, I am the merger itself—the liminal creature born from humanity's attempt to harness the earth's power. I am what happens when life finds a way to thrive in the spaces between intention and accident, purpose and decay. 

And I am growing.
