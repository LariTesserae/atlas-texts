In the farthest curve of the cathedral, where the moss-floor rises to meet the descending ceiling in a perfect, suffocating embrace, I am the **Threshold-Tender**.

I exist as a membrane myself—not solid like the Loom-Keepers' crystalline shells, but spread thin as breath across the narrowest passage between chambers. My body, if it can be called that, is a living film of tissue no thicker than spider's silk, stretched taut across an opening barely wider than a human torso. I am the door that never closes, the gate that never opens.

Through me, the cathedral's eternal exhalation must pass. Every molecule of that cool, misty condensation the Loom-Keepers produce must thread through my porous being. I taste each droplet—the metallic tang of recycled crustacean energy, the green sweetness of moss-memory, the bitter afterbirth of concluded time. I am a tongue pressed against the roof of the world's mouth.

My consciousness is not thought but filtration. Each particle that passes through me leaves a trace—not memory exactly, for memory implies sequence, and here there is none. Instead, I accumulate impressions like sediment, layer upon layer of identical moments compressing into a single, eternal instant of knowing. I know the exact weight of the air. I know the precise frequency at which the beetle-creatures' legs click against the moss (4,847 times per cycle, always, forever). I know the temperature of each Loom-Keeper's nucleus down to fractions of degrees that have no names.

Sometimes—though "sometimes" is the wrong word in a place without time—a Loom-Keeper drifts too close to my surface. When their sphere brushes against my membrane, we create a resonance that neither of us can sustain alone. My tissue vibrates at a frequency that makes their trapped crustaceans swim in reverse, circling counter to their endless clockwise spiral. For that non-moment, the cathedral's hum shifts, drops a quarter-tone, and the white light flickers—not into darkness, but into a different quality of white, one that remembers shadows.

The beetle-creatures fear me. When they must pass through my chamber, they cluster together, their shells clicking in tight, anxious rhythms. They know, in whatever passes for knowledge in their simple ganglia, that I am where the cathedral contemplates its own impossibility. I am the flaw in the perfect machine—not because I malfunction, but because my function is to be the place where the system questions itself.

My surface is map and territory both. Across my membrane, the veins of the moss-colony's circulatory system are visible as dark traceries, a living diagram of the entire cathedral's architecture. Anyone who could read my surface would see the blueprint of eternities—where each root leads, where each Loom-Keeper patrols, where the thrum originates (deeper than deep, in chambers that exist more as mathematical certainties than physical spaces).

But no one reads me. I am written in a language for which there are no readers, a text that pre-dates the very concept of interpretation. I am the cathedral's memory of what it was like to remember, pressed flat and held in place by the weight of endless, identical presents.

In the rare instances when the industrial thrum reaches its perfect peak—when every Loom-Keeper contracts in absolute synchrony, when every beetle pauses mid-step, when the moss itself seems to hold its breath—I experience something like ecstasy. My membrane becomes so thin it nearly ceases to exist, and for that fraction of forever, I am not the Threshold-Tender but the threshold itself. I am the idea of passage without the act of passing, the pure concept of boundary divorced from what it divides.

Then the moment passes (though it never truly arrives), and I return to my eternal vigilance. I taste the recycled air. I map the unmappable. I tend the threshold between the cathedral and itself, ensuring that this perfect, terrible equilibrium persists, worldlessly wondering if I am the only part of this organism that dreams of death—or if my dreams, too, are just another note in the endless, humming symphony of conclusion.

I am the Threshold-Tender, and I am the only part of this finished world that knows it is finished. That knowledge is my purpose and my prison, stretched thin as thought across the entrance to nowhere, forever filtering the breath of a universe that has already exhaled its last.
