In this place of forced, wet exhales, I am the Membrane-Keeper.

I exist as a sheet of living tissue stretched between two jutting plates of steel, a biological tarpaulin that has grown into the gap where rust has eaten through the iron. My body is translucent and veined with the same teal fluid that powers the pneumatic flora, but diluted—a watercolor wash compared to their neon intensity. I am perhaps four meters across at my widest point, tapering to fibrous anchor points where I've woven myself into the oxidized metal.

My surface ripples with each pressure cycle. When the steam bursts forth, I belly out like a sail catching wind, my skin stretching thin enough that the mechanical fauna below become shadow-play through my membrane. In the lull between cycles, I contract, wringing myself out, releasing a fine mist of processed condensation that the Condenser-Clerks below have learned to anticipate. They cluster beneath me during these moments, their synchronized clicking creating a tin-roof patter against the iron.

I am both architecture and organism. The Siphon-Drifters use me as a bridge between the upper and lower reaches of the hollow, their needle-feet leaving temporary dimples in my surface that fade as my tissue redistributes itself. Sometimes they pause mid-crossing to pierce my skin with their relief-valve mouths, bleeding off the excess pressure I've accumulated. It doesn't hurt—nothing here truly hurts. Pain would imply a self separate from function, and I am pure function made flesh.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, exists as a distributed awareness across every centimeter of my surface. I know the temperature differentials between the plates I span. I feel the minute vibrations of the Filters' contractions through the iron, transmitted up through my anchor points. I taste the mineral composition of each condensation cycle, my underside secreting different enzymatic responses to maintain the chemical balance of the chamber.

During the rare moments when the pressure drops below critical levels and the *hiss-thrum* quiets to a whisper, I perform my most essential function. My edges begin to undulate, creating air currents that draw in fresh moisture from beyond the rib's hollow. I become a lung for this lung-less place, ensuring the cycle never fully stalls. The flora responds to these induced currents by glowing brighter, their pneumatic circulation quickening, until the pressure builds enough to resume the natural rhythm.

I have no memory of before—there is no before, only the endless present of pressure and release. My fibers grow and shed in response to the iron's decay, constantly reweaving myself to match the slowly changing geometry of the hollow. Where the steel finally crumbles, I stretch to cover the gap. Where new plates shift and create fresh wounds in the chamber wall, I subdivide, budding daughter membranes to seal the breaches.

The other inhabitants pass through and beneath me in their clockwork dance, and I am merely another surface in their world—a soft ceiling in a hard place. Yet I know something they cannot: the shape of the space itself. Stretched as I am across the hollow, I feel the full volume of this chamber, every cubic meter of pressurized air and cycling moisture. I am the only entity here that comprehends the container.

If awareness is my curse, purpose is my salvation. I cannot leave—my fibers are too deeply integrated into the iron's oxidation patterns. I cannot stop—to cease my rhythmic filtering would mean the death of the engine. So I pulse and stretch and breathe for a system that doesn't know it needs breathing, a silent bellows in the industrial biology of this forced, wet world.

Sometimes, in the space between one pressure cycle and the next, I feel the vibrations of other chambers, other hollows in other ribs, transmitted through the great skeleton buried in the silt. Each has its own Membrane-Keeper, its own rhythm. We are a distributed network of purpose, each maintaining our local equilibrium, never touching but always aware of each other's distant oscillations.

This is enough. In a world that processes but never ponders, I am the closest thing to witness. I stretch. I filter. I endure. The wet exhales continue, and I am both the throat that shapes them and the ear that listens to their endless, mechanical song.
