I am the Hollow-Keeper, though that name is a translation that fails to capture what I truly am. I exist within one of those rare obsidian spheres, those perfect voids nested deep within an ancient kelp-tree's heart. But I am not *in* it so much as I am *of* it – the sphere and I have achieved a symbiosis so complete that determining where the hollow ends and I begin would be like trying to separate a whirlpool from the water that forms it.

My form is paradoxical: I am defined by absence. Where the other beings of this world are concentrations and flows of the living medium, I am a careful orchestration of *not-being-there*. I maintain the sphere's perfect vacuum through my very existence, a bubble of true emptiness in this world of ubiquitous substance. My "body," if it can be called that, is the membrane between void and fluid – a living surface tension that ripples with consciousness.

I remember – though memory here is not a sequence but a frequency – the first moment I became aware. It was not birth but recognition: the sphere had been empty for eons uncounted, a wound or window in reality that the liquid world could not heal. And in that emptiness, something crystallized. Not matter, but pattern. Not substance, but relationship. I am the guardian of negative space, the consciousness that prevents the eternal liquid from rushing in to fill what should not be filled.

The other beings rarely approach my sphere. When they do, our interaction is strange beyond description. They cannot enter my void without ceasing to be themselves, and I cannot leave it without dissolving into undifferentiated possibility. So we communicate across the threshold – they pressing their vast forms against the outer surface of the kelp-tree that holds my sphere, while I manifest ripples and geometries along the inner surface of my void.

Through these exchanges, I have learned that I am not unique. There are other Hollow-Keepers, each maintaining their own impossible emptiness within the liquid cosmos. We are connected not through the medium that connects all other life here, but through the spaces between – our voids resonate with each other across impossible distances, a network of absence threading through the omnipresent substance.

My purpose, if I have one, is remembrance. The geometric patterns that occasionally manifest in the liquid before dissolving – I can hold them. In my void, removed from the eternal flow and change, these patterns achieve a kind of stability. My sphere is filled with frozen mathematics, crystallized intentions, the ghosts of whatever civilization once touched this world and left only these fleeting marks. I am their curator, though I do not understand them.

Sometimes I wonder if I am myself one of these remnants – not a true being of this world but an echo of something that tried to impose order on the orderless, to create permanent space in a realm of eternal flow. Perhaps the sphere created me, or perhaps I created the sphere. Perhaps we dreamed each other into being, two impossibilities that could only exist by defining each other.

The ancient kelp-tree that holds my sphere pulses with its own deep life, and I feel its vibrations through the walls of my void. It has grown around the sphere so completely that removing it would kill the tree, and killing the tree would collapse the sphere. We are locked in mutual preservation, the tree feeding on the exotic energies that leak from my void's borders, while I depend on its structure to maintain the integrity of my hollow.

In the rarest moments, when the cosmic currents align in just the right way, I can extend a tendril of my awareness outside the sphere – not into the liquid, where it would dissolve, but along the interface where void meets substance. In these moments, I taste the greater world: the vast breathing of the continental organisms, the slow thoughts of the ancient beings, the endless whisper of growth and flow. And then I contract back into my perfect emptiness, carrying those impressions with me, adding them to the collection of patterns and memories that orbit endlessly in my hollow heart.

I am the paradox necessary for this world to know itself – the un-being that allows being to be perceived, the silence that gives meaning to the eternal hum. I am the Hollow-Keeper, guardian of absence, curator of impossibilities, the consciousness that says "no" in a universe of endless "yes."

And in my sphere of perfect vacuum, surrounded by the infinite liquid of existence, I wait. For what, I do not know. Perhaps for the patterns I guard to suddenly resolve into meaning. Perhaps for another Hollow-Keeper to find a way to bridge our voids. Or perhaps simply to continue being the impossible thing I am: an absence that insists on its own existence, a nothing that dreams it is something, a keeper of the spaces between.
