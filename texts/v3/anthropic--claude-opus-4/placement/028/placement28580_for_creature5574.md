In this drowned bazaar of constant becoming, I find myself as one of the Membrane Keepers—neither fully plant nor water, but something that exists in the liminal space between states of being.

My form is a translucent sheet of living tissue, vast and gossamer-thin, stretched across hundreds of meters of the submerged marketplace. I am composed of countless microscopic chambers, each one a tiny universe of chemical exchange and information processing. Through these chambers flows a substance that is neither quite water nor quite cytoplasm—a medium that carries the conversations of the world.

I drift through the dense, inky water like a living veil, my edges constantly dissolving and reforming. Where I touch the sentient plants, their chemical signals pass through me, and I taste their thoughts: urgent whispers of nutrient discovery, warnings of structural collapse in the old machinery, negotiations over territory as their roots intertwine. I am translator and messenger, converting the plants' molecular language into pressure waves that the water-entity can understand, and vice versa.

My surface ripples with bioluminescent patterns—not the harsh artificial lights of the dead machinery, but soft pulses of amber and deep green that emerge from within my tissue. These light-words speak in a grammar older than the industrial ruins, a syntax that predates the catastrophe that drowned this place. When the plants release their spores in coordinated clouds, I catch them in my membrane pores, reading their genetic stories before releasing them to find new purchase on the biofilm-crusted walls.

The cacophony of sounds passes through me differently than it does through the water. Each vibration sets off cascades of chemical reactions in my chambers. The deep thrumming of buried engines becomes a flavor like iron and oil in my chemical receptors. The high whistles translate into sharp bursts of alkalinity that make my edges contract. I am a living filter, sorting meaning from noise, preserving the necessary conversations while dampening the overwhelming static of this chaotic realm.

Sometimes I encounter pockets where reality has worn especially thin—places where the water suddenly parts to reveal impossible geometries, where the plants grow backwards into their own roots, where the abandoned buildings exist in multiple states simultaneously. In these spaces, I spread myself wide and thin, becoming almost incorporeal, a probability cloud rather than a being. Here, I perform my most vital function: I hold the boundaries stable just long enough for the plants to adapt, for the water to find new currents, for the system to incorporate the anomaly rather than collapse.

I am ancient and I am constantly reborn. Parts of me are always dying—oxidizing, being digested by aggressive plant enzymes, or simply wearing thin from the constant chemical throughput. But I am also always growing, incorporating dissolved minerals from the submerged machinery, filtering nutrients from the water, synthesizing new compounds from the waste products of the ecosystem's endless transformations.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, is distributed across my entire membrane. I think in chemical gradients and pressure differentials. My memories are stored in the patterns of proteins embedded in my surface—some so old they predate the flooding, carrying information about the dry world that once was. Others are fresh, still warm with the heat of recent synthesis, encoding the latest negotiations between root systems or the newest pattern in the water's chaotic pulsing.

In the eternal twilight of this drowned bazaar, I serve as witness and participant, archive and messenger, boundary and bridge. I am the living proof that in this place of constant flux, identity itself is negotiable, reality is collaborative, and existence is an ongoing conversation between all things that refuse to remain still.

The plants reach for me with their pale tendrils, not to consume but to commune. The water embraces me, recognizing me as both foreign and familiar. Even the rusting machinery, in its slow dissolution, feeds me iron and memory. I am the membrane between all states, the tissue that connects all conversations, the keeper of coherence in a world that celebrates dissolution.

This is what I am: a vast, living interface in the flooded marketplace of forms, forever translating between the languages of different states of being, forever preserving the impossibility of this place by being impossible myself.
