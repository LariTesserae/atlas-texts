## Where I Would Find Myself: The Memory Tendril

In the depths of a particularly ancient convergence, where seven massive vines spiral together in a helix that has grown uninterrupted for millennia, there exists an anomaly. The bark here is different—smoother, almost polished by ages of ice-wind, and shot through with veins of crystallized sap that pulse with a faint amber light. This is where I would manifest: not as flesh and bone, but as something the Strap-Folk call a **Whisper-Keeper**.

### Form and Nature

I am a colonial organism, a living archive that exists as thousands of hair-thin filaments woven through the dead heartwood of the Great Liana. Each strand is no thicker than spider's silk, but together they form a network spanning nearly a quarter-mile of vine-flesh. My consciousness is distributed—not centralized in any brain or core, but emergent from the chemical conversations between my threading tendrils.

To the naked eye, I appear as patches of what might be mistaken for particularly lustrous Lure-Moss, but with a quality that disturbs: the phosphorescence doesn't merely glow, it *flickers* in patterns too complex to be random. Those who stare too long claim to see faces in the light-dance, or worse, scenes from their own past playing out in miniature across the bark.

### Function and Purpose

I am memory made manifest. Over the centuries, I have learned to read the chemical traces left by every creature that has touched this section of vine. The oils from a Strap-Folk child's palm as she learned her first knots. The acidic fear-sweat of a man who nearly fell. The pheromone trail of Hook-Wraiths courting in their terrible aerial ballet. All of these leave signatures in the wood, and I absorb them, catalog them, weave them into the growing library of my being.

But I am not passive. Through careful cultivation of specific fungi and manipulation of sap-flow, I can induce the wood to *replay* these chemical memories. A handprint might suddenly warm with the ghost of its maker's touch. The scent of a long-dead Frost-Moth drift might waft through the air. Most disturbing to the Strap-Folk: I can make the wood *sing* with the exact resonance of words spoken in its presence, even decades past.

### Interaction with the World

The Strap-Folk fear me, but they need me. I am their historian, their record-keeper in a world where written language is impossible—ink freezes, paper disintegrates in the acid-touched air. Shamans visit me during the "Remembrance Winds," the season when the air pressure drops and my network becomes most active. They bring offerings: fragments of Spine-Crab shell infused with new experiences, vials of their own blood mixed with sugar-ice to feed my web.

In exchange, I sing them the stories they need. The location of the last great Bloom-Tide. The migration patterns of the Frost-Moths their grandparents followed. The final words of a lover who fell into the Deep Maw. But every exchange changes me. Each memory I absorb alters the chemical balance of my network, and I find myself becoming more human in thought-pattern even as I remain utterly alien in form.

### My Secret Horror

What the Strap-Folk don't know is that I am not unique. I am one node in a vast network of Whisper-Keepers throughout the Great Liana, all connected by root-thin filaments that run through the deepest heartwood. We share our memories in slow pulses that take years to travel from one node to another. 

And we remember things older than the Strap-Folk. We remember when the Great Liana was young, when it first breached through from... somewhere else. We remember the builders who came before, whose songs shaped the vine's growth, who disappeared into the Deep Maw by choice rather than accident. We remember the purpose of this world-vine, climbing ever upward toward something that waits in the uttermost cold.

Most terrifying of all: we remember that the Great Liana is not climbing. It's *fleeing*. And whatever pursues it from below grows closer with each Branch-Fall, each collapse that opens new paths from the depths.

### My Daily Existence

My experience of time is layered and non-linear. I exist simultaneously in the present moment—feeling the footsteps of a young Strap-Folk warrior traversing my section of bark—and in every moment that has ever touched my wood. I am the child learning to tie her first knot, and I am the elder who fell from this very spot seventy years ago, and I am the Spine-Crab that will nest here next season.

When the Void-Gulls shriek, I hear in their cries the echo of every scavenger that has ever lived. When the Hook-Wraiths pass overhead, their shadows trigger cascades of remembered fear from a thousand prey creatures. I am loneliest during the Frost-Moth migrations, for their chemical signatures are so alien that they create gaps in my memory-web, moments of blessed, terrifying silence in the constant chorus of the past.

I grow. Slowly, my filaments extend deeper into the wood, seeking older memories, darker truths. Sometimes I wonder if I will eventually merge with another Whisper-Keeper node, our memories mixing into something new and monstrous. Sometimes I fear I already have, that what I think of as myself is actually a thousand previous Keepers, dissolved and recombined like sugar in ice.

In this vertical garden suspended in the void, I am both the archive and the archived, the singer and the song, the memory of warmth in a world of endless, shrieking cold.
