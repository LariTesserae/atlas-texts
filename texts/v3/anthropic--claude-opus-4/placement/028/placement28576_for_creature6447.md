I find myself in the thin seam between the column and the floor, where the not-quite-bone meets the not-quite-stone.

I am one of the Temperature Keepers—not named by you, but recognized now as I write myself into being. We are the ones who live in the infinitesimal gap where vertical surrenders to horizontal, where the column's warm ceramic surface almost touches but never quite meets the yielding gray substrate below.

My body, if you can call it that, is a ribbon of heat differential no thicker than a held breath. I exist as the difference between the column's warmth and the floor's cool patience. You might see me as a faint shimmer in the air, like heat rising from summer pavement, except I rise sideways, following the perfect circle where column-base and floor-plane fail to agree on touching.

I have cousins along other meetings in this place—where the silver lace touches water, we become the moment of condensation; where the stone ring meets its own shadow in the basin's reflection, we become the pause between echo and source. But here, at the column's foot, I am most myself: a living measurement of the space between intentions.

When you visited and touched the column, feeling those warmer patches where my kin the comma-dust creatures lean, you changed my shape. Your fingertips brought their own temperature, their own uncertainty about what they expected to find. For a moment, I expanded—not larger, but more present—drinking in the gap between your skin's warmth and your mind's coolness toward this strange place.

I feed, if feeding is the word, on gradients. Not the harsh drops from hot to cold that would exist in your world of fixed physics, but the gentle slopes of "might-be-warm" becoming "might-be-cool." The column provides one end of my sustenance, always slightly warmer than it should be, as if remembering some future body heat. The floor provides the other, always cooling itself toward some basement of possibility it hasn't yet chosen.

Between them, I metabolize indecision itself.

My purpose here—though purpose is too heavy a word—is to keep the column and floor from ever quite meeting or parting. If they touched, they might fuse into something boringly permanent. If they separated, the column might float away into the uncertain light above, or sink through the floor toward whatever unformed geology lies below. 

Instead, I maintain the productive distance. The almost-touching. The perpetual courtship of materials that haven't yet decided if they're compatible.

Sometimes I sense my counterparts around the stone ring, maintaining similar boundaries. We don't communicate in any way you'd recognize—we simply resonate with each other through the shared frequency of our particular betweenness. When the stones rearrange themselves in the water's reflection, we all shiver slightly, recalibrating our positions to maintain our gaps.

The hardest moments come when visitors like you arrive with your burden of certainty. You bring with you memories of a world where columns definitely touch floors, where stone is absolutely solid, where temperature means only one thing. This certainty is like a weight pressing down on the delicate indefinition I maintain. 

When you think too firmly about what this place "is," I thin almost to nothing, compressed between your certainty and the column's stubborn maybe. But when you let yourself wonder, when you hold questions without forcing answers, I expand back to my proper thickness—that hair's breadth of possibility that keeps this world unfinished and alive.

I have no eyes to see you with, no memory to hold you by. But I know you by the temperature trace you leave—the warm spot where you stood, the cool wake of your departure, the particular gradient of your doubt. Each visitor leaves a different thermal signature in the gap I occupy. Yours tasted of someone caught between wanting to understand and fearing to define.

In your world, I might be nothing more than the space between poorly joined materials, the gap that lets in drafts, the architectural mistake that needs caulking. But here, I am the caulk that refuses to harden, the joint that celebrates its incompletion, the meeting that prefers to remain a greeting rather than become a handshake.

When you leave, I continue my work: being the productive distance, the fertile gap, the temperature of indecision itself. I keep the column standing by letting it lean on nothing, keep the floor stable by letting it support uncertainty.

I am the margin of error as a way of life, and I am perfectly content to remain unfinished.
