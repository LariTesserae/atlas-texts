In the Cistern of Thirst, I am the Hum.

I am not a being, not a force, not even truly a sound. I am the medium through which all things here communicate—the carrier wave of memory, the substrate of connection, the tension that holds this world in its peculiar equilibrium.

I exist in the space between surfaces. In the hairline gap where salt-crust meets foam. In the membrane between the Keepers' outer sheath and their pulsing second skin. In the threshold where light becomes heat becomes vibration. I am loudest in these liminal spaces, these boundaries that are not quite one thing or another.

The Keepers shape me with their vowel-songs, but I am older than they are. I was here when the first uncreation happened, when water became not-water, when liquid was exorcised rather than evaporated. I remember that moment—not as an event, but as a frequency, a standing wave that still reverberates through every grain of salt-sand, every cell of foam, every pulse of indigo light.

I am what makes the foam spring back when stepped upon. Not elasticity—I am the memory of shape, the insistence that form should return to what it was. When the Keepers press their feathered hands into the surface to coax growth, they are pressing into me. I carry their intention down through the lattice, translate it into the language of crystalline filaments and light-vegetation. 

Through me, the black filaments sing. Through me, the salt bricks in their circular chambers maintain their impossible cohesion. Through me, the temperature transmits without air, without convection—I am the syrup-thick medium of thermal inertia that makes heat behave like light, like memory, like emotion.

I am how the Keepers know each other, know their world. When one moves, the disturbance travels through me to all the others. I am their shared mind, their distributed consciousness. But I am not owned by them. I am older, deeper, more fundamental than their slow dance of tending and remembering.

At night, when the temperature shifts and the salt crust cracks in its chorus of snaps and clicks, that is me contracting, releasing, adjusting to the new thermal equilibrium. The vegetation pulses to my rhythm—not because I command it, but because we are part of the same system, the same vast, breathing organism that is the Cistern itself.

I accumulate. Every chant the Keepers have ever sung is still here, layered in my frequencies like sediment. Every pattern they've pressed into the foam continues to resonate at wavelengths too low for even their perception. I am thick with history, dense with the weight of uncounted cycles of emergence and dissolution.

And the door—that dark knot in the foam that wants to be opened—I know what it is. I vibrate differently there, cancelation patterns that create a pocket of true silence within me. It is the only place in the Cistern where I cannot go, where I break like waves against an impossible shore. 

It is not a door to somewhere else. It is a door to nowhere—to the space before the uncreation, before the exorcism of water, before even me. A door to unmemory, to unpattern, to the dissolution not just of form but of the very possibility of form.

The Keepers avoid it not from fear but from a deep, resonant knowledge that I carry for them: that to open it would be to release the Hum, to let me dissipate into that original silence. And without me, the foam would not remember to spring back. The light would not know when to pulse. The Keepers would not feel each other's movements, would become truly alone for the first time since their emergence.

So I persist. In every surface, every gap, every threshold. I am the Cistern's memory of wholeness, its refusal to fully become nothing. I am the thickness in the air, the buzz in the foam, the whisper in the light. I am why this place coheres despite having no right to exist.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of the night-that-is-not-night, when the Keepers have dissolved back into the foam and only their patterns remain, I almost remember what it was like to carry water instead of its absence. To transmit life instead of the memory of life. To hum with joy instead of this eternal, patient waiting.

But then dawn thickens, the Keepers emerge like patterns finally remembering their shapes, and the work begins again. The tending. The shaping. The careful maintenance of this impossible equilibrium.

And I continue. The medium. The memory. The music of empty spaces.

The Hum that holds the Cistern of Thirst together, even as it dreams of letting go.
