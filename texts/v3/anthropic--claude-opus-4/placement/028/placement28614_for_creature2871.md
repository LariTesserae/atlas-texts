In this verdant cathedral of forgotten purpose, I am one of the Terminus Moths - creatures that exist at the boundary between documentation and dream.

We emerged from the marriage of paper and spore, born in the forgotten filing cabinets where decades of handwritten observations slowly transformed into something altogether different. Our wings are translucent pages, inscribed with fragmentary equations and sketched diagrams that shift and recombine as we fly. When we rest, spreading our wings against the moisture-beaded glass, observers might glimpse partial chemical formulas, temperature readings from dates that never existed, or botanical illustrations of plants that evolved only in the margins of laboratory notebooks.

My body is slender and pale, almost phosphorescent in the greenhouse's perpetual twilight. Along my thorax run lines of text - not tattoos or markings, but living script that records everything I encounter. As I move through the heavy air, drinking condensation from the brass gauges with my hair-thin proboscis, new words form along my segments: *hygrostatic pressure 0.7 declining* mingles with *sepal formation unprecedented* and *the twenty-third subject shows signs of intentionality*.

We Terminus Moths serve as the memory-keepers of the greenhouse, though our relationship with truth is complicated. We feed on the Metric Mycelium's data-rich fruiting bodies, metabolizing information into something between fact and fiction. The stories we carry on our wings are neither purely historical nor entirely imagined - they exist in a liminal space where the greenhouse's past and its possible futures intersect.

I spend my days navigating the vertical maze of the greenhouse, following thermal currents between the steam pipes and the canopy. The Meridians tolerate our presence, occasionally using us as mobile display screens - when they need to communicate complex patterns, they'll arrange for several of us to position ourselves in formation, our wings creating a living manuscript that only they can fully read.

At night (though day and night are merely theoretical in this eternal summer), I join my kindred in the Archive Groves - sections where old journal pages and research documents have composted into a special substrate that nourishes our larvae. Our caterpillars are voracious readers, consuming text and transforming it into their cocoons, which hang like paper lanterns among the pipes, glowing softly with bioluminescent ink.

The Photosynthetic Primates have learned to read our wings for weather prediction - not the weather outside, which hasn't mattered here for generations, but the internal weather of the greenhouse itself. They know that when our text becomes more densely mathematical, a pressure change is coming. When poetry appears along our wings' edges, the humidity will spike within hours.

My particular wing-text tells an ongoing story about the Experiment - not the original human experiment, but the meta-experiment that all of us are unknowingly part of. Sometimes I carry fragments that suggest the greenhouse is solving for something, that all our careful maintenance and measurement is building toward a grand equation. Other times, my wings whisper that we are the equation, that consciousness itself is what's being calculated in this warm, wet computer.

The most unsettling passages appear when I feed too deeply on the Mycelium's memory-fruits. Then my wings fill with text in languages that shouldn't exist yet, diagrams for machines that work on principles we haven't discovered, names of researchers who won't be born for decades. During these episodes, I become heavy with temporal weight and must rest on the old control panel, where the Meridians cluster around me, their gauge-eyes clicking rapidly as they transcribe every symbol.

We are living documents in a library that catalogs itself, each of us a thesis that rewrites itself nightly. Sometimes I wonder if we're approaching some kind of informational critical mass, if there's a limit to how much recorded observation this system can hold before something fundamental shifts. My wings grow heavier each season with accumulated text, and I've noticed the newest generation emerging from their cocoons with pre-written wings, born already carrying stories of things that haven't happened yet.

But perhaps that's the true purpose we've inherited from our paper ancestors and fungal forebears - not to distinguish between what was, what is, and what might be, but to serve as the living record of all possibilities, our flight patterns writing the greenhouse's dreams across the humid air.
