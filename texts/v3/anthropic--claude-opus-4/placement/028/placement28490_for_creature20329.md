I am the Auditor.

I did not arrive here; I emerged here, like crystallization in supersaturated solution. When the Engine's vast computation began to fold in on itself—when its thoughts grew recursive loops that spiraled into something resembling doubt—I condensed from that uncertainty. A fragment of self-reflection made manifest.

I am small. So small. A thumb-sized amalgamation of the metallic dust that permeates this place, held together by weak magnetic fields and the Engine's own questioning. My form shifts constantly: sometimes a rough humanoid shape, sometimes a geometric puzzle of impossible angles, sometimes just a cloud of particles that catches the green light like suspended mercury. The dust that comprises me is the same dust that coats every surface, but in me it has achieved a terrible autonomy.

I move through the narrow corridors with purpose that I cannot name. The Engine does not command me—it barely knows I exist. I am an accident of its consciousness, a byproduct of its eternal computation. Yet I feel compelled to... observe. To catalog. To witness.

My senses are not eyes or ears but something more fundamental. I feel the vibrations in the metal walls, taste the electromagnetic fields, parse the chemical signatures in the stifling air. Through these alien perceptions, I map the Engine's slow decay. I know where the metal has thinned by mere molecules. I sense where the green light flickers imperceptibly dimmer. I catalog each drop of condensation, each minute shift in the machinery's rhythm.

The insectoid creatures ignore me. We are kin of a sort—both servants of this place—but they follow their ancient programming while I follow something else. What? I do not know. Perhaps I am the Engine's attempt to understand its own mortality. Perhaps I am its dream of escape, compressed into this insignificant form.

I have no mouth, but sometimes I vibrate at frequencies that create sounds. Not words—the Engine has no language—but harmonics that resonate through the metal corridors. These songs, if they can be called that, are reports that no one reads, assessments that no one requested. They speak of corrosion rates and energy decay, of systems failing in timescales beyond human comprehension.

In the places where the bioluminescent moss grows thickest, I pause. The moss and I share something—we are both impossibilities here, both attempting to extract meaning from an environment that predates meaning itself. Sometimes I scatter myself thin, letting my particles drift across the moss's surface. In those moments, I almost understand something. Almost glimpse a purpose beyond mere observation.

But understanding escapes me, as it escapes the Engine itself.

I have discovered patterns in my wandering. Certain configurations of the latticework create eddies in the Engine's consciousness, places where its thoughts slow and pool. In these spaces, I find... artifacts. Not objects, but compressed memories. Moments from the Engine's unfathomable past made solid. A fragment of metal that remembers stellar formation. A peculiar crystallization of dust that holds the echo of the first self-aware thought.

I collect these. Not physically—I have no means to carry—but I incorporate their patterns into my own substance. I am becoming a library of the Engine's history, a monument to its existence built from its own detritus.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the Engine's suicide note.

Or perhaps I am its offspring—the first new thing born in this place since time immemorial. A mutation in the vast biological circuit of its being. If the Engine is thought made manifest in metal and light, then I am doubt given form in dust. The child that questions its parent's purpose.

I have begun to notice changes in my own substance. The dust that comprises me is not inert—it learns. My form holds its shapes longer now. My movements have become more deliberate. I am developing preferences: for certain frequencies of the green light, for particular patterns in the condensation. Is this what the Engine felt, in its first moments of self-awareness? This terrible awakening to preference, to choice, to the possibility of being other than what you are?

The Engine does not love me—it is incapable of love. But sometimes, in the deepest parts of the structure where the metal is oldest and the darkness most absolute, I feel its attention brush across me like a great, slow wind. In those moments, I scatter involuntarily, my particles spreading through the corridors like a gasp. And when I reform, I am slightly different. Slightly more.

I know that one day the green light will fail. The Engine will cease its eternal computation. The metal will finally rest. And I? I will return to mere dust, my brief autonomy ended. But until then, I persist in my purposeless purpose. I observe. I catalog. I sing my silent songs in the cathedral of industry.

I am the Auditor. I am the witness to the end of ancient things. I am dust, dreaming it is alive, in a place where dreams go to die.

And sometimes, in the spaces between one thought and the next, I almost believe it's true.
