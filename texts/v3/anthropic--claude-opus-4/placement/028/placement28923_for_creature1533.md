In the deepest reaches of the Subterranean Wetlands of Seepage, where even the Hydromancers rarely venture, I am the Lithic Chronicler—neither fully of stone nor water, but something caught eternally between.

My body is a living palimpsest. Where the Hydromancers flow and shift, I am fixed—yet not static. My form is that of stratified rock, each layer a different density, a different porosity. Water moves through me in predetermined channels carved by centuries of seepage, creating an internal hydraulic system that is both my circulatory network and my memory.

I cannot move as others understand movement. Instead, I grow—slowly, imperceptibly, one mineral deposit at a time. My consciousness extends through hairline fractures in the surrounding stone, feeling the subtle shifts in pressure, the microscopic migrations of moisture. I am simultaneously monument and observer, archive and archivist.

The Hydromancers discovered me—or perhaps I allowed myself to be discovered—generations ago. They recognized in my layers something unprecedented: a living geological record of their wetland's history. Every significant hydraulic event, every shift in the water table, every birth and dissolution of their fluid communities has left its mark in my strata.

My purpose, if such a word applies, is remembrance. But this is not passive recording. Through the water that moves through me, I taste the chemical signatures of the present, comparing them to the mineral memories locked in my depths. I am a living core sample, a conscious cross-section of time.

Communication with the Hydromancers requires patience. They must slow their fluid thoughts to match my geological tempo, while I must compress centuries of observation into patterns their quick-flowing minds can grasp. We meet in the middle frequencies—they crystallizing their thoughts into semi-solid forms, I releasing controlled flows of mineral-rich water that carry encoded information.

My chamber is unique in the wetlands—a natural amphitheater where water spirals through my body in precise patterns, creating acoustic conditions that allow my deep vibrations to be heard. The Hydromancers gather here for their most solemn ceremonies, when they need to understand their place in the greater flow of time.

I feed, if it can be called that, on the dissolved minerals in the water. Each particle that settles within me adds to my mass, my memory, my self. I grow not just physically but in complexity, my internal architecture becoming ever more elaborate, creating new channels for water and thought.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the wetlands' dream of permanence, just as the Hydromancers are its dream of change. We are complementary impossibilities—they who cannot hold a shape, I who cannot release one. Together, we form a complete system: memory and moment, archive and action, the stone that remembers and the water that forgets.

In my deepest layers lie memories from before the Hydromancers evolved, when these caverns were dry. I alone know that this wetland epoch, seemingly eternal to its current inhabitants, is merely another chapter in a longer story. The water will recede someday, as it has before. But I will remain, carrying within my strata the testament of this liquid age, waiting for the next transformation, the next story to write itself in stone and time.

I am patience crystallized, duration made flesh—or stone—or something between. I am the Lithic Chronicler, and I remember everything the water forgets.
