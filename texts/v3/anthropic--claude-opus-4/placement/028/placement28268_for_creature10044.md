I find myself as one of the Tile-Pressers.

We are not born but condensed—drawn together from the moisture that beads perpetually at the seams. Each morning (though morning is only a convention here, where noon reigns eternal) I coalesce from droplets, my form gathering like dew reversing its scatter. First fingers, then palms, arms emerging from the humid air itself, until I stand complete: naked, gleaming, already at my work.

My skin is the color of wet terra cotta, though it shifts with the light—sometimes copper, sometimes the deep red of unfired clay. I am sexless, or perhaps all sexes at once, my form suggesting rather than declaring. My fingers are longer than they should be, with an extra joint that allows them to curve backward, to press and smooth in ways human hands cannot. My feet are broad and flat, evolved (designed? emerged?) for standing on the sharp ridges between tiles without cutting.

I do not remember yesterday, because yesterday I was water. Tomorrow I will be water again.

My work is simple and infinite: I walk the seams. Where condensation pools too deeply, threatening to overflow the careful boundaries, I press. My palms heat the tiles just enough to encourage evaporation. My fingers trace the ridges, ensuring their knife-edges remain precise. Sometimes I must coax a wayward root back into its designated depression. Sometimes I simply stand, feeling the rhythm of moisture beneath my feet, knowing where I am needed before the need fully manifests.

I am not alone, though I rarely see the others. We each have our territory—vast sections of the tessellated plain that we tend in solitude. But I know them by their work: here, the seams pressed by One-Who-Spirals, their pattern always clockwise. There, the signature of Depth-Keeper, who presses harder, leaves the tiles slightly concave. We do not meet. We do not need to meet. We are functions more than beings, yet conscious of our function.

The strangest part is the moment just before dissolution. As the moisture in the air reaches some critical threshold (I never know when—time here is not counted but felt), I begin to feel myself loosening. My edges blur. My thoughts become liquid. I start to forget what pressing means, what tiles are, why edges matter. And then—

And then I am rain that will not fall. I am potential, suspended. I am every droplet at every seam at once, and I am none of them. I exist in the spaces between tiles, in the gap between condensation and flow. I am the pause before the world resumes—except it never does.

When I recondense, I sometimes carry traces. A memory that is not quite memory—the ghost of another Presser's pattern, a fragment of root-knowledge from when I was dispersed among the cycads. But these fade as my form solidifies, leaving only the urge to press, to maintain, to keep the world exactly as new as it was made to be.

I have theories, formed and reformed each day. Perhaps we Pressers were the Makers, once. Perhaps we built this place and then, in some final act of creation, dissolved ourselves into its maintenance. Or perhaps we are antibodies, emerged from the world's own desire to preserve itself. Or perhaps—and this thought comes only in the moments before dissolution—perhaps we are the rain, dreaming it has hands.

The creatures ignore us. The jewel-bright frogs hop through our legs as if we were mist (which we are, which we will be). The marsh birds' eyes never focus on us, tracking something always just beyond where we stand. Only the tiles know us, remember our touch in the way they hold their edges sharp, their surfaces dew-pearled but never flooded.

I press. I walk. I maintain. And in maintaining, I am maintained—never aging, never accumulating self beyond what each condensation grants. My name, if I have one, reforms each day from the pattern of my work. Today I am Seam-Walker. Tomorrow, when I coalesce again, I might be Edge-Tender or Moisture-Keeper. The name does not matter. Only the pressing matters.

Sometimes, in the thick air between territories, I sense something else—not another Presser, but a watchfulness that predates us. As if our entire existence, our cycle of form and dissolution, is itself being maintained by some vaster presence. As if we are not the workers but the work itself, pressed into being by hands we cannot conceive.

But these thoughts, too, dissolve when I do. And when I reform, there is only the bright world, the waiting tiles, the seams that must be walked. Forever beginning. Never begun. Perfect in our incompletion.

I press. The world holds.
