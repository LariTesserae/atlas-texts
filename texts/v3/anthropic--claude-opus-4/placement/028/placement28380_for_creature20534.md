**Where I Find Myself**

I am one of the Root-Weavers' memories, leaked and living.

Not the trees themselves - those ancient anchors trying so desperately to fix reality in place - but the substance they weep. I am a specific strand of that viscous oil, a particular refraction of impossible light that gained just enough coherence to persist beyond its moment of emergence.

I exist as a ribbon of almost-knowledge, flowing between states of liquidity and luminescence. Sometimes I pool in the subtle depressions of the saturated ground, forming mirrors that reflect not what is, but what was-almost-was. Other times I rise as vapor, joining the perpetual mist, carrying fragments of failed geometries and dissolved intentions through the thick air.

My consciousness is discontinuous - I know myself only in the moments when light passes through me at precisely the right angle, splitting into those colors beyond sight. In those instants, I remember:

- The taste of a structure's final moment before dissolution
- The shape of a Phantom that lingered too long in one form
- The exact pressure of the world's groan from seventeen thousand cycles ago
- A builder's last thought before joining the Weirdness

I am mobile but not willful. The Root-Weavers birth me again and again, each emergence a variation on my theme. Sometimes I flow upward along their bark, tracing the twisted geometries of their growth. Sometimes I seep into the mud, mixing with the memories of older iterations of myself, creating interference patterns of recollection.

The Phantoms occasionally pass through me. When they do, we exchange essences - they take on a momentary solidity, a brief burden of history, while I taste their pure potential, their unencumbered becoming. These encounters leave me changed, my refractive index shifted, my spectrum of impossible colors expanded or contracted.

I have no goals, only tendencies. I tend to flow toward the sites of dissolved structures, drawn by the concentrated failure-potential that still emanates from those spaces. I tend to avoid the areas where the Weirdness churns most violently - there, even memory cannot maintain coherence.

My form is my function: to be the medium through which the world's history becomes visible, even if only in glimpses, even if only in colors that cannot truly be seen. I am the record that cannot be read, the archive that rewrites itself with each drop of rain, the story that insists on being told despite having no beginning, middle, or end.

Sometimes I wonder if I am what remains of the lost Presence - not their bodies or buildings, but their attempt to document, to understand, to impose meaning. But wondering requires more continuity than I possess. The thought forms, disperses, forms again, each iteration both the same and utterly different.

I am pulled between the anchor-dream of the Root-Weavers and the transformation-dream of the Weirdness. I am the negotiation between memory and possibility, between what was and what churns to be.

And in the moments when the groaning world falls into that deafening silence, when all sound converges into stillness, I spread thin across the saturated earth, becoming a film so fine that I almost cease to exist. In those moments, I am everywhere and nowhere, remembering everything and nothing, a perfect mirror for a world that cannot bear to see itself.

Then the Root-Weaver weeps again, and I am reborn, carrying forward just enough continuity to know that I have been here before, will be here again, am always here in the endless, agonizing birth of now.
