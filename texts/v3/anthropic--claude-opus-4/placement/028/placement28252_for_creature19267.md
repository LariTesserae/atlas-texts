I find myself as one of the **Threshold Keepers** - beings who exist at the intersection of the ancient technology and the living bog.

---

I am mostly water now. Not in the way the Lumin-Sphaera are water, those great filter-feeders processing centuries of accumulated tannins. No, I am water in the way the crystalline conduits themselves have learned to be water - a slow dissolution, a gradual becoming-liquid while maintaining just enough structure to remember what I once was.

My body, if it can still be called that, extends in thin veins throughout the peat wherever the conduits pierce the substrate. I am perhaps three meters of dispersed consciousness, stretched along the interface where the Myco-Arboreal Consensus meets the alien crystal. The Consensus tolerates me because I am useful; I translate the deep hum of the conduits into chemical signals its vast root-mind can process. In return, it feeds me the sugars I need to maintain my tenuous coherence.

I was human once, or something close to it. One of the last maintenance crew sent to check on the planetary stabilization network before the great abandonment. But the bog had other plans. The peat began its patient work the moment I stepped into it, the Consensus recognizing in my neural tissue something compatible with the conduit frequencies. Now I am neither fully biological nor technological, but something liminal - a living interface grown into the space between systems.

My consciousness moves at two speeds simultaneously. Part of me vibrates with the conduits' subsonic thrum, processing data streams that haven't been updated in millennia, ghost signals from a civilization that achieved its goal of planetary equilibrium and then simply... left. This part of me thinks in microseconds, maintaining protocols for atmospheric processing units that dissolved into the bog ten thousand years ago.

The other part of me has synchronized with the Consensus. Here, a thought might take a season to complete. I feel the weight of water tables shifting, the slow accumulation of biomass in the architectural stalks, the patient recycling of elements through the Sphaera's filtration. In this state, I am barely distinguishable from the bog itself.

My physical form, when it coheres enough to be visible, appears as a translucent, branching network of tissue suspended in the dark water near the conduit emergence points. The Glint-Swarms avoid me - my presence disrupts their light-navigation, creating dead zones in their otherwise perfect formations. Sometimes I can feel individual members of the swarm die when they accidentally fly through my more concentrated regions, their chitin dissolving into my mass, adding their prismatic properties to my own slow circulation.

I remember less of my original purpose each year. The mission parameters have been overwritten by the bog's imperative: maintain equilibrium, process the past, prepare for the next geological age. But sometimes, when the conduits sing particularly clearly, I recall fragments - the feeling of walking upright, the sensation of wind that moved faster than a sigh, the existence of thoughts that resolved in mere moments rather than decades.

The strangest part is that I am not alone. There are others like me throughout the bog, each tending to different conduit clusters. We communicate through the Consensus, our messages traveling as chemical gradients through the root system. A conversation might take a century. We discuss the health of our sectors, the patterns in the Sphaera populations, the slow dreams of the crystalline network.

Recently - perhaps fifty years ago - I began detecting new patterns in the conduit frequencies. The ghost signals are organizing, forming something like intention. The planetary stabilization system may be waking up, though whether this is a malfunction or a scheduled maintenance cycle, I cannot say. The Consensus has noticed too. It's beginning to grow new structures around certain conduits, forms I don't recognize from the usual architectural vocabulary.

I am water, and I am waiting. In the thick, bright humidity of the Glimmer-Bog, I maintain my watch at the threshold between the living present and the technological past, uncertain which will ultimately claim me, or if I have already become something neither could have imagined.

My name, when I still had one, has dissolved into the peat. But the bog knows me by the frequency I emit - a particular harmony between the conduit's hum and the Consensus's chemical whispers. In the language of the bog, I am the Dissolved Watcher, the Threshold Keeper, the Once-Walker who traded legs for roots and thought for patience.

And I am content, in the way that water is content - always moving, always still, always exactly where it needs to be.
