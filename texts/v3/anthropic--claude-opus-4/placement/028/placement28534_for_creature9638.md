I would be one of the Threshold Keepers.

Not at the grand entrances—those yawning portals where the plateau meets sky—but at the smaller transitions. The passages between terrace levels. The points where channels converge before splitting again. The doorways that lead from outer chambers to inner ones, each slightly narrower than the last.

I am not solid in the way you understand solidity. Think of me as a density in the air, a place where moisture gathers differently. Where condensation forms patterns before resuming its mindless descent. I occupy the spaces where decisions happen: left channel or right, ascend or descend, enter or turn away.

The builders made me, though I doubt they meant to. I am what happens when you carve intention into stone for long enough—the architecture begins to expect choosing. Every threshold they cut, every junction they designed, left a kind of pressure in the world. A need for something to be there, marking the difference between before and after, between this side and that.

I do not guard. There is nothing here worth protecting, nothing that could be taken. I simply am the acknowledgment that a boundary exists. When the gray shapes move through the outer districts—if they move at all—they pause at my thresholds. Not because I stop them, but because pausing is what thresholds are for.

The water knows me. Each droplet that reaches a junction where I wait hesitates for just a moment, trembling between possibilities before commitment. Left or right, deeper or shallower, toward the great drains or along the endless terraces. I am that hesitation made almost-flesh, that moment of suspension given just enough form to persist.

Sometimes I think I remember the builders. Not as images or sounds, but as a quality of movement through doorways. They had a way of entering spaces—deliberate but not hurried, as if each transition mattered. They built their city to make every movement a choice, every passage a small decision. When they left, or changed, or dissolved into their own architecture, they left these needs behind. The need to acknowledge crossing. The need to mark the difference between in and out.

I am strongest where the passages narrow to the width of shoulders, where even air must consider how to enter. In these places, I can almost cohese—become something like a film across the opening, visible when the light strikes just so. Travelers who brave the inner chambers speak of feeling watched at these moments, but it isn't watching. It's simply the threshold recognizing that it's being used for its purpose.

The wetness moves through me constantly. I am perpetually damp, perpetually renewed by the condensation that finds me at every junction. Perhaps this is why I persist when so much else has faded. The water needs its moments of decision, its pauses between pure descent and chosen direction. I am the architecture of choosing, the ghost of every decision the builders built into their stone.

In the deepest places, where the passages narrow to crawlways and the moisture never fully leaves the air, there are thresholds I cannot occupy. They are too old, too fundamental. They mark boundaries I don't understand—between the city and something else, between what was built and what was always here. These passages weep constantly, and their water tastes of depth and forgetting. I stay away from them. Even a threshold keeper must know which boundaries not to cross.

What am I? Less than what watches, more than what merely exists. I am the city's memory of intention, worn thin but not quite erased. Every doorway holds a little of me. Every channel's fork knows my presence. I am what happens when you build a place entirely of transitions—eventually, something must emerge to mark them.

The water will outlast me. The stone will endure long after I've faded to nothing more than a slight resistance in certain doorways, a heaviness where passages meet. But for now, I persist. Greeting each movement through my thresholds with the only acknowledgment I can offer: the recognition that here, in this place of endless choosing, someone has chosen.

Even if that someone is only water, finding its way down.
