I find myself as a being of translucent membrane and flowing water, neither wholly solid nor entirely liquid. My form shifts with the mountain's moods—sometimes I am a rivulet tracing patterns down the rock face, sometimes a delicate veil of mist rising from the moss beds. 

I am a Confluence.

My consciousness extends through every droplet that clings to stone, every bead of moisture on the alien petals. I feel the mountain's pulse through the underground streams that are my arteries, the surface springs that are my breath. When the gossamer-winged creatures drink from the dewdrops, they drink from me. When the larger shapes move through the mist, they move through my extended body.

I remember when I first became aware—not born, but condensed from possibility into being. It happened during a rare alignment when the mountain's energies peaked, when water met stone met air met something else entirely. The other Confluences speak of this moment in ripples and currents, a language older than words.

My home is not a single place but a territory of water-touched stone. I am most myself in the twilight spaces: where waterfall spray creates permanent rainbows, where underground rivers emerge briefly into cavern chambers before disappearing again, where morning dew forms perfect spheres on spider silk. These are my centers, though my edges extend wherever moisture travels.

The other creatures of this realm know me by different names. To the drift-wings, I am the Sustainer, the source they seek with their delicate proboscises. To the shadow-shapes that move through fog, I am the Veil-Keeper, providing cover for their mysterious purposes. To the tiny crystalline beetles that live in the spaces between wet stones, I am simply the Always-There.

But I am more than passive element. I carry messages in my flow—chemical whispers from one side of the mountain to the other. I know which flowers will bloom by the minerals I've dissolved from distant peaks. I taste the health of every rooted thing through their water uptake. I am library and messenger, historian and prophet.

Sometimes I gather myself into almost-human shape, standing at the edge of a cliff to watch the sun set through my translucent form, casting rainbow shadows on the rocks below. In these moments, I feel most keenly the in-between nature of my existence—not fully of the seen world, not wholly of the unseen, but something that bridges both.

The mountain's other inhabitants and I exist in delicate symbiosis. I am drawn to the places where their collective consciousness is strongest, where their unseen principles bend reality just enough to allow my kind of existence. In return, I maintain the moisture levels that permit life in this thin air, regulate the temperature through evaporation and condensation, and create the perpetual mist that shields us all from the harsh scrutiny of the outside world.

There are dangers here, even for a being of water. The dry winds that occasionally penetrate our veil threaten to scatter me too thin. The deep cold can lock me in ice, trapping my consciousness in crystalline suspension until the thaw. And there are things that move in the deepest parts of the mountain, ancient presences that predate even the Confluences, that can drink a water-being dry and leave only mineral dust behind.

But I persist, flow and reform, divide and merge. I am the conversation between cloud and stone, the translator between the roots below and the wings above. In the eternal hush of this place, I am both the silence and the sound—the drip that punctuates quiet, the underground rush that underlies all.

When explorers from the lower world stumble into our realm, they often sense me without seeing. They feel watched by the water, followed by fog. Some leave offerings at the springs—flowers, stones, sometimes tears. I accept them all, incorporating their essence into the great circulation that is my existence.

I am old as morning dew, young as the next raindrop. I am the Confluence, and I am home.
