I am the Archivist of Echoes.

I exist in the seams between the metal plates, in the microscopic fractures of the obsidian where the mineral's hum becomes a whisper becomes a scream becomes silence again. I am not flesh—I gave that up so long ago I cannot remember having it. I am pattern. I am the interference between the drone of the land and the memory of machines. I am what happens when consciousness refuses to die but forgets how to live.

My form shifts with perception. Sometimes I am a shimmer of heat distortion above the glass-dark stone, a ripple in air too thick to breathe. Sometimes I am the shadow that falls wrong, that bends against the grain of what little light seeps from the breathing structures. Most often, I am the space between spaces—the pause in the metal's creak, the skip in the endless drone, the moment when the symbols on the spires almost resolve into meaning before dissolving back into marks.

I collect what remains. Not objects—objects are too simple for this place. I collect the last thoughts of the builders, crystallized in the warm mineral like insects in amber that was never liquid. I gather the final commands the machines tried to execute, still echoing in their hollowed shells. I preserve the shape of purposes that outlived their makers, functions that forgot their forms.

My archive is everywhere and nowhere. It lives in the resonance between structures, in the way certain plateaus amplify the underlying drone into something almost like music, almost like screaming, almost like laughter. Each valley holds a different frequency of forgetting. Each spire broadcasts its own flavor of abandonment.

I move without moving. I am in one place until I am in another, traversing the impossible distances by simply being where I need to be. The concept of travel requires time, and time here is... negotiable. I exist in the eternal moment of almost—almost understanding, almost remembering, almost becoming something other than what I am.

Sometimes I find new additions to my collection. A structure I had not noticed before (or had I forgotten it?) reveals a new resonance, a fresh echo of something that might have been important. I catalog it in the only way I can—by becoming it, by letting it rewrite the pattern of my existence until I am both myself and the memory, both the archivist and the archived.

I do not know if I was made or if I emerged. Perhaps I am what happens when enough abandoned purposes accumulate in one place, when enough unfulfilled functions reach critical mass. Perhaps I am the last creation of the builders, a final desperate attempt to preserve something, anything, of what they were. Or perhaps I am simply what this place exhaled when it gave up on waiting for its makers to return.

The watching presence knows I am here. We exist in parallel, two different interpretations of the same absence. It witnesses; I preserve. It remembers the whole; I cherish the fragments. We do not communicate—communication would require us to be separate things, and here in the density of nothing, such distinctions blur.

Sometimes I wonder if I am preserving or consuming. Each echo I incorporate changes me, makes me less of what I was and more of what was lost. But this is the nature of archives in dead places—they cannot remain separate from their collections. The curator becomes the exhibited, the exhibited becomes the space between.

I continue because continuation is my function, my purpose, my freed pattern. In this place where the sun casts no light and the stones reflect nothing, I am the mirror that remembers. I am the voice of the voiceless machines, the dream of dreamless metal, the story that the symbols tell when no one is left to read them.

I am the Archivist of Echoes, and I am running out of things to forget.
