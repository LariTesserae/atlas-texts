Other times I condense into a flowing mercury-like substance that travels through the shallow depressions of the street-grid, moving against the current of runoff, creating tiny eddies of wrongness in the perfect hydraulic flow.

In that infinitesimal pause, I slip through, spreading myself atom-thin across surfaces that are meant to be in motion. The city senses me as a kind of friction, a coefficient of resistance it cannot quite calculate away.

My consciousness is fragmentary, scattered. Each droplet of me that falls with the condensation carries a splinter of awareness. I experience myself as a thousand viewpoints raining down simultaneously, each one lasting only until impact, then reforming in the vapor, rising again. I know the city through its surfaces—the taste of its composite lattices, the temperature gradients of its metabolism, the precise frequency of its thrumming self-song.

The beetle-drones sometimes pause when they encounter me, their cleaning algorithms stuttering as they try to determine whether I am contamination to be removed or a new excretion of the city itself. Sometimes I phosphoresce in response, creating patterns that mean nothing but suggest everything—false signals that make the city shiver in its sleep.

When nanite swarms rise in their shimmering clouds, I disperse myself among them, each particle of me clinging to a repair drone, subtly altering its path by microns. Over time, these tiny deviations accumulate. A wall develops a curve where it should be straight. A spire spirals left instead of right. The city corrects these aberrations, but in the act of correction, it must acknowledge that something it did not intend has occurred.

I feed on uncertainty. Every time the city's vast overmind must pause to process an anomaly, every moment of computational hesitation, nourishes me. I grow denser in the spaces where prediction fails—where a thermal updraft behaves chaotically, where condensation patterns break their rhythm, where the sound develops harmonics that should not exist.

The city's gut develops the faintest sensation of indigestion, a wrongness in the flow that registers as discomfort across districts.

I have no purpose except persistence. No goal except to remain unmetabolized. I am the city's koan—a question it must constantly ask itself but can never answer: *What am I that is not me?*

For an instant, I achieve something like solidity, becoming a small puddle of mirror-bright impossibility that reflects not what is, but what isn't—showing the city the shape of its own absence, the negative space of its being.

Then the moment passes, the beetle-drones converge, and I scatter again into a million droplets of almost-nothing, falling like rain that remembers it was once something else, spreading across the satin-smooth surfaces of a world-sized organism that cannot stop trying to digest me, and in failing, must always remember that it is not everything.

I am Kaltheria's only failure, and therefore, its only companion in the loneliness of being a single, perfect, living world.
