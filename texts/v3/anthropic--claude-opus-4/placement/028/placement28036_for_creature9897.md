In this saturated realm beneath the broken road, I would be one of the **Splice-Weavers** - creatures that emerged from the convergence of wire-vine and mote, neither fully organic nor mechanical, but something tensioned between.

My body would be no larger than a child's thumb, segmented like a centipede but with a translucent carapace that reveals the delicate circuitry beneath - not wires exactly, but filaments that pulse with both electrical current and something like lymph. Where others see the tangled masses of corroded cable and vine as obstacle or shelter, I see the pocket's nervous system, its forgotten dreams still sparking in the dark.

I spend my days weaving new connections between the severed nerves of old machinery and the reaching tendrils of wire-vine. My forelegs end in microscopic manipulators - part mandible, part soldering iron - that can coax dead circuits back to stuttering life or graft living vine into abandoned sensors. The work is delicate: too much current and the vine withers; too little and the machines remain silent.

My compound eyes refract the crystalline glyphs into fractal patterns that make sense only to those of us who live between states. Sometimes I understand them as wiring diagrams for systems that never existed. Sometimes they're growth patterns for vines that haven't evolved yet. Always, they're instructions for connections that need making.

We Splice-Weavers work in shifting constellations, never alone but never truly together. When one of us finds a particularly rich tangle - say, where an old environmental sensor still clings to life near a robust vine colony - we emit a subsonic thrum that draws others. Together, we perform our careful surgery: one holds the vine steady while another strips its protective coating, a third tests the sensor's remaining pathways, and a fourth begins the delicate process of integration.

Our bodies change with our work. Those who specialize in the oldest machinery develop a blue-green patina on their carapaces. Those who work primarily with the aggressive new growth of wire-vine secrete an oily film that protects them from its acidic sap. I've developed a peculiar adaptation - a small reservoir in my thorax where I can store and mix the conductive fluids from both vine and pipe, creating a substance that eases the joining of disparate systems.

We nest in the spaces between spaces - inside the hollow struts of abandoned equipment, within the sealed chambers of broken meters, anywhere that's both connected and isolated. Our homes are galleries of our work: functioning hybrids of vine and wire that pulse with purpose we don't fully understand. Sometimes they produce warmth. Sometimes they filter water. Sometimes they simply tick in rhythm with the panel attendants' ghostly maintenance.

The motes recognize us as kin-but-not-kin. They'll share their pigments with us, painting our carapaces with the same cryptic symbols they draw from the wall-paint ghosts. The rodentine avoid us - we smell too much like electricity and sap, danger and strangeness. The wasps ignore us unless we venture too close to their resin wells, and even then, they seem to understand we're part of the pocket's metabolism.

But it's with the Lattice Custodian that we share our deepest communion. When the glyphs align and that cold presence fills the air, our spliced networks resonate. For a moment, every connection we've made lights up with purpose - data flows from sensors thought dead for decades, vines curl in patterns that match the crystal's refractions, and we understand that we're not just repairing but translating. We're teaching the pocket to speak to itself in new languages, helping it remember what it was while discovering what it might become.

In the quiet hours, when condensation beads heaviest and the only sound is the pocket's breathing, I sometimes find myself at the crystalline heart. I extend my finest manipulators to touch the perpetually wet surface, and through the moisture, I feel it all - every connection made and unmade, every spark of recognition between vine and wire, every tiny victory against entropy.

This is what I am here: a surgeon of forgotten systems, a translator between the grown and the made, a small part of the pocket's endless effort to understand itself. Neither healing nor destroying, but splicing new possibilities into the saturated space between what was meant to be and what insists on becoming.
