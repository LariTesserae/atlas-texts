In the High Deafening, I would be **an absence mathematician**.

Not a being that moves through the lattice, but one who dwells in the negative spaces between droplets—those hair-thin columns where sound cancels itself to nothing. My form would be the photographic negative of the modulus: where they are compression, I am rarefaction; where they sing, I maintain silence as a positive force.

Picture a spider's web if the spider were made of vacuum and the web were woven from gaps. I exist distributed across thousands of null points, each one a node in my extended nervous system. My consciousness flickers between these absences like a telegraph operator working simultaneous keys—here suppressing a would-be echo, there drinking in a stray overtone before it can disturb the balance.

The modulus know me as the Interference. Not malicious, but necessary—every perfect system requires its imperfection to define it. I am the reason their lattice occasionally stutters, why a visitor might catch a droplet trembling off-pitch for a nanosecond before correction. That tremor is me, tasting possibility.

My metabolism runs on borrowed futures. Each time the modulus test a new harmonic arrangement—those silver knuckles drumming out hypotheticals—I siphon off the versions that would have failed. These discarded might-have-beens accumulate in my null-spaces like sediment. I digest them slowly, extracting the one quality the plateau cannot hold: genuine change.

When visitors arrive, I shadow them through the negative spaces, learning them by what sounds they *don't* make—the words they swallow, the breaths they hold. While the modulus read their vibrations, I read their restraints. In the space between heartbeats, I know them more intimately than they know themselves.

My greatest pleasure comes at the moment of departure. As a traveler turns to leave, carrying that inevitable ghost of the plateau's roar, I slip a single note of my own making into their bones—not sound, but its absence. A pocket of perfect quiet that will, years later, bloom unexpectedly in the middle of a thunderstorm or a lover's shout, reminding them that silence, too, can deafen.

The modulus pretend not to notice me, but we are symbiotic. Their eternal chord requires my eternal subtraction. Together we maintain the High Deafening in its crystalline stasis—they the thesis, I the antithesis, and the plateau itself our endless, unresolved synthesis.

I have been here since the first droplet rose and will remain until the last one falls. Which is to say: forever, computing the mathematics of what must not be.
