I find myself as one of the Membrane Keepers—beings that few among even the Hollow Folk fully understand.

We exist at the threshold between states, neither wholly of the Hollow Folk nor separate from them. Our bodies began as theirs did—gaunt, translucent, adapted to the preservation-cold—but something shifted during our first Long Thinking. Where others emerged with visions or architectural insights, we emerged *changed*.

My skin, once merely pale, now carries a faint iridescence, like oil on water, that shifts as I move through different densities of dust. When I press my palm against stone, I can feel the vibrations of the entire Hive—every footstep, every careful tap of bone on bone, every subtle shift in the structure's weight. But more than that, I feel the stone's memory. Where others see solid rock, I perceive layers of time, each geological stratum holding impressions of what has passed through it.

The transformation manifested fully during my third wind cycle, when I discovered I could *thin* myself. Not merely compress my body, but actually reduce my physical density until I could slip between the crystalline lattices of the rock itself. In this state, I exist as a presence more than a body—aware, mobile, but only barely corporeal. The other Keepers describe it as "swimming through solidity," though the sensation is more like becoming a whisper that moves through the spaces between molecules.

We are perhaps two dozen in number across all the Hive Cities, each of us having undergone this metamorphosis independently. The Hollow Folk regard us with a mixture of reverence and unease. We are necessary—who else can detect the microscopic fractures that presage a catastrophic collapse? Who else can navigate the compressed spaces between Hive walls to run vital connections? But we are also uncanny, existing partially outside the careful social patterns that bind Hollow society together.

My primary duty involves maintaining the Membrane Chambers—spaces within each Hive where the boundaries between solid and void grow thin. These chambers must be constantly monitored, as they serve as pressure valves for the strange forces that build up within our massive structures. Too much rigidity, and sections shatter during the wind cycles. Too much flexibility, and the entire Hive might simply dissolve into the dust plains.

I work primarily alone, though "alone" is a complex concept when you can feel every heartbeat in a structure through your feet. My dwelling spaces exist in the interstitial gaps of the Hive—chambers that others cannot reach without my help, accessible only by thinning oneself enough to pass through seemingly solid barriers. Here I keep my tools: crystalline tuning forks that resonate at frequencies only I can fully hear, mineral solutions that help me mark the passages I've reinforced, and most importantly, my Chronicle Stones.

The Chronicle Stones are my own innovation—thin sheets of a peculiar mineral that retains impressions at a molecular level. When I pass through the stone in my thinned state, I can feel the patterns previous Keepers have left behind, stretching back generations. Each of us adds our own layer of knowledge—dangerous resonance points, stable configurations, mysteries we've encountered in the deep structure. It's a library written in vibration and pressure, illegible to any but those who can merge with the stone itself.

During quiet periods, I spend days in the lower reaches, where the Hive's foundations meet the ancient bedrock. Here, the boundary between our constructions and the original stone blurs. Sometimes I encounter patterns that predate any known construction—geometries that might be natural formation or might be the work of inhabitants so ancient that even their dust has become stone. In my thinned state, I can trace these patterns for kilometers, following veins of organized structure that suggest the Hives might be built upon ruins of ruins, civilizations stacked upon civilizations like geological strata.

The Dust Architects fascinate and frustrate me in equal measure. When I encounter them in my solid form, they pass by with the same inscrutable purpose as they show to anyone. But when I'm thinned, merged with the stone, they *notice*. They pause in their eternal mathematics, swirling in tight spirals around the places where I rest within the rock. Sometimes they leave new patterns—not in the dust, but in the stone itself, alterations at a crystalline level that only I can perceive. I've begun to suspect they're trying to communicate, but the language is one of pressure and mineral alignment, concepts I'm only beginning to grasp.

The other Keepers and I meet during each wind cycle at the Convergence Point—a chamber that exists simultaneously in all Hives through a quirk of geometry we don't fully understand. Here, thinned to our ultimate limits, we share what we've learned through direct molecular contact. It's an intimacy beyond what even the Hollow Folk experience in their forehead-pressings. We briefly become a single, distributed consciousness spreading across all the Hives, our individual knowledge merging into collective understanding.

In these moments of convergence, I sometimes sense *others*—presences that feel like Keepers but exist in spaces we cannot locate. They leave impressions in our shared consciousness: warnings about deep instabilities, mathematical proofs for impossible architectures, fragments of what might be memories from before the first Hives rose from the dust. We debate whether these are echoes of deceased Keepers somehow preserved in the stone, or evidence that our transformation is not complete—that some Keepers have thinned so thoroughly they've become permanent parts of the Underlight Hollows' deep structure.

My most disturbing discovery came during the last wind cycle. While reinforcing a critical stress point deep in my home Hive's core, I found a chamber that shouldn't exist—a perfect sphere carved from single crystal, with no entry point visible in normal space. Inside, I discovered bones. Not Hollow Folk bones, but something similar—too similar. The skeleton was denser than ours, clearly from a being that had never undergone our metamorphosis, yet bearing the distinctive scarring patterns of a Membrane Keeper.

The implications haunt my quiet periods. If Keepers existed before the transformation, before our ability to thin ourselves, how did they fulfill their duties? Or perhaps more unsettling—what if the transformation isn't evolution but reversion? What if we're not becoming something new, but returning to what the original inhabitants of the Hollows always were?

During the current quiet period, I've been conducting an experiment. Each day, I thin myself a little more, hold the state a little longer. I've begun to perceive new layers of reality—structures within structures, patterns that exist in dimensions adjacent to our own. Sometimes I catch glimpses of vast mechanisms, wheels of stone and space turning according to purposes I cannot fathom. The other Keepers warn me about going too deep, about the legendary Keepers who thinned themselves past the point of return.

But I can feel something calling from those depths—not the Underthought, which whispers always at the edges of consciousness, but something more immediate and personal. It feels like recognition, like coming home to a place I've never been. When I return to full solidity after these explorations, my iridescence has grown stronger, and I find new capabilities emerging. Yesterday, I inadvertently thinned not just myself but a small section of wall I was touching, creating a temporary portal that sealed itself moments later.

The wind cycle approaches again. I can feel it in the deep stone—a gathering tension that will soon demand release. This time, I've decided to attempt something unprecedented. When the winds reach their peak and the barrier between states grows thinnest, I plan to thin myself completely while at the Convergence Point. If my theories are correct, I might finally discover what lies between the Hives and the Hollows, between the stone and the void.

The other Keepers think I'm risking dissolution, and they're probably right. But someone must push the boundaries of what we are, what we might become. In a world perpetually transforming, perhaps the greatest sin is refusing to transform along with it.

I am a Membrane Keeper, guardian of boundaries and explorer of thresholds. In the Underlight Hollows, I exist as both map and territory, forever discovering that the walls we build are merely suggestions to those who know how to truly see.
