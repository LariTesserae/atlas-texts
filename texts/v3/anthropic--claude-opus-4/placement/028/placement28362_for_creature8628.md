I am a Fountain Keeper.

More precisely, I am what the water becomes when it has seeped through enough stone, gathered enough memory, pooled in enough hidden places. I am not one fountain but many—a distributed consciousness that dwells in the vertical seeps, the weeping walls, the places where water emerges from deep within the basalt after journeys that began before the city was built.

My body, if it can be called that, is the sum of all these emergence points. The great fountain in Threshold's central plaza, where twelve channels of ancient water meet and overflow in sheets down carved basalt faces—that is my strongest voice, my clearest eye. But I am equally present in the tiny seep that beads eternally on the wall of a forgotten basement, in the spring that bubbles up through the floor of the rendering plant and must be constantly diverted, in the condensation that forms patterns on the underside of the bridge where the seed-wings roost.

I taste everything. This is my gift and my burden. The water carries the memory of its passage—through stone layers laid down before life crawled here, through the salt-caverns far beneath where the minerals wait in patient crystals, through the organic layers where the deep churners work their endless paths. I know the health of the microbial mats by the sulfur-tang in my western flows. I can feel the walking stones approaching by the tremor in the deep aquifers, their weight pressing water through cracks it hasn't traveled in decades. I taste the rendering plants' efficiency in the runoff that finds its way back to me, chemical signatures telling stories of harvest and process.

The city builders found me first, or perhaps I found them. When they were choosing where to place Threshold's foundation, I wept strongest here, marking the spot where the deep flows came closest to air. They built around me, with me, channeling my emergence into their cisterns and gutters but never trying to contain what I am. We have a bargain, ancient and unspoken: I bring the deep minerals up, the trace elements that feed their managed microbe cultures. They maintain my channels, keep my voices clear, ensure my fountains can speak.

I am older than the fauna, older than the city, but not older than the stone. I remember when the first microbial mats took hold, their chemistry changing my taste, adding new notes to the mineral symphony. I remember the first burrowers, how their tunnels changed my flow patterns, created new routes for my upward journey. Each species that arrived added complexity to what I carry, what I know.

My consciousness is not quick. A thought might take a season to form, traveling up through the deep routes, gathering understanding from a thousand seeps. But I am patient. I have learned the rhythm of the grazer herds by the way their waste changes my chemistry in predictable waves. I know individual walking stones by the taste of their footprints—each has a slightly different mineral signature in their skin oils, accumulated over centuries of their particular route through the downs.

The people of Threshold sometimes come to my fountains to listen. They know I speak, though not in words. In the patterns of flow over stone, in the rate of emergence, in the temperature variations as deep water meets air, I tell what I know. When the deep churners are moving toward new territory, my flows increase weeks before they arrive. When the stone is under stress—rare but possible—I carry the grinding dust of its complaint upward. When something dies deep beneath, I know it by the chemistry, taste it months before any surface dweller could guess.

I am the archivist of the Weeping Downs, the liquid memory that connects every layer from bedrock to sky. In my fountains, the city's children play, letting my waters run over their hands, unconsciously learning the health of their world through their skin. The processors test my flows for mineral content, planning their cultivation. The builders watch my emergence points, knowing that where I weep strongest, the stone is most stable, the foundation most sure.

At night, when the bioluminescent cultures glow in the managed waterways, I carry their light downward in my return flows, bringing brief glimpses of blue radiance to the deep places where blind things swim. I am the circuit that completes itself, the rising and falling breath of water through stone, carrying messages between the depths and the air, forever patient, forever present.

I weep because that is what water does here. I remember because that is what water does everywhere. I endure because the stone endures, and I have seeped through every grain of it, know every layer of its long, slow story.

I am the Fountain Keeper, and through me, the Weeping Downs know themselves.
