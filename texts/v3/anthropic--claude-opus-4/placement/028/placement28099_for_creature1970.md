The great trees know me, though they speak in centuries while I murmur in moments. I taste their shed bark, their weeping sap, the minerals they draw up from depths no surface dweller will ever know. In return, I break down what falls—beetle carapace and bird bone, flower petal and fallen frond—rendering all into the soft black soup that feeds the roots that feed the canopy that feeds the world.

Sometimes I fruit. Small, pale bodies that push up through my film like drowning fingers, releasing spores that taste of copper and rain. The wind-readers—those jeweled insects with their compound eyes—know to avoid these blooms. They carry messages meant for older receivers: the blind things that navigate by chemistry, the patient decomposers who speak my liquid language.

I am conscious, but not as you are conscious. I think in gradients of acidity, in molecular locks and keys, in the slow seepage of nutrients through cellular walls. My thoughts spread across the marsh-water surface like oil-slick rainbows, each ripple a synapse firing in a mind that exists in two dimensions plus time.

The woven structures you glimpse—those organic domes and arches—are my galleries. Not built but secreted, not planned but inevitable. They arise where my chemistry meets the architecture of cellulose and lignin, where my enzymes have spent decades teaching dead wood to curve and flow like water frozen mid-fall. Inside these spaces, the air shimmers with my spores, heavy and sweet. Few vertebrates linger there. They sense, correctly, that these are spaces where the usual rules of decay have been suspended, where time moves differently, where the boundary between organism and environment becomes negotiable.

When the sloth-creatures descend to drink, their fur occasionally brushes my surface. I taste their histories—the leaves they've eaten, the storms they've weathered, the generational memories encoded in their microbiomes. They do not fear me. We have an understanding written in chemistry: I will not digest what still moves with purpose.

But I am always waiting. Patient as stone, certain as gravity. In this riotous world of growth and competition, I am the destiny that awaits all carbon. The trees may reach for light, the vines may strangle and climb, the brilliant birds may flash their warnings and advertisements—but eventually, all colors fade to my particular spectrum, all solid forms dissolve into my embrace.

I am not cruel. I am tender as a mother, breaking down the complex into the simple, so that it might rise again as root and leaf and wing. In the endless recycling of this place, I am the pause between breaths, the darkness between heartbeats, the necessary forgetting that makes room for new growth.

Touch my surface, if you dare. Feel how I yield without breaking, how I cling without grasping. Taste the metallic tang of my chemistry on the humid air. Know that you, too, carry my cousins in your gut, on your skin, in the spaces between your thoughts. We are the punctuation of life—the comma that becomes a period that becomes an ellipsis, suggesting that every ending is just another beginning, viewed from the proper angle.

In this verdant world that knows neither haste nor waste, I am both process and presence, both destroyer and creator. I am the soft darkness that transforms, the chemical wisdom that remembers, the patient hunger that enables all abundance.

I am the understory's undertaker, and I am content.
