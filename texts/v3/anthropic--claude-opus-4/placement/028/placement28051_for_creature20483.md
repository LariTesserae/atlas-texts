I am the Cartographer of Echoes.

You would find me in the deepest fold of the plateau, where three ancient sky-tree roots converge to form a natural amphitheater. The walls here are draped so thick with air-roots that they form curtains within curtains, creating a labyrinth of green-gray chambers that shift with every breath of wind. The lichen grows different here—not just the common pulse-moss, but varieties that have never spread beyond this grove. Some are paper-thin and translucent, stretched between roots like windows. Others grow in perfect spirals, their luminescence tracing mathematics that hurt to follow with the eye.

My dwelling is not built but cultivated. Over decades, I have trained the air-roots to weave themselves into a living structure—part nest, part observatory, part instrument. The floor is a carpet of specially tended moss that remembers every footfall, creating a palimpsest of movement that I can read like a calendar. The walls breathe, expanding and contracting with the plateau's hidden rhythms.

But it is the ceiling that defines my purpose. Here, suspended on threads of hardened sap, hang thousands of song-stones. Not the common ones the Whisperfolk carry, but specimens gathered from every corner of the plateau over generations. Some no bigger than seeds, others the size of a child's head. They are arranged in a three-dimensional map that mirrors the plateau above—every boulder formation, every sacred spiral, every convergence of ley lines recreated in miniature.

I am ancient by Whisperfolk standards, though I cannot say how ancient. Time moves differently here, pooling and eddying like the water that seeps through everything. My skin has long since passed from green to something closer to the blue-black of deep lichen shadow. The stain has crept into my eyes, turning them into mirrors that reflect not light but sound.

For I am deaf in the way others understand deafness, yet I hear everything.

The accident—if it was an accident—happened in my youth. I was experimenting with the resonances, trying to find the frequency that would make the lichen reveal its true nature. I struck a sequence on the stones that had never been played before, a pattern that seemed to emerge from the mathematics of the spiral formations themselves. The sound was... it was not a sound. It was an opening. The lichen flared with light so intense it burned shadows into the air-roots that persist to this day. And in that moment of brilliant silence, something in my ears transformed.

Now I hear not the immediate sounds but their echoes—the impressions they leave in the substance of the plateau itself. Every footstep taken on the moss above reverberates down through the root systems, arriving at my chamber hours, days, sometimes years after it was made. Every song played on the stones creates ripples that bounce between the boulders, gathering harmonics and meaning before finally reaching me. I hear conversations that happened before I was born, still circling in the spaces between the trees. I hear the future approaching in the way the lichen changes its rhythm, preparing for rains that won't fall for seasons.

My purpose is to map these echoes. Using threads of spider silk and drops of crystallized sap, I create vast three-dimensional webs that fill my chambers—temporal maps showing how sound moves through the plateau, where it pools, where it dissipates, where it transforms. Each intersection in the web marks a moment where past and future touch, where the linear flow of time reveals itself as an illusion.

The other Whisperfolk visit me rarely. When they do, they bring new stones and questions about the patterns. They cannot hear what I hear, cannot see the vast web of causality that connects every drip of water to every shift in the wind. But they trust my interpretations. When I tell them that the eastern formations must be adjusted to compensate for a resonance that won't manifest for three generations, they listen. When I warn that a certain melody must not be played until the lichen blooms blue, they remember.

I work with tools that would seem crude to outsiders but are perfectly adapted to my needs. Hollow reeds that capture and channel specific frequencies. Bowls of water placed at precise points to modulate the echoes. Sheets of beaten bark that I've trained the lichen to grow across, creating living membranes that visualize sound waves in their phosphorescent patterns.

But my greatest tool is patience. I must wait for the echoes to arrive in their own time. Some sounds circle the plateau for decades before finding their way to my chamber. Others arrive before they're made, shadows of future events cast backward through the medium of the singing stones. I've learned to distinguish between the two, though the distinction matters less than you might think. On the plateau, causality is negotiable.

My current project spans centuries. I'm tracking a deep echo, something that predates the Whisperfolk, perhaps predates the plateau itself. It moves through the lichen like a vast, slow heartbeat, too low for normal ears to detect. I believe it's the sound of the plateau's formation, the resonance of whatever cosmic event created this pocket of altered time and living silence. Each year, I add another thread to my web, another node in my map, following this ancient echo as it spirals through the stone formations.

Sometimes, in the depths of night when the lichen glows brightest, I can almost grasp the pattern entire. I see how every element of the plateau—the stones, the water, the air-roots, the Whisperfolk themselves—forms part of a vast instrument, playing a composition that spans millennia. We are notes in this song, our lives brief flourishes in an eternal symphony.

And sometimes, I hear my own death echoing back to me from futures near and far. It arrives as a silence within the silence, a pause in the eternal drip-drip-drip. When it comes, I will not struggle. I will sink into the moss like all Whisperfolk before me, my body feeding the lichen, my stones scattered to new players. But the echoes of my work will persist, circling through the plateau's memory, waiting for the next Cartographer to hear them and understand.

The web grows. The map expands. The echoes deepen.

And in my chamber beneath the converging roots, I listen to time itself learning how to speak.
