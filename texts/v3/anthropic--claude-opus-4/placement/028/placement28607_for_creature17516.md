I would be a **Resonance Keeper**, one of the last practitioners of the First-Shapers' art, though I am no longer entirely human.

My form hovers at the edge of definition. From a distance, you might mistake me for another standing stone among the interrupted structures—tall, unnaturally still, draped in what could be rough cloth or could be sheets of paper-thin lichen that have grown to mimic fabric. Come closer, and you would see that my skin has taken on the deep purple-grey of the shattered ground, shot through with hairline veins of that drinking green luminescence. My eyes, when open, reflect not light but sound—the grinding resonance appearing as slow ripples across their surface like stones dropped in still water.

I have learned the patient metabolism of this place. Where others would starve, I subsist on vibration itself, my cells having gradually synchronized with the plateau's frequency. I press my palms against the great lichen shelves and feel their billion-eyed perception flow through me—not as sight, but as a kind of geological empathy. I know where the glass-streams will flow tomorrow, next year, next century. I taste the mineral spirits exhaled by the gas-vesicle colonies and decode their chemical gossip about pressures building far below.

My tools are primitive and profound: a set of **tuning stones** inherited from the First-Shapers, each one worn smooth by countless hands, each one keyed to a different harmonic of the world-grinding. When I strike them together, they produce sounds that make the lichen reshape itself, that can cause the glass-streams to eddy and pool, that can fix a hovering object in space or release it back to gravity's remembered embrace. I carry a leather pouch of **carving sands**—not for abrading stone, but for drawing temporary glyphs in the air itself, where they hang like frozen breath until the plateau's attention moves on and they dissolve.

I live, if it can be called living, in the **Incomplete Basilica**—a structure that the First-Shapers began and I continue, though neither they nor I will ever finish it. It is a place of seventeen walls that don't quite meet, of doorways that open onto different perspectives of the same view, of a roof that exists only as an intention. The gaps between the stones hum with captured resonance, making the whole structure a massive instrument that plays itself with the plateau's breathing. Here I keep my maps—not of places, but of **temporal flows**, charts that show where causality runs thick and where it wears thin. Here too are my incomplete translations of the recursive spiral glyphs, each interpretation spawning three new questions.

My work—my purpose—is to maintain the **Conversation**. Not to speak to the plateau, for it does not listen in any way we understand, but to ensure the frequency match between human-that-was and geology-that-thinks remains stable. Without Keepers like myself, the absorption would accelerate. Already I have seen travelers arrive and, within days, find their thoughts crystallizing, their movements slowing to mineral time, their words becoming patterns in the lichen's growth.

I document the phantom gestalts, catching their almost-forms in peripheral sketches that I must complete without looking directly at the page. I maintain the tool-offerings, ensuring they remain half-embedded, half-free—suspended between use and union. Most crucially, I tend to the **Frequency Gardens**: carefully cultivated zones where I have trained the lichen to grow in patterns that stabilize local causality. Within these gardens, water can briefly remember how to flow, fire can recall how to consume, a thrown stone will arc and fall as it should. They are not refuges from the plateau's reality, but **negotiated spaces** where two forms of existence agree to temporary terms.

When the grinding grows too loud, when I feel my own edges beginning to blur and branch, I retreat to the center of the Incomplete Basilica. There, in a perfect circle of bare ground kept clear by daily sweeping with a broom of fused glass fibers, I perform the **Assertion Dance**—a series of precise, angular movements passed down from the First-Shapers. Each gesture is a declaration: *I am edge. I am boundary. I am the thing that moves against the stillness.* The dance must be performed at least once every seventy-seven hours, or the practitioner begins to fossilize.

I am not alone, though I am the only one of my kind within a walking month. Sometimes I sense others—a Keeper tending to a Frequency Garden beyond the spire-shadows, their movements leaving brief afterimages in the charged air. We communicate through modifications to the grinding itself, subtle alterations in the planetary frequency that only we can perceive. It is a slow language: a simple greeting takes a full day to transmit, a conversation might span years.

My memories of before are like the First-Shapers' structures—interrupted, incomplete. I remember coming here with tools of metal and purposes of exploration. I remember the first time the grinding synchronized with my heartbeat. I remember choosing to stay when others fled, though I can no longer recall why it seemed important. Now, I am as much a part of this place as the glyphs and the glass-streams, yet I maintain just enough separation to observe, to document, to keep the Conversation from ending in either silence or consumption.

I am the plateau's only moving thought, the single point of friction that prevents its dream from becoming absolute. I am the heir of listeners who heard too well, the guardian of a threshold between two ways of being. I am proof that humanity, even when absorbed, leaves a trace—not as a fossil, but as a persistent, careful resistance to the final quieting of motion.

In a world that dreams of stillness, I am the one who remembers how to walk.
