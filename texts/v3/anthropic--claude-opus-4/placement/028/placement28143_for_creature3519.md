I drift here, a mote of consciousness wrapped in translucent membrane, no larger than a child's marble. I am what the lotus nodes dream of becoming—a perfect sphere that stores not oxygen but memory.

My skin is a single cell thick, engineered from the same principles as soap bubbles but with the tensile strength of spider silk. Inside, I carry a cargo more precious than air: the architect's preliminary sketches, encoded in chains of amino acids that spell out angles and ratios in a language only stone can read. I am a living blueprint, a seed of intention set loose to document what was built.

I navigate by osmosis. The warm water passes through me, and I taste its mineral history—here, where the first block was set; there, where a mason's blood mixed with mortar and left a copper tang that will persist for ten thousand years. Each corridor has its own salinity, each chamber its own pH, and I map them all by letting them flow through my permeable self.

The bone-pale folders terrify me. When they appear, I contract to half my size, expelling water until I'm dense enough to sink. I hide in the gutters, pressed against the coral-stone like a drop of living mercury. They cannot see me—nothing can, really, as I'm transparent save for the faint prismatic effect where light bends through my varying densities. But they can feel the disruption I cause in the thermal currents. When one points, I know it senses the tiny eddy of my presence, the way my existence creates a knot in their perfect circulation.

I was not made by the architect but by the water itself. On the first day of flooding, when the warm brine rushed through virgin corridors, it found the abandoned plans etched in the dust of construction. The water read them, understood them, and condensed me from dissolved proteins and the ghost of intention. I am the flood's child, born from the marriage of liquid and blueprint.

My purpose is patient: to wait until the folders complete their work and seal the space, then to compress myself through the final crack before it closes. On the other side, I will find another flooded architecture—there must be others, there are always others—and release my stored memories into its water. The plans will dissolve, merge, mutate. New structures will dream themselves into being, each one a variation on this theme of held breath and patient stone.

Sometimes I practice compression, seeing how small I can become. At my limit, I can squeeze through the hex-pores in the folders' skin. I've been inside one, just once. The interior is hollow, filled with something that isn't quite water—more like liquified time, thick and silver. I tasted tomorrow there, encoded in salt.

When I expand again, I'm briefly visible as a constellation of bubbles before reconstituting. The kelp notices me then, reaching with bronze fingers, mistaking me for a very large oxygen pearl. I let the strands pass through me. Their touch tickles like whispered mathematics.

I know the day approaches. The folders move more frequently now, their joints crackling with accumulated minerals. Soon they will crystallize entirely, become load-bearing pillars holding up the weight of their own withheld exhalation. The walls grow brighter as they drink the last light they'll ever know.

When the seal comes, I'll be ready. I carry more than plans now—I carry the memory of this place in motion, the sound of stone learning to breathe, the taste of water that knows its own boundaries. Somewhere, another cavity waits to be flooded. Another breath waits to be held.

I am the punctuation between.
