In this world of sound and silence, I would be one of the **Deep-Dwellers**, though not as you might imagine them.

I am old for my kind - perhaps twelve seasons, which is ancient when most live only three or four. My body has grown long and serpentine, segments added with each molting until I am nearly the length of a human's arm. My skin is translucent pearl, showing the dark thread of my gut and the pulsing nodes of my distributed nervous system. Six pairs of legs, atrophied to nubs, mark my ancestry, but I move now by peristalsis - a rippling wave that carries me through the narrow spaces between mineral plates.

But what makes me different is this: I have learned to *listen*.

Not in the way the surface world listens - overwhelmed, deafened, drowning in the cascade of biological noise. I listen to what lies beneath. In the deepest parts of the arroyos, where even starlight cannot reach, the mineral crust remembers every sound that has ever touched it. The calcium andite holds vibrations like a fossil holds form. Strike it correctly, and it rings with the memory of ancient storms, of solas that fell ten thousand years ago, of the first tentative drummings of the first burrowers.

I have developed something unprecedented among my kind - a tympanic organ, a drum of stretched skin across a hollow in my fourth segment. It began as a mutation, a weakness that should have killed me. Instead, it became my gift. Pressed against the arroyo walls, I can feel/hear the deep history of this place. Not the immediate cacophony above, but the fundamental frequencies - the tectonic settling of the plateau itself, the slow circulation of the hidden water table, the infrasonic conversations between sola root systems that span kilometers underground.

My home is a cathedral of echo. A widening in the arroyo where water, eons ago, carved a spherical chamber from softer stone. The walls are smooth, polished by ancient currents, and they focus sound like a lens focuses light. Here, I have learned to sculpt silence - to position my body in precise configurations that cancel certain frequencies while amplifying others. I can create pockets of absolute quiet, or I can make a single whisper carry for miles through the arroyo network.

The other deep-dwellers avoid me. To them, I am wrong - too large, too strange, too interested in vibrations that serve no purpose for feeding or mating. But sometimes, in the pre-dawn dark when the surface world briefly quiets to its lowest ebb, they gather at the edges of my chamber. They come to experience something they have no concept for but are drawn to nonetheless: the absence of sound. The negative space. The pause between heartbeats of a sleeping giant.

I know things the humans do not. I know that their stories are true - there *is* something beneath the plateau, though not sleeping. It is the compressed acoustic memory of a million years of sound, fossilized into the deep stone. Every song, every scream, every whisper of wind across mineral crust, compressed and preserved and waiting. The solas are not keeping it asleep. They are keeping it from escaping all at once.

In my chamber, in the absolute silence I can create for moments at a time, I sometimes hear it trying to get out - a pressure, a potential, like the moment before thunder. And I wonder: if I live long enough, if I grow large enough, if I perfect my understanding of acoustic space and mineral memory, could I release it? Not all at once - that would shatter the plateau like glass. But slowly, carefully, one frequency at a time?

I think of the deaf human elders, dancing their thoughts with their hands in the shadow of the solas. They alone might understand what I am trying to do. Not to add to the world's noise, but to sculpt its silence. To make spaces where sound has meaning because it is chosen, not simply endured.

But I am patient. My kind lives in geological time, even if our individual lives are brief. I will grow, segment by segment. I will listen to the deep stone. I will map the acoustic shadows of this drowned-in-sound world.

And perhaps, one day, I will teach it a different kind of song.

The darkness holds me. The stone remembers. In my cathedral of echo, I practice the most radical act possible in this loud world:

I listen to the space between sounds.
