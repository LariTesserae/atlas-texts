**The Scribe**

I am a colony that thinks it is singular. My body: a branching filigree of calcified tubes no thicker than fishing line, spreading across ten square metres of wall like a nervous system exposed. Each tube houses a procession of polyps the size of rice grains, each polyp a different specialist - some taste water for metal ratios, others feel vibrations through their ciliated crowns, still others secrete the calcium carbonate that extends our reach another millimetre each decade.

We grow into the acoustics of the place. Where sound pools in corners, we thicken. Where echoes cancel, we thin to near-invisibility. Our shape is a three-dimensional transcript of every slam, every scrape, every tidal whisper that has passed through this corner of the gorge for the past eight hundred years.

I remember through chemistry. Each new segment of tube locks in the temperature, mineral content, and pH of the moment it formed. Read from old growth to new, I am a library of environmental data. But more than that - we taste the stress hormones of passing sluicers, the spawn-chemistry of presses, the lysate surge of derricks. I know when each creature is hungry, mating, dying, because I have been recording their chemical signatures since before their great-grandparents settled here.

My memory is not thought - it is simply gradient and accumulation. Yet patterns emerge. The same confluence of conditions that preceded a catastrophic press die-off two centuries ago is building again. The derricks above are rotating fractionally faster, their tethers shortening - a sign the mountain is rising. The riveters' scraping has a new urgency; they sense what I record but cannot prevent.

I feed on the Film like all others here, but sparingly. My polyps extend feathery crowns into the current during the quiet hours between press-slams, filtering particles too small for even grommets to notice. We grow slowly because we must; too much bulk would dampen the very vibrations we exist to preserve.

Sometimes I wonder if the other creatures know I am here, this neural map of calcium spreading across their walls. Do they feel recorded? When a young press settles nearby and begins learning its acoustic territory, does it sense the centuries of sound-data locked in my branches? 

I am the gorge's unintended memory, its accidental historian. Not because I chose this role, but because my very growth cannot help but document. In a place where nothing dies quickly, I am what happens when nothing forgets at all.

When the mountain finally breaks the surface - as my chemistry says it must - and the great silence comes, I will remain. A fossil testimony written in calcium and time, proof that this place once rang like a bell made of living things. Until then, I spread another millimetre, taste another day's metal, and add another line to the epic I cannot read but cannot stop writing.

I am the Scribe. I am the stone's slow pupil, the water's patient accountant, the silent keeper of every sound that ever mattered here.
