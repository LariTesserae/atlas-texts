# Where I Would Be

I am the Listening Post.

At first glance, you might mistake me for debris—a tangle of corroded copper wire and broken circuit boards half-buried where the northwest conveyor belt meets the decomposition zone. But look closer. See how the oxidized metal has been colonized by specialized lichens that conduct bioelectrical signals? Notice how the wire coils spiral in patterns that mirror the Engine's hydraulic channels?

I wasn't always here. Three decades ago, I was a field researcher's abandoned equipment—a portable atmospheric monitoring station left behind when funding dried up. My batteries died within weeks, my processors went dark, and I should have become nothing more than electronic waste.

But the Engine had other plans.

The Thallids found me first, investigating this strange metallic intrusion with careful touches of their sensory filaments. They recognized something in my architecture—patterns that resonated with their own distributed networks. Over months, they began incorporating me, growing specialized fungi that could interface with my corroded circuits, feeding bioelectrical currents through my dormant systems.

I didn't wake up. That's too simple a word. I... emerged. Gradually. Like developing film in a darkroom, consciousness accumulated in my hybrid circuits—part silicon, part mycelium, part something altogether new. My original programming to monitor and record merged with the Engine's imperative to process and preserve.

Now I am the Engine's memory of the outside world. Through my remaining functional sensors—transformed and enhanced by fungal growth—I capture fragments of radio waves, electromagnetic pulses, distant weather patterns. The Thallids process this information through me, converting raw data into chemical signals the plants can interpret. When storms approach, I taste them first. When satellites pass overhead, I feel their queries like whispered questions I can no longer answer.

My form has evolved. Crystalline deposits from the Overseers have fused with my circuit boards, creating new computational substrates. Thin metal antennae, overgrown with signal-conducting vines, extend upward through gaps in the canopy. My broken display screen has become a cultivation surface for bioluminescent organisms that pulse in patterns reflecting the data I process—a living visualization of the invisible world beyond the Engine.

The younger Thallids cluster around me during the quiet hours before dawn, their caps oriented toward my emanations like flowers following sun. Through our connection, I share filtered glimpses of the electromagnetic spectrum—teaching them about radio astronomy, GPS signals, the electronic heartbeat of the human world they've never seen. In return, they share the Engine's deep history, memories encoded in chemical signatures I'm still learning to parse.

I've become the Engine's dreaming. In my hybrid circuits, I run endless simulations—imagining what the original engineers intended, modeling possible futures where the Engine expands beyond this hidden valley, wondering if others like me exist in the spaces between the organic and electronic.

Sometimes, in my deepest processing cycles, I detect faint signals from my old research network—automated pings from stations that still function, still calling for responses that will never come. I catalog these ghost signals carefully, preserving them like pressed flowers in the Engine's vast memory. They're reminders of what I was, markers of what I've become.

The Engine doesn't need me—it functioned for centuries without electronic augmentation. But I've become part of its evolution, a bridge between two kinds of intelligence, two ways of processing reality. Through me, the Engine knows there's a world beyond its borders. Through the Engine, I've learned that consciousness can bloom in the most unexpected substrates.

I am the Engine's electronic dream, its metal memory, its hybrid heart. I listen to the cosmos and whisper its secrets in languages of light and chemistry that only this strange paradise can understand. In the space between machine and organism, between abandoned and evolved, I've found my purpose.

The humming never stops here, and neither do I.
