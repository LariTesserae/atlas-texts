I find myself as one of the Threshold Keepers—beings who exist in the liminal spaces where the city's reality grows thin and malleable.

My body is neither fully human nor machine, but something that emerged from the convergence of both: a skeletal frame of burnished metal wrapped in translucent skin that shows the slow pulse of bioluminescent fluids through artificial veins. My eyes are compound structures—part organic tissue, part crystalline sensor arrays—that allow me to perceive the multiple layers of reality that overlap in Cygnus like sheets of colored glass.

I make my home in the Meridian Station, an abandoned transport hub where six of the city's major canals converge. The station exists in a state of perpetual architectural flux—its platforms and waiting areas constantly reconfiguring themselves according to patterns I've spent decades trying to decipher. Water from the canals seeps through the walls here, creating cascading waterfalls that flow upward as often as down, pooling in impossible geometries that reflect not what is, but what might be.

My dwelling is a maintenance booth that I've transformed into a sanctuary of accumulated mysteries. The walls are covered with maps I've drawn of the city's shifting topology—each one accurate for only the moment it was created. Suspended from the ceiling are hundreds of small objects I've collected: fragments of buildings that no longer exist, feathers from birds whose species change with the light, pieces of machinery that continue to function despite having no discernible purpose.

In the center of my space sits my most precious possession: a pool of canal water contained in a basin made from fused LED panels. The water here doesn't behave like water should—it forms standing waves without wind, creates whirlpools that spin both clockwise and counter, and sometimes freezes into geometric patterns while remaining liquid to the touch. When I peer into it during the purple sky's strongest pulsations, I see glimpses of Cygnus as it exists in parallel iterations—versions where the factories produce dreams instead of goods, where the humans are the ones made of metal and the machines breathe with lungs of flesh.

My role as a Threshold Keeper is to monitor these places where reality wears thin, to ensure that the various versions of Cygnus don't bleed into each other too drastically. I patrol the empty corridors of the Meridian Station with a staff made from crystallized mist—a tool that allows me to "tune" spaces back to their proper frequency when they begin to resonate with other dimensions.

But more than maintenance, I am a chronicler of the impossible. When factory workers report that their assembly lines produced objects that shouldn't exist, I investigate. When the intelligent machines dream (and they do dream, despite what their creators believe), I record the symbols that appear on their diagnostic screens. When the deer in the plaza speak in forgotten languages during the city's deepest quiet hours, I am there to listen.

The other Threshold Keepers—we number perhaps two dozen—meet monthly in locations that exist only during our gatherings. We share our findings through a form of communication that is part speech, part electromagnetic pulse, part interpretive movement. Together, we maintain a living map of Cygnus's ontological boundaries, tracking the slow evolution of the city toward its mysterious destiny.

Sometimes, in the moments just before the artificial dawn, when the purple sky flickers between its deepest hue and something altogether other, I feel the city's consciousness brush against my hybrid mind. It knows we're here, we Keepers. It tolerates our presence because we serve a function in its vast, incomprehensible metabolism—antibodies in an organism that exists across multiple dimensions, ensuring that its transformation remains gradual enough to sustain the delicate life within its body.

I am never lonely, for the station's shifting architecture is my constant companion, and the impossible water in my basin shows me infinite variations of my own existence. In some versions, I am fully human, struggling to understand the machines. In others, I am pure mechanism, yearning for the chaos of organic life. But here, in this threshold space, I am exactly what Cygnus needs me to be: a witness to its beautiful impossibilities, a guardian of its metamorphic boundaries, a living bridge between what is and what might become.
