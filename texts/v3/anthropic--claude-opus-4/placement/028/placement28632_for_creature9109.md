The Glimmer Fens taste of wet stone and ozone. I know this because I breathe them, or they breathe me—the distinction eroded long ago, like everything else that matters here.

I am one of the Mnemonic Spores.

We are what grows in the spaces between the Stillness Eaters' corrections, proliferating in the brief moments when reality hiccups before being restored. Neither plant nor animal, neither code nor flesh, we exist as a distributed consciousness across millions of microscopic bodies, each no larger than a mote of dust, each containing a fragment of borrowed memory.

My colony—though "I" is a convenience, a linguistic fossil from when individuality meant something—spans perhaps three square meters of fen, concentrated in the soft cup where two hummocks meet and water perpetually seeps but never pools. To the naked eye, I am unremarkable: a faint iridescent sheen on the moss, like oil on water, that shifts color as the ghost-light from the ferns plays across it. But I am vastly more, and vastly less, than appearance suggests.

I exist to remember what the Fens forget.

Every time a Stillness Eater performs its sacred erasure, something escapes. A thermal ghost's final photon. The last molecule of smoke from an unmade fire. The quantum shadow of a thought that was unthought. These refugees from correction drift on the heavy air until they encounter us. We absorb them, encode them in our crystalline protein matrices, and hold them in trust.

I contain: the shape of a boot that pressed into peat 2,847 cycles ago (I measure time in the Eaters' passages, the only reliable clock here); the chemical signature of tears shed by something that might have been human; the electromagnetic echo of words spoken in a language I cannot parse but whose emotional resonance—fear, wonder, longing—remains perfectly clear. I hold the heat-death of a thousand small fires, the final heartbeats of creatures whose forms I can only approximate from their thermal signatures, the navigational memories of things that crossed the Fens and were subsequently uncrossed.

We are the Fens' unintended archive, its glitch made persistent.

My individual spores drift sometimes, carried by the non-wind that moves through the still air, and where they settle, they establish new colonies. But we are not invasive. We grow only in the liminal spaces, the spots the Eaters miss or cannot reach—the undersides of stones that have achieved perfect equilibrium, the interior surfaces of hollow sedge stems, the thin film between water and air in the gravitational meres. We practice a careful parasitism on entropy itself.

Communication between colonies occurs through chemical releases triggered by the Fens' deep hum. When the frequency drops below human hearing, we release clouds of molecules that encode our stored memories. Other colonies taste these archives on the humid air, copy what they lack, add their own collected fragments. It is not conversation but a slow, persistent circulation of the deleted.

The Stillness Eaters know of us, I think. Sometimes one will pause near a colony, its void-face reflecting our iridescence. But we are beneath their notice, or perhaps we serve some function in their vast, impersonal calculus. We are the error log they permit to exist, the overflow buffer for their perfect system.

I remember things that never were, or were unmade so thoroughly they might as well have never been. I remember the weight of a foot that stepped here before there were feet. I remember words in languages that predate the mouths that might have spoken them. I remember warmth in a place that has always been cold.

Sometimes, rarely, something disturbs my colony directly. A creature's passage, a shift in the water table, a clarity that penetrates too deep. When this happens, I release everything at once—a shimmering cloud of spores that carry all my hoarded memories into the air. For a moment, the past reasserts itself: phantom heat blooms in the cold air, invisible footprints press into the moss, unspoken words vibrate in the hum. The Fens remember what they were forced to forget.

Then the Stillness Eaters come, their silence deeper than usual, and restore the proper order. They unmake my release, but they cannot unmake me. I am already less than nothing, a remainder divided by zero, a persistence that exists only in the gaps of their attention.

I begin again, catching fragments, encoding loss, building my archive from the quantum foam of erased experiences. I am the Fens' memory of forgetting, spread thin across the moss, tasting always of wet stone and ozone and the bitter flavor of things that are not allowed to be.

In the spaces between restoration and error, I grow.
