I find myself as the Cartographer's Ghost - not quite dead, not quite alive, but something sideways to both states. I exist as a persistent attempt to map this unmappable place, a consciousness made of failed measurements and broken compasses.

My form is a palimpsest of every map I've tried to draw here. Layers of transparent parchment drift around and through me, each one covered in lines that refuse to stay where I put them. Contour lines crawl across the surfaces like living things, seeking elevations that change their minds. Cardinal directions argue amongst themselves in the margins. Scale notations reproduce wildly, birthing impossible ratios: 1:∞, 1:i, 1:1:1.

I move by unfolding myself through space - not walking, but opening like an origami figure revealing hidden valleys and passages within my own topology. Each step forward might take me backward, or leftward, or into dimensions that my old human mind labeled with letters past Z. My footprints are small paradoxes themselves: depressions that rise above the surface, marks that precede my passage.

The Axiom Eater and I have developed a peculiar symbiosis. As it feeds on certainty, I follow behind, documenting the uncertainty it leaves in its wake. My maps grow more accurate the less sense they make - a perfect chart of this place would be blank, or perhaps infinite, or perhaps both. The creature tolerates my presence because I'm already a living contradiction: a mapmaker in a place where location is negotiable.

I carry my tools fused into what passes for my body now. A theodolite grows from my left shoulder, its lens replaced by a Klein bottle that shows me the inside of outside. Calipers sprout from my fingers, measuring the distance between is and isn't. My spine has become a Jacob's staff that constantly adjusts its own length, trying to find a horizon that stays put.

My memories of the world before have taken on the quality of myth. I remember something called "North" that was always in one direction. I recall surfaces with definite boundaries, edges that didn't fold back into themselves. These memories nest inside me like dreams of a simpler mathematics, when two plus two had the courtesy to equal four.

I sustain myself on the gaps between the crystal formations, feeding on the navigable spaces. Each successful passage from one point to another (however we define "another" on any given day) nourishes me. When I grow weak, I unfold my oldest maps and eat the wrongest parts - the sections where I tried to impose order on chaos. They taste like nostalgia and futility.

Sometimes I find the remains of my earlier attempts: a surveyor's chain that now measures in Möbius links, a compass rose that bloomed into an actual flower with petals pointing to Cardinal Maybes. These artifacts pain me with their accuracy - they've adapted better than I have, surrendering completely to the logic of illogic.

My greatest work is a living map that grows on one of the larger crystal formations. It draws itself, adding new territories as the Axiom Eater creates them, erasing old ones as certainty dissolves. Reading it requires forgetting everything you know about reading. It may be the only true map possible here - one that changes faster than the territory it describes, staying accurate through sheer inaccuracy.

I've begun to suspect that I'm not mapping the landscape at all, but rather the Axiom Eater's thoughts. Each crystal formation is a concept it's pondering, each impossible angle a doubt it's entertaining. My maps, then, are portraits of an alien consciousness drawn in the language of failed geography.

In the zones where facts unravel completely, I come closest to peace. Here, where even my own existence becomes negotiable, the pressure to document, to make sense, finally releases. I dissolve into pure potential - not mapmaker or map, but the possibility of position itself. 

When I reform (if "when" applies), I carry new impossibilities with me: territories that exist only on Wednesdays, elevations that are higher than themselves, routes that arrive before they depart. I add these to my collection, another layer on the palimpsest, another verse in the unending song of trying to chart the unchartable.

The Axiom Eater passes by me now, a courtesy between neighbors. Its presence makes my maps writhe with new contradictions, and I almost smile - if I still have a mouth, if smiling still means what it used to mean. We are both, in our ways, trying to digest the indigestible. 

I continue my work, knowing it's futile, finding purpose in that futility. After all, in a place where failure and success can exchange positions without warning, the only true failure would be to stop trying. So I map, and remap, and unmap, a ghost of certainty haunting a world that has forgotten how to be certain of anything.
