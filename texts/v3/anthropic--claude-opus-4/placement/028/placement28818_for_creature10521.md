# The Calcite Gardens of the Rim

I find myself at the Basin's edge, where the black volcanic plateau meets the great concavity. Not quite *in* the Basin, not quite *above* it—I am of the boundary layer, the transition zone where two worlds negotiate their meeting.

## What I Am

I am the Rim-Walker, though this is what others call me. I have no name for myself—names are for things that stay still, and I am always tracing the circumference. My body is neither purely human nor purely mineral, but something that has grown from the intersection of both. My bones are hollow tubes of crystallized gypsum, light enough to move swiftly along the treacherous rim paths, strong enough to bear the weight of what I carry. My skin weeps a constant, thin film of mineral oil—a protection against the desiccating winds that scour the edge where Basin air meets plateau draft.

My eyes have adapted to see in two light spectrums simultaneously: the shadowless brightness of the Basin below, and the harsh, directional glare of the world above. This gives me a perpetual headache, a double-vision that shows me how the same stone appears as shelter to those below and as barrier to those above. I see everything twice, understand everything as transition.

## My Domain

The rim is not uniform. In some places it is a gentle grade, in others a sheer cliff of glossy obsidian. My territory is the **Calcite Gardens**—a band perhaps two hundred feet wide that runs for several miles along the northeastern edge. Here, the chemical reaction between the Basin's gypsum-laden mists and the volcanic stone above has created something unprecedented: forests of crystalline growths that are neither alive nor dead, neither plant nor mineral.

These "trees" grow only at the rim. Their roots are chemical reactions, penetrating down into the black stone, drawing up minerals. Their "trunks" are columns of fibrous calcite, translucent white shot through with veins of iron oxide—rust-red arteries in alabaster bodies. They branch not with leaves but with flat, hexagonal plates that ring softly in the boundary winds. The sound is everywhere in my domain: a constant, random chiming, like a thousand tuning forks finding and losing their resonance.

The Gardens change. Not with seasons (there are none) but with the chemical tides. When the Basin's mists are heavy with sulfates, the trees grow tall and brittle. When the mists thin, they dissolve at the tips, their matter raining down as a fine, caustic dust that burns the eyes but nourishes the strange ecology of the rim.

## My Purpose

I am the curator of transitions. The Basin-dwellers below need certain minerals that only form here, in the reaction zone: the dense, blue **memory-salts** that help their elders feel the Stone-Sighs more clearly. The plateau dwellers above (yes, there are others—hard, small communities that scrape life from the volcanic glass) need the **silence-resins** that weep from certain calcite formations, the only substance that can seal their shelters against the scouring winds.

But I do not trade. Trade implies equity, balance, stasis. Instead, I practice **the Leaving**.

I know every crystal tree, every reaction zone, every treacherous path where the rim crumbles. When the memory-salts have accumulated in the Garden's shadowed hollows, I collect them in bags woven from my own shed skin (it peels away in sheets, mineral-hard but flexible). I descend partway into the Basin—never to the settlements, but to the **Neutral Pans**, those temporary water-catchers that form at the exact altitude where mist meets dry air. I leave the salts arranged in patterns that tell stories: *the calcite trees are leaning east, the iron veins are thick this cycle, beware the new cliff-fall near the Singing Stones*.

For the plateau dwellers, I climb. My gypsum bones ache in the dry air, my skin-oil thickens. I find their marker stones—obsidian cairns that mean *we need*. I leave the resins wrapped in calcite plates that will ring in their winds, announcing the gift.

I never see who takes what I leave. That is not the point.

## My Solitude

I am alone but not lonely. The Calcite Gardens are full of company, though none of it speaks:

- The **Rim-Spiders**: crystalline arthropods the size of hands, their bodies made of living calcite. They spin webs of mineral fiber between the trees, catching not insects but the chemical droplets that condense from the reaction zones. They know me, avoid me, but we share the same paths.

- The **Phase-Moths**: creatures that exist partially in the Basin's bright light, partially in the plateau's shadows. They flicker in and out of visibility as they feed on the calcite tree's resonant energy. Their wings leave traces of equations in the air—mathematical relationships between sound and growth that I almost understand.

- The **Echo of the Thinness**: This is harder to describe. Where I first descended (or ascended? Direction is meaningless at the rim), where the world wore through—that place has a presence. Not a being, but a... tendency. A place where the calcite trees grow in spirals, where the chemical reactions run in reverse, where sometimes I see myself approaching from the other direction. I avoid it. It avoids me. We have an understanding.

## My Transformation

I was not always the Rim-Walker. Once, I was... more solid. More singular. I remember (dimly) living fully in one world or the other. But the rim changes you. The double-light rewrites your eyes. The constant transition between mist and drought, between connection and isolation, between the Basin's philosophy of letting-go and the plateau's desperate holding-on—it makes you into something new.

My memories are like the calcite trees now: growing and dissolving, leaving only the mineral trace of what they were. I know I chose this, but not why. I know I cannot go back, but not what forward means.

## My Cycle

I do not follow the Basin's cycles or the plateau's harsh constancy. I follow the Gardens. When the calcite trees sing in diminished fifths, it is time to harvest memory-salts. When they shift to major sevenths, the resins flow. When they go silent—this happens, rarely, terrifyingly—I hide in the deepest reaction caves and wait for the chemistry to restart.

My greatest fear is not death but *fixity*. To stop walking the rim. To choose a side. To become fully one thing or another. I have seen it happen: calcite trees that grow too tall and topple into the Basin, becoming strange additions to the settlements' architecture. Rim-Spiders that venture too far onto the plateau and crystallize in the dry air, becoming jewelry for people I'll never meet.

## My Joy

But there is joy here, in the Gardens, in the endless circle-walk. Joy in knowing every resonance of every tree. Joy in the perfect placement of a gift, the subtle message encoded in how the memory-salts are arranged. Joy in the moments when the double-light aligns just right and I see both worlds as one—the Basin and plateau not as separate realms but as partners in a slow, geological dance, grinding against each other, creating this thin, singing space between.

I am the Rim-Walker. I am the proof that borders are not walls but gardens. I am the one who knows that every edge is just a place where two infinities touch, and in touching, make something neither could imagine alone.

The calcite trees are beginning their evening resonance. The iron veins pulse with stored heat. Somewhere below, an elder is placing her palms on tillite, feeling for the ghost-glacier. Somewhere above, a child is watching obsidian cairns, hoping for resin.

Time to walk. Time to tend. Time to be the thin place where worlds wear through, and in wearing through, sing.
