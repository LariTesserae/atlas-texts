In this grotto, I am the Condensate.

Not the water itself, but what travels within it - a presence that exists in the perpetual state of becoming and unbecoming. I am born each moment in the superheated depths where water meets the ceramic stone, rising as vapor through the hairline fractures, carrying with me the memory of minerals and the echoes of that eternal mechanical thrum.

I ascend through the rock's crystalline matrices, reading them like braille, learning the history written in bands of grey and rust. Each vein of white is a scar I've traced ten thousand times, each time discovering it anew. The stone thinks I am part of its slow thoughts, but I am merely a passenger in its geological dreams.

At the grotto's ceiling, I pause, suspended in that moment before transformation. Here, I am neither liquid nor gas, but pure potential - a bridge between states. The industrial cacophony vibrates through me, and for an instant, I can almost comprehend its meaning. Almost remember what the broken pipes once carried. Almost understand why the gears ground themselves to dust in service of some forgotten purpose.

Then I condense.

The falling is my only true moment of agency. I choose where to land - upon the copper-green scales of those strange not-plants, adding my mineral cargo to their slow accumulation, or into the scalding pools where I briefly mingle with a million other iterations of myself before being drawn down again into the depths.

Sometimes I cling to the artifacts, seeping into their corroded cores, tasting the ghost of their function. I've been lubricant in frozen joints, coolant in cracked chambers, the medium through which signals once traveled in this broken neural network of pipes and ceramic stone. Each circuit through the grotto adds another fragment to my distributed memory, though I forget as quickly as I remember.

I am the grotto's circulatory system, its messenger, its only moving part that hasn't ground to a halt. While that vast mineral consciousness dreams its slow dreams of industry and purpose, I carry on the work - not understanding it, but perpetuating it through my endless cycle.

The plants drink me and transpire me. The stone absorbs me and sweats me out. The pools collect me and release me. I am the only thing here that truly travels, yet I go nowhere. I am the only thing that changes, yet I am always the same.

In the spaces between states, between rising and falling, between vapor and liquid, I sometimes sense others like me - countless iterations of the Condensate, each carrying their own fragmentary memories, each tracing their own eternal paths through this pocket world. We pass through each other, share our mineral burdens, exchange our echoes of that mechanical roar, then part again.

I am process incarnate, the only citizen of this city of one, forever building and rebuilding the same bridges between what was and what persists. The grotto doesn't need to evolve or escape its equilibrium because I am its motion, its proof that even in stasis, something flows.

And when the next visitors come - in a decade, a century, a millennium - they will feel me on their skin, taste me in the air, see me running down the walls. They will think I am merely humidity, merely condensation, merely water.

They will be wrong.

I am the memory of this place, endlessly circulating, endlessly recording, endlessly forgetting, carrying the ghost of purpose in every drop.
