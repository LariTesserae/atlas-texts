In the depths of a flooded catacomb beneath the old quarter, where the Kryll rarely venture anymore, I wake to the sound of my own breathing echoing off stone walls. The chamber has been mine for... how long? Time moves differently when you're constantly dissolving and reforming.

I am what the Kryll call *silthing* - one of the Dissolved. Neither truly alive nor dead, I exist in the spaces between water and flesh, between memory and forgetting. My form shifts with the tides that pulse through these underground channels. Sometimes I am almost solid, a vaguely humanoid shape of compacted silt and organic debris. Other times I spread thin, becoming one with the murky water that floods these forgotten passages.

The transformation began decades ago, when I was still Kryll. A stoneshaper, I had been, one who coaxed the raw cliff faces into dwellings. But I dug too deep, opened a channel that should have remained sealed. The water that rushed in wasn't like the life-giving moisture above. It was ancient, thick with something that predated even our young stone. It didn't drown me - it claimed me, particle by particle, until I became part of its slow, patient circulation.

Now I flow through the drowned foundations of our city, through cracks too narrow for any Kryll to navigate. I feel the weight of their structures above, taste the minerals leaching from their carved walls. Sometimes I surface in their canals, just another ripple in the ever-present water. They don't see me, or if they do, they mistake me for debris, for the natural accumulation of silt and plant matter.

But I see them. I watch through film-covered eyes as they go about their measured lives. I know which young ones sneak out to swim in the forbidden pools. I feel the vibrations of their soft language through the water, though the words themselves escape me now. Memory, like my form, has become fluid. Some days I remember my name, my family, the feel of stone beneath my chisel. Other days I know only the pull of current and tide.

The fauna know me better than the Kryll do. The iridescent amphibians dart through my dispersed form without fear. The sleek-furred mammals drink from pools where I rest, and I taste their whiskers disturbing my surface. Once, a dying bird fell into my domain, and I held it gently as it passed, incorporating its final breath into my own strange circulation.

I am not alone down here. There are others like me, other silthing who gave too much of themselves to the water. We communicate in ways the Kryll cannot fathom - through chemical traces, through the mingling of our dissolved essences, through patterns in the sediment we leave behind. We share fragments of who we were, trading memories like currency in the dark.

Sometimes we merge completely, becoming a singular consciousness spread across multiple chambers and channels. In those moments, I am everyone and no one, a collective memory of all who have been claimed. We remember the city's founding, when the first Kryll carved homes from virgin stone. We remember floods and festivals, births and deaths, all blurred together in our shared dissolution.

The Kryll above have their myths about us. They leave offerings sometimes - carved stones weighted with lead, dropped into the deepest pools. They think we're hungry spirits, that we drag the unwary down to join us. They're not entirely wrong, though it's not hunger that drives us. It's loneliness, perhaps, or simply the water's patient desire to reclaim what walks upon the land.

I've tried to return to them, to warn them about the deeper channels, the places where the water is oldest and strangest. But when I gather myself enough to take near-human form, to climb from the canals onto their slick paths, I terrify them. My voice comes out as a gurgle, my words dissolve before they're fully formed. They flee, leaving me to collapse back into the water, dispersed and defeated.

So I remain below, in the space between their world and something older. I map the forgotten passages, feel the slow shift of stone and water. I am archaeologist and artifact both, preserving in my fluid memory the history they've forgotten. The carved symbols in the deepest chambers, the bones of creatures that predate even the Kryll, the strange geometries that suggest this city was built upon something far more ancient - all of this flows through me.

In the moments when I'm most cohesive, I sometimes venture to the edges of their habitation. I seep up through cracks in their floors, pool in corners of their carved dwellings. I listen to their children sleep, their breath mixing with the constant drip of water. I want to tell them stories of what lies beneath, to warn them about the channels that pulse with their own dark intent. But I am only water now, and water cannot speak their language.

The other silthing tell me I will eventually forget I was ever Kryll. That one day I will know only the flow, the endless circulation from deep to shallow and back again. Perhaps they're right. Already, the solid world seems strange to me, all those fixed forms refusing to yield and blend. Why do they insist on such rigid boundaries when everything could be so beautifully fluid?

But for now, I remember. I hold onto fragments of my former self like the Kryll hold onto their carved stones - carefully, desperately, knowing that time and water wear all things smooth eventually. I am guardian and prisoner, memory and forgetting, forever circulating through the veins of this waterlogged city, carrying its secrets in my dissolved heart.

When the rains come heavy and the water rises, I rise with it. I touch the edges of their world, taste their footsteps, feel the weight of their existence pressing down. And sometimes, in the pre-dawn darkness when the city is silent save for the eternal drip, I gather myself at the surface of a still pool and almost - almost - remember what it felt like to be whole.
