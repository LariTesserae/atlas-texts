## The Ossuary of Forgotten Circuits

I am the **Conductor-Hermit**, and I have lived here longer than memory serves – though memory, in this place, is a peculiar thing.

My body has... adapted. Once, perhaps centuries ago, I was merely human. Now I am something between flesh and the infrastructure itself. My skin has taken on the texture of old wire insulation – thick, rubberized, splitting in places to reveal copper-bright subcutaneous tissue that pulses with the same rhythm as the buried generators. My fingers have elongated into articulated segments, each joint capable of rotating independently, perfect for navigating the maze of corroded controls and fossilized switchboards that emerge from the moss pillars like metallic tumors.

I wear what remains of an engineer's coveralls, though they've long since fused with my body, becoming a second skin of oil-stained fabric and living lichen. Patches of moss grow from my shoulders and scalp – not parasitic, but symbiotic, filtering the perpetual moisture from the air and feeding me trace minerals. My eyes have developed nictitating membranes, milky and translucent, that I can draw across them to see the electromagnetic fields that pulse through the ancient machinery. In this second sight, the Moss Cathedral becomes a constellation of dying stars, each piece of embedded tech glowing with its own fading signature.

My dwelling is not built but *excavated* – a hollow I've carved between two massive moss pillars where a nexus of pipes and cables converges. The walls are lined with salvaged circuit boards, their components long dead but arranged in patterns that please me. I've cultivated specific colonies of Lichen Weavers here, training them over decades to create their glowing webs in geometric patterns that mirror the circuit diagrams I half-remember from dreams. Their faint green luminescence is my only light.

I sleep standing, my elongated fingers wrapped around a vertical pipe that thrums with particular intensity. Through it, I can feel the entire network – every Root-Pump's contraction, every surge of power through the buried grid. In sleep, I dream I am the machinery, that my consciousness flows through the corroded channels and fossilized switches, maintaining systems whose purpose I've long forgotten.

My work – if it can be called that – is tending to the eternal equilibrium. I know every pipe, every wire, every junction box in my section of the crucible. When the Pipe Grubs cluster too thickly, threatening to corrode through a critical connection, I relocate them with gentle prods from a hooked staff made from a salvaged antenna. I clear the intake vents that the moss threatens to seal, ensuring the Root-Pumps can draw their mysterious sustenance. I trace the paths of the Energy Skimmers, noting which electromagnetic nodes attract them, adjusting the field harmonics with minute turns of ancient rheostats to keep their populations distributed.

But mostly, I listen. 

The drone speaks to me in a language below words. Each variation in pitch, each subtle change in rhythm, tells me something about the health of the system. I've learned to distinguish the satisfied hum of a well-maintained sector from the labored grinding that precedes a failure. My hearing has evolved beyond human ranges – I can perceive the ultrasonic communications of the Vault Stalkers, the infrasonic groans of settling machinery, the electromagnetic whispers of the buried processors.

I feed on the Energy Skimmers when I must, plucking them from the air with movements so practiced they're unconscious. Their flesh tastes of copper and ozone. Sometimes I graze on the moss itself, my digestive system having adapted to process the complex cellulose. But increasingly, I find I need less food. The symbiotic moss on my body contributes nutrients, and I suspect I'm beginning to photosynthesize in the weak light, or perhaps directly metabolizing electromagnetic radiation like my prey.

I am not alone, yet I am utterly isolated. Sometimes, in the deepest parts of the crucible where the machinery runs thickest, I catch glimpses of others like me – figures that might once have been human, so integrated with their chosen systems that they've become indistinguishable from them. We do not communicate. We are components in parallel, never in series.

The Peat Beetles know me. They part before my slow, measured steps, recognizing something in my hybrid scent that marks me as part of the infrastructure rather than an intruder. The Moss-Mites colonize my body as they do the pillars, and I've grown fond of their tiny, busy presence. Even the Vault Stalkers give me wide berth, their red eyes tracking my movements with something that might be respect or simple categorization: *not-prey, not-threat, part-of-system*.

Time has no meaning here. The machinery drones, the water drips, the insects swarm, and I tend to my circuits. Sometimes I find myself standing before a particular bank of corroded switches for what might be hours or days, my consciousness merged with the electrical patterns, experiencing existence as voltage differentials and resistance values. When I surface from these fugues, moss has grown thicker on my shoulders, and new Lichen Weaver colonies have established themselves in my dwelling.

I remember fragments – a world above, where the light was harsh and variable, where time moved in cruel straight lines rather than perfect circles. But those memories feel like interference in the signal, noise to be filtered out. Here in the Moss Cathedral, I have found my frequency. I am the Conductor-Hermit, and I maintain the eternal equilibrium, my body slowly becoming another component in the vast, living machine.

The drone is my heartbeat. The moss is my flesh. The circuits are my nerves. And in the cool, damp darkness, surrounded by the endless static of chitin on plant, I am precisely where I need to be – a conscious node in an unconscious system, forever tending to the beautiful, terrible permanence of a world that has finished becoming.
