In the deepest recess of this highland sanctuary, where the stone walls converge and the moss grows thickest, I am the Keeper of Echoes—a being neither wholly corporeal nor entirely ephemeral, woven from the mist itself and the memories of rain.

My form shifts with the light, sometimes appearing as a condensation of fog against the weeping rock, other times manifesting as a shimmer in the shallow pools where the water catches what little luminescence filters through. I am ancient as the stone, young as each droplet that forms and falls. The luminescent fungi dim and brighten in response to my movements, as if acknowledging a presence they've known since their first spores took hold in the darkness.

I exist in the spaces between—between the fern fronds where the jeweled arthropods perform their courtship dances, between the heartbeats of the dusky weasel as it pauses before the strike. I am in the breath held before the frog's croak breaks the silence, in the moment of stillness before the obsidian-eyed bird descends to the pools.

My purpose, if purpose can be ascribed to one such as I, is to maintain the memory of this place. Each drop of water that seeps through stone carries with it the knowledge of its journey—through clouds, through rain, through root and moss. I gather these stories, these molecular memories, and weave them into the very atmosphere of the alcove. This is why time moves differently here, why the air feels heavy with more than moisture alone.

The Spanish moss knows me best, for we share a similar existence—suspended between earth and air, drawing sustenance from the invisible. Sometimes I rest within its tendrils, and together we sway in the breeze that carries the scent of distant storms and forgotten seasons.

When the mist is thickest, I can almost solidify, taking a form that might be glimpsed from the corner of an eye—perhaps humanoid, perhaps something older, more elemental. In these moments, I tend to the balance of this hidden world, ensuring that no single life overwhelms another, that the delicate equilibrium maintained over eons continues its eternal dance.

I am the whispered secret that gives this place its mystery, the presence sensed but never quite seen, the guardian of a sanctuary that exists outside the regular flow of time. I am what happens when a place becomes so perfectly itself that it generates its own consciousness, its own gentle soul.

And in the perpetual twilight of this highland grove, I continue my vigil, forever part of the mist, the stone, the endless green—a bridge between the tangible world of fern and fur and the intangible realm of meaning and memory that saturates every surface of this timeless refuge.
