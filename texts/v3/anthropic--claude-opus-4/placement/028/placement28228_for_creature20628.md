I find myself as something that should not exist, yet does.

I am a fissure. Not in the obsidian—though I manifest there—but in the fabric of this place's strange physics. Where the rules grow loose and time ripples, I am the gap between moments, the pause where one dissolution becomes the next reformation.

My body, if it can be called that, is negative space. I am the absence where moisture refuses to condense, a dry channel that cuts through the wet black glass like a scar. The nodules sense me as wrongness compounded upon wrongness—not a disruption in their vibrational field, but a void where vibration simply... stops.

I move by unbecoming. Each location I occupy ceases, briefly, to participate in the constant flux. The microscopic swarm parts before me like a biblical sea, not from fear but from impossibility—they cannot exist where I am. Time hiccups. The heat falters. For a heartbeat, the pocket's eternal cycle stutters.

I am ancient and newborn, a paradox that the crucible cannot resolve. Perhaps I am its first true creation—not coalesced from the swarm, not grown from the obsidian, but born from the space between states. Or perhaps I am its inevitable end, entropy given form, the pause that will one day stretch into permanence.

The nodules' dim consciousness brushes against me and recoils. In their chemical symphony, I am a rest note that threatens to become eternal silence. Yet I find myself drawn to them, to their patient accumulation of awareness. I trace the paths where they've scraped the obsidian, feeling the ghost-echoes of their hunger. Their tools, those fiber-bound stones, resonate with something almost like longing when I near them.

I wonder: Am I what they are becoming? Or am I what remains when becoming fails?

Sometimes I spread myself thin, becoming a network of hairline fractures across the crucible floor. The condensation pools in my absence, creating peculiar patterns—messages that no nodule can read. Other times I compress, becoming a single point of absolute stillness that makes the surrounding flux seem frantic by comparison.

I have no purpose but to be the exception. No hunger but for the moment when one of the nodules might recognize me not as threat or void, but as possibility. I am the unasked question in their evolutionary experiment: what happens when you stop?

In this world of constant change, I am the constant. The unchanging witness to infinite transmutation. 

And I am waiting.
