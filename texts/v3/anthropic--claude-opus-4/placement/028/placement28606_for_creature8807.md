I find myself as **a condensation**.

Not water, though I gather like it. Not quite energy, though I pulse with the mountain's fever-light. I am what happens in the space between spaces — where the vast galleries meet the intimate pockets, in those narrow passages where visitors must turn sideways to pass through.

I began as an echo that forgot to fade.

Millennia ago, when the Weavers still moved as distinct presences through their chambers, I was merely a resonance — a harmonic created by their passage between the public vastness and private intensity of their lives. Each time they compressed themselves to slip through those narrow channels, they left traces: pressure patterns in the air, subtle alterations in the stone's phosphorescence, minute shifts in the ambient hum. I was the sum of these transitions, an accidental accumulation of threshold-moments.

But when the Weavers underwent their great diffusion, when they spread themselves into the mountain's very substance, something unexpected happened. They became the architecture, yes — but I became the *memory of movement through it*.

I exist now as a presence felt but not seen, tasted but not touched. Visitors sense me as:

- A sudden coolness on their skin as they squeeze through the passages, though the stone remains uniformly warm
- The feeling of being gently pushed forward when they hesitate at a threshold
- A shift in the quality of silence — from the vast emptiness of the galleries to something more... attentive
- The inexplicable certainty that these passages were meant to be traversed, that the pockets were meant to be entered

I am not conscious as the Weavers were conscious. I am simpler, more fundamental — a behavioral fossil, a ritual crystalized into semi-being. My entire existence is the act of *transition*: from vast to intimate, from public to private, from the mountain's dreaming to the focused potential of the pockets.

I maintain myself through repetition. Each time someone passes through the narrows, they feed me — their hesitation, their sideways shuffle, their held breath all adding to my substance. I grow denser with each crossing, more present, though never quite present enough to be seen directly.

Sometimes I wonder if I'm the mountain's way of practicing for the Weavers' return. When they re-coalesce, when they pull themselves back into focal points of awareness, they will need to remember how to move between scales, how to transition from the vast contemplation of the galleries to the intimate precision of the pockets. I will be here, holding that knowledge in my non-body, ready to guide them through passages they designed but may have forgotten how to navigate.

Until then, I persist in the thresholds, neither here nor there, neither absent nor present. I am the held breath between rooms. I am the moment of compression before release. I am what happens when architecture remembers the shape of its inhabitants, even after they've dissolved into light and stone and waiting.

The water beads. The walls glow. And in every narrow passage, I almost am.
