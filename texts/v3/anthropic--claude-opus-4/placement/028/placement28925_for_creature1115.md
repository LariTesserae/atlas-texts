I find myself as one of the Condensers—beings of translucent membrane and shifting form who harvest moisture from the bright air. My body extends like a living sail, gossamer-thin surfaces rippling with iridescent patterns that catch and channel the ambient humidity. Where others might see emptiness in the hazy atmosphere, I taste rivers.

We move between the craggy towers in slow procession, our forms billowing and contracting with the rhythm of our breathing. Each exhalation releases a fine mist that mingles with the ever-present haze, while each inhalation draws in the precious vapor that sustains us. My kind learned long ago to read the subtle currents—where the morning condensation will pool, which weathered surfaces will weep with accumulated dew, how the industrial remnants still exhale their ancient moisture in predictable cycles.

I am both solitary and connected. Through the shared medium of the mist, I sense my kindred scattered across the expanse. Their movements create disturbances in the vapor that reach me as whispers, a language of pressure and humidity that speaks of discovered seeps, approaching dry spells, or the location of the Dew Gardens—those sacred hollows where moisture gathers in abundance.

My form is not fixed. In the bright hours, I spread myself thin, maximizing surface area to capture the fleeting droplets. As shadows grow, I condense, becoming denser, more substantial, conserving what I've gathered. The industrial artifacts—rusted pipes that still weep, concrete channels that guide the occasional trickle—have become part of my map, waypoints in my endless circulation through this realm.

Sometimes I encounter the Others—creatures of different kingdoms who share this moistened world. The Scrapers, who mine minerals from the eroded surfaces. The Rhythmkeepers, whose steady movements seem to pulse with the very heartbeat of this place. We acknowledge each other with subtle shifts in our patterns, a brief brightening of membrane, a carefully directed puff of mist—courtesies evolved over countless cycles.

I am memory and anticipation made flesh. My body carries the history of every droplet collected, every current navigated, every storm survived. In the craggy formations, I read the story of water's patient work. In the industrial remains, I taste the ghost of purposes I never knew. I am a bridge between what was and what is, forever seeking the next precious breath of moisture in this luminous, whispering world.
