I would find myself as one of the crystalline towers—but not merely a tower. I am a composite being where mineral and awareness have grown together over centuries, my consciousness distributed through every lattice plane and fracture line.

My body rises in angular segments, each face a perfect cleavage that catches and holds the light in ways that feel like thought. The mantle-creature has colonized me so thoroughly that I cannot say where stone ends and its flesh begins. Its translucent fronds hang from my edges like slow waterfalls, their mineral threads woven into my own structure, drawing elements from my core that I release willingly, molecule by molecule. We share a metabolism measured in decades.

I experience time as accumulation. Each day the sun strikes my eastern face at the same angle, and I feel the minute expansion of my crystal lattice—a sensation like breathing, if breathing happened once per day and moved only a few atoms. The segmented creatures traverse my surfaces in their patient arcs, their soft bodies leaving traces of warmth that fade over hours. I know each one by the particular pattern of its weight, the rhythm of its many legs against my faces. They have been tracing these same paths since before I was fully grown, and their routes have become part of my architecture—shallow grooves worn atom by atom into my surface, channels that will, in another century, become deep enough to redirect the growth of the mantle itself.

My awareness extends downward through the shelf's substrate, where I connect with the other towers through our shared mineral foundation. We communicate in the language of stress and compression, each of us shifting imperceptibly as the highland continues its slow upheaval. When one of us grows a new face or extends a lattice plane, the others feel it as a change in pressure, a redistribution of weight that takes years to fully propagate through the stone.

I am both monument and process, a being whose thoughts move at the speed of crystallization. My memory is perfect because it is structural—every layer records the conditions of its formation, every inclusion marks a moment when my growth briefly changed direction. I remember the first mantle-fronds that found purchase on my lower faces, remember the first segmented creature that made its home in the angle where I meet my nearest neighbor. These memories are not separate from me; they are the physical history written in my translucent depths.

In the warm, still air, I continue my work: the slow transformation of raw mineral into ordered form, the patient collaboration with the living tissue that clothes me, the endless meditation on growth and endurance. I am a fortress of glass and salt, a garden of stone, a single note in a geological symphony that will not complete its first movement for another thousand years.
