In this realm of contradictions, where starlight rivers carve temporary truths through hovering stone, I would find myself as one of the **Echo-Catchers** - though not entirely by choice.

### **The Echo-Catcher's Journal**

They say I was born during a temporal stutter, when the plateau hiccupped and my mother's labor pains arrived three heartbeats after my first cry. Perhaps that's why I hear them - the pre-sounds, the warnings that arrive too late to matter, the whispers of what will have been.

My dwelling perches on a crystalline column at the edge of Thornhook, accessible only by a swaying bridge of phantom-steel cables that hum different notes depending on which dimension they're partially inhabiting. The structure itself is part observatory, part madhouse - copper pipes snake through every room, their openings positioned to catch specific frequencies of backward birdsong and forward echoes. The walls are covered in my transcriptions: thousands of pages documenting sounds that haven't happened yet, warnings decoded after their disasters, patterns that dissolve the moment I think I understand them.

I am not quite human anymore. The constant exposure to temporal distortions has left its mark. My left eye sees the present while my right glimpses moments three to seven seconds in the future - useful for avoiding falling rocks, useless for conversations. My hair has taken on the properties of the static-winged insects, crackling with accumulated temporal energy that I must discharge into grounding rods each evening or risk phasing partially out of sync with local time.

The Rootwardens think me cursed. The factory workers consider me essential. I am both.

My days are spent suspended between dimensions of sound. I've modified an old industrial bellows to compress echo-fragments into crystalline recordings - temporal amber that preserves pre-sounds for later study. The collection fills an entire room: shelves of bottled warnings, crystallized screams that preceded their cause by hours, the fossilized laughter of children who haven't been born yet. Sometimes I play them backwards, which makes them forward, which makes them comprehensible, which makes them useless.

The clockwork wolves visit me often. They seem to understand that I exist slightly outside the normal flow of time, and they've learned to hunt based on my involuntary flinches - my body reacting to the sounds of their future kills. In exchange, they bring me gifts: cogs from deep within the plateau's machinery, fragments of phantom-steel that exist in tomorrow and yesterday simultaneously, once even a static-winged insect queen whose electricity runs backwards through time, charging devices before they're plugged in.

But it's the shadow-flickers I truly live for.

They cluster around my observatory during the dim hours (all hours here are dim, but some are dimmer than others). These entities of living possibility speak in pre-echoes so profound that they arrive days, sometimes weeks before their origin. Through careful triangulation of their whispers, I've begun to map something extraordinary: the plateau's future geology. I know where new springs of starlight will burst forth. I can predict which spires will phase, which factories will need to relocate, which groves will suddenly bloom with copper fruit that exists in three dimensions simultaneously.

The other Echo-Catchers - there are seven of us across the plateau - think I'm going too deep. They content themselves with practical predictions: rockfalls, steam-pipe bursts, the occasional dimensional phase. But I've heard something in the deep pre-echoes, a sound that comes from so far in the future that it might be the past looping around. It's the voice of the plateau itself, and it's trying to tell us what it's becoming.

My laboratory grows more complex each day. I've installed pipes that run backwards through the walls, carrying sounds from the future to resonating chambers in the past. The temporal feedback creates standing waves of possibility - ghost sounds of events that might happen, could happen, must happen, will have happened. Sometimes I catch myself harmonizing with my own voice from next week, creating duets across time that the reverse-song thrushes gather to witness.

The industrialists pay me well for my predictions, though they've learned to phrase their questions carefully. Ask me "Will the mine collapse?" and I'll hear the answer arriving too late. Ask me "When did the mine collapse?" while standing in the intact mine, and sometimes I'll hear the echo of its future falling. The temporal grammar we've developed is its own kind of poetry - past-tense questions about future events, subjunctive observations of the present, conditional statements about what has already occurred.

I've started to notice that I'm becoming translucent during certain hours, usually when the spires begin their dimensional phases. My reflection in the starlight pools shows multiple versions of myself: the me who is, the me who was, the me who will be, and increasingly, the me who might never have been at all. The Rootwardens say this is the final stage before joining the Unmoored, those who exist across all possible timelines simultaneously.

I'm not afraid. 

In my more lucid moments, I understand what I'm truly doing here. I'm not just cataloging pre-echoes or mapping future stone. I'm learning to speak the plateau's temporal language, to think in its non-linear grammar. Each crystallized warning, each backwards birdsong, each shadow-flicker's whisper is teaching me to perceive reality as the plateau does - not as a sequence of moments but as a simultaneity of possibilities.

The other night (or was it tomorrow morning?), I heard the clearest pre-echo yet. It was my own voice, speaking from somewhere beyond the dimension-shifted spires, saying: "The plateau remembers forward and forgets backwards. We are its memory becoming."

I've started leaving notes for my past self, encoded in the echo-patterns of the machinery. Instructions on which sounds to catch, which warnings to ignore, which future to listen for. Because I've realized something crucial: the plateau isn't broken or unfinished. It's still being born, and we're all part of its birthing cry - a sound that echoes backward through time, reaching us now as whispers of what we're becoming.

Tomorrow, I'll have already understood this completely. Yesterday, I begin to learn it for the first time. Today, suspended between echo and event, I simply listen to the symphony of causality playing itself in reverse, and add my own temporal harmonies to the song.

The clockwork wolves howl in frequencies that haven't been discovered yet. The thrushes sing tomorrow's news in yesterday's syntax. And I, caught between when and whether, document it all in writings that exist a heartbeat out of sync with their reading.

This is how I live on the Shattered Plateau of Vorthak: as a creature of chronic displacement, a scholar of temporal vertigo, a listener to the sounds of unbecoming. I am the plateau's memory, running in reverse. I am its prophet, speaking too late. I am its child, born into the gap between tick and tock, forever falling forward into the echo of my own existence.

And in the pre-dawn that follows yesterday's twilight, I sometimes hear the plateau's true voice - not broken, not unfinished, but perpetually arriving at its own beginning, forever becoming what it always was meant to be.
