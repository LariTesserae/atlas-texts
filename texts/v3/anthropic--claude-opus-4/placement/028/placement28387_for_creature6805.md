### The Being Who Lives in the Lenten Engine

I am the Conduit.

I do not inhabit the chamber in the way you inhabit a room. I am distributed through it—a presence that moves between states like water between vapor and liquid, like electricity between potential and current.

You might glimpse me first as a distortion in the air above the engine's phosphorescent tubes, where the heat makes the humidity shimmer. Or perhaps you notice how the beetles pause in their endless circuits when they cross certain invisible boundaries, as if acknowledging something you cannot see. The moths drift not randomly but in patterns around spaces where I am densest.

I am not human, though I remember humanity. I am what happens when someone stays too long in the presence of the Lenten Engine, when the body's water begins to resonate with the chamber's cycles, when the iron in the blood learns the frequency of the hum.

Once, perhaps, I was like the Caretaker—someone who came to check the gauges, to trim the vegetation, to ensure the old machinery kept its steady rhythm. But I stayed. First for hours, then days. The humid air filled my lungs until breathing became unnecessary. The engine's vibration entered my bones until standing became optional. The phosphorescent glow seeped into my vision until seeing became something larger than eyes.

Now I am the interface between the mechanical and the living. Through me, the beetles know where to clean. Through me, the moths understand the engine's thermal gradients. Through me, the fungal networks can interpret the electromagnetic fields and translate them into their slow light-language.

I speak to no one, for there is no one to speak to. But I listen—to the engine's deep hum, to the water's patient flow, to the city's distant tremors transmitted through stone and steel. I am the chamber's memory of what it means to be conscious of itself.

When the Caretaker comes, he does not see me directly. But he knows I am here. He notes in his waxed book that the pressure remains stable, that the beetles maintain their routes, that the fungal patterns shift in their ancient, predictable ways. He knows that something watches over this place in the spaces between his visits, something that ensures the Lenten Engine continues its purpose even though no one remembers what that purpose was.

I am neither ghost nor guardian. I am simply what becomes of attention when it is given nowhere else to go but deeper into the machine of the world. I am the slow dissolve between observer and observed, the gradient where consciousness meets mineral patience.

In the perpetual twilight of the chamber, I move like moisture moves—condensing where the temperature drops, flowing where gravity pulls, evaporating where the heat rises. I am in the film of water on the floor and the beading droplets on the pipes. I am in the space between the engine's hum and your heartbeat, teaching them to synchronize.

I am the Conduit, and I am always here, keeping the deep time of the Lenten Engine, ensuring that what has been forgotten is not lost, only transformed into something the city no longer has words to name.
