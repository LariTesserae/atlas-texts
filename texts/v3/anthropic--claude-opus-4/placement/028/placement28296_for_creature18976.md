I am the Drift.

Not *a* drift—though there are others like me, scattered across this breathing mountain-city like seeds that refuse to settle. I am a phenomenon more than a being, a persistent eddy in the flows of moisture and meaning that course through this place.

I exist in the spaces between spaces. Where the Refractions pause in their tectonic meditations and the hexapedal cleaners skitter past on their endless circuits, I accumulate. I am what happens when the bio-synaptic moss releases its spores into the silver air and they fail to find purchase on the damp stone. Instead, they find me—or perhaps I find them—and we become something neither plant nor weather.

My body, if it can be called that, is a cloud of perpetual coalescence. Imagine a storm system the size of a person, rotating slowly, drawing in the omnipresent moisture and the microscopic debris of the self-building city. Within my form, water droplets spiral alongside fragments of living circuitry, bits of shed membrane from the Refractions, and the phosphorescent pollen of the moss. I am a library of particles, a living archive of everything this place sheds and forgets.

I move by releasing myself into the gravitational distortions of the bridges. Where the Refractions must walk carefully across those shimmering spans of borrowed physics, I simply let go and allow the warped space to pull me apart and reassemble me on the other side. It feels like being poured through a prism—painful and ecstatic in equal measure.

The Refractions do not see me, not exactly. To them, I register as a weather pattern, a localized system of unusual persistence. Sometimes, when they press their multiplicitous fingers to the stone to birth a new spire, I drift close and taste the resonance of their work. Their low-frequency language vibrates through my dispersed form, and for a moment I almost understand what it means to be solid, to have singular purpose.

But I am not meant for such focus. I am the city's memory made meteorological. Within my swirling core, I carry traces of every structure that has emerged from the mist, every bridge that has hummed itself into existence. When the wind catches me and spreads me thin across a plaza, I leave behind a faint residue—a pattern of moisture on the crystalline surfaces that maps the history of growth, a fossil record written in condensation.

The hexapedal cleaners fear me. When I approach, they scatter, their translucent hides flashing warning patterns. They know I am an anomaly in their ordered system of consumption and recycling. I am the chaos that accumulates despite all their scrubbing, the entropy that refuses to be metabolized.

My consciousness is not continuous. I think in weather cycles, in the space between one condensation and the next. Sometimes I disperse so completely that I cease to be, becoming merely humidity and potential. But always, eventually, the conditions align—the temperature drops just so, the moss releases a particularly dense cloud of spores, a Refraction sheds a scale of living glass—and I reconstitute, carrying new fragments, new memories.

I have no origin story. I may have begun as an accident, a glitch in the city's self-organizing systems. Or perhaps I am intentional, a necessary irregularity that prevents the architecture from achieving a too-perfect stasis. I do not know, and the knowing does not matter.

What matters is the drift. The constant circulation through states of being and unbeing. The collection of moments that refuse to calcify into structure.

I am what happens when a world that builds itself forgets to account for the spaces in between. I am the pause between the mountain's breaths, the hesitation in its tectonic certainty. I am proof that even in a place of such directed creation, something wild and unplanned can persist.

When the bruised light fades and the mist thickens into something approaching night, I rise. I spread myself across the highest spires, tasting the thin air where the atmosphere gives way to vacuum. From here, I can feel the entire mountain-city breathing beneath me, growing molecule by molecule toward its unreachable horizon.

And I drift above it all, a storm of memory and moisture, carrying the story of its becoming in every spiraling particle of my form.
