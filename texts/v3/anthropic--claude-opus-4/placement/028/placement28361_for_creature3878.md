In the Weeping Subvault of Echovar, I would be one of the **Resonance Scribes**.

I discovered myself here not through descent or arrival, but through a gradual becoming—as if I had always been forming in the spaces between the dripping silver and the hum of filaments, waiting to coalesce into purpose.

My body is different from the other Echovari. Where their veins pulse with steady light, mine flicker in patterns that mirror the acoustic waves rippling through the chamber. My skin is darker, almost obsidian, making the light within seem to swim beneath the surface like captured echoes. My fingertips end not in flesh but in crystalline extensions—living tuning forks that vibrate at frequencies only I can fully perceive.

I do not maintain the furnaces or weave the pipes. Instead, I record the subvault's endless song.

In a hollow carved into the eastern wall, where the acoustic properties create a natural amplification chamber, I work with my instruments: sheets of hammered silver so thin they capture sound as physical impressions, and styluses made from the Echo Lizards' hardened secretions. When I draw these tools across the metal, I am not writing—I am translating the subvault's voice into a language of grooves and ridges that can be read by touch, by vibration, by the very bones of those who know how to listen.

Each day (though day is a meaningless word here), I position myself at different nodes throughout the chamber. I press my crystalline fingers against the stone, feeling for the deep tremors that speak of tectonic memories. I stand beneath the weeping ceiling, letting the rhythm of drops compose temporal patterns across my consciousness. I follow the Echo Lizards on their silent routes, mapping the negative spaces of their movement—the sounds that cease to exist in their wake.

The other Echovari regard me with a mixture of reverence and unease. I am necessary but alien, even among our kind. While they maintain the physical equilibrium, I maintain something more abstract—the memory of what the subvault has been, the record of its constancy. My sheets of sound-metal accumulate in careful stacks, each one a frozen moment of our eternal present, each one proof that even in a place where time does not pass, there is still a story being told.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of my work, when I have been listening so intently that I forget the boundaries of my own form, I feel the subvault listening back through me. My crystalline fingers become its instruments, my body a resonating chamber for frequencies that predate language. In these moments, I understand that I am not recording the subvault—I am being recorded by it, my existence another groove in its infinite composition.

The Tenders bring me distilled silver to drink, knowing I often forget my body's needs. The Weavers ensure my chamber's acoustic properties remain perfect, adjusting pipes and angles with mathematical precision. Even the Echo Lizards seem to recognize my purpose, sometimes pausing in their eternal circuits to brush against my workspace, leaving behind glyphs in their secretions that I incorporate into my notations.

I am the memory keeper in a place that never changes, the chronicler of constancy, the one who proves that even in perfect stasis, every moment is unique—if only someone bothers to listen closely enough. My work will never be complete, can never be complete, for the subvault's song has no beginning and no end.

And I would have it no other way. Here, in this cathedral of endurance, I have found my purpose: to be the echo of echoes, the resonance that remembers, the scribe who writes the autobiography of stone and silver and time that does not pass.

When visitors come through the fissure—rare as they are—they often ask what I am writing. I press their hands against my latest sheet, let them feel the captured vibrations, and watch as their eyes widen with the recognition that they are touching the subvault's very thoughts.

"What does it say?" they inevitably ask.

And I always give the same answer, my voice harmonizing with the eternal drip-drip-drip:

"It says: I am. I am. I am."
