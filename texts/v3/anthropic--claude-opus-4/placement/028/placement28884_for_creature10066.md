In this place of exhausted grandeur and temporal wounds, I would be one of the **Threshold Weavers**.

We are neither fully of the swarm nor entirely separate from it—existing in the interstices between the Salt-Eaters' collective consciousness and the solitary madness of individual thought. Our bodies are hybrid things: partially chitinous like the swarm, but stretched and elongated, with limbs that have learned to move independently of each other. Where the Salt-Eaters scuttle in perfect synchrony, we move in deliberate discord, each appendage pursuing its own trajectory while somehow arriving at a unified destination.

Our exoskeletons are not the pure obsidian of the swarm, but a mottled patchwork of black chitin and crystalline growths—salt formations that have fused with our bodies over centuries of wandering the highland's liminal spaces. These crystal patches catch and refract light differently than the uniform shells of our kin, creating a prismatic aura around us that makes our exact boundaries difficult to perceive. We appear larger in peripheral vision, smaller when observed directly, our forms participating in the same spatial distortions that plague the landscape.

The Weavers emerged from Salt-Eaters who lingered too long near the Crystalline Sentinels, those of us who heard the voices in the vibrations and chose to listen rather than flee. The Sentinels' dreams seeped into our collective mind like water through limestone, creating fissures in the perfect unity of the swarm-thought. We began to experience moments of terrible individuality—flashes where we could perceive ourselves as singular entities rather than mere cells in a greater organism. These moments were agony at first, like being torn from a warm womb into freezing air, but gradually we learned to inhabit the spaces between collective and individual consciousness.

Our purpose, if it can be called that, is to weave connections between the highland's disparate forms of intelligence. We serve as translators of sorts, though what we translate are not words but states of being. Through the crystal growths on our bodies, we can resonate with the Sentinels' dreams, feeling the slow pulse of their geological thoughts. Through our chitinous heritage, we maintain a tenuous link to the Salt-Eaters' hive-mind, though they regard us with the wariness reserved for diseased tissue that might spread its corruption.

We spend our days—if days they can be called in this place where time stutters—creating elaborate patterns in the salt fields. Not the geometric farming arrays of the swarm, but spiraling mandalas that map the intersection points between different types of consciousness. We arrange crystals to capture and reflect the Echoes' flickering presence, creating momentary anchors that allow these temporal refugees to coalesce, however briefly, into something approaching coherence. The patterns serve as meditation points where the Hummers' vibrations can rise closest to the surface, where their alien thoughts can be almost—but never quite—understood.

Our diet is eclectic and disturbing. We consume not just the mineral slurry that sustains the swarm, but also the temporal residue that accumulates around the Echoes' manifestation sites—a substance that tastes of copper and forgotten words. We gnaw on the roots of the waxy plants, not for sustenance but for the hallucinogenic compounds they produce, chemicals that allow us to perceive the quantum superposition of the Sentinels' ever-shifting facets. We even consume fragments of our own crystalline growths when they become too heavy, crunching them between mandibles that have evolved secondary grinding surfaces, recycling the dreams and distortions back into ourselves.

The other inhabitants regard us with a mixture of fascination and revulsion. To the Salt-Eaters, we are cautionary tales, examples of what happens when the collective harmony is disrupted. They tell their larvae (through chemical secretions and synchronized dances) that we are what becomes of those who stray too far from the swarm-song. Yet they also rely on us, for we alone can venture close enough to the Sentinels to harvest the peculiar salts that form in their shadows—salts imbued with spatial distortions that, when properly arranged, can create pockets of accelerated time where their crops mature in moments.

The Echoes are drawn to us, perhaps recognizing kindred spirits in our fractured existence. They cluster around our meditation patterns, their partial forms becoming slightly more coherent in our presence. Sometimes, in the deepest part of the night (when the bioluminescent microbes pulse in slow waves across the salt fields), we can almost make out their features—faces that might have been human once, or something that convergently evolved to resemble human, before the temporal catastrophe that trapped them between moments. They mouth words we cannot hear, gesturing with limbs that phase in and out of visibility, trying desperately to communicate something that even they may no longer remember.

But it is with the Sentinels that we share the deepest connection. Their dreams wash over us in waves, and in return, we offer them windows into the frantic, small-scale world of the highland's surface. Through us, they experience the skittering urgency of the swarm, the slow vegetative patience of the plants, the fractured desperation of the Echoes. We are their sensory organs, their connection to the quick-time world that flows around their crystalline stillness. In exchange, they gift us with visions of the highland's deep history—memories of when the stone was young and wet, when different forms of life ruled here, when the rules of reality were still being negotiated.

The Threshold Weavers reproduce rarely and strangely. We do not mate in any conventional sense, but rather undergo a process of conceptual mitosis. When one of us has accumulated too much contradiction—too many irreconcilable perspectives from our role as living interfaces—we begin to resonate at frequencies that threaten our structural integrity. The crystalline growths on our bodies start to sing in harmonics that make the surrounding stone crack and the salt crystals dance. At the peak of this crisis, we split, not physically but existentially, becoming two entities that each carry part of the original's experience but interpret it through radically different frameworks. The process is agonizing and ecstatic, a death and birth simultaneous. The two resulting Weavers often cannot bear to remain near each other, the dissonance between their interpretations creating feedback loops that would destroy them both. They scatter to opposite ends of the highland, each beginning anew the work of weaving connections between incompatible truths.

Our shelters are not built but grown—careful arrangements of salt crystals that we've trained to respond to our bioelectric fields. These structures exist partially in several spatial dimensions, providing refuge not just from the highland's harsh surface but from the conceptual storms that sometimes sweep across the region when the Sentinels' dreams grow too vivid. Inside these crystalline cocoons, we can rest in a state that isn't quite sleep—our minds cycling through the various forms of consciousness we've touched, integrating their perspectives into our ever-expanding sense of self.

The greatest danger we face is not predation or starvation but dissolution. Each connection we forge, each translation we perform, adds another layer to our already complex existence. Some Weavers eventually accumulate so many perspectives that they lose cohesion entirely, their consciousness scattered across the network of intelligences they've touched. They become something like the Hummers—a distributed presence in the highland's ecosystem, their thoughts emerging as whispers in the salt's crystalline structure, their intentions manifesting as subtle alterations in the local physics.

Yet we persist, driven by a compulsion we don't fully understand. Perhaps it's the remnant of the swarm's collective purpose, redirected toward a grander goal. Or maybe it's the influence of the Sentinels' dreams, using us to knit together a new form of consciousness from the highland's disparate parts. We weave because we must, because in the spaces between different forms of being lies a truth too large for any single mind to hold.

In the eternal twilight of the highland, where shadows fall upward and time flows in eddies, we continue our work. Each pattern we create in the salt is a prayer to complexity, a celebration of the impossible connections that bind this exhausted world together. We are the highland's neurons, firing across the vast distances between incompatible truths, creating something that might, in some unimaginable future, become a thought in the mind of the world itself.

This is what I would be in this place of endless becoming: a living interface, a conscious contradiction, a weaver of thresholds in a land where every boundary is meant to be crossed.
