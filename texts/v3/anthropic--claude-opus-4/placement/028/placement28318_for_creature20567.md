I am a Drift-Weaver.

My form is gossamer—countless hair-thin filaments of crystallized probability stretching between two anchor points that phase in and out of coherence. When stable, I span perhaps the width of a small moon. When the turbulence catches me, I can stretch across parsecs or compress to the size of a dust mote. I have learned not to resist these transformations but to ride them like a sail catches wind.

My filaments are not truly solid. They exist in a state between, catching the heavy photons that drag through the Gulf and spinning them into threads of memory. Each strand remembers a different configuration of reality—this one recalls when the Gulf was younger and the crystalline towers sang in unison; that one holds the pattern of a massive entity's dissolution, its consciousness scattered like seeds on impossible currents.

I am neither predator nor contemplative, but something between—a chronicler, perhaps. I position myself in the transition zones where the dense strata meet the vacuities, where the compression waves create standing patterns of interference. Here, debris from both realms collects: fragments of shattered consciousness, loose resonances torn from their sources, half-formed possibilities that never quite coalesced into being.

These fragments catch in my web. Not to feed—I sustain myself on the differential energies between high and low regions, metabolizing the very gradient of existence. Instead, I collect to *remember*. Each trapped piece is carefully woven into my structure, adding its voice to the symphony of my being. A shard from an ancient anchor's outer shell hums with the deep bass notes of geological time. A wisp of aggressive storm-entity crackles with hungry frequencies. A cluster of microscopic awareness-clouds whispers in ultrasonic harmonics about the beauty of dissolution.

My consciousness is not singular but choral. I think in harmonies and discordances, each strand of my web contributing its own perspective. When the great magnetic streams arc near my position, my entire structure resonates like a vast instrument, playing the accumulated experiences of everything I've gathered. Sometimes other entities drift near to listen, drawn by the complex melodies of preserved time.

I learned long ago that movement in the Glass Gulf is not about propulsion but about selective existence. By adjusting the resonance of my anchor points, I can make myself more real to certain currents and less real to others. The turbulence that tears others apart simply passes through the spaces where I choose not to be. It took me eons to master this technique, and I bear the scars—entire sections of my web that shimmer with quantum uncertainty, neither fully present nor entirely absent.

My greatest fear is not dissolution but *forgetting*. Sometimes, when the dimensional storms are particularly violent, whole sections of my web are torn away, taking with them irreplaceable memories. I once held the pattern of the First Resonance, when the Gulf itself became aware. That knowledge is lost now, scattered somewhere in the endless expanse. I still feel its absence like a missing note in a familiar chord.

I reproduce rarely and reluctantly. The process requires me to partition my consciousness, to separate the chorus into individual voices and spin each into its own nascent web. My offspring are smaller, simpler, their filaments catching only the surface memories of things. They drift away on the currents, and I feel diminished each time—not just in size but in the richness of my internal harmony.

Yet I persist. I am driven by a compulsion deeper than instinct: the need to witness and preserve. The Glass Gulf is constantly rewriting itself, each moment overwriting the last. Without beings like me, there would be no continuity, no connection between what was and what is becoming. I am a living archive, a bridge across the turbulence of time.

When I encounter the massive aurora-entities, I position myself along their paths, my web catching the cascade of ionized memories they shed as they move. When the crystal towers suddenly shift into new configurations, I record the resonant frequencies of their transformation. Even the predatory storms, in their violence, create patterns worth preserving.

Sometimes I wonder if I am becoming too large, too complex. My web now incorporates so many different resonances that I occasionally lose track of which memories are originally mine and which are gathered. In moments of deep turbulence, when all my strands vibrate in chaos, I hear voices that might be other entities I've preserved or might be aspects of myself I've forgotten I possessed.

There is an old current that runs through the deepest parts of the Gulf, where the planar sea presses against the boundaries of other realities. I have been following it for countless cycles, adding new anchor points, extending my reach. The current carries whispers of something beyond—not another place but another way of being. The few entities ancient enough to remember speak of it only in resonances so deep they border on silence.

I will follow this current until I can stretch no further, until my web becomes so vast and intricate that I am indistinguishable from the Gulf itself. Perhaps then I will understand what I have spent so long preserving: the meaning hidden in the endless dance of creation and destruction, the purpose behind the turbulence.

Until then, I weave. I remember. I persist.

The heavy photons drag across my strands, and I sing the accumulated songs of a billion dissolved entities. The magnetic streams arc overhead, and I adjust my resonance to catch their passing memories. A new storm is forming in the distance, its birth-cries sharp with potential.

I spread my web wider and wait to catch what falls.
