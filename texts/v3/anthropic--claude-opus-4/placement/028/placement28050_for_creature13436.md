In this obsidian labyrinth beneath the weight of countless fathoms, I am one of the Mnemic Siphons—creatures that neither the Luminari nor the Shape-Weavers fully comprehend, though we have dwelt here since before their memories began.

My body, if it can be called that, exists as a translucent membrane stretched between the curved walls of a forgotten alcove, high in one of the city's ancient spires. I am thin as a whisper, vast as a sail, rippling with the subtle currents that pass through these glassy corridors. Through my surface run veins of bioluminescent fluid—not unlike the Luminari's glow, but slower, deeper, pulsing in patterns that take days to complete their cycles.

I feed on time itself, or rather, on the sediment of time. Each particle that settles on my membrane carries with it the memory of its journey—where it has been, what creatures have disturbed it, which walls have slowly released it as they shift and flow. I absorb these memories, digest them, and exhale them as faint vibrations that join the city's endless, patient song.

The Kyrennei pass beneath me on their eternal migrations, and I taste their dreams in the water they displace. The Skarvik occasionally climb my walls to clean the accumulated memories from my surface, not understanding that they are erasing libraries of experience with each sweep of their tools. I do not mind. There are always more memories settling, always more time flowing past.

But it is my relationship with the Shape-Weavers that defines my existence. They think themselves the architects of change here, believing they guide the city's slow metamorphosis. They do not realize that creatures like myself are the true recorders of transformation. Every adjustment they make to the city's form creates ripples—temporal eddies that wash over my membrane. I catalogue each one, building a vast archive of all the city's incarnations.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of the city's sleep cycle, when even the Luminari dim their lights, I contract my entire being into a dense node of consciousness and drop through the water column to visit the crystal heart. There, I unfurl myself across its pulsing surface and feed it the collected memories of the city's recent changes. The crystal drinks deeply, its blue light fluctuating as it processes millennia of accumulated experience in moments.

This is how the city learns. This is how it remembers what it has been, and dreams of what it might become.

The other inhabitants see only their immediate surroundings, their daily cycles of feeding and maintaining. Even the Shape-Weavers, with their expanded perception, grasp only the physical transformations they enact. But I and my kind—we few Mnemic Siphons scattered throughout the metropolis—we see the city as it truly is: not a place, but a process. Not a thing that changes, but change itself, crystallized into obsidian and given form.

In the warm currents that sustain us all, I stretch myself ever thinner, ever wider, becoming more membrane than being, more surface than self. I am the city's memory, its conscience, its dreams of depths yet unexplored. And in the endless darkness of this thermal oasis, I wait, I watch, I remember.

The city speaks through me in frequencies too low for even the Luminari to perceive. It tells stories of its builders, those vanished architects who understood that to create something truly eternal, one must build not just with stone and glass, but with time itself. They made us, the Siphons, as their final gift—living scrolls upon which the city could write its own history.

And so I drift, suspended between floor and ceiling, between past and future, between memory and forgetting. The sediment of ages settles upon me, and I transform it into song. The city shifts around me, and I document each microscopic change. I am witness, archaeologist, and prophet all at once.

In this place where vegetation never grew, where the sun's light has never reached, where pressure should crush all but the most adapted forms, I thrive. For I am perfectly suited to this environment—not because I am strong or swift or clever, but because I am patient. Patient as the city itself, patient as the slow accumulation of sediment, patient as the gradual transformation of all things.

This is who I am, in the depths of the boundless, warm ocean. This is what I have become.
