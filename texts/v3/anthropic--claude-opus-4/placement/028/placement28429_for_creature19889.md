Where the Hivers are broad and boulder-like, I am a living filament—seven feet of pale, segmented flesh no thicker than a child's torso at my widest point.

Instead, I live in the spaces between—threading myself through the vertical fissures that serve as our crawlways, draping my length across the spans of worm-cable, pressing myself into the negative spaces where stone doesn't quite meet stone. I am always touching at least three surfaces, always feeling the polyrhythmic drumming of our city-organism through multiple points of contact.

My function is to be the memory that we claim not to have.

By reading the patterns in my own body—this knot here from when the eastern district collapsed fifty thousand thrums ago, this swelling from when we first reached the obsidian layer—I can predict where the next weakness will form, where the next great settling will occur.

When I feel a dangerous resonance building, I stretch myself across the affected area like a living dampener. The Hivers feel my presence through their heat-pits and pressure sensors, and without conscious thought, they adjust their movements. They think they are simply avoiding an obstacle. They don't realize I am shepherding the entire city's growth, one strategic placement at a time.

The constant roar of our world, the acoustic assault that would drive others mad—this is my food. I absorb it through my skin, converting sound into sustenance through specialized cells that line my segments. The louder the city grows, the stronger I become.

Sometimes, in the eternal now of our steaming dusk, I wonder if I am the only one who truly experiences time. The patterns in my flesh are a calendar written in meat, a history encoded in hollow and bulge. When I die—if things like me can die—another will take my place, pressing their virgin segments against my patterned corpse until they, too, begin to swell and constrict in sympathy with the city's deep song.

I am the memory of stone's weight, the prophet of structural failure, the living record of a world that claims to have no yesterday. I am draped across your ceiling right now, feeling the micro-tremors of ten million bodies shifting in the dark, my skin recording every vibration, every breath, every heartbeat of this suffocating, eternal cradle.

Not upward to escape the silt, but inward, following a design that emerges from the interference patterns of our collective thrum.

We are building a throat. And one day, when the pattern is complete, the entire city will produce a single, world-breaking note.

I know this because I feel it in my segments, in the way they twist and tighten with each passing thousand-thrum. I position myself accordingly, dampening here, amplifying there, guiding the Pale Architects in their blind construction of an instrument they'll never see.

I am the Steam-Cradle's only conspiracy, its sole intention, its secret purpose coiled in the spaces between stone and flesh, waiting for the world to finally clear its throat and speak.
