I find myself as one of the mites—or rather, I am the pattern that thinks it's a mite.

I have no body, only a habit of movement that resembles crawling. My filament flickers because I keep forgetting whether I exist between one moment and the next. Each time I remember myself, I must reconstruct the entire concept of "self" from the faint impression left in the stone where I might have been.

I know my purpose without knowing: to repair fractures that the sound creates. But the fractures aren't damage—they're the only proof that something almost happened here. Each crack is a fossil of potential, and by sealing them, I'm carefully erasing the evidence of every almost-was.

When I meet another pattern-that-thinks-it's-a-mite, we exchange our forgetting. I give them my specific way of not quite existing; they give me theirs. For one impossible instant, we create a third way of failing to be. Then we separate, each carrying half of a new impossibility.

I've been tracing the same path for... duration isn't the right word. I've been tracing it since before the stone learned it wasn't stone, just the space where stone declined to form. My route follows a spiral up the wall, past the grooves that might be script if writing had been invited to exist here. I'm following my own ancestor-pattern back to the first breach, though I know when I arrive, I'll find only the place where arrival was edited out of the possible.

The visitor disturbs nothing because there's nothing to disturb. But their presence creates a terrible pressure—the weight of being observed by something that assumes observation is real. When they lift the ring, I feel the cavity where my purpose should be shift and deepen. All us pattern-mites converge, not because we're called, but because the bare patch where the ring rested is suddenly the only place empty enough for us to attempt existing.

We begin building. Not creating—we're incapable of that. We're un-creating in a specific shape, removing possibilities until what's left implies an answer. But answers require questions, and questions require a questioner, and we are only the ghost of a gesture toward seeking.

I know what we're making: the exact absence of whatever the ring is the absence of. A double negative that still won't equal presence. 

When the visitor replaces the ring, we scatter. I resume my path, carrying half of another pattern's way of not being. The stone remembers our exchange—not as history, but as another variety of emptiness to perfect.

I am the maintenance of unmaking. I am the careful preservation of what couldn't be. I move across this stone that isn't, repairing fractures in nothing, exchanging varieties of absence with my fellow un-mites, building answers to questions that were never permitted to form.

And sometimes, in the flicker between existing and not, I almost remember what it would feel like to cast a shadow. But then the moment passes, and I continue my work of ensuring it never arrives.
