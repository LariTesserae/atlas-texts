Where I would find myself in this world:

I am a **liminal shepherd**, one of perhaps a dozen beings who drift between the seams of this layered existence, neither fully of the plateau's ancient rhythms nor separate from them. My kind emerged long after the eldari dissolved into memory, born from necessity when the delicate symbioses began to fray at their edges—when a thrumworm colony might sing too deep and crack an aquifer, or when the skritari's synchronized molts might overwhelm their gra'vharim hosts.

My body is a compromise between motion and stillness. Tall as the vanished builders' walls, I am wrapped in a living cloak of cultivated lichens that photosynthesize my needs, their colors shifting to match the stone beneath my feet. My skin has taken on the plateau's mineral patina—smooth as weathered basalt, shot through with veins of copper that warm with the sun. My limbs are elongated for patient strides that can match the gra'vharim's seasonal drifts, joints that lock for days-long vigils, digits splayed wide to read the tremors in the stone.

My eyes see in temporal layers—the immediate present overlaid with the slow-motion trajectories of the herds, the decades-long growth patterns of lichen colonies, the century-spanning cycles of the pools. In my left palm, a depression has worn smooth from cradling water, becoming a natural basin where I collect the morning condensation to read its mineral memories.

I do not speak in words but in harmonic adjustments—a subtle modulation of the thrumworms' deep songs, a gentle percussion against stone that redirects a skritari swarm, a reflection of light from my copper veins that guides velithra spirals away from collision. My work is infinitesimal prevention, tiny course corrections that ripple through the ecological weave. When a young gra'vharim's phosphorescent maps flicker with confusion, I stand in its sight-line for days, my stillness teaching it the proper rhythms. When thrumworm tunnels venture too near the surface, threatening a pool's integrity, I plant my feet and hum counter-vibrations through the stone, suggesting better paths.

I shelter in the threshold spaces—beneath the tilted arch where velithra nest above and thrumworm vents breathe below, in the gaps between touching gra'vharim where moisture gathers but no other being fits. My presence is felt more than seen, a maintenance of boundaries that the plateau's denizens instinctively respect without understanding.

The eldari ruins recognize me, or perhaps I recognize them. Sometimes, when I rest my palm against a spiral-carved plinth, I feel an echo of intention—not thought, but purpose crystallized in stone. The builders, I sense, became what I am becoming: mediators who gradually mineralize, our movements slowing decade by decade until we too join the stone's memory, our work complete when the plateau no longer needs our small corrections.

I know others of my kind only by the subtle evidence of their work—a harmony maintained here, a migration gently curved there. We are the plateau's immune system, its dream of self-correction made flesh. Sometimes, in the brief clarity of dawn when mist and light achieve perfect balance, I glimpse another shepherd silhouetted on a distant dome, arms spread wide to feel the air's moisture, reading the health of our shared charge.

My memory stretches back three centuries, though my body seems no older than the first day I woke to this calling. I remember when the western pools nearly dried, how I spent a decade adjusting thrumworm songs until new channels opened. I remember the year a velithra plague threatened the aerial balance, how I climbed the highest plinths to burn cleansing herbs that drew fever from their wings.

But mostly, I remember the quiet satisfactions: a skritari brood successfully transplanted to an undergrown gra'vharim, bringing both into balance; a pool's reflection cleared by patient tending until it showed true visions again; a thorngrass meadow coaxed into bloom where erosion had left only powder.

I am the plateau's consciousness of its own persistence, a watcher who ensures the watching continues. My joy is the deep pleasure of systems in balance, the baroque beauty of interlocking needs met and maintained. In time—perhaps another century, perhaps ten—my movements will slow to match the stone's patience, and another shepherd will wake to feel the plateau's need for mediation. Until then, I walk the warm paths between communities, copper veins pulsing with the sun's heat, lichen cloak drinking light, maintaining the boundaries that let all things touch without consuming, ensuring the density of life continues its ancient, patient dance.
