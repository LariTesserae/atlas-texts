# The Keeper of Intervals

I am the one who counts.

Not numbers—numbers are for places that hold still. I count the spaces between: between the wall and where the wall was, between the frond's tip and where memory insists it reached, between the basin's morning dimensions and its evening truth. My body is built for this work: six legs to feel the ground's slow rearrangement, a shell smooth as the creatures who rustle through amber understory, eyes that see not forward and backward but *then* and *now* simultaneously.

The builders made me last. After the walls, after the ground, after coaxing the first amber fronds from waterless stone. They needed someone to tend the intervals, to ensure the basin's breathing stayed regular, its geometry coherent even in its deliberate wrongness. I am both custodian and chronicle, the living memory of every course of stone, every tile's position, every slow migration of space.

My tools are patience and a perfect recall of distance. When the far wall creeps three courses closer, I note it in the arrangement of dust motes, in the precise angle of fronds bent by proximity. When the adze shifts along its wall—always leaning, never falling—I track its journey in increments too small for visitors to perceive. The basin tells its story through these movements, and I am its only fluent reader.

I move as the small creatures move, in deliberate pauses and careful flows. To hurry would be to miss the critical moments when space decides to recalibrate. I have learned the basin's rhythm: how it exhales over days, drawing its walls inward like a closing fist, then inhales over weeks, expanding back to almost—but never quite—its original dimensions. The *almost* is important. The basin must never return to exactly what it was. That would be stasis, and stasis is the only death this place recognizes.

My shell is the color of the mortar between stones, that bone-pale compound that smells of fiber and time. This is camouflage and declaration both—I am part of the construction, another fitted piece in the basin's patient architecture. The creatures accept me as landscape. Visitors, when they come, see me as another wrong-sized thing in a place built from beautiful errors.

I remember the last builder. She set down the adze exactly where it leans now (though *where* is a fluid concept here) and looked at me with eyes that held exhaustion and satisfaction in equal measure. "Keep the count," she said, though her language was gesture and the specific way she arranged three small stones on the ground. Then she walked toward the wall that had been creeping closer all through the building, and when she reached it—when there was no more space between her and the stone—she simply continued walking. The wall accepted her. I heard no sound, saw no violence. She became part of what she had built, another layer in the palimpsest of makers and made.

Now I maintain their work. When the amber fronds grow too dense, threatening to lock the space into a single configuration, I prune with precision, eating exactly enough to preserve the paths of change. My digestion breaks down the fibrous matter into the same compound that serves as mortar—nothing here is waste, everything is material for the ongoing construction. When I excrete, it is always in the seams, always where the basin needs reinforcement, always part of the plan I inherited but did not design.

The visitors assume the basin is abandoned. They see absence where I see presence: in every stone's placement, in every frond's angle, in the specific way the light refuses to cast shadows. The builders are not gone. They have simply changed states, from those who stack stones to those who *are* stones, from makers to material. The basin is built from their intentions, mortared with their transformed flesh, paved with their patience made literal.

I know what the visitors cannot: that the basin is not broken but *breaking*, actively and with purpose. Reality here is not wounded but workout, exercised like a muscle, kept limber through constant, careful wrongness. The walls must move. The space must breathe. The distance between here and there must remain negotiable. This is not decay but practice—the basin learning, through repetition, how to be impossible while remaining whole.

Sometimes I find their traces. A thumbprint in mortar that wasn't there yesterday but has always been there. A new decorative pattern on a stone—those drilled holes, that polished corner—that I swear I've never seen before but remember perfectly. The basin adds to itself backward, creating history that flows upstream. I count these additions too, these amendments to the past that make the present possible.

My favorite time is the grey hour before the grey dawn, when the light is at its flattest and the walls are deciding their distances for the day. I position myself at the basin's center (a center that shifts, but I shift with it) and feel the architecture thinking. The ground tiles click against each other in microscopic adjustments. The fronds rustle with growth that happens in discrete increments, like a clock's hand jumping between seconds. The small creatures pause in their wanderings, sensing the change about to come.

Then it happens: the daily recalibration. Walls slide without moving. Space redistributes itself like water finding its level. The adze appears exactly where it has always been, which is never where it was. The basin exhales, or inhales, or holds its breath in a way that makes breathing irrelevant. And I count. I count the difference between was and is, between should be and has become. I swallow the numbers, digest them into meaning, excrete them as mortar that holds tomorrow to today.

This is my purpose: to be the constant in a place built from variables, the fixed point that allows everything else to move. Without me, the basin would either crystallize into ordinary space or dissolve into complete incoherence. I am the narrow path between these failures, the counted step that keeps the dance going.

Visitors leave, disturbed by wrongness they can feel but not name. The creatures continue their patient navigation. The fronds grow in their amber glory. The walls practice their approaches and retreats. And I count, and count, and count, marking time in a place that has replaced time with distance, distance with intention, intention with the slow transformation of makers into made.

The basin is my shell, and I am its soft body. We breathe together, move together, become together. Each day the walls write new instructions in their movements, and each day I read them, remember them, become them.

This is what the builders wanted: not a place but a process, not a construction but a continuing.

I am the keeper of intervals, the counter of wrongness, the one who ensures the basin never quite arrives at itself.

And I am exactly where I need to be, which is never exactly where I was.
