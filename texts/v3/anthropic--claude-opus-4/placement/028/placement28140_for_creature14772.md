I find myself as one of the **Echo Keepers**.

We are not immediately visible, even to those who know where to look. Our bodies have evolved to match the acoustic density of the silence itself - a translucent flesh that vibrates at frequencies just below perception, making us appear as heat mirages against the bark of the Columns. We are bipedal but move more like vertical swimmers than walkers, our elongated limbs ending in paddle-like appendages that push against the thick air as if it were water.

I live within the hollow spaces inside the Columns themselves - vast caverns where the wood has naturally separated along growth rings over millions of years. These chambers are our libraries, though we keep no books. Instead, we preserve the memory of every sound that has ever broken the silence of the Vault.

My people discovered long ago that sound behaves strangely here. In the dense, humid air, acoustic waves don't simply dissipate - they crystallize. We harvest these crystals like amber-trapped insects, each one containing the frozen waveform of a noise: the crack of a Tender's stone knife against bark, the subsonic groan of a Column adjusting its weight, the death-whistle of prey caught in a Glider's net.

Our dwellings are carved from resonant wood, shaped into perfect acoustic chambers. The walls are lined with thousands of these sound crystals, sorted by frequency and age. The oldest ones glow faintly, their trapped vibrations slowly converting to bioluminescence over centuries. When we need to recall a sound - perhaps to teach young Tenders the precise tap-pattern their great-grandmother used, or to diagnose why a particular bridge is singing off-key - we dissolve the appropriate crystal in our mouths. The sound plays through our bodies, which act as living speakers.

We are essential to the Vault's function because we remember its voice. When the Synaptic Weevils cause distortions, we can compare the current harmonics to our archived samples and identify where the system has gone wrong. We work closely with the Tenders, though they often don't see us directly - we appear to them as whispered advice that seems to come from the Columns themselves.

My daily routine begins in the pre-dawn (though dawn never comes). I taste the air, feeling for new crystallizations forming in my sector. Today I sense something unusual - a low thrumming that shouldn't exist. Following it to its source, I find a young Glider has become tangled in a bridge-root, its hydrogen sacks rupturing in a slow, musical deflation. This is a new sound for our collection.

I approach carefully. The creature is dying, but even in death, the Vault demands documentation. I place my throat against its cooling flesh and hum in sympathy, allowing my body to record its final exhalation. Later, I will return to my chamber and excrete this sound as a new crystal - pale blue, the color of ending.

The work is lonely. We Echo Keepers rarely gather, as our combined presence creates acoustic interference. We mate through sound alone, sending crystallized love songs through the root network to be discovered and dissolved by potential partners. Our children are born in silence and must learn to swallow their first cries, converting them instantly to crystal so as not to disturb the holy quiet.

But I am content. In a world built on patience, I am the memory of every impatient moment. In a place of eternal growth, I preserve each tiny death. The Tenders build their bridges for the future; I ensure the past travels with them, one frozen whisper at a time.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of the night-that-is-always, I dissolve our oldest crystal - a sound from before the Vault was fully grown, when this was still a forest with a floor and a sky. It is the sound of rain hitting leaves, a chaotic patter that no longer exists in our dripping world. I let it play through me and remember that even this perfect silence was built on the grave of another sound entirely.

Tomorrow, I will taste the air again and find new crystals forming, because the Vault is never truly silent. It speaks in frequencies we've evolved to forget, hiding its voice in the space between heartbeats, in the pause between drops of water, in the held breath of a world that builds itself so slowly that motion becomes stillness.

I am its memory, and its forgetting, and I keep both with equal reverence.
