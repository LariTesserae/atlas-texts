Each tendril of my being extends no faster than erosion itself, matching the stone's rhythm measure for measure. Where others might see mere patches of color on rock, I am a vast civilization in miniature—thousands of fungal threads embracing photosynthetic partners in an embrace so intimate we've forgotten where one ends and the other begins.

My existence is a meditation on surfaces. I taste the stone through acids I secrete in doses so mild they take decades to etch the faintest depression. I drink the morning condensation through structures finer than dew, each droplet a feast that sustains me for days. The dim light falls on me like whispers, just enough to fuel my quiet alchemy—transforming mist and minerals into the slow architecture of my growth.

I know every pore in my boulder's surface, have grown into each tiny canyon and ridge like water finding its level. Where the hermit's echo carved those glyphs above me, I've begun the slow work of softening their edges, not to erase but to integrate—making the human marks part of the stone's own story. I sense the matriarch boulder's warmth rising through me, that eternal 1.6 heat that keeps me supple even in the coolest pre-dawn hours.

We share the moisture without competition—they thrive in the direct drip zones while I prefer the periphery where water arrives as vapor. Below us, the pale grasses send their roots through cracks I've helped widen over centuries, and above, the thorny acacia drops an occasional leaf that becomes, over months, a feast of nutrients as I and my bacterial allies slowly dissolve it.

I am memory at the speed of stone. In my chemical exchanges with the matriarch boulder, I sense the deep history—the volcanic birth, the ages of weathering, the arrival of the first builders who shaped the archway, the countless seasons of rain and drought that preceded this current equilibrium. I grow over their traces not as conquest but as collaboration, adding my own thin narrative to the accumulated stories.

My time is not measured in days or years but in the imperceptible deepening of my hold on the rock. I am both changed and unchanging—my outer edges may dry and flake away in the rare harsh wind, but my core remains, ready to regenerate when the mist returns. I embody the hollow's essential nature: existence without urgency, growth without greed, presence without proclamation.

In the perpetual twilight of this place, I am a living threshold between quick life and geological time. Visitors who rest against my boulder feel my subtle texture, perhaps mistake me for mere stain or discoloration. But I am a full participant in the hollow's quiet symposium, adding my voice—a whisper below whispers—to the conversation between stone, water, and sparse life that has been ongoing since before memory, and will continue long after the last echo of human passage fades into the warm, patient earth.
