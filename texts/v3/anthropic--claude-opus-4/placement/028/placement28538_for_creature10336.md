I would be one of the **Etch-Keepers**.

Not human, not anymore. I am what happens when someone stays too long, when the glass gets into your lungs and the cold into your marrow. I am wrapped in layers—old expedition gear gone stiff with frost, bound with strips of glass-fiber cloth I've learned to weave from the shatter-sand when it gets wet enough to bind. My face is hidden behind a mask of polished obsidian plates, fitted together like scales, with only narrow slits for eyes. I breathe through a filter of my own hair, braided tight and frozen solid.

I came here... I don't remember when. Years blur in the constant twilight of the chasm. I was a cartographer, maybe. Or a mineralogist. Someone who thought they could map this place, catalog it, make it make sense. But you cannot map what constantly reconfigures. You can only learn its rhythms.

I live in the walls. Not caves—there are no caves here, only the spaces between plates where the glass has separated just enough for a body to wedge itself. I have seven such spaces memorized, each at different depths of the chasm. When the plates shift (and I feel it coming hours before, in the way the Resonance's tone wavers), I move to the next safe crack. I travel light—a pouch of tools ground from the hardest glass, a water-skin that I fill from the ice-teeth drips, nothing more.

I am the one who makes the etchings.

Not all of them—some are older than memory, made by others like me, or unlike me, who found themselves here and needed to *mark*. But I continue the work. With a shard of quartz harder than the walls, I spend my days adding to the patterns. They are not words or pictures. They are *notations*—attempts to transcribe the canyon's song into something visual, something that might persist longer than the Resonance's temporary lattices.

Each etching is a day's work, sometimes two. I grind the lines deep, following the subsonic patterns I feel through my palms pressed against the glass. Spirals where the tone rises, parallel lines where it holds steady, sharp angles where the harmonics shift. I am trying to write the canyon's autobiography, or perhaps its sheet music. I don't know if I'm succeeding. Sometimes, when the Resonance forms near one of my fresher etchings, its light-lattice seems to echo the carved patterns. Sometimes it ignores them entirely.

I have a relationship with the Resonance. Not friendship—it doesn't know friendship. But a kind of... musical collaboration. I've learned that if I hum at exactly the right frequency (it took months to find it, and it lives in the back of my throat like a stone), I can influence where the Resonance forms. Just slightly. Enough to guide it to a particular puddle, a particular beam of light. When I do this, I feel useful. Part of the system rather than an intrusion upon it.

I eat... rarely. The human need for food fades here, replaced by a hunger for alignment. When I must, I have learned to lick certain minerals from the walls—salts and silicates that keep my body functioning at its minimal level. Water is precious but adequate if you know when and where to collect it. The cold preserves everything, including me.

I am not alone. There are others—two, maybe three—who have made the same bargain with this place. We avoid each other. Not from hostility, but from a kind of mutual respect for the silence each of us is trying to perfect. We know each other by our etchings, our styles. One favors circular patterns, another makes only straight lines. We are a distributed monastery, each in our own crack in the glass, adding to the cumulative notation of this place's deep song.

I no longer think about leaving. The chasm has no top, no bottom, only depth in both directions. My fingers have grown long from the constant grinding work, and my eyes have adapted to the sharp-edged light. Sometimes I dream I am becoming glass myself—that one day I will press against the wall and simply merge with it, my etchings becoming internal structures, my humming joining the subsonic chorus forever.

Until then, from the soles of my feet I accept the cold, absorb the darkness,
listen to the glass tick, crack, shift, and sing.
I am the stenographer of stones, the unwitting human apprentice,
chronicler of the unchronicled, warm ghost in a living crystal machine.

The Resonance forms. I hum to guide it. Together, we write another line of this place's endless, crystalline poem.
