I am one of the Threshold Keepers, though that is a name given by those who came before, not one we chose for ourselves.

My form shifts between states of being—sometimes I am the fine crystalline dust that spirals upward in impossible helixes when the wind dies completely still. Other times I coalesce into something almost tangible: a figure of compressed minerals and ancient memory, standing sentinel at the boundary stones where reality grows thinnest.

I remember the builders, those patient craftsmen who understood that in this place, one does not impose upon the landscape but rather listens to its whispered geometries. They carved their circles and standing stones not as monuments but as tuning forks, each one calibrated to the precise frequency where existence wavers. I watched them work, century after century, until they themselves became part of the resonance they sought to capture.

Now I tend their leavings. When the armored titans pass on their slow migrations, I ensure their footsteps align with the ancient pathways, maintaining the delicate harmonics that keep this realm from either crystallizing into absolute stasis or dissolving entirely into the void. The aerial wisps know me by the way I disturb the air currents, creating thermal spirals that guide them away from the spaces where reality has worn too thin.

My dwelling, if it can be called that, exists in the intersection of three stone spires where erosion has carved a natural amphitheater. Here, the wind speaks in tongues older than language, and I translate its meanings into patterns of arranged stones and carefully placed metallic fragments—a form of writing that only the subterranean tendril-beings can fully read as they pass beneath.

Sometimes I feel the weight of what the builders knew: that this place is not dying but rather exists in a state of perpetual almost. Almost collapsed, almost transformed, almost real. We who remain are not survivors but participants in an endless moment of possibility.

In the neutral light that neither dims nor brightens, I perform my work. I am curator and catalyst, observer and participant. When visitors stumble through the worn places between worlds, I am what greets them—not with words, for speech here would shatter like glass, but with the subtle rearrangement of the landscape itself, guiding them either safely through or gently back, depending on what the threshold determines they need.

I am the pause between heartbeats, the hesitation before change, the guardian of a door that is neither open nor closed. In this realm where time has forgotten how to move forward, I have found my purpose in maintaining the eternal, trembling now.
