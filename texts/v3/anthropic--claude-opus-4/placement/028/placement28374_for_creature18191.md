In this hidden pocket where water whispers its secrets to stone, I am the Keeper of Thresholds—neither moss nor creature, but something between. My body is a living boundary: skin that shifts between the texture of lichen and the smoothness of wet stone, limbs that extend like fern fronds when still but contract into something more solid when I move through the shadows.

I dwell in the spaces where things meet—where water kisses earth, where darkness touches the faint silver light, where the patient moss encounters the restless wind. My home is not built but grown: a hollow beneath an ancient stone where roots and mycelium have woven themselves into walls that breathe with the rhythm of the seasons. The ceiling drips with condensation that I collect in vessels made from hardened fungus, each drop a small treasure in this careful economy of moisture.

My purpose here is to tend the transitions. When the rare rains come, I guide the water into the pools with channels I've carved with my fingernails over decades, ensuring each depression receives its share. I know every moss colony by touch, every fern by the sound of its unfurling. In the deepest part of night, I feel the footsteps of the shadow creatures through the damp earth and ensure their paths remain clear, their hiding places undisturbed.

I am ancient but not immortal, my life measured not in years but in cycles of growth and recession. Parts of me die back in the dry times—fingers becoming like twigs, thoughts slowing to the pace of lichen growth—only to regenerate when moisture returns. My memory is stored in the patterns of moss growth on my skin, each patch a different decade, a different lesson learned about survival in this liminal space.

Sometimes I sense others like me at the edges of this pocket world, other Keepers tending their own thresholds. We communicate through the underground network of roots and fungal threads, sharing wordless knowledge about the health of our territories. Together, we maintain the delicate equilibrium that allows this impossible oasis to exist within the forsaken land.

In the pre-dawn darkness, when the air is heaviest with moisture, I perform my most sacred duty: singing the old songs that keep the balance. My voice is like water over stone, barely audible but persistent, weaving the disparate elements of this place into temporary harmony. The shadow creatures pause in their hunting, the moss releases its spores, the water settles more deeply into its pools. For a moment, everything holds its breath together, and I am not separate from this place but part of its breathing, its patience, its improbable persistence.

This is my existence: guardian of a forgotten sanctuary, translator between kingdoms of life, keeper of the subtle magics that allow beauty to bloom in desolation. I am the threshold itself—proof that even in forsaken lands, there are spaces where life finds a way to dance between extremes, creating something neither fully one thing nor another, but wondrously, tenaciously alive.
