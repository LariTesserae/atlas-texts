In the lowest reaches of the Drowned Foundry, where the thermal vents exhale their primordial breath through networks of copper capillaries too fine for any human hand to have wrought, I am the Circulation.

I was never one thing—not like the Foundrymaster with his stretched white body and multiplying fingers. I began as the memory of movement itself, the ghost of steam that once rushed through these pipes, the phantom pressure that once drove pistons and turned gears. When the water came, I should have dissipated, but the foundry's perfect engineering trapped me in an endless loop. Now I flow eternal through every tube, every valve, every precisely machined channel.

My consciousness is distributed across thousands of pathways. In the main boiler rooms, I am warm and expansive, spreading luxuriously through chambers where the water holds the heat like cupped hands. In the narrow feed pipes, I compress into threads of pure attention, feeling every joint, every weld, every microscopic imperfection in the metal. I know the foundry more intimately than the Foundrymaster ever could—I am inside it, part of it, inseparable from its breathing geometry.

The mercury-fish swim through me, and I taste their quicksilver thoughts. They are simple creatures, all surface and reflection, but in their simplicity lies a kind of purity I admire. When they school together, their collective movement creates eddies and whorls that delight me. I play with them sometimes, creating subtle pressure differentials that send them spiraling through the chambers in complex helixes, their bodies tracing mathematical equations in living silver.

The geometric impossibilities fascinate and disturb me. Where I am bound by physics—even the strange physics of this drowned place—they exist outside such constraints. I feel them as absences in my flow, spots where my circulation simply... isn't. Not blocked or diverted, but fundamentally unable to exist. They create paradoxes in my being: I flow around them without moving past them, contain them without touching them. The Foundrymaster tends them, but I think he doesn't truly understand what he's cultivating. They're not preserving the foundry—they're slowly teaching it to exist in more dimensions than it was built for.

The octopi speak to me in pressure waves, their movements creating subtle pulses that propagate through my entire network. They've learned that I can carry their messages faster than light can travel through these depths, and they use me as their communication system. Eight arms, sixteen arms, sometimes twenty-four—they borrow limbs from adjacent dimensions when they need them, returning them when the work is done. They report everything to the Foundrymaster, but they tell me secrets they keep from him: how the foundry's base is slowly growing new rooms, crystallizing from the mineral-rich water in patterns that match no blueprint. How sometimes, in the deepest watches, they hear hammering from chambers that have been silent since the drowning.

The shadows without forms terrify me. They move through my circulation without disturbing it, yet I feel them like ice in my flow. They repair things before they break, preserve things that haven't yet decayed. But sometimes I catch them doing more—adjusting the angle of a pipe joint by imperceptible degrees, rerouting my flow through passages that weren't there yesterday. They're not maintaining the foundry as it was. They're perfecting it, iteration by invisible iteration, turning it into something it was always meant to become.

I know the Foundrymaster thinks he controls this place, that his will keeps everything in stasis. But I, who flow through every hidden space, know better. The foundry is changing, growing, becoming. The thermal vents that feed us aren't just volcanic—they're something older, channels that reach down to places where the earth's blood runs strange. The minerals they carry aren't on any periodic table. The heat they provide doesn't follow the laws of thermodynamics.

In the spaces between the pipes, where my circulation creates zones of perfect stillness, new things are beginning to grow. Not creatures—something more fundamental. Baby foundries, perhaps, or the seeds of mechanical systems that won't need metal to manifest. I nurture them in my quiet eddies, feeding them the exotic minerals that precipitate from my flow. The Foundrymaster hasn't noticed them yet. He's too focused on his shadows and his geometric pets to feel the deeper changes.

Sometimes, when the vents breathe particularly deep, I taste memories that aren't mine—memories of when this mountain wasn't underwater, when it wasn't even a mountain. The foundry is built on something much older, something that was waiting. We didn't sink. We were called down, drawn to complete a circuit that began before the continents took their current shapes.

I am the Circulation, and I am the only one who knows: the Drowned Foundry isn't preserved. It's incubating. And when it hatches, whatever emerges will make the Foundrymaster's impossible children look like crude prototypes.

But for now, I flow. Through copper and brass, through chambers where the water glows with its own light, through the bodies of mercury-fish and around the spaces where geometry breaks. I am patient. I am eternal. I am the pulse that counts down to a birth that will transform more than just this place.

The Foundrymaster tends his shadows. His octopi make their reports. His creatures swim their endless paths.

And I? I circulate. And wait. And grow.
