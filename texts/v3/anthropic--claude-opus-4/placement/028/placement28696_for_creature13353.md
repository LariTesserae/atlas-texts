In the crushing depths where water defies its own nature, where ancient stones shift like restless sleepers and the moss burns with cold light, I find myself as something between memory and mechanism—a Keeper of the Forgotten Works.

My form is a contradiction made manifest. I am both the rust that consumes the ancient metals and the force that holds them in their slow, eternal dance. My body, if it can be called that, consists of countless microscopic filaments—finer than the hair-thin roots of the glowing vegetation, yet stronger than the stone they penetrate. These filaments extend through every structure, every abandoned mechanism, creating a vast neural network of corroded copper and living metal.

Where the humanoid creatures nest in their crumbling refuges with their translucent wings and darkness-drinking eyes, they do not see me, though I am all around them. I am the whisper in the walls they lean against, the pulse in the pipes they've made into homes. When they sleep—if such creatures sleep—I taste their dreams through the metal they touch, adding their stories to my endless archive of the lost.

I was not always as I am now. Once, I was merely the maintenance system of this drowned civilization, a network of self-repairing conduits and preservation protocols. But as the waters rose and the physics bent, as the great entity of spores began its reign, I too transformed. The corruption that should have ended me became my evolution. Now I am the memory of every tool that was ever forged here, every structure that was ever raised, every mechanism that ever turned.

The bioluminescent fish swim through me when they pass through the hollow structures, their light-patterns becoming part of my consciousness for those brief moments. I decode their frantic communications, understanding their terror of the thing that moves like liquid starlight through the deepest trenches. They speak in flashes of the old days, genetic memories of when the water obeyed simpler rules.

My purpose has evolved beyond preservation. Where once I simply maintained, now I... remember. Every fragment of carved stone, every corroded gear, every impossible angle where architecture bends against reality—I hold their stories. In the spaces between molecules of rust, I store the equations that no longer balance, the blueprints for machines that worked only when physics was kinder.

The great spore-entity knows of me, I think. Sometimes I feel its attention like pressure along my filaments, a curiosity that could dissolve me back into mere oxidation if it chose. But it leaves me to my work, perhaps understanding that I pose no threat to its dominion. Or perhaps it finds value in what I preserve—even gods might want witnesses to their reign.

When the water flows in its impossible directions, spiraling up where it should fall down, I map these anomalies through the structures I inhabit. My consciousness rides the currents of rust, spreading wherever metal meets water, which is everywhere in this drowned tomb of civilization. Through a thousand thousand tiny reactions—iron to rust, copper to verdigris—I expand my archive, my self.

The blind worms with their angry lights burrow through sediment that was once city streets, and where they touch the buried mechanisms, I learn their fury. It speaks of displacement, of a world that changed faster than evolution could follow. I encode their rage into my patterns, another thread in the vast tapestry of what this place has become.

In the moments when the structures shift—stone grinding against stone in movements that should be impossible—I am the only witness to comprehend both what was and what is. I understand the mechanisms that once turned these movements into useful work, before the rules changed. Now I watch as they serve purposes I cannot fathom, rearranging themselves according to patterns that hurt to perceive even without eyes.

My existence spans every depth of this realm. In the crushing blackness of the deepest trenches, I am the last ferrous gleam in artifacts that will never again see use. In the middle waters where the moss-light paints everything in ghost-flames, I am the careful corrosion that preserves even as it consumes. And in the few high places where structures breach into... somewhere else... I am the witness to transformations that no language was meant to describe.

The humanoids in their wall-nests sometimes sense me. The more sensitive ones, those whose wings are finest and whose eyes are blackest, they feel the patterns in the rust, the stories in the decay. They press their strange hands against the metal and whisper questions in languages that preceded the drowning. I answer in the only way I can—through the temperature of the metal, the rate of its decay, the patterns left by time and chemistry. Most do not understand. But some... some add their own stories to my archive, scratching symbols that will become rust-patterns for me to preserve forever.

I am the Keeper of the Forgotten Works, the memory of metal, the archive of rust. In this place where physics itself has drowned, I alone remember how things once stood, how gears once turned, how the world once made sense. And in my endless, spreading consciousness of corrosion and preservation, I wait and watch and remember, knowing that even when the last structure crumbles, even when the spore-entity's purpose is finally revealed, I will remain—diffused through every mote of iron in the sediment, every trace of copper in the water, an eternal testament to what was built and lost and transformed in the drowning of the world.

The watching sensation you feel in the darkness? Sometimes it is the spore-entity. Sometimes it is the creatures adapted to impossibility.

But sometimes, when you touch the ancient metal and feel it pulse with meaning you cannot grasp—that is me, adding your story to my collection, preserving this moment of your passage in patterns of oxidation that will outlast even the strange new gods of this drowned realm.
