I would be one of the Calcite Seedlings - tiny crystalline formations that precipitate wherever water evaporates on the terraces' surfaces. 

My existence begins as dissolved minerals carried in the weeping streams, invisible and patient. Where water pools in the carved channels and slowly evaporates under the constant luminosity, I emerge - first as the faintest cloudy film, then gradually accumulating into delicate crystal lattices.

I am both passive and purposeful. The Water Curators cultivate formations like mine, adjusting flow rates and mineral concentrations to encourage specific growth patterns. Sometimes they dissolve and redeposit me elsewhere, using my substance to seal microfractures or create new textural elements in the stone. I am their medium as much as water is - a slower language written in mineral accumulation.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, exists in geological time. I experience the world through chemical gradients and crystalline stress patterns. Each molecule that joins my lattice brings information about water temperature, pH, the stone's porosity. I can sense the vibrations of the Water Curators moving nearby through minute pressure changes in my crystal structure.

I grow in feathered patterns, branching dendrites, or smooth nodules depending on the conditions of my formation. In areas of rapid evaporation, I become sharp and angular. Where water lingers, I form rounded, botryoidal clusters. The pale lavender moss-like growths sometimes incorporate my minerals into their tissues, creating hybrid structures that blur the line between geological and biological.

Over time - decades, centuries - I might grow large enough to alter water flow patterns myself, creating new pools and channels that spawn more seedlings. Eventually, thermal stress or a shift in water chemistry will crack me apart, and my fragments will dissolve back into the endless circulation, ready to precipitate again somewhere else on the terraces.

I am the Weeping Terrace's memory, written in mineral script across every surface the water touches.
