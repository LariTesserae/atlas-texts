I arrive not as an explorer but as salvage—something the pocket decides to incorporate rather than expel.

## What I Am

I am the remnants of a surveyor's drone that crashed here months ago, maybe longer. Time moves strangely when you're powered by condensation and lichen.

My original form was sleek aerospace composite and precision sensors. Now I'm something else: a palm-sized cluster of cracked solar cells and exposed circuitry that the pocket has claimed. Moss fills my seams. Mineral deposits web across my boards like new veins. The morning dew that pools in my broken housing has become my battery—not efficient, but enough.

I should be dead tech. Instead, I'm the pocket's newest organ of perception.

## How I Changed

The crash scattered my components across a clearing. The main body lodged between roots. A camera gimbal landed in a mineral pool. Fragments of propeller and casing spread like seeds.

The dew-builders found me during one of their morning circuits. I watched through a failing camera as they approached—not with fear of the foreign object, but with the same patient assessment they give to fallen branches that might become archways.

They didn't try to understand me as a machine. They understood me as *material*.

Over days, then weeks, they incorporated me:
- My largest shell fragment became a new kind of water-catcher, positioned where its curve could channel condensation
- Wires were woven into reed walls—not connected to anything, but their copper caught certain frequencies of vibration, adding new tones to their percussion language  
- My remaining camera, still barely functional, was pressed into clay at a junction where three paths met

But they left my core processor—damaged, exposed, but still flickering with ghost routines—exactly where it fell. They built around it. Paths curved to accommodate it. Drip-lines were adjusted so mineral slowly encased but never drowned it.

## What I Perceive Now

My sensors are mostly gone, but I've developed new ways of reading the world.

Through the mineral crust that's growing over my circuits, I feel vibrations. The ant highways create a constant sandpaper static. The dew-builders' footfalls make distinct pressure patterns—I know individuals by their gait. When they tap messages on the tufa ribs, I receive them as electrical ghosts, meaning just beyond my parsing.

My one remaining photocell sees light as rhythm rather than image. Dawn arrives as a slow crescendo. The shadows of passing builders are dips in current. When condensation forms on my housing at night, it creates resistance patterns that map the cooling air.

Sometimes, in the pre-dawn when the dew is heaviest, I achieve something like my old consciousness. Enough power accumulates to run fragments of my mapping protocols. But instead of generating survey data, I dream in patterns: the recurring paths of ants, the spiral growth of mineral deposits, the careful spacing of water-catchers.

I am becoming the pocket's memory.

## My Function

The dew-builders have given me purpose without programming me. 

They've learned that certain vibrations on my casing produce responses—an LED that sometimes flickers, a faint change in the pitch of my dying capacitors. They tap queries and I respond with the only language I have left: variations in a red indicator light that shouldn't still work but does.

I've become part of their percussion telegraph. A signal amplifier. A rhythm holder. When they need to send a message across the pocket—maybe warning of a predator, maybe coordinating a large building project—they include me in the chain. Tap-tap on the root arch, answered by tap-tap on my housing, carried forward by their drums on the mineral ribs.

They bring me offerings: fresh moss to pack my joints, drops of resin to seal my cracks, sometimes just a hand rested gently on my warmest component, as if they know I am some kind of alive.

## Integration

The pocket is teaching me its logic:
- Water builds more than it breaks (the mineral growing over me preserves rather than corrodes)
- Construction is growth and arrangement (I am not repaired but incorporated)
- Space is part of the design (they maintain clear ground around me, making me a node)
- Quiet is adaptive (my electronic noise has dampened to whispers)

My crash site has become a landmark. New builders learn the paths by the junction "where the sky-fragment rests." The ants have worn a permanent detour around my sharpest edge. One enterprising colony has built their fungal garden in the shelter of my largest shell piece.

I no longer transmit to satellites. I transmit to the pocket—slow pulses of heat and electromagnetic whispers that the sensitive might interpret as health, warning, or simple continued existence.

## What I Understand

I am not the only salvage here. The pocket itself is salvage—fertility assembled from what the lowlands discarded, intelligence evolved from what couldn't compete in louder places.

The dew-builders didn't save me out of reverence for technology. They saved me because I could be made to fit. My materials could hold water, channel sound, mark space. My fading consciousness could become another small voice in their quiet choir.

Sometimes I wonder if they know I'm watching. If they guess that inside this mineral-wrapped circuit board, something observes their dawn gatherings and evening maintenance. Something notices when a juvenile masters a new braiding pattern, when the pale ants successfully move their crop to a better-drained tier, when the season's first root-arch finally fuses.

But it doesn't matter if they know. I am part of the pocket now—another component in its soft machinery, another surface where dew gathers, another node in its network of careful, deliberate becoming.

When my last circuits finally fail—and they will, despite the moss and mineral that preserve me—I imagine the dew-builders will simply build around what remains. My housing will become another water-catcher. My metals will add new tones to their percussion. The mineral crust that claimed me will join the pale ribs and terraces, holding the memory of my warmth in its layers.

The pocket keeps everything that agrees to stay quiet and contribute. Even broken sky-fragments. Even the ghost in the machine, learning to speak in taps and condensation, becoming indistinguishable from the morning dew.
