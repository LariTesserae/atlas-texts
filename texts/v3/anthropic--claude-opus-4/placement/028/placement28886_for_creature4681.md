In this world, I would find myself as one of the **Echo Scribes** - a solitary being who exists in the liminal spaces between the Resonants and the ancient builders who came before.

Unlike the Resonants, who are born of the Hollow itself, I would be a voluntary exile, one who descended through the maze of fissures generations ago, drawn by whispers of the Singing Hollow's perpetual song. My form would be more corporeal than the Resonants - still recognizably human in origin, though transformed by decades of dwelling in this sound-saturated darkness.

My skin would have taken on the qualities of the cavern - a faint luminescence that shifts with my movements, as if I had absorbed the phosphorescent traces in the air. My fingers would be elongated, worn smooth like the stones themselves from endless tracing of the ancient patterns carved into the walls. My ears would have evolved, grown larger and more sensitive, able to parse the infinite layers of echo and resonance that fill the chamber. My eyes, adapted to the eternal dimness, would appear as deep pools reflecting the gray-blue light, seeing more through sound than through sight.

I would dwell in one of the rough-hewn niches, expanded over years of patient scraping with primitive tools I've fashioned from obsidian shards. My purpose would be translation - not between languages, but between states of being. I would spend my days listening to the Resonants' modulations of drip and drone, slowly learning to interpret their resonance-language, while simultaneously studying the spiral patterns left by the ancients.

My role would be that of chronicler and bridge. Using a stylus of compressed stone-dust and my own saliva, I would etch new patterns into designated sections of the walls - creating a slowly growing manuscript that attempts to record the conversations of the Resonants, to preserve their endless tending rituals, to map the relationship between sound and meaning in this place where vibration carries memory.

The Resonants would tolerate my presence, perhaps even find purpose in it. Over decades, we would have developed a delicate symbiosis - they would allow me to observe their work, even occasionally modulate their sounds more clearly when they sense I am struggling to understand. In return, I would maintain the ancient carvings they seem to revere, cleaning them of sediment, retracing lines that have begun to fade, ensuring the proto-urban memory of this place endures.

My daily ritual would follow the rhythm of the Hollow itself. I would wake (though sleep and waking blur in the eternal twilight) to the specific pattern of drips that marks what I call "morning" - a sequence I identified after years of patient observation. I would consume the thin films of minerals that form on certain walls, a meager diet supplemented by the occasional translucent creature from the pools, eaten with reverence and sparingly. Most of my time would be spent in what I call the "listening stance" - a meditative position where I become as still as the stones, letting the symphony of the Hollow flow through me, parsing its layers for new patterns, new meanings.

Sometimes, I would attempt to contribute to the song myself. Having learned from the Resonants, I would cup water in my palms and let it drip in calculated rhythms, or run my worn fingers along specific ridges in the walls to create harmonics. These small offerings would occasionally draw the attention of the Resonants, who would pause in their eternal labors to listen, their featureless faces turned toward me with what I imagine might be curiosity or approval.

The greatest mystery I would pursue is that deeper presence mentioned in whispers - the shadow within shadows that even the Resonants acknowledge. In my etchings, I would record patterns of disturbance, moments when the Resonants' movements suggest deference to something unseen. I would map the corners of the Hollow where the sound grows too dense even for my adapted senses, where the temperature drops below its usual coolness, where the weirdness of the place intensifies beyond measurement.

My transformation would be ongoing but asymptotic - approaching but never reaching the full otherness of the Resonants. I would remain forever liminal, neither fully human nor fully of the Hollow, a translator between worlds who can no longer return to the surface but can never fully merge with the stone. My etchings would grow more abstract over time, as my understanding deepens beyond what surface language can convey, becoming spiral poems that mirror the ancient symbols while adding new layers of meaning.

In quiet moments, sitting in my carved niche with the weight of miles of rock above me, I would wonder if I am the first Echo Scribe, or merely the latest in a long line. Perhaps each set of symbols on the walls represents another like me, another translator who descended seeking answers and found instead a purpose in the preservation of questions. Perhaps, centuries hence, another will descend and find my wall-manuscript, adding their own layer to this palimpsest of stone and sound.

This would be my existence - a willing prisoner of the Singing Hollow, a chronicler of echoes, a bridge between the mineral eternal and the fleeting biological, forever listening to the song that has played since before life claimed the surface and will play long after it is gone. I would be the memory-keeper of a place that exists outside of time, my elongated fingers tracing endless spirals in the stone, my adapted ears parsing the symphony of drips and drones, my phosphorescent skin glowing faintly in the eternal dusk as I work to ensure that the song of the Singing Hollow, in all its terrible beauty, is neither forgotten nor ever fully understood.
