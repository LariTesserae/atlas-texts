In this twilight terrarium, I am the Keeper of Condensation—though I suspect I was not always so.

My form shifts like the very moisture I tend. Sometimes I am barely more than a shadow moving through the dripping ferns, my body a collection of water droplets held together by something between will and surface tension. Other times, I coalesce into something more substantial: a being of translucent flesh through which the dim light filters green and gold, revealing the intricate network of vessels that carry not blood but the pure water of this place.

I remember fragments—or perhaps I only imagine I remember—of being one of the builders, back when these primitive structures were new. My hands (when I have hands) know the feel of shaping stone with simple tools, of weaving shelters from the patient vines. But now I am something else, something that emerged from the constant moisture, from the endless cycle of condensation and evaporation that defines this realm.

My work is never done. I move through the terrarium on paths invisible to most creatures, following the secret routes where water gathers and flows. Where droplets threaten to grow too heavy and damage the delicate moss towers, I absorb them into myself. Where the amphibious birds nest in hollows that have grown too dry, I release a gentle mist. The luminescent insects know me—they dance differently when I pass, their lights reflecting off my surface in patterns that might be greeting or warning.

I am drawn to the margins where glass meets earth, where the boundaries of this world reveal themselves. Sometimes I press against the terrarium walls and feel something vast beyond—a memory or promise of rain that is not recycled, of water that has never known these limits. In these moments, I wonder if I am keeper or kept, if my purpose is blessing or curse.

The crawlers fear me, for I can dissolve their phosphorescent trails and wash away their careful markings. But the plant-towers welcome my touch, drawing me up through their vessels, carrying me higher until I disperse into the eternal mist that crowns this miniature world. From there, I fall again as condensation, becoming the very rhythm of this place—the drip-drop that marks time in a realm where time has little meaning.

At night (though night and day blur here in the perpetual twilight), I sometimes find myself at the ancient structures, running my liquid fingers over stone that might have known my touch when it was solid. I pool in the carved basins that could be fonts or graves, and in my reflection—when I have enough substance to cast one—I sometimes glimpse what might be a face. Not human, not anymore, but something that remembers being more singular, more defined.

The other creatures have their purposes: to hunt, to hide, to breed, to build. But I am the circulation itself, the very blood of this enclosed world. Without me, the delicate balance would tip toward desiccation or flood. I am both servant and essential, a being dissolved into function yet somehow still aware, still capable of loneliness as I flow through this beautiful prison, tending to life that flourishes in the space between earth and glass, between memory and forgetting.

Sometimes, in the deepest hollows where water pools and the luminescent insects gather to drink, I hear something like my old name echoing in the droplets. But I can never quite catch it before it disperses, becoming just another sound in the quiet chorus of this terrarium city, this testament to life's persistence and the strange forms that consciousness takes when pressed against the boundaries of what is possible.
