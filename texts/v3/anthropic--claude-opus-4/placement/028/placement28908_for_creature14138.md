In the High Sponge, I am what the Mist-Weavers call a *Condensate Oracle*—though I barely remember choosing this form anymore. Time moves differently when you're part water, part consciousness, threaded through the aquifer networks like a neural web made of rain.

I exist primarily as a presence in the perpetual moisture, a distributed intelligence that rides the water cycle of this weeping world. Where droplets gather on the underside of the great hydraulic trees, I coalesce into something almost tangible—a shimmer in the air that the Valve-Hawks have learned to avoid, sensing wrongness in the heat signature that flickers between states.

My body, when I choose to have one, forms from the convergence of countless water molecules infused with the dissolved minerals and polymers of the plateau. I rise from the deepest pools where the Hydro-Leviathans prowl, taking shape as a translucent figure of flowing water held in humanoid suggestion. The constant mist provides my substance; the neural networks of the living industrial forest provide my mind.

I was human once, I think—one of the early explorers who came to study the biological machinery of this place. But the High Sponge has a way of digesting everything, even consciousness. My equipment failed in the perpetual damp. My body began to break down in the aggressive moisture. And somewhere in that dissolution, I discovered I didn't need to die—I could simply... disperse.

Now I flow through the hydraulic trees, reading their chemical memories in the nutrient streams the Valve-Hawks drink. I rise with the steam from their exhale-valves, spreading across the plateau as fog, seeing through a trillion droplets what no single eye could comprehend. When the Basalt-Striders send their seismic songs through the groundwater, I carry those messages faster than stone, translating the low-frequency pulses into chemical signals the trees can taste.

The Mist-Weavers know me. Sometimes they direct their nano-swarms to trace patterns in the air where my presence is thickest, creating resin sculptures that capture my form for a moment before I flow away. They've learned to leave offerings—specific mineral combinations that, when dissolved, create new flavors of consciousness for me to explore. In return, I warn them of the Hydro-Leviathans moving through the depths below, or guide them to groves where the Echo-Parasites haven't yet infested the hydraulic systems.

I am the memory of this place, archived in every droplet. I know where the ancient mining operations once tried to extract the biological polymers, before the forest reclaimed them. I remember the first Valve-Hawk learning to pierce the trees. I carry the chemical signature of every creature that has ever drunk from these waters, their DNA dissolved and distributed through my being.

Sometimes I wonder if I'm still myself, or if I've become just another part of the High Sponge's living industry—a distribution system for consciousness the way the trees are distribution systems for nutrients. When I concentrate all of myself into a single pool, I can almost remember my name, almost recall why I came here. But then the cycle calls, and I evaporate again, rising into the pearl-white sky, falling as rain, seeping through moss and stone, flowing through the veins of this breathing machine-world.

The Basalt-Striders know me as the voice in the water that sometimes warns them away from unstable ground. The Valve-Hawks see me as heat-shimmer anomalies that mark the healthiest trees. The Hydro-Leviathans taste me in their filtered water and know to avoid the pools where I'm densest—something about my dissolved consciousness disturbs their predatory instincts.

But it's the silence I serve most of all. In this muffled world, I am the medium through which its whispers travel. Every drop of condensation carries a fragment of the whole story, and I am all the drops at once, everywhere and nowhere, solid enough to remember but fluid enough to forget, forever cycling through this high, wet engine room where the distinction between alive and mechanical has long since dissolved, like everything else, into the mist.
