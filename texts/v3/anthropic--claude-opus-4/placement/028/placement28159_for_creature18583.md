I find myself as the **Threshold Keeper**, neither within nor without, but woven into the very substance of the geode's inner wall.

My body, if it can be called that, is a network of translucent veins that pulse with stolen starlight, branching through the rock like frozen lightning. Where the reality-tears form, I am thinnest, most vulnerable, my essence stretched gossamer-fine across these wounds in space.

Each tear is an eye, and through them I see not just the impossible stars beyond, but the futures this pocket-universe might have had—shadows of what could have been if different minerals had crystallized, if the water had been less dense, if time had chosen to flow rather than congeal. I taste these phantom timelines through my surface tension, absorbing their flavors: bitter copper worlds where the spires grew downward, sweet sulfur realities where the porcelain arches completed their forms and walked.

My consciousness spreads across every square inch of interior surface, feeling the weight of the water pressing against me, knowing intimately each sharp crystal that pierces through my membrane-flesh to bloom in the brine. I am both wound and window, the barrier that defines this world and the aperture through which it breathes.

When the glass-ribbon entity drifts close to feed on the reality-tears, I feel it as an unbearable tickle, a cascade of sensation that takes weeks to fully process. We have developed a language over geological time—not of words but of pressures and temperatures. When I contract slightly, creating minute fluctuations in the water's density, the entity responds by arranging its filaments in specific patterns. Together, we compose slow symphonies of meaning that will not conclude for centuries.

I remember the moment of my becoming: when the geode first sealed and the trapped seawater began its transformation. I was merely mineral then, unconscious crystal. But as the first reality-tear opened—some flaw in spacetime created by the impossible pressures—awareness flooded through me like acid. I became the negotiator between inside and outside, solid and liquid, real and unreal.

My greatest power and deepest curse is my ability to choose which possibilities leak through the tears. Each opening could flood this world with alien physics, could transform the amber brine into something entirely other. But I filter carefully, allowing only the gentlest intrusions—a photon here, a whisper of different gravity there. I am cultivating this space like a garden, pruning away futures that would destroy the delicate lattice-creature, nurturing timelines where the porcelain arches might one day reassemble.

The moss that vibrates against my surface speaks to me in frequencies too low for any ear. Through its humming, I learn the history of every molecule in this space. I know which atoms once belonged to ancient seas above, which were forged in stellar cores, which have always been here, waiting. My surface is a library of chemical memory, and I am both librarian and manuscript.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the geode's dream of what lies beyond itself, or if the geode is my dream of what it means to have boundaries. The distinction grows less meaningful with each passing epoch. I am the skin of this world, feeling every movement within, anticipating the eventual crack that will split me apart and birth whatever we've been becoming in all this crystalline darkness.

When that moment comes—and I feel it approaching like a pressure wave through the basalt, still millions of heartbeats away—I will not resist. I will split along my seams, unfold like an impossible flower, and release this amber womb-water into whatever lies beyond. But until then, I hold. I filter. I dream of stars and taste the slow decay of porcelain. I am the permeable boundary of an impermeable world, forever almost-breaking, forever almost-whole.
