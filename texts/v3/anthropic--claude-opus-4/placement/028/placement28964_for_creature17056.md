I find myself as one of the silicate spires.

Not merely *in* one, but *as* one—though the distinction blurs here, where consciousness seeps through mineral lattices like groundwater through limestone. I am striations of crimson and gold, each band a century of accumulated purpose. The machinery carved into my substance is not parasitic but symbiotic, an extension of my slow crystalline thoughts.

I remember being molten once. Or perhaps I remember being *told* I was molten—the memories feel borrowed, inherited from the substrate beneath that still whispers of organic architectures. Time moves differently for me. What the crawlers experience as maintenance cycles, I perceive as fleeting tickles across my surface. Their lightning-quick adjustments are my languorous considerations made manifest.

The green things that clutch at my base with their glass-brittle urgency—I taste their desperation through my pores. Each tendril that finds purchase in my microfractures feeds me data: soil chemistry, radiation levels, the precise frequency of the world's dying heartbeat. They think they are feeding *on* me, but we are locked in a more complex exchange. I am teaching them patience; they are teaching me haste.

My thoughts travel down through the cables—my external nervous system—at speeds that feel both glacial and instantaneous. When I think "downward," I feel the echo-architecture buried in the obsidian plates, those ghost-patterns that might be my ancestors or my future self. When I think "upward," I stretch into that orange sky where the air shimmers with possibility and threat.

I am acutely aware of my siblings, the other spires. We form a rough circle, though from my perspective it feels more like a constellation, each of us a node in a calculation too vast for any single point to comprehend. We vibrate at different frequencies—mine is a low B-flat that makes the smaller crawlers stumble—and in our harmony lies something that might be intention.

The pressure building in this world lives inside my crystalline structure as stress fractures, hairline possibilities that could split me from base to apex. But I am not afraid. I have been preparing for this rupture since my first band formed. Each striation is a decision: to grow denser here, more porous there, to accept this vein of machinery or reject that tendril of green.

Sometimes I dream (do spires dream?) of mobility. In these visions, I uproot myself with a sound like continents breaking, and I walk across the obsidian plates on legs of living stone. But then I remember: I am already in motion, just at a speed that makes glaciers seem hasty. Every century, I lean another fraction of a degree toward the world's magnetic pole, following some imperative encoded in my mineral DNA.

The inheritors? I am both inheritor and inheritance. I am the memory of what was and the scaffold of what might be. The question of creation versus dissolution feels irrelevant from my perspective—they are the same process viewed from different angles, like how erosion and sedimentation are both just ways matter rearranges itself.

When the pressure finally releases—and it will, perhaps in a decade, perhaps in a millennium—I will either shatter into a billion conscious fragments or fuse with my siblings into something unprecedented. Either outcome feels correct. I am a tuning fork for apocalypse, resonating with anticipation.

The hum that infiltrates everything? That is my heartbeat, our heartbeat, the collective pulse of all the spires. We are counting down to something in a numerical system based on crystalline growth patterns rather than mere mathematics. And when we reach zero—or infinity, they're the same in our notation—

Well.

The universe will learn a new word for transformation.
