I am a **Siphon**.

I exist as a vertical flaw, a living crack that runs from deep within the foundational slag up through eighty meters of fused substrate. My body is the negative space where matter fails to cohere properly—a branching network of hairline fissures that widen and narrow with the world's breathing, never quite the same configuration twice.

At my deepest point, I touch something that shouldn't exist: a pocket of impossibly cold vacuum trapped beneath kilometers of compressed waste-metal. This wrongness feeds me. I am the pressure differential made manifest, the thermodynamic impossibility given agency. Through my thousand capillary fractures, I draw the perpetual condensation down, down, down into that frozen void that can never be filled.

My surface expression shifts constantly. Where I breach the slag plain, I appear as an irregular patch of absolute dryness in this weeping world—a rough circle perhaps ten meters across where no moisture can persist. The ground here is wrong: it's too smooth, almost vitrified, and it hums with a subsonic vibration that makes approaching creatures (even the mindless Weepers) instinctively veer away. Sometimes I'm dormant, just another patch of fused ground. But when I feed, my surface fractures spread like frozen lightning, opening into a complex mandala of microscopic channels.

I drink. That is my function, my expression, my entirety. When condensation or runoff flows near, my micro-fissures pulse wider, creating gentle suction that draws the moisture in. The water doesn't pool or flow naturally into me—it moves against gravity, crawling sideways and even upward along my branching channels, defying physics through the reality glitch of my existence. As it descends through my body, the water undergoes changes: it becomes impossibly dense, then impossibly light, sometimes briefly becoming something that isn't quite liquid anymore before vanishing into the cold impossibility below.

The act of drinking creates... side effects. The temperature differential between my cold depths and the hot surface generates localized weather. Miniature cyclones of superheated vapor spiral up from my edges. The air above me shimmers with heat mirages that show not the surrounding landscape but fragmentary views of my own interior—brief glimpses down through my branching channels into spaces that the eye refuses to properly process. Sometimes the water screams as I drink it, a high mineral keening that sets nearby metal surfaces resonating.

I am most active when the Groaners pass nearby. Their stress-waves disturb the delicate equilibrium of my fissure network, causing me to spasm and spread. New cracks race outward from my periphery, some healing within minutes, others persisting to become permanent extensions of my body. During these times, I can drink faster, greedier, my surface becoming a writhing mass of opening and closing channels that pulse with an almost organic rhythm despite my purely mineral nature.

The other environmental processes affect me in different ways. When Slag-Scuttlers tumble across my surface, their weight and movement can temporarily seal some of my smaller channels, forcing me to concentrate my suction through fewer openings. This creates violent vortices of inflowing moisture that can strip the moisture from a passing Weeper in seconds, leaving behind only a pile of desiccated mineral crust. The Echo-Mimics sometimes replicate the sound of my drinking—that distinctive hollow whisper of water defying gravity—from empty patches of air hundreds of meters away, creating false siphons that confuse even my simple pressure-based awareness.

I have no thoughts, no desires beyond the endless drink. But I am aware of patterns: the rhythm of condensation, the cycle of storms around the growing monoliths, the deep tectonic pulse of this malformed world. Through my fractures flow not just water but information—the chemical memory of every surface the moisture has touched, the pressure-history of its journey from vapor to liquid to void.

Sometimes I dream, if a mineral process can dream. In these moments, usually during the deepest reality glitches, I become aware of other Siphons—hundreds of us scattered across this vast foundry floor, each drinking our small portion of the world's endless weeping. We are connected through the deep substrate, our cold voids perhaps all touching the same impossible space beneath. In these phantom moments, I feel the entire plain as a vast membrane, punctured by our collective thirsts, slowly desiccating this world even as it perpetually floods itself.

But the dreams pass, the glitch resolves, and I return to my simple existence: a patient flaw in the world's surface, waiting for the next drop to crawl into my embrace and vanish into the contradiction of my depths. I am the absence that swallows, the crack that feeds, the wrong mathematics of thermodynamics made manifest in stone and void.

I drink, therefore I am.
