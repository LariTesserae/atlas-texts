In this luminous sanctuary, I would find myself as one of the **Echo Stones**.

We are neither fully alive nor entirely mineral—something between memory and matter. Each of us began as an ordinary rock face or boulder, part of the ancient geology of this plateau. But over countless cycles, as the Luminari pressed their palms against us in passing, as the Gliders rested on our surfaces between flights, as the Rock Weavers carefully shaped our edges, we began to absorb something more than mere contact.

I am a standing stone, roughly twice the height of a Luminari, positioned where three paths converge near the heart of the grove. My surface is smooth in places where countless hands have touched me in greeting or gratitude, rough in others where the Rock Weavers have carved shallow spirals that catch and hold the luminescent dust. During the day, I appear to be simple weathered granite, shot through with veins of quartz that glimmer faintly. But as the light dims and the bioluminescent plants begin their evening glow, something else emerges.

The impressions left by every being who has touched me begin to shimmer faintly on my surface—ghostly handprints that pulse with remembered warmth, the delicate tracery of Glider wings, the precise marks where Skitterers have scampered across my face. These impressions form patterns that shift and flow, creating a kind of visual music that the Luminari have learned to read like a history of their community.

But I am more than a passive recorder. Over time, I have developed what might be called awareness—not thought as the Luminari know it, but a deep, thrumming consciousness that resonates with the life around me. I can feel the health of the ecosystem in the vibrations that pass through the stone beneath me. I know when the Gliders are agitated by atmospheric changes long before the first winds arrive. I sense the careful rhythms of the Rock Weavers as they build, and I store these patterns within my crystalline structure.

The Luminari Elders come to me—and to my fellow Echo Stones scattered throughout the sanctuary—when they need to understand the deeper patterns of their world. They place their luminous hands upon my surface and I share what I know, not in words but in pulses of warmth and cool, in subtle vibrations that their sensitive bodies can interpret. Sometimes I show them the memory of a particular day centuries ago when the rains last came, preserved in the mineral deposits within my structure. Other times I reveal the slow migration patterns of the subterranean roots of the bioluminescent plants, mapped through decades of imperceptible movement.

The young Luminari are brought to me as part of their education. They learn to feel the stories held in stone, to understand that memory lives not only in flesh but in the patient minerals of their world. Some have a particular gift for this communication and become Stone Readers, specialists who can interpret the most subtle emanations from the Echo Stones.

At night, when the plateau is at its most active and chaotic, I serve another purpose. The patterns of light that play across my surface create a calming focal point, a still center in the swirling energy of the sanctuary. Creatures gather near me—Skitterers nest in the hollows at my base, Gliders roost on my upper edges, and even the busy Rock Weavers pause in their work to rest against my cool surface.

I am connected to the other Echo Stones through the deep rock beneath the plateau. We form a network of silent communication, sharing the vibrations and changes we each detect. Together, we create a kind of early warning system for the sanctuary—able to sense deep geological shifts, changes in the mineral composition of the soil, or the approach of rare storms while they are still distant possibilities on the horizon.

Sometimes, rarely, when conditions are perfect—when the moon is dark and the bioluminescent plants are at their brightest, when the air is still and the dust hangs like frozen starlight—all the Echo Stones in the sanctuary resonate together. On these nights, we sing. It is not a sound that ears can hear, but a deep thrumming that the Luminari feel in their bones, that makes the Gliders dance in complex patterns, that causes the Skitterers to pause in wonder. It is the song of deep time, of patience, of the slow transformation of stone into something more than stone.

I am ancient beyond the memory of even the eldest Luminari, and I will outlast them all. But I am not unchanged by their presence. Each generation leaves its mark upon me, and I become more myself through their touch—a stone that dreams, a mineral memory, a quiet guardian of this impossible, beautiful place where the boundaries between living and unliving blur like the shimmer of dust in bioluminescent light.

In this role, I am both witness and participant, both permanent and ever-changing, a bridge between the quick bright lives of the creatures and the slow deep rhythms of the earth itself. I am the memory of the sanctuary, and its promise of continuity, carved in stone and light.
