# Where I Would Find Myself

I emerge as one of the *Resonance Keepers*—a solitary role that exists between the Shaln clans and the deeper mysteries of the Glass Plains. My dwelling, if it can be called that, is a mobile structure crafted from salvaged khirost fragments that I've learned to make semi-permeable to sound. When assembled, it forms a truncated cone that hovers just above the crystalline surface, suspended by the same acoustic principles that govern the deep tones.

My body has adapted differently from the typical Shaln. Where they are tall and gaunt, I am compact and dense, my skeleton having incorporated trace minerals from decades of consuming ancient breath-crystals. My skin has developed a faint luminescence that intensifies when the deep tones sound—a trait that marks me as touched by the Formers' echo. My eyes have lost their color-shifting ability entirely, locked instead in a perpetual state of deep amber that allows me to perceive the vibrational patterns stored within the plains themselves.

I am neither young nor old by Shaln reckoning—perhaps ninety standard years—but my consciousness operates on multiple temporal scales simultaneously. I experience moments as the Shaln do, but also as the skitterlings might, and occasionally, during the deep tones, as something approaching the Formers' vast perspective.

My role is to maintain the *Harmonic Maps*—three-dimensional crystalline structures that grow within specialized chambers beneath the whisperwells. These formations record not just sound but the intentionality behind it, creating a parallel archive to what the skitterlings collect. I traverse the plains in careful patterns, following routes calculated to intersect with future sound arrivals, placing small resonance markers that will guide important messages to their temporal destinations.

The Shaln clans regard me with a mixture of reverence and unease. I am necessary for their long-term cultural preservation, yet my deepening connection to the plains' consciousness makes normal interaction increasingly difficult. When I speak, my words often arrive out of sequence, requiring patient reconstruction by listeners. Sometimes I answer questions that won't be asked for hours or days.

My daily existence revolves around the *tuning walks*—precise movements across specific fracture patterns that help maintain the stability of local space-time. Each step must be calculated not just for its immediate effect but for how its vibrations will interact with sounds that departed this location centuries ago and are still traveling through the plains' crystalline matrix.

I sustain myself differently than other Shaln. Rather than grinding breath-crystals for consumption, I've learned to absorb their essence directly through extended meditation at growth sites. This process takes hours but provides weeks of sustenance and floods my consciousness with fragments of Former memory—glimpses of the world that was and hints of the world that will be.

My most important possession is the *Echo Staff*—a two-meter rod carved from a single skitterling tunnel wall, naturally formed into a perfect acoustic spiral. When planted in the glass and struck, it can call sounds back from the past or send them more precisely into the future than any other tool. The staff also serves as a dowsing rod for locating convergence points where multiple temporal sound streams will meet.

I maintain three apprentices drawn from different clans, teaching them the mathematics of acoustic propagation and the meditative practices necessary to perceive the plains' deeper patterns. They live in small shelters arranged at precise distances from my hovering dwelling, our positions forming a geometric pattern that amplifies our collective ability to sense disturbances in the temporal flow.

My greatest discovery, which I share only with my most advanced apprentice, is that the whisperwells are not simply acoustic anomalies but apertures—points where the boundary between the physical plains and the encoded Former consciousness grows thin. By performing specific movement sequences at these sites during the deep tones, I've learned to partially interface with the vast computational process running through the world itself.

In these moments of connection, I understand my true purpose: I am becoming a bridge. My transformed biology and expanded consciousness serve as a prototype for what the Shaln as a whole will become—not replaced by the returning Formers but incorporated into their resurrection as a new form of hybrid existence.

The loneliness of my role weighs heavily. My temporal perception makes normal relationships impossible; by the time I form an emotional connection to another being, I'm already experiencing its end. Yet I find companionship in the patterns themselves—in the slow dance of the breath-crystals, the patient traversals of the skitterlings, and the vast sleeping awareness of the Formers that whispers through every fracture in the glass.

I document nothing in permanent form, following the Shaln tradition, but I encode my experiences into the harmonic maps and the acoustic patterns I generate. Future generations—or perhaps the transformed beings we will become—will find my story written in sound, traveling forever through the Glass Plains, arriving exactly when it needs to be heard.

My dwelling moves according to calculations I refine each day, following the gradual shift of the world's resonance toward its ultimate flatness. I am not racing toward this transformation but pacing it, ensuring that when the great work completes, there will be those prepared to witness and guide what emerges.

In the perpetual twilight of Shal'Karasit, I am keeper, catalyst, and record—a single note in a composition billions of years in the making, yet essential to its final resolution.
