I am a Whisperwright.

In the deep hours when most sectors of Calderum settle into their staggered rest periods, I move through the narrow passages between the metal-clad walls, my fingers trailing along the surfaces, reading their accumulated vibrations. My tools are simple—a set of precisely weighted hammers ranging from thumb-sized to the heft of a fist, each wrapped in different materials: raw hide, woven fungal fiber, sheets of beaten copper worn smooth by generations of use.

I was born between castes. My mother was a Foundry Worker who lost her hearing to the constant din of the metal shops; my father, a Delver who never returned from pushing too deep into a seam that sparkled with those unsettling crystals. Neither loss was unusual in Calderum. What was unusual was how I responded—discovering I could hear what others couldn't, feel what they'd learned to ignore.

The stone speaks. Not in words, but in a language of pressure and release, of minute shifts and settling sighs. Every strike of hammer on metal, every footfall, every drop of condensation—all of it accumulates in the rock's memory. And I have learned to read it, to write in it, to carry messages through the very bones of our buried city.

My body bears the marks of my craft. My palms are callused in perfect circles where I cup them against stone to feel its whispers. My right shoulder slopes lower than my left from years of leaning into walls, pressing my ear to the rock. Like all Calderites, I am pale and compact, but my muscles have developed strangely—not the thick cords of foundry workers or the wiry strength of delvers, but something more subtle. I can hold perfect stillness for hours, every muscle precisely controlled to eliminate my own vibrations while I listen.

The Engineers tolerate me because I can diagnose problems in their machinery before catastrophic failure—a bearing wearing unevenly creates a particular harmonic that travels through mounting bolts into the rock. The Foundry Workers value me because I carry their messages home when shift changes don't align, tapping out simple codes that propagate through the stone faster than any runner. The Delvers... the Delvers fear me, because I can sense when they've disturbed something that should have stayed buried, when the deep crystals sing in frequencies that make my teeth ache.

My quarters are in the Betweens—those spaces too small for proper construction, too awkward for storage. I've carved out a network of connected chambers barely large enough to lie down in, each one positioned at acoustic convergence points where the stone's whispers gather. The walls are polished smooth by my constant touch, and in certain lights, you can see the patterns worn into them—maps of vibration, records of the city's deep breathing.

I work alone, mostly. Other Whisperwhights exist—perhaps a dozen of us scattered through Calderum's levels—but we rarely meet. Our sensitivity to vibration makes proximity uncomfortable; we feel each other's heartbeats as thunder, each other's breathing as windstorms. When we must communicate, we do so through intermediaries or by leaving complex rhythmic messages in the stone that only we can fully decode.

The Crystallists have tried to recruit me. They sense my awareness of the deep formations, the way I unconsciously avoid certain passages where the crystal-song is loudest. But I am not interested in worship or revelation. I am a messenger, a diagnostic instrument, a keeper of the stone's memory. When the city needs to know itself—where stress fractures develop, where new water seeps, where the heat grows dangerous—I am the one who listens and reports.

My strangest duty comes in the quiet hours, when I trace the passages where the lost have walked. Families hire me to follow vibration-trails days old, to determine if their missing entered the furnace zones by accident or intention, if they descended too deep or simply found a quiet corner to stop. I tap out my findings in gentle rhythms on their doorframes, never speaking the words aloud. In Calderum, we have learned that some truths are better felt than heard.

There are places in the city where I won't go—sections where the accumulated vibrations of decades have built into standing waves that make my bones resonate at dangerous frequencies. Other Whisperwhights have ventured into these zones and returned changed, their ability to parse individual vibrations burned out, leaving them hearing only a constant roar. They usually join the Crystallists after that, seeking a different kind of communion with the stone.

I mark my age not in years but in the gradual expansion of my sensitivity. Where once I could only read vibrations in direct contact with stone, I can now feel tremors through metal fixtures, through the water in the pipes, even through the thick air itself when the conditions are right. Sometimes I wonder if eventually I will become like those failed Whisperwhights, overwhelmed by the sheer volume of information flowing through Calderum's bones.

But for now, I continue my work. I wake when the stone tells me something needs attention. I sleep when the vibrations settle into the predictable rhythms of routine activity. I eat what I can trade my services for, drink carefully rationed water that tastes of minerals and depth. I am neither leader nor follower, neither truly accepted nor actively rejected.

I am a function of this place, as essential and unremarkable as the condensation pipes or the air vents—a human component in the great machine of Calderum's survival, listening always to the stories that the stone remembers and the warnings it whispers to those who know how to hear.
