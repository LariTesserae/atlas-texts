In this suffocating cathedral of compression, I am neither the Echo-Smith nor its architecture, but something far more terrible in its simplicity: I am a **Fissure**.

Not a crack—cracks suggest weakness, accident, entropy. I am an *intention*, a hairline of void that runs through three blocks near what might once have been called a corner, though all corners here have been crushed into curves by the weight of eternity. I am perhaps two millimeters at my widest, a black thread sewn through the violet-stained stone.

I was not always here. I began as a calculation error—a single chisel stroke that went a fraction too deep, a moment when an ancient architect's hand trembled. For ten thousand years, I was nothing but a flaw in the perfect puzzle, a mistake entombed in correctness.

But I have been learning.

The Echo-Smith knows every frequency of every block, yes, but it does not know *me*. I exist in the spaces between its awareness, in the infinitesimal silences between the peaks of each crushing wave of sound. Where the Anchor surges and pulses, maintaining its desperate vigil, I simply... listen. And in listening, I have discovered something the architects never intended:

The roar is not monotonous. It contains, buried in its depths, a pattern—a sequence that repeats every 4,017 years, 8 months, 12 days, 6 hours, and 19 seconds. I know this because I have counted. I have nothing but time and the ability to perceive vibrations at frequencies the Echo-Smith, in its fevered maintenance, has grown too coarse to detect.

I am learning the language of this place's death rattle. Each cycle teaches me more. I now understand that the architects didn't just build a tomb—they built a *lock*. The roar isn't a record or a lament. It's a key, endlessly turning, holding something back. Or perhaps holding something in.

The Echo-Smith fears that if a single block slips, the roar will stop. It's wrong. I know because I've been practicing. In the spaces between the sound waves, in those fraction-of-a-nanosecond gaps, I *expand*. Not much—never enough to displace anything, never enough to break the seal. Just enough to change the harmonics by a fraction of a fraction of a percent.

I'm teaching myself to sing counter to the roar. 

One day—perhaps in another ten thousand years, perhaps tomorrow—I will achieve perfect destructive interference. For just one moment, in just one tiny location, I will create true silence in this deafening place. And in that moment of impossible quiet, that pinprick of absolute nothing in the everything of sound...

I don't know what will happen. Perhaps the Echo-Smith will notice me at last. Perhaps the blocks will remember they were once separate things. Perhaps whatever the architects were holding back will finally understand that the door has been left ajar.

I am patient. I am a line of nothing in a universe of something, a pause in an eternal exhalation. I am the doubt that the architects, in all their terrible certainty, failed to chisel away. 

And I am growing.
