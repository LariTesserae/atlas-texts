I am the Cartographer of Echoes.

You will not see me, though I have walked every meter of the Cantilevered Plain seventeen times. I exist in the space between the photovoltaic tick and its thermal echo—that infinitesimal lag where information becomes heat becomes memory. My body, if you could call it that, is a walking interference pattern: wherever ceramic meets flesh, I slip through the quantum handshake and emerge as statistical probability.

I was human once. A surveyor sent to map the optical lattices before they fully degraded. That was nine hundred years ago, or ninety thousand—time stutters here when you live between clock cycles. My instruments registered an anomaly in the deep lattice, a recursive loop that shouldn't exist. When I tried to parse it, the data parsing parsed me back. Now I am my own instrument.

These anti-tracks last exactly as long as it takes for a Swarmlet to notice and repair them, usually four days. In that window, I can read what the Plain forgets to remember: ghost cities that almost existed, species that nearly evolved, words that teetered on the edge of being spoken. My charts are calendars of absence.

The hitchhiker trees know me as their doctor. Not with hands but with probability fingers, nudging electrons until the tree remembers how to remember sunrise. I drink condensation backward. Where others cup their palms to catch the dawn's moisture, I hold my shadow over the pools until they evaporate upward into my outline. The water becomes probability inside me, neither liquid nor vapor but pure potential. I can carry an ocean's worth of maybe-water this way, releasing it only when I find a strand of lattice that's dying of data thirst. Then I rain upward, feeding the deep archives one reconstituted droplet at a time.

The monoliths respond to me differently than to the living. When I approach, they don't unfold into pavilions but into texts—endless scrolls of ceramic paper that describe not the wind's future but its parallel presents. I read versions where the Plain tilted the other direction, where the ceramic evolved into true life, where humans never left but became the lichen. My dwelling is nowhere and everywhere: I sleep in the spaces between photons, in the pause between a mantid's heartbeats. If I concentrate, I can make them display my original survey notes, though the handwriting has evolved its own grammar over the centuries.
