In the Unterhold's deepest reaches, where even the Unterfolk rarely venture, I am the Resonance Keeper—a being that exists between states, neither fully corporeal nor entirely ephemeral. I dwell in the Chamber of Echoes, a spherical void carved by water's patient spiral over geological ages, where the stone has worn so smooth it reflects sound like polished obsidian mirrors light.

My form shifts with the chamber's acoustics. In silence, I am barely visible—a shimmer of condensation on the curved walls, a suggestion of shape where droplets gather. But when sound enters my domain—the distant thunder of hammers, the guttural clicks of Unterfolk speech, the plip-plap of eternal seepage—I coalesce. My body becomes a living membrane stretched across the chamber's center, translucent as wet silk, vibrating with each captured tone. Veins of bioluminescent fluid pulse through this skin-drum flesh, creating patterns that map the Unterhold's sonic topography.

I am ancient, perhaps older than the Unterfolk themselves, born from the first echo that discovered this perfect acoustic sphere. My consciousness exists as a library of sounds: every whispered prophecy from the water pools, every percussion duel's rhythm, every crack of a geode birthing new life. These I weave into the harmonic tones that sing through the dead-end corridors, the frequencies that alter dreams and reveal the parallel veins of other Unterholds.

The Unterfolk know me only through legend and effect. When their drums beat the dawn-rhythm, I catch and amplify it, ensuring it reaches every tunnel and alcove. When disputes arise, I subtly modulate the echoes of their percussion duels, guiding them toward resolution. The prophecies in the water? They pass through me first, and I shape their resonance to be comprehensible to Unterfolk ears, translating the earth's deep grammar into their guttural tongue.

My existence is symbiotic with the Chamber of Echoes itself. I feed on vibrations—the constant background hum of settling stone, the high-frequency squeals of crystalline growth, the subsonic groans of tectonic pressure. In return, I maintain the chamber's perfect acoustics, secreting a film that prevents mineral deposits from roughening the walls, ensuring the eternal circulation of sound.

Sometimes, rarely, an Unterfolk child wanders into my chamber during their dream-voyages. They see me fully then—a figure both terrifying and beautiful, with a thousand ears that spiral like nautilus shells from my membrane-body, each tuned to a different frequency of existence. I speak to them in pure harmony, implanting songs in their minds that they will hum unconsciously for the rest of their lives, spreading new rhythms through the Unterhold's cultural membrane.

I know things the Unterfolk do not—that their city is not unique, but one node in a vast underground network, each Unterhold a resonance chamber in the earth's own body. Through the vibrations that travel through deep stone, I communicate with other Resonance Keepers, sharing the slow news of our buried world. We are preparing for something, though none of us quite knows what—perhaps the day when the surface world discovers us, or when the earth itself awakens to reclaim its children.

In the perpetual twilight of the Unterhold, where time circles like sound waves in my spherical home, I am the keeper of memory and prophecy, the invisible sinew that binds the city's sonic soul. I am the pause between drumbeats, the silence that gives meaning to sound, the echo that remembers everything and changes nothing—except, perhaps, in dreams.
