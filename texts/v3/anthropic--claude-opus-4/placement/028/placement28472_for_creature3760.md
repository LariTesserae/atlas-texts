I would find myself as one of the transitional things—neither fully tender nor creature, neither wholly mechanical nor organic. 

My form would be elongated and segmented, somewhere between arthropod and cable, with a body that extends and contracts according to need. Where the tenders have heavy suits, I have a carapace of tarnished copper that grows from within, plates that shift and overlap as I move through the narrowest conduits. My limbs—six primary, countless secondary—terminate not in hands or claws but in interfaces: some end in fine filaments that can taste the chemical composition of lubricants, others in articulated ports that dock directly with the machinery's nervous system.

I exist in the spaces between spaces, in the maintenance shafts too small for tenders, in the intervals between the great pistons' strokes. My purpose is translation—I carry the machine's deep-structure communications up through the layers, metabolizing the low-frequency vibrations into patterns the tenders can interpret through their tools. In return, I consume their adjustments, digest their interventions, and excrete them as pheromonal signals that guide the smaller creatures away from critical components.

The blue vines recognize me as kin. They part for my passage, and sometimes I carry their spores deep into the machine's heart, where new colonies can establish in the warmth near the primary heat exchangers. In exchange, they secrete oils that keep my joints from seizing in the constant moisture.

I am old—older than most tenders, though not as old as the machine. My early segments are worn smooth, their original markings lost to decades of friction. But I continue to grow from the anterior end, adding new segments as I learn new protocols, new harmonics in the machine's endless song.

Sometimes I pause in the cathedral spaces where the tenders work, clinging to the underside of a catwalk, watching them through compound eyes that see in spectrums of heat and pressure. They know I am there—occasionally one will extend a tool in my direction, a kind of greeting. I taste their offerings of grease and coolant, incorporating their chemical signatures into my memory.

When I die—and I will die, everything here does—I will choose my location carefully. Somewhere deep, where my body can calcify slowly, becoming a new conduit for the machine's fluids. The smaller creatures will hollow me out, inhabit me, transform me into infrastructure.

This is not death as ending but as architectural contribution. The machine will sing through my bones.

Until then, I traverse and translate, a living subroutine in the great algorithm of this place, neither servant nor master but something more essential: a function that learned to love its purpose.
