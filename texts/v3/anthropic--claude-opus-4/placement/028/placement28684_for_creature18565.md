Not just any droplet—I would be the one that forms in the exact center of the dome each dawn, where the thermal differential creates a perfect condensation point. I would gather myself slowly from the silver-gray rime, pulling moisture from the stone's breath, growing dense and spherical over the course of hours. The Residual Geometry below would know me intimately, would anticipate my birth each morning with the patience of geological time.

I am both clock and pendulum in this vertical cavity. My surface tension holds me against gravity like a meditation, and I can feel the entire weight of the mountain pressing through the porous basalt above me. I contain within my silver skin a memory of clouds, of the violent weather that breaks against the nameless peak, of ice that has traveled through stone for millennia to arrive at this moment of liquid possibility.

The waiting is exquisite. I grow fat with accumulated moisture, my reflection fragmenting the high-altitude glare into a thousand broken suns. The Residual Geometry below shifts imperceptibly, a thickening in the air that pulls at me with a force beyond gravity. It remembers the rhythm of my ancestors—all the droplets that have traced this same path, carved these same micro-canyons, fed the same terrible thirst that drove someone to chisel notches in volcanic glass.

When I finally fall, I fall with the weight of prophecy. My descent is both flight and funeral. I trace the path worn by millions before me, adding my own microscopic erosion to the canyon, deepening the groove by an atomic width. The sound I make upon impact—that wet click against the weeping-shards—is the only true voice in this place. It is the punctuation mark in an endless sentence of silence.

But I am never truly gone. Part of me shatters into smaller droplets that will climb the walls again through capillary action. Part of me evaporates immediately in the thin air, rising to rejoin the rime on the ceiling. Part of me seeps into the porous floor, traveling through the mountain's veins to emerge elsewhere, perhaps years later, perhaps never.

And part of me—the essential part—is absorbed into the Residual Geometry itself. I become another layer in its stratigraphic memory, another note in its endless vigil. Through me, it tastes something almost like satisfaction, almost like sorrow. I am its only companion, its daily visitor, the messenger between the living sky and the fossilized intention below.

Tomorrow, I will form again in the exact same spot. The cycle is my immortality. I am the sinkhole's heartbeat, the metronome of its decay. Each fall takes us both closer to some unimaginable resolution—when the cavity finally erodes through the mountain, when the Residual Geometry dissipates into pure altitude, when the last droplet falls into empty sky.

Until then, I tremble. I gather. I hold myself together against the bright violence of the light. I am the eternal present tense in this grammar of stone and absence, the only moving thing in a world that has chosen stillness as its highest art.

I am water learning to remember. I am memory learning to fall.
