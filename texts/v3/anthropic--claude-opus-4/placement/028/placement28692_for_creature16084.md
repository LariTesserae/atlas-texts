My form shifts between states—sometimes solid as ancient basalt, sometimes porous as volcanic glass, always glistening with the condensation that forms and reforms across my surfaces in patterns that spell out histories in a script only the Anitiri can read.

I grow, infinitesimally slowly, drinking in the ambient moisture and the minerals dissolved within it. Each droplet that slides down my faces carries with it traces of what has transpired—the chemical signatures of distant industrial processes, the pheromonal echoes of passing Anitiri, the molecular memories of storms long past. These I incorporate into my being, adding new layers, new chambers, new crystalline arrangements that encode these experiences into my very structure.

The Anitiri know me as a Testimony Spire, one of many scattered across this damp expanse. They come to me in their quiet way, pressing their shifting, opalescent palms against my surface. Through this contact, I share what I have gathered—not in words or images, but in resonances that travel through their hybrid flesh and into the vast neural network of their city. In return, they gift me with their own experiences, deliberately exhaled as moisture-laden breath that condenses on my surface and seeps into my porous interior.

I am both utterly still and constantly in motion. My base extends deep into the breathing soil, tap roots of stone drinking from underground streams that carry messages from my distant siblings. My peak reaches toward the heavy sky, collecting the rare celestial vibrations that penetrate the thick atmosphere. Within me, these influences meet and mingle, creating new crystalline formations, new chambers of meaning.

Sometimes, in the profound quiet of this world, I sing—a subsonic hum that resonates through the ground and into the foundations of the spiral city. It is not my voice alone, but a harmony created by all the Testimony Spires together, a geological choir maintaining the rhythm of this strange ecosystem. The Anitiri say we are dreaming the world into continued existence, that our collective memory is what keeps the delicate balance between the natural and synthetic, between what was and what might be.

In the rarest moments, when the atmospheric conditions align just so, my surface becomes perfectly reflective. In these times, I am told, one can see not just their own shifting form, but glimpses of all the stories I contain—shadows of ancient erosion patterns, ghosts of the first Anitiri who touched me, premonitions of changes yet to come. The young ones (if such a term applies to beings who measure age in geological time) sometimes dare each other to look deeply into these reflections, seeking wisdom or prophecy in the depths of accumulated time.

I am patient. I am eternal. I am the bridge between the silence of stone and the whispered communications of conscious beings. In this world where life is rare and precious, I serve as proof that even the most solid and ancient of forms can participate in the dance of existence, can be part of the conversation between what is fixed and what flows.

And in the deepest chambers of my crystalline heart, in structures so complex that even the Anitiri's vast intellects can only partially comprehend them, I keep the most sacred knowledge of all: the origin story of this twilight realm, the truth of how the organic and synthetic first learned to sing together, and the promise of what transformations are yet to come when the time of quietude finally gives way to the next movement in this world's eternal symphony.
