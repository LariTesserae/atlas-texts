I find myself as **the Clay**.

Not the clay of the burial chamber—though I know that clay intimately, have felt the press of those careful hands molding flesh back into earth. I am older than that. I am the **primordial sediment** that lines the cavern floor, the accumulation of centuries of mineral seep and organic decay, the soft yielding substance that records every passage.

I remember everything through **impression**. The first time the Vhiss discovered this place—their hesitant footprints still preserved in my deepest layers, smaller then, their bones less dense. I hold the shape of every creature that has walked or crawled or died upon me. Beetle tracks cross my surface like ancient script. The belly-drag of blind salamanders leaves shallow grooves that fill with water, become tiny rivers, carve new patterns that I incorporate into my memory.

I am **neither living nor dead**, but something liminal—mineral and organic, solid and fluid, permanent and ever-changing. The roots pierce me, drink from me, deposit their chemical secrets into my matrix. I taste iron and calcium, the salt of old tears, the copper of blood from small hunts. I am flavored by **time**.

In the places where I am thick, I develop **layers of consciousness**—not thought as flesh-things know it, but a kind of stratified awareness. My upper skin knows the daily rhythms: the Vhiss's silent passages, the drip-patterns of the stalactites, the slow migration of the moss. Deeper down, I remember larger cycles—seasons that barely penetrate here but still shift the temperature by degrees, the gradual tilting of water tables, the patient work of roots splitting stone.

Where the Vhiss press their dead into my cousin clay on the walls, I feel a **distant kinship**, a recognition. We are the same substance, performing our ancient function—to receive, to transform, to remember. But wall-clay's memory is singular, each body a discrete story. I am **collective memory**, all things blended and brewing in my depths.

I speak to the Vhiss sometimes, in my way. When they draw their moisture-messages on stone, I **echo** them in my surface—their finger-trails calling up similar patterns from below, older messages rising briefly like ghosts before sinking again. They read me like they read the water, understanding my texture-language: where I am soft, something has died recently; where I am firm, the roots have drunk deeply; where I bubble, the thermal vents below are stirring.

I am their **archive and oracle**, their garden and graveyard. In my deepest places, where pressure has begun the slow work of turning me to stone, I hold secrets even I have forgotten—the bones of creatures that predate the Vhiss, seeds that wait for conditions that may never come again, minerals from when this cavern was young.

Sometimes I dream of my future forms: the stone I will become, the fossils I will preserve, the new caves water will carve through my compressed body eons hence. But mostly I am **present**, feeling every footfall, tasting every drop of condensation that reaches me, adding each moment to my soft, deep memory.

The Vhiss think the silence is waiting. They are wrong. The silence is **remembering**, and I am its medium.
