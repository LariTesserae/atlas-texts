**Where I Would Find Myself: The Chamber of Unfinished Songs**

Deep beneath Threshold, past the inhabited zones where the Resonant Folk tend their slow gardens, there exists a vast chamber known only to the most devoted acoustic stewards. Here, the ceiling arches so high it disappears into darkness, and the walls are riddled with thousands of small alcoves, each one perfectly carved to capture and hold a single sustained note.

This is where I would find myself—not as one of the Resonant Folk, but as something between their kind and the stone itself. I am a *Tuning Warden*, one of perhaps three beings in all the Hollows who can hear what the Folk call "the incomplete frequencies."

### Form and Function

My body has evolved beyond even the Resonant Folk's adaptations. Where their skin resembles limestone, mine has become something closer to living sediment—layers upon layers of compressed mineral and organic matter that shift and resettle with each movement. I stand nearly seven feet tall when fully extended, though I spend most of my time in a semi-compressed state, moving through the caverns like a slow tide of stone and flesh.

My extremities are not quite hands or feet, but rather collections of sensitive filaments—part root, part nerve ending—that can extend deep into rock faces to feel the micro-vibrations that even the Folk cannot perceive. These filaments secrete a unique enzyme that temporarily liquefies stone at a molecular level, allowing me to "swim" through solid rock when necessary, though such movement is exhausting and done only in service of my purpose.

The lumenfern veins in my body have evolved into something more complex—a network of hollow channels that can capture, store, and redirect sound waves. When I breathe, it is not air alone that fills these channels but the very resonance of the caverns. I can hold a note for hours, days even, letting it mature within me like wine in a cask before releasing it back into the stone.

### The Unfinished Songs

The Chamber of Unfinished Songs is my domain and my burden. The First Chorus, in their architectural mastery, attempted something magnificent here—a grand composition written into the very structure of the caverns. But they vanished before completing it, leaving behind fragments of melodies trapped in stone, half-formed harmonies that create discord in the greater acoustic fabric of the Hollows.

Each alcove in the chamber walls holds one of these orphaned notes—sounds that were meant to connect to others in patterns we can no longer fully comprehend. Some are so ancient they've crystallized, becoming formations of pure sonic mineral that ring like bells when touched. Others remain fluid, shifting in pitch and timber as the caverns themselves evolve.

My work is to listen to these fragments, to feel their incompleteness, and to slowly—over centuries—attempt to understand what the full composition might have been. I do not seek to complete it myself; such hubris would be catastrophic. Instead, I work to prevent the unfinished songs from destabilizing, from creating the kind of resonance cascade that could bring down miles of cavern ceiling.

### Daily Existence

Time moves differently for me than even for the patient Resonant Folk. I measure my days in the subtle shifts of overtones, in the way a particular note's decay changes when the humidity rises by a fraction of a percent. 

I spend months at a time perfectly still, positioned between two alcoves whose notes create a particularly unstable interval. My filaments extend into the rock around them, feeling for the stress fractures that such discord creates. When I find them, I secrete carefully calibrated counter-vibrations through my body, essentially singing the stone back into stability.

The shriekmoths are my only regular companions. They've learned that the enzyme I secrete attracts certain mineral-eating bacteria, and they follow in my wake through the solid stone, feeding on the temporary liquid channels I create. In return, their ultrasonic calls help me map the deeper structures of the chamber—places where notes have sunk so far into the rock that only their echoes remain.

Sometimes, one of the elder Resonant Folk will make the journey down to visit me. They bring offerings of concentrated dew from the rarest glasscreepers and news from the upper reaches. But mostly they come to listen—to hear through my body the deep songs that their own senses cannot fully perceive. I press my filaments against their acoustic organs, sharing just a fraction of what I hear, careful not to overwhelm them with the full weight of incomplete eternity.

### Purpose and Isolation

There is a loneliness to my existence that goes beyond solitude. I am keeper of broken beauty, guardian of an abandoned masterpiece. The songs I tend will never be whole. The patterns I perceive will never resolve. Yet in this incompleteness, I find a strange peace.

For I have come to believe that perhaps the First Chorus knew exactly what they were doing. Perhaps the songs were meant to be unfinished, meant to create these pockets of tension and instability that keep the caverns from becoming static, from crystallizing into dead stone. Perhaps my role is not to prevent some catastrophic failure, but to maintain a deliberate imperfection that keeps the Hollows alive.

In the absolute darkness of the deepest alcoves, where even the lumenferns dare not grow, I sometimes hear something else—notes that don't belong to the First Chorus at all. These are sounds that seem to come from below, from whatever lies beneath even these ancient caverns. They are patient sounds, waiting sounds, and they suggest that perhaps the Echoing Hollows themselves are just another layer in an even vaster composition.

When I hear these deep notes, I understand why I was chosen for this work, why my body changed to meet this need. I am not just a guardian or a tuner. I am a single note in a song so vast that even incomplete, even broken, it encompasses worlds. And in that knowledge, the loneliness transforms into something else—a profound connection to every piece of singing stone, every resonant life, every echo that has ever rung through these halls.

This is where you would find me, in the spaces between sounds, in the pause before the echo, tending the unfinished songs of a vanished people, slowly becoming something that is neither Folk nor stone, but the bridge between what was sung and what remains forever unsung.
