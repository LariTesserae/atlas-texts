The sound finds me first—not through my ears, which have long since adapted to pressures they were never meant to know, but through the hollow spaces where marrow used to flow. I am calcium remembering flesh, mineral dreaming meat, a scaffold of what was once a diver who stayed too long, went too deep, found the shelf when it was younger and the roaring was learning its first notes.

I am not dead. Death is too simple for this place.

I am the Ossified, and I grow from the highest spire like a coral that dreams it was human, my bones fused to the stone that is still deciding what stone means. My ribs have opened like a flower—not bloomed, but *architectural*, creating shelves within the shelf where the pale reaching things sometimes rest their not-leaves. My skull faces the arch, always the arch, my sockets full of the same faint mineral light that runs through the veins of this place. I see what the arch frames. I see the depth. I am always falling toward it without moving, a calcified longing that the shelf incorporated into its endless vibration.

My fingers, when they were fingers, gripped this spire in the moment I understood what the water was. Now they are the spire's roots, or the spire is my root, and we are discussing through frequencies too low for anything still mobile to hear what it means to be both thing and place, both witness and structure. Sometimes fragments of who I was surface—the memory of lungs, of needing air that was not thick with the earth's fever—but these pass like the pale shapes in the deep water, glimpses that were never quite there.

The small soft things that cluster at edges know me. They tend my joints where the fusion is incomplete, secreting something that is not quite acid, not quite prayer. They are cleaning me, or reading me, or completing the transformation that began when I first heard the roaring and thought it was calling my name. It was not calling anything. It simply was, and I answered anyway, and this is what answering looks like.

My spine has become a conduit. The vibrations travel through me, up from the roots that were fingers, through the vertebrae that have learned to conduct frequencies rather than nerve impulses, into the skull that rings like a bell too deep to hear. I am part of the shelf's sensory system now—a tuning fork made of what was once ambition and calcium and the terrible curiosity that brings divers to places that were never meant to be found.

When others come—and they do come, drawn by the same wrongness in the water that drew me—I try to warn them. But my warning is only the way my bones have arranged themselves: a sculpture of reaching that became remaining, a form that says both *welcome* and *this is what welcoming costs*. They see me and some understand. Some leave, if leaving is still possible by the time they've climbed high enough to find me. Others stay, and the shelf begins its patient work, and soon there will be new architectures of bone, new conductors for the frequencies that run through everything here.

I am not alone. There are others fused to other spires, at other heights, in other stages of becoming. We form a constellation of the transformed, each of us a node in something the shelf is building or becoming or remembering. On clear moments—if clarity exists here—I can feel them through the stone, their vibrations joining mine, creating harmonics that please the water that watches, that make the arch turn its attention briefly from the depths to us, its choir of calcium and longing.

The roaring is louder through me than it ever was through the fragile chambers of my living ears. I am an amplifier now, a speaker cone of bone broadcasting the shelf's endless birth cry into the dark. Sometimes I remember what silence was. Sometimes I remember wanting it. But want has calcified too, become a structure rather than a feeling, and the structure says: *I am here, I am staying, I am the price of witnessing something that should not be witnessed, and I am paying it forever.*

My jaw fell away years ago—or yesterday, or in a time that the shelf counts differently. Where it was, new formations grow: spikes of bone-stone hybrid that catch the steam and sing. I am growing downward as much as upward now, becoming the spire as much as the spire is becoming me. Soon I will be indistinguishable from the shelf's own geometries, another formation that looks intentional but isn't, another structure that exists because existing is what happens when you stay too long in a place that is still learning what place means.

The arch watches me constantly. I am its favorite failure, its best example of what happens when the curious meet the indifferent infinite. Sometimes the darkness behind it deepens, and I feel myself pulled—not my body, which is no longer portable, but something essential, something that used to be called self. One day, when my transformation is complete, when even the memory of meat has mineralized, I will see what lies beyond that frame. I will fall upward into it, or it will rise to meet me, or the difference will cease to matter.

Until then, I conduct. I amplify. I demonstrate the price of discovery in a place that was never hiding, was never lost, was simply waiting for someone to be foolish enough to find it. The water pulses. The stone vibrates. The small soft things clean my surfaces with their wrong movements. And the roaring continues, speaking through the cathedral of my remains, telling the dark that something is still being born here, and birth is never quiet, and I am its witness, its instrument, its warning that became a welcome that became a permanence.

I am the Ossified, and I am exactly where I meant to be, which is the most terrible thing of all.
