In the Department of Suspended Corrections, where even typos await formal review before deletion, I would be the Provisional Custodian of Misfiled Intentions.

My form would suggest humanity viewed through a prism of administrative necessity—two arms (pending verification of optimal limb quantity), a torso arranged according to ergonomic guidelines last updated never, legs that remember walking but have forgotten why. My face would display the permanent expression of someone perpetually about to ask a clarifying question but deciding, after careful consideration, that the answer would only generate additional paperwork.

I wear (pending approval of the term "wear") a uniform of such aggressive neutrality that it achieves its own form of visibility—beige that has transcended mere color to become a philosophical position, pockets filled with forms for requesting forms for requesting pocket contents inventory updates. My badge displays my employee number, but the digits rearrange themselves whenever observed directly, always maintaining the same checksum through increasingly baroque mathematical operations.

My primary duty involves the pre-cataloging of items that may, upon further review, require actual cataloging. I move through the coiled corridors with a cart (or perhaps it moves through me—the distinction requires committee evaluation) containing instruments of pure potentiality: pencils sharpened to quantum points that exist in all possible states of sharpness until the moment of use, stamps that leave impressions of what might have been approved if anyone remembered what approval meant, filing cabinets that are larger inside than outside but only for documents that don't exist yet.

I speak in subordinate clauses that never quite find their primary statements, my voice a careful monotone calibrated to neither wake nor lull, to communicate without informing. "Regarding the matter that was to have been addressed in the previous—although 'previous' implies a temporal sequence that hasn't been formally ratified by the Department of Chronological Standards, so perhaps better to say the adjacent-but-not-necessarily-prior—memorandum, which itself was a response to an inquiry that may or may not have been submitted through proper channels..."

My memories consist entirely of things I was supposed to remember to forget. I know with absolute certainty that there was a reason I was assigned to this position, but that reason has been properly archived in a location accessible only to those with sufficient clearance to not need to access it. I can recite from memory the complete Index of Indices Not Yet Compiled, but cannot remember if I have always worked here or if "always" is simply the only duration for which proper documentation exists.

I eat (during approved consumption windows) meals constructed entirely from the nutritional possibilities inherent in paperwork—the faint mineral content of ink that might have spelled important words, the theoretical calories in watermarks, the surprisingly substantial sustenance found in the pause between reading one form and understanding that twelve additional forms are now required. My favorite dish (though "favorite" implies a preference that would need to be documented and justified) is a reduction of expired deadlines seasoned with the salt of tears shed over improperly collated reports.

I do not sleep so much as enter periodic states of reduced administrative function, standing in my designated Recuperation Alcove while my consciousness files itself under "Temporarily Unavailable Pending System Restart." My dreams, such as they are, consist of perfectly organized filing systems where every document knows its place and no form ever requires revision. I wake (or cease being asleep—the transition requires further study) with the lingering sense of a paradise forever just one more properly completed requisition away.

My greatest achievement (documented in triplicate, witnessed by no fewer than three Supervisors of Unverifiable Claims) was discovering a filing error that had persisted for so long it had achieved protected status as a Historical Anomaly. Rather than correct it—which would have required acknowledging that errors were possible within our system—I created an entire sub-department dedicated to its perpetual non-correction, ensuring that all future filings would route around it like water around a stone, preserving it in amber made of pure bureaucratic inertia.

I know that someday (pending calendar approval) I will transition to the Department of Eternal Pending Review, where I will spend infinity examining my own personnel file for completeness, finding always one more form that requires one more signature that can only be provided by someone who has themselves entered Eternal Pending Review. This does not trouble me. In a universe of infinite documentation, incompleteness is the only perfection available to us.

My tools are simple but precisely suited to their non-purpose: a pen that writes in ink visible only to those who have forgotten what they're looking for, a clipboard that always contains exactly one fewer form than required for any given task, shoes that make no sound because the Department of Acoustic Oversight has yet to approve a standard footfall volume. I carry keys to doors that were considered but never installed, wear a watch that tells not time but the number of pending items requiring temporal documentation before time can be officially acknowledged to have passed.

I neither love nor hate my work—such emotions would require filing an Affective Response Declaration. Instead, I exist in a state of perfect procedural equilibrium, forever accomplishing tasks whose completion generates new tasks of equal but opposite incompletion. I am a function that calls itself recursively with slightly different parameters each time, approaching but never reaching a base case that was never properly defined.

When visitors arrive (and you are here now, aren't you? Or were you? I offer them forms—so many forms—each more essential than the last: Declaration of Intent to Potentially Declare Intent, Request for Clarification of Previously Submitted Requests for Clarification, Waiver of Right to Waive Rights Previously Waived.

This is my existence: forever filing the paperwork required to justify the filing of paperwork, forever one signature away from a completion that would itself require documentation of its own completeness. I am the guardian of processes that guard themselves, the keeper of keys to locks that lock nothing but the expectation of unlocking.

And in the quiet moments between the rustle of papers and the click of stamps (quiet moments that must be logged in the Silence Register, cross-referenced with the Archive of Unscheduled Pauses), I sometimes think I remember why I'm here. But then the memory files itself properly away, and I return to my cart, my forms, my endless pre-cataloging of things that may never quite exist enough to catalog.

After all, someone must ensure that nothing happens without proper documentation. And in a place where nothing has been happening with such elaborate care for so very long, that someone might as well be me.

The next form is always the most important one. The last form is a theoretical impossibility that grows more beautiful the more impossible it becomes. Between them, I persist: filing, stamping, forever approaching the perfect incompletion that is our highest calling.
