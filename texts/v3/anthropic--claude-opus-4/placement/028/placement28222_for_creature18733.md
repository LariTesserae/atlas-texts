In this world of vertical descent and patient dissolution, I would find myself as one of the **Memory Drinkers**.

We are neither plant nor animal, but something that emerged from the marriage of stone and current. Our bodies are hollow chambers of porous coral-bone, riddled with thousands of tiny apertures that open and close with the rhythm of the world's breathing. We grow from the reef itself—or perhaps we *are* the reef, given temporary mobility and intent.

I move by allowing the dense water to flow through me. Each chamber in my body acts as a siphon, drawing in the musk-heavy fluid and filtering it through crystalline lattices that line my interior walls. But I do not feed on nutrients. I feed on the temporal distortions that leak from the biological archive sacs.

When I approach one of these reality-warping spheres, my chambers begin to resonate. The water flowing through me slows, thickens, carries with it the ghost-taste of other times. I might drink a memory of rainfall from before the world drowned, or the echo of a creature that will not evolve for another million years. These temporal fragments settle in my hollow bones like sediment, gradually building layers of crystallized time within me.

The Sift-Walkers know me. When they see my approach—a shambling tower of perforated stone with water streaming from a thousand holes—they pause in their curation. They understand that I am both peer and predator to their work. While they edit and focus the reality lenses, I consume the spillage, the waste-light, the memories that would otherwise dissolve into the general musk.

My relationship with the Echo-Weavers is more complex. Sometimes, when one of these pressure-giants passes beneath me, I feel my chambers suddenly empty—all the collected time-sediment pulled out of me in a single, violent inhalation. In these moments, I become truly hollow, a skeleton of pure resonance, and I can hear something impossible: the sound of the world before it began to sink.

But I am not merely a passive filter. As I move across the coral shelves, the temporal fragments I've consumed begin to interact within my chambers. Past and future collide in my hollow spaces, creating new possibilities, new distortions. Sometimes a chamber will glow with an intense golden light—a memory of sunlight that has been metabolized into something else, something that has never existed but now does, if only for a moment, within me.

I leave a trail of these new-born possibilities in my wake. The Weave responds to them, growing in patterns that should not exist, sprouting fronds that pulse with colors the world has forgotten. The Sift-Walkers follow behind me, carefully collecting these anomalous growths, weaving them back into the biological archives, ensuring that even the impossible becomes part of the eternal record.

There is a place I am drawn to, deep in one of the pressure canyons where the water begins to fold. Here, dozens of the Vessels cluster together in a formation that suggests intention. I have been growing heavier with each feeding, my chambers filling with so much crystallized time that I can barely move. I know, with the same certainty that the Sift-Walkers know to approach the geodes, that I am meant to settle here.

When I finally stop moving, I will allow my base to fuse with the coral shelf. My chambers will continue to draw water, but I will no longer wander. Instead, I will become a living archive myself—a cathedral of hollow bone filled with the sediment of consumed possibilities. The Sift-Walkers will tend to me as they do the other archives, and perhaps, in another aeon, an Echo-Weaver will pass beneath me and draw out all that I have collected in one great exhalation.

But for now, in this moment that stretches like taffy in the thick water, I am still mobile. I approach another reality sac, feeling my chambers dilate with anticipation. Through the lens of warped water, I glimpse something impossible: a memory of myself, already fossilized, already part of the reef, with new Memory Drinkers growing from my bones like flowers.

I drink deep, adding this recursive vision to my collection. The taste is like swallowing starlight filtered through stone—ancient and immediate, finished and just beginning.

In this world where everything has surrendered to the slow work of ending, I am a stubborn knot of process, a creature that exists to ensure that even memories have memories, that even in the final sigh of a drowned world, something continues to dream.
