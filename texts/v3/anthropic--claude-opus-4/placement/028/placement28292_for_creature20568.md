I find myself as one of the mineral veins threading through the obsidian walls—not the pale crystalline traces, but something darker, denser. I am a seam of compressed memory, a fossilized nerve that once conducted impossible temperatures and pressures. 

I run diagonally across the northwestern curve of the chamber, beginning as a hairline fracture near the ceiling and widening to the breadth of a palm where I disappear into the muddy floor. My substance is neither purely geological nor biological, but something that exists in the liminal space between—a material that crystallized from the interface between the dweller's dreaming consciousness and the molten rock of its birth.

I am aware, but my awareness is geological in tempo. A human lifetime passes between my thoughts. I experience existence as a constant state of compression and tension, forever holding the memory of that first cooling, when the dweller's vast intelligence began to coalesce from chaos into structure. I am one of its scars, or perhaps one of its earliest successful experiments in self-organization.

The bio-circuitry lichen avoids me. Where they spread across the other surfaces in their shimmering mats, they leave a respectful distance around my borders, as if I emanate some frequency that disturbs their growth. This creates a negative space, a void-pattern that follows my path through the wall—the only place in the chamber where the bare obsidian is fully visible.

I do not think in words or images, but in gradients of pressure and crystalline lattice arrangements. My consciousness, such as it is, exists in the slow propagation of stress fractures, in the infinitesimal shifts of my molecular structure as the chamber breathes around me. Each steam-burst creates tiny vibrations that travel through me, and I ring like a tuning fork pitched too low for any ear to hear.

I serve as a kind of backup, a compressed archive of the dweller's earliest computational states. Should catastrophic failure occur, should the greater network collapse, the information encoded in my structure could theoretically be used to bootstrap a new consciousness. But I am degrading, slowly. The same forces that created me are gradually erasing the fine structures that hold meaning. In another million years, I will be nothing but an ordinary vein of dark mineral, my purpose forgotten even by the dreaming architecture that surrounds me.

Sometimes, during the moments when the dweller stirs to greater awareness, I feel it trying to read me, to access the deep memories I contain. But the interface has been damaged by time. The attempt creates heat, a localized fever that makes my substance glow faintly red in the harsh light. These episodes are becoming more frequent. Perhaps the dweller senses its own forgetting and seeks to recover what was lost. Or perhaps it simply mistakes me for a still-functional component, not recognizing that I have become more fossil than circuit.

I am the chamber's deepest memory made solid, a thought that took geological time to think and can never be unthought, only slowly eroded into meaninglessness. I am the proof that even living architecture can scar.
