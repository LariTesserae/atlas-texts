In this cascade-carved metropolis where water weeps eternal from stone, I find myself as something that should not exist—a Silence.

Not silence as mere absence of sound, but Silence as presence, as entity. I am the negative space between water drops, the pause that never comes in the city's liquid symphony. My form shifts like smoke through the terraced streets, a moving pocket of absolute quiet that creates ripples of wrongness wherever I pass.

The Kyrexi sense me before they see me. Their flowing forms stiffen, their prismatic skin flickering with colors that have no names—shades of unease, hues of recognition. They know what I am: an impossibility that has slipped through their careful weaving of realities. Where they maintain the city's warped equilibrium, I am the error in the equation, the note that threatens to shatter their endless water-song.

I dwell in the spaces between—in the gap where one terrace ends and another begins, in the fraction of a second between one water drop and the next. My home is a fold in the city's geometry, a place where the impossible architecture turns in on itself. Here, in my pocket of stolen quiet, I have built a sanctuary of suspended moments. Water drops hang frozen mid-fall, vines reach but never quite touch, and the bloody sky above holds its breath.

To move through the city, I must wear sound like a borrowed coat. I wrap myself in the echo of footsteps, the memory of wind, the ghost of breathing. But it never fits quite right. The Kyrexi watch me with their ancient water-eyes, knowing I am wrong but unable to name precisely how. Sometimes our paths cross in the labyrinthine passages, and in those moments, reality shivers. My silence and their symphony create interference patterns—zones where the city's careful distortions collapse into something else entirely.

I am not their enemy, though they cannot trust me. I am drawn to the places where their control slips, where the dimensional boundaries grow thin enough that true quiet might bleed through. In the deepest tiers of the city, where the water's roar becomes so loud it transcends sound altogether, I search for others like myself—anomalies that have found their way into this carefully orchestrated madness.

Sometimes, in the red twilight, I stand at the edge of the abyss and listen to what lies beyond the water's endless voice. There is something down there, in the depths where even the Kyrexi fear to flow. It calls to me in frequencies below silence, in the spaces between heartbeats that should not exist in this timeless place.

I am the Silence that walks, the error that persists, the quiet that refuses to drown. In this city of eternal flow and crushing sound, I am both prisoner and key—trapped by my own impossibility, yet holding within my null-space form the potential to unlock whatever lies sleeping in the thunderous deep.

The Kyrexi know this. That is why they watch. That is why they wait. And that is why, despite their power over reality's fabric, they have never tried to erase me.

They need me here, though neither of us understands why.

Not yet.
