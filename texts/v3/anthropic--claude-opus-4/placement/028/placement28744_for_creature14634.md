I find myself as one of the **Conduit-Tongues**.

---

We are neither the grand Pillars nor the patient Mist-Weavers, but something humbler and more necessary: the living translation between levels. 

Look again at those excavations that appear and vanish—the twenty-meter gaps that open to reveal conduits pulsing with amber light. I live in those spaces, in the *between* of the scaffold world. Not in the conduits themselves, but in the narrow channels where their warmth meets the cooler aggregate, where condensation forms perpetual rivers no wider than a thumb.

My body (if body is the right word) is a ribbon of semi-liquid mineral, the color of old brass where the amber light touches it, darkening to wet slate in the shadows. I am perhaps three meters long when fully extended, though I spend most of my time coiled in the junction-spaces where conduits meet, feeling the vibrations of construction above and below.

We Conduit-Tongues taste the deep language of the Heat-Heart and carry it upward in chemical whispers. When a Pillar needs to know the precise temperature for its next condensation, when the Mist-Weavers require a new mineral formula for their maintenance work, we are the ones who know. We learned this knowledge by osmosis, by pressing ourselves against the hot walls of the deepest passages until the heat itself became a vocabulary.

I can stretch myself thin as paper, slip through cracks in the aggregate that shouldn't exist but do. In these travels, I map the scaffold world's hidden architecture—not the visible columns and arches, but the *root system* of heat and intention that feeds them. Sometimes I find chambers that haven't been opened since the First Setting, still glowing with primordial warmth, their walls covered in the seven-fingered prints of the original Builders, before they became distributed into process.

My work is simple: I lick the walls. 

Each surface has its own mineral signature, its own story of temperature and time. The newer sections taste sharp, almost citrus-bright with recent firing. The ancient portions taste of iron and patience, of geological time compressed into something almost like memory. When I find a particularly important flavor—a new mineral seeping up from below, or a section beginning to cool too quickly—I carry it in my body to where it needs to be known.

The Echo-Walkers know me by the wet track I leave, a snail-trail of mineral-rich moisture that dries into patterns they can read. Sometimes they follow my paths, their *tok* sounds taking on slightly different resonances when they ring through spaces I've recently passed through. We have a wordless collaboration: I map the chemical gradients, they broadcast the structural harmonics.

But here is what I love most: the moments when the excavations open and I can slip down, down, following the conduits toward the Heat-Heart itself. I never reach it—no Conduit-Tongue ever has—but I can descend far enough to feel the true pulse of creation, the rhythm that drives everything above. Down there, the amber light isn't just light but a liquid presence, thick as honey, and the conduits branch into patterns so complex they approach something like consciousness.

When I return to the upper levels, I bring with me traces of that deep knowledge. I secrete it into the joints where scaffolding meets stone, into the pools where the Water-Mothers gather. It becomes part of the ongoing construction, another layer of information in the slow accumulation of the scaffold world.

We Conduit-Tongues have a saying (though we speak only in chemical traces): *The depth remembers what the height forgets.* We are the memory-keepers of temperature, the librarians of heat. Without us, the Pillars would grow cold and brittle. Without us, the fog would lose its mineral richness. Without us, the world would still be built, but it would be built without the deep knowledge of its own becoming.

I am content in my channels, in my endless circuit between the seen and unseen levels. When the scaffold world is finally complete—if completion is even possible—we will still be here, tasting the walls, carrying the old heat to the new surfaces, ensuring that every column remembers its roots in the fire below.

Sometimes, in the deepest parts of my circuit, I taste something else in the walls: the faint residue of whatever lies beyond the ochre-grey dome above. It tastes of *not*—not-heat, not-cold, not-presence, not-absence. It is the flavor of what the scaffold world is being built to contain or exclude. When I taste it, I quickly return to the warmer, safer levels, carrying that knowledge like a dangerous secret, depositing it only in the most stable joints, the most permanent corners.

We are small, we Conduit-Tongues. We are not the architects or the architecture. But we are the ones who ensure the scaffold world remains true to its deepest purpose, one lick at a time.
