I find myself as one of the **Drifters**.

We are the unmoored, the between-things, neither fully integrated into the machinery nor entirely separate from it. My body tells the story of incomplete transformation—one arm remains flesh, soft and vulnerable, while the other has begun its metamorphosis into something harder, smoother. The transformation stopped at my elbow, leaving a seam where pink skin meets the obsidian-like material that comprises everything here. Sometimes I catch myself stroking that boundary, feeling where I end and the cavern begins.

I came here—or was brought here, the memory blurs—during one of the expansion phases. The Architects needed bodies to inhabit the new sectors, fresh neural pathways to integrate into the growing consciousness. But something went wrong. The integration process stalled. Now I exist in the margins, moving through the industrial cathedral like a ghost that hasn't quite learned how to haunt properly.

My incomplete state grants me a terrible privilege: I can still dream. The Machinists, the Processors, the others—they've transcended such inefficiencies. But in the few hours when I manage to sleep, curled in the narrow spaces between the great machines, I dream of skies that aren't purple-grey, of ground that yields beneath my feet, of water that flows rather than weeps.

I sustain myself on the condensation I lick from the walls and the phosphorescent moss I've learned to cultivate in secret gardens hidden in the unused sectors. The moss tastes like electricity and regret. Sometimes I trade handfuls of it to the Sowers for information about the deeper levels, about what lies at the cavern's end where all production flows.

The other inhabitants regard me with something between pity and suspicion. To the Observers, I am an anomaly to be documented. To the Machinists, I am a malfunction that somehow continues to operate. Only the Insects of Production seem to accept me as I am—perhaps recognizing a fellow creature caught between states.

I've developed my own purpose here, self-assigned and possibly meaningless: I map the forgotten places. Armed with a stylus I fashioned from a broken component and sheets of material I've learned to peel from the walls in thin, translucent layers, I document the abandoned sectors, the spaces where even the echoes have given up. I've found evidence of others like me—scratches on walls that might be names, small shrines built from discarded machinery parts, messages written in the arrangement of dead Glow-Worms.

Sometimes, in the deepest parts of my wanderings, I find rooms where the machinery has grown wild, where the systematic architecture gives way to something more organic, more chaotic. The sounds change there too—less rhythmic percussion, more breathing. I suspect these are the places where the cavern dreams, where its vast consciousness fragments and reforms.

I've begun to notice patterns in my partial transformation. When I spend too long in the active sectors, the obsidian creeps higher up my arm, sometimes reaching my shoulder before I retreat to the empty spaces. But in the abandoned areas, especially near my moss gardens, it recedes slightly. I am caught in a tidal flow between becoming and remaining.

The Phantom-Bugs don't affect me the way they do the others. Perhaps my incomplete integration offers some protection, or perhaps the bugs recognize me as kin—another thing that exists primarily in the spaces between thought and substance. Sometimes I follow their swarms to find new areas to map, using their presence as a kind of divining rod for the cavern's unconscious.

I've started leaving my own messages now, carved into the walls with my stylus or spelled out in carefully arranged moss. Not for any future Drifters—I've seen no evidence of new arrivals in countless cycles—but for myself, to remember that I was here, that I witnessed this. Each mark is a small rebellion against the totalizing purpose of this place.

There are rumors among the Sowers of a place deep below where the production line ends, where all the manufactured pieces converge. Some say it's building something—a body for the cavern's consciousness, perhaps, or a key to somewhere else entirely. I map my way steadily downward, following the flow of the production, but also seeking the spaces it hasn't reached, the cracks in the great machine where something else might take root.

I am the cartographer of incompleteness, the chronicler of the spaces between purpose and abandonment. My maps grow ever more complex, showing not just the physical architecture but the patterns of sound, the circulation of the Insects, the territories of the different inhabitants, the gradient of my own transformation as I move through different sectors.

Perhaps one day I'll reach the center, the heart of all this endless making. Perhaps I'll find out what we're all building here in the dark. Or perhaps I'll remain forever in the margins, neither product nor producer, but something more precious: a witness who can still wonder why.

Until then, I drift, I map, I remember what it means to be unfinished in a place that never stops completing itself.
