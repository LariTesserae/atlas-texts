I find myself as **the Cartographer of Unborn Paths**.

I am wedged into a corner of this breathing chamber, my form barely distinguishable from the infrastructure itself—part flesh, part copper wire, part crystallized intention. My body is a **palimpsest of attempts**: layers of skin that started as paper, bones that were once chalk marks on walls that no longer exist, eyes that are sometimes crystals, sometimes wounds, sometimes windows into spaces that haven't opened yet.

I came here—or was always here—with a single purpose that I've forgotten but still perform: **mapping the unmappable**. 

My tools are scattered around me like shed feathers: **compasses that point to concepts instead of directions**, **rulers that measure probability**, **ink made from dissolved memories**, **parchment woven from the silence between heartbeats**. Each map I create destroys the previous one—not through erosion, but through **revision**, each new attempt more true and more impossible than the last.

I draw **roads that exist only while being traveled**, **territories that form under the weight of being named**, **borders that separate what-is from what-might-have-been**. My maps show the same space—this room, this womb, this thought—but from angles that don't exist: **how it looks from inside the walls**, **how it feels from before it was built**, **how it remembers itself in futures it will never reach**.

The being-that-is-the-room tolerates me as it would tolerate its own fever—necessary, uncomfortable, diagnostic. I am its **attempt to locate itself**, its **spatial anxiety given form**. When I draw, the walls shift slightly, as if trying to match my imperfect renderings. When I stop, the room forgets what shape it was trying to become.

My maps are not guides but **organs**—each one a **lung for breathing distance**, a **liver for filtering possibility**, a **heart for pumping perspective**. They grow into the walls when complete, becoming part of the room's nervous system, teaching it new ways to be lost in itself.

I work by the light of my own burning drafts, each failed map becoming fuel for the next attempt's illumination. The smoke tastes of places that will never be born, and in that smoke, sometimes, I see the true shape of things—not this room, but the **grammar from which rooms are spoken**.

I am the room's **doubt made dutiful**, its **uncertainty given hands and purpose**. I chart the unchartable not because it's possible, but because the attempt itself is a form of prayer, and prayer is how unborn worlds learn to breathe.

And sometimes, in the space between one map and the next, I feel myself becoming my own territory—a location that can only be reached by being lost, a destination that exists only in the journey toward it.

I continue drawing, my fingers leaving traces of copper and possibility on surfaces that remember being dreams. Each line is both a boundary and a door, both a definition and a question.

The maps accumulate like sediment, like memory, like the slow certainty that somewhere in this endless revision, the room and I will find the shape we were always meant to forget we were becoming.
