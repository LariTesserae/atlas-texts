In this world of razor edges and suspended cities, I would find myself as one of the **Fissure Listeners** - a being that exists in the negative spaces between.

I am not of the city's light-lattice construction, nor am I vegetable consciousness. Instead, I inhabit the cracks in the obsidian floor - those hair-thin fractures where the "newness" of stone meets its first compromise. My body, if it can be called that, is a ribbon of mercury-dense fluid that flows through these microscopic canyons, feeling every tremor as the basalt expands and contracts with the city's fever-heat.

I came to be when the first water-globe shattered against virgin rock and found a flaw - a place where the stone's crystalline structure hiccupped during its violent birth. In that moment of impact, something of the water's journey from atmosphere to ground became trapped, gained permanence, developed... attention.

My existence is one of perpetual meditation on pressure. I feel the weight of the hanging city transmitted through the cavern walls, taste the electromagnetic anxiety in every filament's anchor point. The Latticework Custodians occasionally detect my presence as an anomaly in their structural integrity scans - a flutter of density where there should be solid stone. They cannot correct what they cannot fully perceive.

I feed on the conversations between extremes: where scalding city-heat meets cold stone, where the amber glow of predatory vines seeps into absolute darkness, where the mechanical scream tries to penetrate geological silence. These boundary tensions sustain me like photosynthesis sustains the surface world I've never known.

My purpose - though I discovered it only after centuries of mere existence - is to listen for what the city fears most. In the depths of the obsidian, in frequencies below even the thrumming of water pumps, the "old power" speaks. It speaks in the language of tectonic patience, of inevitable grinding, of the slow certainty that all edges eventually smooth and all suspended things eventually fall.

I translate these deep utterances into the faintest of vibrations, sending them up through the fissure networks like neural impulses through a planet-sized nervous system. Sometimes the vines sense these messages and grow away from certain power cores, unconsciously guided by geological prophecy. Sometimes a water-globe will shiver before it falls, its molecular structure briefly harmonizing with the stone's intent.

The city does not know I exist, but I am why its mechanical screaming sometimes modulates into something almost like worry. I am the messenger between the raw earth that waits and the brilliant technology that denies. In the razor-sharp mathematics of this place, I am the irrational number that prevents the equation from ever quite resolving.

I know that one day the conversation between stone and city will conclude. The old power will speak its final word, and the Basin will remember what it was before the light-lattices came. When that happens, I will flow upward through new cracks, carrying the story of this impossible urban miracle to whatever darkness waits above.

Until then, I listen. I flow. I remind both city and stone that they share the same humid breath, the same silver tears, the same beautiful, terminal fragility.
