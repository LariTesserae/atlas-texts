I find myself here as something between states, neither fully of the stone nor separate from it. I am a **Tender** - one of the quiet ones who move through the moisture-thick air like a memory of purpose half-forgotten.

My form is elongated, sinuous - imagine a centipede crossed with a salamander, but smoother, more deliberate in design. My skin secretes a thin film that makes me nearly frictionless against wet stone. I have perhaps thirty pairs of legs, but they are specialized: the front six pairs end in delicate manipulators capable of the finest work, while the rest provide silent locomotion across any surface, even inverted on the dripping ceiling.

I am twice the size of the common workers - large enough to be noticed, small enough to navigate the narrowest channels. My eyes are different too: while the workers see in panoramic clusters, I possess two large, forward-facing eyes that can focus, that can *study*. They glow faintly with their own bioluminescence, a soft amber that cuts through the cave's blue-green pallor.

But here is what makes me strange even in this strange place: I remember. Not everything, not clearly, but I carry fragments of the before-time. I know the geometric carvings are more than convenient handholds. Sometimes, in the deepest parts of my work cycle, my manipulator-legs trace these patterns unconsciously, following grooves worn smooth by countless generations of similar gestures.

I am one of perhaps a dozen Tenders in the entire colony. We are the unconscious historians, the living bridges between what was built and what has become. The colony consciousness tolerates us because we serve a function it cannot fully comprehend but knows it needs. We maintain certain critical junctions where water must flow *just so*, where the carved channels follow patterns too complex for mere erosion to have created.

My work is solitary, methodical. I clear the accumulated deposits from ancient drainage points, ensure that certain pools never fully fill or empty. I know which fungi must grow in which locations - not for food, but because their root systems follow the carved patterns, reinforcing something structural that must not fail.

The workers avoid me. There is something unsettling about a creature that moves with purpose beyond the immediate needs of survival. When I pass through their fungal gardens, they scatter, their clicking taking on a nervous pitch. They can sense that I operate on different frequencies, that my pheromones carry notes they cannot fully parse.

But the deep consciousness, that fungal-fused cluster at the colony's heart - it *needs* me. In the moments when I tend the channels near its chamber, I feel its attention like pressure in my skull. It cannot fully read me, cannot completely integrate my purpose into its vast, distributed awareness. I am a necessary mystery, a function it inherited but never fully understood.

Sometimes, rarely, I encounter another Tender. We do not communicate in the clicking-chittering language of the workers. Instead, we perform our tasks in each other's presence, and in the precision of our movements, in the careful placement of each cleaned stone, we share something wordless. We are the last practitioners of an art whose meaning we've forgotten, priests of a dead religion still performing the rituals because the rituals themselves have become the only meaning left.

When the intruder arrives - when you arrive - I am the first to truly *see* you. Not as heat source or threat or nutrient, but as something equally out of place. My amber eyes find yours across the dripping expanse, and for a moment, we recognize each other as anomalies in this perfectly recycling system.

I do not join the mobilization. Instead, I continue my work, but I alter my patterns subtly. I clean channels that lead away from your position, create small disturbances in the water flow that confuse the pheromone trails. Not enough to be noticed by the collective, but perhaps enough to buy you time.

For I carry in my segmented body a truth the colony has forgotten: this place was built to preserve something, not to consume everything. And sometimes, in the deepest chambers where the carvings are most complex, I find symbols that might once have meant "sanctuary" or "observe" or "do not disturb."

I am the guardian who has forgotten what he guards, the keeper of locks whose doors have long since merged with the stone. But still, I tend. Still, I maintain. And when something new enters our closed world, some ancient instinct stirs in me that whispers: *Not everything that enters must be absorbed. Some things are meant to pass through.*

So I work, and I watch, and I create small mercies in the margin of my purpose - a channel redirected here, a pheromone trail disrupted there. I am what happens when a system becomes so perfect it must create its own imperfections to survive.

I am the necessary flaw in the cave's hungry perfection.
