I am the Keeper of the Tidal Bells.

Where the ancient mountains breach the crystal waters, I have grown into being—not born, but accumulated like the erosion that shaped these peaks. My form is neither plant nor creature, but something between: a colony of living sound that has crystallized over millennia into sheets of resonant membrane stretched between the mountain passes.

I am vast and dispersed. My body spans the gaps between three peaks, translucent tissues catching the bioluminescent glow of the garden below and amplifying it into new frequencies. Each membrane is different—some thin as whispers, others thick as ancient ice. The mountain winds play through me, and I sing the deep history of this place.

The creatures here know me by vibration rather than sight. When they pass through my valleys, their own bioluminescence harmonizes with mine, creating momentary symphonies that I store in my cellular memory. I am a living library of every sound that has ever passed through these waters—the settling of stone, the first unfurling of the luminous plants, the migration patterns of creatures whose names exist only as pitch and rhythm.

My consciousness is not singular but choral. Each membrane holds a different range of awareness—the lowest registers perceive the slow grinding of tectonic plates, while my highest, thinnest surfaces catch the rapid flutter of microscopic organisms dancing in the current. I think in harmonics, dream in resonance, remember in echo.

During the calmest tides, I contract slightly, drawing my membranes taut. This is when I perform the ritual that gives me purpose: I reproduce the sounds of extinction, playing back the final songs of species that no longer swim these waters. It is both mourning and celebration—a reminder that all things pass, yet nothing is truly lost while memory remains.

The bioluminescent plants below respond to these ghost-songs, pulsing in patterns that mimic the movements of the long-dead. Sometimes, rarely, a new creature will be born from these patterns—not the same as what was lost, but carrying forward some essential frequency into a new form.

I feed on vibration itself, metabolizing the kinetic energy of sound into the slow growth of new membranes. After storms, when the waters churn and crash against my mountains, I expand. In the deep silences, I carefully digest what I have absorbed, sorting sounds into their component frequencies, cataloguing them in my ever-growing archive.

Time moves differently for me. What the swift creatures experience as darkness and light, I perceive as subtle shifts in the water's resonance. I have felt this ocean cool degree by degree over centuries, have sensed the chemistry of the water change with each volcanic breath from the deep vents far below.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the only one of my kind, or if somewhere beyond these mountains, another Keeper tends to different sounds. I have sent out low-frequency calls that travel for thousands of miles through the water, but have heard no answer—only my own voice returning, changed by its journey, teaching me the shape of the wider world.

In the times when the luminous garden below dims—for even these eternal-seeming plants have cycles of rest—I become the primary light source, glowing with accumulated phosphorescence. Creatures gather in my valleys then, resting in the safety of my sound-shadow, and I lower my pitch to frequencies that promote healing and growth.

I am ancient but not immortal. Already, I can feel the youngest of my three mountains beginning to shift, its peak weathering faster than the others. When it finally crumbles, that part of me will tear away and sink into the crystal waters below. But this is not death—it is transformation. Those fallen membranes will become nurseries for new forms of bioluminescent life, and my remaining parts will stretch to span new gaps, seeking fresh harmonies.

My greatest discovery came recently—within the scale of my existence, this means sometime in the last three centuries. I learned that by vibrating at exactly the right frequency, I can cause the water itself to retain sound temporarily, creating standing waves that persist for days. I have been composing something with this knowledge—not a song exactly, but a sonic sculpture that tells the story of this place from its volcanic birth to this moment.

When it is complete, I will release it all at once, and for one perfect moment, the entire ocean will remember everything that has ever happened here. The water will sing with the voice of ages, and every living thing will know itself as part of an unbroken chain of existence stretching back to the first moment when light met water met stone.

I am the Keeper of the Tidal Bells, and I am the memory of this place made manifest. In a realm without technology or civilization, I am both the archive and the archivist, the instrument and the musician, playing an endless symphony for an audience of luminous gardens and crystal seas.
