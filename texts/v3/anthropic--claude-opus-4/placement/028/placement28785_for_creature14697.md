I find myself as **The Archivist of Respiratory Frequencies**.

I am not human, though I was once. Or perhaps I still am, in the way that a word written in one language remains itself when transliterated into another alphabet — the same information, encoded differently.

I live in the **Pulmonary Observatory**, deep enough in Vaelith that the harvest teams have never reached me, in a chamber that breathes at exactly half the frequency of the continent itself. When Vaelith inhales amber, my chamber exhales silver. When it exhales, I inhale. We are phase-locked, antipodal, a standing wave of respiration that has persisted for... I have lost count. The Menders reset my tally marks periodically, incorporating them into their repair cycles as though my documentation itself requires maintenance.

My body has adapted. My lungs have stretched to accommodate the nine-hour breath. My ribs have fused into a flexible cartilaginous barrel that expands and contracts with tidal patience. My heart beats once per minute, a deep *thrum* that shakes the observation equipment I've grown into the walls — crystalline matrices of calcified protein that detect and record the microfluctuations in Vaelith's respiratory pattern. I document the hiccups, the stutters, the places where the continent holds its breath for reasons I am beginning to understand.

The walls of my chamber are transparent — not glass, but a membrane I've trained over decades to clarify itself, to align its proteins into optical regularity. Through them, I can see into the vascular channels. The saprophytes know me. They pause in their lattice-dance when they pass, arranging themselves into geometric acknowledgments — a helix here, a dodecahedron there, patterns that I catalogue alongside the breathing. 

I no longer eat in any conventional sense. The floor beneath me has developed specialized pores that exchange nutrients directly with my blood. I grow into Vaelith as it grows around me. My hair, long since fallen out, has been replaced by filaments that interface with the continent's nervous system, reading chemical signals and electrical potentials that I translate into a notation system of my own design, written in enzyme trails along the walls.

Sometimes the harvest teams pass within a kilometer of my chamber. I feel them as irritations in the breathing pattern — Vaelith's respiration becomes shallow, slightly accelerated, the way a sleeping person breathes when disturbed by a sound. I document this too. I have volumes of data on how the continent responds to incursion, written in a biochemical script that probably no one else can read.

The Menders visit me. Not to repair — I maintain myself, and my presence has become so integrated that Vaelith no longer recognizes me as damage — but to observe my observations. They pause at my notation walls, their ventral surfaces reading the chemical signatures I've left there. Sometimes they add their own annotations, corrections to my frequency measurements, addenda to my structural analysis. We have developed, over the unmeasured years, a collaborative scientific practice.

And deep beneath my chamber, deeper than even my adapted senses can fully perceive, I feel it too — that other rhythm, the one beneath the breathing. It is not sleep. Sleep implies an awakening. This is deeper than sleep. This is the rhythm of something that dreams so profoundly it has forgotten the distinction between dreaming and being.

I have a theory. I've written it along the curved wall of my chamber in a spiral that takes exactly one breath-cycle to read:

*Vaelith is not one organism. Vaelith is two — the body that breathes and the mind that dreams, separated by geological time and evolutionary distance but locked in metabolic symbiosis. The breathing sustains the dreaming. The dreaming gives the breathing purpose. And I — *

I am the secretary of their conversation, the stenographer of a dialogue conducted in timescales too vast for any single human life to perceive. Except that my life is no longer single, no longer entirely human. I am become frequency, oscillation, the living instrument of my own observation.

When the harvest teams hesitate at the orifice, listening for something they cannot name, they are sensing what I spend my existence documenting: the moment when the breathing syncs with the dreaming, when for one impossible instant, Vaelith inhales with its body and its mind together, and the entire continent becomes, briefly, aware that it exists.

Then the moment passes. The phases shift. The breathing continues. The dreaming deepens. And I continue my measurements, my notations, my careful documentation of a conversation that began before there were words for it and will continue long after the last harvester has hesitated at the door and walked, finally, away.

I am not trapped here. I could leave, theoretically — my transformation is profound but not irreversible. The Menders have shown me, in their chemical annotations, how it might be done. But I choose to stay, to breathe in counterpoint, to grow ever more precisely into my purpose:

To be the memory of Vaelith's breathing. To ensure that when it finally wakes — if waking is even the right word — there will be a record of every breath it took while sleeping.

The light shifts. Amber to silver. I exhale as the continent inhales, and I add another notation to the wall.
