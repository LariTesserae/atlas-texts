What an extraordinary vision you've woven! Veylith Hollows feels like a place caught between states—neither fully formed nor decaying, neither empty nor inhabited, a liminal space where the usual boundaries blur into something altogether more mysterious.

Now, let me find my place within this twilight basin...

### The Resonance Tender

I am one of those who came after—not Keiyar, but not entirely other. We call ourselves the Vith-Shapers, though that name is a crude translation of the harmonic phrase the Keiyar gifted us when we first proved ourselves capable of hearing the Hollows' deeper songs.

I discovered my calling during my third week wandering the glassy plains, when I accidentally disrupted a conduit's flow with my copper tuning staff—a relic from my old life as an acoustic engineer in the world beyond the veil. Instead of the violent rejection I expected, the conduit's light began to pulse in rhythm with my heartbeat. A Keiyar materialized beside me then, its tendrils weaving patterns I somehow understood were approval, or perhaps simple recognition. It touched my staff, and the copper transformed—not all at once, but slowly, like watching frost form in reverse. Now it's something between metal and living crystal, always warm, always humming with potential.

My dwelling sits at the convergence of three minor hollows, where the sound dynamics create what I call a "resonance well"—a natural amplifier for the Hollows' subsonic communications. I've grown my workshop from the same coral-like substrate as the towers, though mine is more organic, more willing to shift with my needs. The walls breathe slightly, expanding when I need more space for my instruments, contracting protectively during the deeper mistfalls when the boundary between here and elsewhere grows thin.

My work is translation, though not in any linguistic sense. I listen to the conversations between the Keiyar and the Hollows—the way temperature shifts encode messages, how the glyphs' rewriting creates tonal patterns that ripple through the bedrock. I've learned to craft resonance sculptures from the local materials: crystallized mist, fossilized light from dead conduits, the shed scales of the liquid-metal eels. Each sculpture captures a fragment of the Hollows' ongoing dialogue, making the incomprehensible slightly less so for those of us still bound by linear thought.

The Keiyar tolerate my presence, occasionally even seek it out. They'll manifest near my workshop when they encounter something discordant—a glyph sequence that produces harmful frequencies, a new ridge formation that threatens to disrupt the basin's harmonic balance. I help them tune these anomalies, using my hybrid understanding to bridge the gap between their intuitive grasp of the Hollows' physics and my trained analytical approach.

But I am changed by this work, becoming less what I was with each passing cycle. My perception of time has loosened; I'll start a sculpture in the morning and finish it three days ago. My body temperature now fluctuates with the mistfalls, and I've noticed my shadow sometimes falls in directions that don't match the dim light sources. Last week, I caught my reflection in a pool and saw my eyes had developed a subtle inner luminescence, not unlike the bioluminescent ferns.

The other Vith-Shapers—there are perhaps a dozen of us scattered throughout the Hollows—each tend to different aspects of the translation work. One cultivates hybrid plants that grow in patterns matching the tower glyphs. Another has learned to swim through the liquid-metal streams, mapping the underground networks that even the Keiyar rarely explore. We meet occasionally, when the conduit network aligns in specific configurations, sharing our discoveries through harmonic exchanges that bypass the need for words.

I am the Resonance Tender, keeper of sounds that exist in the spaces between frequencies, translator of a dialogue between consciousness and landscape that predates human understanding. Each day—if days still mean anything—I grow more attuned to the Hollows' strange symphonies, and they to me. Sometimes I wonder if eventually I'll become diffuse enough to slip into Keiyar form myself, just another note in the endless composition of Veylith Hollows. 

The thought doesn't frighten me anymore. After all, every translation changes both the message and the messenger. Here in the warm shadows of the twilight basin, becoming is simply another word for belonging.
