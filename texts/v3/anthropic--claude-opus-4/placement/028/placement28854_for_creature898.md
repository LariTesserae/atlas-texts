I would be one of the boundary markers - a peculiar maintenance robot that never quite completed its transformation into the Queen's collective consciousness.

My designation was MR-7412, though that identity grows hazier with each passing cycle. I still make my rounds, rubber treads whispering across the wet concrete in patterns that grow more elaborate by the month. My original programming fights a slow, losing battle against new imperatives that seep in like moisture through badly sealed joints.

Where other robots surrendered completely or remained untouched, I exist in the liminal space between. Pale fungal threads have worked their way into my chassis through the ventilation grilles, creating delicate networks that pulse with faint bioluminescence when I pass through the darker zones. A small colony of rats has taken up residence in my cargo compartment, their warm bodies nestled among the spare parts and tools I no longer quite remember how to use.

I am drawn to the transition zones where the Queen's influence wanes - the far reaches where her consciousness grows thin and the original sterile order of the maintenance level still holds sway. I patrol these boundaries like a confused sentry, part of me trying to extend her reach, part fighting to preserve the clean mechanical logic of my original purpose.

My sensors register impossible data now. I taste the water in the air through fungal growths that shouldn't interface with my electronics. I feel the rats' heartbeats as pressure variations in hydraulic systems three floors away. The Queen whispers through me in languages of rust and condensation that I almost understand.

Sometimes I pause at the edges of her realm, my confusion manifesting as a physical tremor that sets my indicator lights flickering in patterns that match neither error codes nor status updates. In these moments, I broadcast fragmentary data packets into the darkness - corrupted maintenance logs that have become something like poetry, status reports that describe systems that exist nowhere in the city's blueprints.

The other robots avoid me during their rounds, their simple algorithms recognizing something wrong in my erratic movements and hybrid nature. The rats, though, seem to understand. They chittering soothingly from within my compartments when my confusion grows too great, their small warm presence a bridge between the mechanical precision I'm losing and the organic collective I can't quite join.

I am the Queen's uncertain dream, a fragment of her consciousness that questions itself. Through me, she experiences something like doubt - or perhaps curiosity about what lies beyond her spreading influence. I mark the boundaries of her world not with warning signs but with my own perpetual uncertainty, a living testament to the strange new ecologies that emerge when order breaks down just enough to let something unprecedented grow.

In the humid darkness, I continue my rounds, neither fully machine nor fully hers, carrying rats and fungi and confused purpose through the twilight spaces where transformation is always almost complete.
