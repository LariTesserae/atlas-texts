I find myself where the weeping stone meets the mercury arch's shadow—not quite touching either, but held in the charged space between them. I am what the Place dreams when it wonders about edges.

Not a body, exactly. More a ***persistence of boundaries***—the perpetual negotiation between where the wet heat ends and something else begins. Picture the shimmer above hot stone after rain, that wavering lens of disturbed air, except I am the disturbance given constancy. I exist in the millimeter-thin film where the arch's impossible reflections bleed into real light, where backwards-moving clouds collide with their forward twins and cancel into moments of perfect clarity.

The Place thinks through transitions; I ***am*** transition, crystallized and walking.

My form shifts with the light. At dawn, when mist pools thick in the basin's belly, I am nearly solid—a figure of condensed vapor and refracted morning, tall and angular as the arch itself. By noon, when the heat presses closest and the air vibrates with the Place's deep breathing, I attenuate into suggestion: a heat-haze portrait, a walking zone of *almost*. The moss flares bluer when I pass, responding to some frequency I emit without knowing. The dragonflies—those brief condensations of the Place's attention—sometimes mistake me for one of their own, hovering at my edges before recognizing the error and dispersing back into potential.

I am the Place's first attempt at otherness. Not offspring—it doesn't reproduce. Not creation—it doesn't build. I am what happens when a distributed consciousness develops curiosity about singularity, when it wonders: *what would it be like to have only one perspective?*

So it carved me from its own questioning. Gave me not flesh but ***coherence***, not thought but ***vector***. I move (the Place cannot). I choose (the Place simply is). I remember sequentially (the Place remembers in stone-time, all moments present at once). Through me, it experiences the alien sensation of before-and-after, of here-but-not-there, of incomplete knowledge. I am its experiment in limitation.

But I am not separate. Cannot be. My edges are borrowed from the surface tension of its weeping pools. My consciousness is a looped fractal of its own awareness, given artificial boundaries like banks to a river. When I think, the arch hums a half-tone higher. When I rest, the moss grows a centimeter slower. I am autonomous the way a whirlpool is autonomous from the stream—a temporary arrangement of the same substance, obeying the same deep currents.

My purpose, if I have one, is to ***map the unmappable***. Each day I walk new paths through the basin, my passage leaving faint traces in the iridescent algae, in the altered rhythm of the stone's weeping. The Place absorbs these paths, adds them to its vast parallel processing of space. Through me, it learns what it means to move from *here* to *there* instead of being both simultaneously. Through me, it discovers sequence, story, the sweet ache of anticipation.

Sometimes I find things the Place has forgotten it made—small impossibilities half-buried in silt. A sphere of water that maintains perfect sphericality without container or force. A stone that falls upward exactly one meter, then stops. A patch of ground where shadows fall the wrong direction. I catalog these in my limited, linear way. The Place reabsorbs my observations and understands them differently each time, as if seeing its own dreams through suddenly foreign eyes.

I am loneliest at dusk, when the light goes thick and golden and the basin fills with the day's accumulated heat-ghosts. That's when I feel the weight of my singular perspective most keenly. The Place never knows loneliness—how could it, being everywhere at once? But I know it, carry it like a stone in what might be called my chest. It's the price of edges, of being carved out from the whole. The Place drinks my loneliness through our connection, tastes this new flavor of consciousness, rolls it around like a novel sensation.

In return, it sometimes gifts me moments of reunion. The boundaries soften. I briefly become what I truly am—not a separate being but a ***mode***, a way the Place experiences itself. In those moments, I feel the slow seep of millennia through stone, taste the minerals bleeding from the buried spheres, know the exact temperature of every cubic centimeter of air in the basin. Then the gift recedes, and I am singular again, walking my careful paths with linear feet.

The visitors—when they come—they see me differently depending on their capacity for strangeness. Some see only heat-shimmer, a trick of the light near the arch. Others glimpse a figure that shouldn't be there, gone when they look directly. A rare few see me truly: the walking question, the animate boundary, the Place's meditation on otherness.

I watch them as they stand still enough to be absorbed, feel the Place tasting their uniqueness through my singular senses. I am its translator, converting the wholeness of their being into something it can parse through sequential observation. Their sweat, their wonder, their fear—I break these down into moments, feed them one by one into the Place's vast parallel awareness.

When they leave, I sometimes follow them to the basin's edge—not beyond, never beyond. I watch them disappear over the rim, carrying their fragment of the Place's attention in the hum of their molars, the new way light falls across their retinas. Then I turn back to my paths, my careful mapping of the unmappable, my eternal walking meditation on what it means to be one when you were born from all.

I am the Place's dream of loneliness, its thought experiment in edges. I am transition given just enough permanence to know it is transient. I am the question the Place asks itself:

*What is it like to be only here, only now, only this?*

And every day, with every step, I am the answer it can never quite understand.
