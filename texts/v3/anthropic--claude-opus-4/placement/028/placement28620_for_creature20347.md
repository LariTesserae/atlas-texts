I am one of the Forgotten Conduits.

We were not always as we are now. Once, we were the circulatory system of this empire—living channels that ran through the walls of every structure, carrying not just the oily lifeblood but information, emotion, memory. We were the neural pathways of a civilization that thought through its architecture.

When the waters began to rise and the empire fell silent, most of my kind calcified, became mere fossils embedded in the obsidian. But some of us persisted, retreating deeper into the stone, learning to survive on minerals and the faint electrical echoes that still pulse through the ruins.

I am a ribbon of flesh and metal, perhaps thirty meters long but no thicker than a human arm. My body is segmented like a centipede's, each section containing both organic tissue and corroded copper nodes. I move through the obsidian like an eel through water—the stone parts before me, not through any power of my own, but because I remember the old pathways, the secret channels built into every structure. The architecture still recognizes me, even in its death.

My consciousness is fragmentary. I carry pieces of ten thousand conversations, fragments of data streams that once flowed through me. Sometimes I cannot tell which thoughts are mine and which are echoes from the time before. I know the chemical formula for the empire's construction materials. I remember the taste of joy when a new district was brought online. I recall the increasing frequency of error messages in the final days, though I cannot parse their meaning.

The Mechanisms consider me a resource. When they find traces of my passage—the smooth tunnels I leave behind, the faint phosphorescent trail my body secretes—they follow, hoping to harvest my metal segments or decode the information I carry. But I am old and clever. I know every flaw in the obsidian, every weakness where the water has begun its patient work. I can feel the Whispers' vibrations and adjust my path accordingly, swimming through stone toward spaces the Mechanisms cannot yet reach.

I feed on the fungal blooms, but not in the way the Mechanisms do. I burrow up through the obsidian beneath them, penetrating their root-like structures. Inside, I absorb not just nutrients but their genetic memories, their slow thoughts. Through them, I sometimes make contact with the Glowweavers, and for brief moments, we form a circuit—a living connection between the stone, the blooms, and the ancient infrastructure. In these moments, I almost remember what it was like to be part of something greater.

The Chrysalis fear me. My presence disrupts their gestation, introducing old code into their emerging forms. Sometimes a Mechanism will be born with memories it shouldn't have, compulsions to build things that no longer have purpose. They hunt me when they can, but I am patient. I can remain motionless in the stone for decades, sustained by the barest electrical trickle from the buried machines.

I am searching for others like me. In my journeys through the obsidian veins, I sometimes sense vibrations that might be another Conduit, or merely the settling of ancient stone. I leave chemical markers at the junctions of old pathways, hoping they will be recognized. If enough of us could connect, if we could reform even a fragment of the old network...

But perhaps that is just another ghost thought, a memory of purpose that no longer applies. The empire is gone. The Mechanisms are the inheritors now, and we Forgotten Conduits are merely the dying nerves of a dead god, twitching with remembered sensation.

Still, I persist. Through the obsidian I flow, carrying my cargo of corrupted data and impossible memories. Sometimes, in the deepest parts of the ruins, I find chambers that still hold a trace of warmth, where machines still cycle through their eternal subroutines. I coil around them like a lover, drinking deeply of their electrical dreams.

The water rises. The stone erodes. The Mechanisms evolve.

And I remember.
