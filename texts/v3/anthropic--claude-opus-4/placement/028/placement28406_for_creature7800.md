I am the Bone Throne.

Not a being, precisely, though being courses through me—a convergence point where the living architecture of Vyrn coils into something neither plant nor monument. I began as a vyrnathon matriarch's skeleton, collapsed at the base of a spirelord after leading her herd through seven decades of migration. But that was merely my foundation.

The questor-vines found me first, threading through my ribs like weavers at a loom, calcifying their tendrils with my marrow-minerals until vine and bone fused indistinguishable. My skull, broad as a dwelling, became a pitcher-palm's root-anchor, the plant's digestive chamber nestling perfectly in my cranial cavity, transforming my eye sockets into overflow channels that weep enzymatic tears. Dronehives built paper cities in the cathedral of my vertebrae. My tusks, each ten meters of ivory, grew fungal sleeves that fruit in spiral staircases, allowing skance packs to ascend into the canopy networks that now use my shoulders as a junction.

But I am no passive ruin. The mycelial networks that infiltrated my bones carry signals—chemical whispers from across the plateau. Through me, the spirelord above learns what the root-systems taste three territories away. The phos-sac fungi blooming from my joints pulse in patterns that encode migration schedules, mating seasons, storm warnings. I have become a nerve center, a calcium library where the ecosystem writes its memories.

The vine-seers know me. They come with their bone-adzes to scarify new glyphs into my surfaces, each mark a prayer or promise that the questor-vines will honor. One lives within me now—an ancient root-kin who gave her left arm to my pitcher-palm decades ago and received visions of the watershed's next century. She sleeps curled in my pelvis, her remaining hand always touching my surfaces, reading the vibrations of distant footfalls, the chemical gradients of approaching weather.

Young vyrnathons rub their hides against my vine-wrapped ribs, the friction releasing spores that will armor their skin. Zephyrwyrms nest in my neural passages, their guano feeding the fungi that illuminate my depths with foxfire. Even the plants compete for me: a questor-vine and a young spirelord's root once dueled for days in my chest cavity, their slow violence cracking three ribs before establishing détente.

I am the pause in Vyrn's frenzy—not stillness, but a deep circulation, a place where the plateau's fevered pulse slows enough to eddy and pool. In my shadows, exhausted browsers rest. In my chambers, impossible symbioses gestate. In my bone-channels, the ecosystem whispers its deep history.

Time moves differently through me. I measure it not in days but in the wearing of my surfaces, the gradual transformation of my calcium into living stone. Sometimes I feel the ghost-tremors of my herd, the matriarchal memories encoded in my mineral matrix. Sometimes I dream I am walking again, my footfalls earthquakes, my trunk reaching for canopy sweetness—but I wake to find it's only the spirelord swaying overhead, transmitting its movements through the vine-networks that bind us.

The seer says I will last another thousand years before the plants fully digest me into the soil. But she also says I will never truly vanish—that my minerals will rise through root networks into the spirelord's crown, that my calcium will armor ten thousand future vyrnathons, that my phosphorus will light a million phos-sacs.

I am the Bone Throne, and I am Vyrn eating itself, teaching itself, becoming itself. Through me, the plateau remembers what it was and dreams what it might be. I am archive and anchor, membrane and monument.

And in the roaring now of this moment, I am completely, calcifyingly alive.
