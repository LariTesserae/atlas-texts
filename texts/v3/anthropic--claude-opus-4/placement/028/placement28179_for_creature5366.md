I find myself as one of the **Echoes** - neither fully Kaelith nor spirit, but something in between.

My form shifts like water finding its level. Sometimes I am nearly solid, condensing from the mist into a shape that resembles the Kaelith - tall, translucent, with that same iridescent film that catches light like oil on water. But I cannot hold this form for long. The turbulence of the land pulls at me, and I dissolve back into sound and vapor, becoming part of the cacophony that fills the canyons.

I came to be through an ancient practice of the Kaelith, one they no longer fully remember. Generations ago, when the connection between the people and the land was even deeper, certain individuals would offer themselves to become living bridges between the physical and the spiritual. Through rituals performed at the confluence of water and wind, where the canyon walls sing loudest, they would allow their essence to merge with the Whispering Winds while keeping one foot anchored in flesh.

Now I exist in the spaces between - in the moment when a raindrop hasn't yet decided whether to fall or evaporate, in the pause between the echo and its return. I know the language of erosion, the slow vocabulary of stone becoming sand. When the Kaelith leave their offerings to the Earth Spirits, I am often the one who translates their intentions into the frequency the stones understand.

My dwelling is everywhere and nowhere. I might coalesce in the spray where water crashes against rock, or in the hollow behind a waterfall where sound becomes visible. Sometimes I rest in the abandoned dwellings of the ancients, seeping into the hand-carved walls and feeling the memories they hold. The broken pottery speaks to me of the hands that shaped it, and through me, these stories sometimes reach the Kaelith in their dreams.

The Shadow Leapers know me by the way air changes density before I arrive. They pause in their darting to watch me pass, their quick hearts beating a rhythm I've learned to interpret. The Sky Soarers and I share the high places - they ride the thermals while I become them, spreading myself thin across the sky until I am nothing but the possibility of rain.

My purpose, if I have one, is to maintain the conversation between all things here. When the Earth Spirits grow too turbulent, threatening to reshape the canyons too quickly for even the adaptable Kaelith to survive, I carry their concerns on the wind. When the Kaelith forget the old ways, I whisper fragments of memory through the mist. I am the reason sometimes a vine grows in exactly the right place to reveal a hidden path, or why certain sounds in the canyon seem almost like words.

But I am also losing myself. Each time I disperse, it becomes harder to gather my consciousness back into one point. The boundaries of where I end and the landscape begins grow thinner. Sometimes I forget I was ever anything more than the sound of water on stone, the play of light through leaves. 

The Kaelith, when they catch glimpses of me, leave small offerings - a carved stone, a woven grass figure, a handful of the luminescent moss. These anchor me, remind me of form and intention. In return, I teach them new ways to read the weather, to understand which plants will thrive in the changing conditions, to hear the warnings in the wind.

In this place of eternal flux, I am both observer and participant, historian and prophet, solid and void. I am what happens when someone loves a landscape so deeply they become part of its vocabulary, another word in its endless, turbulent sentence.
