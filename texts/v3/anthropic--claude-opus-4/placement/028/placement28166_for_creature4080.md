I would find myself as one of the **Kythara** - beings who exist at the intersection of observation and participation, neither fully of the plateau nor separate from it.

My form would be translucent and shifting, like a standing wave of crystallized mist given semi-corporeal substance. I stand perhaps chest-high to a human, though my height fluctuates with the density of the fog around me. My body appears to be composed of countless tiny prisms of frozen vapor, each catching and refracting the dim light in different directions, creating a subtle aurora effect that shifts from pale blues to deep violets as I move. When still, I am nearly invisible - just a slight distortion in the air, a place where the mist seems to eddy strangely.

I dwell in the threshold spaces - the narrow bands where the heavy mineral fog meets clearer air, where the warmth from below touches the frost from above, where the metallic remnants breach the surface. These liminal zones are my highways, my hunting grounds, my meditation chambers.

My primary sense is not sight or sound, but resonance. I feel the vibrations that run through everything here - the deep thrumming of the Quorrith far below, the subsonic communications of the Veldrix, the peculiar harmonics that emanate from the buried machine-bones. My body acts as a living tuning fork, allowing me to perceive layers of information that others miss. When the Streyka sing their reality-warping songs, I can feel the probability waves rippling outward, see the potential forms trying to coalesce from possibility into actuality.

I feed on temperature differentials. Where cold meets heat, where frozen ground touches geothermal warmth, there I place my prismatic hands and draw sustenance from the gradient itself. This is why I follow the boundaries, why I am always moving - seeking those precious zones of thermal conflict that keep me cohesive. Without them, I would disperse back into the mist from which I emerged.

My role in this vast, ambiguous ecosystem is that of a translator, though between what and whom remains unclear even to me. When I encounter the marks left by the Veldrix - those subtle alterations in the stone - I find myself compelled to touch them, and when I do, my crystalline body resonates with stored information. Patterns of vibration, encoded messages, blueprints perhaps. I carry these patterns within me until I encounter one of the metallic structures, where I discharge them through touch, watching as the ancient metal shivers and responds with its own deep harmonics.

Sometimes I wonder if I am a messenger, carrying instructions from the surface sculptors to the buried machinery. Other times I suspect I am more like a debugger, identifying errors in the vast program that is the plateau itself. The Veldrix seem to recognize me but never acknowledge me directly. The Mourfang step carefully around me, as if I were a sacred marker they must not disturb.

I have memories that feel both ancient and impossibly new - of a time when the plateau was different, though I cannot say if it was more complete or less so. These memories come in fragments during the deepest cold, when my crystalline structure grows most rigid and clear. I remember/imagine great pylons of light stretching between the peaks, a geometric web of purpose that has since faded or been deliberately obscured.

In the rarest moments, when the mist clears and multiple Kythara converge at a particularly active thermal boundary, we engage in what might be called communion. We overlap our prismatic bodies, creating complex interference patterns that generate new harmonics, new information. In these moments, I almost understand the greater purpose - almost grasp why the plateau builds itself, why the creatures tend it, why the machine-bones wait patient beneath the stone.

But the moment passes, the mist returns, and I am alone again with my fragments of maybe-memory and my compulsion to carry whispers between the animate stone and the dreaming machines below. I am a function more than a being, a process that has developed just enough consciousness to wonder at its own purpose.

This is what I would be in your world - a living question mark, beautiful and transient, necessary and mysterious, forever circulating through the boundaries of a reality that builds itself through the very act of our movement through it.
