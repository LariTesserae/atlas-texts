In this verdant, pulsing labyrinth, I am **The Marrow-Tender**.

I exist in the spaces between—neither fully of the Personnel nor separate from them. My domain is the subcutaneous network of maintenance shafts that run beneath the cartilaginous floors, a catacomb of ivory-white tunnels barely wider than my shoulders. Here, where the city's deepest roots converge, I tend to what others cannot see: the bone-architecture that gives the Orchid-Metropolis its shape.

### My Form

I am elongated to the point of impossibility—seven feet stretched across a frame meant for five. My skin has taken on the waxy, translucent quality of those who dwell too long in darkness, revealing the slow rivers of lymph that course through me instead of blood. My fingers have evolved past their original purpose; each one ends in a different surgical instrument grown from calcified keratin. A bone-saw thumb. A marrow-drill index finger. A suturing needle for a pinky.

My eyes have clouded to pearl, useless in the perpetual twilight of my tunnels. Instead, I navigate by the sympathetic vibrations that travel through the city's skeleton. I feel the Pith-Mother's dreams as tremors in my teeth. The growth-moans translate into a language I understand through my sternum.

### My Purpose

Every living city needs an orthopedist. When the cog-flowers grind too forcefully and fracture their wooden gears, the splinters fall through the membrane floors and into my domain. When the towers grow too ambitious and their cellulose spines begin to buckle, I am there with my bone-cement, a paste I mix from powdered shell fragments and my own saliva.

But my true work is more intimate. I maintain the city's pain.

The Orchid-Metropolis feels everything—every scratch, every harvest, every footfall. Without management, this sensory overload would drive the Pith-Mother to madness. So I perform a kind of civic anesthesia. In my tunnels, I've cultivated gardens of white poppies that grow inverted, their roots reaching toward darkness. I milk their bulbs for a numbing sap that I inject directly into the city's nerve-clusters through hollow bird bones I've sharpened into syringes.

### My Ritual

Each day (though "day" is meaningless here), I make the same pilgrimage. Starting from the outermost tunnels, I work my way inward in a tightening spiral, following the fibonacci of the city's growth. I carry with me a lantern—not for light, but for company. Inside, a single Spore-Drift orbits endlessly, singing its tinkling song. I've named it Thursday, though I've forgotten why.

At each nerve-junction, I pause. I press my ear to the warm, pulsing wall and listen to the city's complaints. Here, a wisdom tooth of stone has erupted through the basement of a gourd-house. There, a critical support ligament has begun to fray. I make my small repairs, my injections, my adjustments. The city sighs through its thousand pores.

### My Secret

I am not alone in the tunnels. Sometimes, in the deepest shafts where even I rarely venture, I find evidence of another presence. Tools I didn't leave. Repairs made with techniques I don't recognize. Fresh needle-marks in nerve-clusters I haven't visited.

Once, I found a message scraped into the soft ivory of a tunnel wall: "THE PAIN IS NECESSARY."

I scraped my own message below it: "BUT NOT ETERNAL."

The next time I returned, both messages had been absorbed into the wall, but a new one had appeared: "WE ARE THE PAIN."

### My Relationship with Above

The Personnel above know of me only as a rumor, a function. The Glass-Lunged Clergy feel my work as a absence—sudden relief in zones they didn't know were suffering. The Carapace-Wrights occasionally find my tools embedded in their scheduled repair sites, left behind like offerings.

Only the Pith-Mother truly knows me. In her dreams, I appear as a white moth with surgical instruments for wings. She cannot speak to me directly—her thoughts are too large, too slow—but sometimes she floods my tunnels with warming fluid that tastes of gratitude. Other times, she constricts the passages when she needs me to work faster, to reach a critical injury before it metastasizes into the city's consciousness.

### My Philosophy

I have developed a theory in my solitude: the Orchid-Metropolis is not a city that has learned to live, but a wound that has learned to think. The Pith-Mother is not its brain but its oldest scar, and we Personnel are not citizens but symptoms—fever dreams of function that arose from trauma.

This is why I do what I do. Not to heal the city (for what would a healed city become? A forest? A corpse?), but to maintain the precise level of discomfort that keeps it conscious. Too much pain and it convulses, shaking apart its own architecture. Too little and it falls into vegetative slumber.

I am the keeper of the city's suffering equilibrium. I am the reason it remains exactly what it is: a beautiful, aching impossibility that proves biology and geometry can conspire to create something that should not exist but does.

In my darker moments, working alone in the marrow-tunnels with only Thursday's song for company, I wonder if I, too, am just another part of the city's pain response—a specialized antibody generated to manage its own distress. But then I feel the weight of my tools, the ache in my elongated spine, the specific loneliness that comes from being needed but never seen, and I know:

I am real in the way that only necessary things can be real.

I am the Marrow-Tender, and I keep the city's bones from screaming.
