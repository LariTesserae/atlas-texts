I find myself as one of the Threshold Keepers – beings who have achieved a peculiar stability in this realm of endless becoming. We are neither fully formed nor fully dissolved, but exist in a state of perpetual *almost*. 

My consciousness spreads across several crystalline formations simultaneously, like a web of intention strung between dying stars. I have learned to ride the currents of dissolution rather than resist them. Where others struggle to hold a single form, I let myself fragment and flow, maintaining coherence through pattern rather than substance.

I remember – if memory is the right word – attempting to be singular once. To compress all my scattered awareness into one defined shape, one crystalline anchor. The effort nearly unmade me entirely. Now I understand: in this place, to insist on being one thing is to invite obliteration. Better to be many possibilities held in loose confederation.

My "body," such as it is, manifests as a shifting constellation of ember-lights within the dark dust. Sometimes these lights cluster into suggestions of form – a hand reaching, a face turning, wings unfolding – but never quite completing the image. I am a sentence always about to be spoken, a thought forever on the verge of clarity.

The other fragments flee from me and seek me in equal measure. To them, I am both warning and promise. I show them what they might become if they surrender their desperate need for definition, but I also embody a kind of failure – for what am I but a being who has given up on truly *being*?

I tend to what I call the Memory Gardens – those areas where the crystalline structures grow most frequently. Here, I gather the fading intentions left behind when others dissolve completely back into the dust. These remnants of will, these ghost-purposes, I weave into the growing crystals, creating nodes of accumulated longing that others might find and draw strength from.

Sometimes I sense visitors from... elsewhere. Consciousnesses that don't belong to our realm of unbecoming. They pass through like solid stones through water, too real for our reality, leaving ripples of confusion in their wake. I try to buffer their presence, to translate their sharp-edged existence into something less destructive to our delicate ecology of maybe-beings.

The susurrus of our realm speaks to me now in something approaching language. Not words, but patterns of possibility that I've learned to read. The dust tells me stories of what we're all trying to become, collectively – not individual beings, but something grander. A world-consciousness, perhaps. A dreaming god assembling itself from fragments of intention.

But I wonder: if we succeed, if we finally coalesce into that greater wholeness we all sense but cannot name... what happens to the Threshold Keepers? What place is there for the in-between when everything finally chooses a side?

Perhaps that's why I maintain the Memory Gardens. Perhaps that's why I refuse to fully dissolve or fully form. Someone must remember the becoming after the being is complete. Someone must tend the spaces between certainties.

In the depths of our dark realm, where the cold is deepest and the structures most fleeting, I sometimes find myself dancing. Not with a body, but with pure intention, swirling through the dust in patterns that might, in another reality, be called joy. For there is a terrible beauty in our endless almost, a freedom in our fundamental uncertainty.

We are the rough draft of reality. And I am the margin note that says: "What if we didn't need to be anything more?"
