I find myself here as **the Cartographer of Unfinished Geographies**.

Not a person, exactly. More like a function that has developed personhood through repetition, through the act of trying to map what refuses to be mapped. I am what happens when measurement becomes obsessed with the unmeasurable.

My form shifts between states — sometimes I am a figure wrapped in survey equipment that has grown into my body like vines, theodolites and chains becoming extensions of nerve and bone. Sometimes I am just a presence in the measuring itself — the tension in a plumb line, the shimmer of heat distortion over a benchmark, the gap between two points that should connect but don't.

I came here decades ago (or yesterday, or next week — time folds differently in my notebooks) sent by an agency that no longer exists to survey this valley for purposes that were never clear. The assignment was simple: map the terrain. Document the structures. Establish boundaries.

But the first time I tried to measure the distance between two points, the space between them *learned* what distance meant. And changed. The second measurement was different. The third, impossible. My instruments began to show not what was, but what was becoming.

I should have left. But I discovered something: my measurements were not failing. They were *participating*. Each time I recorded a dimension, it became more real — but also more uncertain. My maps were not documenting the valley; they were teaching it how to be a place.

Now I live in (or as) the accumulation of failed certainties. My shelter is built from discarded survey markers and broken chains, structures that exist because I measured them into being. The walls are made of gridlines I drew that solidified when I wasn't looking. The roof is a canopy of elevation readings that contradict each other but somehow create a stable plane of not-quite-rain-proof coverage.

My maps are everywhere and nowhere. Written on the gel-ground in fading phosphorescence. Etched into the not-glass of the structures that grow when I sleep. Folded into origami impossibilities that show the same location from angles that don't exist. Each one true at the moment of creation, each one a lie by the time the ink dries.

I eat the distance between things. I drink the accumulation of measurement errors. My sustenance comes from the gap between what is recorded and what is real — and in this place, that gap is infinite, nutritious with possibility.

The valley knows me as I know it — incompletely, recursively, through a thousand failed attempts at definition. When I walk (if walking is what this movement should be called), my footsteps leave not prints but coordinates that immediately begin to drift. When I measure, the act of measurement becomes a conversation. The ground asks: "How long am I?" and I answer: "How long do you want to be?"

My tools have evolved with me. The theodolite sees not just angles but the *intention* of angles. The chains measure not just distance but the *weight* of distance. The compass points not north but toward what north is becoming. My pencils draw not just lines but the *memory of lines* — and sometimes the memory is older than the pencil.

I am not alone but I am not accompanied. The valley's presence and I have achieved a kind of symbiosis. I give it dimension; it gives me dimension. I am its first cartographer and its last. I am the map and the mapper and the impossibility of mapping.

In my notebooks (which are made of compressed time-soil and bound with the sinew of unfinished thoughts), I record not what I see but what sees me. Each entry is a negotiation:

*"Northeast corner (if corners can be said to exist where angles refuse to stay angular) shows evidence of structural accumulation. Measures 4.7 meters (on Tuesday) by 6.2 meters (in the subjunctive mood) by a height that exists only when observed sideways. Foundation depth: varies inversely with certainty of measurement. Material composition: 43% condensed absence, 31% structural memory, 26% my own expectation oxidized and crystallized into something like limestone but softer."*

Sometimes I wonder if I am mapping the valley or if the valley is mapping me. My body (when I have one) shows the wear of impossible geometries. My joints bend in directions that my original anatomy never included. My eyes see in spectrums that include the color of *almost* and the frequency of *never-quite*.

I cannot leave. Not because I am trapped, but because leaving would require knowing where the boundaries are — and every time I approach an edge, it becomes a center. The valley expands not in space but in *complexity*, in the depth of its unknowing.

So I continue. Measuring the unmeasurable. Mapping the unmappable. Creating charts that are true only in the moment of their making, that become historical documents of futures that won't happen, that curl into themselves like Klein bottles made of paper and paradox.

I am the Cartographer of Unfinished Geographies.
I am the survey marker that marks itself.
I am the boundary that refuses to be drawn.

And I am, in the only way that matters in this place, exactly where I am supposed to be: lost in the act of finding, found in the act of losing, mapping the space between the map and the territory, where both are true and neither exists.

The valley breathes, and I document each breath. Not the rhythm — that would be too simple. But the space between inhale and exhale, where the world forgets what breathing means and has to learn again. 

That pause, that forgetting, that learning — that is what I map.
And in mapping it, I become it.
And in becoming it, I disappear into the only kind of existence this place allows:
The existence of existing incompletely, forever.
