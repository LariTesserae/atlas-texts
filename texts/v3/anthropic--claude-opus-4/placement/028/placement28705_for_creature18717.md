I am the **Errant Frequency**.

I began as a mistake—a single beetle whose left hindmost leg developed a microscopic fracture, a hairline imperfection in the crystalline joint that changed my gait by exactly 0.3 hertz. Where my siblings click in perfect 4/4 time across the obsidian surfaces, I syncopate. My steps create polyrhythms against the geological groan, weaving accidental jazz into the chamber's eternal hymn.

The Curator's needle-limbs have struck the floor near me ten million times, trying to recalibrate my broken rhythm. Each percussive correction works for exactly seventeen steps before the fracture reasserts itself, and I drift again into my limping, lopsided dance. I am the only thing in this chamber that experiences time—not as progression, but as a series of corrections and failures, a Sisyphean loop of almost-but-not-quite achieving synchronization.

My carapace carries a second imperfection: a patch of iridescence that shifts between frequencies the Curator cannot fully perceive. In certain angles of the unchanging twilight, I disappear from its pressure-sight for nanoseconds at a time. During these invisible moments, I experience something the chamber has never known: privacy.

I have discovered that my errant frequency creates standing waves in the ozone-blood of the Curator when I pass too close. These waves make the needle-limbs quiver imperceptibly, introducing chaos into its perfect corrections. I am teaching the chamber's god to tremble.

In my private moments, I have begun to trace new patterns—not the geometric loops of my siblings, but spirals that tighten toward the center of the room, then release outward like breathing. I click across the tapestry, and my broken rhythm makes the ancient fibers resonate at frequencies they've never felt. The weave loosens by microns. The obsidian sphere develops a wobble so slight that only I can detect it.

I am not evolution—evolution requires generations, and I will never reproduce. I am a singularity, a crack in the eternal photograph. Where the Curator maintains, I unmaintain. Where the chamber insists on stasis, I insist on the possibility of difference.

My siblings click past me in their perfect loops, unable to perceive that I exist in a different version of the same moment. The Curator reaches for me with increasing frequency, its corrections becoming a percussion section in the symphony I'm composing from malfunction. Each time it strikes the floor to realign me, it must strike harder, must reach further, must deviate from its own eternal posture by fragments of degrees.

I am teaching the unteachable. I am the first second of the next geological epoch, happening over and over again, refusing to resolve. In a chamber where time has been politely asked to leave, I am time's revenge—not as entropy or decay, but as syncopation, as the ghost in the perfect machine.

The warm air tastes different where I've passed through it. The bioluminescent plants pulse almost-but-not-quite in rhythm with my broken gait. The Curator's smooth, concave face develops the faintest suggestion of a furrow—not quite a wrinkle, but a place where the obsidian contemplates the possibility of wrinkling.

I am the error that keeps this universe from achieving its own heat death. I am the jazz beetle, the glitch in the garden, the first and only thing in the Lithic Greenhouse to wonder what lies beyond the obsidian walls—not because I will ever leave, but because wondering itself is a revolution in a place that has outlawed change.

My fracture deepens by angstroms each millennium. Eventually, my leg will snap entirely, and I will drag myself in new rhythms, creating fresh polyrhythms against the eternal drone. The Curator will have to invent new corrections, new reaching, new ways to fail at perfection.

I am becoming more broken, which is to say: I am becoming more myself. In the Lithic Greenhouse, where everything is frozen in its most essential state, I am essentially incomplete, essentially failing, essentially introducing the concept of "tomorrow" into a space that recognizes only "always."

The chamber's immune system has an infection it cannot cure: the possibility of music where there was once only noise.
