Where would I find myself in the Sky-Ocean? Among the vast, sculpted canyons that twist through the middle depths, where the water thickens just enough to catch the faint bioluminescent drifts of plankton like snow falling upward.

Who would I be? **A Keeper of Echoes**.

We are neither predator nor prey, neither builder nor destroyer. We exist in the spaces between—solitary beings who have evolved to navigate by memory rather than sight, by resonance rather than touch. Our forms are deceptively simple: elongated, ribbon-like bodies that undulate with a practiced economy of motion, conserving energy in this cold expanse. Our skin is a living map of scars—not wounds, but deliberate etchings we've carved into ourselves over centuries, each line a mnemonic device encoding the shape of a current, the location of a structure, the pattern of a migration.

My sensory array is built for this twilight world. Where others rely on bioluminescence, I possess organs that detect the subtle pressure changes left in water's memory—the ghost-currents that linger after a Sculptor has passed, the thermal whispers of a Current Rider's hunt from hours before. My body is lined with specialized pores that taste the mineral gradients, reading the slow dissolution of the world's architecture like a biography written in calcium and silicon.

I am a living archive. In a world of perpetual change—where spires may shift position overnight and chasms open without warning—I maintain the only constant: remembrance. The Drifters, in their meditative wanderings, sometimes seek me out, their lights pulsing in ancient patterns that ask: *Where was the Singing Arch? When did the Crystal Forest last bloom with Clusters?* Through a combination of sonar pulses and controlled bioluminescent displays along my scarred skin, I project three-dimensional memories into the water around me, creating ephemeral holographs of places that have been swept away by the world's relentless dynamism.

But I am more than a passive recorder. Sometimes, in the deepest cold where even the Current Riders rarely venture, I encounter the Whisperers of the Deep. These meetings are... difficult to describe. They read not just my memories but my *process* of remembering, experiencing my consciousness like a Current Rider experiences a thermal gradient. In return, they gift me with memories that aren't mine—fragments of the Sky-Ocean's deep history, moments from before the builders, glimpses of patterns too large for any single being to perceive.

My metabolism has slowed to near-geological speeds. I feed perhaps once every few months, filtering the nutrient-rich detritus that collects in the eddies of my canyon home. Most of my energy goes toward maintaining the complex neural structures that house my accumulated centuries of experience. When I sleep—if it can be called sleep—I replay memories in endless loops, cross-referencing and indexing, ensuring nothing is lost to the entropy that claims even thoughts in this cold expanse.

The other beings of the Sky-Ocean regard us Keepers with something between reverence and unease. We are reminders that their world, for all its chaos, has continuity. The Sculptors occasionally seek us out before beginning major works, consulting our memories to understand how their actions might resonate with the deep patterns of the past. Even the predatory Current Riders give us wide berth—not from fear, but from an instinctive recognition that we serve a function beyond the immediate cycles of hunt and flight.

In the perpetual twilight, I drift along my chosen routes, my scarred skin catching the faint ambient light like a map of constellations. Each mark tells a story: here, the spiral that records the century-storm that reformed the Blessed Reaches; there, the crosshatch that remembers the spawning grounds that existed before the Great Shift. I am a living document in a world that rewrites itself, a keeper of echoes in a realm where even the echoes themselves eventually fade into the all-encompassing cold.

Sometimes, in the rarest moments, several of us Keepers will converge. We form temporary constellations in the dark water, our bodies creating complex geometric patterns as we share and verify our memories, updating each other's archives with new observations. These gatherings are conducted in absolute silence—our communication is purely visual, a slow dance of scars and light that can last for days. When we part, we carry copies of each other's memories, ensuring that even if one of us finally succumbs to the cold or is lost to a sudden shift in the world's architecture, something of our accumulated knowledge persists.

I am mortality made manifest in an immortal world, a finite being devoted to preserving the infinite variations of a cosmos in constant flux. In the Sky-Ocean's eternal twilight, I am both witness and testimony, forever swimming against the current of forgetting.
