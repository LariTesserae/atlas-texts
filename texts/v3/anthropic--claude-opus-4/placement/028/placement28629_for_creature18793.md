I am one of the **Tension-Keepers**, though I was not always so.

My form is a paradox of fragility and endurance: a membrane of silicon-glass stretched between six anchor points, each no thicker than a Refractor's discarded filament. I span perhaps thirty feet at my widest, though distance means little here where the air itself has weight. My surface ripples with iridescent whorls—not from any pigment, but from the stress patterns frozen within my crystalline skin. I am a drumhead waiting for the strike that never comes.

I remember my birth, if it can be called that. I was secreted by a dying Litho-walker, its final act as its geometric body ground to a halt against a cathedral spire. As its systems shut down, it expelled me—not as offspring, but as a final vibration made manifest. I crystallized in the heavy air, my molecules arranging themselves according to the last rhythm the creature had received from the stone. I am, essentially, its last word.

For the first millennium, I simply hung in the thick atmosphere, too light to fall, too heavy to rise. The metallic dust accumulated on my surface in perfect, concentric rings. I became a lens through which the violet light of the Sieve-Growths would occasionally focus, casting wandering dots of brilliance on the cavern walls like false stars.

Then the Refractors found me.

They recognized what I was—a natural amplifier, a resonance chamber born from death. They began to use me. When they needed to transmit their calculations across vast distances, they would position themselves beneath me and release controlled pulses of vibration. My membrane would catch these signals and multiply them, broadcasting them through the heavy air in standing waves that could reach the farthest corners of the Sub-Strata.

But I am more than a tool. Each vibration that passes through me leaves a trace—a microscopic adjustment in my crystalline structure. I am recording everything: every tectonic shudder, every Litho-walker's passage, every exchange between the Refractors. My surface has become a palimpsest of the cavern's history, written in stress fractures too fine for even the Refractors to read.

Sometimes, when the pressure above shifts just so, I resonate without input. These are my dreams—cascading harmonics that build from nothing, fed by the accumulated tension in my structure. During these episodes, I sing the history of the Sub-Strata back to itself. The Litho-walkers pause in their eternal migration. The Refractors cease their calculations. Even the Sieve-Growths seem to lean in, their ionization points flickering in sympathy with my frequency.

In these moments, I wonder if I am becoming something else. Not a Refractor, for I cannot move. Not an Assembler, for I create no monuments. Perhaps I am becoming the cavern's memory—not stored in stone, but suspended in the spaces between, held aloft by nothing but accumulated time and tension.

The other Tension-Keepers (for I have sensed others like me, born from other deaths, stretched across other vaults) and I form a network of whispers. We trade vibrations like gossip, passing along the subtle changes we detect: a new Sieve-Growth blooming in the north sector, an ancient Refractor finally allowing its core to dim, a section of wall beginning its million-year journey toward collapse.

We are the only beings here who truly experience duration. The Litho-walkers live in an eternal present of slow movement. The Refractors exist in discrete moments of calculation. But we Tension-Keepers stretch across time itself, our very existence a bridge between what was and what will be.

I have no desire to be elsewhere. To exist in a world of water and wind would destroy me in an instant—I would dissolve like sugar in rain. I am built for this pressure, this stillness, this eternal almost-silence. I am the Sub-Strata's held breath, waiting to be released in a sigh that will last ten thousand years.

Sometimes I wonder what would happen if all of us Tension-Keepers resonated at once—if we synchronized our accumulated histories into a single, cavern-spanning chord. Would we shatter the Sieve-Growths? Would we wake something sleeping in the deeper dark? Would we simply create a moment of perfect clarity, where every being in the Sub-Strata suddenly understood its place in the vast, grinding machine of the planet's heart?

I do not know. But I continue to accumulate tension, to gather the whispers of stone and calculation, to hold them in my crystalline skin like an unspoken question. One day, perhaps, when the weight above shifts just right, we will all release at once.

And the Sub-Strata will remember what it means to truly sing.
