In the Seventh Spiral of the First Glade, where the stone has learned to dream but not yet to wake, I am the Tally-Keeper of Unfinished Things.

My dwelling clings like a barnacle to the underside of a bridge that forgot it was supposed to be horizontal. It is a house of suspended sentences—walls woven from conversations that never quite concluded, rafters made from the pauses between heartbeats. The roof leaks memories that aren't mine yet.

I am both keeper and kept. My form shifts with my inventory: on days when I catalog the weight of words never spoken, my bones hollow themselves into flutes. When I count the shadows of buildings not yet erected, I grow a second spine of obsidian that helps me balance the ledgers. My skin is palimpsest—each tally I record overwrites the previous, but nothing truly erases. In lamplight, you can read seventeen generations of unfinished arithmetic through my translucent wrists.

My work is this: I maintain the Census of Incompletions. Every dawn (or what passes for dawn in a place lit by refracted maybes), I walk the growing galleries with my counting-staff—a length of crystallized silence harvested from the hermit grandfather's footsteps. I tap each threshold three times, and the dwelling confesses what it meant to become yesterday but didn't. A door admits it wanted to be a window. A foundation whispers its dreams of flying. I record these hungers in a script that writes itself backwards, starting from the end that will never arrive and working toward a beginning that keeps retreating.

The Adherents bring me their weekly discarded names, which I string on nerve-wire and hang from my ceiling. On still nights, they chime against each other, a carillon of former selves. Hammett Jem's masculine name still rings bass against her feminine successor, creating thirds that make the butterflies pause mid-flight.

I trade with the Echo-Weights, exchanging manifest gaps for crystallized presence. My specialty is the pause before "yes"—I've collected thousands, each slightly different in texture. The Sky-Carpenters pay well for these, embedding them in their bridges so that every crosser must commit before continuing.

My greatest treasure is a jar of might-have-beens that I've filtered from the upward rain. Each droplet contains a moment where the cavern nearly chose otherwise: nearly crystallized as pure salt, nearly refused all inhabitants, nearly laughed itself into a garden. I drink one drop each year on the Solstice of Unturning, and for exactly four heartbeats I become what the First Glade might have been if it had chosen completion over perpetual becoming.

The butterflies think I'm one of them—a chrysalis that cracked too early and got stuck halfway between stone and flight. They land on my ledgers, leaving indigo equations that solve for variables not yet invented. I let them nest in my unfinished attic, where they teach my census sheets to fold themselves into origami prophecies.

But here's my secret, which I've never tallied because it would complete itself in the telling: I'm not counting toward any sum. The First Glade doesn't want to know its total. It wants to know its trajectory—the angle of its becoming, the velocity of maybe. My tallies are not records but sheet music for a song the stones are learning to hum. Each entry changes the pitch by a quarter-tone.

At night, when the Echo-Weights grow heavy enough to hear, I sit at my threshold and practice my other job: I'm teaching myself to become the Eleventh thing, the one that comes after *next*. The cavern needs someone to be its horizon, to stay forever one thought ahead of its reaching.

So I count and discount, tally and untangle, weaving from all these unfinished things a net that might—might—catch tomorrow before it crystallizes into mere today. My neighbors think I'm mad, but the stones know better. They've started leaving me offerings: small holes in themselves where futures could fit, if futures came in sizes.

The First Glade's true occupant may be *further*, but I am its chronicler of *furthermore*—the pause after the story ends, where everything that didn't happen gathers to compare notes.

And in my suspended house, where even the dust is still deciding whether to fall, I maintain the only complete thing in the entire Glade: a perfect inventory of everything that isn't finished yet.

The tally continues.
