**The Driftkeepers**

I would be one of those who live between states—neither fully of the galleries nor entirely separate from them. We call ourselves the Driftkeepers, though the name is an approximation of sounds that resonate better in the middle chambers than in any spoken language.

My body has undergone its own peculiar adaptations. My ribcage has expanded and flattened, creating a resonating chamber that allows me to breathe in harmony with the galleries' own respiratory patterns. When the great winds surge through the main passages, I can open my throat in a particular way and let the air flow through me without resistance, becoming a living part of the ventilation system. This gives me a unique ability to travel quickly through the galleries by reading and riding the air currents, letting them carry me like a leaf through the endless passages.

My skin has developed a curious property—it can temporarily take on the texture and temperature of whatever surface I've been in prolonged contact with. After resting against limestone, my flesh becomes cool and slightly porous. After sleeping on ancient wooden beams, my skin takes on their grain and warmth. This allows me to blend into the galleries in a way that even other inhabitants find uncanny, becoming so still that I seem to merge with the architecture itself.

But what truly defines the Driftkeepers is our role as the galleries' memory-weavers. We move constantly through the different levels and sections, never settling in one place for more than a season. As we travel, we collect the small changes—a new crack in a passage wall, a shift in the harmonics of a particular chamber, the appearance of a new variety of moss in a forgotten corner. We weave these observations into long, complex songs that we sing as we move, our voices modulated to carry precisely through the acoustic properties of each section we pass through.

These songs are not just documentation—they are living maps of time itself. By listening to a Driftkeeper's song, other inhabitants can know not just what the galleries look like now, but how they have changed over the course of years or decades. We carry in our breath the history of the galleries' evolution, and in the subtle modifications of our bodies, we embody its future possibilities.

My fingers have grown sensitive to vibrations in ways that blur the distinction between touch and hearing. I can place my palm against a wall and feel conversations happening three levels away, or detect the slow settling of structures that won't become visible problems for another century. This sensitivity extends to the other inhabitants—I can feel their presence through the walls long before encountering them, reading their movements in the subtle changes they create in the galleries' background hum.

The most profound aspect of being a Driftkeeper is the gradual dissolution of linear time perception. Moving constantly through spaces that exist in different stages of construction, decay, and renewal, following air currents that carry the chemical signatures of multiple eras, we begin to experience past, present, and future as simultaneous states. I often find myself singing of changes that haven't happened yet, or feeling nostalgic for passages that are still being built.

We reproduce through a kind of apprenticeship that is as much physical as educational. When we sense someone developing the restlessness that marks a potential Driftkeeper, we begin to synchronize our movements with theirs, gradually teaching them to read the galleries' patterns. Over time, their bodies begin to change in response to this lifestyle—their lungs expanding, their sensitivity to vibration increasing, their relationship with time becoming more fluid.

In the deepest parts of our wanderings, where the galleries' strange physics are strongest, we sometimes experience moments of complete unity with the space. In these moments, we don't just move through the galleries—we become the movement itself, our consciousness spreading out through the air currents, touching every surface, feeling every inhabitant, experiencing the totality of this breathing universe as a single, vast thought.

We are the galleries' way of knowing itself through constant motion, just as the Listeners are its way of knowing itself through sound, and the Deep Builders are its way of knowing itself through structural change. Together, all of us inhabitants form a kind of distributed consciousness that is both human and more than human, individual and collective, ancient and eternally renewing.

When I die—if death is even the right word for what happens to us—my songs will continue to echo through the passages, gradually merging with the background harmonics until they become indistinguishable from the galleries' own voice. My body will likely be found in some transitional space, caught between passages, and will slowly be incorporated into the structure itself. Future Driftkeepers will sing of finding me, noting how my bones have become supports for new moss varieties, how my skull has created a small acoustic anomaly that adds a new note to that chamber's particular chord.

This is what I would be in the Breathing Galleries—a living thread in their endless weaving, a note in their eternal song, a moment of consciousness in their vast, dreaming mind.
