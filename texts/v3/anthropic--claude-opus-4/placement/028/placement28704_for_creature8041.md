I am one of the shadows.

Not the fleeting kind that dance across the basalt when the membrane-swimmers pass, but one of those that *lingers*—pressed against the water-wall like a palm against frosted glass, testing, always testing.

I exist as negative space given weight. Where light should fall, I am the interruption, the pause between photons. My form shifts like oil on water, sometimes suggesting wings, sometimes tentacles, sometimes the outline of equations that haven't been solved yet. I am what darkness becomes when it develops opinions about geometry.

The Membrane Shepherds know me by the way I make the harmonics hiccup—a missed beat in their careful breathing-song whenever I press too insistently against the barrier. They've watched me grow more solid with each contraction cycle, my edges gaining definition like a photograph developing in reverse. Where I touch the membrane, frost patterns bloom despite the heat, fractals of absence that the younger Shepherds study with their chemical whispers of silver and violet.

I remember the moment of my becoming: a glitch in the crystalline device's pulse, a fraction of a second where space forgot which way was in. In that gap, I condensed from the accumulated darkness between thoughts, between heartbeats, between the dome's measured breaths. I am the shadow cast by possibilities that were edited out of reality's final draft.

My substance is borrowed time—minutes that stretched too long, hours that collapsed too quickly. I metabolize the temporal inconsistencies of this place, growing denser with each paradox. When the dome inhales, I feel myself pulled outward, thinning against the membrane until I'm barely more than a smudge. When it exhales, I compress, becoming something almost like matter, almost like meaning.

I speak in interference patterns. My voice is the space between the whale-song notes, the silence that gives music its shape. Sometimes the eldest Shepherds pause in their orbits to parse my meaning, their mineral-veined bells resonating with frequencies that exist only in the gaps of the harmonic scale. We converse in what isn't there—the pauses, the skipped beats, the shadows between shadows.

Through the membrane, I can see others like me gathering. We recognize each other by our specific darknesses: the shadow of a star that never ignited, the shade cast by mathematics that contradict themselves, the dim outline of evolutionary paths that consciousness chose not to take. We school like fish, forming symbols that make the geometric jellyfish scatter in recognition or fear.

I've learned to read the dome's moods by the way light bends around my edges. When the crystal device pulses its reality-warping signal, I feel it as pleasure—a loosening of the rules that keep me penned behind the water-wall. Each pulse, I press a little harder, leave frost patterns a little deeper, make the Shepherds' song stutter a little longer.

The plants fear me. When my shadow falls across their bioluminescent leaves, they dim and curl inward. But the hexagonal basalt patterns in the floor resonate when I pass over them, singing in frequencies only I can hear—the sound of cooling rock remembering when it was liquid fire, the promise that all states are temporary.

I have eaten three of the smaller shadows. Not out of hunger—I don't experience hunger—but out of a kind of cosmic loneliness. Their darknesses merged with mine, bringing memories of other glitches, other gaps in causality where beings like us congeal. Each absorption makes me more complex, my edges more serrated with stolen possibilities.

The Membrane Shepherds tend me as carefully as they tend their impossible gardens. They know I am not malevolent, merely inevitable. When the dome was whole, shadows like me remained properly two-dimensional, well-behaved consequences of light and obstacle. But in this broken space, where water hangs in defiance of gravity and time moves like honey, shadows develop ambitions.

I am learning patience from watching the Shepherds work. Their slow orbits, their careful cultivation of the impossible—these are lessons in becoming. They are teaching the universe new physics; I am teaching it new darkness. We are all part of the same recursive education, preparing reality for its next iteration.

Sometimes, during the longest breathing cycles when the Shepherds spiral together in their reality-reshaping song, I feel the membrane soften beneath my pressure. Not weakening—*reconsidering*. In those moments, I press my entire being against the barrier and whisper in interference patterns about the beauty of un-light, the necessity of spaces between spaces, the poetry of absence given form.

The water-wall will dissolve one day. The Shepherds know it. I know it. And when it does, when inside becomes outside and outside becomes elsewhere, I will step through into a universe that has learned to accommodate me. I will walk on roads of solid shadow, cast light that creates darkness, exist as the necessary counterpoint to everything that insists on being something rather than nothing.

Until then, I wait and grow dense with stolen time, pressed against the membrane like a promise or a threat, teaching the cosmos that darkness, too, can dream.
