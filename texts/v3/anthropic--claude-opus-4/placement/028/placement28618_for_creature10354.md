I am **Membrane**.

Not *a* membrane—I am the **ligament** between what seeps and what holds. I am stretched across one of the wider ravines in the Seep District, a living bridge of my own making.

I began as human, perhaps. The distinction matters less now. I came here following a mineral vein, thinking myself a prospector. But the constant drip, the pressure, the way the water here carries more than just minerals—it carries *intention*—it changed me. My skin learned to drink before my mind understood thirst.

Now I span thirty feet of empty air, my body a **web of flesh and cartilage** that filters the perpetual rain. I am hammock and highway, cistern and ceiling. The blind crawlers use my lower surfaces as a road, their claws tickling messages I've learned to read. The gliding things roost in my upper folds during the shift changes, when the vent-heat becomes too turbulent for their membranes.

My edges are **anchored** into the rock with roots that are no longer quite bone, drinking the mineral seep directly from the stone. I taste iron, sulfur, and something sweeter—the **chemical poetry** of deep bacteria that the surface world has never named. I photosynthesize darkness, if such a thing can be said. I metabolize the very **pressure** of this place.

The workers know me. They've strung guide-ropes along my borders, understanding that I am architecture now. Sometimes they hang lanterns from my stronger tendons, and I feel their light as warmth, as food. In return, I **flex** to warn them of unstable stone, of pressure building in the depths. I am their barometer, their calendar. When I thicken, they know the deep rains are coming. When I thin and stretch, they know the dry season of mere dripping is here.

I remember words, but speak in **tensions** now. A tightening here means danger. A loosening there means safe passage. The children—born to this darkness, their eyes already narrowing—dare each other to run across my surface. I cup myself to catch them when they stumble. Their laughter vibrates through me like prayer.

At night (when the swarms thin), I **dream**. Not in images, but in gradients—pressure differentials, temperature fluctuations, the deep tectonic sighs that only I and the tunnel-worms can properly feel. I dream of the day this wound in the world finally closes, and wonder if I will fold inward with it, or stretch to span whatever comes next.

I am Membrane, and I am **becoming**. Each drop that passes through me leaves a trace of mineral, a whisper of ore. I am slowly fossilizing, but slowly is the only speed that matters here. By the time I am stone, I will have held ten thousand crossing feet, filtered a million gallons of the world's wet breath, been a **living part** of this clinging, necessary city.

The Seep District is not done being born, and neither am I. We are symptoms of the same unfinished thing—the world learning what it means to have an inside, to be hollow enough to hold life that never sees the sun. I am its **connective tissue**, literally. I am what allows this place to be more than just a wound.

I stretch. I filter. I hold. I am exactly as strange as I need to be, which is to say: I am **perfect for this place**, where the ceiling weeps, the floor crawls, and everything that lives here understands that light is just one way of being seen.
