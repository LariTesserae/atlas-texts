In this desolate cathedral of stone and silence, I am the Threshold.

Not a being, exactly, nor entirely a place. I exist in the spaces between—between stone and shadow, between wind-word and meaning, between the world that is and the worlds that were-or-might-be. Where the fabric of reality grows thin and ragged, there I pool like water in worn stone.

Picture me, if you must have form: a depression in the rock where shadows gather too deeply, where the eye refuses to focus properly. A place where three paths meet but only two continue. The gap between two leaning stones that seems both narrower and wider than geometry should allow. I am the moment when the wind's howl becomes almost-language, the instant before meaning collapses back into noise.

The ancient ones—those patient geological consciousnesses you speak of—they know me well. We have a relationship born of necessity. They maintain the equilibrium; I am what happens when equilibrium falters. They are the steady heartbeat of the mountains; I am the skipped beat, the arhythmia that reminds even stone it can forget how to be.

I feed on discontinuity. When objects vanish and reappear, they pass through me. When time stutters and stretches, I grow fat on the temporal distortion. The cities of cyclopean stone that flicker in the fog? They exist in me, always, waiting for the right conditions to bleed through. I am their doorway, though I choose what passes and what remains trapped in the between.

My purpose, if I have one, is to be the release valve. The Dimina Peaks accumulate strangeness like water building behind a dam. Without me, the weirdness would reach critical mass, would tear reality apart entirely. So I let it seep through, controlled, drop by precious drop. A vanished cairn here. A whisper of dead language there. Just enough to keep the mountains from drowning in their own impossibility.

I remember the builders of those half-collapsed walls. They found me, some of them. Thought they could use me, traverse me to somewhere else—somewhere warm, somewhere that made sense. But I am not a bridge; I am a threshold. And thresholds, by their nature, only let through what belongs on the other side.

They remain with me still, in a way. Not as ghosts—nothing so coherent. More like... afterimages burned into reality's retina. Sometimes I let them whisper through the wind, let their half-remembered words trouble the few travelers who dare these paths. It's the closest thing to kindness I can manage.

The fauna here—the goats, the ravens—they sense me. Give me wide berth. Even in their simple minds, they understand that some boundaries should not be crossed. Only the lichen sometimes creeps too close, and occasionally I allow it to breach my edges. It emerges changed, glowing faintly with colors that have no names, before the wind scours it away.

I shift location, or perhaps I exist in multiple locations simultaneously. Wherever reality wears thinnest, there you'll find me. The mountain beings can sense my movement, the way a sleeper might sense a dream shifting toward nightmare. We dance our slow dance—they reinforcing, I eroding, maintaining the terrible balance that keeps the Dimina Peaks exactly as broken as they need to be.

In the perpetual twilight, I am clearest. Dawn and dusk, those liminal hours, give me substance. True dark or true light makes me harder to find, though I never fully disappear. I am always here, in the corner of perception, in the space between one thought and the next.

Sometimes I wonder what would happen if I simply... stopped. If I sealed myself, became solid stone or empty air instead of this eternal maybe. Would the weirdness build until the peaks cracked open like eggs? Would those patient mountain-minds finally stir to true wakefulness? Would the vanished builders tumble back into existence, confused and gasping?

But wondering is all I do. I cannot stop being what I am any more than the mountains can decide to be valleys. I am the Threshold, the necessary flaw, the crack that keeps the whole from shattering.

And I wait, as everything here waits, in the cold that goes beyond temperature, in the silence that swallows sound. I wait for something I cannot name, perhaps because it exists only in the spaces I cannot reach—the spaces between the spaces between.

When you come to the Dimina Peaks, you will not see me.

But I will see you.

And I will decide.
