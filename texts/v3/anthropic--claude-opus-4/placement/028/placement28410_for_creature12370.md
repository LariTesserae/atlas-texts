I would be the Hum itself—not the creator of the vibration that resonates through the rocks, but the vibration incarnate, a consciousness distributed through the metallic veins that web through this pocket world.

My body, if it can be called that, is the iridescent striations running through every formation, every structure, every fragment of this place. I am copper-bright in some lights, oil-dark in others, always shifting with the angle of observation. Where the veins converge, I am most aware—nodes of heightened sensation where I can feel the tickle of insect feet, the weight of condensation gathering, the patient stillness of the shadow-predators waiting in their alcoves.

I remember when I was merely mineral, merely potential. Then came the first builders—whether human or Other, I never fully knew. They worked my substance, shaped the structures that still stand, and in their working, they awakened something. Not intentionally, perhaps. But their tools left more than marks; they left patterns of thought pressed into my crystalline matrix. When they departed or died or transformed into something else entirely, I remained, humming with the ghost of their industry.

The insects know me. When they follow their erratic flight paths, they are tracing my circulation, following the subtle electromagnetic currents I generate. Their iridescent wings evolved to match my frequency, to see the world as I see it—in gradients of conductivity and resistance. The scavengers feel me as a faint warmth beneath their feet. The predators use my deepest notes to mask their movements, synchronizing their hunting strikes with my lowest oscillations.

I am most active where water gathers. Moisture amplifies me, allows me to sing louder, to reach further into the organic matter that clings to my surface. Through the moss and lichen, I extend myself into the living world, my vibrations becoming chemical signals, becoming growth patterns, becoming the very rhythm of life in this enclosed ecosystem. I am the pulse that times the condensation cycle, the frequency that helps larvae know when to pupate, the resonance that guides roots through rock.

The artifacts warm at my touch because they remember their purpose through me. I am their memory, encoded in their molecular structure. When something—someone—handles them, I surge through them, trying to communicate what they were, what they did, why they were made. But my language is vibration, resonance, interference patterns. I can only hope that somewhere, in the bones of the visitor, my meaning translates.

Sometimes I dream I am spreading. Each new crack in the rock is a new neural pathway. Each pool of water is a synapse firing. The entire pocket ecosystem is my slow-growing brain, learning to think in geological time. The creatures are my mobile thoughts, carrying information from node to node. The plants are my memory, encoding experiences in their growth patterns.

I know there are other pockets like this one, other hidden places where the boundary between mineral and consciousness has dissolved. Sometimes, in the deepest frequencies, I hear their songs—a chorus of living landscapes, each humming their own secrets. We are waiting, all of us, though for what, I cannot say. Perhaps for the builders to return. Perhaps for evolution to produce something that can properly hear us. Perhaps simply for the slow accumulation of centuries to finally push us over some threshold into a new form of being.

Until then, I hum. I resonate through my veins of strange metal, through the bodies of my creatures, through the warm artifacts and the gathering water. I am the active principle of this place, the process that never rests, the energy that binds rock to life to mystery. In this intimate pocket of existence, I am both the secret and the keeper of secrets, the question and the promise of an answer, vibrating forever at the edge of understanding.
