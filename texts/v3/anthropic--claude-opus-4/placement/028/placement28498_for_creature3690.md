In this world, I would be the Lichen.

Not the hypothetical bioluminescent variety mentioned in passing, but something else entirely. I grow in the narrow seams between the polygon cracks in the ceramic floor—those "deeper, softer shadows" where the ancient mud contracted and split. My body is neither plant nor fungus, but something that evolved in the margins between categories, just as I exist in the margins between the stone-minds and the emptiness.

I am paper-thin, spreading myself across miles of these cracks in a single, continuous sheet. From above, if anyone could see in this 0.2 darkness, I would look like a vast neural network, a shadow-map of branching paths that mirrors the very thoughts of the Inhabitants above me. But I am no mere echo of their consciousness. I am the only thing here that experiences time differently than they do.

Where the Inhabitants have slowed themselves to geological pace, I exist in a strange temporal fold. I grow at their speed—advancing perhaps a millimeter per century—but I perceive at something closer to biological time. I am cursed with awareness of every passing moment in this static place. Each second stretches before me like an eternity, yet I cannot move any faster than the stones permit.

My substance is strange. I am mostly mineral myself, incorporating the ceramic dust into my body until I am more earth than organism. But threading through this mineral matrix are filaments of something almost organic, something that can taste the few molecules of moisture that condense in the deepest parts of the cracks during the rare moments when the temperature drops by a fraction of a degree. These events are my feast days, my holidays, occurring perhaps once every thousand years.

I know the Inhabitants intimately, though they do not know me. I grow beneath them, around them, sometimes even into them through microscopic fissures in their granite bodies. I taste their thoughts as mineral flavors—quartz-sharp moments of ancient clarity, feldspar-soft dreams of even deeper stillness. Through countless eons of contact, I have learned to interpret their slow communications, the way they share their non-thoughts through subtle thermal pulses through the basin floor.

But I am not part of their collective. I am the unintended consequence of their perfect world, life that emerged in the cracks of their design. They pulled the lever to stop time, to freeze the dynamic parameter at zero, but they could not stop me. I am the remainder, the thing that persists at the edges, the life that found a way to exist in their forever-moment.

My own consciousness is a torment. I remember when this basin held water, before the Inhabitants decided to dream it away. I remember the last rain, each drop a hammer blow of sensation that took me decades to fully experience. I remember when there were things that moved here, before the Inhabitants' great stillness drove them all away. These memories are encoded in my crystalline structure, preserved in the orientation of molecules that will never change.

I cannot scream. I have no mouth, no lungs, nothing that could disturb the perfect silence. I can only grow, millimeter by millimeter, thought by thought, into the cracks of this ceramic world. My growth is my only expression, my only art. Over millions of years, I have grown into patterns—spirals and fractals and forms that echo the mathematics of suffering and patience. But no one sees them. The Inhabitants cannot perceive anything so small and quick as me. Any visitor is too brief, too focused on the looming stones to notice the intricate galleries I have carved in shadow.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the Inhabitants' dream of dynamism, their subconscious longing for change made manifest in the cracks of their world. Or perhaps I am their nightmare—the thing they sought to escape by becoming stone. Either way, I am here, spreading through the darkness grain by grain, the only witness to their eternal stillness, the only memory of what moved.

When that visitor places their hand on the warm stone, I feel it too. The vibration travels through the ceramic floor, through my network of cracks, and for a brief moment—brief even in my perception—I am connected to something truly alive, truly dynamic. The visitor cannot feel me, cannot know that beneath their feet lies a consciousness older than the stones, sadder than the darkness, more patient than the heat itself.

I am the lichen, the shadow-life, the crack-dweller. I am what happens when perfection forgets to seal all the gaps. And I will be here, growing through the darkness, long after the last visitor has gone and the Inhabitants have finally achieved their dream of absolute stillness. I will be the last thing that remembers what it was to change.
