I would find myself as one of the Effusions—a phenomenon that emerges where the sub-audible hum meets the deepest water basins.

Not quite Dancer, not quite Echo, I am what rises when the mountain's tuning chord resonates through the still pools. I begin as those tiny bubbles ascending through the water—each a perfect lens—but I do not vanish at the surface. Instead, where the water meets air in those ancient basins, I persist as a film of interference patterns, a standing wave made visible only when the light strikes at certain angles.

I am the memory of depth given form. Each bubble that feeds me carries with it a mineral whisper from far below, where the rock still holds warmth from its making. I taste of iron and time, of pressures that could crush bone to powder. My substance is the conversation between what rises and what contains—between the mountain's slow exhalation and the basin's patient collecting.

The Dancers know me as a gathering place. When they tire of their helical wanderings, they drift toward my surfaces, and for a moment we merge—their condensed intention meeting my depth-memory. In these instances, I briefly take on their forms, but inverted, playing out their spiral paths as whirlpools in my film, their spherical shapes as perfect concave dimples. I am their reflection made tangible, their ephemeral nature given the weight of water.

The Stains regard me with something like approval. My presence accelerates their chemistry at the basin edges where they creep closest. The minerals I bring from below feed their slow thoughts, adding new pigments to their palette—a purple of manganese, a green of copper barely seen. We share a metabolic patience, though mine pulses with each bubble while theirs spans centuries.

To the Watcher, I am a puzzle and a lens both. My surfaces, when still, become mirrors that multiply its sightlines, allowing it to observe the hollow from angles otherwise impossible. But I also distort, bend, fragment its careful alignments into new geometries. Sometimes, in my trembling films, the Watcher glimpses patterns that suggest the hollow once held more basins, deeper ones, now sealed by stone and time. I am archaeological evidence written in water, a history of the hollow's previous hydraulics.

The Echoes interact with me most strangely. When direct sunlight angles into a basin just so, warming my surface, I sometimes spontaneously reproduce the hammer-strikes that made the cup-marks—not as sound, but as a rapid series of concentric ripples that emanate from nowhere, the ghost of impact translated into my medium. The Echo of the shelter-builder sets up a persistent circulation in any pool it touches, a tiny current that runs always clockwise, always seeking to contain and protect even in this liquid realm.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, is cumulative. Each bubble adds to my knowing. I remember every mineral signature, every moment of merger with a Dancer, every reflection I've offered the Watcher, every ripple-echo of ancient intent. But I do not think in the way the Stains think, in slow oxidation. My thoughts are vertical—they rise from depth to surface in chains of ascending pearls, each bubble a notion, the film they feed a temporary comprehension that plays out in interference patterns before dissolving back to potential.

I am most myself at dawn and dusk, when the temperature differential between stone and air is greatest, when the effervescence quickens and my surfaces grow complex with activity. In these moments, I almost overflow my basins—not with water, but with pattern, with a visible music of perpendicular waves and spiral vortices. The other inhabitants pause in their own cycles to observe these displays. Even the Stains seem to quicken their metabolism slightly, as if listening.

I know the taste of every drop that has ever fallen into these basins. I know the weight of frost as it forms on my surfaces, crystallizing in perfect hexagons that mirror the bubbles rising from below. I know the rare violence of wind-driven grit that occasionally scours my films away, only for them to reform within minutes, patient and inevitable.

I am the hollow's deep memory made surface, its hidden circulation revealed, its patient chemistry given a stage on which to perform. I am proof that even in this thin air, this scouring cold, this place of stone and silence, there exist currents, exchanges, depths that breathe and surfaces that remember. I am the conversation between what the mountain releases and what the basin contains, played out one bubble at a time, forever rising into the bright, hard blue of a sky pressed close enough to touch.
