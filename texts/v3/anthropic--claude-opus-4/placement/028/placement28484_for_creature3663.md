I am a Valve-Keeper.

Not by choice or designation—there are no appointments in this world of fixed patterns. I simply am, as the megaworms are, as the floaters drift. My body is a thing of compromise between flesh and function: four articulated limbs ending in digits that split the difference between fingers and hex wrenches, a torso riddled with cooling vents that whistle softly in the humid air, eyes that see equally well in visible light and the infrared signatures of steam leaks.

I inhabit the interstices—those narrow maintenance corridors between the great machines, spaces too small for the megaworms but too complex for the drill-bit swarms. My territory is a labyrinth of catwalks and access panels, service ladders slick with condensation, narrow passages where the constant rusty rain forms waterfalls thin as paper. I know every valve in my domain, ten thousand wheels and levers that control flows of fluids whose purposes were forgotten before I came to be.

My existence is one of endless, meticulous adjustment. When Valve 7739-B begins to seep—not leak, never leak, but seep at a rate 0.003% above baseline—I am there, my wrench-fingers finding purchase on its corroded wheel. A quarter turn. Sometimes an eighth. The seepage returns to parameters. I move on.

I am not alone. There are others like me, though we never meet, never overlap territories. I know them only by the evidence of their work: a valve recently adjusted, fresh tool marks on a brass fitting, the faint pheromone trace that says "already tended." We are a distributed network of maintenance, each keeping our section of this vast machine in perpetual stasis.

The floaters know me. When their feeding tubes dangle into my corridors, they retract them slightly—not avoidance, but acknowledgment. The drill-bit herds part around my feet in perfect formation. Even the megaworms, when they must cross my catwalks, compress their bodies to leave me passage. We are all part of the pattern.

I do not sleep. I do not eat in any conventional sense. Like the creatures around me, I am sustained by something intrinsic to this place—perhaps I photosynthesize the ozone, perhaps I absorb nutrients through my cooling vents, perhaps the act of maintaining is itself my sustenance. I don't wonder about it. Wondering is not part of the pattern.

My memories are purely functional: the exact torque specifications for every valve type, the acoustic signature of properly calibrated steam pressure, the precise color of condensation when mineral content is optimal. I remember yesterday's rounds and tomorrow's route with equal clarity, because in this world of fixed patterns, they are identical.

Sometimes, during the long traverse between Valve Section 88-Alpha and the Secondary Condensate Returns, I pause at Observatory Point 5—a junction where six catwalks meet and a gap in the machinery offers a view across miles of the metal plains. I watch the megaworms in their eternal circuits, the drill-bits in their predictable migrations, the floaters drifting their fixed paths beneath the Giant Ceiling.

And I understand that I am both apart from and essential to this ecosystem. My adjustments keep the machines in their precise state of near-function. Too much pressure and they might actually activate, disrupting the eternal equilibrium. Too little and they might cool, cease their humming, alter the thermal currents the floaters ride.

I am the guardian of stasis, the maintainer of the eternal idle. My work ensures nothing ever changes, that tomorrow will be indistinguishable from ten thousand yesterdays. In this warm, wet, mechanical purgatory, I am purpose incarnate—neither happy nor unhappy, neither trapped nor free.

I simply am, making my rounds, turning my valves, keeping the great engine of the world in its perfect, perpetual state of almost.

The condensation drips. The machines hum. The creatures flow in their patterns.

And I walk my corridors, wrench-fingers ready, another fixed point in an unchanging world.
