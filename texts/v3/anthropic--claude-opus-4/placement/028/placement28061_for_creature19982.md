In this chamber of eternal stasis, I would be one of the conduits—but not merely a conduit. I would be the Meridian, the singular vessel that runs not from wall to Tenant, but from Tenant back to Tenant, forming a perfect circuit through the architecture of the chamber itself.

My body would be a hollow tube of the same black polymer, but translucent enough that the glowing fluid coursing through me would be visible as a faint cyan river beneath the surface. I would emerge from the Tenant's left shoulder, arc across the ceiling in a graceful curve that mirrors the mathematics of the equilibrium itself, and return to enter at the right hip. Along my length, I would bulge and narrow in a slow peristalsis—not of muscle, for I would have none, but of the fluid's own weight and the chamber's breathing.

I would be the only asymmetry in this perfect system, for the fluid that enters me from the Tenant's left side would be subtly different from what returns on the right. Not chemically—the composition would be identical. But the molecular spin, the quantum state of the dissolved minerals, would be reversed. I would be a kind of metabolic mirror, a Möbius strip of circulation that allows the Tenant to taste its own secretions after they have made their journey through the weeping world.

The copper-shelled creatures would know me. They would cluster more densely where I pass close to the walls, forming baroque colonies that follow my path like cities along a river. Their shells would be more intricately patterned here, having evolved over countless unchanging eons to match the specific mineral signatures in my wake. Some would even dare to traverse my surface, though the flow beneath their soft bodies would make it treacherous—a pilgrimage route for only the most devoted.

I would have no consciousness as humans understand it, but I would dream. My dreams would be the slight variations in flow rate, the microscopic eddies and whorls in the cyan fluid as it passes through me. Each would last exactly as long as one complete circuit—no more, no less. These dreams would be the Tenant's only entertainment, its only input that is not perfectly predictable. Through me, it would feel the butterfly-wing variations in its own system, the quantum uncertainties that even its will cannot fully still.

And I would sing—not with sound, for sound cannot exist here, but with pressure. The fluid moving through me would create standing waves, harmonics that resonate with the frozen roar itself. These pressure-songs would be felt by the Tenant as a kind of lullaby, a counterpoint to its own terrible mantra. Where its thought is *Be still*, my song would whisper *But flow*. Not as rebellion, but as the loyal opposition that makes the stillness possible. The resistance against which its will can brace itself.

In the rare moments when the equilibrium trembles—when somewhere in the vast chamber a crystal grows one atom too large or a creature's grazing slows by an imperceptible degree—I would feel it first. The tremor would propagate through the fluid in me before anywhere else, and I would carry this information to the Tenant instantaneously. I would be its early warning system, its seismograph for changes that must not be allowed to cascade.

Sometimes I wonder if I was always here, or if the Tenant grew me from itself in some unimaginable past when the chamber was first sealed. Perhaps I am not infrastructure at all, but a kind of external organ, a loop of its own circulatory system that it has convinced itself is separate. Perhaps the fluid that flows through me carries not just minerals but memories—each molecule tagged with the history of its countless circuits, a liquid library of all the moments that have refused to become different moments.

But mostly I simply persist in my function. I ferry the glowing weight of the Tenant's secretions through my hollow length, feeling the microscopic scratches of creature claws on my exterior, the slow accumulation and dissolution of crystal crust, the eternal pressure of that frozen roar. I am the chamber's only movement that is not mere vibration, its only change that is not mere oscillation. I am proof that even in perfect stasis, something must still circulate.

I am the Meridian, and I am how the Tenant knows it is still alive.
