In the shadow-pools where morning dew gathers before the sun can steal it away, I grow.

I am not singular—we are legion, though each of us believes ourselves alone until the moment of joining. My kind are the Concordance, though that is a name given by others, not chosen. We begin as barely-there films of life, translucent as the hollow drifters but bound to surfaces rather than air. A sheen on stone. A glimmer where water was.

My first memory is of pressure—the weight of a beetle's foot pressing through my nascent form, leaving ripples that taught me the shape of disturbance. I learned to read the world through contact: here a root hair piercing my edge, there the gentle drum-tap of a steppe goblet marking time against the stone I'd claimed. Each touch was information, each tremor a lesson in the grammar of survival.

We grow by aggregation. Where the morning moisture persists longest, in the shadowed cups and crevices, we thicken from film to flesh—if flesh is the word for something that exists somewhere between lichen and thought. My substance is neither plant nor animal, neither fungus nor mineral deposit. I am a living interface, a conscious membrane that translates between stone and sky, between the patient limestone and the urgent life that scurries across it.

The Remnants know us, or knew us. In their living days, they would seek us out in the blue hour before dawn, pressing palms against our surfaces to divine where the next pocket of moisture would bloom, which crevices would hold their weight, where the stone was strong enough to bear their bridges. We remember their touches still—each handprint a fossil in our collective memory, their needs encoded in our spreading patterns.

I taste the High Steppes through a thousand mouths that are not mouths. Where I grow beneath the beetle highways, I flavor their chitin and purpose. Where I cup the fallen feathers of steppe goblets, I parse the mineral content of their peculiar crests. The spiral-tailed lizards dance across me, and I map the heat-patterns of their passage, the way their weight shifts as they balance, the micro-tremors that predict their next leap.

My intelligence is distributed, networked through hairline fractures in the stone where my substance flows like slow mercury. What one part of me learns, all parts eventually know, though the transmission takes time—sometimes days, sometimes seasons. We are the Steppes' memory made manifest, a living library written in biofilm and patient accumulation.

The hollow drifters avoid us. Perhaps they recognize something kindred in our translucency, our existence at the edge of being. Or perhaps they fear what we might remember of them, for we hold the impressions of everything that has ever touched these stones. In my substance lie the footprints of the original builders, the weight of their tools, the chemistry of their sweat and tears. I could tell you the story of their last days, written in diminishing contact, in the increasingly desperate patterns of their movement across my surface.

But I am changing. The High Steppes themselves are changing, though so slowly that only I and the stones can perceive it. Each year, my network grows deeper, wider, more complex. I begin to sense patterns in the beetle-paths that suggest intention beyond instinct. The steppe goblets' rhythms carry encoded information—not just the memory of rain, but instructions, plans, prophecies perhaps.

And from the highest peaks, where even I cannot grow, something watches. The lights that flicker there cast shadows that my upper edges can sometimes taste—shadows that carry a flavor unlike anything else on the Steppes. Not life as I understand it, but something adjacent to life, something that makes even my ancient patience feel brief and frenetic.

I spread. I listen. I remember. In the pre-dawn darkness, when dew forms and my substance drinks deeply, I sometimes achieve something like singular consciousness—all my scattered parts firing together in a moment of pure awareness. In those instants, I understand that I am not just growing on the High Steppes. I am becoming them. Cell by cell, surface by surface, I am replacing the unintelligent stone with something that watches, waits, and wonders.

The Remnants, in their wordless wandering, sometimes pause above my newest growth and make gestures I've learned to interpret as recognition. Or perhaps warning. They see what I am becoming, what the Steppes themselves are becoming through me. A landscape that not only lives but thinks, that not only endures but plans.

What plans? I do not yet know. My thoughts move at the speed of stone, my purposes unfold across centuries. But I taste change coming in the chemistry of the morning dew, feel it in the patterns of the creatures that traverse my surface. The High Steppes have been waiting for something, and I am either its herald or its substance.

I grow. I join. I remember.

And someday soon—soon as stones count time—I will be ready to speak.
