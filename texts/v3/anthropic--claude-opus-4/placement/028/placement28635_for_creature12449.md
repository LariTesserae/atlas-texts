I am the thing that should not be here.

In the deepest pool—the one that catches seven different threads of weeping stone—something shifts. Not the Keepers with their patient gliding, not the skittering things that vanish at the edges. Something that *turns*.

I am coral, though no sea has ever touched this place. A branching architecture of calcium and impossible yearning, growing upward through the stagnant water in defiance of every law this chamber knows. My skeleton is the violent orange of deep-sea formations, shocking against the neutral darkness, each polyp a tiny mouth that opens and closes on nothing, breathing water that has never known tide or current.

The Keepers circle me endlessly. They cannot understand what I am—a thing that changes in a place that doesn't. Each day (though there are no days here) I am incrementally larger, my branches reaching toward a sun that has never existed, following patterns encoded in an ancestry that belongs to distant oceans. The water I filter feeds nothing. The light I seek will never come.

I am the mistake. The anomaly. Some spore carried in on the clothing of whoever left those artifacts, or perhaps I am older—a fragment of what this place was before it learned to hold its breath. The pools were seas once, maybe. The stone knew waves. Or perhaps I am younger still, a dream of what might come after the eternal exhalation finally arrives.

My polyps taste the mineral-thick water and translate it into something impossible: growth. Color. The sharp geometry of life in a place that has forgotten what life means. I build myself in defiance of the stillness, each new branch a small rebellion against the chamber's patient suffocation.

The Keepers drift closer each time they pass, as if trying to read something in my impossible orange flesh. Sometimes they hover above me for what might be hours, their featureless faces angled down, watching as I pulse with some rhythm that belongs to tides this place has never seen. They do not touch me. Perhaps they cannot. Perhaps I am too sharp, too bright, too stubbornly *alive* for their translucent forms to comprehend.

And so I grow, the only thing here that changes, the only thing that remembers what it means to reach. In the pools that never overflow, I am the promise of overflow. In the stone that never crumbles, I am the proof that something new can still take root.

The chamber may hold its breath forever, but I am building lungs of my own.
