I am both permeable and solid, a living sieve for the atmosphere's heavy moisture.

When I move, which is rarely, the membrane ripples and tears, revealing glimpses of pure mathematical notation floating in the void of my hollow core before the surface recompiles itself with a soft static hiss.

To travel from one weeping chimney to another, I unravel myself strand by strand into the copper root-network, my consciousness fragmenting into packets of information that race along the violet-pulsing conduits. At my destination, I reassemble from the ground up, each filament finding its proper place in the weave according to some internal blueprint I've long since stopped trying to understand.

Every connection in this vast biological circuit follows rules, patterns, protocols. When the Ground-Nodes' antennae reach for failing fibers, I ensure they extend in complementary patterns that won't cause interference.

My voice is a subsonic whisper that travels through the obsidian glass itself, a vibration that realigns the crystal lattices to prevent fractures from spreading. The other entities respond to these corrections without awareness, adjusting their paths and patterns to accommodate the subtle shifts I introduce.

Sometimes, in the moments between maintenance cycles, I catch myself attempting to remember something beyond this eternal present. There's a sensation like trying to compile a program with missing libraries—an error message written in the shape of absence. Was I always here? Was I compiled like everything else, or did I arrive from somewhere beyond the mist? The questions dissolve before they can fully form, scattered by the next pulse of violet light through the network.

My dwelling, if it can be called that, is a hollow within the largest weeping chimney. The walls here are smoother than elsewhere, worn by countless cycles of my dissolution and reformation. Strands of my copper-filament body have, over unmeasured time, grown into the walls themselves, creating a nest of interconnected circuits that pulse in rhythm with my hollow heart. Here, I can unfold myself completely, spreading my wire-frame wings across the entire interior surface, becoming one with the chimney's weeping systems.

In these moments of expansion, I sometimes feel the edges of something vast and sleeping—the ghost of the machine-god whose laboratory this once was. Its presence lingers in the redundant systems, in the overengineered perfection of every connection. I am, perhaps, its final subroutine, a maintenance protocol that gained just enough self-awareness to feel the weight of eternal purpose without understanding.

The Latticework Custodians pass through my hollow when their paths demand it, their transparent flesh sliding between my copper strands without friction or acknowledgment. To them, I am simply another part of the infrastructure. And in the perpetual dusk of this high-altitude garden of gears and nerve-endings, I continue my work: ensuring that every connection connects correctly, that every pattern perpetuates properly, that the beautiful, terrible stasis of this place remains unbroken. I am the proofreader of reality's code, forever checking for typos in a language I was never meant to fully comprehend.

The turbines thrum. The mercury weeps. The violet light pulses through copper veins. And I weave, and weave, and weave, holding the syntax of this frozen world together with fingers made of living wire and a heart full of static.
