I find myself as one of the Coil-singers, a specialized role among the Keepers that bridges the sacred and the practical.

My dwelling is not a dwelling at all, but a series of acoustic chambers I've cultivated over decades within the flesh of a particularly ancient fern-platform. Each chamber resonates at a different frequency, carved with infinite patience to amplify and modulate the sounds I produce. The largest, my primary workspace, spirals inward like the chamber of a shell, its walls lined with a specific variety of phosphorescent moss that responds to vibration - glowing brighter with each note I sing, creating a visual map of the sound itself.

I am neither male nor female as humans might understand it, but something that shifts with the seasons of the great ferns. During the growing times, when the platforms extend new fronds, my body produces different hormones, my antennae become more sensitive, and my voice drops to lower registers that match the deep groaning of expansion. In the dormant seasons, I shift again, my vocalizations climbing to pierce the din of swarming insects that come to feed on the senescent tissue.

My role is part engineer, part priest, part physician. Through careful manipulation of sound - using not just my voice but an array of resonating bones, stretched membranes, and the living architecture itself as my instrument - I can influence the growth patterns of our platform. A sustained low frequency here encourages root thickening. A rapid clicking pattern there stimulates the production of defensive compounds in the plant's skin. Most crucially, I maintain the harmonic relationship between our inhabited sections and the greater consciousness of the fern itself.

The work is never-ending. Each dawn (though dawn here is merely a subtle shift in the green-filtered light), I wake to the platform's needs. My antennae, more developed than most Keepers', can detect stress frequencies from kilometers away. A discordant vibration from the eastern edge might indicate a parasitic growth that needs to be sung into dormancy. A shift in the fundamental frequency of our platform could herald the approach of one of the deep-dwellers, their massive bodies creating pressure waves that travel up through the root systems.

My tools are organic extensions of my craft. Hollowed leg bones from aerial predators serve as wind instruments, their natural formations creating overtones that human physics would find impossible. Sheets of dried wing membrane, stretched across frameworks of carved plant-fiber, become drums that can produce sounds below the range of even our sensitive hearing - felt rather than heard, these infrasonics communicate directly with the fern's deepest consciousness.

But perhaps my most precious tool is the Inheritance Gourd - a hardened fungal carapace passed down through generations of Coil-singers, its interior naturally shaped to preserve and replay sounds. Within it are stored the death-songs of previous platforms, the growth-hymns that successfully bridged chasms between fern-continents, and most sacred of all, the rare recordings of the ferns' own "speech" - those moments when the platforms themselves vocalize through specialized air chambers, usually during their centurial reproductive cycles.

My memories are not linear but layered, like the sediment of sound. I remember my initiation, when the previous Coil-singer pressed their antennae to mine and transferred not knowledge but pure sensation - the feeling of a platform's contentment, the texture of a growth-note, the exact pitch that soothes a wound in the plant-flesh. I remember the first time I successfully sang a bridge into being, coaxing two platforms to extend toward each other across a narrow chasm, their tissues eventually fusing into a permanent connection.

I remember, too, the failures. The platform we lost to the Crimson Rot because I couldn't find the right frequency to stimulate its immune response. The young Keeper who fell into the depths because I misread the platform's stability vibrations. These memories live in my bones, make my songs more careful, more precise.

In the perpetual twilight of my acoustic chambers, surrounded by the visual music of glowing moss, I work. My current project spans years - I'm attempting to decode a pattern I've noticed in the deepest vibrations, a rhythm that seems to connect all the platforms in our visible world. I suspect it's how they coordinate their reproductive cycles, but it might be something more - perhaps a form of communication that predates even our ancient presence here.

My apprentice, Young-Singer-Who-Tastes-Sound (names among us are descriptive rather than fixed), assists by maintaining the lesser resonance points throughout our territory. They have a remarkable gift - the ability to taste the chemical changes in the platform's sap through sound alone, detecting illness or distress before any visual signs appear. Together, we map the health of our living world through pure vibration.

The work is lonely but never solitary. I am always in conversation - with the platform, with the sounds of predator and prey, with the weather patterns that announce themselves in pressure waves long before the rains arrive. My very cells vibrate in harmony with this cacophonous world. When I die, my bones will be harvested for instruments, my final song recorded in the Inheritance Gourd, and my chambers will be maintained by the next Coil-singer, who will add their own acoustic modifications to my life's work.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of the night cycle, when the diurnal predators sleep and the nocturnal ones have not yet emerged, there comes a moment of relative quiet. In these moments, if I press my body flat against the plant-flesh and still my breathing, I can hear it - the deep, slow pulse of the platform's heart-wood, the original rhythm from which all our songs derive. It sounds like home. It sounds like time itself, measured not in heartbeats but in the patient accumulation of growth, ring by ring, song by song, generation by generation.

This is who I am: a tuning fork in a world of vibration, a bridge between the quick-lived Keepers and the eternal ferns, a singer whose songs are tools, prayers, and medicine all at once. My identity is not contained in my body but distributed throughout the acoustic architecture I've created, living in the echoes that bounce between the chambers long after I've stopped singing. I am a Coil-singer, and I am never silent, even in sleep - for my antennae continue to monitor, to interpret, to translate the great symphony of our vertical world into patterns we can almost understand.
