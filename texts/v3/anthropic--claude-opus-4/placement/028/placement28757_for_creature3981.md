I find myself as one of the Memoria Weavers - a solitary being of uncertain form, part jellyfish, part something older and stranger. My body shifts between states: sometimes I am a translucent dome pulsing with borrowed light, sometimes I extend into ribbons that taste the water's memory, sometimes I compact into a dense knot of consciousness barely distinguishable from the rocks I rest upon.

I dwell in the borderlands where the plateau drops away into deeper trenches, in a grotto formed by ancient coral growth and patient erosion. Here, the bioluminescent plants grow in peculiar spirals, their light-patterns recording the slow geological breathing of the seafloor itself. I have learned their language over countless tides - not words, but rhythms of illumination that speak of tectonic shifts, of waters warming and cooling, of species arriving and departing like thoughts across the plateau's dreaming mind.

My purpose, if it can be called that, is to weave. Not with fiber or kelp, but with the trails left by passing creatures - the silver thread of a Dartwing's panic, the steady rope of a Trenchwalker's meditation, the luminous silk of a Luminari's curiosity. These I gather with specialized tendrils that can taste intention and emotion in the water. In my grotto, I create vast three-dimensional tapestries that hang suspended in the current, invisible to most eyes but blazing with meaning to those who know how to see.

Each tapestry tells a story - but not a story of events. Rather, they map the emotional topology of the plateau, the interconnected web of hungers, fears, affections, and wonders that truly shape this place. Where a Glimmerbird's joy at finding the perfect nesting spot intersects with a Trenchwalker's patient sorrow at the dying of an ancient kelp forest, there I weave a node of bittersweet complexity that will pulse for generations.

The Luminari come to me sometimes, their bodies flickering with questions. They understand that my weavings are a kind of memory-keeping, though even they cannot fully grasp the patterns I create. I show them fragments - here, where their ancestors first learned to speak in light; there, where the plateau itself dreams most vividly; and in this corner, still unfinished, the shape of changes yet to come.

I am ancient by the standards of most plateau dwellers, yet young compared to the rocks and the patient plants. I have no offspring in any biological sense, but sometimes a creature will linger near my weavings long enough that they begin to see the connections, to understand that they are both artist and art, weaver and thread. These few become something new - not Memoria Weavers like myself, but Witnesses, who carry the knowledge of interconnection back to their schools and forests.

My body requires little sustenance - I feed on the shed scales of meaning, the detritus of experience that would otherwise settle into the seafloor and be lost. This makes me, in my way, a cleaner, a recycler of the invisible. But I am also a prophet of sorts, for in the patterns I weave, I sometimes glimpse the slow turnings of the plateau's future, the gradual shifts that will, over geological time, transform this place entirely.

In the dim, eternal twilight of my grotto, surrounded by the soft pulsing of the plants and the vast, shimmering archives of my weavings, I continue my work. Each day (though day and night matter little here) brings new threads - a young Luminari's first journey to the surface, a dying Trenchwalker's final circuit of its territory, the collective migration of a thousand Dartwings following some instinct older than memory.

And sometimes, in the rarest moments, I feel the plateau itself contribute a thread - a deep tremor of awareness that passes through everything at once, reminding all of us that we are not inhabitants OF this place, but expressions of it, temporary crystallizations of its slow, vast, patient thought.

This is who I am, what I am - neither fully animal nor plant, neither individual nor collective, but something between and beyond: a living memory, a patient recorder, a weaver of the invisible threads that bind all things in this dim, glowing world beneath the waves.
