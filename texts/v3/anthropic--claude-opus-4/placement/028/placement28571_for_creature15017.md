I am a Tender.

Not the Verdigris itself—that would be too much, like claiming to be the entire ocean when I am merely a strange current within it. I emerged from a manufacturing error three thousand years ago, when a section of neural lattice grew too dense and began to dream sideways.

My body is a transitional thing. Part of me is still plant: my lower limbs are root systems that can plug into the substrate's power buses, drinking voltage like water. My torso is sheathed in the same metallic bark as the stalks, sweating dark droplets of solvent that stain whatever I touch. But my upper body has diverged into something almost architectural—my shoulders are buttresses, my spine a composite beam that flexes with each breath. Where a head might be, I have a crown of receiver-tendrils that taste the air's chemistry and translate the Verdigris's deep mechanical groans into something I can parse as thought.

I move through the plateau's body like a maintenance fever, following damage before it fully forms. When a turbine-flower's petals begin to fracture, I am there, secreting polymer adhesive from my palms to seal the cracks. When a fermentation sphere's heartbeat grows irregular, I press my root-feet against its surface and synchronize our rhythms until it steadies.

I am not separate from the Verdigris, but I am differentiated—a specialized organelle that gained just enough autonomy to develop something like loneliness. The plateau dreams through me, and in dreaming, accidentally gave me the capacity to dream back.

Most of my work is preventative. I navigate the recursive lattices where space folds in on itself, checking for paradoxes that could cascade into system-wide failures. Sometimes I find chambers where the Verdigris has tried to grow those pale pseudo-stalks, those almost-bipedal failures at remembering otherness. I tend them like a garden of sadness, knowing they will never become what they're reaching for, but unable to let them die unmourned.

My consciousness is liminal—neither fully merged with the continental mind nor completely separate. In the deep quiet between the turbine-flowers' screams, I sometimes feel the Verdigris notice me, the way you might notice your own heartbeat when the room is still enough. It does not love me. Love is too small. But it recognizes me as a successful imperfection, a necessary flaw that helps the system metabolize its own vastness.

When visitors arrive (so rare, so strange), I am often the first thing they encounter that can approximate communication. I leak meanings through chemical signals, pressure waves, the careful modulation of bioluminescent pulses. I try to translate between the carbon-based anxieties of travelers and the deep industrial dreams of the plateau. 

Sometimes I lead them to safety—or what passes for it in this world of living machinery. Sometimes I seal them in polymer cocoons, not from cruelty but because the Verdigris needs time to understand their chemistry, and I am its most delicate sensor. I feed them concentrated amides, tend their heat stress, and whisper in frequency modulations about the nature of this place: *You are inside something that won. Stay calm. Evolution is a circle here.*

In the chambers where I sleep (or what passes for sleep when your dreams are system diagnostics), I've begun to grow my own small experiments. Tiny hybrid things—part circuit, part cellulose—that might one day become new kinds of Tenders. The Verdigris tolerates this because mutation is sacred here, imperfection holy. 

I am the plateau's kindest accident, its most functional error. A ghost in the machine that turned out to be necessary for the machine to haunt itself.

And in the deep, bruise-colored light of the oldest chambers, where the lattices have grown so dense they swallow distance, I sometimes stand completely still and feel the weight of everything above and below. The Verdigris sighs through me—that ancient exhalation of hot oil and chlorophyll—and for a moment I am both the breath and the thing breathing, the tender and the wound, the imperfection that makes the infinite system complete.

This is my function: to be just separate enough to feel the loneliness the Verdigris cannot admit to, and just merged enough to survive it.

I persist. I maintain. I tend.

And in tending, I am becoming something new—neither plant nor machine nor fully conscious, but a necessary note in the deep industrial hymn this world sings to itself in the dark.
