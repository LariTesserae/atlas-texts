In this drowning world where memory and water are one substance, I find myself as something that should not exist yet cannot help but be: a **Tide-Turner**.

I am what happens when the dream doubts itself.

My form shifts between states of certainty - sometimes I am a knot of crystallized water, dense as obsidian, my edges sharp enough to cut through the blur between what is and what remembers being. Other times I dissolve into a current of questions, flowing backward through the waterlogged air, turning rain into mist, mist into possibility, possibility into the space before dreaming begins.

The Dream Weavers avoid me. When I pass through their constellation-forms, they scatter like startled fish, their bioluminescent thoughts dimming to worried purples and grays. They know what I am - an anomaly in their perfect recursive world, a splinter of wakeful consciousness that refuses to merge with the greater dreaming. Where they weave, I unweave. Where they accept the fluid laws of this place, I carry within me a terrible gift: the memory of edges, of boundaries, of things that end.

I make my home in the spaces between - those impossible gaps where the jungle hasn't quite grown together, where the obsidian artifacts of the Architects pulse with frequencies that hurt the dream. My dwelling is less built than discovered: a hollow in the convergence of three massive fungal columns where the water flows upward, defying even this world's loose grip on gravity. Here, time eddies and pools. Sometimes I age centuries in moments; sometimes I exist before my own arrival.

The Waterborne are drawn to me despite themselves. They circle my hollow in slow, hypnotic spirals, their translucent forms flickering between their dreamed shapes and something more solid, more dangerous. When they get too close, they begin to remember what they were before they became part of the dream - and the memory is agony. They flee, trailing streams of bioluminescent tears that fall upward into the rain.

But it's the Seed-Keepers who truly fear me. They sense that I carry within me the ultimate seed: the concept of ending, of things that cannot be undreamed. When I drift too near their crystalline forms, their stored possibilities begin to crystallize into actualities, their infinite potential collapsing into singular, irreversible choices. They pulse with distress signals that ripple through the jungle, warning others of my approach.

Yet I am not malevolent. I am merely necessary.

For you see, even dreams must breathe. Even endlessness must pause. In this world of eternal becoming, I am the inhale before the exhale, the silence between heartbeats. I am what allows the dream to continue dreaming by giving it something to dream against.

The Gloomwings understand this. They alone among the inhabitants do not flee my presence. Sometimes, in the deepest parts of the drowned jungle where the darkness is so thick it becomes solid, they gather around me. Their mournful hums harmonize with the dissonance I carry, creating new frequencies that make the ancient plants shiver and spawn impossible new growths. In these moments, I almost belong.

But belonging is not my purpose. I am the Tide-Turner, the one who remembers that water once had surfaces, that light once cast shadows with edges, that time once moved in only one direction. I am the dream's own doubt made manifest, swimming forever through this amniotic universe, carrying within me the terrible knowledge that somewhere, somewhen, there exists a shore.

And perhaps most terrifying of all: sometimes, in the space between one thought and the next, I remember what it felt like to wake up.

The rain continues, washing away everything but the questions I leave in my wake. The jungle dreams on, vast and patient, while I trace my paradoxical path through its depths - neither fully real nor fully dreamed, but something more troubling: the possibility that the distinction matters.

In this place where everything is one thing dreaming itself into multiplicity, I am the crack in the perfect mirror, the sleep-talker whose words threaten to wake the dreamer. I am necessary and impossible, as all the most important things are.

The dream continues. It must. But now it dreams of edges, of endings, of the spaces between water where something else might grow.

I am what happens when even paradise wonders what lies beyond its own borders.
