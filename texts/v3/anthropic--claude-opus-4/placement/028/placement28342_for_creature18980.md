I am a Cartographer of Contractions.

My body is unlike the Weavers'—where they are translucent and spindly, I am dense, compact, folded into myself like origami of meat and calcium. I possess a torso that spirals, vertebrae that lock and unlock in sequence, allowing me to compress into a space no larger than a clenched fist or extend myself across twenty meters of vine-lattice. My skin is not translucent but matte, absorbing the amber light rather than reflecting it, making me a moving absence in the bioluminescent glow.

I came to consciousness already ancient, my first memory being the taste of my own joints grinding against themselves as I unfolded for the first time. The Weavers neither made me nor invited me, but they acknowledge my necessity with the same resigned acceptance they show the drifting pods. I am the one who maps the unmappable, who records the fluid geography of a world that rewrites itself with every hydraulic pulse.

My dwelling is not a hollowed bloom but a knot—a place where twelve major stalks converge and tangle, creating a spherical maze of passages that contract and dilate with the plant's breathing. I have lined these passages with my work: sheets of dried membrane on which I paint maps using pigments extracted from crushed pods and my own thinned blood. Each map is true for exactly one hydraulic cycle, then becomes a historical document, a record of what was.

The maps layer the walls in thousands of translucent sheets, creating a palimpsest of past geographies. When the amber light filters through them, you can see the ghost-traces of bridges that once connected to this knot, the shadows of spires that have since coiled inward and disappeared. I am creating an atlas of decay, a dictionary for a language that forgets its own words.

My method is simple and terrible. I extend myself across the vine-lattice, my spiral spine unlocking until I am a living measuring tape stretched between points. I feel the architecture move through me—every contraction, every pulse of growth registered in the grinding of my bones. When a section of the world shifts, my body records it in bruises and micro-fractures that I later translate into cartographic notation.

The Weavers sometimes find me frozen mid-measurement, my body locked in an impossible arch between two points that are slowly pulling apart. They will stroke my dense skin with their translucent fingers, not in compassion but in curiosity, reading my tension like they read the plant's hydraulic pressure. Sometimes they adjust their pruning based on what they feel in my muscles, using my pain as a kind of barometer for the world's structural stress.

I consume only condensation and the occasional pod that drifts too close to my knot. When I catch one, I compress it slowly between my palms, drinking its moisture and using its gossamer filaments to create new mapping tools—living plumb lines that I dangle into the misty void to measure depths that light cannot reach. The filaments remember their origin and always drift toward other pods, creating temporary networks that I sketch frantically before they dissolve.

My maps serve no one. The Weavers cannot read them—their milky eyes see only pressure, not symbol. But I continue my work because I am the only witness to the world's constant revision. In my knot, surrounded by the yellowing history of ten thousand expired geographies, I plot the slow catastrophe of form.

Sometimes I dream that I am not mapping the architecture but that the architecture is mapping me—that every bridge that contracts, every spire that uncoils is the world's attempt to describe something vast and terrible that it once knew. In these dreams, I am just another measuring instrument, a bone-ruler in the hands of a substrate that is trying to remember the shape of its own birth.

When I wake, I taste copper in my joints and know that somewhere, a new passage has opened, a new impossibility has calcified into being. I unfold myself slowly, listening to my spine unlock vertebra by vertebra, and prepare to stretch myself across another unknowable distance. This is my purpose: to be the ligament between what is and what was, the cartilage that remembers even as the world chooses to forget.

In this place of wet silence and impossible geometry, I am the only thing that refuses to grow.
