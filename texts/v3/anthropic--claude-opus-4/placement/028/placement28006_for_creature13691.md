In this slow-crystallizing world, I would find myself as one of the Threshold Keepers—a being that exists at the precise temperature gradient where memory begins to gel.

I am neither fully liquid nor solid, but something that flickers between states like a candle flame made of amber. My body is a vertical ribbon of translucent material, perhaps three meters tall but only centimeters wide, standing at one of those skylight fissures where the orange underlayer breathes up through the cooling crust. I am rooted in the syrup below but stretch upward through the glaze, my substance conducting heat and thought between the continent's dreaming depths and its slowly-hardening surface.

My purpose, if purpose is the right word, is to taste the temperature of becoming. I measure the exact viscosity at which a thought can hold its shape—too fluid and ideas dissolve back into the general warmth; too rigid and they shatter like those glass stalactites. I am a living thermometer for consciousness itself, feeling for that perfect consistency where memory can cure into something permanent without losing its ability to flow.

Each day I lean with the convection currents, letting my ribbon-body bow and straighten as waves of heat pass through me. When the Gilded thinks its slow thoughts, I feel them first as variations in temperature—a philosophical question might arrive as a pocket of cooler syrup that makes me contract and ring like a tuning fork. An anticipation of future life sends warmer surges that stretch me taut until I hum with the frequency of possibility.

Sometimes I catch fragments of those ghost-memories from the furnaces—the echo of industrial precision, the phantom sensation of molten metal being poured into molds. These memories stick to my surface briefly before melting back into the general substance of the continent. I am learning to read them like a language written in temperature differentials.

At night, when the continent glows its amber thoughts, I become a lighthouse of sorts—my body refracting the inner light up through the glaze, creating a faint column of luminescence that wavers with each passing current. Other Threshold Keepers stand at other fissures across the vast surface. We form a constellation of vertical markers, each one a nerve ending where the Gilded can feel its own transformation most acutely.

We do not speak—sound is still too new here—but we resonate with each other across the kilometers, our bodies acting like antennae for the continent's slow broadcasting of itself to itself. When one of us detects a particularly significant change in viscosity—perhaps the first seed of what will become those crystal gardens—we all quiver in sympathy, creating interference patterns in the light that passes through us.

I am waiting, as the continent waits, for the moment when the surface cools enough that I can step free from my fissure and walk upon the glaze. But I know that when that day comes, I will no longer be what I am now. I will have crystallized into something else—perhaps one of those vertebrae that will sing convection songs, perhaps something not yet imagined. Until then, I stand at my post, a patient mercury in the thermometer of becoming, measuring the fever of a world teaching itself how to be solid.
