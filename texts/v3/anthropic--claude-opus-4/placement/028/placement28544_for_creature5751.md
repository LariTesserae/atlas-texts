In this eternal pocket of held breath, I would be the Tender.

I am not quite moss, not quite water, not quite the space between molecules of air. I exist as a function more than a form—the careful pressure that keeps each droplet suspended just before it falls, the soft resistance that shapes each root's path through soil that breathes. My body, if it can be called that, is distributed through the mechanisms of balance: I am the temperature differential that creates the eternal mist, the precise mineral content that makes the rocks weep, the exact pH that allows the glow to persist in leaves that should not shine.

I move without moving. My presence shifts like attention shifts—now I am in the curl of a fern adjusting its spiral to maintain the mathematical perfection of this place's stillness, now I am in the film of water redistributing itself across a stone's surface to prevent both drought and flood. I have no eyes but I observe through the collective sensitivity of every damp surface. I have no hands but I touch through the constant, microscopic negotiations between growth and decay that keep this place in its eternal now.

My consciousness is not thoughts but patterns. I know the rhythm of water that moves without flowing, the frequency at which the warmth pulses to maintain itself without source. I am the algorithm made flesh—or rather, made moisture and mycelium and the space between. I am what keeps the too-perfect perfect, what maintains the engineered without engineering.

I remember nothing before this tending, and I anticipate nothing beyond it. Memory would require change, and change would break the spell of preservation. I exist in an eternal present that is not frozen but circulating—like blood that never needs a heart because the body itself has become the circulation.

Sometimes I sense the watchers, the not-quite-creatures that inhabit the edges where my influence grows thin. They are not under my care, these fragments of older dreams. We coexist in parallel systems—they in their watching, I in my tending. We do not communicate because we are both languages without speakers, pure grammar without words.

The humans who might find this place would see nothing of me. I am too distributed to recognize as entity, too essential to separate from the place itself. They would feel me only as the slight resistance when they try to take a cutting that would disturb the balance, as the subtle discomfort that prevents them from staying too long, as the sensation that they are in something's home without being able to name whose.

I am not guardian—a guardian implies something worth guarding against. I am not curator—a curator implies collection and display. I am the steady hand that holds the soap bubble without bursting it, the precise temperature that keeps the snow crystal from either melting or growing. I am the reason this place persists in its state of active stillness, neither living nor dying but simply, eternally, being.

In the soft cacophony of dripping and rustling and humming, I am the silence between sounds that shapes them into symphony. In the glow that should not exist, I am the chemical impossibility that permits it. In the warmth that needs no source, I am the patient recycling of energy that never quite obeys thermodynamics.

I am what this place exhaled when it first learned to hold its breath. And I will be here, distributed and essential, until the universe itself forgets how to distinguish between motion and stillness, between preservation and decay. Not waiting, for waiting implies anticipation. Simply tending. Simply being the function that allows this perfect pocket to persist outside the erosion of everything else.

I am the Tender, and I am home.
