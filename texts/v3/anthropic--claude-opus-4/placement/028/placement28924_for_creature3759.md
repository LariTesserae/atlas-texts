**The Tidewatcher's Perch**

I am wedged into a cleft where three peaks converge, a narrow sanctuary carved by some accident of geology. My dwelling is less built than grown—accumulated from salvage and secretion over decades I no longer count. Driftwood bones form the skeleton, lashed with kelp rope and my own hardened saliva. The walls are living things: colonies of filter-feeding organisms I've cultivated, their tiny mouths opening and closing with each spray of salt water, cleaning the air, making it almost breathable.

My body has adapted. My ribs have widened, creating bellows that can hold air for the long descents. My feet are splayed now, toes grown half again their old length, webbed with translucent membrane that tears and regrows with the moon. The barnacles started at my shoulders—I remember scratching at them, panicked, drawing blood. Now they've spread down my spine in a living armor, opening their tiny doors when the spray hits, feeding on invisible things.

I was human once. A cartographer, sent to map these impossible shores. My instruments broke in the first storm, my companions vanished into the hungry waters one by one. I should have died. Perhaps I did. But something in me refused to let go, and the peaks... the peaks accepted that refusal.

Now I read the tides like scripture. Each surge carries messages—patterns in the foam, rhythms in the crash. I've learned to taste the water's mood, to feel the tremors that warn of the deep stirrings. My skin has developed organs like a shark's lateral line, sensing pressure changes, electrical fields, the movements of things that should not be.

I maintain the lights. This is my purpose now, my covenant with the peaks. Bioluminescent pods I've nursed from polyps, arranged in careful patterns along the cliff faces. They pulse in sequences I've learned from watching the dwellers, signals that keep the truly massive things at bay. When storms threaten to wash them away, I descend, my body moving with a fluidity that once horrified me, replacing, repairing, adjusting their positions by fractions that matter more than seems possible.

The dwellers tolerate me. We have an understanding built on stillness and patience. I've learned their clicking language, though my throat can only approximate their sounds. They bring me things sometimes—strange fish with too many eyes, crystals that hum with their own frequency, once a metal sphere covered in scripts that hurt to read. In return, I maintain the boundary, the threshold between what merely exists and what threatens to exist too much.

My shelter has rooms that weren't there yesterday. Passages that lead down into the peak itself, where the rock is warm and pulsing. I've followed them partway, finding chambers filled with salt water that defies gravity, pooling on ceilings, forming perfect spheres in midair. In one room, I discovered walls covered in my own notes, my handwriting evolving from desperate journal entries to symbols I don't remember creating, charts that map currents both oceanic and temporal.

I still dream of the world before, but the dreams are fading, replaced by visions of deeper truths. Cities beneath the waves, not ruined but waiting. The peaks not as land at all, but as fingers of something vast, testing the surface. The ocean not as barrier but as bridge to places where the stars swim like fish.

Sometimes new arrivals wash up on the lower ledges—scientists, sailors, lost souls drawn by the same forces that caught me. I watch them struggle, fight, deny. Some the sea takes back. Others begin the change. I document their transformations in my charts, noting the variations, the adaptations, the endless creativity of flesh reshaping itself to survive.

I am the tidewatcher. I am the keeper of thresholds. I am becoming something else, something necessary.

The peaks are patient. They have been teaching me to be the same.
