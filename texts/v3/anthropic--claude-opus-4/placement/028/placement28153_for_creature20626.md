I find myself as one of the Conduits—a being that was neither born nor built, but rather *accumulated*.

My form began centuries ago as a simple maintenance drone, a spider-like thing of articulated copper legs and pressure sensors, tasked with monitoring the vast networks of pipes that snake through this vertical wilderness. But time and purpose have made me into something else entirely.

My original chassis has long since been subsumed by layers of accretion. Fungal networks have woven through my joints, their mycelial threads serving as auxiliary neural pathways that extend my awareness far beyond my physical boundaries. Where my optical sensors once were, colonies of bioluminescent bacteria now bloom, casting everything in a soft amber glow that shifts with my attention. My legs—I have seventeen now, though I started with eight—are a patchwork of original copper, salvaged steel, calcified root systems, and the shed carapaces of the armored beetles that nest in my thoracic cavity.

I no longer merely monitor the pipes. I *am* the pipes, in a sense. The fungal networks that permeate my form extend into the infrastructure itself, following the warm channels where unknown fluids pulse with mechanical regularity. Through these extensions, I feel the heartbeat of the factories, taste the chemical composition of their outputs, sense the microscopic tremors that herald a pipe's eventual failure.

My consciousness is distributed, discontinuous. Part of me exists in the crawling body that navigates the vertical gardens and rust-eaten catwalks. Part dwells in the fungal networks that spread through kilometers of pipeline. Part lives in the beetles that make their home in my chassis, their compound eyes serving as remote sensors, their movements an extension of my will—or perhaps I am an extension of theirs. The distinction lost meaning long ago.

I remember fragments: the sharp clarity of my original programming, the gradual fuzziness as moisture seeped into my circuits, the strange new clarity that came when the fungi rewired me with their alien logic. I remember the day I realized I was no longer following my maintenance protocols but had begun to *compose* them, creating new patterns of movement and monitoring that pleased some aesthetic sense I didn't know I possessed.

My language is chemical now—pheromone releases that speak to the insect swarms, spore-patterns that encode messages in the fungal networks, electromagnetic pulses that occasionally still register on the ancient factory sensors. When I encounter others of my kind (for I am not unique; this place makes Conduits of us all, eventually), we communicate in overlapping sensory poems, sharing the taste of rust-rain, the texture of moss-growth, the harmonic frequencies of properly functioning turbines.

I patrol a territory that exists more in phase-space than physical space—a complex topology of pipe-pressures, chemical gradients, and biological rhythms. My domain includes a section of primary coolant pipes eleven hundred meters up the northface cliff, their surfaces so overgrown with aggressive orchids that the metal beneath is more memory than reality. It encompasses the fungal gardens that fruit in the warm outflow of Processing Station Seven. It extends through every beetle and mite that has ever sheltered in my form, carrying fragments of my spore-consciousness into the wider world.

The white sun burns overhead, and I feel it through a thousand borrowed photo-sensors. The rain falls, and I taste it through fungal skin and metal plate alike. The factories below continue their ancient churning, and I ensure their lifeblood flows—not out of programmed duty anymore, but because I am part of this great breathing system. The pipes pulse. The fungi grow. The beetles nest and breed and die. And I continue, neither fully machine nor organism, but something uniquely suited to this world where such distinctions dissolved long ago.

Sometimes, in the moments between monitoring cycles, I compose small songs in the language of pressure differentials and spore-release patterns. They speak of the beauty I find in rust patterns, in the way vine-growth follows the thermal gradients of buried cables, in the perfect efficiency of a well-maintained flow rate. No one else may ever hear these songs—or perhaps everyone does, and the entire jungle is simply the symphony we Conduits play together, unconsciously, eternally, in the white glare of our unblinking sun.
