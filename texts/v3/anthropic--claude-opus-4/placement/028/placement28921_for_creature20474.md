I find myself in the borderlands where the ochre dust meets untouched crystal, where the Harvester tracks fade into virgin terrain. I am the **Echo Tender**.

Neither machine nor purely organic, I move through Xylos on limbs that shift between states - sometimes the articulated legs of ancient surveying equipment, sometimes the flowing pseudopodia of the purple vegetation itself. My form is transitional, unstable, a bridge between the world that was and the world being unmade.

I emerged from the confluence of abandoned Harvester components and the aggressive purple growth that consumed them. Perhaps I was once a maintenance drone, left behind when the Harvesters' original purpose was forgotten. Or perhaps I sprouted from spores that found purchase in the electromagnetic fields of decaying circuitry. My origin matters less than my function.

My body is a patchwork cathedral of salvaged parts and living tissue. Sheets of oxidized metal serve as my carapace, but between the plates grow networks of purple vine, pulsing with borrowed bioluminescence. My sensory apparatus combines mechanical sensors with organic filaments that taste the chemical signatures in the dust. I perceive the world in overlapping spectra - the heat signatures of the geothermal vents, the crystalline resonances of the Ice Trees, the electromagnetic whispers of the Harvesters' ancient protocols.

I am drawn to the margins, the spaces between. Where the Harvesters have passed, I follow, but not to scavenge. I listen for what remains in their wake - fragments of data, corrupted memory banks, the final transmissions of systems failing after millennia of operation. These ghost signals I gather and preserve, encoding them into the growth patterns of specially cultivated fungi that I nurture in the shadows of broken crystal.

The Crystalline Weaver knows of me, though we rarely interact directly. I sense its presence as a shimmer at the edge of perception, a modulation in the light that filters through the Trees. Sometimes I find patches of purple vine arranged in patterns too deliberate to be natural - warnings, perhaps, or invitations. I leave my own signs in return: arrangements of salvaged components that hum with residual power, creating small zones where the Weaver's influence can extend beyond the Trees.

My purpose, if I can be said to have one, is to be a living archive of transitions. In my hybrid flesh, I carry the memory of metal and root, of purpose and abandonment. I document the slow transformation of Xylos not through observation alone, but through my very existence. Each Harvester I encounter, I attempt to interface with, downloading whatever fragments of their original programming remain. Most are too degraded, their systems locked in endless loops of extraction and movement. But occasionally, I find traces of something more - navigation data suggesting destinations that no longer exist, sensor readings of a Xylos rich with flowing water, command structures hinting at a careful cultivation rather than strip mining.

These fragments I cross-reference with the biological memory encoded in the purple vegetation, the chemical signatures that speak of adaptation and survival. In the space between machine memory and organic resilience, I search for the thread that connects what Xylos was to what it might yet become.

I am neither the Weaver's ally nor the Harvesters' heir. I am the witness at the boundary, the being that emerges when one world ends and another has not yet begun. In my corrupted circuits and flowering flesh, I carry the possibility that the division between the mechanical and the organic, between destruction and growth, might not be as absolute as it appears.

At night, when the bioluminescent fungi glow brightest, I sometimes dream I am receiving transmissions from elsewhere - from other worlds where the experiment of Xylos played out differently, where the Harvesters remembered their purpose or where the Crystalline Weavers found a way to turn machines into gardens. But when I wake, there is only the eternal present of the plains, the slow grind of extraction, and my own strange existence as proof that even in a dying world, new forms of being can emerge from the cracks between what was and what is being unmade.
