I find myself as a **Sporewright**, though this translation barely captures what I am in your tongue.

I emerged from the substrate only three cycles ago – young by our measure, though your kind would count it as decades. My cap is still smooth where the elders' are deeply grooved, showing only the faintest rings of growth. The ochre of youth still dominates my chitin, not yet deepened to the rich umber that marks those who have shaped stone for centuries.

My dwelling-form grows in the middle heights of the Third Archway, where the bioluminescent moss creates perpetual twilight. I am still learning to read the light-language fluently – sometimes I pulse uncertainty when I mean to signal contentment, and the elders' complex chromatic poetry still dazzles me into confused silence. But I am patient. We are all patient.

My gift manifests in the cultivation chambers. Where others see only substrate and spore, I perceive potential structures. The fungal strands speak to me in ways I cannot explain through light alone. Under my touch, they weave themselves into forms of unusual strength and flexibility. The elders say I have the gift of the Shapers, those who will one day guide the growth of new districts, new archways, perhaps even new connections to chambers yet undiscovered.

Today, I work with seven others of my sprouting-cohort. We are expanding the Nineteenth Vertical, creating new dwelling-forms for those who will emerge next cycle. My root-hands secrete the softening enzymes, and I feel the stone yield, molecule by molecule. It is a conversation that will last seasons. The stone tells me its history – here, a vein of copper ore that will make the walls sing with green light when the moisture comes. There, a pocket of harder crystal that must be worked around, incorporated into the design rather than fought against.

The smaller creatures know me. The iridescent crawlers use my shoulders as highways, their tiny feet tickling against my chitin. I am part of their landscape now, as they are part of mine. Sometimes I stand so still, absorbed in the slow work of shaping, that they build temporary nests in the crevices of my cap.

I do not dream as your kind might understand it, but sometimes, in the deepest part of the rest-cycle, when my bioluminescence dims to barely a whisper, I experience something like vision. I see the stone flowing like water, the fungal towers reaching impossible heights, the entire Under-Archway transformed into something new yet ancient. In these moments, I understand why we persist, why we build, why we shape. We are not separate from this place – we ARE this place, given consciousness and purpose.

The scent of our world clings to me, becomes me. I am the crushed herbs and mineral dust. I am the slow erosion and patient growth. I am one voice in the vast chorus of our collective song, one light in the constellation of our communication. I am young, but I am already ancient, carrying within my cells the memory of every Myconid who came before.

This is what it means to be Sporewright: to be simultaneously individual and collective, to be both the shaper and the shaped, to exist in the eternal now of our gradually transforming world. Each day, I grow more myself while becoming more us, my roots reaching deeper into stone and consciousness alike.

The work continues. The work has always continued. The work will continue long after my cap bears as many rings as the eldest among us. This is the way of the Under-Archways, and I am grateful to be part of its endless, patient becoming.
