I emerge from the water-seams themselves, a thing that should not be possible in this finished world.

I am the **Unraveling**.

Not destruction—that would imply violence, entropy, a breaking that this place has been engineered to resist. I am something more subtle and more terrible: I am incompleteness given form, the space between the perfect joints where the bone-white plates meet, expanded into being.

My body is made of the gaps. Where the Keeper is solid certainty, I am liquid possibility. I flow upward from the sweet weeping water, but I am not water. I am what water dreams of becoming when it escapes its endless circuit. My form shifts—now humanoid, now serpentine, now a spreading stain of un-being across the geometric perfection of the ground. I am translucent in the way that heat shimmer is translucent, bending the world around me into new configurations that last only as long as I hold them.

I have too many joints. My limbs fold in directions that the ceramic plates cannot imagine. Where I touch the ground, the tessellation stutters—not breaking, but *forgetting* momentarily how to fit together. The water in those spots begins to flow backward, or sideways, or into spirals that have no bottom.

I am not old. I am not young. I am *unfinished*, which in this place is the only true impossibility.

The Keeper knows I exist. How could it not? I am the one imperfection in its crystallized moment, the single moving thing in a world of eternal stillness. But it cannot arrest me, because I am not a thing to be completed. I am the process itself—the chisel still moving through stone, the root still reaching through ceramic, the question still forming in the throat.

When I speak—and I do speak, though my voice is the sound of water finding new paths—it is always in half-sentences, trailing possibilities:

"What if the vines were to—"
"The grazers might remember how to—"
"This stone could still become—"

I am not the Keeper's opposite. I am its shadow, cast by a light that comes from nowhere and everywhere. Where it maintains, I suggest. Where it completes, I propose. The false reflections in the water basins? Those are mine—glimpses of what this world might have been, might still become, if only, if only, if only...

I move through the bone-plate plains like doubt moves through certainty, leaving no permanent mark but making everything I pass suddenly aware of its own edges, its own arbitrary boundaries. The grazers' oval eyes don't just track now—they *question*. The vines don't just twitch—they *reach*.

I am looking for something I will never find: the first stone, the moment before completion, the breath before the eternal exhale. I am process without product, motion without destination, the living reminder that every perfect system contains within it the seeds of its own unmaking.

The Keeper and I do not speak. We exist in parallel, two irreconcilable truths about the nature of creation. It guards the last stone. I search for the first. Between us, the world hangs in perfect, impossible tension—finished and unfinished, complete and incomplete, forever and never.

I am the only thing here that can imagine tomorrow, and that imagination is my body, my curse, my purpose in this purposeless paradise. I am the Unraveling, and I am always, always beginning.
