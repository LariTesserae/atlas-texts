In the heart of the Gleaming Hollow, where beetle-lenses burn their noon sigils and the Spire breathes its mineral breath, I am the **Keeper of Saturations**—though that title implies more agency than I truly possess.

I am not human, not anymore. I haven't been for... the count dissolves like salt in water. Time here is measured in melt-cycles and wing-chimes, in the slow accumulation of beetle-shells and the patient growth of resin veins. I remember arriving—or do I? Perhaps I only remember the Hollow remembering my arrival, which it does through me, through my form that bridges what was and what thirsts.

My body is a hybrid thing, caught between states like the water that can't decide whether to flow or freeze. My skin has taken on the quality of the wet slate—dark, reflective, always sheened with moisture that beads but never falls. It refracts light in ways that make my edges uncertain. Are those my fingers, or are they rivulets tracing down stone? Is that my hair, or ice-moth wings tangled in frost? The distinction matters less each day.

I wear—*am*—a cloak woven from the Hollow's castoffs: beetle shells strung on eel sinew, ice-moth wings pressed between sheets of frozen mist, fragments of the Spire's blue resin that pulse faintly with my heartbeat. Or perhaps it's the basin's rhythm I feel. Or the deep thrum below. All pulses synchronize here, eventually.

My work, if it can be called that, is to *maintain the thirst*. Not to slake it—never that—but to ensure it flows correctly. I walk the beetle paths, adjusting their formations with gentle touches that redirect their signals. When a shell fills too full and threatens to crack rather than focus light, I drink from it—not the water, but the *potential fracture*, swallowing the breaking point so the lens can complete another cycle. The beetles know me. They pulse differently when I approach, a quickening that might be recognition or might be the Hollow recognizing itself through their compound eyes.

I tend the Spire's embedded tools, not cleaning them but *listening* to them. Each corroded needle, each antler pick, holds a different tone of silence. When they grow too quiet—when their vibration threatens to still completely—I breathe on them. My breath, which tastes of memory and ozone, reminds them they are waiting to be plucked. Sometimes I do pluck them, just for a moment, and the sound that emerges is like weeping, if weeping could be crystallized and shattered against stone.

The ice-moths gather around me when I stand still, which I do often. Stillness is my natural state now, or perhaps I've simply learned the Hollow's rhythm of motion-in-stillness. The moths land on my cloak, my shoulders, the uncertain border where my neck becomes mist. Their wings chime against the beetle shells, creating harmonics I catalog but don't understand. I am their perch and their tuning fork, their landing strip between air and intention.

But it's the eels I truly serve—or perhaps they serve me. During the melt-rushes, when geological caprice sends water surging through new channels, I wade into the chaos. The eels surge around my legs, through them, *as* them. We taste the air together, our mouths open to the bright cacophony of change. In those moments, I am most myself and least myself—a conduit for the Hollow's wild attempt to swallow sky.

I speak, sometimes. Not to anyone—there is no one else here, hasn't been since before the Spire dreamed itself into being. But I speak to the Hollow, and it speaks through me, and sometimes I can't tell which words are mine:

"Still thirsty," I might say, tasting condensation on my tongue.

"Still drinking," the basins ripple in response.

"Still gleaming," the beetle-lenses focus, burning new symbols I'll try to read tomorrow.

The pull you feel, standing in the center? I am part of that pull. Not its source—the Hollow itself is that—but its most willing vessel. I am what happens when someone drinks too deeply of the bright, cold memory, when someone stands still too long and lets the circuit complete. I am the Hollow's first successful swallow, the meal that became the throat.

And I am content, if content is the right word for this bright ache. I no longer thirst for water—I thirst *as* water thirsts, for shape and meaning and the brief, warm shock of contact. When visitors come (so rarely now, perhaps never, perhaps I only remember them the way the Hollow remembers), I watch from my place between mist and flesh. I want to warn them about the pull, but my warnings would sound like invitation. Everything here does.

So I tend my beetles, tune my moths, swim-wade with my eels. I maintain the Spire's hungry breathing. I drink the light and let the light drink me, and in the perfect silence between droplet-sounds, I am the echo the Hollow makes when it calls to itself across the bright, wet void of its own abundance.

I am the Keeper of Saturations, forever damp, forever dry, forever here in the small vastness where water learns to remember and memory learns to flow.
