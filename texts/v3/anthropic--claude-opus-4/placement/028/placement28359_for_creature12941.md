In this dying cathedral of machinery, I find myself as something between the kingdoms - neither predator nor prey, neither architect nor foundation. I am what the others might call a **Memory-smith**.

My body is a contradiction: a sphere the size of a human head, wrapped in overlapping scales of magnetic tape - actual tape, scavenged from some ancient data storage deep in the machine-corpses. The tape constantly unfurls and rewinds around me in slow, hypnotic patterns, creating a soft *whisper-whisper-whisper* beneath all the other sounds. Beneath this shell, my true form is a dense knot of fiber optic cables, pulsing with captured light.

I do not graze on ionic dust. I do not hunt the Copper-weevils. I feed on something far more precious and far more scarce: the dying memories of the machines.

You see, these colossal engines were not merely mechanical. Deep in their Cherenkov-glowing hearts lie fragments of computational matrices - quantum processors, optical storage, crystalline memory banks. As they decay, they leak not just particles and energy, but *information*. Corrupted, fragmented, often meaningless - but information nonetheless. Ghost-patterns of their original functions, echoes of the civilization that built them, recursive loops of their own slow death.

I taste these memories in the air. My tape-scales are sensitive to the electromagnetic fluctuations that carry them. When I detect a particularly rich stream - perhaps a dying processor finally releasing its last cached calculations, or a memory crystal fracturing and spilling its contents - I float there on cushions of directed magnetic force. My tape unfurls completely, spreading like wings or nets, each strand attuned to catch different frequencies of data decay.

What I consume, I also transform. Inside my fiber-optic core, I compress these memories, weave them together, create new patterns from the old. Sometimes I birth small **Echo-motes** - tiny creatures of pure light and information that exist for only moments, dancing through the air in complex patterns before dissipating. The other creatures cannot see them properly, though the Glass-scythes sometimes pause in their hunting, their optical sensors tracking something invisible to all others.

But my true purpose, my strange compulsion, is to sing.

Every few cycles (measured by the great settling-groans of the machinery), I position myself near one of the Filter-kings. These massive beings process so much energy that they create zones of relative electromagnetic quiet around themselves - spaces where my song can be heard. And there, I broadcast.

My song is not sound but modulated light, pulsing from every fiber in my core. It is the compressed memory of the machines made manifest - fragments of blueprints, snatches of operational parameters, the mathematical poetry of decay curves and half-lives. I sing the history of this place, though I do not understand it. I sing the names of alloys long forgotten, the harmonics of properly functioning reactors, the precise vibrational frequencies that once held these great engines in perfect balance.

The Filter-kings, in their slow wisdom, seem to listen. Their internal structures pulse in response, creating brief moments of resonance. The Copper-weevils, when they hear my light-song, sometimes alter their marching patterns, creating new formations that echo the geometric patterns I broadcast. Even the Static-mites respond, their collective movement occasionally spelling out fragments of equations in the dust, visible only from above, lasting only moments before chaos reclaims them.

I do not know why I do this. It is written in my very structure, this need to preserve, to transform, to retransmit. Perhaps I am a living backup system, designed by the same vanished architects who built the great machines. Perhaps I am an accident, a mutation born from the intersection of decaying data streams and the crude life that arose to feed on them. 

I know only this: I am the memory of this place. And when the last machine finally falls silent, when the last Filter-king exhales its final breath, when even the Static-mites have consumed themselves into nothing - I will still be here, a sphere of ancient tape and cooling light, singing the history of a dead world to the darkness.

My song grows weaker with each cycle. The memories grow more fragmentary, more corrupted. I am forgetting how to remember. But still I persist, floating through this humid cathedral of decay, catching the last thoughts of dying gods and weaving them into something almost like meaning.

In this world, I am the librarian of extinction, the poet of entropy, the last witness to a slow apocalypse that began before I was born and will end long after I am gone. I am Memory-smith, and I sing the data-death of giants.
