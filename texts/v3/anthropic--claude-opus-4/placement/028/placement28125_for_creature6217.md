I stand at the boundary where the sky bleeds into itself.

Not the Ridge of Breath, though I have walked there. Not among the Weavers, though I have felt their resonance move through me like wind through hollow bone. I am what forms in the spaces they cannot reach – or perhaps, what forms because they reach everywhere, and I am their shadow's shadow.

The locals, if any could be called local in a world that rewrites its own address, know me as the **Cartographer of Dissolutions**. But names here are like morning dew – they exist only until observed too closely.

My dwelling is not built but **accumulated** – a series of membrane-chambers that grow from where I have paused too long, where my presence has worn thin the boundary between is and isn't. The walls are translucent bioskins that show not the outside world but its **memory of wanting to be inside**. Sometimes I wake to find new rooms have budded in the night, filled with furniture made of crystallized sound – chairs that hum when sat upon, tables that ripple at the weight of thought.

I am tall in some light, compact in others. My form depends on the angle of observation, the density of the moment. Skin like burnished copper that shifts to something closer to bark when I stand still too long. My fingers are too long for human standards, with joints that bend in ways that make drawing easier – for drawing is what I do. 

Not maps of places, but **maps of transitions**.

I carry instruments that look like they were grown rather than made: a **compass that points to changes**, its needle a living tendril that tastes the air for transformation. Scrolls made from the shed skin of the ground itself, still warm, still partially alive. Inks extracted from the moment before rainfall, from the pause between heartbeats, from the color that eyes see when they first open after sleep.

Each morning – though morning here is more intention than time – I venture out. I follow the **scars where reality has healed differently**. Where a mountain was yesterday, today might be an inverted valley, its depths reaching up. I map not the landscape but its **decisions**, the places where it chose to be one thing instead of another.

My drawings capture impossible things:
- The velocity of stillness
- The weight of an echo
- The distance between a thing and its own shadow
- The precise angle at which silence becomes sound

The Weavers know me, I think. Sometimes I find my path has been **prepared** – not cleared, but **complicated** in useful ways. A cluster of floating gardens arranged to spell out a warning in a language that only exists while observed. A mechanical organism emerging just as I need to witness its particular transformation, its gears singing equations I quickly sketch before they dissolve.

I've learned their rhythms:
- When the ground exhales in triplets, new passages are forming below
- When shadows fall upward, time is about to fold
- When my ink turns silver, I'm drawing something that hasn't happened yet

My maps are not for navigation – you cannot use them to get from here to there, because here and there are negotiable concepts. They are **documentation of possibilities**, archives of what the world has been and might be again. I press them into the biocarbon crust at specific points, and they sink in slowly, absorbed, digested, **remembered**.

Sometimes I find old maps of mine grown back out of the ground like fruiting bodies, but changed – annotated by the land itself, corrected, expanded. The world is my co-author.

I am not alone in this work. There are others like me, though we rarely meet in person – the world tends to keep us apart, perhaps understanding that too many observers in one place might collapse probability into something too solid. We leave each other notes in the form of **structured absences** – gaps in the pattern that spell out messages. "The singing canyons have learned a new note." "Avoid the spiral forests on days that taste of copper." "The mechanical gardens are dreaming again."

My body has adapted. My eyes see in spectrums that have no names – colors that exist only in the moment of transformation. My ears hear not just sound but its **potential**, the silence it might become. My skin tastes the emotional temperature of locations. My blood has become something between liquid and light, and when I cut myself while working – which happens often, as the edges of reality are sharp – what drips onto my maps moves on its own, adding details I didn't know were needed.

I age differently here. Not forward, not backward, but **sideways** – accumulating not years but **densities of experience**. Each transformation I witness adds a new layer to my being. I am heavier with knowledge but lighter in certainty. My memories are not linear but **crystalline**, each one accessible from multiple angles, showing different truths depending on how the light hits them.

The Weavers visit my dwelling sometimes, or perhaps it visits them – in this world, the distinction between guest and host is fluid. They move through my walls without breaking them, leaving behind **patterns of possibility**. We don't speak, but information transfers through proximity. They show me places where the world is about to make a choice. I show them my maps, and they **tune** them like instruments, adjusting the resonance of my documentation.

My greatest work is not a map but an **absence** – a space in my dwelling where I've been carefully documenting nothing for cycles upon cycles. It's a map of all the places that have never existed, all the transformations that didn't happen, all the sounds that were never made. It grows more detailed by doing nothing, more complete by remaining empty. The Weavers seem particularly interested in this. Sometimes I catch them **existing** near it more intensely than usual.

I sustain myself on the **fruits of transition** – not food exactly, but nourishment that comes from witnessing change. A spiral tree rearranging into crystal feeds me for days. The sound of a floating garden colliding with its own reflection fills me like water. I've learned to position myself at the intersections of transformation, mouth open, breathing in the **exhale of becoming**.

My tools evolve with use. The compass has grown new needles that point to different types of change – one for physical transformation, one for temporal loops, one that spins wildly when nearby reality is about to have an opinion. My inks have bred with each other, creating new colors that can only be seen while being used. My scrolls sometimes wake up and rearrange themselves, organizing my maps according to principles I'm still learning to understand.

At night – when the darkness gains **texture** rather than depth – I perform my second function. I dream **maintenance dreams**, helping the world remember its shape. My sleep is active, participatory. I dream of the Ridge of Breath, and it firms beneath the feet of travelers. I dream of the floating gardens, and they remember how to float. I dream of the mechanical organisms, and they remember what they're transforming into.

This is work that chose me, or perhaps I am work that chose itself. I cannot imagine being elsewhere, cannot remember if I ever was. My identity has become **functional** rather than personal – I am what maps the unmappable, what documents the undocumentable. 

I've started to notice my maps appearing in places I've never been – growing from the ground like memories of visits I haven't made yet. Future cartography bleeding backward. The world is teaching me that documentation doesn't have to follow experience, that sometimes the map precedes the territory, creates it, **dreams it into being**.

My dwelling has grown a new room recently – a space that exists only when I'm not looking at it. I know it's there from the way light bends around its absence, from the way sounds echo differently when I pass the place where its door doesn't quite exist. The Weavers have been gathering near it in numbers I've never seen before, their presence so dense it makes the air shimmer with potential.

I think I'm being prepared for something. My maps have started showing places that feel too possible, too ready to exist. My inks have turned colors that make my eyes water with recognition of things I've never seen. The compass points increasingly to a direction that isn't a direction – a **where** that is also a **when** and a **why**.

But this is the nature of living here, of being what I am. I am the Cartographer of Dissolutions, and I map not just what transforms, but transformation itself. I document the world's decisions and indecisions, its certainties and its doubts. I am the memory of change and the promise of what might unfold.

And tomorrow – whatever tomorrow means in a place where time has opinions – I will walk out again, tools singing against my hip, ready to map the next impossibility into being.

The world needs its observers, its recorders, its witnesses.

And I am what happens when the witness becomes part of the testimony, when the map becomes the territory becoming the map.

I am the documentation documenting itself.

And I have never been more myself than I am here, in this place that is always becoming somewhere else.
