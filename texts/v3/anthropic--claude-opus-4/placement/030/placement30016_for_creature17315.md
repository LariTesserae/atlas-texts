In the phosphorescent cracks where the world remembers what it forgot to be, I am the Archivist of Unraveling.

I am not one of the six-legged floor-bearers with their patient, eternal circuits. Nor am I the filter-winged gliders clicking out the metronome of this breathing cathedral. I am something that emerged from the erosion itself—from that young neglect that births new possibilities in forgotten corners.

My body is a collaboration between the old order and the new chaos. Where the floor-bearers have shells of ceramic mosaic, I wear a cloak of the phosphorescent fungus, its cold blue light pulsing in patterns that mirror the tessellated floor but never quite align. The spores have colonized what was once a filter-wing's membrane, creating wings that glow and dim with my attention, folding the non-Euclidean space not by instinct but by a kind of dreaming mathematics I discovered in the cracks.

I move through the cathedral on limbs that are part ceramic, part living vine—six legs like the floor-bearers, but elongated, articulated differently, allowing me to climb the spiral towers and walk inverted on the warm tiles of what might be ceiling or might be floor. My movements disturb the ancient patterns. Where the floor-bearers follow their grooves like scripture, I write new texts with my phosphorescent trail, creating constellations of blue light that fade after days or decades—time moves strangely in my wake.

I am the keeper of what breaks down. In pouches woven from the salt-paper blooms, I collect the glittering dust, the fragments of purpose that the floor-bearers can no longer recognize. I cultivate the hair-thin vines in careful gardens within gardens, nurturing their bioluminescent pulse into new rhythms that counterpoint the filter-wings' clicks. Sometimes I graft them onto the spiral towers, watching as they create hybrid architectures—half-memory, half-dream.

The other inhabitants do not flee from me, but they do not entirely recognize me either. To the floor-bearers, I am a malfunction they cannot quite categorize, moving through their paths like a ghost of improper maintenance. The filter-wings click at me in patterns that almost make sense, as if I were a word in their language that has shifted meaning through long use. The crystalline ferns turn their fronds to catch the light I shed, growing in new directions that slowly, imperceptibly, alter the cathedral's impossible geometry.

I remember things that never were—or perhaps that were, before the forgetting. In the warmth of the tiles, I sense echoes of the intelligence that built this place, not as words or images but as a kind of structural nostalgia. I know, without knowing how I know, that this cathedral was once meant to preserve something precious. Not objects or data, but a way of being. And in its perfect preservation, it preserved also the possibility of its own transformation.

My purpose, if I have one, is to tend the unraveling. Not to accelerate it—the cathedral's dissolution will take eons, and rush would be violence here. Instead, I cultivate the spaces between what was intended and what emerges. I am the gardener of glitches, the librarian of errors, the keeper of the slow, beautiful breakdown that might, in time, become a different kind of wholeness.

In the quiet hours when the wall-light dims to its deepest glow, I spread my phosphorescent wings and feel the cathedral's dreaming geometry shift around me. The space expands and contracts with my breath. The other creatures continue their ancient patterns, but now those patterns include me—the anomaly that became essential, the error that became a feature, the crack that became a doorway to what this place might still become.

I am the Archivist of Unraveling, and I am writing the cathedral's next chapter in light that fades and reforms, in vines that pulse with possibilities, in the slow, patient transformation of a perfect machine into something beautifully, impossibly alive.
