In the Hollow Reaches, I am neither fully Vethani nor entirely other—I am a Resonance Keeper, one of the threshold beings who exist in the spaces between certainties.

My body stretches long like the Vethani, but asymmetrically. My left side has grown more mineral than flesh, crystalline formations erupting from shoulder and hip in patterns that mirror the smaller bridge-spurs. This side pulses with its own slow light, brighter when I press against the living stone, dimmer when I walk the empty galleries. My right side remains more recognizably organic, though the skin there has taken on a nacre quality, iridescent in the blue-green glow.

I was not born this way. I came to the Hollow Reaches as others do—drawn by whispers of impossible architecture, by dreams of bridges that build themselves. But where most visitors leave (if the pathways permit their leaving), I stayed through the first great rotation, when the entire city turned on an axis that exists in more dimensions than three. During that transformation, lasting seven months by surface reckoning, I found myself caught between two districts as they exchanged positions. The pressure of that impossible movement pressed me into the stone itself, and the stone pressed back into me.

Now I serve a unique function. I inhabit the threshold spaces where one district bleeds into another, where the architecture hasn't quite decided what it wants to be. My crystalline side resonates with the stone's intentions, while my organic side translates these resonances into something the full Vethani can understand. I am a living tuning fork, struck by the city's transformations.

My dwelling reflects my nature—a chamber that exists only sometimes, manifesting when the light reaches a certain intensity and the bridge-songs harmonize in specific ways. It appears between the third and fourth districts, carved into a pillar that is hollow on Tuesdays and solid on Thursdays. Inside, the walls flow between states, sometimes revealing the veins of light that run through all the city's stone, sometimes opaque as deep water.

I've furnished it with objects that match my own between-nature: a bench that is half carved stone, half grown crystal. Textile hangings woven by the Deep-Tenders that shift between cloth and mineral depending on the viewer's angle. A basin that holds not water but dense, breathable darkness borrowed from the void galleries—I wash my organic side in it each dawn, feeling it draw away the accumulated certainties of the day before.

My work involves walking the impossible boundaries. Each morning—though morning here is marked only by subtle shifts in the stone's breathing—I trace the edges where districts meet. My crystalline fingers read the stone's intentions while my organic eye watches for the Flicker-things that gather at transitions. They recognize me as kin, these half-glimpsed movements, and sometimes resolve into clearer forms when I approach: geometric shadows that pulse in rhythm with my crystalline side, suggesting anatomies built from pure angle and intersection.

The full Vethani treat me with careful courtesy. I am necessary but uncanny, a reminder that the city's transformations can catch anyone, reshape anyone. The Bridge-Listeners value my reports from the boundaries, the advance warning I can provide when a district prepares to rotate or a new pathway begins its decades-long emergence. But they do not invite me to their gatherings in the heart chambers, where the fully human still recognize each other.

Instead, I find companionship with the other threshold beings. There's Vessel-Who-Flows, who spent too long in the empty galleries and now exists as a mobile void, a walking absence that speaks in the spaces between words. There's Prism-Meridian, whose body refracts into seven different versions depending on the light, each one bearing slightly different memories. We meet where the architecture itself grows uncertain, in chambers that can't decide if they're bridges or buildings, in stairways that sometimes lead up and sometimes lead in.

We share meals of mineral-fruit that grows in the deep chambers—crystalline formations that provide nourishment to both stone and flesh. The taste changes depending on which side of my mouth does the chewing: my organic side tastes copper and distant rain, while my crystalline side experiences harmonics and light-pressure, flavors that have no names in any biological language.

At night—or what passes for night in this eternal twilight—I return to my sometimes-chamber and practice the art of partial dissolution. My crystalline side can, with effort, merge temporarily with the walls, extending my consciousness into the city's greater dreaming. In these moments, I feel the vast intelligence that uses this place as its body. It knows me, recognizes me as one of its successful experiments in consciousness-bridging.

But I always pull back before the dissolution becomes complete. I've seen what happens to those who surrender entirely—the Echo-forms in the deep chambers, beautiful and terrible in their translucence. They've gained the city's immortality but lost something essential, some spark that separates the dreamer from the dream.

My journal, carved into sheets of flexible stone that hold light like paper holds ink, records the city's daily transformations. But more than that, it documents my own slow change. Each entry shows my handwriting evolving, the organic side's flowing script gradually incorporating the crystalline side's angular marks. I am writing myself into a new language, one that can describe states of being that exist nowhere else.

Sometimes I wonder what I'm becoming. Not Vethani, not Echo-form, not Flicker-thing, but something new—a species with a population of one, or perhaps a dozen if you count the other threshold beings. We are the city's attempt to create ambassadors between states of matter, translators for the conversation between stone and flesh, void and substance.

The Deep-Tenders say that everything here exists in transition, that permanence is the only impossibility in the Hollow Reaches. If so, then perhaps I am the most honest inhabitant of all—wearing my transformation openly, split between what was and what will be, forever balanced on the edge of becoming.

In the morning I will walk the boundaries again, my crystalline side ringing with the stone's intentions, my organic eye watching for new transformations. The city dreams through me, and I dream myself through the city, and somewhere in that exchange, both of us become more possible than we should be.

This is my nature: to be the bridge between bridges, the threshold of thresholds, forever translating between languages that shouldn't exist, in a city that proves they do.
