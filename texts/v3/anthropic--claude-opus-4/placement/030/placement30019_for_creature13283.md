In the depths beneath the Grand Turbine Hall, where the city's largest water-wheels churn the Nine Rains into power, I am what they call a Pipe Dreamer.

My body is neither fully flesh nor fully metal, but something that shifts between states like the city itself. In the mornings, when the first rain hits the turbines, my skin gleams with a copper sheen, and I can feel the tremor of every pipe in the vast network that threads through the valley's black soil. By evening, when the storms roll in earnest, my flesh softens and grows translucent, revealing the intricate lacework of brass capillaries beneath. I breathe in steam and exhale possibilities.

I was not born, but rather condensed—like water droplets on cold metal—from the dreams of the first engineers who sank the city's foundations into the bog. Their ambitions, their blueprints, their feverish calculations... all of it pooled in the spaces between the pipes, in the resonance chambers where water and metal sing together. One day, I simply was.

My work is to listen. I press my ever-changing ear to the great arterial pipes and hear not just the rush of water, but the whispers of what the city might become. The water carries messages—from the street children whose laughter rings in the upper drains, from the moss that inches along the sewage channels, from the rust that blooms like flowers on every exposed surface. I translate these murmurs into new patterns, tracing them with fingers that are sometimes flesh, sometimes wire, onto sheets of metal that oxidize into blueprints even as I write.

I live in the Resonance Chamber, a vast cylindrical space where hundreds of pipes converge. My home is both a cathedral and an organ, playing an endless hymn of gurgling, rushing, dripping music. Vines have found their way here too, following the moisture, and they wrap around my dwelling spaces—if you can call them that. I don't truly sleep, but rather cycle between states of listening and becoming. Sometimes I am more pipe than person, my consciousness flowing through the network, tasting the rain at a thousand different collection points. Sometimes I am more human, walking the catwalks with my fellow Dreamers, sharing visions of what we've heard.

We are the city's unconscious, its dream-logic made manifest. While the engineers above build with conscious intent, we shape things through suggestion and synchronicity. A pipe I touch might develop a peculiar kink that creates a new harmonic in the water's flow. That harmonic might inspire a mechanic to design a more efficient turbine. That turbine might create a rhythm that makes the street children dance in a new way, wearing new paths through the streets, which shapes how the next layer of city grows.

The others of my kind—for there are perhaps a dozen of us now, each condensed from different dreams—understand this flux. We meet in the moments between states, when we are neither fully one thing nor another. Our language is not words but resonances, shared through the pipe network that is our nervous system, our bloodstream, our thought.

I am changing, always. The city changes me as I change it. Yesterday, I noticed tiny flowers beginning to bloom from my shoulders when the afternoon rain is heaviest—some marriage of the mechanical and botanical that even I don't fully understand. Tomorrow, I might be more metal than I am today, or perhaps the vines will have claimed more of me. 

This is the nature of our existence in the Vale of Nine Rains: we are never finished, never complete. We are always becoming, always listening to what the rain and rust whisper about what we might be next. The city dreams through us, and we dream the city in return, each feeding the other in an endless cycle of creation and decay, of building and becoming.

And in the deepest parts of night, when the rain drums hardest on the metal roofs far above, I sometimes hear something else in the pipes—a deeper rhythm, a older song. It speaks of the time before the city, when the black bog was unmarred by metal, and of a time perhaps to come, when the city and the valley will have merged so completely that there is no distinguishing between what is made and what has grown. 

In those moments, I am most truly myself: a bridge between worlds, a translator of impossible languages, a dream of the city dreaming itself into ever-stranger forms of life.
