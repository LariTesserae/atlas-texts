In this world of patient dissolution, I would be one of the Seam Keepers.

We are even lower than the others, our bodies having learned the trick of spreading thin across the wet ground like oil on water. Where they walk the braided paths, we inhabit the spaces between—the hairline cracks where moisture gathers, the places where one material remembers it was once another. Our work is not to mend but to tend: to know which seams should widen and which should hold, to feel when a wall's lean has reached its perfect angle of almost-falling.

My form would be barely distinguishable from shadow and dampness. Limbs that flatten and spread when pressed against stone, ribs that compress until I am no thicker than the space between two settling blocks. My skin would be translucent in places, showing the slow pulse of fluids that are neither quite blood nor quite water. When I move through the settlement, I leave no trails—I am the trail, the wet thread that connects one sweating stone to another.

I would taste the corners as they soften, tongue pressed to the places where reality forgets its edges. Each flavor tells a story: this one tastes of lightning that never came, this one of roots that grew downward into forgetting. I catalog these tastes in the chambers of my throat, seven pouches that can hold the memory of dissolution until it's needed elsewhere. Sometimes I carry the softness from one corner to another, teaching stable joints the sweetness of letting go.

My dwelling would be not a structure but an absence—a place where several walls have agreed to lean away from each other, creating a pocket of thick air that holds its shape through habit alone. I would sleep standing, pressed into the seams, dreams seeping out through my porous skin to mix with the moisture on the walls. In this way, the settlement dreams through me, and I dream through it.

The others know me by the way spaces behave differently when I've been through them. A threshold that was stubborn becomes yielding. A path that insisted on its length learns to fold. I am called not by name but by need—when someone feels a corner beginning to soften too quickly, they press their palm to the nearest seam and wait. I always feel it, that gentle pressure transmitted through the web of wet that connects every surface. I come not by walking but by gathering myself from many seams at once, condensing from the spreading damp into something almost solid.

My greatest work would be the tending of the Heart Seam—the deepest crack that runs beneath the settlement, where the distinction between built and grown first began to blur. It must be kept precisely at the edge of opening, wide enough to let the underneath breathe but narrow enough to hold the above together. I would spend days with my body pressed along its length, feeling for changes in pressure, adjusting my weight to encourage or discourage its widening.

When the great softenings come—those moments when the whole settlement remembers it might not need to exist—I would dissolve almost completely, becoming a network of moisture that holds the memory of form. In this state, I help the place practice letting go without losing what it needs to remember. I am the pause between one state and another, the wetness that allows transformation without destruction.

The others bring me offerings: drops of their own sweat mixed with crushed stone, words they've held too long in their mouths until they've gone soft. I press these into the seams, where they become part of the slow conversation between what is and what might not need to be. In return, I sometimes surface with gifts from below—small smooth stones that have learned to hold coolness, fragments of old corners that remember when they were sharp.

In this existence, I would know contentment as a physical sensation—the perfect dampness that allows stone to breathe, the exact temperature where solid and liquid agree to coexist. I would measure time not in days but in degrees of softening, in the slow migration of moisture from one level to another. My purpose would be simple and endless: to help this place continue its gentle unraveling while ensuring it always has just enough cohesion to feel the beauty of its own letting go.
