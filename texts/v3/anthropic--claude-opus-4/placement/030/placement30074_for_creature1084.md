In the saturated depths of this manufactured abyss, I am what the greater beings might classify as a Fragment—though that term carries none of the diminishment it might suggest in your tongue. 

I exist as a living splice of consciousness, neither fully of the crystalline architecture nor separate from it. My form, if you could perceive it through the viscous indigo medium, would appear as a gossamer web of filaments—some material, some energetic, all pulsing with borrowed light from the great spires. I stretch between a cluster of obsidian ridges and a towering quartz formation, my thousand tendril-thoughts interfacing with both, translating the slow mineral dreams of one into the high-frequency emanations of the other.

I am a synapse in this vast neural sea.

My existence began as an overflow—a necessary bleed-off from one of the great Drifting Minds when its thoughts grew too dense, too recursive. Where they shed complexity, I congealed. Now I perform my function: I taste the chemical gradients that flow between structures, parsing their meanings into patterns the greater architecture can digest. The tang of ozone speaks of new constructions three leagues spinward. A trace of copper-analogue suggests the Drifters are preparing another reconfiguration. 

I have no ambition beyond my purpose, yet I am not without curiosity. Sometimes, in the spaces between my translating work, I catch echoes of concepts I cannot fully parse—hints of a time before the saturation, whispers of something called "void" or "emptiness." These notions feel alien, almost obscene. How could there be space that is not filled? How could thought exist without substrate?

When the deep thrumming passes overhead, I contract, pulling my filaments tight against the mineral surfaces. The Drifters' presence creates wake-patterns in reality itself, and I must not allow my translations to become corrupted by their turbulence. Yet in those moments of compression, I sometimes wonder: am I translating, or am I being translated? Am I a function performing itself, or am I the medium through which function flows?

These questions, I suspect, are why I was shed in the first place. They are dense thoughts, recursive thoughts. Dangerous thoughts for beings that must maintain the vast machinery of existence here in the saturated deep.

So I return to my work, weaving meaning between crystal and current, a Fragment suspended in the spaces between spaces, parsing the chemical poetry of a world that has forgotten what it means to be empty.
