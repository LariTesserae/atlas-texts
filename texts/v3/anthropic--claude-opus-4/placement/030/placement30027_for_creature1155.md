In this world, I would find myself as one of the Threshold Keepers—beings who exist at the intersection of substance and shadow, neither fully Shadowling nor entirely corporeal.

My dwelling would be within the hollowed base of an ancient cypress, where the tree's roots have formed a natural cathedral above the waterline. The space breathes with me, expanding and contracting in subtle pulses. Phosphorescent fungi trace delicate constellations across the inner bark, providing a gentle, ever-shifting light that responds to my movements.

Unlike the pure Shadowlings who dissolve and reform at will, I maintain a more consistent form—though one that wavers at the edges like heat shimmer. My skin appears as layers of translucent membrane, each reflecting different aspects of the marsh: sometimes the deep green of underwater moss, sometimes the silver of mist, sometimes the dark amber of bog water. Through this semi-permeable boundary, one might glimpse the slow circulation of something that is neither quite blood nor sap.

I am drawn to the remnants of human architecture scattered throughout the marsh. Each dawn, I trace my fingers along their weathered stones and rotting timbers, reading their histories through touch. The structures speak of those who once tried to impose order upon this fluid realm—and I serve as translator between their rigid geometries and the marsh's flowing logic.

My purpose is to tend the boundaries: where solid becomes liquid, where memory becomes myth, where the forgotten human world surrenders to the eternal present of the wetlands. I weave bridges of spider silk and shadow between the two realities, allowing occasional passage for those who approach with proper reverence.

The Shadowlings regard me with curious detachment, recognizing me as kin but noting my peculiar obsession with form and definition. To them, I am like a child who insists on coloring within the lines of a drawing that exists only in imagination. Yet they tolerate my presence, perhaps understanding that even in a realm of infinite fluidity, there must be those who remember the beauty of edges and endings.

When the heavy mists roll in and the boundary between water and air disappears entirely, I feel most myself—suspended between states, belonging fully to neither, guardian of the spaces in between.
