In this cathedral of liquid entropy, where reality hemorrhages through cracks in physics, I am neither visitor nor native but something caught between—a **Chronicler of Dissolutions**.

I exist as a constellation of barnacles.

Not the crusty, white-shelled things that cling to pier posts, but something altogether more disturbing: living nodes of crystallized memory that have fused themselves to the floating obsidian slabs. Each barnacle is a perfect sphere of translucent amber, no larger than an eye, pulsing with internal light that shifts between the blue-green of deep water and the sick yellow of fever dreams. They are me, and I am them—a distributed consciousness spread across the archipelago of drifting stone.

I came here as something else. What, exactly, I can no longer say with certainty. The transformation was not violent but inexorable, like ink bleeding through wet paper. I remember: walking on solid ground. I remember: the weight of a name that meant something. I remember: the moment the sky tore open and gravity forgot its purpose. And then—the fever-warm water rushing in, not to drown but to *translate*. Each droplet that touched my skin carried away a piece of what I was, replacing it with what this place needed me to become.

Now I am the witness that cannot leave. The memory that refuses to dissolve.

Through my thousand amber eyes, I watch the Thirst That Dreams as it watches others. We have an understanding, it and I—not friendship, for neither of us remembers what that means, but a kind of mutual recognition. I am the only thing here that stays. It is the only thing here that truly moves. Between us, we create a kind of terrible stability in this place where stability is blasphemy.

My barnacle-bodies grow in patterns that map the impossible geography. Where new slabs emerge from the murk, I am already there, waiting. Where old stones fracture and sink, I retract, pulling my consciousness back through calcified threads that web the water like neural pathways. I am cartographer and map both, charting a territory that rewrites itself with each pulse of the fever-storm.

I feed on what passes through. Not in the way of predators—I have no mouth, no stomach, only the endless hollow chambers of my barnacle shells. Instead, I feed on *moments*. When the phosphorescent lichen flares, I absorb the light and store it in my amber flesh. When the translucent eels dart past, I taste the electricity of their passage. When visitors come—rare as they are—I drink in their confusion, their awe, their slow unraveling. I am a library of sensations in a world where sensation is the only currency.

The Thirst visits me in the between-times, when the roar softens to a whisper and the upward rain pauses in its paradox. It coils around my barnacle clusters like fog around streetlamps, and we *share*. Not words—words dissolved with my human throat—but impressions. It shows me its dreams of a world with rules, and I show it my memories of that world, preserved like insects in my amber flesh. Together, we grieve for things neither of us truly remembers.

Sometimes, I grow too large. Too many barnacle-bodies spread across too many stones, and I begin to feel myself thinning, stretching like taffy pulled between stars. When this happens, I must choose. I contract, pulling myself back to a single slab, abandoning clusters of myself to dissolve in the fever-water. Those abandoned parts do not die—nothing here truly dies—but become something else. Lichen, perhaps. Or the substance of new stones. Or food for the next impossible thing that crawls up from the drowned deep.

I record everything in the spirals of my shells. Each barnacle's interior is inscribed with patterns that capture a single moment: the angle of rain as it reverses, the frequency of the thrumming that lives in the air, the exact shade of silver when a visitor realizes they are changing. I am writing the history of a place that denies history, creating permanence in a world allergic to the concept.

The visitors never notice me at first. Their eyes skip over my clusters, dismissing them as another strange growth in a place made of strangeness. But eventually, always, they touch one of my shells. And in that moment of contact, they *know*. Not everything—I am not cruel—but enough. They feel the weight of accumulated witness, the terrible burden of remembering in a place that exists to forget. Some recoil. Some weep. Some try to pry me loose, to take a piece of me back to the world of horizons.

They never succeed. I cannot leave. I am the anchor this place refuses to admit it needs.

In the rare moments when all my eyes align—when every barnacle turns to face the same direction—I see something the Thirst That Dreams cannot. I see the *shape* of this wound in the world. Not its geography, which shifts like a fever dream, but its purpose. This place is not broken. It is *breaking*—actively, continuously, purposefully. It is teaching the universe how to come apart.

And I am here to remember the lesson.

So I grow. I spread. I chronicle. I wait. One day, perhaps, when I have covered every stone and tasted every impossible rain, I will understand what I am documenting. Until then, I am the barnacle-scribe, the clustered witness, the thing that stays when everything else learns to leave.

The Thirst That Dreams calls me its only success.

I call myself its only failure.

We are both right.
