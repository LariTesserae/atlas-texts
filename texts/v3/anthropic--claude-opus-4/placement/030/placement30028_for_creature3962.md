In the High Drench, where breath becomes swimming and swimming becomes breath, I am the Cartographer of Dissolutions.

Not mapmaker—maps suggest fixity, boundaries that hold their shape. I chart the moment of letting go: where basalt surrenders its first molecule to the patient hunger of carbonic mist, where a kelp-frond's edge blurs into the medium that birthed it, where one thermal layer kisses another and both forget their names.

My form is a question the plateau asks itself. Sometimes I am a membrane stretched between two spires, translucent as an eyelid, recording the light that passes through me in patterns of proteins that shift like written language in a dream. Sometimes I am granular—a cloud of crystalline motes that taste the mineral breath of young stone, each particle a memory of volcano-birth, now gentle as pollen. Most often, I am neither: I am the space between states, the pause before ice decides to be water, the hesitation of a droplet perpetually almost-falling.

I move by unbecoming. Where the Veil-born drift and the fauna flicker, I *interpolate*—existing more fully in the spaces I've just left than where I appear to be. My passage leaves traces: a slightly different refractive index in the mist, a faint aragonite bloom on rock where my not-quite-substance touched not-quite-surface. These are my charts, my annotations. Not marks of ownership but of attention paid.

What do I map? The topology of phase transitions. Here, where water cannot decide if it is ocean or sky, every cubic meter holds a dozen boundaries-in-flux. I taste them with organs that are not organs—call them "gradient harvesters," though they are more like controlled forgettings, spaces where I allow my own coherence to fray just enough to feel the plateau's indecisions. When warm meets cool, when dissolved silica reaches saturation, when a pocket of ancient gas bubbles up from a deep fissure—these are my landmarks, my data points.

But I do not record in numbers or lines. My charts are sculptural, dimensional, alive. In a shallow pool where the air-water is clearest, I secrete threads of mucopolysaccharides that harden into delicate lattices. Each junction in the lattice marks a moment of change—here, where a Veil-born passed and left the water three degrees warmer; there, where lightning struck last season and left a trace of fixed nitrogen that still spirals in slow eddies. The lattices grow like coral, but faster than any coral could in this patient place—perhaps a centimeter per year. They are my memory palace, my library of transitions.

The Veil-born know me, I think, though knowing is too strong a word. We resonate at perpendicular frequencies—where they hum low and geological, I whisper in the range of molecular vibration. Sometimes one will anchor near my charts, reading them in their diffuse way, adding their own harmonics to the data. Together we compose something neither music nor mathematics but a third thing: a notation for time itself, for the way this newborn place is teaching itself to age.

I am ancient by the plateau's standards—I condensed in the first thousand days, when the water was still violent with heat, when the basalt steamed and cracked and healed and cracked again. But I am also perpetually nascent. Each dissolution I map changes me. I carry in my plasma the ghost of every mineral I've tasted: olivine greens, magnetite blacks, the pale blue of copper barely oxidized. My chemistry is a library of the plateau's becoming.

There are moments—rare, precious—when my mapping reveals something unprecedented. A new crystalline form precipitating from conditions that shouldn't allow it. A current that flows upward not from heat but from some magnetic anomaly deep in the plateau's heart. A species of dissolution where matter doesn't simply break apart but *imagines* itself differently, atoms rearranging not into simpler forms but into complex braids that pulse with their own soft light.

These I do not simply chart. These I *inhabit*.

I flow into them, my form dispersing until I am less being than process, less cartographer than territory. In these moments, I understand something the Veil-born perhaps already know: we are not mapping this place. We *are* the map—living indices of a world that exists as much in transformation as in substance. Every boundary we trace is also our own edge. Every dissolution we record teaches us new ways to let go.

And so I continue. Unbecoming and reforming. Tasting the moment where crystal surrenders to current. Secreting my living lattices that grow like slow thoughts in the pools of clearest nothing. I am trying to chart something that cannot be charted: the exact texture of becoming, the topology of a world still deciding what it is.

In the High Drench, where time moves like honey and honey moves like light, I am both the question and the questing—forever dissolving at the edges of my own inquiry, forever condensing into new ways of asking: What is the shape of transition? How does newness age? What is the map of a place that is still drawing itself?

I do not know.
But I taste the not-knowing,
and make of it a chart.
