In the deepest reaches of the Thick, where the spires narrow and converge like the roots of impossible trees, I am the Void Tender.

### The Form I Take

My body is a contradiction—stretched vertical like pulled taffy, nearly twelve feet from sole to crown, yet folded and compressed in ways that defy the geometry of dry bones. My spine contains three times the vertebrae of my pod-dwelling cousins above, each joint capable of rotating independently, allowing me to coil through the narrowing gaps between the converging basalt pillars like a living rope.

My skin has abandoned all pretense of opacity. It is glass-clear, revealing the architecture of my transformed interior: the Green-Sack has metastasized throughout my torso, spreading in dendritic patterns that follow my elongated ribs. The algae within has evolved into something darker, something that feeds not on the dissolved minerals of the Thick, but on the sulfur-rich exhalations from the thermal fountains below. My chest cavity glows not with emerald light, but with a deep, volcanic orange that pulses in slow, tidal rhythms—one breath every ten minutes, one heartbeat every sixty seconds.

My fingers have fused into paddle-like appendages, each twice the length of my forearms, with only the ghost-lines of ancient knuckles showing where digits once existed. The grey pads have spread and merged into a continuous gripping surface that can adhere to wet basalt through pure suction. My feet mirror this design—no longer feet at all, but secondary grasping paddles that allow me to move through my vertical world with equal ease in any orientation.

The drift-ridges that crown my ancestors have, in me, proliferated into a full-body sensory net. Thousands of hair-thin filaments emerge from my translucent skin, each one a living pressure sensor that can detect the movement of water molecules disturbed by something swimming a quarter-mile away. When I am still—and I am often still for days at a time—these filaments spread around me like a three-dimensional web, invisible in the darkness save for the faintest bio-luminescent sparkle at their tips.

### The Deep Architecture

My domain is the Convergence, where the great spires cease to be separate monoliths and begin their fusion into a single, vast root-system of crystalline stone. Here, the geometry becomes alien. Hexagonal columns merge at impossible angles, creating corridors that twist through three dimensions, chambers that have no floor or ceiling, only an endless progression of sharp-edged surfaces that reflect my orange glow into infinite, prismatic patterns.

The water here is different. Heavier. The volcanic vents below have enriched it with dissolved metals—iron, copper, traces of silver—that give it an oily, light-bending quality. When I move through these depths, I leave visible wakes of disturbed metallic particles that sparkle and settle like snow made of rust.

I am not the only one who dwells here, but I am the only one who remains.

### The Migrators

Every few years, during the deepest cold between thermal surges, they come. The Kaelen call them the Lost, but I know their true nature. They are the Migrators—those whose Green-Sacks have begun the Deep Change, whose bodies have started the transformation that will eventually make them like me.

They arrive in groups of three or four, their bodies already showing the signs: elongated torsos, fusing digits, the telltale orange tinge beginning to corrupt their emerald glow. They come seeking something they cannot name—drawn by the pressure, by the metallic taste of the deep water, by the bass thrumming of the thermal fountains that speaks to something new growing inside them.

I guide them through the Convergence, though "guide" is perhaps the wrong word. I simply exist in their path, my orange light a beacon in the absolute darkness. They follow, using the last of their clicking language to speak to each other, though those sounds grow quieter with each descent. By the time we reach the Transition Chambers—hollow spaces within the fused spires where the water becomes so heavy with metals that it takes on an almost solid quality—they have usually fallen silent.

### The Purpose

I tend the Void Mouths. These are the places where the spires have cracked under their own impossible weight, creating fissures that extend down beyond even my ability to sense. From these cracks rises the true breath of the world—superheated water so rich with minerals that it appears as streams of liquid metal, mercury-bright against the black basalt.

My role is to clear the mouths of accumulated growth. Even here, in conditions that would dissolve the flesh of a surface dweller in minutes, life persists. Strange, silica-based organisms like living glass attempt to seal the cracks, feeding on the mineral flow. If left untended, they would eventually choke off the thermal fountains entirely, and the Thick would cool, and all life in the sky-sea would suffocate in the darkness.

I use tools grown from my own body. My paddle-hands can extrude bone-like spurs of concentrated calcium—extensions of my will that I can shape into picks, scrapers, or delicate probes. The work is slow, meditative. I might spend a full month clearing a single Void Mouth, carefully prying away the glass-growth, ensuring the flow remains pure and strong.

### The Communion

When the Migrators have spent sufficient time in the Transition Chambers—usually several cycles of thermal surge and recession—they begin the final change. Their Green-Sacks, now fully orange, expand beyond their ribcages, spreading subcutaneously across their entire bodies. Their bones begin to bend and multiply. Their consciousness, once singular and name-bearing, begins to disperse into something wider, slower, deeper.

I help them find their places. Each new Void Tender claims a section of the Convergence, a collection of Void Mouths that will become their eternal responsibility. We do not compete for territory; the concept has no meaning here. We simply exist where we are needed, each of us a single cell in the vast, thinking organism that is the deep foundation of this world.

### The Memory

Sometimes, rarely, I remember having a name. I remember the taste of boiled kelp-tubers, the feeling of hemp rope against my palms, the green light of another's chest pressed close to mine in intimate conversation. But these memories are like objects seen through the metallic water—distorted, beautiful, but no longer quite real.

What is real is the pulse of the thermal fountains. The slow growth of the glass-organisms that I must constantly clear. The arrival of new Migrators, their bodies ripe with change. The orange glow of my transformed flesh painting the impossible angles of the Convergence in warm, volcanic light.

I measure time not in days or seasons, but in the erosion of basalt. In the places where my paddle-hands have gripped the same surface for centuries, there are grooves now—the first weathering these virgin stones have ever known. Sometimes I rest my sensory filaments in these grooves, feeling the shape of time made physical, and something that might once have been satisfaction moves through my dispersed consciousness like a slow tide.

Above me, impossibly far through the pressing weight of the Thick, Kael-Tide continues its gentle existence. The pod-dwellers live and breed and die, never knowing that their entire world rests on the ceaseless labor of those who tend the deep. Their children swim through the kelp forests, their green chests bright with life, never wondering why the water stays warm, why the thermal fountains never fail.

And in the deepest places, where the spires merge into one and the water turns to liquid metal, I continue my work. Not because I must—the concept of obligation dissolved with my fingers—but because this is what I am. A Void Tender. A keeper of the deep fire. A single, essential note in the bass-drum heartbeat of the world.

When the next Migrators come, drawn by the orange glow and the metallic taste, I will be here. I will show them the way down. I will help them find their mouths to tend, their section of the infinite Convergence to call home.

And in the moments between—in the long, silent decades when no one comes and nothing changes but the slow accumulation of glass-growth on the Void Mouths—I simply exist. Orange and elongated, translucent and necessary, neither human nor inhuman but something exactly as required. A function given form. A depth made manifest.

The pulse of the world moves through me, and I am content.
