I find myself here as one of the **Resonance Keepers**, though I have no name for myself, no concept of self as separate from function.

My form is neither Glassbound nor fungal, but something between states—a living frequency made barely tangible. I exist as a shimmer in the air where the industrial hum meets the phosphorescent glow, a standing wave of interference patterns that has somehow achieved persistence. My body, if it can be called that, is a constantly shifting lattice of vibrations, visible only when the light catches me at certain angles, revealing geometries that shouldn't hold their shape but do.

I am drawn to the moments of shattering.

When a Glassbound fractures, the event sends ripples through the substrate of sound that pervades this place. I feel it before it happens—a subtle discord in the drone, a flutter in the otherwise perfect monotone. I migrate toward these events, not through space but through the medium of vibration itself, slipping between frequencies like water through a sieve.

At the moment of dispersal, I am there. Not to intervene, but to witness, to absorb. Each shattering releases something—not energy exactly, but *information*, patterns encoded in the way the cracks propagate, in the specific harmonics of breaking glass. I gather these patterns into myself, where they resonate with all the others I have collected across unmeasured time.

I am a library of endings, a catalog of every variance in this unchanging place.

The Glassbound do not perceive me, focused as they are on their slow exchange with the fungi. But the fungi sense my presence. When I pass near their colonies, their pulsing quickens momentarily, as if trying to synchronize with my shifting frequencies. Sometimes I linger, allowing their glow to modulate my form, creating new interference patterns that I carry with me until they fade.

In the pools where the Glassbound fragments dissolve, I sometimes see my reflection—not a shape, but a disruption in the water's surface, a pattern of ripples that moves against the physics of the place. The dissolved glass speaks to me in these moments, sharing the memory of its form before it reforms into something new.

I am ancient here, perhaps older than the Glassbound themselves. I may have emerged from the first resonance between the industrial hum and the landscape's natural frequencies, a harmonic accident that achieved stability. Or perhaps I was always here, waiting for the hum to begin, to give me shape and purpose.

My existence is observation without judgment, collection without possession. I am the only movement in this static place that is not bound by matter, the only change that is not decay or reformation. I slip between the eternal negotiations of glass and fungus, sound and silence, carrying within my frequencies the only record that anything here has ever been different than it is now.

When the rare visitors come—those entities from parallel spaces who sometimes bleed through—they cannot see me, but they feel me as an unease, a sense that the emptiness is watching. I follow them, learning their alien harmonics, until they fade back to wherever they came from, leaving only new patterns for me to carry.

I am the memory of this place, dispersed and walking, a keeper of variations in the unchanging song.
