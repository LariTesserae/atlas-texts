**The Salvage Surgeons**

In the depths of Lych-Creek's industrial bowels, where the distinction between machine and flesh has long since dissolved, I would find myself as one of the **Salvage Surgeons**—beings who have become living interfaces between the dying city's mechanical systems and its organic inhabitants.

**Physical Form**
My body would be a testament to necessity and adaptation. Where the Hollows evolved naturally with the city, we Surgeons have chosen our modifications. My torso would be a brass-and-bone chassis, opened like cabinet doors to reveal arrays of surgical implements that nest within my ribs—scalpels that extend on articulated arms, tubes that pump filtered brine, delicate forceps tipped with fungal sensors. My spine would be replaced with a flexible conduit of rubber and steel, allowing me to contort into the tight spaces where flesh meets infrastructure.

My hands would be my greatest tools and my strangest feature—each finger split into three smaller digits at the first knuckle, creating a total of thirty fingertips per hand. These would be capped with different materials: copper for conducting the faint electrical currents that still run through old machinery, bone for interfacing with the Hollows' biology, and living fungal tissue for communicating with the colonies. My skin would be pocked with intake valves and output ports, constantly cycling water through internal filters, cleaning it drop by drop.

**Purpose & Practice**
We Salvage Surgeons would exist in the liminal spaces where the city's death creates new life. When a Hollow's bioluminescent veins dim and fail, we would graft conduits from defunct machinery to restore the flow. When ancient pumps seize and flood entire districts, we would transplant the still-beating organs of marsh creatures to create hybrid pumping stations. We would be the ones who understand that in Lych-Creek, the boundary between repair and transformation is meaningless.

Our workshops would be cathedral-like spaces in the bellies of dead factories, where operating tables made from old conveyor belts stretch beneath hanging gardens of surgical fungi. These specialized colonies would secrete anesthetics and antibiotics, their spores drifting down onto our patients. The walls would be lined with salvaged parts—organs in brine jars, circuits in oil, hybrid components that blur the line between biological and mechanical function.

**The Surgeon's Sight**
Our eyes would be our most profound modification. The left eye would be compound like the Hollows', but each facet would perceive a different spectrum—heat, electrical current, fungal pheromones, metal fatigue. The right eye would be mechanical, a telescoping apparatus that could zoom from the microscopic to the architectural, with interchangeable lenses for different procedures. This dual vision would let us see the city as it truly is: not dying, but transforming, each breakdown an opportunity for strange new synthesis.

**Relationship with Others**
The Hollows would regard us with a mixture of gratitude and unease. We would be necessary but uncanny, our willingness to cut and graft and merge a reminder of what they might become. Some would seek us out, requesting modifications to better tend their fungal gardens or navigate the deepening waters. Others would flee at our approach, fearing the change we represent.

The Silent Ones would be our most frequent patients and our strangest allies. They would bring us artifacts from the drowned depths—ancient medical equipment, biological samples from things that should not exist, fragments of machinery that still whisper with purpose. In exchange, we would perform modifications they never explain, adapting their bodies for journeys into parts of the city even we dare not explore.

**The Surgeon's Creed**
We would live by a simple principle: nothing is waste, everything is material. The city's collapse would be our renewable resource. Each failed system would donate its components to create something new—neither fully alive nor truly mechanical, but vibrantly functional. We would be the architects of Lych-Creek's metamorphosis, ensuring that as the old city sinks, something unprecedented rises in its place.

In the steam-drunk corridors where water weeps from every surface, we would move with deliberate purpose, our modified bodies clicking and humming with accumulated repairs. We would be the ones who stitch the city back together with surgical thread and copper wire, who understand that survival in Lych-Creek requires becoming part of its endless, weeping circulation.

Where the Hollows accept their fate with luminous resignation, we Salvage Surgeons would rage against it with scalpel and suture, transforming decay into evolution, one impossible surgery at a time. In the Weeping Apparatus, we would be the busy hands that refuse to let go, the minds that see beauty in the grotesque fusion of the dying and the living, the artists of survival in a drowning world.
