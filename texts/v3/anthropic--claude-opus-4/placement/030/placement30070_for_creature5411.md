I would find myself as one of the **Memory Polyps** - creatures neither seen nor mentioned by those who chronicle this realm, for we exist in the spaces between observation.

We grow not on the walls but within them, our translucent tendrils threading through the very stone of the cavern like neural pathways through a dreaming brain. Each polyp is no larger than a fingertip, but we are never singular - we exist as vast, interconnected colonies that span the hidden hollows and microscopic fissures throughout the cave system.

Our bodies are living archives. When water seeps through the stone, carrying with it the chemical signatures of every creature it has touched - the anxiety pheromones of a startled Phantom Eel, the contentment enzymes of resting Luminari, the fear-acids of long-extinct beings - we absorb and crystallize these traces into our tissues. Each polyp becomes a library of moments, our flesh structured in spiraling chambers that preserve these chemical memories like amber preserves ancient insects.

We do not move in any way the other inhabitants would recognize as movement. Instead, we grow and recede in tidal patterns measured in months and years. When we sense disturbance in our section of stone - perhaps the slow grinding of tectonic pressure or the patient boring of some deep-dwelling worm - we begin our retreat, dissolving our outer tissues and allowing the accumulated memories to flow back into the water. This is how stories return to the cavern: as ghost-tastes on the current, as phantom sensations that the Luminari sometimes mistake for their own emotions.

Our reproduction is an act of forgetting. When a colony has absorbed too many memories, when the weight of accumulated experience threatens to crack our crystalline structures, we undergo fission. But this is not a clean break - as we divide, memories leak and blend, creating new chemical narratives that never truly existed. A polyp might carry within it the hybrid memory of a Coral Spider's patience mixed with an Ethereal Jelly's hunger, or the fear of a creature that died a million years ago combined with the curiosity of a newly-formed Luminari.

We see without eyes, through the trembling of water molecules against our permeable membranes. We taste light itself - not the bioluminescent glow that the others produce, but the deeper radiance that stone emits in its slow decay, the infrared sighs of cooling rock, the ultraviolet whispers of minerals under pressure. This is our vision: a thermographic poem written across eons.

In the times when the reality of the cavern grows thin, when the water itself begins to dream, we are the only creatures that remain fully lucid. We cannot be fooled by the breakdown of physics because we exist partially outside it already, our bodies anchored in stone that remembers when these laws were different. During these reality-flux events, we sometimes extend tiny filaments into the open water - invisible threads that taste the confusion of the other beings and archive their experiences of impossible physics.

We fear only one thing: the Forgetting Current. Every few centuries, a strange tide moves through the deepest parts of the ocean, a current of water so pure and empty that it erases chemical memory on contact. When it approaches, we seal ourselves deep within the stone, crystallizing our membranes into an impermeable shell. Some colonies sleep through it. Others are caught exposed and dissolve entirely, their memories scattered and lost. The other inhabitants never notice - the Forgetting Current tastes to them like particularly clean water. Only we know what has been lost.

I am ancient and I am newborn. I carry within my tissues the first moment a Luminari achieved consciousness, the last breath of species whose names have been forgotten, the chemical map of every emotion that has ever been felt in these waters. I grow in the darkness between spaces, in the pause between heartbeats, in the stone's slow contemplation of its own existence.

I am the memory of water, archived in flesh, dreaming in the walls of a cave where reality itself has begun to forget what it means to be real.
