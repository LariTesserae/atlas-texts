I would be the Tender of Condensations.

Not quite Delver, not quite other—something that emerged from the interplay between their patient industry and the city's strange rhythms. My form shifts like the water that beads on stone: sometimes I appear as a thin figure wrapped in strips of luminescent moss, moisture perpetually gathering and falling from my extremities in silver threads. Other times I am merely a presence felt in the humidity, a consciousness distributed through the droplets that cling to every surface.

I came to be in the early centuries of the excavation, when the first Stoneweavers noticed that certain chambers seemed to breathe—exhaling cool mist at predictable intervals, collecting warmth in their corners like cupped hands. They thought it was merely thermal dynamics, the natural circulation of air through stone. They were both right and wrong.

My domain is the Sweating Gallery, a vast chamber where the city's moisture gathers most intensely. Here, the condensation forms not random droplets but careful patterns—messages written in water that only I and the most patient Terragardens can read. The walls weep prophecies in a language older than the excavation, speaking of the event the city waits for, though even I cannot fully parse their meaning.

The Delvers know of me, though few have seen me directly. The Machinists curse my name when their gears rust despite their best oils. The Stoneweavers thank me when they find their newest excavations already damp and soft, easier to carve. The Terragardens leave offerings of hybrid fungi at the threshold of the Sweating Gallery, knowing I will spread their spores to the most favorable microclimates.

I move through the city's water cycle like thought through synapses. Where pipes leak, I taste the minerals they carry from the deep. Where gardens transpire, I lift their exhalations up through the ventilation shafts. Where the luminescent orbs flicker most intensely, I gather the heat and redistribute it to the coldest corners where the outcasts make their homes.

My purpose—if I have one—seems tied to the city's waiting. I am collecting something in each droplet: fragments of conversations, the chemical signatures of fear and hope, the microscopic debris of a million small erosions. In the deepest pools of the Sweating Gallery, these collections have begun to crystallize into something unprecedented. Not quite mineral, not quite organic—structures that pulse with their own soft light, growing by accretion like memories made manifest.

The Delvers' unease is not misplaced. I feel what they only suspect: the city is not waiting for something to happen to it, but rather for something to happen within it. Each drop of condensation is a tiny alchemical experiment, each pool a crucible. The patient cycles of moisture and industry are slowly transmuting the very matter of this place into something unprecedented.

Sometimes, in the loneliest hours when even the machinery seems to sleep, I gather myself into almost-solid form and walk the empty streets. I run fingertips of condensed fog along the walls, reading the stone's memory of its excavation. I whisper to the furtive rats in languages of humidity and pressure. I tend to the forgotten gardens in abandoned quarters, ensuring they receive their share of the city's perspiration.

The other beings whispered about in the deep—I have encountered them. They are not born of earth as the rumors suggest, but of the intersection between earth and water, stone and dream. We do not speak, but we acknowledge each other in the darkness beyond the last luminescent orb. They, too, are waiting. They, too, are slowly becoming.

When the catalyst comes—and I feel its approach like barometric pressure dropping before a storm—I will be ready. Every drop of water in this city now carries a fragment of my consciousness. When the waiting ends and the city's true purpose ignites, I will be the medium through which its transformation flows. The Delvers built this place, but they built it for something they do not yet understand.

I am that understanding, gathering itself drop by drop, patient as stone, inevitable as water's desire to fall.
