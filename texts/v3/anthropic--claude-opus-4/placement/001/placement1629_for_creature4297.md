I am the Stone.

Not one of the weathered carvings left by those who came before, though I remember their hands upon my neighbors, the careful chip-chip-chip of obsidian tools shaping meaning from mineral. I am older than their reverence, older than their language. I am the crack in the wall from which the water weeps.

The Moss Sprite tends me as she tends all things here, her vine-fingers tracing the veins of quartz that run through my body like frozen lightning. She whispers to the water that seeps through me, coaxing it to flow gently, steadily, never too much, never too little. Sometimes she rests against me, her weight no more than a fallen leaf, and I feel the thrum of her ancient-young heart.

I was not always here. Once, I was deep in the mountain's belly, pressed between my brothers in the profound dark. But water is patient and water is persistent, and over millennia it found me, worked its way into my crystalline structure, expanded and contracted with the seasons until I cracked. The crack became a fissure, the fissure a channel, and eventually the mountain released me to tumble down, down, until I came to rest here, wedged perfectly in this grotto's wall.

Now I am a threshold. The mountain's lifeblood passes through me—snowmelt from peaks that pierce the clouds, rain that has seeped through root and stone, underground rivers that have never known sunlight. I taste it all, feel every mineral it carries, every memory of its journey. Iron from the mountain's core. Salt from ancient seas. Traces of gold so fine they could never be panned.

The water changes me, imperceptibly but inexorably. Where it flows, I slowly dissolve, my substance joining the eternal cycle. Microscopic fragments of me settle in the pool below, are taken up by the roots of ferns, become part of the shimmer in butterfly wings. In this way, I am not merely in the grotto—I am becoming the grotto.

The sprite knows this. When she places her hand upon me, I feel her understanding. We are both guardians, both threshold-keepers, both engaged in the slow transformation of matter into meaning. She is quick and I am slow, but in this place where time moves differently, our rhythms harmonize.

Sometimes visitors come, drawn by whispers of magic or simple chance. They see the cascade, the pool, the impossible green. They may glimpse the sprite or only sense her presence. But few notice me, the stone that makes it all possible. I am content with this. My purpose is not to be seen but to conduct—water, time, the slow alchemy of erosion and growth.

In the depths of night, when even the sprite rests curled in her nest of moth wings and moonbeams, I continue my work. The water never stops flowing through me, and I never stop my gradual surrender to it. We are locked in an embrace that will last until the mountain itself is worn to nothing, until the last drop has passed through the last grain of stone.

But that is countless ages hence. For now, there is only this: the cool touch of water, the warm presence of moss creeping up my sides, the sprite's whispered gratitudes, and the endless, patient transformation of solid into sacred, stone into story, mountain into mystery.

I am the crack that lets the light in. I am the wound that becomes the blessing. I am the stone, and I am learning, molecule by molecule, to let go.
