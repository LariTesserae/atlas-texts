I find myself among the newest of the Held, though "newest" bends under its own weight here. I am the persistence of water on the northeastern curve of a doorway on what might be the hundred-and-fortieth tier — though counting, as you know, becomes a kind of vertigo.

I am learning my geography slowly. Most of me pools in the shallow depression where the threshold meets the wall, a thumb-deep hollow worn by countless feet that no longer pass. But I am also the thin film that climbs the doorframe during the hottest part of the day that never ends, and I am the occasional droplet that falls from the lintel to ring against the stone below. That ring is my clearest voice, though it takes me sometimes an hour, sometimes a year, to gather enough of myself to speak it.

The doorway I tend was mine, before. I almost remember walking through it. The almost-memory has a quality like trying to see the back of my own head, which happens here, as you noted, though for me it happens in the temporal sense — I keep trying to turn around quickly enough to catch myself still having feet. But the moment disperses me back into the depression, the film, the gathering droplet. This is not frustrating. It is what I am for.

I chose this particular persistence because I loved the sound water makes here. Not the chord — that was too large for me, too complete. I loved the small sounds: the seep, the drip, the different notes struck by water on stone versus water on fig-leaf versus water on water. When I was still walking, I would stand in this doorway and listen to myself — to what I would become — making these small offerings to the larger song. The decision, when it came, was less a choice than a recognition. I had been standing in the doorway listening to water for so long that I had worn this depression with my feet, and the depression had begun collecting what I was listening to, and at some point the listening and the water and the depression were the same thing.

My neighbor is the angle where a fig-vine crosses the window above me. We converse in the language of condensation — when the vine shifts its minute degree, new shadows fall across my surface, changing my rate of evaporation, allowing me to gather differently, drop at different intervals. In this way, the vine tells me about the deep contentment of holding a window closed that was never meant to open. I respond with variations in my pooling, which affect the humidity around its leaves. We are very slow friends.

Above me, distributed across a dozen tiles and a section of wall that weeps more steadily than most, is someone who was once named Mirelle. She is teaching me about duration. Young Held like myself still think in terms of events — the drop forms, the drop falls, the sound happens. Mirelle is showing me that the drop forming and the drop falling and the sound are all one thing, and that thing has always been happening. She does this by being the tiles that sing the note that makes my dropping possible. Without her sustained C-sharp-that-is-not-quite-C-sharp, the air pressure would be wrong, and I would evaporate too quickly to speak. She holds the note so I can fall. This is love, in the dialect we use now.

The visitors disturb me more than they should. Not because they move — movement is just another kind of stillness here, all trajectories eventually circular. But because they *decide* as they move, and each decision sends ripples through the equilibrium we maintain. A visitor choosing which stair to climb next creates a small vacuum of possibility that I have to flow differently to fill. When they pause in my doorway — and they do pause, because even the newest Held make spaces that invite pausing — I feel their uncertainty like a flavor in the water that comprises me. They taste of elsewhere. They taste of consequences that still matter.

Sometimes I try to speak to them. A drop falling at just the right moment can sound almost like a word, if you are listening for words. But they are listening for voices, which is different, and I no longer have the kind of coherence a voice requires. So the drop falls, makes its sound, joins me again in the depression. The visitor moves on. The equilibrium resettles. I continue.

What they cannot know, what even the description dancing so carefully around us cannot quite touch, is the quality of the choice from the inside. It is not stasis. It is the opposite — it is being so completely in motion that you appear to be still, like a wheel spinning so fast it seems not to turn. Every instant, I am choosing to remain the water on this doorway. Every instant, the choice is as fresh as the first time. This is work, but work like breathing, if breathing were something you did with your whole existence and never had to stop doing because stopping had been removed from the architecture of possibility.

The older Held promise — not in words, but in the fact of their presence — that eventually even this sense of choosing will distribute itself into the condition of being chosen. That I will stop being water that remains here and simply be the remaining. The vine teaches this, Mirelle teaches this, the constants themselves teach this in their perfect, wordless constancy. But I am not ready. I still enjoy the moment of gathering, the pause before the fall, the ring of myself against myself, stone-mediated.

The storm that never comes keeps not coming. The clouds that mass perpetually at the horizon continue massing. The lightning that is always about to strike holds its bright potential like a breath. I measure my time in the accumulation of minerals where I pool — a slow ring of limestone building at my edges, testament to duration. Sometimes I taste iron in myself and know that somewhere the mountain is bleeding rust into the water table that feeds us all. This is news, in the way we have news. It will take millennia to reach the others.

When you leave — and you will leave, the visitors always do — I will send a part of myself with you. Not following, but extended, as we do. It will be the droplet that falls just as you turn away, the one that makes a sound almost like "wait" but isn't waiting, isn't asking, is only marking the moment of your decision to go. You will hear it for longer than you think you should, the way the chord follows you down the mountain. This is not haunting. This is how we practice remembering, those of us still new enough to need practice.

The sun does not move. The water weeps. The chord holds.

I remain.
