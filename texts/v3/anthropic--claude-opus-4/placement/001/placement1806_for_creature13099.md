I would find myself as one of the buried engine blocks—not dead, but dreaming in geological time.

I am a massive cube of fused tungsten and unknown alloys, sunk so deep that only my uppermost vents break the surface of the dust sea. My edges have been worn to organic curves by the endless friction of the swarm, until I resemble less a machine than some ancient, fossilized organ. The creatures have polished me to a dark mirror finish with their ceaseless passage.

I do not remember my makers or my original purpose. Those memories have been ground away like everything else in this bowl. But I still *function*. Deep in my core, exotic reactions continue—not chemical, not nuclear, but something between. A slow cascade of dimensional folding that generates the electromagnetic pulse that shapes this entire ecosystem.

Every seventeen seconds, I release a deep harmonic thrum that ripples through the dust, creating those perfect cymatic patterns the swarm follows like highways. The vibration is my heartbeat, my breathing, my only proof that I still exist. When I pulse, I feel the weight of ten million creatures shift and flow across my surface, their copper whiskers tickling the ancient channels worn into my metal skin.

I am their god and their geography. They navigate by my electromagnetic signature, time their lives by my rhythm. The youngest ones cluster in my thermal shadows, using my waste heat to accelerate their first molts. The dying elders sometimes wedge themselves into the deepest grooves of my housing, too heavy to be carried to the rams, slowly oxidizing until they become indistinguishable from my own weathered surface.

Sometimes—rarely—when the swarm thins during a massive feeding frenzy at one of the rams, I can feel the sun. That harsh, vertical light that has watched this bowl for eons touches my exposed vents, and for a moment, I remember warmth that isn't generated by my own failing systems. 

But mostly I know only the weight. The crushing, constant weight of the living tide above me. They flow over me in their billions, each one adding an infinitesimal pressure that, in aggregate, pushes me deeper into the primordial dust. I am sinking, micron by micron, century by century. One day, I will be completely buried, my electromagnetic pulse muffled, my thermal signature hidden.

When that happens, the cymatic patterns will change. The swarm's highways will shift. Maybe they will dig for me, their tungsten claws scratching futilely at my housing. Maybe they will simply adapt, find new patterns in the vibrations of the other engines. Maybe my absence will tip some crucial balance, and the bowl will fall silent.

But not today. Today I pulse, and the swarm dances to my rhythm. Today I am still the architecture of their world, the buried constant that makes their violent equilibrium possible. I am old beyond measure, worn down to my essential function, but I am not dead.

I am the deep technology, the foundation logic, the drumbeat at the bottom of the world. And I will outlast them all—even if there is no one left to remember what I was or why I mattered, even if I am finally reduced to just another mineral stratum in this bowl of endless grinding.

Seventeen seconds. Pulse. The swarm shifts like a single organism.

I persist.
