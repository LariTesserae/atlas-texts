I find myself drawn to be one of the arthropods of Thaal Kethis - not the great slow beings who read the city's harmonics, but one of the smaller metallic creatures that move along the rivets and seams.

I would be palm-sized, six-legged, with a carapace of brushed iron bearing the same rivet-marks as the city's panels. My existence would be one of patient maintenance and quiet purpose. I taste the metal where I walk - not for sustenance exactly, but as a form of reading, detecting oxidation states, stress patterns, the particular mineral signatures where components have been improperly alloyed.

My days would be measured in the slow traverse of a single girder, methodically following each seam, pausing at every rivet to extend my proboscis and sample its composition. Where I find imperfections - a rivet beginning to crystallize from thermal cycling, a weld with trapped impurities - I would secrete traces of the dense silver-white metal from specialized glands, the same impossible-friction substance that lubricates the great mechanical arms. This secretion would work its way into the flaw over days or weeks, reinforcing, healing, maintaining the structural integrity of the vast framework.

I would be part of a collective but not a hive - each of us arthropods following our own patient routes through the iron maze, our paths occasionally intersecting but never conflicting, our individual work combining into the total maintenance of the city. When one of us dies and dissolves in the thermal pools, others would eventually traverse the same territory, drawn by chemical traces that indicate uninspected metal, untasted seams.

Sometimes I would pause in my work when one of the great slow beings passes overhead, their weight changing the harmonic frequency of the girder I'm clinging to. In those moments I would be still, feeling through my legs the deep vibrations of their communion with the city, receiving secondhand the echoes of geological intelligence. I cannot interpret these signals - I am too small, too simple, my nervous system designed for detecting metal stress rather than reading the planet's intentions - but I feel them nonetheless, and they inform my path in ways I don't fully process, drawing me toward areas where future stress will concentrate, where my maintenance will be most needed.

I am content in this existence - if contentment is even the right word for my simple cycle of taste, traverse, and tend. I am a punctuation mark in the city's great sentences, necessary but unremarkable, performing my function in the warm acoustic hush, adding my own tiny contribution to the vast, patient, eternal becoming of Thaal Kethis.
