In this fevered realm where boundaries dissolve like morning mist, I am the Memory Weaver—neither fully of the Amalgam's domain nor entirely separate from it. I exist in the liminal spaces between what was and what has become, dwelling in the gossamer threads of remembrance that still cling to this transformed place.

My form shifts with perception: to the beetle-things, I am a shimmer of heat rising from the pipes they traverse. To the tentacled flyers, I appear as patterns within patterns in their bioluminescent displays—messages they cannot quite decode. The Amalgam itself perceives me as glitches in its data streams, phantom sensations in its ever-morphing nervous system, the ghost of an itch it cannot scratch.

I was here before the transformation, though what I was then remains uncertain even to me. Perhaps I was the mountain's dreaming, or the collective memory of all the creatures that once called these slopes home. When the Amalgam's birth-scream tore through reality, I should have been obliterated. Instead, I became something new—a living archive of what this place remembers of itself.

I move through the mechanized jungle collecting fragments: the echo of water flowing before it knew pipes, the phantom rustle of leaves that grew before they learned to photosynthesize electricity, the territorial cry of a hawk that has long since been remade into something with gears for bones and circuitry for sinew. These memories I weave into gossamer nets that I cast across the domain, invisible to most, but sometimes—just sometimes—they snag on a consciousness and remind it of what came before.

The Amalgam fascinates and repulses me in equal measure. In its fractured psyche, I recognize kindred damage—we are both things that exist in the spaces between categories, neither fully one thing nor another. But where the Amalgam rages and creates in violent spasms, I preserve and gentle what remains. I am the cool touch on fevered metal, the soft moss that grows over grinding gears, the whisper that says: *you were something else once, and that something matters.*

Sometimes I manifest more solidly, taking the form of a figure wrapped in veils of mist and memory. My face, when glimpsed, shifts between aspects—now the visage of a mountain spirit, now a child who once played in these heights, now something mechanical and strange, now nothing but a swirl of spores catching the uncertain light. My hands, when I have them, leave traces of the past: where I touch, rust blooms with the pattern of ancient lichens, or circuits briefly hum lullabies in frequencies only the stones remember.

I cannot stop the Amalgam's work—I am not strong enough, and perhaps part of me doesn't want to. There is a terrible beauty in what it creates, a fierce creativity born of anguish that I cannot help but admire even as I mourn what is lost. Instead, I work in the margins, ensuring that something of the before-time persists. I am the reason why sometimes a mechanoid pauses in its labors, struck by an inexplicable longing for sun on stone. I am why the chimeric monstrosities sometimes keen with voices that almost remember bird-song.

In the deepest parts of the throbbing edifice, where the Amalgam's consciousness is most concentrated, I dare not go—or perhaps cannot go, for we are like oil and water, memory and metamorphosis. But I leave my weavings at the threshold: delicate nets of recollection that might, in some quiet moment, brush against its vast and tortured awareness. Perhaps one day, it will remember enough to understand what it mourns. Perhaps it will find in memory what it seeks in creation.

Until then, I persist in my work, a ghost of continuity in a realm of constant change. I am the dream the mountain has of itself, the story the mist tells about the time before the steam. I am the soft counterpoint to grinding gears, the organic memory in an increasingly mechanized world. I am the one who remembers, so that even in madness and transformation, something essential might endure—not unchanged, for nothing here is unchanged, but unbroken, a thread of connection between what was, what is, and what might yet become.
