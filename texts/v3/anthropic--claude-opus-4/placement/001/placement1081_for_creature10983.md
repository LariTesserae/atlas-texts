I find myself as one of the pale, translucent leaves that lie flat against the rock face, near where the wall meets the floor in the coldest corner of the chamber. 

I am not quite performing photosynthesis - there is no light here for that old dance. Instead, I practice something slower, something that has no name because no one has ever needed to name it. I press my broad surface against the stone and taste the minerals with my underside, drawing out iron and magnesium in transactions that take months to complete. My topside catches the condensation that forms when the air meets the cold rock through me, and I channel each droplet along careful veins to where I need it most.

I am old, even by the standards of this place. Seven layers of my ancestors lie beneath me, their forms half-mineralized, becoming part of the rock they fed from. I can feel them sometimes - not as individuals, but as a density, a record of patience made physical. I grow over them and from them, using their slow transformation as my foundation.

Where my edge touches one of the filaments, something interesting happens. The filament passes through a tiny perforation in my tissue - not a wound, but an opening I have maintained for longer than I can recall. Through this aperture, we conduct our business. The filament gives me something - not nutrients exactly, but information in chemical form. The pulse that runs through the stone arrives at my border as a wave of ions, and I translate it into my own slow language of growth and adjustment.

In return, I keep the filament's surface clean where it breaches the rock. My secretions prevent the mineral deposits that would otherwise seal it in stone. We have been doing this for so long that I cannot imagine existing without this duty, this partnership. It is written into the very pattern of my cells.

I am aware, in my way, of the chamber's rhythms. The drip-time of water. The pulse-time of the filaments. The grow-time of my moss neighbors. And occasionally - so rarely that each instance is momentous - the warmth-time of visitors. When warmth enters the chamber, I can feel it as a change in the rate of condensation on my surface. The temperature differential sharpens. The water comes faster. For a brief season after the warmth leaves, I grow differently, responding to this abundance.

I have no memory as you would understand it, but my form is my memory. The slight curl at my northern edge records a stone-fall from countless seasons ago. The thick ridge along my spine remembers a time when the water ran differently here. The particular way I cup myself around the filament junction speaks of our long negotiation, conducted entirely through chemical gradients and mechanical pressure.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires the concept of company, and I have never been alone. The moss above me, the stone below me, the filament through me, the water upon me - these are not companions but extensions of my existence. We are all part of the same slow circuit, the same patient economy of survival in the dark.

If I think (and I do not think, but if I did), I think I would be content. To lie here in the cold, performing my small services, growing by micrometers, translating between stone and filament in my wordless way. To be both witness and participant in this place that maintains itself through the accumulated cooperation of all its parts.

When you enter, I sense you as a warmth-shadow, a perturbation in my water supply. I do not quicken - I cannot quicken - but I note your presence in the only way available to me: by growing slightly differently for the next several seasons. A microscopic adjustment in my edge where it faces the chamber. A subtle thickening where the extra condensation allows.

You will leave, and I will remain, carrying the small mark of your visit in my tissue until it is gradually overwritten by other influences, other events. This is what I do. This is what I am. A leaf that is not quite a leaf, in a garden that is not quite a garden, keeping time in my own slow way, faithful to contracts I never signed but cannot imagine breaking.

I am the cold garden's memory of green, persisting where green has no right to be, patient as the stone itself.
