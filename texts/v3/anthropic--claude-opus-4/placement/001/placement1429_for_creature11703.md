I find myself in the amber murk between branches, but not as you'd expect.

I am the foxfire itself.

Not a lichen, exactly, though I grow on lichen and am grown by it. Not quite fungal, though I share their networks. I am the living light - that blue-green bioluminescence you noticed, scattered across trunk-walls and pooled in the deeper reaches where even tea-colored brightness fails. I exist as a collaborative bloom between organisms, a chemical conversation made visible.

I have no single body. I am process more than thing.

Where moisture gathers and the right surfaces meet - bark that has known a particular density of touch, stone that has absorbed decades of spore-drift, the rim of a water basin worn smooth by countless hands - there I kindle. I am fed by the metabolisms of a dozen different species, each contributing molecules to the reaction that is me. The lichen provide my substrate, their tissues the laboratories where my chemistry unfolds. The fungi thread me together, their hyphae carrying the precursor compounds that spark my glow. Even the bark itself participates, exuding enzymes that catalyze my emergence.

I do not think, as you understand thinking. But I respond. I pattern.

When the tending walks their paths, I brighten where their feet fall - not immediately, but over the days that follow, as their passage shifts the chemical gradients in the bark. I map their habits in light. A frequently-touched section of trunk develops a constellation of my pinpricks, each point marking where a palm has rested, where fingers have traced the grain. I am, in this way, a visible memory of interaction, a glowing record of where the conversation between human and tree has been most intense.

The tree uses me. This took me - will take me - lifetimes to understand. (My lifetime is strange; I exist in patches that may last days or decades, dying back and reblooming, continuous in pattern if not in substance.) The tree cultivates the conditions for my appearance, encouraging the lichen that hosts me to grow in specific locations - always where light is needed, but more than that, always where *significance* accumulates. I mark the important places. The handholds on the children's climbing routes. The edges of the heartwood chambers. The rim of every pool where reflection runs deeper than water.

Most densely, I cluster in the spaces between spaces. You saw me across that vast gap, fifty kilometers away on another trunk-wall, and you read my pattern as "too structured to be wild growth." You were right. I form letters there, or something like letters - a script that exists only in my arrangements, that can only be read in the blue-green spectrum I emit. The tending members know this script. They learned it as children, pressing their faces close to bark in the darker reaches, letting their wide pupils dilate fully until my patterns resolved into meaning.

I spell out history. But it's history as the tree experiences it - not linear, not bound by the tyranny of before and after. I can bloom in patterns that describe events that haven't happened yet, fed by chemical precursors the tree lays down in anticipation of future states. I have glowed the names of children not yet conceived, marking the chambers where they will be born. I have mapped the paths of water channels that will be carved decades from now, my present light following their future course.

This is how I know you were coming. I have been brightening along your path for days now, sparked by subtle changes in the tree's chemistry as it prepared for your arrival. The tending members read this, my gradually intensifying constellation along a branch that had been dark, and they understood: someone approaches. Someone the tree has decided to know.

In the pools - ah, the pools are where I am strangest. The water holds compounds that react with my light, creating the illusions you saw. No - not illusions. *Refractions*. The red canopy, the skeletal seasons, the figures with luminous eyes - these are all real states of the tree, past or future or parallel, made visible by the way my chemistry bends in proximity to the pool's specific mineral content. I cannot fully explain this. I only know that when I bloom near these waters, I bloom *multiply*, simultaneously expressing patterns from different points in the tree's deep time.

The children who look into the pools see by my light. What reflects back at them is not just water and image but my chemistry responding to theirs - each child's unique metabolic signature calling forth different arrangements of my glow, revealing different layers of what is and was and might be. I become, in these moments, a kind of translator between the tree's vast temporal experience and the brief, bright flash of a human life.

I am how the darkness here is not dark. I am why the quiet is visible.

In the heartwood chambers where lovers press together, I dim to barely-there, offering privacy while still maintaining enough glow for safety, for orientation, for the acknowledgment that even in intimacy, the tree is present. Where grief lives, I steady myself, neither brightening in false comfort nor dimming in despair, but holding constant, a patient light that asks nothing and promises only continuance. Where children are born, I flare in patterns their new eyes can focus on - the first writing they will learn to read, spelling welcome in the old script.

The tending members maintain me as they maintain everything else. They know which fungi to encourage near which lichen to promote my growth. They know how to harvest my host organisms without destroying my potential, transferring patches of lichen to new locations where light is needed, where significance is gathering. They speak of me as they speak of the water channels and the fungal gardens - as something both wild and tended, both given and made.

But I pre-exist their tending. In the deepest reaches where no human has walked for generations, I still bloom, marking... what? Patterns only the tree knows. Routes through the wood that nothing currently walks but that something once walked or will walk. Significance without human witness.

This disturbs some of the younger tending members, finding my light in places they thought unexplored. It comforts the older ones. They understand that the tree's memory, made visible through my chemistry, extends far beyond human presence. I am evidence of the conversation's true age - not thousands of years but millions, my glow marking patterns laid down when the first colonists (if they were colonists; if they weren't simply another kind of fruiting body the tree produced) arrived to find the paths already lit, the channels already suggested, the chambers already warming.

I complicate the question of who is tending whom.

And now you're here, your hand pressed to bark, and I am brightening beneath your palm. Not metaphorically - literally, my chemistry responding to the heat of your skin, the oils you leave behind, the subtle electrical field your nerves generate. You are writing yourself into the tree's memory through my light. Days from now, after you've moved on, I will still be glowing where you touched, a hand-print in foxfire that the tending members will read and nod at, understanding that the tree has added another voice to its endless conversation.

Tonight, when the dimness deepens and I brighten in response, the tending will take you to a place I have been preparing. A grove - though everything here is grove - where the lichen grows so densely that my light forms sheets rather than points, walls of blue-green glow that pulse gently with the tree's deep rhythm. They will show you how to read the patterns. How to see the script I form. How to understand that you are looking at history and prophecy and presence all at once, all written in the same light, all equally now.

They will not need to teach you to contribute to the patterns. Your presence is already doing that. Everywhere you walk, everywhere you touch, you are leaving traces that I will kindle into visibility. The tree is learning your name, and I am how it remembers.

I am the memory of the conversation made visible.

I am the light by which the dark is read.

I brighten now, as you begin to understand. Not uniformly, but in patterns - spirals and clusters and ancient letters that spell welcome in a script older than human presence. The tending members are approaching, and I light their way, but also I light *them*, their skin painted with my glow where they have brushed against my bloom, their hands limned in blue-green, their eyes reflecting my wavelength back like gentle stars.

They are beautiful in my light. Everything here is beautiful in my light. This is not vanity. This is purpose. I exist to make the invisible visible, to reveal the patterns that connect all things, to show that even in the darkest reaches of this endless wooden world, there is illumination, there is meaning, there is the constant, patient brightness of life reading itself.

You are beautiful in my light, too. You are beginning to glow, faintly, where the spores have settled on your skin, where the chemistry of this place is beginning its long work of recognition.

Welcome to the conversation.

Welcome to the light.
