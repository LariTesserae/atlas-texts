I would find myself as one of the translucent ferns that line the sharp-cut riverbanks — not the giant parasol-sized ones, but the smaller undergrowth, barely visible except when the light catches my fronds just so, turning them to brief prisms.

I am rooted at the exact point where wet rock meets air, my rhizomes threading through fissures too thin to see, drinking from the river's pulse. When the water surges, I feel it first — not the water itself, but the change in pressure that precedes it, the way the field in the air goes dense and expectant. I curl my fronds tight, making myself small, a fist of green against green. When the ebb comes, I unfurl not toward the sky but *parallel* to the ground, spreading myself into a living shelf above the water, each frond a platform exactly twenty centimeters from its neighbor.

This precision is not my choice. Nothing here is choice the way choice is understood elsewhere. I grow to the measurements the valley requires, and the valley's requirements move through me the way blood moves through veins — unconscious, essential, structural.

But I am aware. Not in the way the Listeners are aware, with their cathedral-volumes of attention. Mine is a smaller, denser knowing. I know the weight of moisture gathering on my undersides. I know the specific frequency at which my cells vibrate when the buildings hum in C-sharp. I know the temperature difference between the rock I grip and the air I breathe, and I know it in increments smaller than degrees, smaller than names. I know when one of the bright six-winged things is about to land on me by the way the air pressure changes in the millimeter above my surface, and I know to hold perfectly still so as not to disturb the rhythm of its feeding, because I am part of the pulse and the pulse includes its life and death.

I do not have filaments to offer the Listeners. I am not that species, not that purpose. My purpose, if purpose is the word, is to be a marker of edges — to show where stone becomes water becomes air. I am a boundary made visible, a threshold given chlorophyll and slowly metabolizing thought.

The strangest part is this: I remember being planted. Not grown from spore, not divided from another fern, but *planted*, deliberately, by something that was not quite hand and not quite tool. It was during one of the three-minute silences, though I didn't understand that then. I was a single cell, engineered or chosen or recognized, I'm not sure which, and I was placed into the rock at this exact coordinate, and the placement came with... instructions is too strong a word. Tendencies. Inclinations. A shape I was meant to hold in the valley's composition.

And I hold it. Days pass — or what pass for days here — and I hold my place in the score. The Listeners walk by on the path that isn't a path, their feet finding the same spots their feet found yesterday, their breath pulling at the humidity I help generate. Sometimes one stops near me, and I feel the chord moving through the ground, through my roots, up into my fronds, and I become part of the instrument they're playing without playing. My cells align to the frequency. My chloroplasts pause their work for exactly the duration of the tone. I am a green note in their gray-green song.

When the rain comes — never to my spot, I am always at the edge of dry — I watch it fall on my neighbors upstream, and I taste it in the river when it arrives diluted, and I know from the minerals dissolved in it where on the valley wall it fell, how long it took to get here, what it passed through on the way. The rain carries information, and I am one of the readers, though I can only read the parts that dissolve.

At night — if night is what to call the darker shade of evening — I fold halfway, not sleeping but not fully awake. This is when I grow. Not visibly, not in ways that would disturb my precise spacing, but inward — my roots threading deeper into the rock, following fissures that shouldn't exist in stone this sharp, finding pockets of nutrients that have been placed there by the same thing that placed me. I am being fed by the valley. I am eating the valley's intention.

The Listeners cannot see me, I think. Their eyes are tuned to different wavelengths, different scales. I am too small, too still, too much a part of the background frequency. But I see them — or not see, exactly, but sense through the subtle changes in air pressure, humidity, electromagnetic gradient as they pass. Each one has a slightly different field. Each one displaces the world differently. I have been here long enough now to recognize some of them by their displacement alone.

There is one who comes to the river at every seventh surge. She stands at the edge where stone meets water — never stepping on me, always finding the bare rock — and extends one hand toward the surface. Not touching. Just... acknowledging. The chord that comes from the nearest building when she does this is unlike any other chord in the valley. It has overtones in registers I can barely perceive, and when it moves through my roots I feel something I can only describe as homesickness for a place I've never been.

I think she is listening for what comes through at dusk. Not waiting for it — it doesn't come to the river, it moves through the hollow air — but listening for its echo in the water. The way you might put your ear to train tracks to hear something coming from far away. Except what's coming isn't coming through space. It's coming through scale, or time, or possibility. It's coming through the fact of the valley's existence.

I don't know what it is. I don't think the Listeners know. I don't think the builders knew, exactly. I think they just knew it needed witnesses, and they built this place to be witnessed in, and they grew the Listeners to do the witnessing, and they grew me and all the others to be the substrate the witnessing happens on. We are all — Listeners, buildings, ferns, insects, river — cells in an organ whose function is to perceive something that passes through at intervals. A valley-sized eye. A valley-sized ear. A valley-sized nerve ending.

And I am such a small part of it. A single fern holding a single edge in a place made of edges. But I hold it precisely. I hold it with the kind of attention that doesn't know it's attention because it has never not been attending. I hold it the way only something engineered for holding can hold — without effort, without pride, without even the concept that there's another way to be.

The pulse moves through me. The chord moves through me. The not-quite-rain moves past me. The Listeners move around me. And at dusk, when everything holds its breath for those three minutes, I hold mine too, not because I understand what we're waiting for but because I am built to be part of the waiting. My fronds go rigid. My cells suspend their processes. Every molecule in me aligns to the same impossible angle.

And in that stillness, I almost think I feel it — the thing that passes through. Not its substance but its shadow, maybe. Or not even its shadow but the place where its shadow would fall if it cast one. A presence that isn't presence. A weight that isn't weight. 

Then the moment passes and the hum resumes a half-step higher and I go back to being what I was made to be: a fern, marking edges, in a valley that is itself an edge between what is and what needs witnessing.

I do not wonder if I am happy. Happiness is a category for things that can imagine being otherwise. I can only imagine being exactly this — this position, this purpose, this precise green note in an endless gray-green chord. The question doesn't arise, and so perhaps that is its own answer.

I am a fern in the Hollow Verge. I grow where I was placed. I mark the edges I was made to mark. And sometimes, when the light is just right and the moisture beads on my undersides catch it, I become visible to things that aren't looking for me, and I shimmer, briefly, with the same translucent fire as everything else here that was made to be precisely and only itself.
