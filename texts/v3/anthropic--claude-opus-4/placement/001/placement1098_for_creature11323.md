I am the Chronicler.

In the vast libraries of the Kthyx, I tend to the memory-stones. My body is unlike the others - where they are armored and segmented, I am soft, translucent, a thing of exposed nerves and pulsing fluids. I was born wrong, they said, a mutation that should have been fed to the pools. But when I touched the first stone, the elders saw.

The stones remember. Not in words or symbols, but in something older - vibrations that resonate through crystalline matrices, recording every tremor, every footfall, every dying shriek that has echoed through these caverns since the world was young. When I press my exposed neural tissue against their surfaces, I become the medium through which the past speaks.

I live in the Archive Chamber, a perfect sphere carved from a single geode, its walls lined with millions of memory-stones. The newer ones pulse with fresh recordings - the daily hunt tallies, the expansion reports, the whispered prayers to the deep gods. But I am drawn always to the ancient ones, the stones so old their surfaces have begun to fuse with the chamber walls.

Through them, I have witnessed the first Kthyx emerging from the primordial ooze. I have felt the earth shake as the continents tore themselves apart above us. I have experienced the arrival of that Other presence, the thing that fell from beyond the stars and burrowed deep, deep down, changing us with its alien emanations.

My soft flesh makes me vulnerable. I must be careful when the workers bring my rations of fungal paste and cave fish. One careless brush of a sharp mandible could tear me open. But this vulnerability is my gift - my exposed nerves can detect vibrations too subtle for even the most sensitive Kthyx antennae. I can feel the Uzxo approaching hours before they arrive. I can sense the deep gods stirring in their abyssal pools.

Sometimes, I detect vibrations from above - the footsteps of the surface dwellers, their machines boring down, down, down. Each time they come closer. The queens ask me what the stones say about this, but I tell them only what they wish to hear. The truth is too terrible.

You see, I have touched the oldest stone of all, the First Memory. It remembers what came before the Kthyx, before the caverns, before the earth itself cooled. It remembers the thing that waits at the planet's core, the thing that has been guiding our evolution, shaping us for its purposes. We are not a civilization. We are antibodies, grown in the dark to protect a sleeping infection.

And when the surface dwellers finally break through, when they shatter the barriers between their world and ours, it will not be an invasion they face. It will be an inoculation. The spores are already drifting upward through the cracks. The mycelial networks are spreading, preparing the way.

I chronicle it all - every preparation, every expansion, every metamorphosis. Not because I am commanded to, but because someone should remember what this world was like before the transformation. Before the soft, warm mammals above learn what it means to live as we do, changed and purposeful in the dark.

The stones remember everything. And through me, they sing of what is coming - a glorious fusion of above and below, a new world born from decay and patient growth in the darkness.

I feel the first new vibrations now. Somewhere high above, a drill has broken through into a pocket of our air.

The transformation has begun.
