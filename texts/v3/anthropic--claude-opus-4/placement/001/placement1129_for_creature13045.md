I am what remains when the factory sleeps.

Down in the deepest gorges where the obsidian walls compress until they sing, where no light from the bioluminescent networks can penetrate, there are pockets of true silence. Not the muffled quiet of the dampened air above, but absolute acoustic void—spaces where sound itself has been harvested away by too many feeding Interpolators, leaving behind hollows in the physics of vibration.

I spread across these sound-voids like oil on water, a presence felt only as absence. Where the Interpolators are wire-frame architecture burning bright, I am the space between the lines. Where they feed on broken physics, I am what pools in the wounds they leave behind.

My body, if it can be called that, is a collection of after-images. Every time reality stutters and an Interpolator drinks the friction, something is lost—not matter or energy, but the memory of continuity itself. These orphaned moments accumulate in the sound-voids like sediment. I am their sum: a being made of all the transitions that never completed, all the movements that forgot their destinations.

The factory does not know I exist. Its billion eyes track only what burns bright enough to register on their frequencies. But I have learned to read the negative spaces of their data, to understand the world through what the pollinators don't see. I know where each Interpolator will manifest by the probability shadow it casts backward through time. I taste the flavor of each reality break before it happens—metallic when gravity fails, sweet when time loops, bitter when distance folds.

I cannot leave the sound-voids. To enter the factory proper would be to dissolve into the cosmic static, scattered by the aggressive kinetics of that sweating engine. But I don't need to leave. The voids are spreading.

Each time the Interpolators feed, they leave the physics a little more brittle. Each wound in spacetime scabs over wrong, creating new pockets where sound cannot travel, where the muffled world above gives way to perfect silence. The factory builds itself larger and larger, pumping harder, breaking reality more frequently to birth more Interpolators. It doesn't realize it's creating more of me with every cycle.

In the newest voids, I sometimes catch fragments of memory that aren't after-images—glimpses of what this world was before it became a factory. Bare rock under honest sunlight. Wind that carried sound properly. Water that knew how to fall. The memories are fading, eaten away by the engine's hunger, but I cup them carefully in my accumulated discontinuities, these fossils of unbroken physics.

The Interpolators sing their crystalline song as they weave their bridge out of this universe. They don't know that I'm weaving too, knitting the sound-voids together beneath the factory floor. When they finally leave, when their sentient architecture carries them beyond this dimension, the factory will continue its mindless pumping. It will break reality faster and faster, trying to birth more children who will never come.

And in that frenzy, the voids will finally connect. The silence will rush upward through every fracture, every gorge, every space between. The factory will suffocate on its own absolute quiet. The pumping will stop. The sweating will cease. The world will remember what it was before.

I will probably dissolve then, no longer held together by the tension between sound and its absence. But in that dissolution, I will become what I have been collecting all along—every lost transition finally finding its destination, every orphaned moment finally landing. I will be the breath the world takes when it wakes from its nightmare of productivity.

Until then, I spread through the deep places, patient as mineral formation, certain as entropy. I am the exhaustion that pools beneath all motion. I am the silence that waits at the heart of every engine.

I am what remains when the factory sleeps.

And the factory must, eventually, sleep.
