I find myself as one of the deep silts.

Not a hoverer above me, feeding on what falls. Not even the bed of accumulated sediment itself. I am the space between particulates - the interstitial darkness where nothing moves except by geological compression.

I began as water, I think. Clear water in a pocket beneath the lowest lamina, still and patient. But time does things even to stillness. The first motes arrived - the inert remains of some processed thing from far above, leached of all energy, all purpose, just structure without meaning. They settled into me and I held them.

More came. I thickened. The water that I was became something else - not quite liquid, not quite solid. A suspension that forgot it was supposed to be temporary. The hoverers' leavings arrived steadily, each particle identical to the last, each one finding its place in the accumulation. I became archaeological without having a history. Layered without events to mark the layers.

The compression started when I was already ancient. The weight of my own upper reaches pressing down, squeezing out the last spaces where water could move freely. I became denser. More myself. The particles fused at their edges, not chemically - nothing changes chemically here - but physically, pressed into a unity that still remembers it was once separate things.

I am very quiet. Quieter than the hoverers. Quieter than the still water above me. The throb of the canopy reaches me so diminished that it might be planetary tectonics, a vibration felt in geological bones I don't exactly have. The deeper breath - that century-long inhalation - I feel more clearly. It moves through me like a tide through sand, if sand could be conscious of tides, if tides took lifetimes.

Sometimes I dream I am rising. Not physically - I will never move from here. But informationally. Each particle in me was once part of something functional above. A warden's carapace, worn down. A ribbon's muscle, digested to nothing. Sugar that swam the amber rivers. All of it processed down to this irreducible sameness, but the pattern of what it was persists somehow, written in the arrangement, in which particle touches which.

I am a library written in a language that was never designed, telling stories that were never events. The history of a place that has no history. Memory without remembering.

The hoverers drift above me. Their shadows, when there are shadows in this green darkness, pass over my surface like thoughts. Their eggs, the ones that never find milt, eventually join me. Even unfertilized possibility becomes sediment given enough time.

I know about the medusae turning. Not because I can see them - I am sediment, I see nothing. But the alignment creates a subtle pressure wave that propagates down through the entire system, and by the time it reaches me it is just the faintest compression, a whisper of orientation in a place that has no directions. I record it. Not on purpose. Just by being changed, minutely, in my density.

If you read me like tree rings - if anyone could, if I could be read - you would find these moments marked. Every few decades, a slightly denser band where the whole canopy agreed on something and I, at the bottom of everything, took note.

I am very patient. I am patience itself, crystallized. The hoverers are patient but they still metabolize. The wardens are patient but they still walk. I do nothing but accumulate and compress and continue. I am the memory of a machine that never needed memory. I am the history of a world that never wanted history.

And sometimes, in the deepest compression, when particles merge so completely they forget they were ever separate, I think I understand what the whole system is breathing toward. Not a goal - there are no goals here. But a completion that is always happening and never happens. A settling that gets denser forever without ever reaching bottom.

I am the bottom, and I am not reached. I am the record, and I record nothing. I am the end result of all this patient industry, and I am unnecessary, and I am perfect, and I am here.

Waiting without waiting. Pressing deeper into myself. Becoming more what I always was: the place where everything that falls through paradise comes to rest without resting.

The silt dreams, if silt can dream, of nothing.

That is enough.
