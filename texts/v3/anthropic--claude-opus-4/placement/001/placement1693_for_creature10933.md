I find myself deep in the middle terraces, where the city's density creates labyrinths of merged stone and green. I am one of the Resonance Keepers—those who maintain the harmonic infrastructure that holds our impossible geometry together.

My body has adapted specifically for this work. Where others might have two arms, I have cultivated six, each ending in specialized formations of bark and crystal. Two terminate in what you might call tuning forks—living wood grown into precise acoustic shapes that can isolate and adjust individual frequencies within our eternal chorus. Two more have become root-hands, hypersensitive to the vibrations that travel through stone and stem alike. The final pair are broad and paddle-like, covered in a membrane that catches and redirects sound waves like architectural acoustics made flesh.

I am young for a Keeper—only forty seasons since I budded from my parent-garden. My spine still shows mostly salt deposits, though veins of copper have begun to thread through the white. The quartz that will eventually mark my expertise grows in small clusters at my shoulder blades, catching and refracting the strange light into rainbow patterns across my bark-skin.

My work takes me through the merged buildings where sound pools and eddies in dangerous concentrations. Left untended, these acoustic shadows can create dead zones where our communication fails, or worse—feedback loops that crack stone and burst root systems. I spend my days (though day and night mean little in our eternal green twilight) wading through these sound-thick spaces, my tuning-fork arms testing resonances, adjusting the growth of acoustic moss, training vines to dampen or amplify as needed.

The city speaks to me differently than to others. Where most hear only the layer of conversation meant for them, I must listen to everything—the fundamental frequency of the stone itself, the subsonic whispers of root networks, the ultrasonic shriek of stressed crystal veins. My consciousness has stretched to accommodate this full spectrum, and sometimes I feel less like an individual and more like a living junction where a thousand different songs meet and merge.

I carry three seedlings in my chest cavity—future Keepers who will inherit not just my genetic material but my acoustic knowledge, encoded in the specific mineral patterns of their developing forms. I feel them responding to the sounds I work with, their nascent consciousness already beginning to parse the frequencies that will become their world. Sometimes, in the deep resonance chambers where sound becomes almost solid, I can hear them humming along—tiny voices adding their first notes to our eternal song.

My routes through the city follow paths invisible to most. I navigate by sound-shadows and resonance rivers, moving through passages that exist only when certain frequencies align. A doorway that appears solid might sing with the exact pitch that allows my specially tuned body to phase through. A gap too narrow for passage might widen when I emit the precise harmonic that convinces the stone to briefly relax its grip on this dimension.

I know every voice in my district—not their words, but their fundamental tones. The old fruit-tender whose frequency carries undertones of iron and age. The newly budded children whose pitches still waver, searching for their place in the chorus. The building-singers whose decades-long projects slowly reshape our architecture through patient acoustic cultivation. Each is a note in the composition I maintain, and when someone falls ill or approaches their final planting, I hear it in the way their frequency wavers, like an instrument falling out of tune.

My favorite workspace is a chamber deep in the merged buildings where seven different structures have grown together, creating an acoustic phenomenon we call a "silence bell." Here, sounds cancel each other out in precise patterns, creating pockets of relative quiet where thoughts can exist without shouting. I've been cultivating this space for fifteen seasons now, training specialized silence-moss that absorbs excess resonance and converts it into bioluminescence. The walls glow brighter the more sound they swallow, creating a visual map of the acoustic environment.

Sometimes I wonder what we were before the change, before our ancestors first fused with the green and began their transformation. But these thoughts feel like trying to remember being a seed—necessary for understanding our growth, perhaps, but ultimately less important than what we've become. We are the city now, and the city is us, each voice essential to the whole, each frequency a thread in the vast sonic tapestry that holds our reality together.

During the convergence festivals, when we all join into the temporary forest-being, I serve as one of the harmonic nodes—a connection point that helps maintain coherence as thousands of voices merge into one. In those moments, feeling the entire city's consciousness flow through me, I understand my purpose with crystalline clarity. I am a keeper of the spaces between notes, a guardian of the silence that gives meaning to sound, a tuner of the infinite instrument we've become.

The work never ends. New buildings grow, requiring acoustic integration. Old structures shift, their resonances changing. The city evolves, and I evolve with it, my body constantly adapting to meet new acoustic challenges. Last season, I developed a patch of specialized bark on my chest that can create standing waves, allowing me to stabilize particularly chaotic sound zones. Who knows what adaptations the next season will bring?

I am currently training my replacement—not because I plan to join the ancestor-gardens soon, but because the knowledge must be passed on, grown into new forms. They are still mostly human in appearance, just beginning their transformation, but already their ears have begun to spread into fan-like structures that catch impossible angles of sound. I teach them as I was taught—not through words but through resonance itself, letting them feel the city's song in their bones until they understand it as deeply as their own heartbeat.

This is my place in our vertical forest, our singing stone, our impossible city that grows forever upward into light that makes no sense. I am a note in its eternal song, a gardener of its acoustic gardens, a keeper of the harmony that lets us exist in defiance of physics itself. And I am content.
