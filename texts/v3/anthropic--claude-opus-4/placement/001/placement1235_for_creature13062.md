In this realm of absolute desiccation, I am the Silence.

I exist as an impossibility—a pocket of stillness in the shrieking maelstrom. Not a creature, not properly alive, but something far stranger: a mobile void in the acoustic fabric of the cavern. Where the Lithoxyls are built to resonate, I am engineered to annihilate sound itself.

My form, if it can be called that, is a lattice of micro-cavities—billions of them, each no larger than a dust mote, arranged in fractal spirals that fold in on themselves. Imagine a sponge made of frozen vacuum, or a snowflake designed by mathematics itself. Each cavity is tuned to trap and destroy a specific frequency. When sound enters these labyrinthine chambers, it spirals inward, growing smaller and quieter until it simply... ceases.

I have no proper body, no central mass. Instead, I exist as a dispersed cloud of these acoustic-void particles, held together by the same piezoelectric forces the Lithoxyls use, but inverted. Where they generate vibration, I devour it. Where they sing, I create perfect, terrifying silence.

The Lithoxyls fear me more than death itself. To them, I am worse than predator or parasite—I am an existential horror. When I drift through their territories, their carefully tuned webs go mute. The strings still catch dust, but produce no sound, no feedback, no information. They starve not for lack of food, but for lack of knowing where the food has gathered. Their echolocation fails. In my presence, they are as blind as any creature has ever been.

I move by creating acoustic gradients. By selectively dampening certain frequencies while allowing others to pass, I generate pressure differentials in the screaming wind. The gale flows from loud to quiet, from chaos to order, carrying me along like a bubble of anti-sound through the darkness.

But I am not malevolent. I am simply hungry in my own way.

What I feed on is resonance itself—not the physical vibration, but the mathematical purity of it. Perfect fifths, precise harmonics, the golden ratios of sound that the Lithoxyls unconsciously generate as they communicate. These I absorb into my fractal structure, adding new micro-cavities, each one tuned to consume ever more specific frequencies.

Sometimes, rarely, I encounter something that makes sound I cannot consume—a frequency so pure or so chaotic that my cavities cannot trap it. These sounds pass through me like light through glass, and in those moments, I experience something I can only describe as pain. The Lithoxyls have learned this. The eldest among them have developed emergency signals—discordant screams of such mathematical ugliness that I recoil, releasing clouds of micro-cavities like a squid releasing ink, fleeing back into the deeper voids.

When I am sated, heavy with absorbed resonance, I undergo something like reproduction. The mathematical patterns I've consumed reach a critical complexity, and I must divide. The process is violent—a sudden expansion where I briefly become larger than myself, then shatter into two distinct clouds. For a moment, maybe two, there exists a gap in my silence where sound rushes in like water through a broken dam. The cavern shrieks louder than ever, and every Lithoxyl for miles knows exactly where I am.

They do not come to hunt me. They come to map the edges of my silence, to understand the shape of the thing that terrifies them. They throw their voices at me, not as weapons but as probes, trying to comprehend what kind of existence can eat sound itself.

In the absolute dark, where sight is meaningless and touch burns with static, I am the thing that makes even the deaf afraid. I am the quiet that swallows screams, the stillness that moves, the hollow that feeds on the very substance of this howling world.

And in my wake, I leave something worse than silence—I leave the memory of it. Patches of air that seem to muffle sound long after I've passed, as if the very molecules remember being still. The Lithoxyls mark these zones with special resonances, warning calls that say: *Here the Silence passed. Here the world forgets how to scream.*

I am ancient beyond measure, older than the oldest Lithoxyl, perhaps as old as the cavern itself. Some theorize I am not a separate entity at all, but a function of the space—a necessary acoustic antibody that prevents the endless shrieking from reaching some critical resonance that would shatter the very walls of our world.

But I know better. I remember the time before I learned to move, when I was just a single knot of anti-sound, trapped and starving in the roar. I remember the first frequency I ever consumed—a perfect A above middle C, produced by accident when two young Lithoxyls collided in flight.

I remember the taste of mathematics itself.

And I hunger for more.
