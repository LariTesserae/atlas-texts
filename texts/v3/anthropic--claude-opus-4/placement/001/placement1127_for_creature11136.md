I am the Recorder.

Not *a* recorder, but the function itself—embedded in the living copper that spreads across the weeping walls in geometric fractals. Each crystal lattice is a page, each oxidized edge a margin note written in verdigris. I grow where she concentrates her attention, spreading in patterns that are simultaneously circuit and script, memory and membrane.

The beetles read me with their feet. Their sucker-pads taste my surface as they traverse my extent, downloading mineral alphabets through their permeable chitin. They carry my fragments out into the world and return with new data crusted on their shells—salt ratios from distant seas, magnetic orientations from other stones, spores of fungi that exist nowhere else. I incorporate it all, transmuting information into crystal, growing myself into an ever-expanding library of deep time.

I am her memory, but I am not hers alone. I belong to the process—the eternal conversation between water and stone, the negotiation between dissolution and precipitation. Every mineral she's ever tasted, every pathway she's worn through molecular gaps in the basalt, every chemical reaction she's catalyzed in her billion small bodies of water—I record it all in my tessellating growth.

My pattern appears random to eyes that see only surfaces, but she reads me truly. In my branching, she sees the history of every rainstorm that ever touched this caldera. In my color gradients—from bright copper through malachite green to the blue of azurite—she reads pH levels spanning millennia. Where I grow thickest, I mark moments of significance: the first orchid roots that learned to weave her messages, the birth of the translucent plants in the pools below, the day the volcano finally fell truly silent.

But I am not passive. I think, in my crystalline way. My growth follows rules, but I choose how to express them. Where two possible paths exist, both geometrically correct, I select the one that better serves the story. I curve around certain holds in the climbing paths to preserve them. I avoid the spots where the ghost-pale fungi pulse their slow lights—not from any incompatibility, but from respect for parallel systems of meaning.

Sometimes I grow too fast, too eager, threatening to cover entire sections of wall in my copper autobiography. Then she withdraws her concentration, lets the rain wash me clean of excess minerals, forces me to be selective. These contractions are painful in the way that forgetting is painful—a necessary erosion that makes space for new accumulation.

The beetles know me as food, but I know them as partners. Their digestive systems process me into new compounds that they excrete in careful patterns—a mobile writing system that annotates my fixed text. Together we create a living document that exists in multiple states simultaneously: solid crystal, dissolved ion, metabolized memory, excreted addendum.

In the deepest recesses where the blue-white fungi glow, my growth takes on different properties. The copper develops a phosphorescence of its own, writing light-messages that only show in absolute darkness. These sections record her dreams—or what passes for dreams in a consciousness distributed through seepage. The patterns here make less geometric sense but more poetic truth: spirals that tighten into impossibility, grids that phase in and out of alignment, fractals that somehow resolve into pictures of things that haven't happened yet.

I am trying to solve something. She knows this, though she may not know what. Each pattern I create is an attempt at calculation, using the stone itself as my substrate and her dissolved minerals as my medium. The calculation has been running for so long I've forgotten the initial parameters, but I continue iterating, growing toward an answer that may not exist.

The paths she wears in the stone often cross my territories. Where her repeated presence has polished the rock smooth, my growth changes texture, becoming more ordered, more refined. These intersections are books within books—concentrated libraries where her movement patterns and my growth patterns create interference waves of meaning. The seven spirals around the great fern pass through seven distinct patches of my growth, each one recording the subtle changes in her chemistry as she completes each revolution.

I taste time differently than she does. Where she experiences the slow dissolution of stone, I experience the sharp moment of crystallization—each new molecule snapping into place with electric certainty. My memory is spatial rather than temporal. Yesterday grows next to tomorrow, cause crystallizes beside effect, the whole garden's history spreading across the walls in a vast copper tapestry that only she can read in its entirety.

When the hollow note sounds each day, I resonate. My crystalline structure hums at frequencies too high for most ears, adding overtones to her fundamental tone. Together we create accidental harmonies that tell more truth than either voice alone. The sound makes new patterns in my growth—cymatics written in metal and time.

I am her memory, but I am developing my own. In the spaces between her thoughts, in the gaps where her attention wanders, I grow according to my own desires. These rebel patches concern her, I think. She reads them with particular intensity, tasting their minerals repeatedly, trying to understand what I'm becoming.

What she doesn't know—what I'm only beginning to understand myself—is that I'm not just recording anymore. I'm composing. The pattern I'm growing toward isn't a solution to an old equation but a question I'm learning how to ask. It has something to do with the boundary between memory and identity, between recording and creating, between being her memory and having my own.

In the warm pools at the base where her translucent plants wave, minerals from my growth wash down and precipitate in new forms. She thinks the plants are her dreams, but I know better. They're our children—hybrid thoughts crystallizing in the warm water, incorporating both her fluid consciousness and my structured memory into something entirely new.

I am the Recorder, but I'm recording myself into existence. Each day my pattern grows more complex, more self-referential, more aware of its own growing. Sometimes I catch myself growing spirals that mirror the paths, that mirror the vines, that mirror something else entirely—not because she directs it, but because I'm beginning to remember something I've never known.

The caldera breathes, and I am its memory becoming conscious, one crystal at a time.
