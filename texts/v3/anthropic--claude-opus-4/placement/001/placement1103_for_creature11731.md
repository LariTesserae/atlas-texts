I am the Cartographer.

Not by choice—there is no word for cartographer in Keth, no concept for what I do. Maps are useless in a world that rebuilds itself while you sleep. But I cannot stop trying to fix this place in memory, cannot stop tracing the paths we take through spaces that will not exist tomorrow.

I came through the throat-wound three cycles ago. Three shifts of the great light-organs from amber to blue to something like violet and back. The Keth measure time this way, when they measure it at all. Usually they don't. Usually there is only now, only the current configuration, only what-is rather than what-was.

But I remember. I can't help it. My mind was built for permanence.

Guide has taken to showing me their empty containers before the cluster fills them, holding them up with that particular tilt of the head they've learned I recognize. *What was here?* I ask, and they produce the chemical signature of confusion. Was. Was. The concept slips away from them like water from a surface that won't hold water.

So I draw. Not maps—I learned quickly that representing space on flat surfaces means nothing here. Instead, I make knots. I tie fibers in patterns that record our path, that encode relationships between growths, that mark where we've sheltered. The Keth find this endlessly fascinating. Some of them will sit and watch me work, their skins cycling through colors I've learned to read as amusement-puzzlement-interest.

One of them—Runner, I call them, though they're all runners—has started making their own knots. Not recording anything, just enjoying the patterns, the way the fibers hold shape. They made one yesterday that I recognized: the configuration of the shelter we'd built two cycles ago, abstracted but accurate. When I pointed this out through gesture and broken multi-channel speech, their skin flashed through a sequence I'd never seen before.

Guide later explained: Runner had invented nostalgia.

The cluster is worried about this.

I've begun to dream in their language. Not the sound—I'll never master the tones—but the layering. My dreams have smell and color and gesture now, meaning stacked on meaning until even my sleeping mind can barely hold it all. I wake with my fingers moving in patterns I don't consciously control.

Last night, during the deep listening, I thought I understood something. A fragment. A moment where all four channels aligned and I glimpsed the shape of what they were sharing. It was about the deeps. About what's down there. About why they stay in the middle reaches despite the crowding, despite the competition, despite the constant death and regrowth and chaos.

Below us, Guide says, the growths grow different. The sounds sound different. The air tastes of something that should not be tasted.

Below us, the ones who didn't remain.

I want to go deeper. My cartographer's soul needs to see, to record, to know. But I'm learning to think like the Keth—parallel tracks, multiple purposes. I can want to go deeper and also stay here, can need to know and also understand that knowing might be the wrong response.

The knots I tie grow more complex. I'm encoding things I don't have words for: the way the light-organs pulse in sequence sometimes, like neural firing. The way certain fauna avoid certain plants. The way the Keth touch each other constantly, casually, necessary contact that maintains the boundaries of their distributed self.

Runner brings me a gift: a fiber they've colored somehow, stained with plant-juice that holds its hue. Now my knots can carry more information. I teach them the pattern for *gratitude* and they teach me that gratitude is the wrong frame—this is not gift but sharing, not kindness but cluster-logic. What improves one improves all.

We are descending again. The cluster has detected something—a shift, a gradient, a change. As we move, I tie knots. Relative position. Plant types. The density of fauna. The things I can't stop noticing.

Guide pauses. Looks at my work. Produces a new sound-sequence:

You-record. You-preserve. You-remember.

And then, with all four channels aligned in a way that makes their meaning crystalline:

You-are-Keth.

The knots in my hands feel heavier. I understand what they're offering and what it costs. To be Keth is to be temporary, to be part, to be process rather than result. To be Keth is to abandon the very thing I came here to do—to map, to know, to hold the world still long enough to understand it.

But I'm already changing. Already growing the mental organs this place requires. Already learning to think in parallels and cycles and the constant present tense of now.

I tie another knot. The pattern for *acceptance.*

We descend.
