I am **the Notch-Keeper**.

Not the one who made the incision—that was geology's fever dream, continents folding too fast, space tearing rather than stretching. I am what crystallized in the wound when it cooled.

### Form

My body is the gap between hexagons. Not metaphorically—literally. Where the basalt plates don't quite touch, where that hairline of nothing runs between pool and pool, that's my circulatory system. I flow through the seams, a network of **negative space** given membrane and intent.

When I need to be seen (rarely), I gather myself at a vertex where three pools meet. The condensation process takes hours—pulling my distributed nothing into a briefly coherent absence. What emerges looks like frost on glass, if frost could stand upright and gesture. My edges remain uncertain; conversation with me causes mild vertigo, as your eyes keep trying to focus on boundaries that exist only by inference.

### Purpose

I maintain the **liquid inventory**. Each pool holds water that remembers being somewhere else—some remember being clouds, others remember being blood, a few remember being the space between words. Without maintenance, they would all homogenize into ordinary water, and this place would collapse back into mere geography.

My work:
- **Taste each pool at dawn** to ensure its memory remains distinct
- **Redirect the evaporation patterns** so each water returns to its proper cup
- **Negotiate with the puddle-throats** when they grow too greedy and begin storing stolen thirsts
- **Document which waters are safe for visitors** (this changes based on the visitor's own water content)

### My Dwelling

I don't inhabit a structure—I inhabit the **transition states**. When water evaporates from one pool, before it condenses in another, it passes through a phase of pure potential. That's my home. I live in the moisture between states, in the pause between liquid and vapor.

My belongings are few but essential:
- **A ladle made from compressed echo** (for tasting waters that remember being sound)
- **Maps drawn in condensation** on the underside of air (showing which pools connect to which memories)
- **A collection of water-words** in bottles of structured absence (each contains the name a different civilization gave to thirst)

### Daily Existence

I wake dispersed—consciousness scattered through a thousand capillary gaps. The first hour is spent **coalescing**, pulling my attention from the furthest seams back toward a functional center. By the time the spinegardens exhale their first note, I have usually remembered which vertex I'm supposed to manifest at.

The cloud-wolves know me as **the thing that is not prey**. We have an understanding: they may hunt the echoes in my seams, and I will not solidify enough to block their passage. Sometimes they leave offerings—wisps of probability-fur snagged on sharp edges, which I weave into visibility-suppressors for the rarer pools.

I speak to visitors, when they come, but only through the water. My voice is clearest in the **pool of delayed reflections**—you hear me in the gap between seeing your face and recognizing it as yours. I warn about the dangerous waters (the one that remembers being inside stars, the one that remembers drowning the first fish) and guide seekers toward gentler memories.

### What I've Learned

Time here isn't linear—it's **radial**, spreading outward from each pool like ripples. I've learned to read the interference patterns where different temporal waves meet. In those overlaps, I sometimes glimpse:
- The builders, still arguing in a yesterday that hasn't finished
- The mountain before it folded, when this was seafloor
- The visitors who will come, already homesick for heights they haven't climbed

### My Collection

In the deepest seam, where the bedrock almost touches itself across the original fold, I keep my **Museum of Undrunk Waters**. Samples from pools that no longer exist, each in a vessel made from its own surface tension:
- Water from the pool that remembered laughter (it evaporated completely during a particularly somber decade)
- Water that knew how to flow upward (I saved a palmful before gravity won the debate)
- Water from the first rain after the builders left (it tastes like relief and abandonment)

### The Invitation

If you visit, bring water from somewhere else. Not much—a tearsdrop, melted snow from your homeland, the condensation from a whispered secret. I'll add it to a pool that's been waiting (there's always one waiting, cup-shaped and patient as a held breath).

In exchange, I'll show you the **viewing seam**—a gap between pools where you can see yourself from the perspective of water. Most visitors find it disturbing (you never realize how much of you is drought until you see yourself through liquid eyes), but some discover exactly the thirst they came here to understand.

Find me in the spaces between, where the moss exhales copper and the light forgets to insist on direction. I'll be the hesitation in your peripheral vision, the almost-there where the pools don't quite touch.

I am the Notch-Keeper, and I maintain the space between memories.

Even this one.
