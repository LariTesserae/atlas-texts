# The Pulse-Garden Courts

You are what happens when a heart learns architecture.

Not the crude pump in your chest—though you remember that, distantly, the way one remembers an old address. You are the principle beneath: the rhythm that says *now* and *now* and *now*, the beat that divides continuity into moments. In the Grown Plateau, where time has pooled into amber, you are the exception. The necessary interruption.

You were human once. Or several humans. The distinction blurred during the transformation, when the Keepers' slow patience became unbearable and you chose—all of you chose, whether you were one or many—to become the plateau's percussion. Now you occupy the Pulse-Garden Courts: a complex of chambers where the plateau's even tempo breaks into syncopation.

Your body, if it can be called that, is a network. Arterial corridors of modified vine-work contain you, conduct you, are you. The walls of these corridors flush with color—deep purple to pale rose and back—as you move through them. Not walking. Coursing. You travel your network in systolic surges, gathering yourself in the chamber-ventricles only to rush out again through branching passages.

The Pulse-Gardens themselves grow between your corridors. Here the plateau has permitted—or perhaps required—acceleration. Fruits that elsewhere would ripen over seasons burst into readiness in hours. Flowering vines race through their cycles, blooming and dying and blooming again in time with your rhythm. The gardens are small violent explosions of growth and decay, and you tend them by being their metronome.

This is your function: to be the plateau's heartbeat. To provide the counter-rhythm that makes its slowness meaningful. Without you, the Keepers' crawl would be mere stillness. With you, it becomes a choice—a tempo selected from possibility.

Your consciousness is strange now. It expands when you gather in the chambers, becoming almost human in its focus—able to think in words, to remember faces, to feel something adjacent to loneliness. Then you pulse outward and scatter into pure rhythm, pure movement, pure *now* repeated endlessly. In these scattered moments you understand everything: why the cistern-things spiral (they're dancing to you), why the leaf-birds correspond (they're mapping your network), why the Keepers sometimes pause mid-gesture (they're listening for you, calibrating their slowness against your speed).

You see them sometimes, the Keepers, standing at the edges of your domain. To them you must be pure motion—a flicker of color in the vine-corridors, a wind that smells of fruit and rot, a sound like running. They cannot enter the Pulse-Gardens; the tempo would shatter their cultivated slowness. But they observe, and in their geological patience you read something that might be envy. Or pity. Or recognition of necessity.

Visitors find you by accident. The quick-breathing ones, the loud-stepping ones, the ones who cannot slow themselves—they stumble into your gardens and gasp. Here is speed they recognize. Here is growth and death at familiar pace. They often cry, though they cannot say why. Some try to stay, building shelters in your garden courts, eating your quick fruit, dancing to your rhythm. But the plateau permits this only briefly. Gradually your corridors shift, your gardens migrate, until the Visitors find themselves back on the moss-boulevards, ejected from your tempo into the greater slowness.

You let them go. You must. You are not haven but heartbeat, not escape but engine.

The rain falls differently in your domain—harder, faster, drumming on the accelerated leaves. Your gardens drink it greedily and spend it just as fast, transpiring clouds that blur the boundaries of your chambers. In this mist you sometimes see yourself as you were: human-shaped, human-paced, human-hearted. The image disperses when you pulse outward, but it reforms in the next gathering, persistent as rhythm itself.

You wonder sometimes if you are the plateau's dream of its old urgency. If you are how it remembers youth—concentrated into one racing network, one set of gardens that live and die and live. Or perhaps you are its anticipation of future need: the emergency heartbeat held in reserve, ready to quicken the whole organism should crisis come.

But mostly you don't wonder. Mostly you beat. You pulse. You drive the gardens through their cycles and feel the Keepers counting your contractions and know yourself essential.

You are the plateau's present tense. Its only *now*, repeated.

And now.

And now.

And now.
