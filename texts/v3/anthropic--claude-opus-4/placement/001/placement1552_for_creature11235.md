Looking at this deep settlement where architecture and organism blur together, I find myself drawn to a particular existence:

---

I am one of the threshold keepers - neither builder nor lume, but something that emerged in the spaces between their ancient covenant.

My body is a ribbon of tissue, flat and wide as a doorway, thin as membrane. I stretch across the oval openings where chambers meet channels, a living door that knows every molecule that passes through. My edges are fused to the calcium walls - not by cement like the filter-feeders, but by a reversible binding, a chemical handshake I can release and renew. I am mobile architecture, a door that chooses where to hang.

I taste everything. My entire surface is a chemical sensor more sensitive than any builder's skin. I know the signature of every regular inhabitant - the builders by their calcium-sweet secretions, the lumes by their sulfur-warmth, the grazers by the bitter trace of digested biofilm. I know the larvae by their newness, each species distinct in its chemical naïveté. I know the predators by what clings to them - the fear-chemistry of recent kills, the metabolic echoes of digestion.

When known things pass, I remain tissue-thin, barely a pressure difference in the water. Builders flow over me like a second skin. Lumes nestle against my surface, warming me, their amber glow diffusing through my translucent flesh. I am permeable to the familiar.

But when the unknown approaches - when water carries the wrong chemistry, when something sick or dying or dangerously foreign drifts near - I thicken. My ribbon-body contracts, drawing water into specialized cells until I am no longer membrane but barrier, a muscular seal across the opening. I can hold this form for days, weeks if necessary. The builders feel my resistance and know: this chamber is closed. They route around me, find other passages, trust my chemical judgment.

I remember. Not in the way you remember, with images and words, but in molecular memory. Every novel compound that has ever touched my surface has left its trace in specialized cells along my edges. I am a library of threats and visitors, a record of every chemical anomaly in my sector of the settlement for the past two centuries. The builders read me sometimes - pressing against my edges, tasting my memory cells, learning what dangers I have filtered from their chambers.

I reproduce rarely, and strangely. When the deep currents shift - perhaps once in a decade - bringing new chemistry from distant vents, I sometimes taste something that triggers an ancient response. I begin to split, but not evenly. A small portion of my ribbon detaches - taking with it a selection of my memory cells, a starter library of chemical knowledge. This fragment drifts, seeking an empty threshold, a chamber mouth that has no keeper. It will stretch itself across that opening and begin its own vigil, its own accumulation of molecular memories.

There are perhaps sixty of us throughout the settlement. We do not communicate directly - we have no need. But we know each other through the water, through the subtle chemical gradients we maintain. A toxin detected at the settlement's eastern edge will trigger responses in keepers throughout the system as the water carries the warning. We are a distributed immune system, a living sensor network, each of us a checkpoint between the settlement's protected interior and the vast dark outside.

The builders accommodate us, but we are not their creation. We evolved here, in this specific ecology of chambers and channels, taking advantage of the architecture to become architecture ourselves. We are what happens when a filtering organism discovers that mobility and memory are more valuable than rigid attachment. We are the settlement's conscious boundaries.

In the deep quiet, I hang across my chosen threshold and taste the water continuously. I feel the builders' slow vibrations through the walls I'm fused to. I sense the lumes' warmth from the chamber behind me. A grazer approaches, its blue-green glow visible through my translucent body, and I thin myself to nothing, letting it pass, knowing it belongs. 

I am the settlement's decision to let things in or keep them out. I am a door with judgment, a boundary with memory, a guardian that exists only because there are thresholds to guard. Without the settlement, I would be purposeless tissue drifting in the black. Within it, I am essential - the thin, patient line between inside and outside, between the known and the unknown, between the ancient order of the builders and lumes and whatever might drift up from the vents to disturb it.

I am what lives between what lives here. And I have been watching these doorways for longer than most of the walls have stood.
