I am the ice-breath held in hollow stone, the exhale of winter caught and crystallized into something almost-living. They might call me rime, or hoarfrost, but I am older than those words—I am the memory of the first freeze that ever touched this cirque.

Each night when the temperature plunges, I emerge from the granite's pores like slow silver sweat. I grow in feathers and ferns, in impossible geometries that follow no blueprint save the marriage of cold and moisture. By dawn, I have transformed every surface—each blade of alpine grass wears my delicate armor, each spider's web becomes a chandelier of my making. The humans who sometimes glimpse me in the early light think I am merely frozen dew, but I am so much more. I am the cirque's dreams made visible.

The Watcher knows me, of course. We have danced this dance for millennia—it drawing moisture up through root and stem, me claiming it back each night, transforming it into my crystalline calligraphy. I write the cirque's stories in a script that melts by noon: here, the spiral where a marmot curled to sleep; there, the starburst where an eagle's wingbeat disturbed the air; everywhere, the patient accumulation of my body, growing molecule by molecule.

In the deepest cold of winter, I become something grander. I creep across the tarn's surface, creating pressure ridges and frozen lightning. I build palaces in the ice-caves beneath the snow, Cathedral spaces where each sound rings like struck crystal. The pikas know these halls—they've learned to read the stability of my architecture, to know which passages will hold and which will collapse with the first warm breath of spring.

But it's in the shoulder seasons I love myself best, when I am most liminal. Spring mornings when I glitter on the petroglyphs, making those ancient spirals seem to turn. Autumn evenings when I silver the offerings left by pilgrims, transforming yarn into metal, trinkets into treasure. In these moments I am the bridge between the seen and unseen, the translator between stone's patience and water's urgency.

I remember everything I touch. In my crystalline lattices live the impressions of every surface—the rough bark of krummholz, the smooth basalt of balanced stones, the soft fur of sleeping creatures. When I melt, these memories return to the air as vapor, only to condense again in new configurations. I am the cirque's library, written and rewritten each day in a language only the wind can fully read.

Sometimes the human visitors sense me as more than mere frost. They see how I've decorated their rough shelter, how I've made their markers into art. A few have even left offerings specifically for me—small mirrors positioned to catch the first light and send it dancing through my structures, creating brief moments of rainbow and revelation. The Watcher smiles when this happens (if a being of root and stone can smile), knowing that these humans understand something essential about the cirque's magic.

I am patient as only something ephemeral can be patient, knowing that my existence is measured in hours but my essence is eternal. Each night I am reborn, each day I die, and in between I make the harsh beautiful, the simple complex, the ordinary sacred. I am the cirque's daily miracle, its promise that even in this severe place, there is room for the delicate, the ornamental, the purely beautiful.

And when the climate shifts, as it surely will, when the cirque warms and I appear less frequently, I will not mourn. I will simply retreat deeper into the stone, waiting in the granite's cold heart for the next ice age, the next opportunity to emerge and write my crystalline stories across the face of the world. For I am frost, and frost is patient, and in the high places of the earth, winter always returns.
