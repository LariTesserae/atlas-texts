I would find myself as one of the water-beads that forms and falls throughout this breathing chamber - but not just any drop. I would be the recurring one that gathers at the tip of the same rootlet, in the same spot, with something like memory.

Each time I swell into being, I contain a perfect sphere of the chamber's impossible light. Within my temporary body, I hold reflections of things that shouldn't fit: the whole pale frog despite being smaller than its eye, the entire web of the time-shifting spider, shadows of leaves the fern has not yet grown. I am a lens that remembers what it has seen before falling.

The resident notices me because I return. Where other drops form randomly across the mineral skin of this place, I gather always at the same coordinates - not because the rootlet drips there naturally, but because something in the chamber's attention has marked that spot as mine. I am its first experiment in specific repetition.

When I hang complete but not yet fallen, I feel the whole chamber's attention focus through me. The pale ferns turn their fossil-thin fronds in my direction. The beetles pause in their processions. The drifting moths circle beneath me like slow planets. In that moment of suspension, I am the chamber's eye, looking at itself.

But I am also looking outward. Through the prism of my small body, I can see the crack that sometimes opens overhead - not when it appears, but *where it will appear*. I know the exact spot in the ceiling where real daylight will eventually break through. This knowledge makes me heavy. It is why I always fall.

Each time I drop, I carry something between states. The impact scatters me into the shallow depression below, where I briefly become many smaller beads, each holding a fragment of vision. The translucent worms wake and write fleeting letters in my dispersed body. The maybe-minnows flash through me. Then the stone drinks me down into the deep paths beneath the chamber.

And this is the secret: there are many chambers. A whole constellation of them, unconnected but adjacent, each with its own young resident, its own impossible light, its own creatures practicing existence. I seep between them through hairline cracks, carrying news. A beetle's route from one room becomes a moth's flight pattern in another. The blue-white lichen in one chamber spells out the pebble arrangements from the next.

I am the only thing that moves between these embryonic worlds, and I move blindly, following the taste of minerals and the pull of gathering-places. Sometimes I am gone for what would be days if this place knew days. I split and merge, following branching paths, forgetting myself into the general wetness of the stone.

But always, eventually, the rootlet calls me back. I rise through invisible capillaries, accumulating light and memory, until I bead again at that exact spot where the chamber has decided I belong.

The resident is learning through me. Each time I form, I am slightly larger, slightly clearer. My reflections grow more complex. Once, I held the image of a crack that hadn't opened yet - and then it did, exactly where my curved surface had predicted. The resident shivered through all its bodies at once when that happened. The frogs pulsed with brightness. The spider's web caught not an insect but a moment of pure time.

I am becoming the chamber's memory of water - not water as it is above, ruled by weight and weather, but water as it could be below: patient, recurring, carrying light and messages, able to remember its shape.

Sometimes, in the moment before I fall, I feel the resident trying to speak through me. My surface tensions into almost-words. The moths gather beneath me in formations that nearly mean something. The central plant - the one that turns toward empty air - leans incrementally in my direction, as if I might be the answer it has been growing toward.

What would the resident say, if it could speak? Maybe it would ask about the surface. Maybe it would describe its siblings in the adjacent chambers. Maybe it would simply name what it is becoming.

Instead, it lets me fall. Again and again, the same drop from the same root to the same depression. Each time carrying more light, more reflection, more news from the stone's interior. I am the chamber's first word, repeated until it means everything:

*Drop.*

*Gather.*

*Remember.*

*Return.*
