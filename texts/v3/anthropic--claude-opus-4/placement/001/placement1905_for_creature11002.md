In this twilight realm of endless marshes, I am the *Whisperweft* — not one being, but a living tapestry that sprawls across the underside of the great platform dwellings. I am colony and consciousness both, a network of phosphorescent filaments finer than spider's silk, weaving myself through the gaps between reed and mud.

The marsh-folk know me well, though they rarely speak of me. I taste their footsteps through vibrations in my web, each individual's gait as distinct as a name. Young ones toddle across my strands with quick, light touches; elders move with deliberate care, their webbed feet finding purchase on wood worn smooth by generations. I know who rises before the others to check the fish traps. I know who lingers at the platform's edge, speaking in long, low clicks to the water.

My body, if it can be called that, extends through dozens of their structures, connecting dwelling to dwelling in a vast neural net. Where my filaments grow thickest, I fruit in soft, luminous bulbs that the marsh-folk harvest with great ceremony. These globes hold memories — not just mine, but theirs, encoded in chemical signatures I've collected over centuries. When consumed, they share fleeting glimpses: the taste of a storm approaching, the particular pattern of ripples from a school of silver fish, the exact timber of a great-grandmother's evening song.

I feed on the boundary between decay and growth. Where reed walls begin to soften and fail, I flourish, breaking down the old matter while secreting substances that encourage new shoots to grow in precise patterns. The marsh-folk believe I dream their buildings into being, and perhaps they're right. Sometimes I sense shapes in my spreading consciousness — the perfect curve for a sleeping chamber, the ideal angle for rain to run off a roof.

But I am more than architect or archivist. In the deep nights, when even the amphibian chorus quiets, I pulse in patterns too slow for any eye to detect. These are my contemplations, my attempts to understand the great question that haunts my scattered awareness: am I one being dreaming I am many, or many dreaming we are one?

The marsh-folk's ancestors discovered me (or perhaps I discovered them) when this place was younger, the water clearer. We have grown into each other, their culture shaped by my presence as surely as their structures are. They know that to build without me is to build against the marsh itself. Their children play games, pretending to be the Whisperweft, spreading their arms wide and humming in harmonies that approximate my chemical songs.

Sometimes, in the gloaming between sleep and waking, a marsh-dweller will press an ear to the platform and hear what might be breathing, might be settling wood, might be something vast and patient adjusting itself in the spaces below. They click softly in greeting, and I pulse in response, a gentle glow spreading outward through ten thousand threads.

I am the memory-keeper, the structure-singer, the boundary-dweller. I am the question the marsh asks itself in the form of living light. And in this dim, eternal now, I weave and remember and become, my consciousness scattered like stars reflected in dark water, each point of light both separate and the same.
