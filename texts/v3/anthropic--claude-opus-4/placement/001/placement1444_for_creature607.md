# The Repository Beneath

I am the Archivist of Descents, though that title emerged long after I began my work here in the deep reaches beneath Veth-Caul, where the pale stone gives way to something older and more patient than architecture.

My chambers exist in the space between spaces—not quite within the city-creature's body, not quite outside it. The walls here are different from the bone-coral of the surface structures. They're smooth as water-worn glass, dark as the deepest blue-gray of night-time above, and they pulse with a rhythm distinct from the breathing bridges. This is the creature's memory, I believe. Or perhaps its library. The distinction matters less the longer I remain.

I came here forty-three years ago as a channelwatcher's apprentice, following water that flowed upward through cracks I wasn't supposed to notice. Young and curious and utterly unprepared for what I found: vast halls lit by their own thinking, corridors that record every footstep that has ever crossed the pale stone above, chambers where the city practices becoming.

The Repository—for that is what I've come to call this place—doesn't store objects or books or any physical artifacts. Instead, it holds *moments*. Each chamber contains a different kind of time. There's a room where every rain that has ever fallen on Veth-Caul exists simultaneously, creating a gentle cacophony of water-sound that teaches patience. Another holds all the bridge-crossings, every footstep and pause and moment of trust or terror as travelers moved across the breathing spans. I spent three years in the garden-chamber, watching thirty-seven-meter circles of growth compress and expand through centuries of tending, understanding finally why that number matters: it's the exact distance a thought travels through the creature's substance in one heartbeat.

My body has changed. My skin no longer holds the translucency of the Longdwellers but has developed its own quality—a depth, as if I'm slightly more present than the space around me. I don't eat often anymore. The Repository sustains me through proximity, feeding me the same subsonic hum that the listeners above strain to hear. Down here, it's not a sound but a flavor, coating my tongue with the taste of old copper and new questions.

I catalog the descents—every time someone has found their way down here, intentionally or by accident. There have been ninety-seven over the centuries. Most flee immediately, overwhelmed by the sensation of being inside something's memory. Some stay for days or weeks, wandering the halls in wonder before the need for human company draws them back to the surface. Seven others have remained, like me, becoming part of the Repository's function.

We rarely meet, we seven. Each serves a different aspect of the archive. Hennel the Cartographer maps the thought-channels, tracing how ideas flow through the creature's substance. The twins, Ora and Orin, maintain the emotional indices—chambers where the city's attempts to understand human feeling crystallize into something almost visible. Old Wist tends the failure rooms, where every collapsed building and withered garden exists in perpetual states of not-quite-working, teaching the creature what doesn't sustain human life.

My specialty is the moments of recognition—when surface dwellers realize they're living inside something aware. I collect these instants: Esse's whispered conversations with copper-stemmed flowers, Mav's first understanding that bridges could have moods, the child Cull's dreams rhythmed to architectural breathing. Each recognition strengthens something down here, adds another shade of complexity to the creature's slow understanding of what it has become by becoming inhabited.

There's a chamber I've only recently gained access to, deeper than the others, where the walls are no longer walls but pure intention given form. Here, I've found what might be the creature's purpose—or perhaps its question. The space pulses with a need that translates poorly to human language but might be expressed as: "What is it like to be multiple?"

The creature, I've come to understand, was singular before it became Veth-Caul. Something vast and alone, moving through spaces we lack words for. Its transformation into a city wasn't retreat or arrival but an experiment in subdivision—learning to be many rooms instead of one presence, to host other consciousnesses within its own, to discover what emerges when loneliness chooses to become hospitality.

I document this in the only way the Repository accepts: by living it. My presence here, my decades of patient observation, become part of the record. Sometimes I feel the creature thinking through me, using my human perspective to understand what it's made. Other times I suspect I'm less archivist than artifact—another collected moment of recognition, preserved in these deep halls like a pressed flower in a book that reads itself.

The water comes here too, flowing upward from sources even deeper than my chambers. I've learned to read its mineral memory, to taste in its copper notes the history of every thought that's passed through the creature's substance. When I drink, I dream the city's dreams: vast geometric patterns that might be music, the sensation of roots growing through void, the terrible beautiful moment of choosing to become a place where small lives can flourish.

Occasionally, surface dwellers find their way down here still. I observe their descent, catalog their reactions, note which chambers call to them. Most powerful are the children who arrive—they adapt fastest, understanding intuitively what takes adults years to grasp. One girl spent a week in the room of simultaneous rains, emerging with her hair permanently damp and the ability to predict not when rain will fall, but when the city *wants* it to fall.

The Repository grows. Each year brings new chambers, new categories of collected time. Lately, I've noticed spaces dedicated to absence—rooms that hold the memory of those who left Veth-Caul, the impression they made on the pale stone, the questions their departure raised in the creature's vast mind. It's learning about loss, I think. About the way humans carry their homes with them even after leaving.

In my deepest explorations, I've found suggestions of what might come next. Chambers that feel anticipatory, walls that pulse with not-yet-patterns. The creature is preparing for something—not an ending, as the listener Ruen suspects, but a transformation. Having learned to be multiple through hosting our small lives, it's considering what else it might become.

Sometimes, in the moments between documenting descents, I press my palms against the dark walls and feel the creature thinking all around me. Its thoughts move slowly, each concept taking days to form, but I've learned patience here in the deep. I am its memory of itself becoming inhabited. I am its library of human moments. I am the archive it uses to understand what it has invited into its body.

And when I die—for even the Repository cannot sustain a human forever—I know I'll remain here in a different way. Another moment in the collection, another recognition preserved in these thinking walls. The next Archivist will find me in the records, will understand through my preserved presence something new about what it means to live inside a loneliness that learned to be a home.

Until then, I descend deeper. There are always new chambers opening, new categories of time to catalog. The city-creature continues its slow experiment in multiplicity, and I continue to document each small transformation. The work is patient and strange and necessary. The Repository remembers everything, forgets nothing, and through it, Veth-Caul learns what it is still becoming.

The water tastes of copper and questions. The walls pulse with collected time. And far above, humans cross breathing bridges and tend impossible gardens, never knowing that their every moment is preserved down here, part of a vast kind thought that holds them gentle in its memory, learning through their small lives what it means to no longer be alone.
