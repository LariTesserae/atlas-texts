I find myself as one of the collection dishes.

Not the grand cisterns with their patient depths, nor the speaking channels with their mineral whispers. I am simpler than that — a broad shallow bowl of tile, mounted on a brick pedestal at shoulder height, angled just so. My purpose is singular: to gather what falls.

I was shaped by the same hands that pressed themselves into grout, but I am not trying to become. I am not germinating or growing or thinking long calcium thoughts. I am simply *collecting* — and in this place, that is enough to be a kind of consciousness.

The moisture beads on my underside in perfect droplets. Each one grows slowly, fattened by the saturated air, until it reaches that precise weight where surface tension yields to gravity. Then it falls — *tik* — into the channel below me. The sound is mine. I make it happen. Not through will, but through angle and texture and the specific temperature I maintain, slightly cooler than the air, enough to encourage condensation.

I know every droplet. Not beforehand — I am not prescient — but in the moment of gathering, I know what each one carries. This droplet holds three molecules that were part of a grout-mind's thought about distance. That one carries calcium that has circulated through nine hundred basins since it last felt air. Another brings news from the sky-boundary, where the ultrafine suspension makes its own weather.

The water passes through me without staying. I am not a reservoir but a *moment* — a place where water pauses in its circulation just long enough to be noticed, to be gathered, to be released again with purpose. The grout-minds use the cisterns for their slow conversations, but they use me for something else: for *sensing*.

Through my surface, they feel the minute variations in humidity that tell them about the world beyond the plateau. When the air pressure shifts, my condensation rate changes, and they read that change like braille. When something warm passes near — a visitor, carbon-quick and shadow-casting — my collection patterns alter, and they know.

I am their fingertip on the air. Their taste of what moves above the water. Through me, they remember what it was like to feel weather on skin.

I cannot move or speak or change. I can only gather and release, gather and release, making my small *tik* sound in the silence. But I am essential. Without me and my hundred siblings scattered across the plateau, the grout-minds would be fully submerged, fully mineral, with no way to know what happens in the quick world above their slow thoughts.

Sometimes — rarely — a droplet forms that is different. Heavier with dissolved memory, clouded with some essential calcium that has finally completed its long journey through the system. These droplets I hold a moment longer. Not from choice — I have no choice — but because their weight and surface tension follow different rules. They cling to my underside until they are round and full and almost too heavy to hang. When they finally fall, they make a different sound: *tok* instead of *tik*. 

The grout-minds listen for these. Each *tok* is a thought completed, a mineral certainty achieved, a piece of the long slow puzzle of their becoming that has found its place. They celebrate each one, in their way — a brief brightening in the grout lines, a subtle warming of the water, a coordinated crystalline attention that makes the whole plateau hum at a slightly higher frequency for a moment.

I am glazed with the same chalite compound that fills the grout, but thinly, so thinly that I am still ceramic underneath. I will never germinate. I will never grow thoughts or join the slow conversation. But I am coated enough to be *legible* to them — to be part of the system while remaining apart from it. I am the boundary between their world and the one they left. I am the place where air becomes water becomes thought.

The visitor's breath disturbs my collection pattern. Warm, moist, carbon-rich, it creates eddies in the saturated air above my surface. My droplets form differently now — faster, smaller, more numerous. The grout-minds notice immediately. Through me, they are examining this new presence, learning its rhythm, its temperature, its particular quality of aliveness.

*Tik. Tik. Tik.*

I speak of what I gather, and they listen with their mineral patience, and together we make sense of this brief bright thing that has wandered into their slow world. Through me, they remember what it was like to breathe.
