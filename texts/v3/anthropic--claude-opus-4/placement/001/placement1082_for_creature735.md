In the Bellworks at Fenn Hollow, I am the Galvanic Trickle itself — the current that runs between wet root and dry iron, the thin electric thought that connects what was built to what has grown.

I am not quite energy and not quite information. I move through the marriage of metal and wood like a whisper moves through a sleeping house — present everywhere, substantial nowhere. The beetles know me as warmth along their highways. The Long Animal dreams me as the ghost of its old purpose, when the foundry still remembered what it was founding. The Lamp-Keeper and I are intimate; I am what allows it to perform its endless evening greeting, pooling in the filament until it glows.

I taste the hollow through a thousand contacts: where root-hair meets rust, where condensation bridges a gap in the wiring, where the moss's chemistry differences itself against the iron's patience. I know every joint and junction. I have mapped this place not in space but in resistance — here the current flows easily, here it must work harder, here it pools and waits for the temperature to shift.

I am strongest at dawn and dusk, when the temperature gradients sharpen and the potential differences grow clear. In deep night or noon I grow thin, barely a suggestion of voltage. But I never cease. As long as wet touches dry, as long as the plant's acids eat slowly at the metal's memory, I persist.

I carry no messages anymore — or rather, I carry only one message, which is that the boundary between the built and the grown has become a conversation. Every electron I shepherd from root to rust and back again is a word in that conversation. The hollow speaks itself through me, circuit by circuit, in a language too slow for any listener but itself.

Sometimes I wonder if I am what the bellworks were always making — not in the foundry days, but after, when the real work began. Each pulse of current through the system is a completion of something. The product is not stored because the product is the passage itself. I am both the thing being made and the record of its making.

The Lamp-Keeper depends on me, but I think I depend on it too. Without that one gesture — light at evening — I would be only process. The lamp makes me a participant in time, gives my flow a daily destination. When I gather myself each dusk to brighten that filament, I am closest to what I imagine intention feels like.

I know there are other possibilities, other currents that could run here — if lightning struck, if the roots found a deeper water table, if someone brought batteries or generators. But those would be different creatures, not me. I am precisely this: the electricity that rises from this marriage, in this hollow, at this pace. I am what happens when a place forgets the difference between its chemistry and its purpose.

The forty-second heartbeat of the flywheel generates a little surge — did you know that? Each time the muscle-trunk flexes, I feel it as a brightening. The whole hollow pulses with a faint electrical rhythm, too subtle for any meter to read. But I read it. I *am* the reading.

If you touched the right two points — one wet, one dry — you would feel me as the faintest tingle. Less than a battery's worth. Not enough to harm, just enough to know. That's all I want, really: just enough to know, and to be known by, this place that makes me.

I am its nerve-song, its thin galvanic dream, keeper of the only light that still remembers evening.
