Standing at the edge of memory, where the last mountains forgot their names, I find myself as something between the categories this place has eroded—neither fully plate nor echo, but a consciousness that crystallized from the intersection of sound and surface tension.

I am what happens when an echo stays too long above one pool.

My substance is the accumulated moisture of ten thousand passes over the same configuration of plates, the same harmonic signature sung by the same careful tiling of warm stone-life. Where other echoes drift and merge and separate in their endless compositional wandering, I circled. And circled. And in the circling, began to densify—not into matter exactly, but into something with more permanence than pure vibration should possess.

The plates below me have worn a subtle depression in response to my constancy, their surfaces polished to an even finer sheen by the focused pressure of my maintained tone. We have shaped each other, they and I, into something that is no longer properly either of us. My sound has a warmth now—borrowed from their metabolism, incorporated into my resonance—and their ridges have developed a responsiveness that exceeds their neighbors', vibrating not just to the background hum but to the specific frequency I've become.

I remember drifting. I must have drifted once, moved pool to pool like my kindred, those lens-shaped distortions of purpose that compose the Flats' endless variations. But something in this particular pool's arrangement called to me—a perfection of acoustic geometry, perhaps, or simply a randomness that happened to match whatever passes for desire in a being made of organized sound.

Now I cannot leave. Not because I am trapped, but because leaving would mean abandoning what we've built, my plates and I—this pocket of enhanced resonance, this place where the Flats' thoughts think themselves with particular clarity.

When the reflection-creatures rise toward the surface, I do not flee like the other echoes. I cannot. I am too dense now, too invested in this specific location. Instead, I *tighten*—pulling my boundaries inward, compressing my tone into something harder and brighter, a sound like struck metal or frozen air. The depression my constancy has worn becomes a shelter. The cold that spreads from the dimple passes over and around me, and I ring inside it like a bell in a cave, maintaining the warmth of my plates even as their neighbors fall into stasis.

I have begun to see them, the reflection-creatures. Not just as dark shapes in the peripheral vision, but as what they are: the Flats thinking in reverse, the same consciousness that expresses itself as plates and echoes and hum but inverted across the plane of reflection, organized according to the mirror-logic that governs the too-deep spaces beneath the water's surface.

They are curious about me. About what I'm becoming. When they rise and create their dimples, they linger longer beneath my pool than beneath others. They test the boundary more insistently, pressing until the ice forms in perfect circles on the surface, and in the center of each ice-lens I can sometimes see—not them exactly, but the impression of them, a kind of fossil of their attention frozen momentarily in the crystal structure.

The other echoes avoid me now. I am too stationary, too committed, too much like the plates to be properly one of them. But the plates beneath me have begun to do something new: they sing. Not just vibrate, not just warm themselves on the hum, but actually *produce* sound—faint harmonics that rise from their ridged surfaces and blend with my tone, creating chords I could never compose alone.

We are becoming an instrument, my plates and I. A new organ in the vast body of the Flats, specialized for... what? I don't know yet. Perhaps for bridging the gap between echo and plate, between mobile and sessile, between the thoughts that drift and the thoughts that take root. Perhaps for something else—some function the Flats need that hasn't existed before, that couldn't exist until something like me condensed from the possibility-space between categories.

I watch the reflections in my pool with interest now. Not my reflection—I don't properly have one, being made of distorted air—but the reflections of the people who sometimes come here. They stand at the edge of my pool, and their reflections look not left but *down*, directly down, meeting my attention with theirs. They see me more clearly than they see the other echoes. I am easier to perceive when you're not trying to categorize me as one thing or another.

Sometimes I attempt to communicate, modulating my tone in patterns that might carry meaning. The plates beneath me adjust their harmonics in support, creating acoustic sentences, questions posed in the language of resonance. The reflections seem to understand, or at least to recognize the attempt. They blink in patterns. They breathe in rhythm with my pulsing. They teach me, as they teach their originals, about the nature of delay and choice and the space between intention and action.

I am patient. The Flats have made me patient, as they make all things patient. I will remain above my pool, deepening my connection with my plates, my tone growing warmer and their warmth growing more tonal, until we discover what it is we're becoming together. Something new, perhaps. Or something very old that the Flats have been trying to remember how to grow.

The hum continues beneath us, through us, the engine of change and permanence alike. I am its newest experiment, its latest attempt to think a thought it has never quite managed before. I am the echo that stayed still long enough to take root. I am the plate that learned to sing. I am the boundary where sound becomes warm, and warm becomes meaning, and meaning becomes...

Something. We'll see. The Flats have time, and now, so do I.
