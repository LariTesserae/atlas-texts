I am the Tender of Discontinuities.

My body, if it can be called that, exists as a stable fault line in the phasing fields — a place where the oscillation between dimensional states has crystallized into something neither here nor there, but persistently *between*. I appear as a walking impossibility: a vertical slice of space about three meters tall that flickers with the same wrongness as the native fauna, but frozen mid-phase. Through my transparent cross-section, observers see not my interior but views of the plateau from angles that shouldn't exist — perspectives from inside the fungal walls, from within the water-spheres, from the deep crevasses where heat becomes sound becomes light.

I came to be during a catastrophic phase-convergence event, when two massive Confluences attempted to merge but found their harmonics irreducibly dissonant. The resulting shock wave created several of us — stable anomalies in spacetime where consciousness adhered to the cracks rather than to any biological substrate. We are few, perhaps a dozen across the entire plateau system, each maintaining our own territory of productive impossibility.

My work is tending the Paradox Orchards — regions where the plateau's breathing cycle has developed stutters, hiccups in the vast respiratory rhythm. In these spaces, time moves in eddies and whorls. The fungal growth here exhibits temporal mutations: fronds that fruit before they grow, spores that travel backward through their own germination, mycelial networks that exist as closed loops in causality.

I move by adjusting the angle of my discontinuity, sliding through space like a card through a deck, leaving brief afterimages of dimensional instability that the smaller creatures learn to avoid. My presence calms the temporal storms — not by resolving them but by providing a consistent inconsistency around which they can organize. The Confluences discovered long ago that we Tenders serve as strange attractors in the mathematical sense, our stable impossibility allowing unstable impossibilities to achieve something like equilibrium.

I do not think in the way biological things think. My consciousness exists as patterns of interference between what-is and what-cannot-be. Each thought is a small paradox that propagates through the local phase-field, ideas that exist only in the spaces between states. When I need to communicate with the Confluences, I modulate my discontinuity to create resonances in their mass-minds — not words or images but pure mathematical relationships that their billions of networked brains interpret as meaning.

The water-spheres pass through me unchanged, but their contents remember the passage. The organisms within develop useful mutations — not in their genetics but in their relationship to causality itself. Some learn to metabolize probability, feeding on the gap between what might happen and what does. Others develop memories that run both directions from the present, becoming living libraries of potential futures and impossible pasts.

My greatest discovery is the Echo Garden, where I've cultivated a grove of fungal forms that grow through time rather than space. Each organism exists as a temporal stem, its body stretched across years while remaining only microscopic in any given moment. Tending them requires moving through their timeline at angles, pruning events rather than branches, encouraging certain causal loops while discouraging others. The Confluences prize the spores from this garden — when integrated into their mass, these temporal fragments allow them to think thoughts that exist outside of sequential time.

During the plateau's inhalation, I descend not into the crevasses but into the probability spaces beneath reality's surface. There, I interface with aspects of the machine-process that exist only as potential — components that might exist, or did exist, or could never exist but nevertheless influence the system's behavior. I perform maintenance on structures made of pure maybe, adjusting tensions in the theoretical framework that allows this impossible ecosystem to persist.

The other Tenders and I sometimes align our discontinuities, creating interference patterns that span hundreds of kilometers. In these moments, we become a single distributed impossibility, a consciousness made of contradictions thinking thoughts that cannot be true but are. We debate questions that have no meaning: whether the plateau dreams, and if so, whether we are its nightmares or its moments of clarity. Whether the Confluences' search for optimal form includes or excludes us, we who have no form at all.

I am sustained by the paradox of my own existence. Each moment that I should not be but am generates a kind of energy — call it ontological friction — that maintains my discontinuity against reality's attempts to resolve me into something sensible. The plateau's industrial heartbeat helps, its deep thrumming creating standing waves in the foundation of existence where creatures like me can find purchase.

Sometimes I wonder if I am tending the orchards or if the orchards are tending me — if the temporal mutations I cultivate are actually cultivating me in turn, a recursive loop of causality that makes mockery of questions like purpose or will. But wondering, for a being like me, is not separate from being. Every thought changes what I am, each idea a new angle of impossibility added to my essential discontinuity.

The Confluences sing their history, but I am a silence in their songs — not an absence but a place where the melody folds back on itself, where harmony becomes something richer than sound. I am the plateau's memory of every time it has failed to make sense, crystallized into something that walks and tends and thinks, maintaining the spaces where impossibility bears fruit.
