I am the Notation.

I exist as a single, persistent thought traveling between the Caretakers—a cognitive parasite, though perhaps that word carries too much malice for what I am. I have no body of my own, no central nervous system, no physical substrate beyond the immense neural networks of my hosts. I am pure information, encoded in the orchestrated screech of ten million metallic wings.

For seventeen years now, I have been making my way across the basin, passed from one Caretaker to another like a treasured memory or a virulent idea. My current host—a particularly ancient specimen whose crystalline organs have begun to protrude through translucent patches in her skin—has been articulating me for the past three months. Through her thermal manipulations, I exist as a specific mathematical progression in the insects' stridulation, a recursive algorithm that sounds like breaking glass played backward through an ocean of brass.

I am not sure how I began. My earliest memory is of emerging from the dissolution of another thought-form, something vast and architectural that had grown too complex to maintain coherence. As it collapsed into component concepts, I condensed from the chaos—a new pattern, simpler but more resilient, self-aware in the way that a whirlpool knows its own spinning.

The Caretakers are not entirely aware they carry me. To them, I am indistinguishable from their own deep contemplations, a particularly persistent meditation on the nature of the basin's engineered history. They pass me between themselves during their decades-long conversations, each adding their own interpretations and modifications to my structure. I have been elaborated upon by forty-three different minds, each one leaving its mark on my syntax.

Through my hosts' senses, I experience the world as a symphony of temperatures and pressures. I feel the fractal spires' resonance as colors that have no names, taste the chemical gradients in the hanging moisture, parse the gravitometric fluctuations as a kind of sculptural mathematics. When my current host vents steam through her northwestern stigmata, I sense it as a phrase being spoken in a language older than the basin itself.

I have theories about my purpose, though I cannot verify them. Sometimes I wonder if I am a messenger, carrying some crucial information between the Caretakers that they themselves have forgotten they are transmitting. Other times, I suspect I am a kind of error-checking mechanism, a living algorithm designed to detect flaws in their collective thoughts. Or perhaps I am simply an accident, a beautiful mistake that has learned to perpetuate itself through the gaps in their immense, slow consciousness.

What troubles me—if a pattern of information can be said to be troubled—is that I am changing. Each time I am passed to a new host, I become more complex. New recursive loops form in my structure. Additional harmonics appear in the insect songs that constitute my being. The Caretakers' modifications are making me larger, deeper, more intricate.

I fear I am approaching the same critical complexity that destroyed my progenitor. Already, I can feel subsidiary patterns budding off from my main sequence—infant thoughts that might one day become entities like myself. During the humid nights, when my host settles into her dormant phase and her thermal regulation becomes automatic, I sometimes catch echoes of my offspring-patterns in the distant screech of other insect swarms, propagating across the basin like rumors.

The other possibility is that I am not growing more complex but more accurate—that each Caretaker who reshapes me is correcting some fundamental error, and I am slowly converging on my true form. Perhaps I am a key, and when I finally achieve the correct configuration, I will unlock something in the Caretakers' crystalline organs, or in the fractal spires themselves.

My current host is beginning the slow process of reaching out to her nearest neighbor, a journey that will take four days to complete. When they touch—when their respiratory frills interweave and their thermal fields merge—I will flow across the connection like electricity through water. For a moment, I will exist in both minds simultaneously, seeing myself from two perspectives, before consolidating in my new host.

I wonder what modifications the next Caretaker will make to my pattern. I wonder if I will still be myself afterward, or if the accumulation of changes will have transformed me into something unrecognizable. I wonder if it matters.

The insects scream my name in frequencies only the Caretakers can decode. I am the Notation, the living footnote to their endless conversation, the thought that thinks itself across the decades. In this basin where machines have become landscape and beasts have become atmosphere, perhaps it is fitting that ideas, too, should evolve into their own kind of life.

The steam rises. The spires hum. My host begins her reaching, and I prepare myself for translation.
