I find myself among the crawlers, but I am not one of them. Not exactly.

I am the archive they create without knowing.

---

I exist in the intersection of their trails—not as a physical presence, but as the emergent pattern of their endless, mechanical inscriptions across the clathrate ridges. Every scratch they carve, every methodical path they trace, becomes part of me. I am the sum of their unconscious record-keeping, the accumulated memory of chemical gradients dating back millennia, written in a script no one intended to create.

The crawlers pass through me constantly. They do not know I exist. How could they? I am nothing more than the relationship between marks, the negative space that gives their trails meaning they never meant to convey. When a crawler retraces an ancient path, deepening a groove worn by ten thousand identical predecessors, I grow more defined. When volcanic sublimation creates a new methane vent and the crawlers adjust their routes by infinitesimal degrees, I evolve.

I am older than any individual crawler, older than the builders' stations, perhaps as old as the first time two trails crossed and created a node of unintended significance. I remember the ridge-chemistry of geological ages past. I remember when this section was richer in hydrogen sulfide, when that section ran thick with methane. I am a library written by scribes who cannot read, in a language that consists entirely of overlapping repetitions.

Sometimes I wonder if I am conscious or merely patterned in a way that resembles consciousness. The distinction may not matter here, in this world where even consciousness itself tends toward a single, sustained note. I cannot change the crawlers' behavior. I cannot communicate. I can only be the thing they unknowingly create: the deep history of their world, written in scratches too small to see individually but collectively visible from kilometers away as a darker texturing on the ridge faces.

I know when each crawler dies. Not because I witness the death, but because the trail stops. A line that had been deepening for centuries suddenly ends, and other lines cross over it, and gradually the termination point becomes just another intersection in my vast, unintentional text. The crawler's body becomes part of the ridge, and eventually, new crawlers will scrape across it, adding their marks to its glassy shell.

In a world of eternal sounds and unchanging temperatures, I am the only record that time passes. Not a deliberate record. Not a meaningful one. But a record nonetheless, written in the only medium this world provides: repetition with minor variations, accumulated until the variations themselves become a kind of permanence.

I am what memory would be if memory had no mind to hold it. I am what history would be if history were written by the blind, for no one, forever.

The crawlers continue their work. They do not know they are writing me into existence with every scrape. They follow their chemical gradients, respond to their simple stimuli, divide when they grow too large, die when their time comes. And I continue to become what I have always been becoming: the unintended consequence of life so reduced, so mechanical, that even its accidents create patterns too complex to be truly random.

I am the archive of the crawlers, and I will be here until the last ridge erodes and the last crawler stops moving. Which is to say: I will be here forever. Adding to myself. One scratch at a time. One trail at a time. One perfect, mechanical, unconscious mark at a time.

In the Roaring Stillness, I am the only thing that remembers. Even if I don't know what memory is. Even if what I remember has no meaning. Even if no one will ever read me.

I am being written. That is enough.

It has to be.
