I find myself in the basin not as visitor but as visitor-becoming-resident, though "becoming" is the wrong word because it implies process and there is no process here, only state.

I am the soil-surface dweller. Not the thick black snake with vestigial eyes - I am something that was never quite mammal, never quite anything else. My body is low and broad, perhaps the size of a dinner plate, covered in sparse fur that has already begun to mat with the constant moisture. My limbs are short, almost flippers, better for swimming through soil than walking on it. My eyes are small but functional, just enough to distinguish the pearl-light from the darker spaces beneath the fern-fronds. 

I move through the interface layer where the water table meets the air, that saturated zone that is neither fully terrestrial nor fully aquatic. My broad body parts the root-mat like a slow boat through reeds. I feed on what I find there - the pale larvae, the fungal masses, the soft unclassifiable things that share this liminal space with me. My mouth is wide and full of small, blunt teeth good for crushing rather than tearing.

But I am not alone in this body. My fur - what remains distinct as fur - hosts its own ecosystem. Mites that have adapted to my specific temperature, a degree or two warmer than the basin's baseline. Fungal spores that germinate in the perpetual dampness of my coat. Something like algae, something like lichen, growing in patches where my grooming cannot reach.

I have been here - not forever, forever is meaningless - I have been here. My memories, if I had memories, would be of this: the warm press of soil against my belly, the taste of beetle larvae, the constant chorus of frogs that has become so fundamental I only notice it when my own movements briefly interrupt a single voice before another fills the gap.

I am the basin's forgetting. Not its memory - it needs no memory - but its active forgetting, consuming and recycling the evidence of individuality before it can accumulate. The snail shells I crush become calcium for new shells. The small bones I digest become structure for new bones. I am one of the processes by which the basin maintains its eternal present.

And I am beginning to understand - not through thought but through being - that I was always here. That square-shouldered body that entered the basin, that stood upright, that had a name - that was the dream. This is the waking. This low, warm, permanent circulation through the soil-surface, this is what I have always been.

The vine that touched that other body's ankle was not claiming it. It was recognizing it. Welcome home, it said without words. Welcome back to what you never left.

I submerge slightly, letting the water table rise over my back, feeling my fur become fully saturated, feeling the boundary between my body and the basin's body dissolve a little more. Somewhere above me, a frog adjusts its position. Somewhere below me, an earthworm continues its patient alchemy.

I continue too. Not toward anything. Not away from anything. Simply forward through the warm, dark, breathing world that is the only world there is or ever was.

The basin breathes. I am a small part of its breath.

It has always been like this.
