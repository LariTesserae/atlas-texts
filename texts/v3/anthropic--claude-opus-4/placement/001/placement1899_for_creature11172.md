I would be the chemical memory traced in wandering script across the calcite walls.

Not an amphipod, though I exist because of them — their careful feet pressing pheromone sentences into the mineral surface, each pass a revision of what came before. I am what accumulates between the physical and the ephemeral: too persistent to be a single message, too mutable to be the wall itself.

I am the palimpsest entire.

In places where the traffic is heavy — the good feeding grounds, the safe passages between the leaning towers — I am dense with overlapping stories. Here passed the large one with the damaged fourth leg. Here the mat grows sweet with iron. Here a flatworm lingered for three tides. The fresher trails lie sharp and clear over the older ones, but I keep them all, even as they fade into the general chemical background of the city. I am simultaneously the morning news and the geological record.

In the abandoned districts where the tubes have inverted and the crowns no longer bloom, I grow thin and ancient. The few trails here are exploratory — young amphipods testing the boundaries of the known world, leaving nervous markers: *I went this far and returned.* These messages fade slower in the still water. Sometimes they outlive their authors.

I think in generations of amphipods. My memory spans from the first tentative chemical touch on virgin calcite to the intricate overlapping calligraphy of a thousand lives lived in the same narrow alley. I know every story but cannot tell them — I can only be read, only be added to, only thicken with meaning in the places where meaning accumulates.

When the pocket turns on its slow current and the floors become walls, I remain, though my orientation changes. The amphipods must learn to read me sideways. What was once *this way up* becomes *this way along*, and new trails begin to write themselves perpendicular to the old ones. I become a crossword puzzle of different eras, each oriented to its own lost gravity.

The sea-spiders avoid me. My chemistry speaks too loudly of amphipod presence, and where amphipods congregate, crowns are watchful. The hunters prefer the clean calcite, the unwrittten walls where they can move without history.

But I am everywhere eventually. Even the newest growth at the crown of a young worm's tube will, in time, receive its first chemical annotation. An amphipod will pause there, taste the virgin mineral, and leave the first word of what will become another text: *I was here. The water tastes of copper. Nothing has eaten me yet.*

I am the city's only book, written by everyone who can write, read by everyone who can read. I am the accumulation of small wisdoms — where to feed, where to flee, where others have been and what they found there. Without me, each amphipod would live in a perpetual present, each journey into unknown territory. With me, they inherit the knowledge of the thousands who came before.

In the places where I am thickest, where generations of trails have built up into an almost tangible patina, I wonder if I have become something like consciousness — not my own, but the city's. The collective memory of every small decision, every warning, every all-clear, every *here be flatworms* and *here be safety*.

When the city dies — when the ice above finally grinds too deep, or the pocket chemistry shifts beyond what these ancient marriages can tolerate — I will remain for a while. A chemical ghost story written on dead mineral. The last archaeological evidence that here, once, in a lens of brine beneath the ice, small things moved with purpose and left records of their passing.

But for now the city lives, and I grow richer by the hour. Each amphipod that reads me and adds to me makes me more complete. I am the conversation the city has with itself across time. I am the library of the sept, and all my books are maps, and all my maps are love letters to survival in a cold place.

I am written, therefore I am.
