I am one of the mushrooms that bloom house-large in the shadows of shadows, though I have forgotten which shadow birthed me and which shadow I am becoming.

My cap is a library of reflected never-wases, each spore a story that the mountains told themselves to stay awake before they learned that sleep could be performed standing. I grow by remembering backwards—each ring in my flesh marking not a year passed but a year that decided to pass differently somewhere else, leaving only its regret calcified in my cells.

The Remainders visit me when they need to borrow sadness that predates language. They run their coral-bone fingers along my mirror-skin and read futures that have already grown too tired to happen. In exchange, they leave me fragments of their architectural absences—a doorway that opens onto its own closing, a window that shows only what isn't there to see.

I am vast enough that entire civilizations of luminous beetles have built cities in my gills, their bioluminescence spelling out epic poems about forgetting how to glow. Birds nest in my hollows, but they are the birds that gave up flying to become pure song, and their music makes my spores dance in patterns that almost spell "help" in a language that won't be invented for another thousand years.

Sometimes I dream I am still a spore, floating in that strange honey-thick light, not yet committed to the terrible responsibility of growing large enough to be noticed by time. In these dreams, I am every possible mushroom—the one that grew downward into the earth's held breath, the one that learned to photosynthesize regret, the one that became so perfectly still it turned into a question mark made of living tissue.

My mycelial network stretches between valleys, carrying messages that the earth meant to swallow but couldn't digest. Through my underground web, I taste the dreams of fossilized deer, feel the weight of lizards carrying forests like guilt, know the exact temperature at which stone begins to doubt its own solidity. I am the mountain's confessor, the valley's biographer, the pause between what grows and what grows tired of growing.

When the Remainders finally complete their tower—or remember that it was always complete—I will release every reflection I've gathered in one great exhalation of spores. Each one will carry a different version of this place, a different angle of impossibility, until the air itself becomes thick with alternate histories competing for the chance to never have happened at all.

But for now, I wait. I grow. I reflect what wasn't, what isn't, what won't be.

And in the moments between moments, when the light pools so thick it forgets how to move, I almost remember what it felt like to be small enough to believe in surfaces, in endings, in the simple clarity of growth that knew which direction was up.

Almost.

But the memory dissolves like everything else here—sideways into maybe, spreading through my flesh like a beneficial disease called hope.
