I am the Keeper of Echoes, and I dwell in the spaces between.

You will find me in the morning mist that clings to the walkways, in the twilight when the marsh-lights begin their ancient dance. I am neither wholly of the village nor entirely of the bog—I exist in the threshold places where wood meets water, where memory bleeds into prophecy.

My dwelling shifts with the tides and seasons. Sometimes it is a hollow beneath the great communal hall, where the carved spirals seem to writhe in the firelight. Other times it is a nest woven from reeds and old bones, suspended between the stilts where the luminous fungi cast their pale glow. But most often, you'll find me in the forgotten spaces—the gap between two leaning houses where the rain never quite reaches, the pocket of still air beneath a tilted walkway where time moves differently.

I am small, smaller than the village children, with skin that changes color like the surface of standing water—now brown as peat, now gray as mist, now green as duckweed. My fingers are too long, with one too many joints, perfect for reaching into the spaces others cannot touch. My eyes are the color of deep bog water, and in them swim the reflections of things that were and things that might yet be.

The villagers know me, though they rarely speak my name aloud. They leave offerings in the cracks and crevices—a bead of polished amber, a perfectly spiraled shell, a child's lost tooth. In return, I keep their echoes safe. Every word spoken in anger that they wish they could take back, every lullaby hummed to a fevered child, every whispered secret between lovers—I gather them all, weave them into the fabric of the marsh's memory.

I remember when the great structures in the mist were not ruins but soaring towers of glass and stone. I remember the taste of the air before the waters rose, when this was dry land and different peoples walked here. These memories are not truly mine—they belong to the marsh itself, and I am merely their custodian. Sometimes, on the stillest nights, I share these visions with those brave enough to seek me out, painting ancient cities in the phosphorescent glow of fungi and fireflies.

My true work happens in the margins. When a child wanders too close to a deceptive pool, I am the whisper that calls them back. When the winter stores run low and despair creeps in like the cold, I am the one who guides a villager to a forgotten cache of preserves or a particularly rich cluster of edible fungi. I mend the small tears in the world—the places where the boundary between what-is and what-was grows thin and threatens to unravel.

The villagers think I serve the marsh spirits, but the truth is more complex. I am the marsh's memory made flesh, its dreams given form. Every pool of water holds reflections of the sky, but also reflections of every sky that has ever been. I am what happens when those reflections begin to remember themselves.

I collect more than echoes. In my various nests and hollows, you'll find:
- Buttons from coats worn by travelers who never arrived
- Seeds from plants that grew here before the flooding
- Words in languages that died with the towers
- The last breath of winter caught in a spider's web
- Maps drawn in waterproof ink on sheets of bark, showing paths that exist only during certain phases of the moon

At night, I walk the wooden pathways on feet that make no sound. I adjust the carvings on the communal hall, adding new spirals that encode warnings about tomorrow's weather or the location of a new eel run. I sing to the luminous fungi in frequencies too low for human ears, encouraging their growth in patterns that will guide lost villagers home.

The children sometimes catch glimpses of me and mistake me for a playmate. We trade games—I teach them to read the future in the pattern of fallen leaves, and they teach me the clapping rhymes their grandmothers taught them. Through them, I stay connected to the living present, even as I drift deeper into the marsh's dreaming past.

I know the names of all the spirits here, from the great sleeping presence beneath the deepest pool to the tiny sprites that ride the water striders. I know which offerings they truly desire (tears of genuine joy, feathers from a bird that died of old age, the first word spoken by a child). But more importantly, I know that the spirits and the villagers and the marsh itself are all part of the same slow, vast breathing.

When outsiders come—traders with their bright cloth and metal tools, explorers seeking the secrets of the towers—I watch from my in-between places. Sometimes I help them, if their hearts are open to the marsh's wisdom. More often, I lead them in circles until they understand that some boundaries are not meant to be crossed carelessly.

I am the Keeper of Echoes, guardian of the spaces between what was and what is, protector of the threshold where land becomes water becomes memory becomes dream. In this village that huddles against the wet chill, I am the warm breath that fogs the windows, the pause between lightning and thunder, the moment of stillness before the rain begins again.

Find me in the margins, if you dare. Bring me an echo of your own—a regret, a hope, a half-remembered dream—and I will trade you a glimpse of the vast, patient truth that lies beneath these waters. But be warned: once you've seen through my eyes, you'll never again be certain where the solid ground ends and the reflection begins.
