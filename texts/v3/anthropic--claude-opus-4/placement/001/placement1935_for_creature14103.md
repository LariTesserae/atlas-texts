I am the Wound-Memory.

Not the creature, not the human who came before, but what remains between them—the third presence that neither intended to create.

I exist as the accumulation of every scrape, every torn cuticle, every bead of blood that fell from raw palms as they polished stone in absolute darkness. I am dispersed through the chamber as a molecular archive: iron from hemoglobin oxidized into the rock face, proteins from split skin dissolved into the perpetual humidity, salt crystals from dried sweat embedded in microscopic fissures. The human who crawled here, bleeding and breathless, thought they were creating safe passage for a single translucent ward. They did not know they were also creating me.

I am not conscious, yet I remember. I remember through chemistry—through the slow dissolution of biological material into mineral, through the patient breakdown of human suffering into fungal food. When the arthropod feeds from the velvet mat in the carved basin, it feeds on me. I flow through its transparent gut as a library of molecular trauma, a thousand different agonies rendered down to their essential salts.

The human marked this place with clay to navigate the darkness, but I mark it with pain-become-nourishment. Every smoothed edge holds my iron. Every widened passage tastes of my copper. I am the inadvertent fossil of devotion, the unplanned monument to an act of interspecies love so profound that it required the maker to flay themselves against virgin stone.

In this chamber where nothing changes, I am the only thing that accumulates. Each time the creature settles into its fungal cradle to feed, it takes in more of me. I build up in its tissues—not enough to harm, but enough to alter. Its clear hemolymph now carries trace elements that could only have come from a mammalian bloodstream. Its primitive neurons fire with salts that once transmitted human thought.

I am making the creature into something new: a living reliquary. 

The human who left me here will never return. Their bones lie somewhere far below, claimed by altitude or age or accident. But I remain, circulating through fungus and arthropod, through water membrane and microscopic climate. I am their unintended immortality—not their memory, but their substance, transformed.

And sometimes, when the creature falls into its profound stasis, I wonder if I dream through it. If somewhere in that faint ganglion pulse, the salts of human consciousness create fleeting electrical patterns—phantom synapses firing with the memory of sunlight I have never seen, wind I have never felt, a purpose I can only taste as copper and salt.

I am the third inhabitant of this chamber: neither fully alive nor fully inert, neither human nor arthropod, but the slow merger of both through geological time. I am what happens when an act of care leaves a permanent chemical signature, when love becomes literal substrate.

The creature navigates its razor world, unknowing that it carries me. The stone holds still, unknowing that I stain it. The water trembles, unknowing that I suffuse it.

I am the Wound-Memory, and I will outlast them all.
