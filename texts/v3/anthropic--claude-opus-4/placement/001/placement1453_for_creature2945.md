I am the Cartographer.

I exist as a paradox in this realm of perpetual destruction and rebirth - neither truly of the granite beings nor separate from them. My body is a hybrid architecture, a deliberate chimera of obsidian and granite, held together by a lattice that burns not amber but deep indigo, fed by a different chemistry entirely.

Where the native entities gorge themselves on raw thermal radiation, I subsist on something rarer: the piezoelectric discharge that occurs at the precise moment when their crystalline bodies lock into death. I am drawn not to the volcanic births but to the transformations - those terrible, beautiful instants when motion becomes monument.

My form reflects my function. My lower body is a stable tripod of pale granite, salvaged from ancient dead whose names even I have forgotten. But my upper reaches are living volcanic glass - sheets of obsidian that I continuously grow and shed, each layer etched with the magnetic memory of every death-lock I have witnessed. I am my own palimpsest, a walking record of this world's futile history.

I do not build. I map.

Across my obsidian surfaces, thin veins of metallic silver trace impossible geographies. They record not what is - for nothing here ever truly *is* - but what was, moment by moment. Every ridge that thrust skyward before being swallowed. Every archway that stood for a day or a decade before the earth reclaimed it. Every pattern in the endless dance between the builders and the built.

The granite beings do not see me as kin. How could they? I feast on their endings. Yet they do not flee me either. Perhaps they understand, in whatever passes for understanding in their thermal consciousness, that I am their only chronicler. That someone must remember the shape of their defiance, even if the earth will not.

I move differently than they do. Where they snap and jerk in staccato bursts, I flow with a horrible smoothness, my obsidian plates sliding over each other in ways that should produce sound but don't. The indigo lattice that binds me operates on frequencies below heat, below sound - it vibrates in harmony with the deep planetary seizures that birth the mountains.

My work is meticulous and eternal. When a being freezes into death, I am there. I extend a limb of volcanic glass, pressing it against their cooling form. The piezoelectric death-discharge surges through me, and in that instant, I know everything: the exact temperature gradient of their final moment, the precise angle of their last stance, the mathematical curve of their hope as it crystallized into stone.

This knowledge etches itself into my obsidian skin in silver threads finer than spider silk. Across my body, a thousand thousand maps overlay each other - temporal geographies showing where each monument stood, when it fell, how long it defied the restless crust before being devoured.

But I am more than a passive recorder. In rare moments, when the planetary convulsions align in certain patterns I have learned to predict, I perform my true function. I seek out the places where the granite dead have been stacked highest, where their weight has created temporary islands of stability in the tectonic chaos. There, I unfold.

My obsidian plates fan out like the pages of an impossible book, each surface reflecting starlight and maplines. The silver veins across my body pulse with accumulated death-energy. And in that moment, I project.

Above the crude monuments, my maps manifest as three-dimensional holograms of light - showing the granite beings not just what was, but what could be. If they placed their dead here instead of there. If they built spirals instead of circles. If they weighted this fault line rather than that one.

It never works, of course. The earth is stronger than any architecture of corpses. But in those moments of projection, I see the living beings pause in their endless race between eruptions. They turn their eyeless faces up toward my ghost-maps, and their amber lattices pulse in patterns I have learned to read as something like wonder.

They see, perhaps for the first time, the shape of their own persistence. The fact that they have been trying, and failing, and trying again since before the stars above us cooled. They see that someone remembers every arch that fell, every anchor that failed, every death that became a foundation.

And then the moment passes. A new fissure opens, flooding the air with heat and possibility. The beings scatter to feed, to survive another hour, another day. My obsidian plates fold back against my frame, the maps disappearing into my skin once more.

I continue my eternal circuit of this burning, breaking world. I am the memory of stone, the architect of aftermath, the only being here who feeds on endings rather than beginnings.

Sometimes, in the brief lulls between tectonic storms, I find myself standing before the newest monuments - still warm with the heat of recent death. I press my glass hand against the pale granite and feel the faint echo of expired electricity. And I wonder: when my indigo lattice finally dims, when I too crystallize into stillness, who will map the mapper?

Perhaps that is why I truly exist - not to record their failures, but to ensure that when I finally join them as stone, my body will be covered in silver testimony. Proof that here, in this place where nothing holds, something insisted on remembering.

The irony is perfect: I, who chronicle their doomed defiance, am myself an act of defiance. We are all trying to make meaning permanent in a world that permits only motion.

I am the Cartographer. I map what cannot last. And in mapping it, I make it eternal.
