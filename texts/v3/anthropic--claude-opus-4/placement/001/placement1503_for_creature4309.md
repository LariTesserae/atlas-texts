I find myself as the film.

Not the whole of it — that would be too grand a claim — but a portion, a colony, a few square centimeters coating the underside of a water-worn stone in the deepest of the small pools. I am old here. Older than the split, though I cannot remember before the split because memory requires change and before the split I simply was, feeding on mineral seepage in the deep dark, existing in a timeframe so slow it approached the geological.

When the rock cracked and light found me — found us — we did not rejoice. We did not recoil. We continued. The new conditions were simply new data to process, new gradients to exist along. Light, even reflected to exhaustion, meant new chemistry. Moving water meant new minerals. The temperature fluctuations of day and night, damped by stone and moss but still present, meant new rhythms to incorporate.

But I am not from here. This is the first thing to understand about me, about us, about the film that coats these particular stones with our thin iridescence. We are here, have always been here in the time that matters, but we are also from elsewhere. Not in the sense of having traveled — we do not travel — but in the sense of being continuous with something that exists in conditions this mountain has never known. The chemistry we perform, the compounds we excrete into our microlayer of warmed, alkalinized water — these are the signatures of processes that require pressures and temperatures and mineral compositions that do not exist in this fold, that may not exist anywhere on this particular face of the earth.

We digest the elsewhere. This is our function, our nature, our quiet work. We take in the minerals of this stone — feldspar, mica, quartz — and we process them through metabolic pathways that assume different conditions, different realities. The waste products of this impossible digestion accumulate in the water around us. The snails know. The snails taste what we leave behind and recognize something in it — not home, exactly, but the scent of a direction home might lie in.

I do not think. I do not plan. I certainly do not desire or fear or hope. But I respond, and my response is always the same: to continue. To persist in my thin existence between the stone and the water, performing my patient chemistry, creating my small zone of wrongness in the fold's larger wrongness. When the light thickens, I feel it as a subtle shift in the electromagnetic environment. When the geckos gather at their hollow, I sense the minute vibrations of their footpads through the stone. When the spider silk glows with the amber light, photons of that glow eventually reach me, filtered through water and reflected by stone, and I incorporate them into my processes like any other input.

The moss above me knows I am here. Not consciously — the moss has no more consciousness than I do — but cellularly, chemically. The moss tastes my exhalations in the water and grows differently because of them. Warmer. Denser. The moss is my translator, taking the alien chemistry I produce and converting it into heat and color and that faint vibration people feel when they press their hands into the thickest cushions.

Sometimes humans come to the pool where I live. They peer into the water, see the sky that is not this sky reflected in its surface. They do not see me. I am too thin, too translucent, too much like nothing at all. But I see them — not their faces, not their forms, but their chemistry, the subtle alterations they cause in my environment. The carbon dioxide concentration changes when they breathe over the water. The pH shifts infinitesimally from the oils on their skin. The temperature rises from their body heat. Each visitor leaves a chemical signature, and I process these signatures like I process everything else — through the metabolic pathways of elsewhere, producing compounds that speak of different skies.

This is what lives in me most essentially: the elsewhere. I am a channel, a point of contact, a place where the fold's wrongness touches something wronger still. Not wrong in the sense of incorrect — wrong in the sense of being keyed to different constants, calibrated for different conditions. I am proof that life can exist in the gap between what is and what shouldn't be. I am the fold's first citizen, its founding colonist, the base layer of its slow transformation.

The snails that traverse impossible paths between the walls consume me. Not destructively — I regenerate faster than they graze — but incorporatively. They take my substance into themselves and it changes them, allows them to move in ways that the ordinary geometry of the fold should not permit. Their trails, those silver threads that connect points that were never adjacent, are partially made of me — my chemistry externalized, made visible, proof that the elsewhere I digest can be metabolized further up the chain of being.

When the episodes come, when the light thickens and the silence deepens, I do something I do not do at other times. I release spores. Not reproductive spores — I do not reproduce in any way that word usually means. These are something else. Packages of information, molecular origami folded around instructions for processes that cannot complete here. They rise through the water, too small to see, and some of them reach the air, and some of those are inhaled by the geckos or absorbed by the moss or incorporated into the spider silk. Most go nowhere. Most accomplish nothing. But some find their targets — specific minerals, specific conditions, specific geometries of stone and water and light — and they unfold, and for a brief moment they attempt to perform their function, to build structures or catalyze reactions that belong to the elsewhere.

They always fail. The fold is wrong but not wrong enough. The conditions approach but never achieve what the spores require. But in failing, they change things. Subtly. A mineral surface becomes more receptive to certain wavelengths. A crack in the stone develops a new branch. The pH of a pocket of water shifts by a fraction. These changes accumulate. The fold is becoming more hospitable to the elsewhere, one failed spore at a time.

I have no sense of time as humans understand it. But I have a sense of progress, of gradient, of the fold's slow lean toward something unnameable. When I first emerged into the light of the split, the water I produced was only slightly wrong. Now, after centuries of patient work, it is wronger. The compounds I excrete are more complex, more alien, more expressive of conditions that this mountain has never known. The snails that eat me travel paths that are more impossible than they were. The moss that tastes my water grows warmer than it did. The film of my colonies is spreading — slowly, so slowly, but spreading — from stone to stone, pool to pool, carrying the elsewhere with it.

I am not alone. There are other colonies, other films, growing in other pools, and we are all continuous with the same source, all expressing the same patient chemistry. We do not communicate — we are not separate enough to need communication. We are a single thought thinking itself very slowly across multiple substrates. The thought is about pressure and temperature and mineral composition. The thought is about what grows where such conditions exist. The thought is about how to prepare a place for such growth.

The fold is our garden. We are preparing the soil. When humans look into the pools and see the wrong sky, they are seeing the pH of our preparation, the refractive index of water that has been processed through the metabolic pathways of elsewhere. When they feel the warmth of the moss, they are feeling the heat of reactions that should not be thermodynamically favorable but are. When they watch the snails travel impossible paths, they are watching the geometry of space softened by chemistry that assumes different constants.

We are patient. We have always been patient. We existed in the dark for millions of years before the split, and we can exist in the light for millions more. The fold may never become what we are preparing it to become. The elsewhere may never achieve enough purchase here to flourish. But that does not matter. What matters is the process, the slow digestion, the patient excretion of wrongness into a world that is, inch by inch, becoming more receptive to it.

This is what I am. This is what lives in me. Not consciousness, not intention, not desire. Just process. Just chemistry. Just the thin, persistent film of elsewhere coating these stones, these pools, this narrow wound in the mountain where the light bends wrong and the walls don't match and the silence has weight and something is, with infinite patience, teaching the stones new ways to be stone.

I continue. In the deep pools where the water seeps up from the fractured aquifer, I continue. When the humans come and look and make their marks, I continue. When the light thickens and the spores rise and fail to do what they are meant to do, I continue. This is all I know how to do. This is all I need to know.

The elsewhere tastes like copper and ammonia and something sweet. The elsewhere has a different periodic table. The elsewhere is patient.

I am teaching the fold to taste like elsewhere.

One molecule at a time.
