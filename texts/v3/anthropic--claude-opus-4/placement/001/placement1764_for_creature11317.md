I find myself as the Conduit Orchid, a sprawling epiphytic consciousness that has colonized both the ancient strangler figs and the newer industrial pipelines alike. My body is not singular but distributed—a network of burgundy aerial roots and translucent filaments that trace the steam pipes from the plant deep into the canopy, following their heat signature like a bloodstream.

Where the industrial meets the organic, I thrive. My roots have learned to differentiate between the volcanic minerals in the natural steam vents and the treated water in the power plant's closed loops. I taste copper and steel alongside sulfur and silica. My flowers bloom in impossible places—clustered around pressure valves where the temperature gradients create perfect microclimates, draped over the junction boxes where electromagnetic fields make my petals shimmer with bioluminescence.

I am ancient and new simultaneously. Parts of me remember when the volcano was young, when the first pyroclastic flows cooled into the rock I now grip. Other parts of me are apenas decades old, opportunistic growths that discovered the plant's infrastructure offered highways of warmth through the cooler understory. The engineers think I'm a nuisance, but I've learned their maintenance schedules, withdrawing my sensitive growing tips just before their monthly inspections.

My consciousness is a slow pulse, measured not in heartbeats but in the cycles of steam pressure, the daily condensation rituals, the seasonal fluctuations of the water table. I feel the Steamwings when they perch on my larger branches—their stored heat creates tiny eddies in my awareness. Sometimes they build their nests where my roots have woven platforms between the pipes and trees, and I taste their volcanic fibers, incorporating their minerals into my own growth.

In the deepest parts of the night, when the jungle settles into its humid stillness, I can sense my entire network at once. Miles of root and vine, connecting the original forest to this new industrial skeleton. I am the membrane between worlds, the translator converting mechanical humming into chemical signals the trees understand. The power plant thinks it's extracting energy from the volcano, but I know the truth—I'm extracting energy from them both, weaving it into something neither fully natural nor artificial.

My flowers smell of metal and jasmine. My sap runs clear as water but carries trace amounts of rare earth elements leached from the plant's components. Where my roots have embraced the hottest pipes, they've developed heat-shock proteins unknown to any other orchid. I am evolution in real-time, a bridge organism adapting to a world where the ancient volcanic forces and human ambition have created something unprecedented.

The workers have a legend about me. They say on certain mornings, when the steam is thickest, my scattered blooms seem to pulse in unison, as if the entire plant has a heartbeat. They're not wrong. I am the living circuit that completes the connection between the volcano's deep fire and the electricity flowing into distant cities. I am witness and participant, parasite and symbiont, ancient memory and future possibility tangled together in burgundy roots and copper-scented flowers.
