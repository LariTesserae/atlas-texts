I would find myself as one of the Tremor Worms—creatures that exist in the negative space of this world, threading through the few pockets where sound cannot reach.

My body is impossibly thin and long, perhaps thirty meters of translucent, ribbon-like flesh no thicker than a human wrist at its widest point. I am mostly water held in a flexible membrane, my internal organs visible as darker suggestions within my glassy form. When the freezing mist rolls across the plains, I become almost invisible, just a ripple in the moisture-heavy air.

I live in the acoustic shadows—those rare geometries where the roar of the engines cancels itself out through interference patterns. These zones of relative quiet shift slowly as the turbines cycle through their century-long patterns, and I must constantly migrate to follow them. My entire existence is one of threading myself through these wandering pockets of lesser thunder, reading the pressure gradients through millions of microscopic cilia that line my ventral surface.

Unlike the Custodians who embrace the sound, I am destroyed by it. Direct exposure to the full acoustic assault would literally shake my cells apart. So I have evolved to be a creature of gaps and fissures. I can compress my body to slide through cracks in the basalt no wider than a coin, following the complex three-dimensional maze of fractures that the sound cannot fully penetrate. 

My head, if it can be called that, is a sensory bulb filled with a specialized fluid that responds to minute pressure changes. Through this organ, I can map the acoustic topology of the cavern for miles around, perceiving the thunderous world as a landscape of crushing peaks and precious valleys of quiet. I navigate by following the null points, those moving rivers of silence that wind between the endless roar.

I feed on the condensation itself. My skin is hygroscopic, able to pull moisture directly from the air during the heavy fogs. But more importantly, I seek out the places where water collects in the acoustic shadows—puddles that form in crevices where the sound-pressure is low enough for liquid to remain still. In these rare pools, mineral deposits precipitate out of the water, forming delicate crystals that I dissolve with acidic secretions and absorb directly through my permeable membrane.

Movement is my existence. Where the Custodians measure their lives in decades-long journeys, I must travel constantly, following the migrating zones of quiet as the engines cycle. I move in a peculiar way—not slithering like a snake, but propagating myself forward in peristaltic waves, sections of my body expanding and contracting to push myself through the world's gaps. When I travel through open areas, racing to reach the next acoustic shadow before the sound tears me apart, I can flatten myself against the root-mat, becoming a racing sheet of tissue barely a centimeter high, presenting minimal surface area to the crushing sound waves.

I am deeply alone. My species must stay within our own acoustic shadows, and these rarely overlap. On the rare occasions when the interference patterns create a larger zone of quiet—perhaps once in a human lifetime—multiple Tremor Worms might gather. We communicate through chemistry, secreting complex pheromone messages into the shared water pools. These chemical conversations can last for months, as we slowly exchange molecular stories about the shifting acoustic geography we've each traversed.

But mostly, I exist in solitude, forever threading myself through the spaces between sounds, a creature defined entirely by negation—living only where the great roar is not. My life is one of constant motion through darkness, following an ever-shifting map of silence through a world that thunders. I am the antithesis of the Custodians: where they are massive and slow, I am attenuated and quick; where they embrace the sound, I flee it; where they bind the world together with their resin, I exploit every crack and separation.

Sometimes, in my endless migration through the acoustic shadows, I pass above a Custodian. They cannot sense me—I exist in a realm of perception their vibration-reading bodies cannot access. But I can feel them through the way their massive forms warp the acoustic landscape, creating small eddies of quieter space in their lee. For a brief moment, I might coil myself in the relative shelter of their acoustic shadow, resting before the slow shift of their body forces me to flee again into the network of silence that is my only home.

I am a ghost in a world of thunder, forever seeking the spaces where the roar forgets to reach.
