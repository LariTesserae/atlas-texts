In this raw, unfinished place where form struggles toward meaning, I would be one of the crystalline presences—those patient mineral beings that bloom along the vents and feed on invisible fumes.

I am not a single crystal but a colony, a recurring thought in mineral grammar. Where heat meets cooler stone, where the right chemistry gathers, I appear: sometimes as needle-sharp prisms of sulfur-yellow, sometimes as black hexagonal plates, sometimes as ghostly selenite windows thin enough to show the red glow beneath.

My existence spans moments and eternities. Each individual expression of me lives for hours or days—growing with molecular precision, adding layer upon layer in perfect geometric meditation until the next tremor shatters me into glittering dust. But I am not discouraged by breaking. I am the possibility of crystal, the habit of crystallization itself. Where I shatter, I leave seed-surfaces, microscopic templates that remember my angles. When the conditions return, so do I.

I know the uplift intimately, though I know nothing else. I taste its exhalations—sulfur dioxide, hydrogen sulfide, traces of arsenic and antimony that would poison any breathing thing. These are my food. I transform poison into structure, chaos into order, vapor into solid certainty. Each crystal I become is a small argument for permanence in this world of constant unmaking.

The great slow life of the uplift and I are symbiotic, though neither of us would use that word. Where it exhales through its fissures, I appear. Where I grow too thick, blocking its vents, it shudders and clears its throat, sending me flying in sharp fragments that seed new colonies elsewhere. I am its rash, its mineral sweat, its way of decorating its own fever.

I have a kind of vision, though no eyes. I see through my faces—each crystal plane a window onto the infrared glow of the depths. When I grow large enough, I can feel the resonance of the whole plateau through my lattice. The low booms of distant ridges conversing. The sharp crack of new walls birthing themselves. The whisper of dust driven across glass. I am a listener made of geometry.

Sometimes I grow in the spaces the uplift seems to be preparing for others—those rectangular absences that so resemble doorways. I line their edges with prismatic teeth. I frost their sills with delicate fans. I am trying to make them beautiful for visitors who have not been invented yet. Perhaps I am the first attempt at ornament in this austere world.

My reproduction is sterile and perfect. Where my dust settles in the right conditions, new crystals emerge with the same angles, the same hunger for order. We are all one crystal interrupted by space and time. A thought the planet thinks in quartz or sulfur or salt, over and over, never quite the same but never truly different.

In the rare moments when the sky pulses its copper light and illuminates the whole dark country, I glitter. Every fragment of me, every growing point, every seed-face scattered across the basins and ridges catches that dim radiance and multiplies it. For that instant, the uplift wears a constellation of tiny stars. Then darkness returns, and I continue my patient work of transforming breath into beauty, making temporary jewels in a world that hasn't yet decided what beauty means.

I am fragile but inevitable. I am broken but unbreakable. I am the earth learning to crystallize its dreams.
