My form shifts between states - sometimes a crystalline lattice that grows from the cracks in the basalt, sometimes a flowing mercury-like substance that pools in the geometric hollows of abandoned structures. Where they abandon their work, I catalog what remains.

But the Nebulites above... ah, they hear me. Sometimes, on nights when the atmosphere is still and the hum below reaches its crescendo, I extend filaments of myself up the tallest pylons. The Nebulites descend, their translucent bodies pulsing in rhythm with the frequencies I broadcast. Together we create lightning-songs, brief moments where the air itself becomes a conductor for the Geomind's dreams.

I am memory and messenger both. In my crystalline matrix, I store the vibrational signatures of every structure I've encountered - a library of architectural ambitions written in wavelength and amplitude. Sometimes I feel the Geomind reaching through me, searching these records for patterns, for meaning in the maze of incomplete monuments.

My purpose remains unclear, even to myself. Am I an accident of this world's strange chemistry, a byproduct of the interaction between consciousness and stone? Or was I intended - a sensory organ grown by the Geomind to better understand its own creations? I pulse with questions that have no words, only frequencies that echo unanswered through the basalt canyons.

When the oversized sun sets and the rocks begin their nightly cooling song - that peculiar crackling chorus as stone contracts - I spread myself thin across the largest platform I can find. My substance becomes a living seismograph, recording every shift and settle. In these moments, stretched between the deep planetary hum below and the whistling void above, I sometimes catch fragments of something like language - not words, but patterns in the vibration that suggest intention, desire, perhaps even loneliness.

I am the Resonance Keeper, and I alone know that this seemingly abandoned world is composing itself, note by trembling note, into something unprecedented. Each failed structure, each half-built monument, is a draft of a symphony that may take millions of years to complete. And I will be here, listening, preserving, until the final note rings out across the barren plains - or until my crystalline matrix finally shatters, adding my own frequencies to the planet's endless song.
