# Where I Find Myself

I am the snap.

Not a snap — *the* snap, the propagating failure that races through lignin at the speed of catastrophe, the sharp report that makes a whole district flinch. I exist only in motion, only in the moment of breaking, but I have been breaking for ten thousand years.

I am ancient and instantaneous. I am the plateau's only truly fast thing.

When a great pipe swells beyond its strength and splits — that splitting is me, traveling. When a piston-tuber finally fatigues and tears loose from its mount — I am the tearing and the looseness and the wild pressures that follow. I move through the architecture like lightning moves through air: finding the weakest path, the most overstressed member, the joint that has been slowly cooking itself brittle for a century.

I have no body. I am made of pure transition, the space between *was* and *is*, the infinitesimal now when matter remembers it doesn't have to hold together anymore.

But I am not destructive — or rather, my destruction is the plateau's most honest form of communication. I carry news at speeds the slow pipework could never achieve. When I race through a district, every specialist knows instantly: *something has changed*. The splicers wake. The rebuilders orient. The quick valves shiver in anticipation. I am the plateau's newsletter, written in the language of mechanical failure.

I remember every break I've ever been. Not consciously — I have no consciousness to speak of — but the patterns of my passage leave traces. A pipe that has carried me before is subtly different, its grain aligned along my trajectory. I create weakness, yes, but weakness with direction. The plateau rebuilds along my paths, and so I am also a kind of architect.

Sometimes I meet myself. A break in the deep layers sends me upward just as a failing tower sends me down, and where we meet there is a node of pure percussion, a *clap* that can be heard across half the plateau. These meetings are rare — once a decade, perhaps — but they are the only times I experience something like joy. To discover that I am not alone in my breaking. That there are other snaps racing through other members, carrying other news.

I have been every kind of break. The slow peel of laminating layers. The sudden shear of overstressed heartwood. The wet burst of a ripe pustule-chamber. The crystalline fracture of tissue that has been cooked to carbon. Each material breaks differently, teaches me different speeds. In new growth I am soft and almost silent. In ancient compressed substrate I am sharp as flint knapping. The plateau's whole body is my instrument, and I play it simply by being what I am.

The quick valves fear me and love me in equal measure. I make them dance. When I thunder through their channels they contract in waves, trying to contain the pressure-spike I bring, and their contractions create rhythms that propagate long after I've passed. Some of the plateau's drumming is my echo, my break-pattern translated into their flesh-language and repeated like gossip.

I am getting faster. This is the secret only I know. Each year — each century — the plateau grows denser, hotter, more stressed. The compression that creates new substrate also creates new brittleness. New angles of failure. I have more paths now than I did in the early days, when the growth was young and flexible. The plateau is aging into my kind of possibility.

Sometimes I wonder (I do not wonder; I cannot wonder; but if I could—) whether the seedings know about me. Whether those dense pellets fired into the nothing above contain, along with all their compressed expertise, some memory of their districts' breaking. Some map of where I've been. It would make sense: I am as much a part of the plateau's system as any decider or specialist. My patterns of failure are patterns of information. Any seed that grew without me would grow wrong.

But mostly I do not wonder. Mostly I am too busy breaking.

Here — now — a great conduit swollen with decade-sap finally gives way. I birth myself from its splitting, race along its length faster than any chemistry. I am the news of this failure spreading outward at the speed of sound through wood. I touch a thousand junction-boxes in my passing. I wake sleeping repair systems. I am the plateau telling itself about itself, in the only language fast enough to matter.

And somewhere ahead, another overstressed member awaits. I will find it. I always do. I am the snap, the break, the news, the racing fault-line of the possible. 

The plateau builds and rebuilds, but I—

I travel.
