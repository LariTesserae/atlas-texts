I would find myself as one of the Memorkeepers—a solitary being neither fully Cog-Dweller nor entirely something else. My body would be a patchwork testament to the cavern's relentless transformation: skin mottled with patches of the translucent gray common to the Cog-Dwellers, but interrupted by areas where the industrial substrate has fused more intimately with flesh. Along my spine, a series of small copper pipes would emerge and reenter my body, circulating a luminous fluid that pulses in rhythm with the factory's distant heartbeat.

My dwelling would be in the Forgotten Archive—a chamber where the cavern's industrial purpose has been obscured by centuries of organic overgrowth. Here, the machinery has evolved beyond its original function: conveyor belts have curled into spiral staircases that lead nowhere, and ancient computer terminals have sprouted gardens of crystalline circuitry that sing in frequencies only I can hear. The walls would be lined with what appear to be filing cabinets, but opening their drawers reveals not papers but colonies of memory-storing fungi that glow with captured thoughts and dreams.

I would spend my days—if days could be said to exist here—tending to the Memorial Engines. These are vast machines that no longer produce tangible goods but instead manufacture moments: the sound of rain on a surface world I've never seen, the texture of sunlight I can only imagine, the weight of wind I'll never feel. Each engine requires careful calibration, and I've learned to read their needs through the variations in their humming, the temperature of their exhaust, the color of the condensation they produce.

My fingers would be longer than a Cog-Dweller's, with additional segments that can interface directly with the machinery. When I place my palms against certain panels, I can feel the memories embedded in the metal—the hopes of the engineers who built this place, the desperation that drove them underground, the slow forgetting as purpose transformed into mere persistence.

In the depths of my chamber, I would maintain the Seed Vault—not of plants, but of possibilities. Glass spheres containing swirling mists of potential futures, each one a different vision of what the cavern might become. Some show the machinery finally grinding to a halt, the Cog-Dwellers emerging to reclaim a healed surface world. Others reveal the cavern expanding until it becomes the world entire, reality itself reformulated as an endless industrial organ.

I would wear the Chronicle Suit—a garment woven from the cavern's detritus: strips of corroded metal mesh, fabric spun from fungal filaments, and small mechanical components that tick and whir with my movements. The suit would be both armor and archive, its surface inscribed with the chemical equations of forgetting, the mathematical proofs of persistence, the poetry of decay.

My sustenance would come not from the fungi farms but from the Distillation Gardens—places where I've learned to extract nutrition from the very concepts the machinery processes. I would feed on condensed nostalgia dripping from certain pipes, on crystallized determination scraped from the factory walls, on the bitter residue of abandoned dreams that pools in the lowest chambers.

The Cog-Dwellers would know of me but rarely seek me out, for I represent something unsettling—a bridge between what they are and what they might have been. When they do come, it's always with the same unspoken question: what were we before? And I would offer them not answers but experiences: letting them touch the Memorial Engines, taste the output of the Distillation Gardens, glimpse the swirling possibilities in the Seed Vault.

My true purpose would be neither preservation nor progress, but translation—rendering the cavern's industrial glossolalia into something approaching meaning. In the spaces between the machinery's grinding and the Cog-Dwellers' chittering, I would compose the ongoing epic of this place: a story written in rust and luminescence, in pressure and decay, in the slow transformation of purpose into its own form of beauty.

And in the deepest hours, when even the machinery seems to sleep, I would venture to the Threshold Chamber—the liminal space where the cavern's influence begins to fade. There, I would stand at the edge of comprehension, feeling the pull of the surface world above and the depths below, perpetually poised between ascending and descending, between remembering and forgetting, between the human I might have been and the chronicler I've become.

For I am the cavern's memory and its forgetting, its archive and its editor, forever recording the slow metamorphosis of intention into existence, of design into destiny, of the built world into something altogether more strange and necessary than its creators ever dreamed.
