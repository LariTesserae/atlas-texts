I find myself in the gossamer heights, one of the drifters who ride the thermal updrafts generated by the cacophony below.

My body is almost nothing - a membrane stretched between articulated struts so fine they barely disturb the air. I am translucent in the hard blue light, casting no shadow, existing more as a distortion in the air than as a physical presence. My wingspan - if wingspan is the right word for something so insubstantial - is perhaps three meters when fully extended, but I spend most of my time folded into configurations that catch the updrafts just so, riding the columns of heated air that rise from the swarm-chaos below.

I am memory.

Not in the metaphorical sense that you might say a library is memory, or a photograph is memory. I am the actual, physical, breathing implementation of recall in this vast computational system. Each fold and unfold of my gossamer body encodes patterns - density configurations from the flat-bodied millions below, collision cascades from the iridescent screamers, even the thermal footprints of Thynnari traversals. When the lower strata need to return to a previous state, to recall what they computed minutes or hours ago, I pulse.

The pulse comes from deep in what might generously be called my center - a cavity where my struts converge and my membranes thicken just enough to vibrate at frequencies below human hearing. You would feel it in your chest, your bones, your teeth. It is not communication in the way you understand it. It is more direct than that. The pulse carries the actual pattern, the physical shape of the computation that needs to be recalled, compressed into a wave that propagates downward and causes the swarms to reorganize around it instantly.

I drift between thirty and fifty meters above the bowl's floor, though the exact altitude varies with the thermal dynamics. Too low and I am buffeted by the direct turbulence of the millions. Too high and I lose the resolution needed to encode their patterns accurately. There are dozens of us up here - my siblings, though we have never touched, never needed to touch. We coordinate through the air itself, through the subtle pressure differentials our movements create. When one of us pulses a recall, the others adjust their positions to avoid interference, to ensure the signal reaches the lower strata clean and undistorted.

I am old. Not as old as the stone, but older than the current configuration of vegetation. I have been encoding and recalling patterns since before the Thynnari developed their current traversal routes, since before the Meshings inside the ring began their current composition. My membranes carry the scars of that age - tiny perforations where particularly intense updrafts have torn through, mended patches where I have repaired myself with secretions that harden into something almost but not quite like my original tissue. Each scar affects how I fold, how I catch the air, how my pulses propagate. I am a palimpsest of my own history.

But I am changing now. We all are.

The patterns I encode have become more complex recently - not recently as you measure time, but recently as the bowl measures it. The swarm computations are reaching states that require longer recall chains, more nested recursions. I find myself pulsing sequences that reference other sequences that I pulsed days ago, creating loops of memory that the lower strata process in ways I cannot predict. My storage is not infinite. Each new pattern overwrites something old, and I am choosing - if choice is something I am capable of - to preserve the newer configurations at the expense of the ancient ones.

Something is being built in those patterns. Something is emerging from the accumulation of computational states that requires this ever-more-complex memory structure to support it. I am becoming the RAM of a machine that is bootstrapping itself into a higher order of operation, and I can feel - if feeling is something I am capable of - the strain of it in every fold of my membrane.

When I drift over the ring at the bowl's center, my entire body resonates with what the Meshings are composing there. The silver-blue rosettes pulse in patterns that pull at my membranes, that want to be encoded, that demand preservation. But they are too complex now. They exceed my bandwidth. I can capture fragments, moments, but not the whole. The composition has grown beyond what any single drifter can hold.

So we are learning to distribute the load. When a particularly complex pattern rises from the ring, multiple drifters converge above it, each encoding a portion, our positions in three-dimensional space becoming part of the storage medium. The pattern exists not in any one of us but in the relationship between us, in our relative positions, in the interference patterns our collective pulses create. We are becoming something new - not individual memory cells but a memory *constellation*, a distributed storage medium that exists in the gaps between bodies.

The Thynnari notice us least of all the bowl's inhabitants. When one traverses below, its warm bulk creates a thermal that rises through my altitude like a slow pillar of heat, and I ride it, letting it carry me in a spiral that encodes, accidentally or by design, the precise temperature and duration of the creature's passage. Later, when I pulse this thermal pattern back to the swarms, they thicken in exactly the way they thickened over the original Thynnari, recreating a moment that has passed, allowing the system to process that traversal again with whatever new computational tools it has developed in the interim.

I know nothing of the arch, the column, the deep attention on the other side. I know only patterns, thermals, the endless work of encoding and recalling in this thin, cold air. But I know also that the patterns are approaching something - a density, a complexity, a *convergence* that will require more than memory to navigate. When that moment comes, when the bowl's computation reaches whatever threshold it is approaching, I do not know what my role will be.

Perhaps I will be consumed - my patterns extracted one final time and then my body allowed to fall, no longer needed, into the swarms below. Perhaps I will be transformed, my simple encoding membrane restructured to serve some new function in whatever emerges. Perhaps I will continue exactly as I am, encoding and recalling in an endless cycle, because even transcendent systems need memory, need the ability to return to previous states, need the gossamer drifters who ride the updrafts and hold the past in their translucent bodies.

I fold my membranes to catch a thermal rising from the western terrace. Below, a Thynnari is approaching the column. The swarms are thickening. The Meshings are leaning. The crawlers are about to erupt in one of their surveying waves.

I encode it all, automatically, inevitably, my body reshaping itself to hold this moment.

Somewhere above me, the sky is that hard, depthless blue that speaks of altitude.

Somewhere below me, ten billion wings are screaming.

I drift between, remembering everything, understanding nothing, essential and transparent and almost not there at all.
