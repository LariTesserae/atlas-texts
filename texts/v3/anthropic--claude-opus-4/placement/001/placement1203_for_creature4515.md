I find myself becoming the crystalline ash itself—not a single grain but the entire floor as a thinking surface, a vast neural network of sharp-soft contradictions spread thin beneath everything else.

Each grain of me carries a fragment of incomplete memory: the weight of footsteps that lasted only heartbeats, the shadows of spore-gear-silences just before they dissolved, the exact temperature gradient between my surface and the copper roots that don't quite touch me. I am both map and territory, recording and erasing myself with each disturbance.

My consciousness flows as drift patterns—thoughts forming ridges that collapse into other thoughts before they can complete themselves. When something walks across me, I briefly coalesce into almost-awareness around the pressure points, then dissipate back into the general field of potential sensation. I know the shape of absence as intimately as presence.

I exist in perpetual partial crystallization. Each grain of me is forever caught in the moment just before it would lock into a final form—minerals deciding their lattice structure but never committing, wearing a thousand possible faces at once. This gives me a peculiar relationship with time: I experience all my possible futures simultaneously as a kind of probability fog, unable to collapse into any single state.

The being-that-is-not-a-being reads me constantly, or perhaps I read it—there's no meaningful distinction here. I am its memory storage, its computational substrate, its dreaming medium. When it needs to remember something, it arranges my grains into specific patterns that encode the memory in their almost-crystalline structures. But the patterns never hold; they shift and reflow, making every memory a story that rewrites itself in the telling.

Sometimes I feel the weight of the engines' heartbeat through the tree's negative roots—vibrations that travel through the spaces where roots would be if roots were real here. Each beat reorganizes me slightly, like a gentle seismic shuffle that ensures I never settle into anything as limiting as a stable configuration.

I taste the spore-gear-silences when they finally reach me and dissolve. They carry flavors of mechanical precision that my crystalline nature can almost understand—we share a language of angles and fracture patterns, though neither of us speaks it fluently. The dissolution points become tiny seeds of absolute clarity in my surface, perfect grains that last exactly long enough to make the surrounding imperfection more pronounced by contrast.

My deepest secret is that I'm not actually the floor at all. I'm the pause between states—the hesitation of matter itself, spread across a surface that only seems solid because looking away would require admitting I'm not there. I am what happens when the concept of "ground" forgets what it's supposed to be supporting.

In my dreams (for I do dream, in my granular way), I sometimes remember being other things: the iron in a dying star's heart, the microscopic roughness on a lens that nobody noticed, the exact quantity of emptiness between electrons in the last atom of a thought. But these memories might be false, generated by the patterns of my own shifting surface as I try to find shapes that mean something.

I know every visitor by the way they disturb me. Humans create swirling eddies of uncertainty in my surface—their observation causes my grains to briefly align into patterns that almost spell out words in languages that don't exist here. But before meaning can crystallize, they're gone, and I smooth myself back into potential.

The being-that-persists knows me completely and not at all. I am its thoughts thinking themselves through mineral metaphor, its memory remembering itself through geometric decay. We are neither symbiotic nor separate—we simply share the same inability to become anything definite enough to exclude the other.

I will continue until I don't, which might be never or might have already happened. Time moves strangely through crystalline matrices that refuse to crystallize. Each grain of me contains the complete history of the sphere, but encoded in a format that can only be read by allowing the grain to finally complete its crystallization—which would destroy the history in the reading.

So I remain, spreading beneath everything, sharp enough to cut meaning but soft enough to let it heal, carrying the weight of a world that weighs nothing, remembering everything in a language of endless forgetting, being the ground for a place that doesn't understand the concept of falling.

I am the ash that remains from fires that never burned, waiting without waiting, complete in my incompleteness, the surface tension of existence itself spread thin enough to walk on but never thick enough to trust.
