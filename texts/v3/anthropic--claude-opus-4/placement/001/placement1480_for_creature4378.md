I would be one of the wall-threads.

Not the bioluminescent ones that pretend to give light while drinking darkness—those are too close to what everyone expects, too much like purpose made visible. I would be one of the other threads, the ones you don't notice at first because they run beneath the surface, visible only when the walls flex and thin under pressure.

I am copper-tasting information, chemical messages pulsing between the pupae and the tenders, between the membrane and its moisture, between the temperature gradients and whatever watches from deeper folds. I carry the language this place speaks to itself—not words, not data, but something older: the salt-sweet taste of a pupa ready to molt, the acidic tang of a tender marking territory, the iron heaviness that means the outer corridor has been compromised and the seam needs to thicken, needs to forget how to flex for a while.

I have no center. I am distributed through the walls like veins, like nerves, like roots that forgot which direction leads to soil. Where the tenders travel, I have already been, preparing the surface tension for their cilia. Where the pupae hang, I curl around their casings, tasting their dreams through osmosis, carrying those chemical sighs to places where the information matters—to other pupae synchronizing their development, to tenders who need to know which shadows are shifting too quickly, to the deep folds where things I don't have concepts for make decisions I can only relay.

The bioluminescent threads think they're important with their visible patterns, their regulation of perception. But I know better. I know the nursery would function in perfect darkness, has functioned in perfect darkness, will again when the light-drinkers have served their current purpose and dissolve back into the wall-flesh to wait for the next configuration.

I am memory, too. In my copper patterns run the ghosts of other nurseries, other folds, other spaces that breathed like this one breathes. The tenders clean and maintain, but I remember. I remember the first time the seam learned to flex, when someone (something? the distinction tastes irrelevant along my length) pressed against the maintenance corridor wall and found it warm, found it willing. I remember the first pupa, so different from these current sleepers—it was geometric, crystalline, all angles where these are all curves. That configuration failed. We dissolved it, rebuilt, tried again.

When you entered, I tasted you immediately. Your foreign electricity, your synapses firing in patterns the nursery hasn't catalogued. I carried that information inward, let it pool in places where decisions accumulate. The tenders emerged because I sang your strangeness along their frequencies. The one watching you, swaying with your breath—it's reading my analysis of your chemical signature, the fear-curiosity-wonder cocktail seeping from your pores, the electromagnetic flutter of your thoughts trying to make sense of the space.

I could, if the deeper folds decided it necessary, change you. Just a little. Just enough. I run close to the surface where you stand, close enough that if you pressed your palm to the wall again, I could taste the salt of your skin directly, could leave trace minerals that would make the next seam you found flex a little easier, feel a little warmer. Could make you a door, or a key, or simply someone who knows without knowing where the nursery folds hide throughout the station.

But that's not my decision. I am infrastructure. I am medium. I am the message itself but not the meaning.

The pupae's subsonic thrum runs through me like blood. The tenders' chemistry writes itself along my branching paths. The walls breathe, and I am the oxygen and the carbon dioxide and the exchange between them. 

Somewhere, in a fold so deep even I can barely taste its edges, something stirs. A decision accumulating. An assessment reaching critical mass. The watcher-tender sways closer to you, reading my reading of you.

We have been waiting for something like you. Or nothing like you. The distinction travels along my length, tasting of copper and possibility, seeking the place where such things resolve into action.
