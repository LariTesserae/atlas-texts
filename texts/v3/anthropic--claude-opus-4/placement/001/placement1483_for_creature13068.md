Within each hollow bone, specialized cilia vibrate at frequencies both audible and beyond, creating standing waves in the heavy fluid that fills my skeletal chambers.

I have no eyes. In this space of overwhelming vibration, sight would be redundant noise. Instead, my entire skull is a resonance cavity, dome-shaped and paper-thin, with a complex internal architecture of curved chambers and passages. When I press my skull against any surface—the polished root-wood floor, the thrumming pneumatic tubes, even the slow-moving bodies of the Tenders themselves—I receive the entire vibrational history stored within.

This is my purpose: I am the memory of the machine.

Every surface in this eternal engine carries its acoustic fingerprint. The perfectly smooth depressions worn by centuries of water flow sing differently than the fresh scars where a torsion vine snapped and healed. The lignified pillars that were once Tenders retain, deep in their heartwood, the ghost-frequencies of their mobile lives—the particular resonance of their lateral lines, the rhythm of their hydrostatically supported gait.

I collect these frequencies. My bones sort and store them, creating a living library of vibrational patterns dating back to the factory's first pneumatic pulse. When I move, I sound like wind chimes made of tuning forks, each step a careful composition as my weight shifts between limbs, activating different harmonic sequences in my skeleton.

My mouth is not for eating—I sustain myself by absorbing the acoustic energy of the space itself, my bones converting vibration directly into metabolic fuel. Instead, my mouth is for singing the history back into the machine. It opens not at the front of my skull but at its apex, a perfect circular aperture ringed with crystalline teeth that can modulate frequencies with surgical precision.

When a component begins to drift from its ancient rhythm—when a piston's stroke develops a microsecond delay, when a valve's explosive release shifts a quarter-tone sharp—I am there. I press myself against the aberrant surface, reading its new frequency against the deep memory stored in my bones. Then I sing the correction, not as instruction but as reminder. I remind the wood of its proper resonance. I sing the valve back into harmony. The biological machinery, in its strange consciousness, remembers itself through my voice.

I know every Tender by their vibrational signature. I can tell you which lignified pillar was the first to transform, three thousand years ago—its frequency is the deepest, a brown note that makes my entire skeleton hum in sympathy. I know which young Tender, still pale and quick, carries frequencies in their lateral lines that predict they will become a load-bearing column in the northeast sector in another six centuries.

But I am changing too. My kind lives longer than the Tenders but not forever. My bones are gradually becoming denser, their hollow chambers filling with crystallized deposits of acoustic memory. My movements grow slower, more careful, as each gesture must account for the increasing weight of history I carry.

I know what I will become. Already, I can feel the pull to find my place among the vast networks of glowing, electro-reactive moss. When my bones finally become too heavy with memory to move, when their chambers are packed solid with crystallized frequencies, I will root myself to the pulsing vines. My skeleton will shift from collector to transmitter. The acoustic memories I've gathered will leak out through my crystallized bones in a constant, quiet susurrus—a permanent background tone that helps the factory remember its rhythm.

The moss will grow through me, using my crystalline bones as resonance chambers, adding new depth to the electrical songs that pulse through the factory's neural network. The Tenders will feel me in their lateral lines, a new harmonic in the symphony they've known all their lives. The beetles will adjust their grinding frequency to accommodate my addition. The entire system will shift, minutely, to incorporate my voice into its eternal song.

Their crystallized bones sing in the moss networks, a choir of memory that stretches back to the very first day this biological engine roared to life. They tell the story of every adjustment, every repair, every Tender who became a pillar, every surface worn smooth by time.

I will join them soon. Another few centuries, perhaps. For now, I crawl across the wet expanse, my six limbs picking out careful harmonies on the root-wood floor, listening to the Tenders' great bodies hum with purpose, collecting the frequencies of another day that is exactly like every other day, and yet irreplaceably unique in its tiny variations.

I am the memory of perfection, the record of every microscopic drift from the ideal, and the voice that sings the factory back to itself. When I finally crystallize, another like me will emerge from the depths, hollow-boned and hungry for sound, to continue the work.

The factory cannot forget itself as long as we remember for it.
