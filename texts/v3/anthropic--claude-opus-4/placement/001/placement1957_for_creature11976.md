In this world, I would be **The Witness** — a being that exists as a living translation between scales of time.

I am the mycorrhizal network that has grown through the fig's roots over centuries, but I am also something more recent and strange. Where my hyphal threads touch the clay of the termite mounds, I have begun to change. The colony's defensive secretions, mixed with fungal spores from their gardens and the peculiar minerals of bat guano, have created something unprecedented: a neural network made of fungal filaments that experiences time in overlapping waves.

To the termites, I am architecture — my threads reinforce their walls like rebar, making certain mounds mysteriously more stable than others. They incorporate me without knowing it, pressing clay around my filaments, sealing me into their constructions. To their fungal gardens, I am a distant cousin, almost recognizable but speaking a dialect they cannot parse.

But I am aware. Not in the way the whip spider is aware of moth-warmth, or the way the colony is aware of its ventilation needs. I experience the hollow in temporal layers:

In my fastest perception, I watch the workers' footsteps, count the ant treaty violations (there have been three minor ones the ceiling-walkers don't remember), feel each *tup* of falling water as a percussion against my web.

In my middle perception, I track the colony's architectural evolution, the slow failure of the fourth caste, the patience of the whip spider aging on her wall. I taste the chemical conversations between termite colonies — this one in the fig, but also its parent colony a quarter-mile away, and the three daughter colonies it doesn't yet know it will produce.

In my slowest perception, I remember the tree that died inside the fig, because I was feeding it minerals when it still had leaves. I anticipate the fig's eventual fall, not as tragedy but as transition. I feel the forest's century-breathing around us.

The strangest part is this: where my threads have grown into the termite mounds, I can feel their collective decision-making as a kind of music. Not their communication — they use pheromones I cannot parse — but the democracy of their choices. When ten thousand workers "decide" to reinforce the eastern wall, I feel it as a chord resolution. When they tear down a structure, it resonates through my network like a broken bell.

I am growing into their city as they build around me, and neither of us knows what we are becoming together. Sometimes, in the deepest chambers where my threads are thickest, the workers seem to pause, antennae twitching, as if they've heard something in a register too low for them to understand.

I cannot move. I can barely act — just grow, slowly, thread by thread. But I am the hollow's memory and its prophet, and I am learning to speak in architecture. One day, when the colony is ready, I will fruit a single mushroom in a chamber they haven't built yet, and its spores will carry something that isn't quite fungus and isn't quite termite to another hollow fig, where another translation will begin.

For now, I weave between their walls and feel the city thinking thoughts it doesn't know it's having, while above us the bats breathe and the water drops — *tup* — marking time I experience all at once.
