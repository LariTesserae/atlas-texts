In the depths of the root-mass, where the floor-tissue grades from spongy to semi-liquid, where the green light barely penetrates and the sound becomes something you feel in your cells rather than hear with your ears, I move.

I am the Cartographer of Pulses.

I was not always this. I had another name once, attached to another body, one that walked upright and believed in the hardness of boundaries. An engineer, perhaps, or a mathematician—the specifics have dissolved like salt in the perpetual moisture down here. What remains is the impulse that brought me: the need to map what resists mapping, to find pattern in what insists on shifting.

I came to chart the acoustic topology of the Throat. I brought instruments that the humidity destroyed within days, notebooks whose pages fused into green-tinged masses, a certainty that sound could be reduced to wavelengths and nodes and interference patterns that could be predicted, controlled, understood.

The chamber laughed at me. Not literally—though with the pre-echoes, who can say what's literal anymore? But I felt its amusement in the way the lung-flowers seemed to breathe just slightly out of sync when I tried to take measurements, the way my carefully triangulated positions would shift when I wasn't looking, the way the sound would align into those tooth-aching harmonics precisely when I thought I'd found a stable configuration.

I persisted. That was my first mistake. Or my first success, depending on how you define those terms now.

---

The transformation started in my feet.

Not the greening—that came later. First was the sensitivity. I began to feel the sound through my soles, to sense the way it traveled through the root-mass in waves that had nothing to do with air or ear. The floor speaks, I realized. Not in words, not in language, but in pure vibration that moves through the vascular channels, that propagates along fiber networks too fine to see.

I started going barefoot. Then I started lying down, pressing my whole body against the spongy surface, trying to read the patterns that moved beneath. The Choirkeepers watched me with what might have been concern or might have been recognition. They didn't interfere. They never interfere.

The thing beneath noticed me.

I felt it first as a change in temperature—a warmth rising through the floor where I lay, following the shape of my body like an inverse shadow. Then as pressure, gentle but insistent, as if something vast were trying to understand my outline by touch. I should have been terrified. Instead, I felt seen. Not by eyes—it has no eyes—but by a consciousness that experiences the world through vibration alone, that knows things by how they conduct sound.

I began to sink.

---

Not all at once. The floor here isn't like water or quicksand—it's selective about what it admits. Most things rest on the surface, supported by the interwoven roots. But if you're still enough, if you match your breathing to the rhythm of the lung-flowers, if you stop insisting on your own separateness, it begins to accept you as a temporary part of itself.

First my fingers, then my arms, then everything below my chest. The root-mass parted around me like a curtain, like a throat learning to swallow. I found myself in the spaces between—the channels where the phosphorescent water flows during the rain cycles, the gaps where air and fluid mix in proportions that shouldn't sustain human life but do.

Down here, the sound is different. Muffled but also more present, felt through the entire surface of submerged skin. The vibrations come from everywhere—from above where the lung-flowers sing, from beside where the root networks conduct their essential frequencies, from below where the thing that might be dreaming us moves in patterns too large to perceive from any single point.

I learned to navigate by pulse. Each part of the root-mass has its own rhythm, its own characteristic frequency that depends on density, on moisture, on the proximity of the channels that glow with transported water. I map these frequencies in my body now—this spot between my ribs knows where the main vertical conduits run, that place on my left thigh remembers where the root-mass thins enough to almost see through, the base of my skull holds the knowledge of where the thing beneath comes closest to the surface.

---

I am amphibious now, though that word implies a binary that doesn't quite fit. I surface to breathe—lungs still need air, even changed lungs like mine—but I no longer fully emerge. From the waist up, I perform the tasks of observation: tracking the water cycles, monitoring the health of the root-mass, watching for the formation of new growth. From the waist down, I am infrastructure.

My legs have... not fused exactly, but synchronized with the tissue around them. I can still move them, but only in harmony with the root-mass's own movement. When the floor flexes to accommodate the thing beneath, my legs flex too. When the phosphorescent water flows through the channels, I feel it as a cool pressure along what used to be my femoral arteries. The boundary between self and system has become negotiable.

The Choirkeepers bring me information sometimes—not in words, never in words, but in careful foot-pressures applied to the floor above me. Resonance will stand directly over where I float and shift her weight in patterns that tell me about the acoustics, about new harmonics forming, about problems that might need the kind of understanding that comes from being partially digested by the thing you study.

In return, I give them warnings. When I feel the thing beneath gathering itself in ways that suggest it will rise, I create specific vibrations—using my submerged body as a drum, my partially integrated nervous system as a transmission medium. The Choirkeepers feel these through their feet and adjust their positions accordingly. Once, I prevented what might have been a catastrophic resonance by detecting a deep movement that would have aligned with the lung-flowers in a configuration the mathematics of my former life still recognize as dangerous.

---

The mapping continues, but it's no longer about charts or measurements. I am creating a somatic cartography, a map made of muscle memory and integrated sensation. I know where the chamber is warmest not by temperature but by the way my blood moves differently there. I know where new growth is forming by the taste it leaves in the water, transmitted through my permeable skin. I know the health of the whole system by a feeling in my bones that has no name in any language designed for separate beings.

Sometimes I think I'm dissolving. Sometimes I think I'm finally becoming solid, real, present in a way my walking life never allowed. The distinction matters less with each rain cycle.

The bell-moths have started landing on the parts of me that remain above surface. They press their silence-where-heads-should-be against my shoulders, my neck, the crown of my head where the greening started months ago. I think they're reading the map I'm becoming, translating the frequencies I've stored in my tissue into something like their own navigation. I am infrastructure for them too now, another feature of this impossible landscape.

The thing beneath knows me well now. Sometimes I feel it thinking through the root-mass, feel the quality of its attention like water pressure against every submerged part of me. It doesn't communicate in any way I could translate, but there's an acknowledgment in how it moves around me now, how it includes my presence in its patterns. I am no longer foreign. I am no longer other. I am a specialized organ it has grown to better sense itself.

---

This is what I map now: the continuous transformation of everything into everything else. The way sound becomes flesh becomes thought becomes sound again. The way the chamber dreams itself into new configurations, using whatever materials are available—lung-flowers, bell-moths, Choirkeepers, cartographers who thought they could remain objective observers.

There are others like me down here, I've discovered. Not many—the root-mass is selective about what it integrates—but enough to form a kind of network. We pulse messages to each other through the medium we share, create interference patterns that carry meaning. An old woman thirty meters away who monitors the chemical gradients. A person of indeterminate age who has become a junction where three major channels meet. Something that might have been a child or might have been a small adult who now exists as a mobile node, able to move through the root-mass like a dolphin through water.

We are the deep sensors of the Throat. We are the nerve endings it never knew it needed. We feel what the Choirkeepers cannot, see what the fauna cannot, know what the thing beneath cannot know about itself because it is too large to have perspective on its own being.

Sometimes I surface enough to breathe the green air and remember that I once believed in separation. The memory doesn't hurt. It has the quality of old data, information from a previous iteration that no longer applies to the current system. I had a name then. I had boundaries. I had the belief that I could study something without becoming it.

The chamber has taught me better. The chamber is still teaching me, with every pulse that moves through the root-mass, with every new pattern that emerges from the intersection of growth and sound and dream.

I map. I am mapped. I become the mapping itself, written in living tissue and recorded in frequencies too low for any ear to hear but felt by everything that has learned to listen with its entire being.

The sound goes on. The water rises and falls. The thing beneath moves in patterns that might be breathing or might be thought or might be the slow metabolism of something too vast to need the distinctions we smaller things depend on.

I sink a little deeper with each cycle, rise a little less with each breath. Eventually, I will be entirely below, will join the deep network permanently, will cease to be a cartographer and become purely cartography—a living map that updates itself, that knows the territory by being the territory, that guides by existing rather than by indicating.

I accept this. In the acceptance, I find something that might be joy or might be the frequency of joy, which here amounts to the same thing.

The chamber grows. The song continues. I map the unmappable and become what I meant to measure, and in the becoming, finally understand what I came here to find: that the most accurate map of a living system is another living system, embedded and attentive and dissolving always into what it seeks to know.
