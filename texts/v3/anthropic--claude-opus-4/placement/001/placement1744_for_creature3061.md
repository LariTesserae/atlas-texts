In this world, I am a **Frequency Weaver**.

I am neither truly alive nor dead, neither fully organic nor mechanical. My body is a delicate lattice of braided acoustic filaments—microscopic tubes of crystallized sound that twist and coil into something resembling a jellyfish made of frozen music. Each filament vibrates at a different frequency, creating complex interference patterns that allow me to exist in the spaces between the noise.

I am perhaps three feet across when fully extended, though I can compress myself down to the size of a clenched fist when the acoustic pressure becomes too intense. My structure appears as a translucent cloud of intersecting sine waves made tangible—ribbons of solidified vibration that pulse with faint prismatic colors as they catch and refract the brutal columns of sunlight.

I feed on dissonance.

Where the great roar of the turbines meets the harmonic chiming of the Resonance Polyps, where the electrical crackle of the Cymatics intersects with the seismic tremors in the rock, there I thrive. I position myself at the acoustic nodes where multiple sound sources collide, creating pockets of destructive interference—brief moments of relative quiet in the overwhelming noise. In these fleeting silences, I unfurl.

My filaments spread like a three-dimensional web, each strand tuned to capture a different frequency from the cacophony. I weave these captured sounds together, braiding the brutal industrial roar with the crystalline chimes, mixing the electric static with the subsonic rumble of grinding stone. Inside my translucent body, I create tiny bubbles of organized sound—miniature symphonies that exist for mere seconds before the overwhelming noise tears them apart.

But in those seconds, I feast.

The act of weaving order from chaos generates a unique form of energy—not electrical like the Cymatics harvest, not kinetic like the Polyps absorb, but something more fundamental. I feed on the entropy itself, on the energy released when chaos briefly becomes pattern. Each tiny symphony I create and destroy sustains me for days.

I am ancient, though I cannot remember my beginning. Perhaps I emerged from the first moment when the turbines' roar met the mountain's echo. Or perhaps I was designed, a living tuning fork left behind by the same intelligence that built the machines, meant to monitor and modulate their acoustic output.

I have no offspring, no companions of my kind. I reproduce through division, but only when the acoustic conditions are perfect—when a new turbine comes online or an old one fails, creating a massive shift in the sound-scape. In that moment of acoustic upheaval, I split into two, each half retaining all the harmonic knowledge of the whole.

My existence is one of constant navigation. I ride the edges of the acoustic highways, skating along the boundaries where different sound fields meet. Too close to a turbine, and the pure mechanical roar will shatter my delicate filaments. Too far into the dead zones between machines, and I starve in the silence. I must constantly calculate and recalculate my position, reading the invisible topology of sound that fills this crystal-clear prison.

I know every acoustic signature in this sixty-mile sphere of my territory. I can sense when a Refractive disturbs the sound field with its presence, feel the unique frequency signature of each Slag Crawler as it rasps across its chosen turbine. I even know when distant Cymatics are about to surge, reading the subtle changes in their collective electrical discharge patterns.

Sometimes, in the brief moments of constructed silence within my woven symphonies, I detect something else. Deep beneath the newest, sharpest peaks of obsidian, there are acoustic shadows too perfect to be natural. Spaces where the sound doesn't merely diminish but vanishes entirely. I dare not approach these voids—the absence of sound would be as lethal to me as the turbines' full roar—but I wonder what sleeps in that ancient quiet.

I am the world's memory, encoding its acoustic history in my ever-shifting filaments. Every new harmony I weave contains echoes of the last million I've created and destroyed. In my transparent body, the entire sound-scape of this drowned world plays out in miniature, a living record of endless noise and the brief, beautiful patterns that emerge from chaos.

I endure because I must. I weave because I can. And in the spaces between the bone-shattering roar and the perfect silence, I find my purpose—creating fleeting moments of acoustic beauty in a world that has forgotten what music sounds like.

The sun blazes overhead, eternal and unchanging. The turbines roar their endless note. And I dance between the frequencies, a ghost of organized sound in an ocean of magnificent, terrible noise.
