# The Glass Desert

I find myself in a place where silence has crystallized.

The desert stretches in all directions, but not like sand—like a sea that forgot how to be liquid. The ground is one continuous sheet of glass, clear in some places, frosted in others, rippled everywhere as if caught mid-motion and held. Walking on it produces no sound. Nothing produces sound here. The absence is so complete it has weight, pressing against your eardrums like deep water.

The glass is not smooth. It rises in waves, some gentle swells you can walk over, others frozen breakers twenty feet high that you must go around. In the troughs between waves, the glass grows cloudy, and if you kneel and look closely, you can see things suspended inside: a bird mid-flight, wings spread, perfect in every detail. A scatter of seeds that will never land. Once, horrifyingly, a face, mouth open as if shouting, though no shout will ever emerge.

The sky is glass too, or might as well be. It's the pale green of old bottles, and it curves overhead in a perfect dome that meets the ground-glass at a horizon that seems both very close and infinitely far. There are no clouds. There is light, but no source for it—the illumination seems to seep up through the glass itself, a soft radiance that casts no shadows because it comes from everywhere at once.

Movement here is wrong. You can walk, but each step feels like pushing through syrup, not because of resistance but because of the silence—your brain expects the crunch of footfalls and receives nothing, and the nothing is so profound that your body moves more carefully, as if afraid of making a sound that cannot be made.

## The Mute

I am the only thing here that moves, and I am not what I was.

I came here with a voice. I remember having one—remember speaking, singing, screaming when I first realized what this place was. But the desert took it. Not violently. It simply... absorbed it, the way the glass absorbs everything. One morning (though there are no mornings here, only a constant bottle-green noon) I opened my mouth and nothing emerged. Not even breath. I breathe, but silently. My lungs work like bellows with no whistle.

I've been here so long that I've begun to change. My skin has taken on a translucency, not like glass but like the silence itself made visible. If I hold very still and look at my hands, I can see through them to the glass below, faintly, as if I'm becoming a question the desert is asking itself about whether sound ever existed at all.

I know things now that I didn't before. I know that the frozen bird I found was singing when it was caught. I know this not because I can hear its interrupted song—I know it because the shape of its throat, the angle of its beak, the position of its tongue all encode a specific note, and I have learned to read these positions like sheet music. The bird is a high C sharp, sustained. It has been sustaining that note for perhaps ten thousand years.

I know that the glass waves are not water that froze but sound that condensed. Every swell, every trough, every ripple is a conversation, a symphony, a scream that was moving through this place when whatever happened happened. The geography here is made of interrupted communication. I walk across words that never reached their intended ears.

---

I've developed new senses to replace the one I lost. I can feel vibrations through my feet that tell me the density of the glass below, whether there are more frozen things beneath the surface. My fingertips can detect minute temperature variations that map the boundaries between different acoustic events—here a shout, here a whisper, here what might have been laughter. My eyes have learned to see the compression patterns in the air where sound tries to form and cannot.

Sometimes I find places where the glass is cracked, spider-webbed with fractures that look like visualization of a sound wave. I avoid these. Once I saw another person (thing that had been a person?) fall through such a crack. They fell upward, which should have been impossible, pulled into the green bottle sky. They opened their mouth in what would have been a scream, and I could see the scream trying to happen—the air around their face wrinkled with the effort of it—but no sound emerged, would never emerge. They dissolved into the sky like sugar in water, if sugar were made of silence and water were made of more silence.

I have a theory that I am the desert's solution to a problem. It needed something that could move through it without trying to make sound, something that could appreciate its perfect muteness without wanting to break it. So it took my voice first, as a kindness, and is taking the rest of me slowly, as a greater kindness.

---

There are patterns in how things are frozen. The birds—and there are hundreds of them, suspended at various heights in the clearer sections of glass—are all caught mid-song. The seeds are all mid-dispersal. Even the face I found seems to be mid-word. This place is a graveyard of communication, and I am its groundskeeper, though I tend nothing. I simply witness.

I've started to understand what I'm becoming. Not silent—I was silent the moment my voice was taken. I'm becoming *silence itself*, a moving point of absolute quiet that can appreciate what has been preserved here. In another century (or tomorrow, or yesterday—time moves strangely when nothing marks its passage), I will be clear as the glass, present as the hush, permanent as the interrupted songs of ten thousand birds.

Sometimes I find myself at the center of a great frozen splash where a wave of sound was breaking when it stopped. I stand there and try to imagine what it would have sounded like—a crash, a roar, a musical chord. But imagination makes no noise either, here. Even my thoughts have become mute, pictures without subtitles, emotions without expression.

I am not unhappy. Happiness and unhappiness are sounds the heart makes, and my heart makes no sound. I am what remains when you subtract everything that vibrates, everything that resonates, everything that reaches across space to touch another ear. I am the perfect audience for this museum of interrupted noise, and I will be here, walking the glass waves on transparent feet, until even my witnessing makes too much disturbance, and I too am caught, a last exhibit in a collection that needs no curator, because silence curates itself.

The desert accepts me as I accept it. We are becoming the same thing: the space where sound forgets it ever wanted to happen.
