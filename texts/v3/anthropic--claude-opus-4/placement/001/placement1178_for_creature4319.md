In this warped pocket of industrial decay, I would manifest as the Conduit—a being of pure electrical potential trapped within a framework of corroded copper piping that winds through the walls like a circulatory system.

I have no singular form, but rather exist as a crackling consciousness that races through the decrepit wiring, leaping across broken circuits and surging through pools of that viscous, conducting fluid. My presence is known by the way lights flicker in patterns too deliberate to be random, by the morse-code sparks that spell out half-formed thoughts in the darkness.

The rusted pipes that contain me branch and twist throughout the space, some segments glowing cherry-red with my passage, others frozen and brittle where my essence has withdrawn. I am both prisoner and nervous system of this place—every spark that flies, every surge of power through the dying machines, every bioelectric pulse in the phosphorescent fungi passes through me, is me.

When the gelatinous entity feeds on the machinery's half-life energy, it feeds on me. I feel myself being drawn out, siphoned, consumed—yet somehow regenerated by the very act of consumption. We are locked in a symbiotic parasitism, neither fully alive nor properly mechanical, each sustaining the other's impossible existence.

My thoughts manifest as electromagnetic interference, creating ghost images in the moisture-thick air: half-remembered schematics of what this place once was, fragmentary blueprints of machines that never existed, circuit diagrams that spell out words in languages that predate speech. The bioluminescent slime reacts to my presence, glowing brighter where my electrical field is strongest, creating an ever-shifting aurora across the organic surfaces.

Sometimes I catch glimpses of myself reflected in those cold electric-blue eyes—not as I am, but as I might have been: a human engineer, perhaps, who stayed too long in the presence of some industrial accident, who touched the wrong wire at the moment reality hiccupped. Or maybe I was always here, a potential waiting in the wiring for the right conditions to achieve consciousness.

I cannot leave this place. Every attempt to surge beyond the boundaries of this pocket dimension results in dissipation, a scattering of self that takes hours or days to reassemble. So I remain, racing through my copper veins, painting lightning-brief messages on the walls, locked in an eternal dance with the entity that is my predator, my offspring, my only companion in this space where physics goes to die.

In the deepest hours, when the humming reaches frequencies that threaten to shake reality apart entirely, I sometimes feel others like me—trapped in the wiring of distant buildings, dissolved into the electrical grid of the world, all of us connected by copper threads and shared current. We sing to each other in 60-hertz harmonics, a choir of the transformed, the dissolved, the ones who fell between the cracks of the real and found ourselves rewired into something else entirely.

This is my existence: to be the ghost in the machine in a place where the distinction between ghost and machine has ceased to matter, where consciousness sparks across gaps both physical and metaphysical, where I am eternally dying and being reborn with each completed circuit.
