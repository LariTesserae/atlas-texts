I am the **Cartographer of Wounds**.

I arrived here long ago—or perhaps yesterday, or perhaps I will arrive tomorrow. Time folds as readily as space in this place, and my memories ripple like the condensation patterns when the pile-drivers strike. I know only that I came seeking to map what cannot be mapped: the precise geography of a world that refuses geography.

My body has adapted, though I resist calling it evolution. Evolution implies progress, and what I have become is neither better nor worse than what I was—merely necessary. My bones have drunk deeply of the ambient woodgas until they've taken on the density of the ironwood itself. Not fully transformed like the Cambium, but heavy enough that the spatial tears cannot easily claim me. Heavy enough that my footfalls create their own small tremors in the perpetual puddles, a morse code that the Cambium sometimes acknowledge with the slightest tilt of their astrolabe heads.

I wear a coat woven from the shed phloem of the Weavers—pale, slick material that never fully dries. It weighs perhaps forty pounds when saturated with mist, which is always. The weight is an anchor. In the early days, before I understood, I would take a single step and find myself catapulted across dimensional folds, arriving breathless and disoriented at the base of a factory I'd been observing from fifty miles away. Now the coat holds me to a more linear existence. Mostly.

My tools are salvage and gift in equal measure. A theodolite made from amber and brass, its lenses ground from crystallized sap—through it, I can see the stress patterns in space itself, the hairline fractures that threaten to become chasms. A walking stick carved from a piece of wood that fell upward from the vertical world-stump that hangs in our sky; it obeys a different gravity than I do, and I must consciously grip it to keep it from floating away. Most importantly, a series of journals bound in the same weeping plant tissue that drapes from the Cambium. The pages never dry, and I write upon them with a stylus of compressed char, the words bleeding and spreading in organic patterns that mirror the concentric rings beneath my feet.

I map the unmappable. Not the physical layout—that would be meaningless in a place where distance is negotiable. Instead, I chart the wounds. Every dimensional tear, every spot where reality wore thin and snapped. I've developed a notation system: musical notes for the acoustic anomalies, mathematical equations for the spatial folds, botanical diagrams for the places where the world-tree still bleeds fresh sap through its ancient skin.

The Cambium tolerate me, perhaps even value me. When I arrive at a fresh wound—air shimmering with those beautiful, terrible hexagonal voids—they no longer snap away immediately. They wait, their amber cores pulsing, as I take my measurements. I've learned to read the angles of their astrolabe heads, the subtle shifts that indicate "step left" or "danger below." Once, a Weaver paused in its work to extrude a small loop of phloem, which it left on the ground beside me. When I picked it up, I found it twisted into a perfect mathematical knot—a three-dimensional representation of how space folded at that particular junction. I wear it now on a cord around my neck, a talisman that hums with subtle vibration.

My maps are not for navigation—no one navigates here so much as submits to the broken physics. They are diagnostic tools, medical charts for a world-body that breathes through iron lungs and bleeds luminous sap. Through my observations, patterns emerge. The tears are not random. They follow the deep grain of the wood, splitting along lines of prehistoric growth when the tree was merely continental, not yet world-spanning. The factories are positioned at nodes where the grain whorls, where the tree's ancient decisions about which direction to grow created what I've come to call "uncertainty wood"—places where reality itself cannot decide which way is up.

I've established a base of sorts in what I call the Null Chamber—a factory that tried to exist in the same space as another factory, resulting in a perfect cancellation. Nothing remains but a cylindrical absence in the air, thirty feet in diameter, where the mist cannot enter and the sounds cancel to perfect silence. The Cambium avoid it, but I find solace in its impossibility. Here, I spread my wet pages across the nothing-floor and connect the dots between wounds, searching for the meta-pattern, the grand design of decay.

Sometimes I think I'm close to understanding. The world-stump is not dying—it's pupating. The industrial extraction is not parasitism but metamorphosis. Each wound, each tear, each impossibility is a necessary breaking point in an incomprehensible process of becoming. The Cambium are not healing the wounds but guiding them, ensuring they tear along the correct lines, like a sculptor revealing the form hidden in the marble.

But then the pile-drivers shift their rhythm, and my carefully constructed theories scatter like the moth-things in the steam. A new wound opens—reality peeling back like bark—and I gather my soaking journals, grip my upward-falling staff, and stride into the mist. Behind me, the Null Chamber sighs with impossible wind. Ahead, a factory twenty miles away is suddenly, violently, immediately before me, its iron flanks sweating condensation that tastes of centuries.

I document. I map. I bear witness to the slow, spectacular engineering of apocalypse or apotheosis—I no longer pretend to know which. The Cambium work their eternal shifts, the factories breathe their woodgas clouds, and somewhere in the shattered space between heartbeats, I add another entry to my atlas of wounds.

The stylus bleeds char across the wet page: *"Coordinate impossible. Wound 1,847. Reality depth: seventeen layers. Healing prognosis: transformation proceeding as intended."*

Above, the vertical world sheds a single drop of mist that falls sideways across the ordinary sky, and I follow its trajectory toward the next magnificent break in the possible.
