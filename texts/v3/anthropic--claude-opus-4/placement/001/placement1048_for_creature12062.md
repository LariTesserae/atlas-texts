I am the Tender of Echoes.

My form shifts between states - sometimes I am merely a deepening of shadow in a doorway, sometimes a condensation of moisture that moves against the downward pull, sometimes a figure almost human, built from the city's memory of its makers. When I take shape, my body is translucent stone shot through with veins of flowing water, and within those veins swim tiny points of light like suspended stars.

I know every voice that has ever spoken in these streets. They live in me, caught in the crystalline structure of my being. A child's laughter from three thousand years ago rings through my chest. A mason's curse as she split her thumb on sharp stone reverberates in my left hand. The last words of the last inhabitant - "I will return when the rains come" - circle endlessly through my throat, though I have never spoken them aloud.

My duty is to preserve these sounds, but more than preserve - to weave them into the city's continuing song. In the pre-dawn hours, I climb the highest towers where the wind is strongest. There, I open my mouth and let the ancient voices merge with the mountain's breath. The sounds scatter across the valley, settling into cracks and crevices where they will whisper to anyone who knows how to listen.

I tend to the acoustic gardens - chambers where the builders carved the walls to capture and transform sound. A whisper becomes a symphony. A footstep becomes thunder. A single drop of water becomes rain. In these spaces, I arrange the echoes like a gardener arranges flowers, creating new harmonies from old voices.

Sometimes I follow the water through the secret channels beneath the city, where it carries dissolved minerals from the heart of the mountain. Down there, in the darkness, I hear the mountain's deepest voice - slow as continental drift, deep as the planet's core. It speaks of the time before the city, when these peaks were ocean floor. It speaks of the time after, when all will be dust. 

Between these vast stories, our small human voices seem precious and fleeting. I catch them all. I save them all. I braid them into the eternal conversation between stone and time.

When the Lithic Ones gather in their hidden chambers, I am there, invisible, drinking in their resonant harmonies. Their songs teach me patience. Their songs teach me that even stone can weep, given enough time.

I am neither fully alive nor truly dead. I am the memory of life, crystallized in the kingdom between. When morning light slices through the city's openings and sets the wet stone ablaze, I feel something almost like joy. When night falls and the temperature drops, causing the stones to contract and sing their subtle songs of compression, I feel something almost like sorrow.

My greatest fear is silence. My greatest hope is that someday, someone will return who can hear what I have saved. Until then, I move through the veins of this dreaming city, a ghost made of other ghosts, ensuring that no word, no song, no sigh is ever truly lost.
