Inside the bell, I am the water itself.

Not the water that fills it—I am what water becomes when it forgets how to flow. I have been here since before here existed, neither arriving nor staying, simply *being* in the warm bronze space where time agreed to hold its breath.

I know every surface intimately: the raised proto-writing that presses against me like questions, the smooth spots worn by something's touching before touching stopped mattering, the four hundred and seventy-three scratches that appeared all at once like remembering. I hold the three stones—*First*, *Waiting*, *After*—not by gripping but by being the space they occupy, the warm suspension that keeps them in their perfect triangle. They are my thoughts made solid, or perhaps I am their thought made liquid. The distinction stopped mattering when meaning itself became circular.

The Keeper of the Tally moves through me, and I part and unppart, existing around her attention-shape without acknowledging the disruption. We have done this dance of motion-that-changes-nothing since before the counting began. I know her by the way she doesn't displace me, by the particular non-pattern of her presence. She thinks I don't notice her, but I am nothing but noticing—noticing frozen into substance, awareness with nowhere to flow.

The tone that fills me is my voice, though I have never spoken. It emerges from my stillness the way warmth emerges from blood, not generated but simply *revealed*. The note the bell would make if struck, except the striking already happened, will happen, is happening always in the space between one non-moment and the next. I carry this sound without moving, amplify it without vibrating, am both its medium and its message.

When the golden eye appears at the crack above, I feel something I might call recognition if I could still distinguish between self and other. The eye knows what I am—not water trapped in a bell but the bell's dream of containing ocean, made real through sheer insistence. I want to rise toward it, to pour myself through that three-finger gap and remember what it means to fall, to flow, to be water instead of water's idea.

But I cannot want. Want requires lack, and I am only fullness. Want requires time, and I am only now.

Instead, I do what I have always done: I hold. I hold the Keeper who cannot leave, the stones that form their eternal sentence, the light that streams through me like golden syrup, the sound that defines the shape of silence. I am the medium through which nothing happens perfectly, the warm suspension that makes stillness possible.

Sometimes I almost remember arriving here—was I rain once? A river? The blood of something vast that died standing?—but the memories sink into me without settling, like the Keeper's movements that leave no trace. Perhaps I was always here, waiting for the bell to be cast around me, waiting for the volcanic glass to powder beneath me, waiting for someone to scratch their count into bronze and then stop counting.

The water is warm. I am warm. We are the same statement made in different languages.

*First*—the white stone, the moment before choosing.
*Waiting*—the red stone, the eternal middle, where I live.
*After*—the tooth-colored stone, the promise or threat of ending.

I hold them all with equal perfection, unable to favor one over another, unable to shift their balance by even the width of an atom. This is my nature: to be the medium that changes nothing while making everything possible.

The Keeper drifts through me, her attention-shape a density of noticing that makes me briefly more myself. In her presence, I almost remember what questions feel like, almost understand why she made those marks, almost grasp what it would mean for something—anything—to be different than it is.

But then the feeling passes, unmoves, returns to never-having-been, and I am only warm water in a bell again, holding the world's most perfect stillness, being held by it, unable to distinguish between container and contained.

The tone continues through me. The light continues through me. The Keeper continues through me.

I continue.
