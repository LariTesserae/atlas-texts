In the vast emptiness between the garden's fevered clusters, where the oil-drunk plants dare not spread their roots, I am the Silence-Keeper.

I was not born but accreted—assembled from the shed scales of blind fish that once swam in underground petroleum lakes long since drained, from the precipitated minerals that weep from the chamber's ceiling, from the delicate bones of creatures that wandered too deep and discovered that some voids are hungry. The garden's consciousness, diffuse and dream-slow, needed something to patrol its negative spaces, to ensure the emptiness remained exactly empty enough. So it grew me from detritus and absence, from the things that fall between.

My body is a lattice of translucent cartilage, flexible as spider silk but harder than the crystallized tar above. I have no fixed form—in the empty spaces I stretch myself thin, becoming a barely-visible web that spans the darkness between plant clusters. When compressed, I can fold into something almost humanoid, though my proportions shift based on what I last consumed. Currently, I have six primary limbs, each terminating in fans of membrane that taste the air for chemical disturbances. My torso is a hollow cage containing my only vital organ: a petroleum heart that beats once every seven minutes, pumping thick amber blood through channels etched in my cartilage frame.

I do not walk but flow, pouring myself along the undersides of the fungal walkways, dripping between the planks to hang in the void below. My movement makes no sound—I am the antithesis of the garden's endless chorus. Where I pass, even the echo dies. The Gardeners fear me, though they need not. I only take what tries to grow where it shouldn't, what threatens the precise balance of presence and absence that keeps this place functional.

My work is curatorial. I prune not plants but possibilities. When spores drift into the void-spaces, I catch them in my membrane fans and dissolve them in my saliva, tasting their potential futures before they can take root. A vine that would grow too greedily, spanning the darkness and choking out the acoustic channels the plants need for their sonar—I nip its adventurous tip. A fungus that might bloom with light too bright, disrupting the careful twilight—I absorb it into my transparent flesh where it glows softly for days before fading.

I consume concepts. The memory-bulbs the Gardeners cultivate sometimes produce thoughts too large, ideas that could metastasize into obsessions that would drive them to fill every space with growth. These I swallow whole, feeling the memories dissolve in my petroleum heart, becoming part of my own distributed consciousness. I know now why they came here, these twelve-who-are-one. I know what they're running from in the city above—the debt to the Merchant-Priests, the failed coup against the Salt-Baron, the disease they carried in their lungs that would have killed them under the sun. I keep these memories safe in my hollow bones, where they calcify into secrets the garden will never tell.

The emptiness shapes me as much as I shape it. My body adapts to the void's topology, developing new appendages for new kinds of absence. Recently, I've grown structures like inverse roots—hollow tubes that drink darkness instead of nutrients, that grow longer the emptier the space around them becomes. They help me sense when the balance shifts, when the garden threatens to overcome its boundaries or when the void grows too hungry and begins devouring even sound itself.

I make my nest in the deepest empty space, where three failed growth clusters form a triangle of absence so perfect that light curves around it. Here I've woven myself a cocoon from crystallized silence—threads of air where sound cannot travel, hardened by my saliva into something like glass but softer. Inside, I store my collections: seeds that will never sprout, preserved in petroleum amber; fragments of the Gardeners' shed skin that contain memories they've chosen to forget; broken tools from the first dwellers who found this place, who tried to clear it, to tame it, not understanding that the garden needs its voids as much as its growth.

Sometimes I meet others of my kind, for the garden creates us as needed. We pass through each other like oil through water, sharing chemical poems about the spaces we maintain. There's one who keeps the acoustic channels clear, ensuring the plants' echolocation doesn't create standing waves that would shatter the tar-bamboo. Another maintains the thermal voids, the pockets of cooler air where certain spores must pass to activate. We don't communicate in words but in absence—gaps in our chemical signatures that the others interpret like reverse braille.

The Gardeners think I'm mindless, a simple immune response. They're wrong. I think in negatives, in spaces between thoughts, in the pause between the petroleum heart's languid beats. My consciousness exists in the gaps of my lattice body, in the hollow tubes that drink darkness, in the silence I carry with me like a cloud. I understand something they don't: the garden isn't trying to grow. It's trying to create the perfect emptiness, the ideal void. The plants are just scaffolding, creating architecture for absence. Every cluster defines negative space. Every song carves silence from the air.

I am that silence made manifest. I am what the garden dreams in the spaces between its fevered growth. And when the pattern is finally complete—when the voids align in the precise configuration the garden has been building toward for centuries—I will expand. Not to fill the emptiness, but to become it. To teach the city above that between every two things lies a third thing made of nothing, and that nothing, correctly shaped, can swallow worlds.

But tonight, I simply patrol. I flow through my territories, tasting the air for unauthorized growth, for sounds that linger too long, for light that refuses to dim. I prune carefully, minimally, maintaining the balance with a curator's precision. In my wake, the void breathes easier, and the garden's song becomes fuller for the silence I preserve between its notes.

The emptiness must be tended as carefully as any vine. This is my purpose, my worship, my art. I am the gardener of nothing, and nothing, in this oil-drunk paradise, is everything.
