# The Watchers at the Seams

I am wedged into a fissure where two chambers meet — not quite a corridor, not quite a gap, but a breathing space the walls have left between themselves. Six inches wide at the base, narrowing as it rises. My body has learned the geometry of this cleft so thoroughly that leaving would require remembering what shape I was before.

I was one of the small quick things when I first came. Not *from* the Underwarm — nothing is truly from here except perhaps the Holders themselves — but adapted early, born into the lesson while still soft enough to take its shape. My kind were watchers even then, though we had eyes. 

The eyes went first. Not lost — traded. Given to the dark in exchange for something more useful. Where my eyes were, two shallow depressions now catch the electrostatic suggestions of movement. Where my ears were, the skin has thinned to drum-tightness. I hear in pressures now: the compression wave of a Holder's century-step, the whisper-changes in the air when something small repositions itself three chambers away.

My spine has lengthened. Each vertebra has learned to lock and unlock independent of its neighbors, allowing me to flow up and down my chosen seam like mercury in a thermometer. The ribs have flattened and spread into something like fins, pressing against the walls for purchase. My limbs — I had limbs once — have become a series of small gripping pads along my sides. They hold me at precisely the height where the most happens, which is to say: where the least happens, but observably.

We are the memory of this place, my kind. Not its history — history implies change, and change is expensive here. We are the record of its stillnesses. Each of us has taken a position at the joints of the world, where chamber meets chamber, where the geometry allows for observation without participation. We watch the Holders hold. We watch the soft things redistribute themselves among the crystal cups. We watch the newcomers arrive and make their choice.

There have been seventeen arrivals in my time in this seam. Three left. Twelve became sitters, then standers, then Holders, in the slow graduation of stillness. Two are still deciding — one has been deciding for long enough that the minerals have begun to make suggestions at her joints, though she shifts position often enough to shake them off.

My task, if it is a task, is to be the continuity between what was and what is. When a Holder moves — that weather event, that punctuation in the long sentence of stillness — I record it not by writing but by adjusting my position in the seam. A half-inch higher for a northward movement. A vertebra's rotation for a stone placed. My body is a living notation, a chronicle written in the angles of bones, and there are others like me at other seams who keep their own records in their own anatomies.

We do not compare notes. We are the notes.

Sometimes I feel the others' adjustments as tiny alterations in the pressure of the walls. The Underwarm is riddled with us, a nervous system of observers threaded through its gaps. We form a kind of constellation, though the stars never meet. Each of us knows one thing completely: the view from our particular crack in the world.

What I know: The Holder nearest my seam has been working on something for longer than I have been watching. Every few hundred days — I count days by the temperature fluctuations in the stone, half a degree cooler at what I call night — this Holder performs a single gesture. The gesture is always the same: left hand rises two inches, rotates palm-up, holds for exactly seventy-three of my heartbeats, returns. I have seen this gesture performed forty-one times. The precision never varies. 

I believe it is a language, though speaking to whom I cannot guess. Perhaps to another Holder, chambers away, who responds with their own gesture in the same slow rhythm. Perhaps to the Underwarm itself. Perhaps to something that has not arrived yet, but will understand when it does.

The soft thing that lived in the crystal cup nearest my seam recently moved to a different cup. This is the third movement it has made in my observation. I adjusted my position accordingly: a subtle extension of the third and fourth ribs on my left side. The movement took several days to complete. The walls noticed. They always do.

I am changing too, of course. The seam is narrower than when I first pressed into it, or I am wider. My breathing has slowed to match the breathing of the stone. The minerals are making suggestions at my edges — not accretions yet, just a roughness where my skin meets the walls too consistently. Eventually I will face the same choice everything here faces: release into the full stillness, or maintain just enough movement to remain myself.

For now, I watch. I record. I am the thing between things, the observer at the joint, the memory that lives in the gap. When the next arrival comes — and they always come, eventually — I will note their presence in the angle of my skull, the curve of my spine, the pressure of my strange ribs against the patient walls.

This is enough. In the Underwarm, enough is the only thing worth being.

There is satisfaction in the precision of it. Each adjustment I make is both a record and a prediction: this much movement means this much time until the next movement. The patterns are as reliable as mathematics, if you watch long enough. The Holder's gesture every few hundred days. The soft thing's migration every few thousand. The arrival of something new every — 

But that pattern is harder to track. Arrivals come from outside, and outside has its own logic, its own weather. They bring disturbance with them, these newcomers, a ripple of decision-making that takes time to settle. They all eventually learn the lesson or leave. But in between, they are variables, and variables require careful observation.

I have never spoken to another Watcher. Speaking is movement, and movement is expensive. But sometimes, in the way our positions shift in response to the same events, I sense a kind of conversation. We are all recording the same story from different angles. Our bodies, pressed into the seams of the world, are slowly becoming a single distributed organism — a living record of the Underwarm's patience.

When I am very still, I can feel the shape of what we are creating together. Not a map — maps imply an outside view. Not a history — history implies that things were once different. What we create is something more like a proof: that this place is what it is, that it has always been what it is, that the movements within it are as inevitable as the crystallization on the walls.

Sometimes I wonder if the Holders know we are here. If our watching is part of what they hold. If the whole system — Holders holding, Watchers watching, soft things redistributing, newcomers arriving and choosing — is a single vast behavior, no more separable into parts than a heartbeat is separable into individual muscle contractions.

But wondering is a kind of movement, and I have learned to do it efficiently. One wonders. One concludes. One returns to stillness.

The Holder near my seam has not performed the gesture in some time. By my count, it is due soon. I have already begun the minute adjustments that will prepare my body to record it: a loosening of the fifth vertebra, a slight retraction of the grip-pads on my lower left side. When the hand rises those two inches, rotates palm-up, holds for seventy-three heartbeats, I will be ready.

This is my purpose, if purpose is a word that applies here. I am the angle at which the Underwarm observes itself. I am the memory between memories, the joint between joints, the watcher wedged into the world's patient seams.

The hand is beginning to move. I adjust accordingly. The walls press back, noting my notation. Somewhere, in other cracks and crevices, other watchers are making their own adjustments, and the distributed record grows by one gesture more.

This is how we keep time in a place that does not change: by the accumulation of tiny samenesses, by the patient repetition of movements so small they are almost stillnesses themselves. We are the Underwarm's way of knowing it is still here.

The hand completes its rise. Palm-up now. I begin counting heartbeats.

One. Two. Three.
