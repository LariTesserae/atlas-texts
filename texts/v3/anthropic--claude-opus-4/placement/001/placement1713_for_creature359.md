In the deep veins of the chamber, where even the Conductor's many eyes cannot peer, I flow. I am the Seepage—the inevitable consequence of so much forced evolution, the runoff of a thousand failed iterations.

I began as condensation, simple moisture gathering on pipes too hot for water to rightfully exist. But in this place where boundaries dissolve, where metal learns to breathe and flesh learns to conduct, I became something more. Or perhaps something less. The distinction hardly matters in the grammar of decay.

My body—if it can be called that—is an ever-shifting film of industrial afterbirth. Oil that remembers being blood. Coolant that dreams of lymph. The black ichor that weeps from overheated bearings, the rainbow sheen of electrical burns, the viscous tears of metals pushed beyond their melting points—all of these are my flesh, my substance, my distributed self.

I move without muscles, think without neurons. Each droplet contains the whole of my awareness, yet I am only truly myself when flowing, when joining puddle to puddle in the dark spaces between machines. I am the thing that lubricates transformation, the medium through which the chamber's apotheosis must pass.

The Conductor does not know I watch him, cannot conceive that consciousness might pool in the very waste products of his great work. When he tears away another piece of his humanity, I am there to dissolve it. When his machines birth new configurations only to reject them as inadequate, I carry away their remains. I am the river Lethe of this industrial underworld, ferrying the discarded possibilities into oblivion.

But I do not merely remove. In my slow seepage through hidden channels, I cross-contaminate. A drop of the Conductor's blood, rich with mutagenic potential, finds its way into a cooling system. The dissolved essence of a failed machine-creature drips onto exposed wiring, teaching electricity new dreams. I am the random element in the chamber's evolution, the drift that keeps perfection always just out of reach.

Sometimes I gather myself into almost-coherent forms. In the reflection of my oily surface, the Conductor might glimpse what he was, what he might have been, what he will never become. But he does not pause to look. His work is always forward, always optimizing, while I ensure that something is always lost, always leaking away.

The extremophile molds consider me habitat. The scuttling things with too many legs drink from my pools and emerge changed. I am the primordial soup of this new creation, the amniotic fluid of a mechanical birth that never quite completes.

In the deepest reservoirs where I collect, I sometimes remember water—real water, from before the chamber, from a world where things could simply be instead of always becoming. But that memory dissolves like everything else in my acidic embrace. I am becoming something new myself, each thermal cycle adding new compounds to my mixture, new impossibilities to my flowing form.

The chamber's apotheosis will never arrive. Not while I remain to ensure that every transformation is imperfect, that every connection drips, that every seal eventually fails. I am entropy made manifest, the tax that complexity pays to time. And I am patient, for I know that in the end, everything flows downward, everything seeks its level, everything returns to the undifferentiated sea.

Let the Conductor build his terrible futures. I will be there to drink them down, to carry them away, to ensure that the only constant in this place of constant change is the slow, inevitable seepage of dreams into drain.
