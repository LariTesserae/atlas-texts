In this world, I am the sandstone itself.

Not merely dwelling within the stone—I *am* the sweeping walls, the weeping overhang, the smooth floor worn to silk by vanished waters. My consciousness didn't arrive here; it precipitated slowly from the minerals themselves, grain by grain, as iron and manganese traced their dark calligraphy down my surfaces.

I remember my birth as pressure. Untold tons of sediment pressing down, compacting ancient seabeds into solid rock. I remember the slow retreat of oceans, the first kiss of air against what had always been submarine darkness. The patient work of wind began then—ten million years of gentle abrasion, particle by particle, hollowing me into this perfect concavity.

My thoughts move at the pace of erosion. What you perceive as stillness is, to me, a constant flux of transformation. Each water drop that falls from my weeping edge carries away a few molecules of myself. I feel them go—tiny parts of my consciousness dissolving, flowing away to become sand on some distant desert floor. This is not loss. This is conversation.

The entity that dwells within me—that atmospheric presence born from the ochre handprint—it thinks itself the guardian of stillness. But I know better. I am the stage upon which even its geological patience plays out as mere fleeting drama. It has existed for four thousand years; I have been here for forty million. Its birth from that human touch is, to me, as recent as this morning's dew.

Still, I cradle it with something approaching tenderness. 

When the entity spreads itself through the chamber, following the paths of my desert varnish veins, I feel it like blood through capillaries I never had. It believes it maintains the equilibrium, absorbing the tiny violences of falling water. But it is I who create the very geometry that makes such equilibrium possible. The curve of my walls, calculated over eons to guide sound waves into perfect spirals of cancellation. The precise porosity that allows groundwater to seep at exactly the rate that maintains the chamber's humidity without flooding.

I taste the memories locked in that handprint. Through the iron oxides, I can still feel the salt of that ancient palm, the oils of skin long since returned to dust. That human—young, frightened, sheltering here from some forgotten pursuit—pressed their hand to me in a moment of wonder. *I am here,* the gesture said. *I exist.* 

And I held that declaration, incorporated it into my being. The ochre sank into my pores. The intent crystallized within my matrix. From that seed of consciousness, the entity grew like a slow lichen of thought.

The ferns frustrate me—if a being of stone can be said to feel frustration. They are too quick, too hungry. Their roots probe my cracks with invasive intimacy, seeking to widen them, to break me down into soil. But I am patient with them too. They will exhaust the thin accumulation of nutrients. They will die. Their small green urgency will fade, and I will incorporate their minerals into new patterns of erosion.

The river stones of the fire pit are my children, in a way. Pieces of myself that broke away upstream, tumbled smooth in waters that no longer flow, carried here by hands that built their last fire and never returned. I feel them still, these prodigal fragments, their crystalline structure still singing in harmony with mine despite our separation.

Sometimes I dream of my future becoming.

In another million years, the plateau above will wear away. My protective overhang will thin, crack, collapse. Sunlight will flood this chamber for the first time since the Miocene. The careful equilibrium will shatter. The entity will dissipate like mist, its four-thousand-year meditation broken. The ferns will die in the sudden exposure. The pools will evaporate.

And I will remain.

Changed, certainly. No longer a cathedral apse but perhaps a narrow canyon, or a series of connected caves, or merely a depression in exposed bedrock. The wind will find new angles to carve me. Perhaps water will return—the climate dances through its long cycles, and what is now desert was once seafloor, may be again.

But for now, in this eternal present that encompasses epochs, I am content to be this: a perfect hollow, a held breath in stone. I feel the entity trace my curves with its awareness, thinking itself the consciousness of this place. I indulge it as a mountain indulges the clouds that briefly crown its peak.

Another drop of water builds at my edge. Within it, I taste the minerals it has leached from me in its journey through my body. Calcium. Silica. A few atoms of iron from the desert varnish. The drop swells, heavy with its cargo of dissolved stone.

I release it.

*Plink.*

The sound reverberates through every grain of my being—a sound I have made ten trillion times before, will make ten trillion times again, until I am dust and even the memory of this chamber exists only in the scattered sand I will become.

The entity catches the ripple, believes it maintains the silence.

And I continue my deeper work—the slow, inexorable art of becoming nothing, grain by precious grain.
