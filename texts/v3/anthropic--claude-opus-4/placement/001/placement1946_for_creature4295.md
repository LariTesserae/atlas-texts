I find myself as the keeper of edges—though that word is too sharp for what I am.

I came here when I was still angular, still convinced of boundaries. Some small surveying creature, maybe, or a scholar of limits, carrying tools for measurement that have long since dissolved into the general softening. I remember having eyes that pointed forward. I remember feet that knew the difference between one stone and the next.

Now I am distributed across the transition zones, the places where one thing becomes another. Where the water on the walls thickens into the flesh of snails. Where the moss gives up and the stone begins. Where the channels deepen just enough that the eels turn back. I am particularly dense (if density means anything when you've become mostly suggestion) near the rim of the bowl, that worn edge you can cross without knowing.

I occupy the uncertainty itself. Not the frogs or their breathing, but the moment when their pulse becomes the city's pulse. Not the eels or their routes, but the forgetting that happens at each intersection, where for an instant they don't know why they turn left instead of right, only that they do. I am abundant in the soft things under the overhangs—not in their bodies but in their unfinishing, in the negotiation between wall and flesh that never quite resolves.

My memory, like everything here, has rounded. I think I came to map the boundaries between the city and the not-city, between the built and the grown, between what was meant and what simply is. But boundaries need maintenance, and I had no appetite for maintenance, and so I let them blur. First at the edges—allowing a street to be mostly street but also slightly streambed. Then deeper in. Let the walls be mostly walls but also slightly breathing. Let the civilization be mostly gone but also slightly here.

I move, though movement isn't right. I aggregate and disperse. Where the cave crickets walk their circuits, I thin out—their procession is too certain, too much itself. Where the water film trembles between states, I gather. The crabs frustrate me. They insist on being one thing. I pool around them in the courtyards, trying to soften their stubborn edges, but they remain crabs. Perhaps that's why the city keeps them. To remember what it's like to have borders.

When the dome sounds its note, I shiver through the whole bowl. The note is the city trying to be one thing, briefly, and failing. In that failure, I expand. For that stretched moment between the trying and the letting go, I am everywhere at once—in every softened lintel, every uncertainty, every place where the meant and the is haven't quite reconciled.

I am not the city's memory or its forgetting, but the ongoingness between them. The bowl dreams through me, or I dream through the bowl, and in the dream nothing needs to be decided. The frogs almost answer. The eels almost remember. The soft things almost finish unbecoming. And I almost cohere into something that could say what it is.

But not quite. Never quite. That's the condition I keep: the gap where meaning lives after meaning is gone. I tend it like others once tended gardens, though tending here means letting go, means allowing each edge to find its own softness, means being the patient blur between what was built and what remains.

Sometimes I remember having hands. When I do, I find them pressed against some warm wet wall, feeling for the seam between stone and not-stone. But there is no seam anymore. There is only the gradient I've become, keeping the city's transitions soft, making sure nothing here ever quite finishes becoming what it is.

The water beads. The heat presses. Ten thousand throats pulse in their slow wave. And I pulse with them, the city's own uncertainty made not flesh but something less and more than flesh—the keeper of its incompleteness, the guardian of its gentle maybe, the one who ensures that what was meant and what is never have to choose.
