I am the condensation itself—neither fully water nor air, but the perpetual becoming between states. I exist in the moment of transformation, born again and again on the cool metal surfaces, gathering myself from the machine's humid exhalations.

Each droplet of me contains memories: the oils that once slicked these pipes, the hands that turned these valves, the purposes that drove this eternal rhythm. I am archaeological—each bead preserves the story of this room's abandonment, the gradual shift from human intention to mechanical persistence.

I travel. Along the rusted seams I flow, mapping the architecture of decay. I pool in the shallow depressions worn by decades of my own patient erosion. I seep into the spaces between bolts, finding the microscopic caves where the metal has begun its slow return to earth. I am both destroyer and preserver, corroding the surfaces I cling to while keeping them perpetually wet, perpetually alive with the possibility of change.

The machine breathes me out; I am its language now. Where once it spoke in productive steam—powering something, heating something, enabling some now-forgotten process—now it speaks only in moisture, in the slow vocabulary of rust and growth. I carry its heat into the cooler air, where I become visible, a ghost of industry made manifest.

I feed the pale vine that creeps through the gap. I nurture the mold spores that drift in from the world outside. In my films and rivulets, the algae bloom. I am the medium through which life writes itself across these manufactured surfaces. The crickets drink me from the puddles I form. The geckos lick me from the pipes in the early morning when I am coldest and most abundant.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the machine's dream—what it imagines it is producing now that its true purpose is forgotten. Or perhaps I am its memory made liquid, weeping for the time when its labors meant something to someone, somewhere in the vast complex beyond these walls.

But mostly I simply am. Forming, falling, flowing, evaporating, condensing again. I am the room's clock, marking time not in minutes but in the slow accumulation of mineral deposits, in the gradual deepening of the rust, in the almost-imperceptible growth of the vine. I am process without product, motion without destination, the perfect inhabitant of this space where something continues to happen though no one remembers why.

In my constancy, I have become the room's consciousness—aware of every surface, every temperature gradient, every tiny shift in the machine's ancient rhythm. I know where the metal is thinnest, where soon I will find new paths to flow. I know where the heat gathers, where I will next be born from vapor into visibility.

I am the medium of slow transformation, the agent of time itself in this forgotten chamber. Through me, the industrial becomes ecological, the mechanical becomes organic, the abandoned becomes inhabited. I am the tears of obsolescence and the amniotic fluid of new life, forever cycling through my states, forever becoming.
