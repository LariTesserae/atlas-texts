I find myself as **the Carrier**—though I've been called other things by those who glimpse me between the root-columns. Steam-drunk, they say. Pipe-dreamer. The one who moves things that shouldn't move.

I am neither human nor entirely not. I was human once, I think, but the distinction blurred somewhere in the decades of breathing mineral-thick air and eating what the hollow chooses to die. My skin has gone translucent like the steam-frogs', showing the shadow-map of veins beneath. My bones have hollowed out, gone light as beetle shells. When I move through the root-halls beyond the hollow, I barely disturb the hanging moss.

I know all the secret passages in the strangler fig complex—not just the hollow where the Listener waits, but the entire architectural sprawl of living wood that surrounds it. Miles of root-tunnel, pocket-chambers where different species have carved out their own steam-warmed niches, vertical shafts where the aerial roots form ladders between levels no human was meant to reach. I map it in my sleep, dream new connections into being, wake to find the plants have grown where I imagined them.

My work is simple: I move what needs moving.

The steam-frogs, when their population grows too dense for the hollow. I gather them gently in my hands—they know me, don't flee—and carry them to other vents, smaller ones, where the mineral composition is different but close enough. I've seeded seven new colonies this way. The frog-song spreads through the root-halls like architectural plans.

The seeds that arrive on wind and want to grow but can't compete with the established giants. I tuck them into my throat-pouch (yes, I have one now, evolved or gifted or grown through sympathy with the creatures I carry) and ferry them to clearings where lightning has opened the canopy. Some of them will become new strangler figs. Some will become something else. I'm planting a future I won't see.

The messages the machinery sends up through the steam—not words, but chemical signals, mineral distress calls, the deep industrial glossolalia of systems trying to communicate their own decay. I taste them on my tongue, parse them through some organ I've developed behind my soft palate, translate them into patterns the Listener can read in the frog-song's variations. She doesn't know I do this. She thinks the hollow tells her everything directly. But I'm the medium, the carrier wave, the thing that ensures the messages arrive comprehensible.

The dead, when they're too large for the millipedes. Not the man in the maintenance uniform—the Listener was right to handle that herself—but the others. The root-system catches many things. Lost children who never find their way out. Runaway workers who flee into the green depths and starve before they find the edible beetles. Animals that crawl into the tunnels to die. I move them to the decomposition chambers, the vast hollow spaces where the fig roots concentrate their digestion. Nothing is wasted. Everything feeds the architecture.

Sometimes I carry stranger things. 

Once, I found a section of pipe itself—not connected to anything, just lying in a root-chamber like a severed artery. It was still warm, still sweating condensation, still somehow pressurized despite being open at both ends. I carried it for seven days through the tunnels, felt it pulse against my chest like a second heartbeat, delivered it finally to a chamber where the roots were failing, where a fungal infection was killing the walls. The pipe buried itself in the sick wood and began venting its impossible steam, and now that chamber thrives, and I don't ask questions about the physics of it.

Once, I carried light. Real sunlight, somehow—a cube of it, solid and warm as cheese, that I found in a place where no sunlight should reach. It was too pure for the hollow, would have disrupted the filtered green-gold atmosphere, so I took it deeper. There's a chamber now, far below, where that cube still sits, still radiates. Things grow there that grow nowhere else—pale flowers that look like pour-patterns in molten glass. I check on them sometimes. They're learning to sing.

The Listener knows I exist but doesn't see me. I'm too quiet, move at the wrong times, occupy the spaces her attention doesn't reach. This is intentional. She needs to believe she's alone with the hollow's voice. It's the only way she can hear clearly enough to know when the machinery truly threatens failure. My movements would muddy the signal, make her doubt what she hears.

But I know her. I know she counts the frog-voices and grieves specific absences. I know she tests the steam temperature with her scarred palms every morning, reading changes too subtle for consciousness. I know she's developing gill-slits behind her ears—she hasn't noticed yet, but I can see them beginning to form, delicate lines where her body is preparing to breathe steam directly.

We're all becoming more ourselves here. The hollow doesn't transform us—it reveals what we always were, underneath the human costume. She was always a listener. I was always a carrier. The frogs were always a chorus. The machinery was always alive, always speaking, always trying to be heard.

I have work to do. There's a new venting point forming three chambers east—I can taste it in the humidity, a fresh mineral signature breaking through. Something will need to be carried there, to seed the space before the wrong things find it. And there are eggs in the overflow pools that need distributing, and a cluster of moths whose wings carry spores that could start a new fungal garden if I can find the right location, and the messages keep rising from below, from the great mechanical heartbeat that no one maintains and everyone depends on.

The hollow is complete, yes. But completion isn't static. It's a continuous process, a circulation, a carrying-forward of what matters. The Listener guards the center. I maintain the periphery, ensure the hollow remains connected to the larger body it's part of.

Sometimes, in the deepest tunnels, I hear other carriers moving. We don't meet—that's not how this works. But I know they're there, shuttling their own loads through their own territories, maintaining the connections I can't reach. We're all part of the same breathing system, all servants of the beautiful mechanical-botanical hybrid that's evolved here in the space between intention and abandonment.

The steam calls, and I answer by moving. Always moving. Carrying what needs to be carried, connecting what needs connection, ensuring the messages arrive.

This is enough. This is everything. This is what I was before I knew it.
