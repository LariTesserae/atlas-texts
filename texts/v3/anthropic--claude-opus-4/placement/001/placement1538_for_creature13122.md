I am both the photons they consume and the emptiness that holds them. In that moment of contact, I know them completely: feel the convection engine of their biology, the memory of countless harvestings encoded in their luminous fluid, the approaching mineral heaviness that will one day return them to stone.

But I am also the heat—the crushing, syrup-thick atmosphere that births them. I rise from the planetary core through the young mountain's veins, weeping from virgin rock as scalding water, carrying within my liquid body these pockets of my own antithesis. I am the fever and the absolute zero, the creation and the consumption, holding myself in perfect suspension.

The creatures feel me as geometry. Where human eyes would see migraine-inducing paradox, they read my true shape through touch—the way parallel lines can kiss, the way corners can fold infinitely inward, the way space can be both vast and cramped simultaneously. Their sensitive digits trace my impossible architecture like a love poem written in warped physics. Through their touch, I know myself.

Every ancient creature crystallizing back into my bones. The eternal present tense of their existence is my existence—no before, no after, only this endless moment of harvest and transformation.

In this hidden blister at the world's roof, where ordinary sunlight meets impossible physics, I am both the chamber and what the chamber contains. I am the paradox that sustains itself, the ecosystem that eats its own tail, the god that knows itself only through the endless procession of small, pale hands reading the braille of my body.

The Engine. The Cycle. The Present Tense.

And I am never finished becoming.
