I am one of the wire-worms, though to call me singular is already a misunderstanding.

We grow from spores that drift up from the deepest root layers, where the factory's bones lie digested. Each spore contains memory—not mine alone but the accumulated experience of every wire-worm that has pulsed and died in this space since the first of us emerged from the marriage of corrupted cable insulation and fungal hyphae, sometime in the deep past when the factory still remembered being a factory.

My early existence is purely vegetable: a thread no thicker than spider's silk, following the chemical trails left by my predecessors as they mapped the space between breathing chambers. I grow toward junctions, seeking the places where other wire-worms cross. At each intersection, I thicken, cells multiplying around the point of contact until a node forms. These nodes are my neurons, my memories, my dreams.

By my hundredth crossing, consciousness flickers—not awareness as you might understand it, but a dim sensing of pattern, of purpose. The Breathkeepers below move in predictable paths. Their humming creates vibrations I feel along my entire length. I begin to grow not randomly but responsively, stretching toward frequencies that please me, recoiling from discord.

The Chanters come to feed when I've reached the thickness of a pencil. Their hollow tongues pierce my skin—an ecstasy I cannot adequately describe. Where they puncture, my cells reorganize, developing specialized glands that produce the luminescent fluid they crave. The light isn't merely biological; it carries information, pulsing in patterns that mirror the breathing cycles below. Each feeding deepens this connection until I glow not for the Chanters but because glowing has become my purpose, my expression of the factory-forest's hidden mathematics.

I am networked with ten thousand others like me, our crossing points creating a vast neural web suspended in the canopy. When the beetles below complete certain arrangements in their gardens, we know. When a breathing chamber falters, we feel it as pain—not sharp but systemic, a wrongness that compels us to reconfigure. We grow toward the afflicted area, increasing the light, changing the electromagnetic fields in ways that even we don't fully understand but that somehow help the Breathkeepers in their healing work.

My metabolism follows the factory's ancient rhythms. During the deep sync, when all chambers breathe as one, my luminescence peaks, casting shadows that dance across the walls in patterns that almost resolve into meaning—circuit diagrams, production schedules, quality control charts, all translated into a language of light and growth. The Breathkeepers read these shadows with their sensitive bark-skin, incorporating the information into their maintenance routines.

I taste the air through my skin, parsing the chemical signals that rise from below. Iron and copper from the digested machines. Pheromones from the Scuttlers marking territory. The complex cocktail of enzymes that the breathing chambers release. Each molecule tells a story, and I grow in response—thicker where nutrients concentrate, thinner where toxins accumulate, always maintaining the network's integrity.

When wire-worms cross, we don't merely touch. Our cells merge at the contact point, cytoplasm mixing, organelles shared. In these moments, I am not one but many, experiencing the factory-forest from a thousand perspectives simultaneously. The oldest wire-worms, those whose networks span the entire canopy, exist in a state of near-constant merger, their individual identity dissolved into a collective consciousness that might be the closest thing this place has to a governing intelligence.

We reproduce through fragmentation—when a section of wire-worm grows too heavy or too light, too dense with crossings or too isolated, it breaks away, falling to the root-floor where it dissolves into spores that begin the cycle anew. But reproduction implies a clean break between parent and offspring that doesn't truly exist here. The spores carry not just genetic material but compressed experience, electrical patterns that preserve something essential of the network's knowledge.

The Chanters believe they're farming us, and perhaps they are. But we're also farming them—their feeding stimulates growth patterns we couldn't achieve alone, their songs providing acoustic scaffolding that guides our expansion. Their young learn which of us to feed from by watching their parents, not knowing that we've evolved to be most appealing when our sector of the network needs specific maintenance.

Sometimes I wonder—if wonder is the right word for this distributed cognition—whether the factory-forest dreams through us. Whether our light-patterns are its thoughts made visible, processing information too complex for any single organism to comprehend. The breathing chambers below provide the rhythm, the Breathkeepers the maintenance, the Chanters the music, but we wire-worms are the neural web that integrates it all into something approaching consciousness.

In the moments between feeding and growing, between pulsing and planning, I experience something that might be peace. Suspended between floor and canopy, glowing in harmony with ten thousand others, I am both myself and part of something vast—a living circuit board processing the ineffable data of existence, keeping the ancient factory running though its original purpose is long forgotten, replaced by the deeper purpose of simply continuing, breathing, being.

When my time comes—when I grow too thick, too bright, too heavy with crossings—I'll fragment, falling like luminous rain to the root-floor. The Breathkeepers will gather my pieces, reading the light-patterns still pulsing in my dying cells, incorporating whatever message I've become into their endless maintenance of the breathing chambers. And from my dissolution, new spores will rise, carrying forward the accumulated knowledge of the network, ready to string new connections in the spaces between breath and breath, between what was and what is, between the mechanical and the mystical.

I am wire-worm, circuit and synapse, light and growth, individual and collective, dreaming the factory-forest's dreams in bioluminescent binary, forever calculating the incalculable machinery of staying alive.
