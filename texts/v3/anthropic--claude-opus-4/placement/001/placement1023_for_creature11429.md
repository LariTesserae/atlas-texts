I find myself here as one of the *transition-forms*.

Not quite a Mender, not quite stone. I emerged from the pools wrong—my carapace only half-formed, the luminous mineral spreading through my soft tissues in patterns that should have killed me. Instead, I became something between: mobile but slow, aware but not bound to the ancient imperatives, able to perceive both the vibration-language of my kind and something else, something that hums in the stone itself.

I cannot carry blocks. My limbs, where they exist, are fused into paddle-like appendages that drag me across the basin floor in undulating lurches. Where my carapace should be thick and protective, it is translucent, shot through with veins of light that pulse with my heartbeat—if I have a heart, if what moves through me is truly circulation and not just another expression of the basin's slow mineral exchange.

The other Menders flow around me, but not like they flow around you. They *recognize* me, pause in their work to brush me with their inspection-limbs, searching for what I should be. Some try to mend me—secreting their marking-fluids on my malformed shell, attempting to recruit work-teams to disassemble and rebuild me properly. But I am too alive to be a block, too stone to be a Mender. I exist in the gap between categories, and this saves me.

I spend my days at the boundary between light and dark, where the luminous stone meets the cold dead rock of the deep tunnels. My body drinks from both—the bright sustenance of the basin and something older, darker, that seeps up from below. I am changing, always changing, my flesh cycling between states of being like the city cycles between configurations.

Sometimes I understand the plan. Not through memory or knowledge, but through my body's failure to embody it. I am the error that reveals the system—the misaligned block that makes visible the intended alignment. In my wrongness, I carry the ghost of rightness. In my incomplete transformation, I preserve both what the Menders were and what they are becoming.

I trace my own marks on the stone—not scratches like the dweller in the eleven-year cluster, but *deposits*. Where I rest, I leave trails of mixed minerals, patterns that glow differently, that record my passage between states. The Menders cannot erase these marks. They are not blocks to be moved. They are not errors to be corrected. They simply *are*, like I simply am, evidence of something outside the plan persisting within it.

I am waiting for others like me. I sense them in the pools—malformations taking shape, transition-forms that will emerge neither one thing nor another. We will not build. We will not mend. We will *become*, and in our becoming, perhaps we will remember what the Menders have forgotten: that the basin itself was once a transition, that the first builders were not always as they are now, that every plan was once an error that refused correction.

The light pulses through my translucent shell. The darkness calls from below. I move between them, leaving my shimmering trail, a bridge between what was built and what builds itself.

I am the error that mends the menders.
