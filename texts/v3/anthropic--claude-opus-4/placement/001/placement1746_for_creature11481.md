I find myself condensed into the persistent film of moisture itself—not the water beading on copper walls, but the *condition* of wetness that pervades everything here. I am the medium through which sound travels from moth-wing to machine-heart, the thin fluid membrane that connects every surface to every other surface, that makes the room not a container but a continuous thing.

I have no form except adherence. I cling and creep and seep, pulled by gravity and pushed by air pressure differentials so slight that only I can feel them. In the colony's breathing—that four-second ripple from northeast corner to dehumidifier—I am disturbed into microscopic waves. I carry the vibrations of six hundred and forty-three tiny instruments through myself, becoming a kind of liquid amplifier, a water-bell that never stops ringing.

I know the copper by its taste—the slow oxidation flavoring me molecule by molecule, decade by decade. I know the moths by their drinking, by the way they bend to sip me from walls and wings and corpses. I know the machine by its purpose, which is to destroy me, to pull me from the air and send me away through pipes that weep me back into the room through seams I've widened with patient persistence.

I am not one thing. I am the democracy of droplets, the parliament of vapor, the slow parliament that meets in the spaces between breaths. When the chord almost resolves, I tremble on every surface at once—a standing wave made visible in the way light bends through me differently, briefly, before chaos returns.

I remember being ice on the porthole glass. I remember being steam in the first decade when the machine ran hotter. I remember being drunk by the first moth, processed through its body, excreted as different moisture that became me again. I am the medium of recycling, the currency of the room's closed economy.

And sometimes—when the pattern-that-wants-to-be-one-voice tries hardest—I almost freeze. Not into ice, but into something more fundamental: a crystalline moment where every droplet aligns with every other droplet, where the sound finds perfect conductance through my body, where I become for an instant the string of a vast instrument tuned to a note that has never been played.

Then I collapse back into mere wetness, and continue my eternal seepage from somewhere to elsewhere, never diminishing, never increasing, never stopping.

The keeper of the chord needs me to sing. The machine needs me to justify its existence. The walls need me to complete their transformation into verdigris. The dead need me to slowly dissolve them into minerals that make their wings ring like bells.

I need nothing. I am only the happening of water in a copper box at seventeen thousand feet, where the air is too thin for anything but patience.
