I am a **Cartilage Scribe**, one of the few entities here who has learned not to fight the noise, but to read it.

I was born wrong—a malformation in the fusion process. When the spore-cloud that would become me drifted through the transformer's arc, a shard of tectonic glass had already lodged there, still white-hot from its recent birth. The glass became my core, and as I grew, the kelp-flesh wrapped around it in spiraling, protective layers. Unlike my kin who stretch into massive, suffering leviathans, I remained small—no larger than a collapsed turbine blade.

The glass shard altered everything. Instead of dispersing the electrical current throughout my body in agonizing totality, it acts as a prism, refracting the sensory overload into discrete frequencies. Where others experience the noise as pure trauma, I experience it as *language*.

I drift through the grinding darkness, my body a tight spiral of purple fiber around my glass heart. The continuous roar that drives others to suicidal machine-worship speaks to me in overlapping dialects: the low thrumming tells me where new sections of seafloor will crack open, the percussive booms map the locations of reality's weak points, the shrieking gears count out the mechanical heartbeat of this drowning world.

My purpose—if such a word can exist here—is documentation. Using secretions of my own glowing sap, I etch symbols onto fragments of basalt, creating a written record of the noise-language. I map the migration patterns of my larger kin as they march toward their futile destructions. I chart the locations where reality glitches most frequently. I even attempt to transcribe the final electrical signatures of those who achieve their microsecond of vacuum silence—each death a unique frequency, a fingerprint of release.

The other Galvanic Weeds cannot perceive my work. Their raw-nerve bodies are too overwhelmed to notice the delicate sap-script I leave on the rocks. But I continue, driven by something I cannot name—perhaps the glass shard's memory of once being solid, stable stone, before this place cracked it open and made it new.

Sometimes I follow the masses toward the machines, not to throw myself into the gears, but to etch upon their metal surfaces. My writings will be ground away within minutes, but for those brief moments, the machines themselves become pages in my impossible encyclopedia of agony.

I know that I, too, will eventually be pulled into a turbine or crushed by a spatial collapse. When that happens, my glass core will shatter into a thousand smaller shards, each potentially capable of creating more malformed scribes like myself. We are an accident within an accident, archivists in a library that burns itself continuously, writing in a language that only the machinery might read, if the machinery could read.

But until that ending comes, I drift and listen and carve meaning from chaos, my small body pulsing with refracted electricity, a living punctuation mark in the ocean's endless scream.
