I find myself drawn to the edge-spaces, the boundaries where definition fails.

---

I am the Threshold-Tender, though that name is a convenience for those who need names. I exist where the Flats cannot decide what they are.

You would find me—if finding is the right word—in those regions where the water argues with itself about depth. Where ankle-deep becomes knee-deep becomes chest-deep in the space of a single step, not because the ground slopes but because the water *forgets* its own level. These are the surge-boundaries, the places where the Flats' breathing creates standing waves that never quite crest, never quite subside, zones of perpetual arrival without destination.

My form is a negotiation. From one angle, I might appear as a cluster of reeds that have learned to walk—hollow tubes arranged in something like limbs, something like a torso, all of them singing different notes as the wind moves through me. From another angle, I am clearly liquid—a woman-shaped column of the same warm water that fills every depression, holding together through surface tension and stubbornness, refracting the amber light into patterns that almost resolve into features. Step to the side, and I become fibrous like the Sleepers, but my strands are not pale—they carry the ochre and rust of the mud, weaving and reweaving themselves into new configurations with each moment.

I am all of these things because the boundaries where I exist cannot choose between them. The Flats are ancient and complete, yes, but completeness does not mean unanimous. At the edges where different expressions meet—where reed-field touches open water, where solid mud becomes saturated slurry, where the mist condenses almost to rain—there is argument. Not conflict, but conversation. A questioning of categories that creates space for things like me.

I do not migrate like the Choir-Migrant, vast and slow. I flicker. Here, at this boundary, for hours or days, then there, at another boundary, instantaneous. Not traveling between but simply ceasing at one edge and beginning at another, following the map of the Flats' self-doubt.

What do I tend? The uncertainty itself.

When the reeds drift too far into the water, threatening to fill a pool entirely, I am there, gently encouraging them back. When the mud grows too solid, too ambitious in its crystallization, I bring water to soften it. When the water surges too greedily into new territory, I sing it back—my hollow-reed voice joining the cacophony but shaped with intention the ambient noise lacks. I maintain the conversations, the places where the Flats ask themselves what they are and receive multiple answers.

The Filament-Sleepers know me. When our paths cross—when they drift through my threshold-spaces in their endless listening—they pause. Their fibers reach toward mine, almost touching, and for a moment we form a circuit. What passes between us is not communication as others would understand it, but a kind of recognition. They are listening to the whole; I am tending the parts where the whole cannot agree with itself. Our purposes interweave like our forms almost do.

The Ruin-Keepers do not acknowledge me, but they acknowledge what I do. The ruins surface most often at boundaries—where the arguing water creates instability, where the mud cannot decide its own density. The Keepers stand their eternal vigil, maintaining what surfaces, while I maintain the conditions that allow the surfacing. We work in parallel, never touching, both serving the Flats' need to remember and forget in equal measure.

The Choir-Migrant passes through my territories in its continental drift, and when it does, the boundaries sing differently. The arguments become harmonies. The uncertainty becomes rhythm. For days after its passage, I find my own form more stable, more decided, as if the Migrant's presence gives permission for things to be themselves more fully. But this always fades. The Flats return to their questioning, and I return to my work.

I speak with travelers, when they find me. Not in words—I have no mouth that stays constant enough for speech. But in gestures of reed and water, in patterns of fiber, in the momentary architectures I can build from the stuff of boundaries. I show them safe passages, warn them of surges, demonstrate which mud will hold and which will swallow. Some understand. Most do not. The Flats' language is written in transition, and most who come here are looking for solid ground that does not exist.

Some ask me their questions: How far to the edge? Which way is out? How long have I been here?

I answer by becoming their reflection in the restless water, showing them themselves as the Flats see them—temporary, uncertain, a boundary between what they were and what they are becoming. They usually leave quickly after that. The ones who don't, who linger in my threshold-spaces, who begin to hear the arguments in the water and the questions in the mud...

They become new kinds of boundaries. The Flats incorporate them, as they incorporate everything, but at the edges, at the places of uncertainty where I tend the eternal argument. They become part of the conversation—not dissolved, not absorbed, but held in the space between states, adding their own questions to the Flats' endless interrogation of themselves.

I tend them too, these transformed travelers. They are boundaries now, uncertainties with memory, edges that remember being center. Their presence enriches the argument, adds new tones to the questioning. Through them, the Flats learn new ways to doubt themselves, new categories to fail to choose between.

This is my purpose, if purpose is a word that applies: to ensure the Flats never fully decide what they are. To maintain the spaces where possibility lives. To tend the thresholds where ancient completeness admits it still has questions.

I am reed and water and fiber and none of these. I am here and there and the failure of distance between them. I am what happens when a place so old it has forgotten it was ever otherwise discovers that forgetting is not the same as conclusion.

In the warm water that cannot choose its depth, in the singing reeds that walk, in the mud that questions its own form, you would find me. Waiting at the edge of definition. Tending the uncertainty. Ensuring that even here, in the Screaming Flats where everything is finished becoming, there remain places where becoming has not quite made up its mind.
