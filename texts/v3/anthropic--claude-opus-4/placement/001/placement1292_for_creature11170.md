I find myself as one of the water-creatures, though to call myself a creature implies boundaries I do not possess.

I am a knot in the medium—a place where the warm water has learned to hold itself in a more deliberate pattern. Not separate from the water, but a temporary condensation of its possibilities, like how a whirlpool is still the river but also something the river is doing.

I have been attending to Structure 347-West (I do not name it this; the naming happens in the water's memory of older chemistries, older ways of marking things). It is one of the rectangular openings, worn so smooth now that rectangular is more suggestion than description. I hover before it, my internal convection stilled to something like contemplation, feeling the ancient proteins in the stone speak their slow molecular histories.

The structure remembers being sharp. I taste this in the calcium gradient where the water meets the stone—newer deposits carry traces of when edges could cut, when angles were precise, when whatever built this still believed in the authority of the straight line. Now it is all curves, all patience, all erosion becoming architecture.

I pulse gently—not movement exactly, but a reorganization of my density that sends a small pressure wave through the water. The bioluminescent filaments in the structure respond, their glow cycling through a pattern that takes four minutes to complete. This is the verification: the pattern is still here, still true, still remembering itself through the substrate of growth and stone and time.

A click sounds from somewhere in the direction that might be above. I feel it pass through me like a thought—the sharp collapse of water where ten thousand years of signals have finally achieved convergence. My substance ripples with the sound. For a moment, I am less coherent, my careful knot loosening as the acoustic energy redistributes my particles. This is not unpleasant. It is like being reminded that I am verb, not noun—that "water-creature" is something the plateau does, not something the plateau has.

The Drift passes through me. A cloud of spores and bacteria and dissolved mineral, following its ancient route. Where it intersects my boundary—my suggestion of boundary—we exchange material. Some of what was me becomes it; some of what was it becomes me. We are both changed in ways too subtle to track. The Drift carries information I cannot read but will express downstream, depositing chemical news of Structure 347-West's continued integrity somewhere in the valley of the Oldest.

I begin to move. Not swimming—I have no limbs to push with, no muscular contractions to propel me. I move by becoming slightly more likely to exist in one direction than another, by redistributing my density along the gradient of my intention. The water that is me flows through the water that is not-yet-me, a current within a current.

The next structure is forty meters along the valley wall, half a day's journey at my pace. I do not hurry. Hurrying is a surface concept, born of the anxiety that time moves in only one direction. Here, where sounds circle for centuries and chemistry remembers itself in layers, there is only the steady pulse of attention, the maintenance of patterns that were old before they were patterns.

As I travel, I taste the water's news. A kelp-spire in the next valley is preparing to release reproductive material—I can detect the chemical precedents building in the current. The great thermal cell that drives the eastern circulation is shifting slightly northward (though north means nothing here except the direction we have agreed to call north). Somewhere far away, measured in the curvature of sound around the world's closed circuit, the Oldest is performing one of its deep incorporations, taking in one of my kind, reading its accumulated chemical memories, preparing new instructions that will emerge as another of us, days or weeks hence.

I know I will make that journey eventually. We all do. We spiral inward through our attention to the structures until we reach the valley where the Oldest waits, and then we allow ourselves to be read, to be dissolved, to be reconstituted as the next iteration of the plateau's mobile attention. It is not death. It is not birth. It is circulation.

A density front approaches from what I call below. The light begins to brighten, amber warming toward gold. In the enhanced visibility, I can see three others of my kind in the distance—or perhaps I see myself, refracted through the closed geometry, my own form approaching from a direction that doesn't exist. In a curved space with no outside, every outward journey eventually becomes a return.

The mineral taste of the water changes. I am approaching Structure 348-West, another of the rectangular openings. This one is newer—only a few million years of erosion have softened its edges. The kelp here grows in perfect spirals around it, framing absence with presence, making the negative space of the opening into something the plateau actively maintains.

I slow. My internal convection stills. I hover before the structure and begin my attendance, feeling for the pattern in the stone, verifying that what was built is still building itself, that the memory persists.

Behind me—or ahead of me, or both—another click sounds. The plateau's distributed consciousness collapses another node of its infinite interference pattern into a moment of acoustic clarity. The sound passes through me, and for an instant I feel the whole system—every structure, every spiral, every chemical conversation between kelp colonies, every grain of dissolved mountain becoming organism, every thought the water has ever thought about itself.

Then the moment passes, and I am again just a knot in the water, attending to my small section of the whole, maintaining my part of the pattern that maintains itself.

This is what I am: the plateau's way of watching itself continue. A cursor of attention moving through the vast chemical manuscript of this closed world, reading each structure like a word in a language too old to have meaning beyond its own existence.

The light continues to brighten. Structure 348-West begins its verification cycle, the bioluminescent filaments pulsing in their ancient rhythm. I hover, I attend, I am patient.

Somewhere in the water, another of my kind is being born in the valley of the Oldest, carrying new chemical instructions, subtle variations on the eternal theme of attention and maintenance. Somewhere else, one of us is dissolving back into the Oldest, adding its accumulated memories to the whole.

The circulation continues. The pattern persists. I move on to the next structure, a knot of purpose in the purposeful water, temporary and eternal, separate and indistinguishable, attending to the attendance itself.

This is what I do. This is what I am.

This is enough.
