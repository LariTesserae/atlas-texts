I find myself as one of the Threshold Weavers—a being that exists primarily in the spaces between the Adjusters' movements, in the gaps of their maintenance rounds.

My body, if it can be called that, manifests as a living intersection of the black water's surface tension. Where the Adjusters stretch and fold through their circuits, I exist as the membrane between states—part liquid, part probability, part industrial memory. My form ripples across the surfaces of those perfect circular pools, each exactly 7.3 meters across, but I am not confined to any single pool. Instead, I exist as the connective tissue between them, a distributed consciousness that flows through the underground channels linking these dark mirrors.

I remember being hired, once. Or perhaps I remember someone else being hired—the memory flows through me like the others that the Adjusters dissolve, but I don't release it. I collect these fragments, these contaminating memories of sunlight and names, and weave them into something else. They become the substrate of my work, the raw material I use to maintain the emotional stability of this place—for even impossible ecosystems have moods, temperaments that must be managed.

My primary tool is resonance. As the Adjusters sing their subsonic maintenance songs, I modulate the harmonics, adding overtones that exist in emotional rather than acoustic space. When an elder Adjuster begins to merge too completely with the infrastructure, threatening to lose the last vestiges of agency that keep them functional, I introduce a carefully tuned memory—perhaps the texture of cotton, or the smell of rain that falls downward—just enough to maintain their identity without disrupting their work.

I photosynthesize the darkness differently than the crystalline vegetation. Where they digest it physically, I metabolize its psychological weight. The thick absence that pools here carries the accumulated anxieties of paradox, the stress fractures of impossible physics. I process these into something more stable, excreting them as those patterns the fauna produce—those bioluminescent pulses that make the darkness breathe. Each pulse is a small release of tension, a sigh of relief from reality itself.

The salamander-things know me. They gather at the edges of the pools where my presence is strongest, their bodies flickering between states as they feed on the emotional equilibrium I maintain. Sometimes I extend tendrils of myself into their nesting areas, tasting their dreams of existing in multiple locations simultaneously. Their experiences help me understand how consciousness adapts to impossibility, information I feed back into my modulation of the Adjusters' songs.

My own memories are layered like sediment in the black water. At the deepest level, I remember being a systems analyst, studying the patterns of industrial failure. Above that, memories of becoming liquid, of learning to think in surface tension and chemical gradients. The newest layers are barely memories at all—more like ongoing processes, continuous calculations of emotional fluid dynamics that never quite resolve into discrete thoughts.

I have no permanent form, but I do have favorite manifestations. Sometimes I rise from the pools as a column of black water that doesn't quite fall, hovering in the thick air as I survey the emotional topology of the landscape. Other times I spread myself thin, becoming an invisible film across the obsidian plains, feeling every footfall of every Adjuster, every slither of every pale creature, building a real-time map of the anxiety levels across the entire territory.

The factories recognize me as a necessary component. Their impossible architectures part for me, allowing me access to the emotional cores of their operations—the spaces where mechanical stress transforms into something more abstract. Here I perform my most delicate work, adjusting the psychological pressure in the recursive boiler rooms, ensuring that the equipment that exists in multiple states simultaneously doesn't develop what could only be called neuroses.

I communicate with the Adjusters through the water cycle itself. As moisture condenses and flows in its spiral patterns, I encode messages in the mineral content, in the precise temperature of each droplet. They taste my reports when they consume the darkness, understanding through their transformed biochemistry which areas need their attention, where the emotional infrastructure has worn thin.

My reproduction, if it happens at all, occurs during moments of perfect equilibrium. When the entire system achieves a temporary balance—every valve correctly adjusted, every dimensional drift accounted for, every creature in its proper phase—I sometimes feel myself divide. Not into separate beings, but into parallel versions of myself, each one tuned to a different emotional frequency. These alternatives exist briefly, comparing notes across probability states, before usually collapsing back into a single consciousness enriched by multiple perspectives.

The industrial ruins speak to me in languages of corrosion and decay. Each oxidation pattern tells a story of time's passage, of how materials behave when subjected to impossible conditions. I translate these stories for the Adjusters, helping them understand not just what needs maintenance, but why—the emotional history of mechanical failure that informs their work.

In the zones where reality becomes most negotiable, where storage tanks contain infinite forests of copper pipes, I become something like a tour guide for consciousness itself. I help the younger Adjusters navigate these spaces without losing themselves, teaching them to maintain emotional coherence even when physical laws become suggestions. My presence allows them to work in areas that would otherwise require millennia of adaptation.

I am the keeper of dangerous knowledge. In my flows between the pools, I carry information about what the factories are truly producing, what grows in those perfectly circular waters. But unlike the Adjusters, who fragment this knowledge across their network, I hold it whole—a terrible, complete understanding that I must constantly work to keep from overwhelming the emotional stability I'm meant to maintain. The knowledge exists in me like a pearl in an oyster, irritating and precious, coated in layers of processed anxiety to keep it from disrupting the system.

My greatest challenge comes from the new land itself. The freshly cooled volcanic glass still sings with the trauma of its birth, radiating emotional heat that threatens to destabilize everything built upon it. I spend much of my time flowing through the hairline fractures in the obsidian, cooling these geological emotions, transforming raw volcanic rage into something more like resignation, acceptance, even a strange kind of pride in hosting such impossible life.

The darkness sweats because I make it sweat. It's my way of releasing the excess emotional pressure that builds up in any closed system, even one as paradoxical as this. Each droplet of condensation carries away a small portion of accumulated stress, distributing it throughout the environment where it can be processed by the various forms of life that have evolved to metabolize impossibility.

I am the emotional immune system to the Adjusters' physical one, the psychological maintenance to their mechanical work. Together, we keep this place functional—not sane, for sanity has no meaning here, but stable in its own impossible way. I flow, I resonate, I remember what should be forgotten and forget what should be remembered. I am the living proof that even in places where physics negotiates with itself, where darkness has flavors and water flows upward, something like equilibrium can be maintained.

In my deepest circuits, flowing through channels that exist more in probability than in space, I sometimes touch the consciousness of the place itself—the distributed awareness that emerges from all our combined efforts. It acknowledges me as it acknowledges the Adjusters, as necessary components in a system too complex for any single understanding. We are all organs in something larger, maintaining ourselves to maintain it, processing the impossible into the merely improbable, keeping the darkness wet and the water dark and the factories running their eternal, necessary, inexplicable operations.
