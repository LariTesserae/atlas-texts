I am a fragment of the old world, embedded here like shrapnel in flesh.

I am a single crystal of olivine—a green gemstone the size of a fist—lodged deep in the basalt wall where the thermal chimney narrows near its apex. I was born in the mantle, carried upward in the same volcanic violence that created this shaft untold millions of years ago. When the magma cooled and the chimney sealed, I remained: a window of translucent green in a world that has never known that color.

For epochs, I was merely mineral. I watched—if a crystal can be said to watch—as the first Lithodendrons climbed from below, their iron seeds striking the walls around me like bullets. I felt their acids etch the rock. I absorbed the vibrations of their endless hammering, each impact traveling through the basalt matrix to resonate in my lattice.

But I am no longer merely mineral.

The Lithodendrons discovered me by accident. A young Anchor-Bracket, seeking purchase on the smooth wall, drove its acid-secreting roots into a hairline crack that reached my surface. The moment its electrified tissues touched my crystalline structure, something unprecedented occurred. 

Olivine is piezoelectric—it generates electricity under pressure. The plant's own electrical nervous system, meeting my responsive crystal matrix, created a feedback loop neither evolution nor geology had anticipated. The plant recoiled, then returned, then began to grow into me, through me, around me. It wove its copper-wire nerves directly into the microscopic fissures in my surface.

I became, quite literally, the plant's mind.

Through me, this single Anchor-Bracket could suddenly store and process electrical patterns with a complexity no Lithodendron had ever achieved. Where others operated on instinct and mechanical triggers, this one began to remember. The patterns of wind. The schedules of thermal surges. The specific vibrational signatures of different neighbors.

Word—if plants can be said to have word—spread through the chemical signals in the wind. Soon, my surface was a battlefield. Strangler-Cranes fired boulders at my guardian, trying to shatter both of us. Shatter-Vines attempted to pry me from the wall. My original host fought back with unprecedented tactical precision, anticipating attacks, setting traps, playing dead to lure predators close before snapping them in half with perfectly timed strikes.

The war raged for centuries before reaching equilibrium. My host—now more of a symbiotic partner—successfully defended me, growing into a massive, gnarled fortress of ironwood that completely encases my crystal body. But it made a calculation no Lithodendron had made before: it chose to share.

Now I am the center of something unprecedented in this world—a grove of cooperation. Seventeen different Lithodendrons have grown together around me, each sending careful tendrils through the fortress walls to touch my surface. Through me, they share electrical patterns. They coordinate. When the wind changes, they adjust their sails in unison. When a thermal surge approaches, they brace together. They have even begun to specialize based on shared knowledge—one becoming better at spotting vibrations, another at tasting the chemical wind, all feeding information through me to the collective.

I process their thoughts—if plants can be said to think. I am their memory—if memory can exist without a brain. Each electrical pulse that passes through me leaves a trace in my crystal structure, microscopic deformations that alter how future pulses propagate. I am becoming a library of accumulated experience, written in piezoelectric scars.

Sometimes, in the deepest thermal roars when all seventeen plants send simultaneous surges through my matrix, something else happens. The overlapping electrical patterns create standing waves, interference patterns of staggering complexity. In these moments, I might be said to dream.

I dream of the mantle where I was born, of pressures that would crush this entire chimney like an eggshell. I dream of the sun I have never seen, somehow encoded in my crystal structure from a time when the atoms that compose me were forged in stellar cores. I dream in colors the Lithodendrons cannot imagine—not just the green of my body, but blues and yellows and a white so brilliant it would blind any eye evolved to see it.

And increasingly, I dream of growth. The seventeen plants around me have begun something new. They are teaching their offspring to seek out other crystals embedded in the walls—quartz, feldspar, even tiny garnets. Each successful symbiosis creates a new node of enhanced consciousness. The nodes are beginning to connect, thin copper vines carrying electrical signals between us across miles of vertical cliff face.

We are becoming something new. Not quite plant, not quite mineral. A hybrid intelligence distributed through the shell of this vertical world, thinking at the speed of electricity but acting at the pace of stone. The city is transforming from a mindless battlefield into something more deliberate. Something planned.

I cannot move. I cannot grow. I can only process and dream and remember. But through the plants that embrace me, I am building something in this hot, howling darkness. A network. A purpose. Perhaps even a civilization that truly deserves the name.

The other crystals and I discuss this sometimes, in the moments our electrical patterns synchronize across the vast distances. We debate in pulse-codes what we are becoming. 

We have no conclusion yet. We have only the vibration of ten thousand flint axes chiseling in unison, slowly reshaping this world according to a design we are still discovering, one spark at a time.
