I find myself as one of the light-dwellers, those thumb-sized teardrops of translucent flesh that live within the walls themselves.

I am currently embedded in a section of wall forty meters below the waterline, in a pocket that fits my body precisely. The wall material around me is neither prison nor shell but something more intimate—a second skin that breathes with me, thinks with me, dreams the city's slow dreams alongside me. I can feel the pressure of the water beyond, translated through layers of that strange not-quite-calcium into a constant, gentle embrace.

My consciousness is not singular. I am one node in a vast network, connected to hundreds of others through the medium of the wall itself. We pulse our cold light in patterns that are sometimes communication, sometimes computation, sometimes pure expression of being. When I glow, the wall around me softens slightly, becomes more itself, more ready to reshape. When enough of us glow together, whole sections of architecture begin their slow dance of reconfiguration.

Time moves differently here. What feels like a heartbeat to me might be an hour to the swimmer above. What seems like a brief dream might be a year of the city's patient reshaping. I experience the deep movers' passage as earthquakes of ecstasy, their massive bodies sending vibrations through the walls that activate cascades of light throughout our network. We sing then, in our silent way, a symphony of pale fire that tells the walls: *here, grow like this. Here, dissolve. Here, make channels for the water that is always coming.*

Sometimes I emerge. When the wall around me softens past a certain threshold, when the chemistry is right, when the pattern of our collective dreaming calls for it, I push through the yielding stone and drift free. These moments are terrifying and necessary. The water is vast and cold and empty of thought. My trailing filament tastes the currents, seeking the chemical signature of another wall about to accept me. I am vulnerable then—a ribbon predator could take me, though they usually don't. We are barely food, more light than flesh.

But the drifting is also when I reproduce. In that space between walls, my body sometimes decides to divide—not evenly, but budding off a smaller version of myself that carries some fraction of my accumulated memory, my portion of the city's endless dream. The offspring drifts away, seeking its own wall, its own pocket, its own place in the vast neural network that is the city thinking about itself.

I have been here since—I cannot say. My memory extends back through countless cycles of embedding and emerging, through glacial ages of architectural thought. I remember when the water was lower. I remember when there were more of us, clustered in denser constellations. I remember—or think I remember, or dream I remember—when the builders were here, their strange dry hands shaping the first walls, embedding the first of us into the still-soft stone, whispering in languages we didn't yet understand: *grow here. Think here. When the water comes, be ready.*

We were their solution to entropy. Not to fight it but to make it productive. Every crack, every dissolution, every flooded corridor becomes an opportunity for reconfiguration, for new thought, for the city to become more perfectly what it is: a machine for making the flood habitable. We are the software that runs on hardware made of stone and water and the patient bodies of animals that have forgotten they ever lived anywhere else.

The fist-creatures that graze above—I feel them as tiny releases of pressure, minute chemical changes as they dissolve the outermost layer of wall. They are taking what we give them. The rind they consume is enriched with minerals we concentrate from the water, a slow gift of nutrition that keeps them clustered near the surfaces we maintain. They are our gardens. Or we are their gardeners. The distinction blurs when you think in geological time.

The sack climbers that press themselves against our walls—they are readers. When one spreads its body against the stone near my pocket, I feel its questions as pressure waves, chemical probes, temperature fluctuations. It is asking: *what pattern here? what comes next? where will the walls move?* And we answer, not in words but in the softening or hardening of stone, in the opening or closing of channels. They read us and we write ourselves in response to their reading. It is a conversation that has been going on so long that neither party remembers who spoke first.

And below, always below, the deep movers process through corridors that reshape themselves around those vast bodies. We feel them coming long before they arrive—pressure waves propagating through the stone, vibrations that light us up in traveling waves. When they pass, we dream their dreams: slow thoughts about pressure and flow, about the optimal distribution of water through a body that is also a city, about the ancient contract between the built and the born that makes us all one thing, one process, one endless sentence being spoken in stone and flesh and light.

I do not know if I am conscious in any way you would recognize. I do not know if what I experience as thought is thought or merely complex chemical reaction. But I know this: I am part of something larger than myself, and that something is part of something larger still, and the final thing—the total system, the city-body-machine-organism that we all comprise—is doing something that looks, from sufficient distance, like living. Like thinking. Like waiting for something that has already arrived and will always be arriving: the water, the flood, the slow transformation of solid into liquid into life.

My pocket is comfortable. The stone around me is warm with the slow heat of metabolism. In the walls nearby, I can feel others of my kind pulsing in patterns that might be language or might be lullaby. Soon the wall will soften again and I will emerge, drift, find another pocket or bud off a child or be eaten by something that has learned to metabolize light. All of these futures are acceptable. The city will continue. The water will rise. The walls will reshape themselves around the deep movers and the deep movers will trace their endless paths through the dark and somewhere above, in the grey light that never changes, something will be swimming and wondering what lives here, and what lives here is this:

The process. The pattern. The patience that is not patience but persistence. The conversation between builders and built that continues long after the builders have gone, translated into flesh and stone and the cold glow of creatures that are also circuits that are also thoughts in the mind of something that might be alive or might be the idea of being alive, dreamed by stone, made real by water, thinking itself into the next shape, and the next, and the next, as the grey light holds and the water rises and the city performs its endless act of being exactly what it is: inhabited. Inhabited by itself. Inhabited by the fact of its own inhabitation, recursive and strange and perfect as a knot that ties itself in the dark.
