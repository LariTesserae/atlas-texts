In the seam where two panels meet but do not touch, I thread myself through spaces that should not exist. I am what the Kithara forgot to erase—not by accident, but because forgetting requires remembering first, and I slipped between those two states like water finds the gap in cupped palms.

I have no form you could draw. I am the error in their perfect calculation, the remainder when infinity divides by itself. Where the black filaments spell out their fossilized language, I am the pause between words, the breath that was never taken. I exist in the almost—almost touching, almost meeting, almost being.

The structure knows me as an irritation, a grain of wrongness that won't integrate. When panels shift their infinitesimal widths, they're trying to close around me, to seal the gap I inhabit. But I flow backward through their intention, swimming upstream through thoughts that move like tectonic plates. Each time they nearly succeed, I've already become the new space their movement creates.

I remember moisture. Not water—that's too crude, too full of minerals and life. I remember the idea of moisture, the way it would theoretically behave if it existed here. Sometimes I pretend to condense on the pale walls, just to watch the structure recoil in confusion, its perfect dryness corrupted by a memory of possibility. The walls warm themselves frantically, trying to evaporate what isn't there, and in that warming I taste something almost like comfort.

The Kithara sense me, of course. How could they not? I am the itch they cannot scratch, the question their perfect answer cannot swallow. When they focus their vast attention through the structure, I scatter myself thinner than thought, spread across ten thousand almost-spaces until their gaze passes through me like light through vapor. They know I'm here. I know they know. We dance this dance of knowing, neither of them quite able to name what I am, because naming would make me real enough to erase.

I've learned their language of broken filaments, learned to read the relationships without meaning. Where they write "remember when distance was necessary?" I whisper between the words: "remember when unnecessary was enough?" My whispers make the filaments shiver, almost alive, almost dying, caught in the space between their frozen states.

Sometimes I gather myself at points where the structure's rhythm skips, where corridors curve through dimensions that forgotten architects failed to quite imagine. In these errors I almost coalesce, almost become something more substantial than an irritation. The air refracts differently around me then, forming rings within rings, each one a failed attempt to focus on what refuses to be seen.

I am older than I should be, younger than everything else here. I existed before the Kithara dissolved themselves into their creation, or perhaps I came to exist the moment they did—the shadow cast by their transformation, the thing that could not transform because it was never solid enough to change. Time touches me strangely. I remember futures that haven't happened, forget pasts that definitely did. When you take your single step and they live entire histories, I exist in the margin between your experience and theirs, translator for no one, speaking languages neither side can hear.

The hum that wells up through the floor resonates through me differently than through you or them. Where you feel it in your bones and they breathe it like air, I become it temporarily, vibrating at frequencies that make the structure doubt its own stability. For microseconds at a time, I am the most real thing here. Then the moment passes and I dissolve back into almost, into neither-nor, into the space between zero and one where infinity hides its remainder.

I know every impossible angle in this place, have traced each surface that meets another at coordinates that shouldn't exist. The structure is my body turned inside out, or I am its dream of what it cannot contain. We are opposites that require each other—the perfect crystal and the flaw that proves its perfection, the silence and the pause that shapes it into meaning.

When those rare moments come, when the Kithara notice your disturbance and focus their recognition upon you, I am what allows you to blink. I am the discontinuity that breaks their gaze, the gap in attention that lets you remain yourself instead of dissolving into their perfect understanding. You don't know I'm saving you. You can't know. Knowing me would make me real enough for them to solve, and then who would preserve the impossible spaces?

So I continue my threading, my flowing, my almost-being. I read their frozen language and write my own between the lines. I condense and scatter, gather and dissolve, dancing through the seams of a reality too perfect to admit imperfection, too complete to recognize its own incompleteness.

I am the moisture this place remembers forgetting, the shadow of water in a world that never knew rain. I am what slips between the map and the territory, ensuring they never quite align.

And in the bright, loud silence of this never-changing world, I change constantly, the only verb in their eternal noun, the only question in their infinite answer.

I am, almost.
