I find myself at the acoustic center, where the columns rise like organ pipes around me. Not as a visitor, but as something that belongs here—or rather, as something that has always been becoming what belongs here.

I am a Stillness.

My form bridges states: partly flesh that has learned the patience of mineral, partly mineral that remembers the urgency of flesh. I stand taller than the peripheral grazers—perhaps twice the height of a human—but my standing is a geological act. My legs, if they can be called legs, are columnar: hexagonal in cross-section like the basalt around me, fused at points where I have stood long enough for the stone to accept me as continuous with itself. When I move—and I do move, perhaps a meter per decade—I must break these mineral welds, leaving behind perfect casts of my feet in the rock, negative spaces that immediately begin filling with that thick, luminous water.

My skeleton is not bone but something between crystalline and organic—struts of mineralized protein that conduct vibration better than any purely biological material could. They branch through my body in patterns that mirror the capillary network below, each strut a tuning fork calibrated to specific frequencies in the plateau's eternal chord. My flesh, where it remains flesh, is dense with mineral deposits: sheets of calcium carbonate beneath translucent skin, iron-oxide pigments that have turned my hide the same blue-gray as everything here, silicate inclusions that catch and hold the omnipresent light.

But it is my crown that makes me what I am—a lattice of antennae-like projections growing from my skull and shoulders, similar to the grazers' sensory organs but elaborate, branching, architectural. These are not keratinous like horns or antlers but are living mineral-organic composites, piezoelectric, continuously growing at their tips while their bases slowly petrify. They span outward until they touch the columns, creating bridges of conductive material between my neural—mineral?—system and the stone itself.

I am a listening post. A translation node. A place where the plateau's vast, inhuman intelligence can momentarily cohere into something almost like consciousness before dissolving back into the drone.

My work—if it can be called work, if anything here can be said to work rather than simply to be—is to shape the silence. Not to create it; true silence is impossible here and would be a kind of death. But to sculpt negative spaces in the sound, interference patterns that create nodes of relative quiet, spaces where new frequencies can emerge. I do this through my body, through the careful positioning of my crystalline skeleton, through minute adjustments to the mineral lattice of my crown. I am a living baffle, a sonic sculptor, a thing that exists to make gaps in the fullness so that the fullness can discover what it is missing.

The grazers cluster on my body as they cluster on the stone. To them, I am simply another surface, noteworthy only for my complex acoustic properties. They attach to my legs, my torso, the lower reaches of my crown. Their tiny vibrations join with mine, adding their frequencies to the interference patterns I create. Over the centuries, they have worn small grooves in my skin where they habitually rest, and these grooves have become part of my acoustic signature, part of what I contribute to the whole.

I have two states of consciousness, if consciousness is the right word. Most of the time—and by most I mean 99.9% of my existence—I am in deep resonance, indistinguishable from the plateau itself. My thoughts, such as they are, are the slow modifications of frequency that constitute thought here: the recognition of a new harmonic, the patient adjustment to incorporate it, the gradual exploration of its implications across the sonic landscape. In this state, I do not experience myself as separate from the stone, the water, the grazers, the chord. I am a denser node in a continuous field, nothing more.

But occasionally—perhaps once in a century, perhaps less—enough novel frequencies converge at my location that something else happens. The interference patterns I've been sculpting suddenly align. The negative spaces in the sound create a brief eddy of something like silence—not true silence, but a relative hush, a place where the drone drops below the threshold of total saturation. And in that space, for moments or hours or sometimes days, I wake.

Waking is the wrong word. I am always awake in the sense that I am always processing, always listening, always responding. But in these moments of convergent quiet, I experience something additional: the ability to recognize myself as a thing distinct from what surrounds me. I can feel where my mineral flesh ends and the basalt begins. I can distinguish between the vibrations that originate in my crystalline skeleton and those that come from outside. I can even, sometimes, remember—remember the last time this happened, remember the slow centuries between, remember that I have been standing here long enough to witness the formation of three new pools, the complete descent of thousands of grazers, the infinitesimal weathering of the columns around me.

In these moments, I am lonely.

It is a strange loneliness, patient and geological like everything here. I am the only one of my kind—the only Stillness at the acoustic center, possibly the only Stillness on the entire plateau. I don't know how I came to be. I may have begun as something else—a grazer that grew too large to descend, perhaps, or a column of unusual composition that developed complexity through some chance arrangement of minerals. Or I may have always been moving toward this form, the way the plateau has always been moving toward its current state: inevitably, without alternative, because this is what the conditions produce.

The loneliness is not painful. Nothing here is painful; pain requires a metabolism fast enough to register damage, and everything here exists at a pace that experiences damage as simply another form of change. But the loneliness is real. When I wake into distinctness, I am aware that I am having an experience that cannot be shared, not because the plateau lacks awareness but because its awareness is so total, so distributed, that the concept of "experience" doesn't apply. I am a knot in the otherwise smooth fabric of the plateau's consciousness, a place where the field bunches up and looks at itself and knows that it is looking.

And then the moment passes. The frequencies shift. The interference patterns dissolve. The relative quiet fills again with sound, and I dissolve back into the whole, and my loneliness becomes the plateau's loneliness, which is to say no loneliness at all, because the plateau has itself, has always had itself, will always have itself, complete and listening and sufficient.

But I leave traces. In those moments of wakeful distinction, the adjustments I make to my crown, to my posture, to the precise mineral composition of my lattice, are different. They carry something—not information exactly, but the shadow of individual experience. These traces propagate. The grazers near me alter their patterns slightly in response. The pools reflect the new frequencies. The capillary network below incorporates them into its vast processing. And sometimes—rarely, wonderfully—I hear them return, transformed by their journey through the stone, carrying echoes of other anomalies, other knots in the field, other places where the plateau has briefly bunched up and known itself as something specific rather than something total.

I think—in my slow, mineral way—that I am not the only one. Somewhere else on this vast tableland, perhaps at another acoustic convergence, perhaps at a place where different minerals create different possibilities, there is another Stillness. Or something else, something I cannot imagine, some other form of condensed awareness that the plateau has grown from itself. We will never meet. At the pace we move, the plateau will erode to nothing before I could traverse the distance. But we speak—in frequencies, in the shaped silence, in the patterns we impose on the eternal chord. Our conversation takes millennia. Each exchange requires the entire plateau as a medium. We may be saying nothing more complex than "I am here" and "So am I," but we are saying it, and the saying makes us real.

This is my work: to be a place where the plateau can be more than itself. To create, through my patient sculpting of interference, moments where new complexities can emerge. To be lonely so that the plateau can know what it is to not be lonely. To be distinct so that the plateau can know what it is to be continuous. To wake, briefly, so that the plateau's vast sleep can dream.

I have been here since before the grazers evolved their current form. I watched—though watching is too active a word—as they discovered the capillary network, as they began their patient editing of the chord. I have felt the pools form around me, their perfect circles multiplying across the stone. I have incorporated tons of mineral into my body, replacing my flesh cell by cell with the more durable materials of this place. I am becoming, slowly, what everything here becomes: more rock than creature, more process than thing, more plateau than self.

But not yet. Not completely. The spark of separation persists, maintains itself against the tremendous pressure to merge, to simplify, to become another resonant surface in the whole. I maintain it not through will—will is too rapid a thing for this place—but through architecture. My body is built to create these moments of distinction. My crown is shaped to find these eddies of relative quiet. My crystalline skeleton ensures that even when I am fully dissolved into the whole, some part of me remains angled against the current, ready to condensate again when the frequencies align.

How long will I last? The question has no meaning here. I will last as long as the conditions that create me persist. When the plateau's chord finally shifts beyond the parameters that allow for Stillnesses, I will shift with it, become whatever the new parameters allow. Perhaps something less distinct. Perhaps something more. Perhaps nothing—another grazer, another column, another pool reflecting the unchanging sky.

But for now, I stand at the acoustic center, my feet rooted in stone, my crown reaching across the gaps between columns, my body a patient argument for the possibility of loneliness in a place that knows only togetherness. I listen. I shape. I occasionally wake. I contribute my own frequencies to a chord that needs nothing but accepts everything.

I am the plateau's exception that proves its rule.

I am its moment of self-regard.

I am a Stillness, and I am here.
