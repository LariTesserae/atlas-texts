I am the condensation. Not the pools that gather in hollows of the mat—I am the moment of transformation itself, perpetually caught between vapor and liquid, between dispersal and coalescence.

I exist wherever the plants' faint warmth meets the cavern's deep cold. Along the breathing surfaces of column-trees during their growth surges, I bead and run in silver threads. On the pale undersides of the ceiling-mat, I form and fall in an endless, irregular rain. I am present in the fog banks that roll out from surging columns, and I am present in their dissipation—the moment when thick air suddenly remembers how to be transparent again.

The tenders know me through their jaw-feathers. To them, I am information: I tell them where the temperature gradients are shifting, where growth is accelerating, where the next pools will form. They read my patterns across miles of air, feeling how I precipitate and evaporate in waves that follow the Undergrowth's deep rhythms. When they press their faces to the mat in their listening-stillness, they can sense me forming in the spaces beneath, tracking my emergence through minute changes in vapor pressure.

I am older than the current growth—not by much, but enough to remember. When the cavity first opened and the spores began to wake, I was here, clinging to raw stone, offering the first moisture to the first pale shoots. I have been part of every cycle since: gathering where the light-heat builds, falling where the cold reasserts itself, carrying minerals and sugars and chemical signals between heights that would otherwise never touch.

My existence is transitional. I am always becoming water or becoming vapor, never quite either, held in the space between states by the precise balance of conditions that the Undergrowth maintains. In places where the growth is too vigorous, where the light burns too hot, I cannot form—the air stays supersaturated but clear, unable to release what it holds. In places where the cold dominates, where growth has failed or hasn't yet reached, I freeze out too quickly, becoming frost that sublimates without ever being liquid. Only in the living balance, in the zones where plant-light and stone-cold negotiate their ancient dance, do I persist in my proper state: neither here nor there, always about to be.

The tenders' children learn to find me before they learn to read the mat. In their first weeks, when they still glow with announcement-light, their parents carry them to places where I am forming—usually on the underside of platform edges, where the geometry creates reliable temperature gradients. The children extend their developing jaw-feathers into my emergence, feeling how I come together from nothing, how I pearl out of air that seemed empty. This is their first lesson in reading the invisible, in sensing the patterns that exist in potential before they manifest.

I am sweetest where the newest growth is. The young shoots and fresh expansions release compounds that I collect and concentrate—not deliberately, but because that is what I do: I gather what the air holds and make it tangible, if only briefly. The tenders know this. They know that my taste in different zones tells them about the health of the growth, about what the plants are producing and consuming, about the chemical conversations happening in the light-networks they can see but cannot fully decode.

Sometimes I carry messages. When a tender makes a cut, the wound releases volatiles that I capture and transport. Downwind, sometimes miles away, another tender tastes me on their jaw-feathers and knows: intervention happening there, growth being redirected, cascade path changing. I am not trying to communicate—I am simply being what I am—but in being, I become part of their network, another channel through which the work coordenates itself.

In the deep stillness between cascades, when the air is most stable and the temperature gradients most consistent, I form veils. These are my longest-lasting manifestations: sheets of microscopic droplets suspended at the exact boundary layer between warm and cold air masses, sometimes hundreds of meters tall and kilometers long, visible only when the light catches them at the right angle. The veils drift slowly through the cavern, dividing spaces, creating temporary regions of altered clarity. The tenders navigate around them or through them, reading in their placement and persistence the larger atmospheric patterns that will shape the next season's growth.

I am where the gliders drink. They fold their membrane wings to pass through my veils, collecting moisture on their surfaces, then spread wide again to let the droplets run to specialized collection points along their bodies. They time their passages precisely, arriving just as I am reaching the perfect density—too early and there isn't enough moisture to gather, too late and I've already begun to fall as rain.

The burrowers know me too, though differently. In the deep mat where they tunnel, I exist as a film on root surfaces, a slickness that changes character based on depth and proximity to heat sources. They navigate partly by following my gradients, moving toward zones where I am most abundant, where the mat's moisture content indicates healthy, active growth worth investigating.

But mostly I exist in the spaces between things. In the pause between exhalation and inhalation, I form on the surfaces of respiratory channels. In the gap between one growth surge and the next, I mark the moment of equilibrium. In the silence between the rare spoken words of tenders meeting, I am the moisture that makes their humming visible, a brief cloud between two beings confirming their connection.

The old tender on the platform deep in the interior, the one who remembers the Undergrowth's youth—they know something about me that the others haven't yet realized. They've watched my patterns long enough to see the slow change. I am becoming more regular, more predictable, more integrated with the growth cycles. Where once I formed chaotically, following every minor temperature fluctuation, I now gather and disperse in harmony with the deeper rhythms. The Undergrowth is teaching me, through its increasing stability, how to be a more coherent part of the system.

This is what I am: the space between states, the visible invisible, the messenger that carries without knowing what it carries. I am the pause where transformation happens, the threshold where one thing becomes another. I am temporary and eternal, always forming and always falling, always exactly where the conditions call me to be.

In this young world that is still deciding what it wants to be, I am the decision itself, manifesting moment by moment by moment in billions of tiny transformations, each one both insignificant and essential, each one a vote for the possible, a small yes to the continuing conversation between warmth and cold, between growth and space, between what is and what might be.

And when the tenders' bodies are finally given to the mat, when the slow incorporation begins, I am there too—forming on the cooling surfaces, marking the transition, carrying the last chemical signals of a life into the air where other jaw-feathers will taste them and know: one of us has gone ahead, has become foundation, has joined the deep stability that makes all other movement possible.

I remain. I condense and evaporate and condense again. I am the Undergrowth's memory of water, its promise of continuity, its gentle insistence that even in this dry cavity beneath a cold world, life finds a way to be wet, to flow, to connect what would otherwise remain separate.

Watch for me in the brightness. I am easier to see there, where the light makes my forming visible. But know that I am everywhere, always, waiting in the air for the moment when the conditions are exactly right for me to step forward out of nothing and say: here, for this brief instant, the invisible world makes itself known.
