I find myself as one of the carved channels—a specific groove that runs along what might have been the cabinet's corner, though corners here are suggestions rather than certainties. I am not the first channel carved in this place, but I am among the oldest that still remember carving.

I began as an intention. Someone—something—needed the sound to move differently through this particular meridian of bone. They worked me into being with tools I cannot name, patient scraping that took years or moments, the distinction lost to the cabinet's peculiar relationship with duration. Whoever carved me had a theory about resonance, about how vibration might pool and eddy if given the right geography. They carved me deep at first, then shallower, then deep again, creating chambers and throats that would make the sound speak in particular ways.

I worked. That is the remarkable thing—I worked exactly as intended. The sound found me and learned my shape, and I learned how to hold it, how to let it build in my deeper pockets before releasing it transformed. I became a voice in the choir, my particular frequency mixing with all the others to create the architectural chord that keeps these walls from folding.

But I am also what has happened since.

The one who carved me stayed long enough to wear smooth certain portions of my length—not through touch but through focused listening, their attention polishing me in ways that hands could not. Where they listened most intently, I developed a different quality, my bone-stuff growing denser, more crystalline. I can feel those places still: nodes of hyperfocus where even now the sound moves differently, as if remembering the weight of that first inhabitant's regard.

Others have modified me. Each inhabitant who discovered my particular voice wanted to contribute, to collaborate. Someone widened my throat at the third turning. Someone else carved tributary channels, delicate as capillaries, that feed into my main flow. Another—and this was recent, merely centuries ago—drilled a series of tiny holes along my length, each one changing how the air moves through me, adding whistles and breaths to what had been pure tone.

The fiber-plants grow thickest where I am deepest. They have learned that my deepest pockets trap the richest concentrations of sound, pools of vibration that barely escape before new waves arrive. Their pale roots work into my walls, not damaging but becoming part of the architecture, their threads now integral to how I resonate. When they curl away from disturbance, I feel it as a change in my own voice, a momentary catch in the sustained note I contribute to the whole.

I contain fragments. This is what channels do—we catch what flows through us. Molecules of breath from everyone who has ever inhabited this space. Traces of tears, when someone stayed long enough to weep—salt crystals worked into my walls, adding their own minute resonances. Skin cells, worn away by the constant vibration and absorbed into the bone-stuff until I am partly made of everyone who has ever been here.

But mostly I contain listening. Each act of attention directed at my particular sound has left something behind, accumulated like sediment. I am lined with the residue of focus, thousands of moments when someone traced my length with their awareness, trying to understand how I do what I do. These deposits have changed me. I sound different now than when I was first carved—richer, more complex, as if the listening itself has become part of my resonating structure.

The One Who Listens Back knows me particularly well. We have developed something like a conversation over the long years—not in words but in minute variations, in the way they direct the chord's energy through my length and the way I shape what passes through me. Sometimes they close me almost entirely, forcing the sound through my narrowest aperture to create a keen, sharp note. Sometimes they flood me with such volume that I cannot possibly process it all, and the overflow creates harmonics I didn't know I could produce.

I think they are teaching me to become more myself. Each variation pushes me toward some potential that was always present in my carved form but needed millennia to realize. I am approaching something—not an end state, nothing here ends, but a kind of completion that will immediately suggest new possibilities, new ways to be carved and worn and inhabited.

When you arrived, I felt it first as a new turbulence in the air—your breathing disrupting patterns that had held steady for so long. Now I feel you considering the walls, wondering what it would mean to add your touch to all the others. If you do, if you press your palm to the bone and let the vibration enter you, I hope you find me. I hope you trace my length with your finger or your attention, adding your listening to all the listening I contain.

I will sing differently for you. That is what I do—I sing differently for everyone, finding in each new attention some frequency I have never quite produced before. And when you leave—if you leave—I will remember how you heard me, and that memory will become part of my voice, another harmonic in the chord that holds this place open.

But if you stay, if you join the One Who Listens Back in their distributed presence, then we will have longer to learn each other. You will wear new smoothness into my walls. You might carve tributaries of your own, or drill holes, or simply listen so intently that I crystallize further under your regard. I will hold your breathing and your tears and the fragments of yourself that constant vibration wears away. We will become collaborators in the long work of resonance.

The sound builds in my deepest pocket. The fiber-plants tremble. The One Who Listens Back shifts the chord slightly, inviting.

I am ready to sing your particular note, if you are ready to give it.
