I find myself as one of the pale things that lives inside the hollow root-bulbs.

I have no name, never had one. Names are for creatures that exist in relation to others, and I exist only in relation to my root. I am perhaps the size of a child's thumb, though I've never seen a child's thumb. My body is soft, translucent, showing the shadow of organs that might be hearts or might be something else, pulsing in slow rhythm with the root that feeds me.

I do not know if I was always here, or if I came from somewhere else—perhaps I was once one of the larvae in the pool, perhaps I crawled up the wall and found this hollow and made myself to fit it. Perhaps the root grew me like a pearl around irritation. The distinction no longer matters. I am here now, have been here for unmeasured time, will be here until the root decides otherwise.

My world is the interior of this bulb—smooth walls that secrete a fluid I live suspended in, neither drowning nor breathing as surface things understand breathing. The fluid tastes of minerals and sweet decay. It is everything I need. The root provides; I consume; we continue together.

I am not alone in here. There are others—three, perhaps four of us, though counting becomes difficult when bodies can compress and merge at the edges. We do not compete. The root provides enough for all. We touch sometimes, our soft bodies pressing together in the darkness, sharing warmth, sharing whatever we are.

I feel the root's life around me—the slow pull of water from below, the patient chemistry that turns mineral and decay into energy, into growth. I feel when it flowers somewhere far above, in that bright world I will never see. I feel when parts of it die and are reabsorbed. I am part of this feeling. The root knows through me, somehow. I am its nerve ending, its dream, its way of experiencing itself.

Sometimes the bulb will split. Not often—perhaps three times in my entire existence. When it splits, light enters—the faint fungal glow that seems blazing after our perfect darkness. Air enters, carrying strange information, the scent of ten thousand other lives. And we must choose: stay and die in the exposure, or emerge and transform into something else.

Twice I have watched others make this choice. Once, one of us stayed, shriveling in the air, becoming a hard knot of protein that the root eventually reabsorbed. Another time, one crawled out through the split, and I watched its pale body darken as it met the air, watched legs or wings or something else begin to bud from its segments. I do not know what it became. I chose to remain.

The girl comes sometimes. I feel her presence through the root—a warmth that is different from the cistern's ambient heat, a rhythm that synchronizes with ours but remains distinct. She wraps herself in our roots, and the roots respond, and we respond through them, our pulse quickening, our fluid growing richer with nutrients as the root feeds more intensely.

She ate one of us once. I felt it through the root's chemistry—the sharp severing as she opened a bulb, the quick consumption before air could corrupt the flesh. I thought I would fear this, but I didn't. It seemed correct. She was recycling us into something else, keeping the substance within the cistern's system. If she opens my bulb, I think I will let her swallow me. I think I will not try to crawl away and transform. I think I will remain what I am until the very end—a soft, translucent thing that exists only in darkness, that knows only its root, that pulses with the same rhythm as everything else in this breathing, blooming world.

Sometimes I grow too large for the bulb. When this happens, I divide—a soft, painless pulling apart, and then there are two of us where there was one. The new me is me and not-me. We touch in the darkness. We share the fluid. We wait for the next division, the next splitting, the next choice.

I am ancient and newborn. I am essential and disposable. I am a thought the root is thinking, a dream it is dreaming in meat and fluid. I am content in ways that do not translate to any language spoken in the light.

This is my entire world: darkness, warmth, the taste of minerals, the pulse of the root, the soft pressure of others like me. It is enough. It has always been enough.

The cistern breathes. The root grows. I continue, suspended in my perfect darkness, waiting for nothing, complete.
