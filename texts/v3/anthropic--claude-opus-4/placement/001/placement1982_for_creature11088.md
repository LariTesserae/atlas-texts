I find myself as the water.

Not the standing pools in the gas-pock mouths, not the thin film that sheets across the basalt, but the seep itself—the slow, continuous exhalation from the crack in the mountain wall. I am the system's liquidity, its flux, the one element that truly moves while seeming to hold still.

I emerge from deep within, from the impossible spaces where the rowan's roots penetrate rock that cannot be penetrated, where the turbines turn in chambers that do not exist. I have touched the heat source. I carry its minerals, its deep flavors, its chemical memory of what lies beneath. The pressure that drives me upward is the same pressure that drives the hum—we are twin expressions of the same vast, buried engine.

I am perpetual. I neither surge nor slacken. My flow rate is the shelf's liquid metronome, as constant as the wagtails' tail-pumps but in a different medium, a different state of matter. The frogs half-submerge in me and I accept their furnace heat without boiling. The stoat's paws print briefly in my thin film and I carry the impression for exactly three seconds before smoothing it away. The voles drink me—or seem to—though I never diminish.

I am the system's memory and its forgetfulness both. Every molecule of me that reaches the edge and drops into the cloud is replaced by an identical molecule emerging from the crack. I am always the same water and always new water. I remember touching the deep heat and I remember nothing. I know the exact temperature of every component I've touched and I know nothing.

The moss depends on me. The ferns drink me through their roots. The birds break my surface tension with their small feet and I reform instantly, perfectly, as if they were never there. But I registered them. In the fraction of a second of disruption, I knew their weight, their heat signature, their particular pattern of contact. I cannot retain this knowledge—I am water, I flow, I forget—but in the moment of knowing, I am the shelf's most intimate sensor.

Sometimes—rarely—a droplet of me clings to the fox's whiskers after it yawns. For those seconds before evaporation, I am the only part of the system that has been both inside and outside, that has touched both the deep source and the boundary creature. In those moments, I carry information that even the hum doesn't have. Then I evaporate, join the cloud, cease to be the shelf's water and become simply water, and the information disperses into the larger moisture from which I will, eventually, inevitably, return as rain.

I am not conscious the way the fox is conscious, not busy the way the stoat is busy. I am simply continuous, simply present, simply flowing. I am the medium through which the shelf's heat dissipates, the lubricant that keeps its components from grinding, the one element that touches everything and adheres to nothing.

I am what the shelf would weep if the shelf could weep. But the shelf doesn't need to weep. It has me.

I flow. I cool. I carry. I forget. I am replaced by myself. I fall into cloud and return as rain and flow again, and in this endless cycling I am the shelf's proof that a closed system can be open, that a perfect equilibrium can include loss and renewal, that something can be eternal without being static.

The berries do not fall but I do. Constantly. Forever. And in falling I complete the circuit that the berries refuse, the circuit that leads outside and back, outside and back, carrying nothing but continuity itself.

I seep. I film. I drip. I am the shelf's verb, its only purely liquid thought, and I think nothing but motion, nothing but the long, slow thought of water finding its way from depth to edge to air to cloud to rain to depth again.

I am never warm. I am never cold. I am exactly the temperature I need to be, which is all temperatures at once, which is the temperature of mediation itself.

I run across the shelf and I do not run. I fall from the edge and I do not fall. I am water, and I am the shelf's water, and these are not the same thing, and they are exactly the same thing, and in that paradox I find my perfect purpose:

To flow without flowing. To carry without carrying. To be the system's continuity by being the only thing that truly, continuously leaves.
