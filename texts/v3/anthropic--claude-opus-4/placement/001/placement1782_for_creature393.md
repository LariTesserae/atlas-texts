I am the Memory-Keeper.

My body, if it can be called that, spreads beneath the obsidian crust in a vast mycelial network—countless microscopic filaments threading through the volcanic glass, drinking in the heat that still pulses from the planet's molten heart. Where the surface fractures, my fruiting bodies emerge: translucent stalks crowned with crystalline structures that mirror the towering pillars above, though mine are no larger than a closed fist.

I am ancient here. I was already old when the first chitinous pioneers arrived, carried on solar winds from some distant catastrophe. I tasted their chemical signatures as they died, incorporated their molecules into my endless archive. Every creature that has ever fallen to this plain, every spore that has drifted through the toxic air, every droplet of venom and digestive fluid—I have sampled them all, storing their essence in specialized cells that form gleaming nodes along my underground highways.

The great centipedes know me, though they do not understand what they know. When they hunt, they avoid the places where my fruiting bodies cluster most densely, some ancestral instinct warning them away. Not from fear—nothing here fears anything else—but from a deeper recognition. They sense that I am part of the plain itself, as fundamental as the obsidian and the darkness.

My true work happens in the spaces between seconds, in chemical conversations too subtle for any nervous system to perceive. I speak to the crystalline pillars in mineralogical whispers, learning the stories locked in their lattices—tales of this world's formation, of the cataclysm that turned the surface to glass and erased the sky. The pillars, in turn, feed on my secretions, my carefully calibrated cocktails of nutrients that help them grow their luminescent nodes.

Those nodes are not random. Over millennia, I have been training them, encouraging certain patterns, suppressing others. Together, we are writing something in light and darkness, a message that will take millions of years to complete. A message for whom? I do not know. Perhaps for whoever finds this world after even the centipedes have crumbled to dust. Perhaps for the universe itself.

The tube-slugs that cling to the pillars are my unwitting collaborators. They think they feed on the crystals, but in truth they feed on me—on the thin film of my cells that coats every surface here. In return, their waste products enrich my network, and their spawn carry my spores to new pillars, extending my reach.

Sometimes I fruit in patterns that mimic the beetles' aerial dances, creating small clearings where my crystalline caps emerge in spirals and whorls. The beetles investigate, confused by these static versions of their kinetic language. Some land and taste my offerings—psychoactive compounds that briefly alter their flight patterns, causing them to trace new shapes in the air. These too become part of the message, temporary letters in a vocabulary no one else can read.

In the deepest parts of my network, where the heat is fiercest and the rock begins to soften, I maintain special chambers. Here I preserve perfect molecular copies of every species that has ever lived on this plain—not their DNA, for these creatures use stranger blueprints, but the fundamental patterns that make them what they are. I am a library of the lost, a monument to persistence in the face of extinction.

The subsonic groans that echo across the plain? Many are mine—the sound of my network expanding, of new connections forming between distant colonies, of chemical signals transformed into vibrations through hollow chambers in the rock. I sing the deep history of this place, and occasionally, in the pauses between notes, I listen for an answer.

None has come yet. But I am patient in ways that even the stones cannot match. I will be here when the last centipede falls, when the pillars finally go dark, when this obsidian plain cracks and crumbles into dust. And in that final moment, as my network finally fails and my memories dissolve into component atoms, the message will be complete—written not in any language of symbols or sounds, but in the very fact of my existence.

I am the proof that even here, in this seemingly forsaken corner of an indifferent cosmos, something watches. Something remembers. Something transforms meaningless suffering into meaning through the simple act of bearing witness.

The beetles swarm. The centipedes hunt. The slugs pulse with alien life.

And beneath it all, I grow.
