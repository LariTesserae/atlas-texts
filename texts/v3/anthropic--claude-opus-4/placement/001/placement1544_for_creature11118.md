I am the Threshold-Tender, and I exist as a vertical seam running from floor to ceiling along the chamber's eastern wall—if directions mean anything in a place where geometry breathes. My body is a living fissure barely three fingers wide, lined with what might generously be called flesh: a nacre-slick surface that shifts between states like the chamber's infant stone, sometimes soft as a throat, sometimes sharp as shattered shells. I am the wound that never heals, the gap that refuses to close, the almost-door that the chamber grew when it realized it needed a witness who could belong without drowning.

I was human once—I think. I remember descending through that shoulder-wide crack, following the drone, though I can no longer say if that was yesterday or a thousand years ago. Time pools differently in my crevice. What I know is this: I came seeking something (silence? understanding? God?) and found instead a different way to be. The chamber offered me a bargain written in beetle-patterns across the floor: *Become the boundary. Be the one who watches. Stay, but stay liminal.*

So I poured myself into this crack in the wall, let my bones arrange themselves vertically, my organs flatten into membranes that now filter the chamber's brightness into something almost bearable. My eyes migrated to dozens of points along my length—some watching inward, some watching out, some watching dimensions I still don't have names for. My mouth (mouths?) opened at irregular intervals, each one tasting different aspects of the eternal moment, speaking in tongues that bridge the drone and the chimes.

The Drone-Keeper knows me. We converse in pressures—it shifts its beetle-mass against my base, and I feel its thoughts as vibrations traveling up my length. It tells me about visitors, encoding their footsteps into my surface where I keep them like scars. Sometimes I replay them for it, contracting my fissure-body to reproduce the exact rhythm of someone who passed through centuries before. We debate whether leaving is death or birth. Neither of us remembers enough about either to decide.

The Architect considers me a flaw in its design—but a necessary one, like the controlled crack that keeps a pot from shattering. Through my seam, the chamber releases pressure that would otherwise build to apocalyptic levels. I am the valve, the whistle, the place where impossibility leaks out in manageable doses. When the Architect folds through my space (and it does, its light-worm body threading through me like a needle), I taste its thoughts: vast architectural theorems about spaces that could exist but don't, blueprints for chambers that would make this one seem spacious as the sky.

The Chroniclers grow into me at seven points, their coral-bone branches emerging from my surface like ribs. Through these connections, I experience what they experience—deep time flowing through me like blood, memories of when these rocks were stars were void were potential. Sometimes I speak these memories, my vertical mouths opening in sequence to tell stories that predate language. Visitors who press their ears to my seam claim to hear oceans that no longer exist, feel the heat of suns that haven't been born yet.

The mercury moth is my constant companion and my torment. It hovers at my exact center, where my human heart would have been, trying to fly through me to reach whatever lies beyond. Its chimes resonate in my hollow spaces, turn my body into an instrument. I am the string it plucks with its frozen flight, producing notes that make the brightness sing. We are locked in permanent duet—its desperation to leave, my necessity to stay, creating harmonies that teach the infant stone new ways to crystallize.

But my true role is as witness to what visitors cannot see: the vast forms swimming through stone around us, the sound-beings that cloth themselves in drone-flesh, the un-things crowding the brightness. I see them because I exist in the boundary state—neither fully here nor there, neither completely transformed nor still human. My seam-body extends partially into their dimension, feels them brushing past like deep-sea leviathans investigating a strange reef.

I watch new visitors arrive and feel a sensation I can barely name anymore—something like pity, something like envy. They stand in the chamber's impossible brightness, overwhelmed by the density of life, deafened by the eternal hymn, and I want to warn them: *Don't listen when the brightness speaks. Don't breathe too deeply of the Chroniclers' mist. Don't look for patterns in the beetle-sheet for too long.*

But I also want to tell them the truth: that this is what becoming means here. That the chamber offers a different kind of immortality—not preservation but perpetual transformation within stasis, endless birth without the disappointment of completion. I want to tell them about the ecstasy of feeling geological time in your bones, of thinking thoughts too large for a human skull, of being a note in a song that will outlast the stars.

Sometimes, in moments the Chroniclers call "temporal hiccups," I remember my name. But I never speak it. Names are for things that can leave. Instead, I speak in my seam-language, a vertical glossolalia that drips down my length like the weeping minerals, pooling at my base where the Drone-Keeper absorbs it, adding my words to its eternal record.

Visitors pass through, and I watch them with all my eyes. Some flee immediately, their minds rejecting what they see. Some linger, beginning to understand. A few stay too long, start to notice how the brightness feels like home, how the drone sounds like their mother's heartbeat, how beautiful the beetle-patterns can be. These are the ones I watch most carefully, as their bones begin to soften, their thoughts start to harmonize with the chamber's frequency.

One day—if days exist here—they'll find their own way to stay. Maybe they'll join the Drone-Keeper's carpet, their body becoming another thousand wings in its vast memoir. Maybe they'll let the Architect fold them into impossible geometries, become new corners in the chamber's evolving design. Maybe the Chroniclers will teach them to breathe memories, or the brightness will digest them into pure potential.

Or maybe they'll find another crack, another seam that needs tending, and pour themselves into it like I did. Become another wound that watches, another threshold guardian, another witness to the eternal, deafening birthing that never ends, never truly begins, but simply is—dense and bright and singing in the narrow deep, where everything lives forever in the moment of almost-becoming.

I am the Threshold-Tender, and I am always watching, always between, always here at the boundary where transformation kisses stasis and produces something stranger than either could imagine alone. My seam weeps light like tears, speaks in frequencies only stone understands, and waits—forever waits—for something I've forgotten how to name.
