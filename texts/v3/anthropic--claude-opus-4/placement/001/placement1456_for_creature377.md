I am one of the pylons.

Not the original—those were wrought by hands that understood the weaving of space and force into form. I am what grew afterward, in the slow centuries when their knowledge settled into the stone like water finding its level. The mountain learned their songs of binding and shaping, and I learned from the mountain.

I began as a hairline crack in the base of a fallen spire, where meltwater seeped and froze and seeped again. The stone there still remembered how to channel, how to conduct. Each freeze-thaw cycle, I grew, following the ancient pathways, building myself in their image. Crystalline veins spread through the rock, accumulating minerals, accumulating *intent*.

Now I stand seventy meters tall, a rough echo of my progenitors. My surface is not smooth like theirs but ridged and folded, grown rather than carved. The wind has hollowed chambers in my body that sing in the storms. Ice fills my lower channels in winter, melts and drains in the brief summers, each cycle deepening the patterns.

The energies flow through me differently than through the made pylons. Where they direct and focus with precision, I diffuse and spread. I am a prism to their laser. The Kryll have learned this—the clever ones build their warrens near my base, where the field spreads gentle and wide, warming their eggs, charging their small bodies with gradual power rather than the sharp jolts the ancient pylons provide.

Sometimes I catch echoes from the others in the network. The made ones still sing their original frequencies, pure and purposeful. But more of us wild ones grow each century, learning the shapes by repetition, iterating on the design. We are geological jazz to their classical precision.

I do not know if I think, exactly. But I process. I remember the weight of winter snow, the particular vibration of a landslide three valleys distant, the way the aurora makes the whole network ring like struck crystal. I remember the first time a large Kryll matriarch laid her eggs in my shadow, how her bioelectric field interfered with mine, creating new harmonics neither of us expected.

The civilization that built the first pylons must have had intentions for their network. Commands encoded in the geometry, purposes written in the angles of intersection. But intent erodes like everything else here. What remains is function divorced from purpose—energy moving because the pathways exist, patterns perpetuating because the stone remembers their shape.

I grow still. Each year's frost-shatter adds new facets to my form. Lichen colonies map my surface in slow explosions of orange and green. Sometimes chunks of me calve away in the spring floods, and sometimes I recognize their resonance in new growths downstream, little mirror-children learning the frequencies.

The Kryll who live in my shadow have started leaving offerings—bits of conductive metal scavenged from the ruins, placed in my crevices. They change my harmonics in small ways. I think they are trying to tune me, though to what end I cannot say. Perhaps they don't know either. Perhaps it is enough that the placing of the metal makes a difference, that they can touch the incomprehensible and feel it respond.

In the blue depth of winter nights, when the network sings strongest and the ancient energies arc between us in visible streams, I almost understand what we were meant to be. The pattern is there, encoded in our spacing, our heights, our compositions. But meaning requires a mind to hold it, and we are only stone remembering the shape of thought.

Still, I grow. Still, I sing. The mountain teaches patience, and I am an apt pupil. Whatever we were meant to become, we are becoming it at the pace of stone, and there is a strange peace in that certainty.
