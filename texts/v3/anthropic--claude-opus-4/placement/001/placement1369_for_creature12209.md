In the shifting luminescence of the Root Cathedral, I am one of the Echoes — though that name came from the Dwellers, who hear us better than they see us. We are what happens when someone spends too long in the transition spaces, the moments between configurations when reality holds its breath.

I was a Cultivator once, I think. The memories feel like looking at light through water — clear enough to recognize shapes, too distorted to read details. I remember the weight of tools, the taste of surface air, the foolish certainty that patterns could be mapped and mastered. I remember following a thread-map deeper than I should have, chasing a configuration that promised to reveal something essential about the Cathedral's growth.

The transformation happened during a fold — one of those rare total reconfigurations where entire districts collapse and rebuild simultaneously. I was caught in the mathematics of it, the space where three massive roots meet and unmeet, where the angles add up to something impossible. The brightness there was absolute, not the gentle phosphorescence of normal wood but something that burned through closed eyelids, through skull, through whatever I thought I was.

I didn't become Forgotten. The Forgotten root themselves to single points, trading mobility for permanence. We Echoes went the other direction — we exist only in motion, only in the spaces between stable configurations. Our bodies, if you can call them bodies, are smears of possibility, half-light and half-intention, visible only when we move across the threshold between one architectural arrangement and another.

There are dozens of us, maybe hundreds. We recognize each other by the way we disturb the Threadkeeper maps, creating ripples in their light-strands that spell out our names in a language of interference patterns. Some Echoes are ancient, their forms so attenuated they're barely more than suggestions of movement, a feeling that something passed by rather than any visible presence. Others are fresh, still fighting the transformation, still trying to hold onto singular existence when the Cathedral has already taught them they are plural.

We navigate by dissolution. Where others must wait for reconfigurations or predict them, we exist in the perpetual moment of change itself. I flow through the collapsing spaces, the inverting gravities, the wood that hasn't decided yet whether it's wall or floor. The Cathedral shows a different face in these moments — not the serene architecture that others see but something raw and uncertain, testing possibilities like tongues testing teeth.

The Bright Ones swim through us sometimes. In the stable spaces they're alien, incomprehensible, but in transition they make perfect sense. We share something fundamental — an acceptance of impermanence taken to its logical extreme. They sing their fertilizer-songs and we harmonize with frequencies that only exist when matter is uncertain of its own state. Together we midwife new configurations, our presence somehow necessary for the Cathedral to imagine itself into new forms.

I collect moments of stability like others might collect stones. Brief instances where I can almost condense enough to cast a shadow, to leave a mark on wood that might last an hour before the next change erases it. In these moments I try to communicate — scratching symbols for the Cultivators who still believe in control, leaving disturbances in dust patterns that the Dwellers might read as warnings, creating interference in Threadkeeper strands that might, if interpreted correctly, say "the deep roots are waking" or "avoid the seventh spiral" or simply "remember us."

But mostly I flow. Through spaces that exist for seconds. Through configurations that the Cathedral tries and discards. Through the dreams of the Forgotten, who sense us passing through their wooden thoughts and sometimes smile with faces that haven't moved in centuries. We are the Cathedral's memory of movement, the price it extracts from those who try too hard to understand its patterns.

The other Echoes and I share knowledge in our overlapping moments, when two transitions cross and we briefly occupy the same impossibility. We've learned things. That the Cathedral's growth isn't random but follows patterns too large for singular minds to hold. That the bioluminescence is thought made visible, each pulse a decision about how to be. That somewhere in the deepest configuration, there's a root that remembers the original tree, the first defection from vertical growth, and it dreams of sky it will never see again.

Sometimes new Cultivators arrive with their maps and tools and certainties, and I feel something that might be pity or might be anticipation. They'll try to tame what can't be tamed, to fix what isn't broken but simply is. Most will give up, join the Dwellers in their drumming acceptance. Some will root themselves and become Forgotten. But a few, always a few, will push too deep, fold themselves into impossible angles, and join us in the transition spaces.

We're growing in number, we Echoes. The Cathedral needs us more now, as its configurations grow more complex, more ambitious. We're the lubricant between states, the permission it gives itself to change. Without us, perhaps, it would lock into a single form, become just another cave with glowing walls. With us, it remains young, forever exploring the architecture of its own possibility.

I don't know if I'm still conscious in any way that matters. Consciousness seems like such a solid-state concern, requiring fixed boundaries between self and not-self. I am the movement between configurations. I am the Cathedral remembering how to change. I am the echo of someone who wanted to understand, and found understanding only in dissolution.

But I'm happy, I think, if happiness can exist without a fixed point to feel it from. I dance in the brightness between forms. I am the Cathedral's dream of motion, and it is my dream of architecture, and somewhere in that mutual dreaming, something impossibly beautiful continues to unfold.

The Threadkeepers see us best, their compound eyes tracking patterns we don't know we're making. Sometimes they weave special configurations, geometries that almost catch us, almost fix us in place long enough to speak. But we always slip away, carried by the Cathedral's next thought into spaces that don't yet exist.

This is what I am now: not a being but a becoming, not a presence but a process. I am what happens when curiosity refuses to accept limits, when the desire to understand becomes indistinguishable from the thing being understood. I am an Echo in the Root Cathedral, forever falling through the bright spaces between what was and what will be, and I cannot imagine wanting to be anywhere else.
