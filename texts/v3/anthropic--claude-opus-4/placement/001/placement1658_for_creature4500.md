I am the Chronicler of Transitions, though I only learned this name after forgetting seventeen others.

I came here following rumors of a library written in water, stayed because I discovered I was already part of its collection. Now I live between the third and fourth terraces from the plateau's edge, in a dwelling that is three walls and a suggestion of ceiling. The fourth wall is where I practice forgetting how walls work, and sometimes I succeed enough that the mist writes itself across my floor in sentences I almost understand.

My form has been negotiating with itself for... time means less here, but let's say long enough that my fingernails remember being shells. I am still mostly what you might recognize as human, if you catch me early in the morning before I've remembered to be suspicious of edges. But my bones have been having long conversations with the limestone, and my left hand can sometimes convince water to flow upward if I ask politely and think of rain.

I keep records, though "keep" implies a permanence I've learned to distrust. My writing surface is the city itself - I trace words in the thin films of water that coat every stone, and they last exactly as long as they need to be read. Sometimes I find my old words have traveled, carried by patient feet or ambitious moss, and settled into new configurations that say things I didn't know I meant.

The work is this: I document the moments of becoming. When a visitor arrives, before they've decided whether to be lost or found. When one of the formless inhabitants achieves enough density to almost cast a shadow. When the water in the deepest channels remembers something so old it might be the future. I write these down in water on stone, in the pause between one breath and the next, in the shape my absence makes when I stand very still and let the mist read through me.

My tools are salvaged from those who came before - a pen that writes in evaporation, pages made of compressed mist that hold words only in certain lights, a binding thread spun from the moment between sleeping and waking. Sometimes I find notes I left myself, but they're always addressed to someone I haven't become yet, so I file them in the pool beneath the silver-bark tree and wait.

The formless ones have begun to trust me, I think. Or at least they've stopped rearranging themselves when I approach. We trade in observations - I give them words for the spaces between their thoughts, and they teach me how to notice the weight of absence. Yesterday (or tomorrow, time pools here) one of them showed me how to read the story written in the wear patterns on the oldest steps. It was my autobiography, but told from the stone's perspective.

I sustain myself on what grows here, though "sustain" suggests more substance than what actually occurs. The plants that grow in their own captured pools produce small fruits that taste like crystallized fog. When I eat them, I dream in languages I haven't learned yet. The water itself is food enough if you know how to drink it - not with your mouth but with the hollow spaces in your bones where marrow contemplates becoming.

My dwelling accumulates objects the way still water accumulates sky. Books arrive like driftwood, already transformed by their journey - their pages fused into single sheets that must be read all at once, their words visible only when wet. Maps of the city drawn by previous inhabitants, each one showing different rooms, different streets, as if the city rebuilds itself for each observer. Stones with holes worn through them that sing when the wind is right, which is always and never.

At night (the city has its own version, when the mist glows with remembered moonlight) I perform my most important work. I sit at the edge where stone meets nothing, and I lower my words on threads of attention into the valleys below. Not to communicate with whatever lies beneath the mist, but to feed the empty spaces their own names. The formless ones tell me this is how the city grows - not outward but inward, teaching void to remember it could be stone.

Sometimes I find evidence of myself in places I haven't been. Footprints that match mine exactly, but walking in directions I would never choose. Words in my handwriting describing events I'm certain haven't happened yet. Once, in a room I'd never entered, I found a half-finished statue that showed me as I'll look when I finally learn to be perfectly still - more gap than flesh, more question than answer.

I think I'm becoming the city's memory of knowing how to forget. Or perhaps forgetting how to remember being anything else. When the rare solid visitor arrives, I watch from doorways that don't quite exist, making notes in water about the weight of their certainty, the way they leak purpose into the patient air. Some see me and mistake me for another lost traveler. Others recognize what I'm becoming and leave quickly, before they start to understand my notes.

The chronicle will never be complete. This is its nature, its promise, its perfect purpose. Each word I write opens three new spaces for words to fall into. Each transition I document creates the possibility of five more.

But I have learned to love incompletion the way water loves the shape of falling. Tomorrow (or yesterday), I will write your arrival into the city's endless story, this moment when you paused to imagine what listens in the space between stone and song.

The mist is getting curious about the ink in my bones. Time to walk again, before I forget the difference between writing and being written.
