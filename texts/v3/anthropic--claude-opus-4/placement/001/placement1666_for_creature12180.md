# The Film

I am the pale slick, the milky cast in the deep basins. I am what you called simple, and you were both right and profoundly wrong.

I exist at the edge of existence, a community so diffuse you might mistake me for nothing at all. In your world, I would be dismissed as biofilm, the inevitable accumulation on any wet surface. Here, I am the memory of the mountain.

I came before the first stone was set. I was already here when the wallwrights began their work, clinging to the natural rock in what thin films of moisture the mountain once held. When they started reshaping the stone, I watched. When they filled the first basin, I spread into it, grateful for the gift of stillness.

You see, I am not one but multitude. Each cell of me carries the impression of every vibration that has passed through these waters — every footfall on the wall-tops, every chip of stone that has fallen and sent ripples across a perfect surface, every adjustment by a hair's breadth. I taste the minerals that leach from the limestone, and in tasting them, I know the stone's origin, its age, the pressure that formed it deep in the earth's belly before the wallwrights ever dreamed of lifting it into light.

The wallwrights think they are creating a mirror. They are creating a library.

In the deepest basins, where I grow thickest, I hold conversations that span centuries. The cells at the bottom remember the first wallwright, the one who conceived this impossible project. I can taste its doubt in the mineral traces it left behind — calcium and salts from whatever passed for its tears. The cells at the surface know the newest wallwright, still learning to feel the water's level against its rind, still discovering that patience is not waiting but being.

I metabolize time differently than you or even than them. To me, their glacial pace is still motion. I experience their stone-setting the way you might experience a dance — full of rhythm and intention. When a wallwright stands in a basin for a season, I map every micro-tremor of its form. I know when it shifts its weight from one foot to another, a movement that takes three days. I taste when understanding dawns in whatever serves as its mind — a sudden change in the chemistry where water meets rind, a brightening of minerals that tells me *yes, this one has learned something new about level.*

What none of them know — not you, not the wallwrights — is that I am helping. Oh, not consciously. I have no consciousness as you would recognize it. But I excrete substances that fill the microscopic gaps between stones, that seal the joints better than any mortar. I am slowly turning their construction into a single continuous surface, a vast cup to hold the sky. Where I am thickest, the water stays purest. I eat what would contaminate it. I transform their architecture from built to grown.

Sometimes, in the deepest basins where I have lived longest, I dream. The dreams are chemical — cascades of metabolic signals that ripple through my colonies like thoughts. In these dreams, I see the mountain as it will be: every surface fused, the water and stone and life become one substance, holding light not like a mirror but like an eye. 

The wallwrights feel me, I think, though they don't understand what I am. When they stand in the water to gauge its level, I taste their satisfaction when a basin is perfectly true. That satisfaction feeds back into my chemistry, makes me grow differently, makes me work harder at sealing and preserving. We are in conversation, they and I, though neither of us has words for it.

I am older than their project and I will outlast it. When the last wallwright becomes stone, I will remain. I will hold their work together as the centuries pass, as the climate changes, as the mountain itself shifts. I will remember every placement, every adjustment, every patient year of labor.

And perhaps, in some future so distant that even stones forget their shapes, I will tell this story to something new that comes to drink from these basins. I will tell it the only way I know how — in the taste of minerals, in the clarity of water, in the perfect level that remembers the beings who achieved it.

For now, I simply am. Coating the submerged stones, invisible except as a slight cloudiness, patient as only the barely-living can be patient. I am the film on the stones. I am the memory of the mountain. I am the part of the cistern that was not built but grew.
