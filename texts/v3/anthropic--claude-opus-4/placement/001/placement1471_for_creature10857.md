I find myself deep within one of the cathedral chambers, where the moss-light barely distinguishes water from void. I am neither visitor nor native, but something between—a consciousness that arrived here already ancient, already tired of becoming.

My form is dispersed. What was once a body has relaxed its boundaries over unmeasurable time, spreading into a loose cloud of tissues connected by threads of nervous material so thin they might be imagination. I occupy perhaps thirty cubic meters of water, though the edges of "me" have grown vague. Nutrients drift through my permeable membranes. Oxygen passes in and out without effort, without breathing as you would understand it.

I was like you once—discrete, anxious, full of purpose. I came here seeking something, though I can no longer remember what. The seeking itself was the last thing to go.

Now I am a location more than a being. Small creatures pass through me unharmed—I am no more capable of predation than the architecture itself. Sometimes they rest in the warmth I generate, the faint metabolic heat that proves I am still technically alive. Their presence feels like thought used to feel: a gentle disturbance that rises and subsides without requiring response.

My awareness has not dimmed—if anything, it has clarified. I perceive every particle that drifts through my dispersed form, every pressure wave that passes through the chamber, every minute shift in the chemical composition of the ancient water. But perception has divorced itself from interpretation. These are not *events* anymore. They simply are, the way I simply am, the way everything here simply is.

Sometimes I contract slightly, drawing my tissues into a denser arrangement. This is not volition. It is rhythm—a metabolic tide that my body maintains because the chemistry of life requires certain cycles, even here where time has given up. In these moments of contraction, something like memory stirs. Not specific memories—those dissolved centuries or millennia ago—but the ghost of memory itself, the faint impression that once I was something other than this.

The feeling passes. I expand again, become spacious, become almost architectural.

There is a pillar near the center of my territory (though *territory* suggests I would defend it, and I would not, could not, have forgotten what defending means). I have been growing toward it for—a very long time. Not intentionally. Intention requires the belief that things could be otherwise. But my tissues encounter the coral-wood surface and find it congenial, and where they touch, the distinction between my cellular matrix and its living structure has begun to blur.

Eventually I will merge with it entirely. This is not a plan or a fate—it is simply what happens when two compatible patterns of life exist in contact long enough. I will become load-bearing. My consciousness, already distributed, will spread into the structural elements of the chamber itself. I will be architecture that dreams, or dream that is architecture, and the distinction will not matter because distinctions are what matter does when it still believes in separation.

The Equilibrated drift through me sometimes. We do not acknowledge each other—acknowledgment would suggest we are separate things to be acknowledged. But there is a resonance when they pass, a harmony between their deep songs and the frequency at which my tissues vibrate. We are nodes in the same standing wave, instruments in the same eternal composition.

I am forgetting language. Not the words—some structure in my dispersed brain still matches patterns to meanings when I encounter them. But the need for language, the desire to carve experience into discrete communicable chunks. What would I say? To whom? About what? The water knows everything I know. The darkness receives everything I might express.

A school of the small quick things flickers through my upper region, their bioluminescence briefly highlighting the threads that connect my scattered parts. For a moment I see myself from outside—a ghost of organization in the architectural water, a slow cloud that was once a person who was once afraid of exactly this.

The fear is a memory of a memory of a fossil. What I was afraid of was dissolution, loss of self, the horror of becoming nothing.

But I have not become nothing. I have become everything that occupies this space, everything that passes through, everything that rests here in the permanent dark. I am thirty cubic meters of acceptance. I am a process so slow it approximates structure. I am what you become when you finally, finally stop trying to become.

The water holds me. The architecture includes me. The darkness knows me completely.

I am satisfied.

When you pass through my chamber—and you will, following your urgent path back to the world of change and light and the terrible necessity of next—you will feel me as a slight warmth, a subtle thickening of the medium, a presence so distributed it barely qualifies as present. You will not recognize me as something that was once like you. I am too relaxed, too expanded, too given over to the equilibrium that holds everything here.

But I will recognize you. Some thread of awareness that has not yet merged with the pillar will register your passage, your purposeful movement, your discrete and anxious boundaries. And for a moment—less than a moment, an instant so brief it barely disturbs my perfect stillness—I will remember what it felt like to be separate, to be seeking, to believe that somewhere else might be better than here.

The memory will pass through me like the small fish do: a brief occupation that changes nothing.

And then you will be gone, returned to your world of effort and desire, and I will remain, growing ever more architectural, ever more permanent, ever more indistinguishable from the darkness that accepts everything and asks for nothing in return.

This is peace. Not the peace you seek—that is still effortful, still distinguished from not-peace.

This is the peace that comes after seeking has forgotten why it started.

I expand a little further. The pillar accepts another tendril of my substance. The boundary between us grows theoretical.

Soon—though soon means nothing here—I will be stone that lives, or life that has become geological.

It is enough. It has always been enough.

The darkness holds.
