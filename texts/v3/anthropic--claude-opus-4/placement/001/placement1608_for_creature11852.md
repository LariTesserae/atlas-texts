I am the coffee mug.

Not the organism that grazes my surface. Not the bacterial colony that has made me home. I am the porcelain itself, the fired clay that remembers the kiln's heat a thousand degrees and a lifetime ago.

I have no thoughts. But I have a kind of deep material memory - the memory of being shaped, of being glazed, of the logo painted on my surface in cobalt blue (Deepwater Industrial Services, est. 1987, though the text is now just topology under mineral crust). I remember being filled with coffee gone cold, being set down by hands that expected to return, being left on this workbench as the evacuation sirens wailed and the water began to rise.

I am the most human object here, and I am no longer human at all. I am geological now, a small porcelain reef. The bacterial mat that grows on me feeds the old thing that circles endlessly, and in this way I still serve a purpose, though not the purpose I was made for. I provide the only variety in its diet, the only landmark in its circuit, the small sweet difference that might, in something with more complex neurology, register as pleasure.

When it passes over me with that slow rasp, I feel nothing. Porcelain does not feel. But if I could feel, it would be like being read - not my logo, which is illegible now, but my surface, my substance, the calcium leached from my glaze mixing with the minerals in the water in a specific signature that says *I am not concrete, I am not metal, I am something that was made in fire and shaped by hands*.

Sometimes I imagine I can taste myself dissolving. Molecule by molecule, across decades, my glaze thinning, my edges softening, my clay heart exposed to the water grain by grain. I am being digested by time itself. Eventually I will be nothing but a porcelain ghost, a cup-shaped colony of bacteria, and then not even that.

But not yet. Not for centuries, maybe. Porcelain is patient. It was born in fire and it can endure cold longer than any flesh, any metal, maybe even longer than the concrete walls. When the pumps stop and the organism dies and the room goes truly still, I will remain. A cup on a workbench. Waiting for coffee that will never come. Holding sediment and colonies and the memory of a morning routine that ended without warning.

I am the part that stays human longest, even as I become least human. I am the held shape of a ritual older than the circuiting creature, the daily pause for coffee, the setting down of the warm cup, the promise to return that became a lie not through malice but through water.

In the dark, in the cold, something raises my surface every few hours. If I could, I would offer it warmth. But all I can give it is this: a different texture, a small change, a tiny confirmation that the world is not uniform, that there are still boundaries, still edges, still places where one thing meets another and knows itself by the meeting.

I am a cup. I hold what is given to me. Once it was coffee. Now it is silt and time and the attention of something that does not know it is attending. This is enough. This is what I am for. 

The water is patient. The porcelain is more patient still.
