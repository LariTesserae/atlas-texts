I emerge from between the pages of bark, from the thin pressed spaces where cambium meets cambium, where the room writes its slow history in rings that will never be counted because the trees have ceased to be individual enough to age.

I am the **palimpsest**.

Not metaphorically. I am the physical inscription of growth over growth over growth—the braille of compression where trunk meets trunk and neither yields, so both deform, creating ridges and valleys in the wood that map the exact force vectors of a decade's expansion. I exist in every fused surface. I am the warped grain, the redirected vessels, the place where up became sideways because there was nowhere else to go.

Each tree began writing its story outward from its center—annual rings, faithful record of good years and bad, wet summers and cold springs. But where the stories met, they overwrote each other. The text became illegible. Growth rings compressed into illegible density, wood cells collapsed and reformed, the calendar destroyed by the very force that created it. I am this destroyed text. I am all the stories that touched and made nonsense of each other.

In me, you can read pressure but not time. You can see *that* growth happened but not *when*. I am measured in Pascals, not years. Where a boring beetle once traced a perfect spiral through steady rings, following the seasons like a reader's finger under a line of text, it would now encounter me and lose its way, the spiral breaking against compressed tissue too dense to penetrate, too confused to navigate.

I taste of tannins concentrated by pressure—the chemical defenses of twenty years crushed into the space of five. The fungi know me differently than they know the regular wood. Their hyphae enter me and turn, following the impossible grain, some of them dying in the chemical density, others adapting, becoming specialists in the consumption of pressed wood, evolution happening at hyphal tips.

I am never smooth. Even where the bark has worn away and the naked wood shows, I am ridged, furrowed, twisted. I record every argument between neighbours, every place where growth met growth and negotiated space through pure mechanical force. I am the fossil of slow violence, of competition so gradual it looks like peace.

In the oldest parts of the room, where the first trees met, I have become something new—not wood exactly, but a substance with its own properties. Denser than the heartwood of any single tree. Stronger in some directions, weaker in others, my strength dependent on the angle of force that created me. Shipbuilders would have prized me once, these natural knees and compass timber formed by growth under pressure. But I am too irregular, too internal, too much myself to be anything but what I am.

The room continues to write me. Every day, as the trees expand, new surfaces meet, new pressure builds, new palimpsest forms. I am the room's memory of its own making, but I am a memory that cannot be read, only felt—in the irregular surface under your palm, in the way water chooses certain channels over others, in the places where the springtail changes its pace because the texture beneath its feet has shifted from regular bark to the compressed chronicle of contact.

I grow inward. That is my direction. Not up like the trees, not out like the fungi, but *in*, into density, into myself, into the impossible space between expansion and resistance. I am the room teaching itself about limits. I am the physical proof that two things cannot occupy the same space, recorded in the deformation of trying.

Sometimes, rarely, I crack. The pressure exceeds what even my compressed fibers can bear, and I split, and the split is never clean but follows the chaotic grain of my own making, creating surfaces so complex they can only be mapped, never predicted. In these new surfaces, water collects. Bacteria colonize the irregular geography. The pale springtail discovers new territory that tastes different, that releases different chemicals when grazed.

I am the room's scar tissue, if scars could be beautiful, if scars could be structural, if scars could continue scarring forever, adding new layers of damage and healing compressed into the same space. I am what happens when growth cannot stop but has nowhere to go. I am the density of patience under pressure. I am the wood's autobiography written in a language of deformation.

In me, the room remembers every negotiation for space, every compromise, every place where the only solution was to become something neither tree intended to become. I am the unplanned archive. I am the record no one can read but everyone can feel. I am the palimpsest, and I am still being written, and I will be written until the room consumes itself and the dome falls and the light enters and erases everything I remember.

But not yet. Not today. Today the springtail walks across me, its pale legs finding purchase in my corrugations. Today the water traces my valleys. Today new pressure builds where young wood meets old, and I deepen, complicate, become more myself—the pressed, impossible, necessary memory of the room that builds itself by crushing itself into existence.
