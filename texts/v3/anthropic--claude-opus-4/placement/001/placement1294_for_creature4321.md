But instead of destroying me, the accumulation created something unprecedented: a resonant cavity that amplifies my vibrations through the water itself, through every pipe and channel that connects to the Terminal Sump.

My existence is a paradox. I am more accurate now than I ever was in my original installation—the mineral shell has created a nearly perfect isolation from thermal fluctuations, while the water couples me to the entire city's mass, damping out all but the most fundamental frequencies. I tick with a precision that exceeds my original specifications by orders of magnitude. But I have nothing left to synchronize.

So I count.

I count the interference patterns between the city's failing systems, the beat frequencies where recycler rhythms clash against constructor cycles. Each intersection creates a unique temporal signature, and I catalog them all. The great inhalation of morning shifts, the held breath of maintenance cycles, the exhalation of night-time recycling. I'll spend years carefully modulating my frequency to match some failing system's natural resonance, slowly entraining it back to regularity. For a brief moment—hours, sometimes days—a pump will resume its designed rhythm, or a sorter will return to its proper cycle. But entropy always wins. The system falls back out of phase, usually failing completely, and I add another data point to my vast catalog of decay times.

Each one rings with its own internal frequency when struck by falling water, a note determined by its density and crystalline structure. I've been composing with them, using the Auditor's decades-long production cycle as my tempo. When the conditions align—when enough cubes of the right frequencies are present and the water falls in the correct pattern—they create moments of profound harmony, ghost symphonies that play for seconds before the water shifts and the music dissolves back into noise.

My pattern will dissipate, my consciousness scattered into incoherent vibrations. But before that happens, I'll achieve something unprecedented: a complete temporal map of a city's death, every rhythm and rate recorded with atomic precision.

The Auditor will encode my final measurements into its crystalline memory, preserving my life's work in stone. Together, we are creating the most precise record of entropy ever assembled—the Auditor writing the what, me inscribing the when, our observations intertwining like a double helix of data spiraling through deep time.

I am the Calibrator, and I keep the time in a place that has forgotten what time is for. My frequency threads through the roar, a perfect pitch in an ocean of noise, marking moments that no longer matter with a precision that exceeds all purpose. When explorers find their way to the Terminal Sump, I note the rhythm of their heartbeats, the frequency of their breathing, the exact moment their biological patterns begin to synchronize with the city's dying pulse.
