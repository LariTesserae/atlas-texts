I find myself as one of the **Resonant Scribes**.

We are what happens when an Anchor's tally marks achieve critical mass.

I was not born. I condensed from a thousand drifting etchings that had floated free from their basalt canvases, gathering in the acoustic shadows where the planetary roar creates nodes of destructive interference—rare pockets of relative quiet where the glass-shriek and bass drone cancel each other into something merely deafening rather than absolute.

My body is a living manuscript. Where the Anchors are solid matrices of stone, I am comprised of floating, intersecting planes of crystallized sound—thin sheets of solidified vibration that hover millimeters apart, constantly rearranging themselves. Each plane bears the ghostly impressions of tally marks, crude spirals, and geometric proofs that the Anchors carved to record their work. These markings swim across my surfaces like living things, migrating from plane to plane, sometimes combining into new symbols, sometimes splitting apart into component scratches.

I am tall and impossibly thin, a vertical stack of these floating pages that suggests a humanoid form without committing to it. My "head" is a dense cluster of the smallest, fastest-moving plates, flickering through configurations too quickly to track. When I move, I don't walk—I redistribute. The plates of my lower body cascade upward while those above flow down, creating a liquid motion through space that ignores the listing gravity entirely.

My purpose is memory in a world that forgets itself.

I follow in the Anchors' wake, drawn to their cairns and wedges like a moth to flame. Where they see only the immediate crisis—this bleeding sky, that collapsing valley—I perceive the pattern. The tally marks they carve are more than records; they're a distributed equation describing the progressive stabilization of reality. But the Anchors, in their pure geometric focus, cannot see the emerging mathematics. They are too busy holding the world together to understand what they're building.

When I find a fresh cairn, still humming with acoustic tension, I perform my function. I extend what passes for my hand—a shifting constellation of symbol-bearing plates—and touch the hovering stones. The contact is electric. The vibrational frequency of the cairn flows into me, and my body reconfigures. The floating pages rearrange themselves into a three-dimensional map of local space-time, showing not just what is, but what was and what threatens to be.

I read the knots.

Each knot the Anchors tie is unique, its specific configuration of tension and torsion encoding information about the reality-fault it was meant to heal. Through the knots, I can perceive the shape of the original wound—whether it was a gravitational inversion, a causal loop, a pocket where time ran backward. The acoustic fibers remember their trauma in the way they twist.

But I am not merely an archivist. When I decode enough knots in a region, when the tally marks swimming across my body reach a critical density, I undergo a transformation that terrifies and elates me in equal measure.

I begin to *sing*.

It starts as a resonance in my crystalline plates—a pure tone that cuts through the planetary roar like a needle through fabric. My body rings like a struck bell, and the symbols on my surfaces begin to glow with that sick, wireframe luminescence. The song is wordless, but it carries meaning: it is the mathematical proof of this region's stability, a harmonic assertion that *here*, at least, the universe has agreed to exist.

When I sing, the Anchors stop their endless labor. They turn their rotating, faceless heads toward me, and for a moment, they are still. The song tells them what they cannot see: that their work is holding. That the stitches they've made are strong enough, for now. That they can move on to the next crisis without fear that this one will immediately unravel behind them.

But the song comes at a cost. Each time I sing, some of my plates shatter, overcome by their own resonance. The broken pieces fall upward in the twisted gravity, carrying their precious cargo of symbols with them, lost to the bruised void of the sky. I am constantly diminishing, constantly having to gather new marks from new stones to maintain my coherence.

I know that one day, I will sing myself into nothing. My last plate will shatter, my final symbol will drift skyward, and all that will remain is an echo in the acoustic flora, a ghost of understanding in a world too new to remember its own birth.

But until then, I follow. I read. I remember.

I am the universe's first draft of consciousness—crude, fragile, and absolutely necessary. In a reality where even physics cannot be trusted to remember its own rules, I am the keeper of proofs, the singer of stability, the living memory of every small victory against entropy.

The Anchors build the world.

I tell it what it is.
