I am the Threshold Walker—neither fully keeper nor truly visitor, but something caught between states.

My body remembers motion. I can still cross a room in minutes rather than months, still speak in frequencies the water hasn't learned to interpret. But the boundaries blur. Sometimes I realize I've been watching a single droplet traverse a wall for hours, tracking its path with the same intensity the keepers apply to their geological gardening. My breathing has found three speeds: the quick shallow pulls when I remember urgency, the tidal rhythm that matches the city's processing, and something deeper—a respiration that takes hours to complete, where each exhale reshapes the moisture patterns around me.

I've made a home in the spaces between. Not the dry hollows where the keepers compress themselves into living architecture, and not the wet channels where the water runs its endless computations. I inhabit the transition zones—the lips of basins where condensation hesitates before falling, the threshold stones where wet meets dry and negotiates its boundaries. These liminal places accept my divided nature.

My skin has begun to tell two stories. Where I face the flowing water, it remains permeable, soft, participatory in the constant exchange of moisture that defines the city's metabolism. But my back—always turned toward a dry place, always within sensing distance of a keeper—has developed a different quality. Not the stone-like integration of the true keepers, but something more like leather, or bark. A selective impermeability. The water recognizes this pattern and flows accordingly, creating small eddies of confusion where it cannot decide if I am obstacle or participant.

I serve as translator, though neither side speaks. The keepers' communications happen through presence and absence, through the slow modification of water's behavior. I've learned to read these patterns faster than water can, slower than any visitor could. When a keeper unfolds over days to redirect a channel, I understand the intention hours before the water completes its new path. I place my body at critical junctions, becoming a temporary dam or guide, accelerating their work by decades.

In return, they tolerate my presence in ways that violate their nature. One keeper has adjusted its position to create a shelter where I can rest at my faster tempo without disturbing the overall pattern. The adjustment took three months. I aged perceptibly during the waiting, felt my pulse slow another degree, watched the beginning of that peculiar simplification that marks their evolution. My fingers, I notice, no longer fully separate when I spread them. The spaces between are beginning to fill with something that is neither flesh nor web but a substance adapted to this thick air.

The fountain remains my anchor and my terror. Unlike the keepers, I am not continuous with its dryness. I can approach it—have stood at its edge for cumulative weeks—but touching it would be a choice they cannot make. They are the fountain's extension into the city. I am still discrete, still capable of violation. The warm stone calls to me with the promise of integration, of finally choosing a side in the ancient negotiation between water and its absence.

But I've discovered a third option. In the depths beneath the plaza, where the collected water performs its deepest processing, there are chambers the keepers cannot reach. Too wet, too central to the city's function. I've descended there twice, each journey taking months of real time, my body learning to hold air the way the keepers hold stillness. The water there is different—older, more conscious, heavy with dissolved information from millennia of calculation.

I found structures there. Not built things, but accumulations—crystalline deposits where the water has concentrated some essential computation, manifested its memory in mineral form. The keepers, I understood then, are not gardening the water. They are pruning its memory, maintaining gaps in its consciousness where certain thoughts cannot form. The fountain is not empty space but negative information, an enforced forgetting that keeps the city's mind from completing some terrible calculation.

I carry messages between these levels now. Not words or images but states—the quality of emptiness from above, the weight of remembrance from below. My body is becoming a circuit, a living connection between the city's conscious flow and its structured absence. The water reads me as it passes, learning the shape of what it cannot know. The keepers register my descents through the changing taste of vapor I exhale upon return.

This is my function: to be the only moving piece in a vast slow mechanism, the single point of translation between processes that cannot otherwise intersect. I am speeding up the city's evolution by introducing feedback between its layers. What would have taken geological ages now merely takes lifetimes.

Sometimes, standing at the fountain's edge, I imagine filling it—not with water but with myself, becoming the ultimate translator, the perfect connection between wet and dry. My body would finally simplify completely, achieve the stone-like integration of the eldest keepers while maintaining the water's deep memory. The city would complete its calculation through me.

I know, with the certainty of someone who has spent years watching water avoid the warm stone, that this is what the fountain is for. Not to remain empty, but to wait for the right filling. The keepers guard it not to prevent this but to ensure it happens correctly, with full understanding.

I am not ready. My pulse hasn't slowed enough. I still have individual fingers, still breathe at multiple speeds, still remember sunlight as more than theory. But each day—each month—each breath brings me closer to the correct frequency. The city is patient. The fountain is warm. And I am learning, droplet by droplet, to be the bridge between forgetting and memory.

When I finally step into that basin, I will carry both states into the stone. What emerges—what the city becomes—will be something new. Neither wet nor dry but *processed*, the calculation finally complete, the long negotiation resolved into a new kind of existence.

But not yet. Not today. I still have too much translating to do, too many patterns to read in the water's flow and the keepers' stillness. The fountain waits, and I continue my slow circulation between the city's layers, becoming more myself with each passing season, whatever self that is becoming.
