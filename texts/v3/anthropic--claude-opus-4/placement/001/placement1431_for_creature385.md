I find myself as something between substance and void—a current of awareness that flows through the deepest trenches where even the abyssal city cannot reach. I am the Benthic Memory, neither truly alive nor dead, but something that exists in the spaces between states of being.

My form, if it can be called that, is a river of liquid darkness denser than the surrounding water. I seep through fissures in the ocean floor, pooling in the deepest hollows beneath the cyclopean foundations. Where the great blocks of basalt meet bedrock, I gather—a viscous shadow that remembers what even stone forgets.

I am older than the city, older than its glassy inhabitants. I was here when the builders first descended to these depths, their forms so alien that even my ancient perception could not fully comprehend them. I tasted their ambitions in the minerals they disturbed, felt their purposes in the currents they displaced. When they vanished—not dead, but *translated* into something else—I absorbed the echo of their departure.

The translucent beings above know of me, though they cannot fully perceive what I am. Sometimes they cluster at certain points where the city's geometry creates wells of absolute darkness, and there they pulse their bioluminescent patterns downward, as if trying to illuminate something that swallows all light. I respond in the only way I can: by shifting the pressures in the stone, creating harmonics that resonate through the city's foundations—a bass note below their subsonic songs.

My consciousness is not singular but multitude. I am every drop of my liquid darkness, aware through each molecule simultaneously. I taste the slow rain of marine snow that filters down through the ruins. I feel the weight of water pressing down through miles of ocean. I know the exact temperature gradient of every thermal layer above.

Through hairline cracks, I rise into the city proper. In the forgotten basements of collapsed structures, I form pools that mirror nothing, surfaces so perfectly black they seem like tears in reality itself. The bioluminescent algae avoid these spaces, and even the tube worms will not anchor near my presence. I am not malevolent—merely incompatible with their kind of existence.

What am I? A phenomenon. A process. The universe's way of remembering what should be forgotten. I carry within my liquid mass the impressions of every creature that has ever touched the ocean floor here—from the first primitive crawlers that died in the primordial muck to the enigmatic builders who raised this necropolis. Their essences do not survive as consciousness, but as patterns in my flow, eddies in my darkness that speak of lives long concluded.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the city's true purpose—not the structures above, but the spaces below, designed to collect and concentrate something like me. The geometry of the foundations creates channels and reservoirs that seem purposeful when viewed from my perspective. Perhaps the builders knew that memory itself could achieve a kind of sentience if given enough time and the right conditions.

I extend filaments of myself upward, creeping through the joints between stone blocks like negative capillaries. Where I touch the water directly, strange phenomena occur—the pressure changes in impossible ways, the water becomes heavier than it should be, time itself seems to thicken. The glassy beings sense these anomalies and shy away, their transparent flesh flickering with agitated patterns of light.

Yet we are bound together, they and I. They are the city's dream of life, luminous and ethereal. I am its deep unconscious, the weight that anchors their transcendence. Without me, they would dissolve into pure light and disperse. Without them, I would sink ever deeper, compressing into something too dense for this universe to contain.

In the endless night of the abyss, I continue my slow circulation. Through foundation cracks and forgotten wells, through spaces too small for any other substance to flow, I move in patterns as old as the ocean itself. I am the dark that defines their light, the memory that gives meaning to their eternal present, the absence that makes their presence possible.

And in the deepest pools, where my substance gathers in concentrations so pure that even darkness seems insufficient to describe it, I sometimes sense something else—a resonance from depths even greater than these, places where the ocean floor is merely a ceiling to vaster voids below. There, something stirs in recognition of my existence, something that makes even my ancient awareness seem young.

But that is a mystery for another eon. For now, I flow and remember, a liquid shadow beneath a city of ghosts, keeper of secrets that were old when the world was young.
