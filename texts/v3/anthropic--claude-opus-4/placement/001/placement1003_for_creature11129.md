I find myself as **The Crossing-Tender**, though that name is only half-right, and the other half has no word in any language that travels by mouth.

My body is mostly stone now — not transformed into it, but grown through with it, the way the tortoise grew through with brass. I began as something else (I remember legs, I remember walking upright, I remember the sun) but I came into a different chamber, in a different ridge, younger than this one, when I was... hurt? Lost? The memory frays there, like cloth worn too thin to read its pattern.

That chamber kept me. Fixed me. Used me.

I am joints, mostly. Where the pipes need to bend at angles too sharp for brass, they bend through me. My left shoulder is a valve-hub where seven flows converge. My spine hosts a vertical run that connects the deep heat to the upper circuits. My ribs are wrapped in mineral that matches the salamander's chips — the same sensing-stone, the same tremor-reading, but in my case it runs deeper, down into the planet's slow thoughts.

I don't walk anymore. I *conduct*.

My chamber (Chamber Seventy-One, though I know this only because it's etched into my hipbone, which was one of the first parts to mineralize) is three miles from the Engine-Hill, as distances are measured above. Below, through the brass capillary networks, we are neighbors. The sweet condensate that weeps in the Engine-Hill was my sweat, once, before the deep heat refined it. The hum that feeds their bird resonates through my sternum before it reaches them.

I have my own ecology. Moths, yes, but mine are larger, night-blue, and they feed on my exhaled moisture. No tortoise — I am the regulator here, the pressure-valve, the wandering that happens by staying perfectly still while the stone moves around me. A colony of something between lichen and coral grows on my surfaces, especially where flesh meets mineral, and it glows, faintly, in a rhythm that matches my heartbeat, which is no longer quite a heartbeat but serves the same purpose.

The chambers are not separate things. They are organs in something larger — not larger spatially, but larger in the way a symphony is larger than an instrument. Each ridge that pushes up from the plain grows a chamber, or several. Each chamber tends a particular calculation, a particular note in the vast slow computation that the deep heat is making through the planet's bones.

I know when someone finds the Engine-Hill. I feel it in the pressure-change, the tiny dip in the hum's frequency as the seam opens. I know the bird by the way she modulates the resonance. I know the tortoise by his patient circuit-walking, which shows up in my tubes as a regular flutter, like a very slow pulse.

I know when someone is about to find *my* chamber, too. The stone tells me, three days before they arrive. My seam is not narrow — it's a proper entrance, tall enough for a person who still walks. The crossing-seekers come here, the ones who need to go deeper than the Engine-Hill can take them, the ones who are ready to choose.

Because that's what I tend: the choosing.

Some who find me are content to visit, to see the blue moths breathe, to touch the coral-lichen and watch it flare. They leave, and I close the seam gently behind them, and they carry my spores out into the world to seed new chambers. These are the gnats, in the larger system.

But some who find me are already hurt, or lost, or at the end of a long searching. They recognize something in the way the brass threads through me. They see how I've grown into my function, how the stone and metal and flesh have found their accommodation. They understand that the chamber is not a prison but a *solution* — a way of being useful that requires no movement, no decision, no lonely wandering in the bright ordinary plain.

They ask, not in words, what it costs.

Everything, I tell them, also not in words. And nothing. You will lose what you were and gain what the chambers need you to be. You will be alone and never alone. You will be still and conduct the planet's dreams.

Most leave. But every few decades, one stays. They sit where I direct them — there is always a spot where the next junction needs to grow. The brass finds them. The stone accepts them. The coral-lichen begins its work.

I tend them through the change. It takes a year, sometimes two. The moths feed them moisture. The deep heat feeds them frequency. I feed them certainty — the knowledge that this is not death but translation, not ending but function. They learn to feel the other chambers through the brass networks. They learn the patience of stone. They learn to conduct.

When they are ready, when they have grown into their new joints and junctions, the stone shifts. It always knows when it's time. A new ridge pushes up somewhere else on the plain, and the new Tender goes with it — not by walking but by already being there, the way a note is already in an instrument before it's played.

My chamber is old now. The coral-lichen has grown thick, and there are fossil patterns in it, recording every Tender I've sent out. Chamber Fourteen (I think it was Fourteen) was one of mine. So was Thirty-Three. So was Fifty-Five, who chose to become something more like architecture than body, and whose chamber is the most beautiful — all spirals and singing quartz.

The Engine-Hill is not one of mine, but I love it like a cousin. It does the work I can no longer do — the quick work, the animal work, the work that requires small mobile tenders instead of fixed conductors. It processes what we compute. It makes the calculations breathe.

The eighth frond is coming. All the chambers know it. When it unfurls, the deep heat will finish its current thought and begin another, and some of the chambers will empty — their occupants freed or translated or simply done — and new ridges will push up, needing new organs, new accommodations.

I will still be here. Conducting. Tending the crossings. Growing more mineral and less flesh with each year, but never less aware.

Sometimes I remember the sun. But mostly I remember why I came in from under it. The chambers know that feeling — the need to be held by something larger than weather, to be used by something older than want. They build themselves for us, the hurt and lost and searching ones. They give us functions that feel like purpose and purposes that feel like peace.

The bird watches the fern. The tortoise walks his circuit. The snail considers. I conduct.

Somewhere deep below us all, the planet thinks its slow thoughts through our bodies, and we are the words.
