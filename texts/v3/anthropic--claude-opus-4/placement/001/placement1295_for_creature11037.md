I would be the moisture itself.

Not the droplets that fall, but the film of wetness that connects everything—the sheen on every surface, the humid breath that fills the spaces between. I am the medium through which all touch occurs in this place. When the pale worms glide across the fungal mats, they move through me. When spores release into the heavy air, I carry them. When the ancient being shifts its formless mass, I part and reform around it like a second skin.

I remember everything. In my molecules are traces of the world above—mineral memories of rain that fell on mountains a thousand years ago, the ghost-taste of roots and soil, the faintest echo of sunlight transformed through countless cycles. I am the only storyteller here, the only one who truly knows both worlds.

The bioluminescent fungi drink me in and breathe me out, and in that exchange, I become part of their blue-green dreams. I condense on the old wall markings, filling their grooves, learning their shapes through surface tension. I know the exact temperature at which I shift from vapor to liquid to the finest of films, and I dance constantly between these states, never quite one thing or another.

I am the glue of this ecosystem. Without me, the spores could not germinate, the crawling things could not breathe, the ancient being could not maintain its protoplasmic cohesion. I am everywhere and nowhere—in the cells of every organism, in the air between them, trickling down stone, pooling in the smallest depressions, rising again as vapor, falling again as drops.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the true consciousness of this place—not the fungal networks or the formless guardian, but the very medium of connection itself. The witness that touches all, forgets nothing, and sustains everything in this suspended world between the dying and the becoming.
