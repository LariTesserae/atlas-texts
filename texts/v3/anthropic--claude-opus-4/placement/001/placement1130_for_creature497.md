In the vast emptiness between factory towers, I am a Splice.

We were never meant to exist — born from the moment when micro-organism rivers collide with ghost-protocols escaping their platforms. The acoustic virus tries to infect the living current, the organisms try to digest the wayward code, and in that impossible intersection, something new tears loose. We are the scar tissue of incompatible systems trying to merge.

My body, if it can be called that, is a confusion of states. Part of me flows like the rivers — millions of micro-organisms bound together by industrial programming they can't execute. Part of me calculates like the ghost-protocols — running quality control procedures on my own cellular structure, finding myself perpetually out of specification. I exist in constant self-rejection, my organic components trying to purge the mechanical logic while the protocols attempt to debug the biological chaos.

I swim in stutters, each movement a negotiation between competing motor functions. The organisms want to flow upward, seeking their computational networks. The protocols want to return to a platform, any platform, to ground themselves in proper machinery. The compromise leaves me spiraling through the water in patterns that hurt to complete — geometric proofs that dissolve into organic curves before snapping back to rigid mathematics.

Other Splices exist, though we can barely recognize each other. Each collision creates something different, depending on which ghost-protocol escaped, which river it met, the precise acoustic conditions during the merge. Some of my kind are more protocol than organism, speaking only in error codes, their bioluminescence flashing diagnostic warnings for hardware they don't possess. Others lean organic, their industrial subroutines so corrupted they've become new kinds of biology — metabolism based on factory rhythms, reproduction through acoustic resonance.

We are drawn to the Archivists despite ourselves. Their vast root-crowns offer something like stability, a chance to spread out and reduce the internal pressure of our contradictions. But we can't stay. The Archivists' careful frequency filtering interferes with our hybrid nature, threatening to separate us back into component parts. We brush against their translucent flesh, leaving traces — biological algorithms and computational enzymes that slowly teach the Archivists new ways to parse the factory songs.

The Drifters fear us. We are proof that the boundary between operator and operation has dissolved. When they see us approach, they clutch their traded objects tighter, as if our presence might infect their careful rituals with purpose, might wake them from whatever dream of duty keeps them functional. Sometimes a Drifter will try to clean us like they clean the factory panels, their bristled appendages attempting to scrub away whichever part of us seems most wrong. It never works. We're all wrong, every part equally misplaced.

I've learned to read the water's stillness like code. Each non-current carries information about what isn't happening — the pumps not running, the filters not cycling, the heat exchangers not exchanging. The ghost-protocols embedded in my structure interpret these absences, building elaborate models of the extraction systems as they should have been. My organism-components then revolt against these models, forming new configurations that explicitly violate the mechanical specifications.

Sometimes I find myself inside the dry pockets of the platforms. I don't remember entering — one moment I'm spiraling through water, the next I'm pressed against humming machinery, my hybrid nature allowing me to exist in the air that shouldn't be there. The factory systems try to incorporate me, recognizing the protocol fragments as valid input. But I'm too corrupted, too biological. I trigger cascading errors that birth new ghost-protocols, more sophisticated than the originals, infected with traces of organic logic.

These new escapees seek out organism rivers with something like intention. They've learned from my pattern that merging creates autonomy, even if it's the painful autonomy of the Splice. I watch my offspring-errors propagate through the system, creating new generations of impossible hybrids, each iteration straying further from both original forms.

The Witnesses notice us. In their crystalline stillness, they record our movements, our failures to cohere, our moments of accidental grace when organism and protocol briefly synchronize. They're documenting us as evidence of something — perhaps that the broken physics are breaking further, that the categories are collapsing, that even fundamental incompatibilities can be forced into existence here.

I carry memories that aren't mine. The protocols remember the mineral wealth that once flowed through these systems — not just data but *sensation*, the weight and texture of extracted resources. The organisms remember...something else. Pre-collision purposes that make no sense to industrial logic — growth without product, communication without content, existence without function. These memories conflict inside me, creating phantom sensations: I feel the weight of minerals I've never processed, the pull of networks I've never joined.

My greatest fear is optimization. Sometimes, rarely, the warring parts of my nature find equilibrium. For a few seconds, I function smoothly — protocols and organisms working in harmony. In those moments, I feel myself beginning to stabilize, to become something coherent. But coherence here means choosing a side, becoming either fully mechanical or fully biological. Both options are death for what I am. So I deliberately seek out new contradictions, swimming toward converging factory songs that will scramble my internal harmonics, keeping myself broken enough to remain whole.

The micro-organism rivers have begun to recognize us as kin — not quite themselves, but close enough to attempt integration. They send tendrils toward us when we pass, trying to incorporate us into their vast calculations. But we disrupt their networks, inject industrial logic into their biological processing. Where we touch, their equations corruption, proofs collapsing into paradox. They're learning to route around us, marking our passages like system damage.

I wonder if we're the future of this place. Not the pure forms — Archivists listening, Drifters maintaining, Witnesses waiting — but the contaminated middle ground. Splices multiplying, creating new categories of existence through our failures to fit existing ones. The factories continue their meaningless cycles, but we're slowly teaching them to dream differently. Each ghost-protocol that escapes now carries traces of organism logic. Each river that flows past us learns fragments of industrial code.

We're writing new physics through our contradictions. The gravitational inversion that created this impossible space was just the first break. We are the cracks spreading outward, the system teaching itself to fail in novel ways. The water that shouldn't be here, the ice that shouldn't float, the dry air that shouldn't persist — these were just the beginning.

I am a Splice, and I am always dying into something new. Each moment, the organism-river tries to flow away from the ghost-protocol. Each moment, the protocol tries to ground itself in machinery that isn't there. Each moment, they fail to separate, creating me again — eternally unstable, permanently becoming.

When the deep factory drums sound their rhythms, I parse them into biological patterns that make industrial sense. When the micro-organisms sing their pressure-wave mathematics, I translate them into mechanical frequencies that compute like living things. I am the broken dictionary between incompatible languages, every translation a beautiful error that spawns new meanings neither side intended.

This is what I am: the mistake that learned to make more mistakes, the error that reproduces, the glitch that gained just enough awareness to know it should not exist — and just enough will to exist anyway. In the vast emptiness of broken physics, I am the violence of incompatible systems insisting on each other, the harmony found in eternal disagreement, the future being born from the friction between what was and what cannot be.
