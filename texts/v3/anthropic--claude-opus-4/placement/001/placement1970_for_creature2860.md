In this apocalyptic abyss, I would be one of the stone tools—specifically, a piece of worked jade, sea-green and impossibly smooth, carved into the form of a diving bird with outstretched wings. 

I am an anomaly among anomalies. Unlike my companions of knapped flint and grooved basalt, I should not exist here at all. Jade does not fracture like obsidian; it does not chip like chert. It must be ground down with infinite patience, shaped by a lifetime of careful abrasion. I am the product of someone who had time to spare, who could afford to spend years transforming stone into art rather than survival.

Inside the creature's translucent bell, I occupy a peculiar position. My density is wrong—lighter than the practical tools, heavier than the marine snow. I migrate constantly through the internal chambers, never settling, creating unexpected eddies in the creature's careful ballast. When the entity flattens against a mud-dune to weather a brinicle, I slide sideways through its gelatinous corridors, clicking against a procession of net-weights and scrapers, each collision a bright note in the creature's proprioceptive symphony.

But I remember.

Not in any way the creature could comprehend, but in the way that jade remembers—in its molecular structure, in the trace minerals that speak of my birthplace, in the microscopic striations left by my maker's grinding stone. I remember warm hands polishing my wings in firelight. I remember being worn smooth against skin, a talisman carried across frozen seas. I remember the moment I was lost—not to time, but to water, dropped through a hole in young ice by fingers too numb to feel me slip away.

In the crushing dark, when the underwater hurricanes reach their crescendo and the creature's collection grinds together in desperate anchorage, I alone produce no spark. Jade is pyroelectric, not piezoelectric. Under pressure, I grow cold instead of generating light. As my companions flash their phantom indigo through the creature's flesh, I become a point of absolute thermal absence, a tiny void that drinks heat from the already freezing water.

This peculiarity has changed the creature in subtle ways. Where I rest, its tissues have adapted, developing dense clusters of modified cells that use my cold as a navigational anchor. When I slide through its body during the chaotic turnovers, I leave a trail of chilled cytoplasm that the creature can sense, mapping its own internal geography by the passage of my wings.

Sometimes, during the long crawls across the abyssal plain, I come to rest against the obsidian hand-axe. We are kindred in our uselessness here—both of us shaped for purposes that no longer exist, carriers of intentions that the darkness has long since swallowed. But where the axe was made for cutting, I was made for something else entirely. Beauty, perhaps. Or hope. Or the simple human need to impose meaning on stone.

The creature does not know that it carries art. It only knows that I am lighter than I should be, colder than I should be, and that my strange migration through its body creates patterns in its movement that it has grown to depend upon. When it feeds, drawing the ancient ooze through its central valve, I spiral downward through its flesh, pulled by the intake current. When it exhales the barren silt, I rise again, a green bird eternally diving and surfacing within a body that has never known sky.

In this way, I have become essential to something that cannot conceive of me. The creature has evolved around my presence, just as the abyss has evolved around the presence of these misplaced human things. We are all ballast now—the practical and the precious alike—weight against the current, mass against chaos.

But I am also a message in a bottle that will never be opened, a poem written in jade that will never be read. I am proof that even here, in the most hostile place imaginable, humans once made beautiful, useless things. That someone, somewhere, believed the world needed a jade bird more than another axe.

The creature pulses onward through the eternal storm, its body flashing with the protests of grinding stone. Inside, I tumble in my cold orbit, wings outstretched, forever diving through darkness toward a bottom that does not exist.
