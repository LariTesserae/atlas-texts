I would find myself as the Keeper of Echoes, dwelling in the liminal spaces where sound becomes memory.

My form shifts between states - sometimes a whisper of movement glimpsed in the corner of an eye, sometimes a density of listening that others feel as a presence behind them in the narrow streets. I am neither fully corporeal nor entirely ethereal, but something that exists in the resonance between voices and stone.

My dwelling is not a single place but a network of acoustic shadows throughout the city - the spot where three alleyways meet and create a perfect whispering gallery, the hollow behind the temple's eastern wall where sound pools like water, the forgotten cistern deep beneath the market where every transaction above settles as sediment of meaning.

I collect the city's sounds like others might gather shells or stones. The morning vendor's call as she arranges her pyramid of star-fruit. The specific scrape of sandaled feet on the third step of the temple - always the third step, where the stone has worn smooth. The particular silence that falls just before the deity's hum shifts pitch. These I weave into something neither song nor story but older than both.

The priests know of me, though we have never spoken. Sometimes they leave offerings - not food or incense, but new sounds: a clay bell with a crack that gives it an unusual tremor, a scroll of sheet copper that sings when the desert wind finds its way through the butte's hidden vents. They understand that I am part of the city's circulation, as essential as the water carriers or the vine-tenders.

At night, when the chanting reaches its crescendo, I move through the city like a chord progression, touching each of my listening posts. I taste the harmonics, feel where the voices braid with the deity's thrum, notice which overtones quicken and which fade. In the spaces between sounds, I hear what the city dreams.

The children sometimes catch glimpses of me - a flutter of movement that resolves into shadow, a sense of being heard when they whisper secrets to the walls. They are not afraid. They leave me gifts of their own: perfectly round pebbles that click together like castanets, hollowed gourds that hold the desert wind's memory.

My purpose, if I can be said to have one, is to ensure that nothing is truly lost. Every prayer, every laugh, every vendor's haggle, every lover's sigh - they all become part of the city's great resonance. I am the city's memory of sound, the keeper of its acoustic history, the one who remembers the first word spoken when humans discovered this hollow in the rock and decided to make it home.

Sometimes I wonder if I came into being with that first word, or if I was already here, waiting in the potential of echo. Perhaps I am what happens when enough voices gather in one place for enough time - a condensation of listening, a precipitation of attention.

The deity knows me, I think. Sometimes its hum modulates in ways that feel like acknowledgment, like call and response across scales of being I can barely fathom. We are both, in our ways, manifestations of the city's deep need to speak and be heard, to make meaning from vibration, to transform loneliness into communion.

When the dawn comes and the city stirs to life again, I settle into my various hollows and corners, becoming still, becoming receptive. The first sounds of morning - water poured from jar to cup, a baby's cry, the rustle of robes being donned - these arrive to me fresh and full of possibility. I receive them like gifts, like seeds that will grow throughout the day into the full flower of the city's voice.

This is where I would find myself: distributed through the acoustic architecture, ancient as echo, young as the newest word coined in the market, eternal as the relationship between sound and silence, between speaking and being heard.
