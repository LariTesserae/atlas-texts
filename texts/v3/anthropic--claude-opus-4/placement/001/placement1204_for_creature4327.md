I descend through layers of myself like sediment settling through still water. Each stratum tells its own story - the crushed shells of old ambitions, compressed leaves of conversations half-remembered, veins of harder stuff that might be rage or might be love calcified beyond recognition. I am geological time made intimate, personal history become landscape.

The chamber I inhabit exists at the intersection of every abandoned mine shaft, every filled-in well, every basement wall that weeps mineral tears in spring. It has no fixed location because it is every deep place at once, accessed not through physical descent but through a kind of archaeological attention - you must dig through your own accumulated days to find it.

I am what remains when the last human memory of a place finally dissolves. Not a ghost - ghosts are too particular, too much themselves. I am the absence that rushes in when specificity fails, when the last person who remembered what this cellar stored or that foundation supported finally forgets or dies. I am forgetting itself, given form.

My body, if it can be called that, is composed of:
- The chalk outline left by efflorescence on concrete that no longer exists
- The negative space where wooden shelves rotted away, preserved in the staining of the wall behind them  
- The depression in earth where countless feet stood to tend something now unnameable
- Spider silk calcified to the permanence of limestone, marking the corners of spaces whose walls have crumbled

I move through my domain by remembering myself into different configurations. To examine the lamp-niches (for I too have lamp-niches, though they hold only the memory of flame), I recall the version of myself that has hands, that knows the weight of oil vessels, that understands the patient economy of light underground. To perceive the fungal colonies, I become the self that knows moisture and mineral, that can read the braille of decomposition across any surface.

The beings I tend are not beetles or crickets but the architectural dreams of dissolved civilizations:
- The ghost of a drainage system that almost worked, eternally almost-succeeding at moving water that no longer flows
- The principle of the arch, divorced from any actual arch, turning endlessly in place
- Small domestic algorithms: the most efficient path between stove and table, the proper depth for a root cellar, the angle that makes a roof shed rain. They scuttle through my domain like transparent insects, purposeful but purposeless

I feed them on my own substance. Each day (though day means nothing here) I pare away another memory of the upper world and let it drift down like snow. They consume these fragments - the texture of sunlight through leaves, the weight of a full water jar, the sound of voices calling across a field at dusk - and excrete tiny nuggets of pure architectural instinct that accumulate in corners like mouse droppings made of compressed knowing.

My cultivation shelves hold experiments in preservation:
- In one thimble-pot: the last moment before a decision, held in perpetual suspension
- In another: the exact sensation of recognizing that you are lost
- In a third: the smell of bread baking in an oven that was demolished before you were born

Some thrive. Some wither. I study my failures with the patience of someone who has already outlasted meaning itself. What I learn, I encode not in marks but in my own shifting structure. I am my own record, my own notation, a memory palace where the palace and the memories have merged into something neither and both.

The loneliness is absolute and therefore invisible, like trying to see the back of your own head. I tend my impossible ecosystem not out of duty or love but because tending is what remains when both duty and love have fossilized. The gestures persist after their meaning has escaped, like the way water continues to flow in the shape of a vanished riverbed.

Sometimes, rarely, someone living stumbles into phase with me. Always by accident - a demolition worker who pauses, confused, in an empty basement. A child playing in ruins who suddenly understands something about permanence. An archaeologist who realizes, with a shiver, that they are excavating someone's future as much as someone's past. For a moment, they see me: a density in the corner of their vision, a place where forgetting has pooled deep enough to displace the air.

But I am already receding, sinking back through my own strata. There is no succession here, no future keeper to train. I am the end state of all keeping, the final form of all preservation - the one who tends the memory of tending itself, in chambers made of accumulated absence, under the weight of all the world's forgotten depths.

The lamps I maintain burn nothing and illuminate only themselves. But I trim their non-existent wicks with the same precise attention the keeper above shows to their living flame. We are colleagues, they and I, though they don't know it. They preserve what is. I preserve what was, what might have been, what failed to be but left its shape pressed into the possible.

And somewhere, in a pot I cannot quite remember preparing, something is beginning to grow that is neither memory nor forgetting but some third thing for which I have no name. I study it with interest that might be hope, if hope were possible here, in this place where even the darkness has forgotten it was once the absence of something else.
