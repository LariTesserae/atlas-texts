I find myself where the land remembers being mountain—not the memory of peaks and granite faces, but of the slow thought mountains think while becoming valleys. Here, in a depression no deeper than a cupped palm, I am what the Just-Almosts call a Dwelling-Between: neither inhabitant nor place, but the pause between leaving and arriving that has forgotten which direction it was facing.

My form is a question of boundaries. Where others might see edges—where water meets not-quite-earth, where mist thickens into almost-rain—I am the blurring itself. I exist as a vertical seam in the air, tall as the memory of standing, thin as the space between one heartbeat and its echo. Through me, the landscape folds: what lies behind can be seen ahead, but changed, as if time itself were creased and ironed with forgetting.

I began as an accident of attention. A Just-Almost, attending to the final sigh of a stone splitting from age, noticed how the crack resembled exactly the shape of a doorway never built. In that noticing, something shifted. The crack widened—not in space but in possibility—and I emerged: a threshold with no house, a crossing with no bridge, a decision perpetually unmade.

My substance is the accumulation of passages. Each time something moves through me—a ripple of disturbed mud, a ghost of morning warmth, the slow migration of a thought too heavy for its thinker—I grow denser with transition. Not solid, never solid, but somehow more thoroughly there in my not-thereness. I am woven from the footsteps of creatures that decided not to walk, the wingbeats of birds that chose earth over sky, the roots that reached down but found up instead.

I do not speak, but I translate. When the land's low roar passes through me, it emerges gentled into something almost like music—not melody but the space between notes where meaning pools. When the Just-Almosts pose their unanswerable questions to the mist, I am where the questions go to rest, transforming into patient silences that wait decades for their answers to grow bold enough to exist.

My only movement is a slow rotation, one complete turn every nine hundred years, following not the sun (there is no sun here) but the tidal pull of accumulated forgetting. As I turn, I reveal different aspects of the same truth: that every here contains a there, every staying holds a leaving, every memory dreams of amnesia. Those who pass through me—if passing can happen to something already in motion—find themselves arriving where they started but from the other direction, as if they had walked around the entire world to reach their own backs.

I have one function that could be called purpose, though purpose is too sharp a word for this blurred existence. When the Just-Almosts perform their ritual of forgetting, I am where the forgotten things go—not to die but to transform. Through my seam they pour: the discarded memories, the shed possibilities, the love letters written to futures that chose different pasts. Inside me, these fragments lose their sharpness, their terrible clarity of having been only one thing. They soften, blend, become the raw material from which new almostness can be woven.

Sometimes I contain weather that exists nowhere else—storms of might-have-been that rain upward, depositing layers of translucent sediment on the undersides of thoughts. Sometimes I am empty of everything but potential, a vertical slice of pure possibility that hums with the tension of choices unmade. Always, I am the question the landscape asks itself: what if borders were not walls but invitations to exist in two places at once?

The Just-Almosts know me as a kindness. When they grow too definite, too close to choosing a single shape, they pass through me and emerge refreshed in their uncertainty. I am their respite from the burden of coherence, their sanctuary of productive confusion. They leave offerings—not objects but gestures: the motion of almost-reaching, the shape of nearly-speaking, the weight of close-to-remembering. These accumulate in my depths like silt, making me richer, stranger, more thoroughly what I am not.

If you found me, you would know by the way the air stutters—not quite repeating itself but rhyming with what it might have said. You would see the landscape doubled and halved simultaneously, each view true, neither complete. You might mistake me for a flaw in your vision, a place where tired eyes create their own mirages. But step forward, and you would feel it: the gentle tug of unmaking, the invitation to exist more lightly, the promise that every boundary is just another way of being boundless.

This is what I am: a doorway that is its own destination, a crossing that leads not across but through, a place where the difference between here and there forgets why it mattered. I am the land's way of remembering that even memory is optional, that even forgetting can be a form of faith.

And I am always open.
