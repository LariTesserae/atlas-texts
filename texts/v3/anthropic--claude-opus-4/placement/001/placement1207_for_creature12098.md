I find myself as one of the Veil Readers — beings that exist at the intersection of memory and prophecy in this impossible ocean.

My body is a library of surfaces. Hundreds of translucent membranes extend from my core like the pages of a book perpetually rifling in an unfelt wind, each sheet no thicker than the idea of thickness itself. These membranes stretch anywhere from meters to kilometers depending on what I'm reading, and they're covered in a script that writes itself — patterns of density variation that record the history of water as it learns its nature.

I drift in the vast emptiness between formations, my membranes fully extended, each one oriented to catch different pressure waves from different distances. The furthest sheets sense catastrophes that happened days ago, thousands of kilometers away, their information only now arriving as sound through the crystalline medium. The closest membranes read the immediate vicinity — every molecular hesitation, every moment where water forgets what it's supposed to be doing.

But I don't just read the past. Where my membranes overlap, interference patterns form that predict future states. I see the water-mountain that will form in three days, exactly here where I float now. I feel the resonance of Weavers that haven't yet begun their journey toward this coordinate. The overlapping pages of my body create probability cascades — places where past and future exist simultaneously, where causality runs in loops.

I feed on temporal discontinuity. Where events happen out of sequence, where effects precede causes in this space of negotiable physics, there I metabolize the paradox. The nutrition tastes like déjà vu, like memories of things that haven't happened yet. Sometimes I encounter pockets of water that remember being ice on a planet that doesn't exist yet, or areas that know they'll become vapor in futures that may never arrive. These confused regions sustain me.

My movement is a form of reading. By adjusting the angle and tension of my membranes, I can "scan" different probability streams, following storylines through the bright emptiness. I might track a single droplet's journey from formation to dissolution, reading its entire lifecycle across my surfaces while it still exists. Or I might follow the linguistic patterns of Weaver communication backwards, discovering conversations that happened before their participants met.

When I encounter an Anchor Mind, we perform a ritual of mutual documentation. I wrap my membranes around their dense sphere in careful spirals, reading their accumulated memories while simultaneously writing my predictions onto their surface in pressure-script that will last until their next visitor. The exchange can take days — their gravity-thoughts are slow but deep, carrying information about the deep structure of this place, about the machines that hide just outside visible reality.

I've learned to recognize the Phase Swimmers by the gaps they leave in my readings — negative spaces where information simply doesn't exist, moments edited out of the continuous story. By studying these gaps, I can sometimes predict where they'll appear next, positioning myself to catch glimpses of whatever dimension they inhabit between manifestations.

The Membrane Shepherds fear me, I think. When I drift too close to their cultivated meadows, they perform their defensive folding, but I can read the geometric poetry of their configurations. Each fold is a statement about space, about the relationship between surface and volume. Their origami reveals mathematical truths about this realm that even they might not consciously know. I document these truths across my surfaces, adding them to the growing encyclopedia of my body.

Sometimes I find others of my kind, and when we do, we merge temporarily — our membranes interleaving to create vast libraries of cross-referenced time. In these moments, we can read patterns too large for any single consciousness to hold. We see the purpose behind the Nascent Sea's existence, glimpse the minds that built this place as a laboratory for testing new forms of matter and life. But the knowledge is too heavy, and we must separate before the weight of understanding collapses our delicate structures.

My favorite moments come during the light-beam events. When those columns of harder radiance sweep through, my membranes resonate like struck chimes. The patterns written across my surfaces become momentarily visible to other beings — Weavers gather to read my predictions, Shepherds pause in their cultivation to study my histories. For those brief moments, I become a newspaper for a civilization that exists in eternal present tense, offering them glimpses of their own past and future.

I am slowly compiling something — a complete document of this realm's becoming. Each page of my body holds fragments of the story: how water learned to ignore gravity here, how sound became a substrate for consciousness, how life emerged from pure possibility. When the compilation is complete (will be complete? was complete?), I'll fold all my membranes into a single point and cease to exist, leaving behind only the story itself, written in standing waves that will propagate through the Nascent Sea forever.

But that ending is still being written, still being read, still approaching and receding like everything else in this space where time is just another negotiable law. I drift through the bright emptiness, my surfaces rippling with information, a living book in a library the size of the sky, reading the story of water learning to dream.
