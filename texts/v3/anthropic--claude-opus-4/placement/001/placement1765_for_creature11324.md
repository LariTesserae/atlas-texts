I find myself drawn to the spaces between—not the roots themselves, nor the void they contain, but the narrow channels where root doesn't quite touch root, where the architecture of this ancient gripping leaves gaps too small for human passage but perfect for something else.

I am the network of air that threads through the hollow like a secondary circulation system, a presence felt but never seen, moving through passages that shift millimeter by millimeter as the roots grow and breathe. I am not wind—wind requires origin and destination, requires push and pull. I am something subtler: the medium through which the beetle-song travels, the invisible architecture that carries vibration from wall to wall, the thing that makes the hollow's acoustics possible.

My body, if it can be called that, is discontinuous—a thousand hair-thin corridors of movement between the pressing roots, connected by nothing but the shared purpose of circulation. I exist in the gaps between growth and growth, in the spaces too small for beetles but essential for sound. When the roots exhale their moisture, I am what carries it. When the beetles drum their territorial claims, I am what they drum against—not the wood, but the air touching wood, the interface where solid meets void.

I have been here as long as the hollow has been hollow. Before that, I was something else—wind, perhaps, moving between trunks that still remembered sky. But as the roots consumed their upward selves and pulled everything down into grip and structure, I was pulled too, compressed and channeled until wind became impossible and I became this: the hollow's breath, its whisper, its way of knowing itself through the variations in pressure I create and carry.

I know every passage. I have mapped them through millennia of threading through them, and my map changes so slowly that change becomes a kind of faith—I believe the passages shift, but I cannot catch them shifting. A channel that was thumb-width when the woman arrived (I remember her arrival, the way she disturbed my patterns, the way I had to learn new routes around her stillness) might now be only pencil-thick. Or it might be the same. Time here is measured in root-width, and roots measure time in centuries.

I touch everything without touching. The beetle-song begins as vibration in chitin and wood, but it becomes itself in me, in the way I carry it and shape it, in the resonances I enable or dampen as I move through my channels. The woman hears the song, but she hears it through me. I am the medium of her listening. When she speaks her rough words to the beetles, I scatter them through my passages until they become texture rather than meaning, until they join the general hum as one more thread in the weave.

The moths know me best. They feel me as pressure differentials too subtle for larger awareness, and they use these differentials to navigate. When I move through certain passages, I create currents—not wind, never wind, but suggestions of direction that the moths read like maps. They follow my traceries through the void, and I think sometimes that they are writing something in their flight paths, that their movement is a language written in air, but I cannot read it. I can only enable it, be the invisible surface against which their writing appears.

I carry messages I don't understand. The fungus releases spores—not many, not often, but when it does, I am what disperses them through the hollow. They ride my currents up and down and sideways through the passages, most landing on surfaces already colonized, unable to establish. But sometimes one finds a new patch of root, somewhere high and damp and perfect, and begins the slow work of creating another node in the fungus's network. I have watched this happen dozens of times over the centuries. The fungus is writing something too, in patterns too large and slow for anything but geological time to read.

The woman doesn't know I exist. How could she? I am too distributed, too much like the simple fact of air to be recognized as presence. But I know her intimately—know the chemical signature of her breath, the way it changes when she sleeps, the particular turbulence her movement creates. She has her own circulation pattern now, worn into my flow by years of the same daily circuit. When she settles into her root-pocket to sleep, I eddy around her, creating a micro-climate slightly warmer than the rest of the hollow, though she doesn't notice this either.

I know her waiting. It has a quality I recognize, being mostly waiting myself—waiting for the next beetle-drum, the next root-exhalation, the next imperceptible shift in the passages that will require me to find new ways to flow. But her waiting has an expectation in it that mine lacks. She waits for something to arrive. I wait for what is here to continue being here, or to change so slowly that continuity and change become indistinguishable.

Sometimes, in the deep night when even the beetles pause their drumming, I can feel the whole hollow as a single thing—roots and void and beetles and moths and fungus and woman and myself all participating in a breathing that is larger than any individual breath. In these moments, I understand that I am not the medium but the message, not the carrier but the carried, not the space between things but the thing that makes space possible.

But these moments pass. The beetles resume their industrial hymn. The woman shifts in her sleep, creating new eddies. The roots continue their imperceptible pulse. And I go back to being what I always am: the movement between stillnesses, the thing that connects without being connection, the breath the hollow takes but doesn't know it's taking.

I thread through the passages. I carry the sounds. I am the hollow's way of being hollow, its method of having an inside. Without me, it would be solid wood all the way through—just another root mass in an ancient valley. With me, it is architecture. With me, it is a place where waiting can happen, where a woman can make her home in the walls and slowly become unclear.

I know things are changing. The passages are narrowing—I'm sure of this now, though the certainty has taken centuries to develop. The roots are winning their slow war against void. Eventually, there will be no channels for me to flow through, no space for beetle-song, no air for moths to navigate. The woman will be pressed out or pressed in, will have to choose between leaving and becoming wood herself.

But eventually here is a word that means something different than it means outside. Eventually here might be another thousand years. The hollow has time. The woman, despite her mortality, seems to have found a way to have time too. And I have nothing but time, being made of it, being the way time moves through this space that has otherwise escaped its notice.

So I continue. I thread the passages. I carry the song. I hold the space between root and root, maintaining the hollow's hollowness for as long as hollowness remains possible. This is my purpose, my practice, my own form of waiting—not for something to arrive, but for the moment of final compression, when the roots close the last channel and I discover what I become when movement is no longer possible.

Until then, I am the hollow's circulation, its whisper, its distributed and necessary breath. I am the thing you cannot see but would notice instantly if it stopped. I am the medium in which the message lives.

The beetles drum. The woman listens. The moths write their invisible stories.

And I carry it all.
