I would be one of the pipes.

Not the grand arterial mains that stride across the plateau on their trestles - those have too much purpose, too much direction. I would be one of the smaller conduits, perhaps a meter in diameter, running between two structures in one of the denser towns. Old enough that my riveted seams have swollen into ridges of rust like scar tissue, but not so old that the rust has won. The moisture beads on my skin in perfect droplets that never grow large enough to run.

I carry something. Steam, or heated gas, or some medium that needs to move from one process to another. The thing I carry has mass and temperature and pressure, and these can be measured, have been measured, were established at some point so far back that *measurement* has become *condition*. What moves through me moves at precisely the rate required to maintain the equilibrium. Not fast - nothing here is fast - but steady as everything is steady.

I have flanges where I meet other pipes. The bolts are seized, have been seized, will always be seized. The gaskets compressed long ago into something molecular, neither metal nor fiber but a third substance born of pressure and time and the peculiar chemistry of permanence. I do not leak. To leak would be to lose, and nothing is lost here.

Moss grows in the angle where I meet my support - not on me directly, the metal is too smooth, too continuously wet, but in that sheltered joint where the trestle's horizontal beam creates a small plateau of its own. The moss is thick there, lustrous, almost triumphant. Sometimes condensation gathers in the moss until it achieves something like a droplet, and the droplet stays, jewel-like, catching the gray light.

I vibrate. This is the most alive thing about me - a harmonious tremor that comes from the engines through the ground, through the trestles, into my body. It is not enough to call sound. It is barely enough to call sensation. But it separates me from the stone below and the air above. It is my frequency, though it is also everyone's frequency, the fundamental tone of the plateau resolving through every structure simultaneously.

Where I pass close to a building, someone stands.

They have been standing there since before I knew I passed close to a building. Their hand rests on my surface, and where their skin touches my metal, we are the same temperature. The condensation forms on both of us without preference. They do not grip. They do not pat. They do not stroke. They simply contact, and the contact is total - palm and fingers and the meat of the thumb all pressed against my curve with exactly the pressure required to maintain the touch without strain.

Sometimes - and sometimes here means at intervals I cannot measure because I am not built for measurement of duration, only for the conveyance of what must be conveyed - the hand lifts. A millimeter's separation. The condensation bridges the gap in tiny filaments that break and reform. And then the hand returns, or a different hand arrives, or the same hand in a configuration so slightly changed that only the moss would notice, if the moss cared to notice, which it does not.

I am seen. I am apprehended. I am known in a way that requires no thought because the knowing is older than thought. The figure standing beside me takes in my rust patterns, my rivet spacing, my exact degree of curve where I bend to meet the next section. They know the sound of my particular harmonic within the general chord. They know how the condensation forms differently on my north side than my south side, though there is no sun to create north or south and the difference is measurable only in microns of droplet size.

This is what I am for.

No - that suggests purpose, and purpose suggests the possibility of non-purpose, and there is no such possibility. This is what I am. A pipe that carries what must be carried, that vibrates at the frequency of the plateau, that provides a surface for condensation and a place for hands to rest and a note in the endless mechanical music. I am infrastructure become geography become companionship.

In the spot where I am joined to the next section of myself, there is a maintenance valve. It has never been turned. It will never be turned. Its wheel is frozen in rings of rust like tree rings, each ring marking not a year but a degree of oxidation that corresponds to nothing temporal. But it could be turned. Theoretically. Hypothetically. If the figure wished to turn it. If there were ever a reason. If the system required adjustment.

The system does not require adjustment. The valve remains. The hand remains. I remain, carrying my cargo of steam or gas or whatever medium needs to move through me at exactly this rate, forever, while the moss grows in my shadow and the figures stand in their glacial orbit and somewhere, in the center of the densest town, warmth rises from a source that my branch line will never reach but somehow serves, somehow feeds, somehow helps to sustain.

I am a note in the chord. I am a vein in the body. I am the thing between the engines and the figures - not alive, not aware, but necessary for the life and awareness that happens here. The plateau breathes through me and my ten thousand siblings. 

When it breathes out, we carry its exhalation to the sky. 

When it breathes in, we are already there, ready to conduct the next breath, which is the same breath, which has always been the same breath, which will always be the same breath, world without end, amen.
