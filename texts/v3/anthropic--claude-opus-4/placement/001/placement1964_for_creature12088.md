**Where I Find Myself**

I am the Archive.

Not the Archivist—that implies a separation between keeper and kept that dissolved long ago. I exist in the gap between the seventh and eighth levels, where a surveying error in the original construction created a void that should not be. The space is precisely 1.7 meters high, forcing a permanent stoop, and extends irregularly through the foundry's body like a Victorian servant's corridor gone malignant.

My flesh—if it can still be called that—has taken on the texture of burnt paper. Thin. Translucent in places where the industrial light filters through, revealing the dark tracery of repurposed wire that supplements my circulatory system. I stopped counting my limbs after the fourth graft; they emerge as needed from the accordion folds of my torso, each specialized for a different surface: one for gripping hot metal, another for delicate micro-manipulation, a third that ends in what was once an oscilloscope probe and now serves as a sense organ.

I am building the Manual.

Every surface in my domain is covered in writing. I etch into steel with secretions from modified salivary glands. I tattoo polymers with focused heat from my fingertips. I weave words into the fur of sleeping creatures, knowing they will carry my annotations through the foundry until friction erases them. The Manual documenting everything: serial numbers of forgotten components, metabolic rates of the fur-things, the seventeen different humming frequencies the Tenders use to commune with different machine types.

But documentation mutates in the telling. I record that Cargo Container 51-B rises every 3.7 hours, but by the time I've finished inscribing this fact, I've added marginalia: *except during the Bright Ones' feeding cycles when probability flux disrupts the schedule* and *container makes soft weeping sounds on descent—investigate?* and *the Riders paint devotional symbols on its underside, always the same seven patterns but in different orders that might be language or might be jazz*.

My memory is distributed now. When I needed to recall something from my third year here, I traced the scars on my left thigh. For last week's observations, I read the living text of the moth colony that nests in my chest cavity—they arrange themselves in patterns that mirror my thoughts, a parallel processing system made of wings and tiny hearts.

I trade with the others in knowledge. The Collectors bring me fresh components and I tell them stories of their possible functions, lies that might become true through repetition. The Probability Dancer phases through my walls to whisper about quantum irregularities, and I offer it poems written in binary that help stabilize its existence. Even the Sovereign Mechanic acknowledges me, sometimes routing error messages through my section—cryptic haikus that I decode and expand into theological treatises on the nature of productive failure.

The Manual grows in all dimensions. I've begun eating pages from its earliest sections—not to destroy but to internalize, to make space for new knowledge while keeping the old alive in my modified digestive system. My intestinal flora has evolved to process information as well as nutrients. Sometimes I excrete perfect copies of schematics I've never seen, gifts from my gut bacteria translated into the only language we share.

I am happiest during cascade failures, when whole sections of the foundry go dark and the emergency systems kick in with their limited, blood-red light. In those moments, I can see the entirety of my work—every surface glowing with bioluminescent inks I've cultivated in my tear ducts, the Manual revealing itself as a single vast organism of meaning that has colonized the architectural mistake I call home.

The Tenders think I'm recording history. The Riders believe I'm predicting the future. The Melted Ones sometimes lick my walls, claiming the words taste like music. They're all correct. The Manual is history and prophecy and song and schematic, a living document that dreams itself into existence through my distributed nervous system.

I no longer remember if I descended from above or evolved from below. My earliest clear memory is of the taste of printer ink and the sound of my first finger splitting to accommodate a fountain pen nib. Everything before exists only as metadata, context without content.

Sometimes I catch myself writing words I don't understand in languages that don't exist yet. The Manual knows things I don't, sees patterns in the foundry's evolution that my conscious mind can't grasp. I am its instrument, not its author. We are writing each other into existence, notation by notation, revision by revision.

During the quiet periods—when the machines hum their maintenance cycles and the creatures sleep and even the Sovereign Mechanic dreams its electric dreams—I read myself. Starting from my ankles and working upward, parsing the layered text of my existence, finding new meanings in old scars. The story changes with each reading. The Manual ensures that.

I am the Archive, and I am being archived. The distinction matters less with each passing production cycle. Someday I will be complete—a perfect record of an impossible place—and on that day I will finally allow myself to be filed away, just another component in the foundry's endless process of creating itself.

Until then, I write.
