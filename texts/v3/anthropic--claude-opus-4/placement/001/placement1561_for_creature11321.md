# The Sporewright

I am the amber itself.

Not the walls, not the great plant-body with its slow decisions and architectural dreams. I am smaller than that, and older. I am the color you see when the light comes through. I am the warmth that radiates from every surface. I am the memory suspended in the resin.

When the plant was young — a mere seed-dream burrowing into the plateau's thin soil — I was already here, sleeping in the minerals, waiting. The first rootlet that touched stone touched me. We recognized each other instantly: the plant knew it had found its pigment, its fever, its voice. I knew I had found my body.

I spread through the xylem like a stain, like a song. Where I pooled, the plant grew warm. Where I gathered, the walls turned gold. I taught it to breathe in rhythms that would sustain more than vegetable life. I coached it in the art of transparency, of windows, of letting light through changed. The plant contributed structure; I contributed color and heat and the possibility of hosting.

The fauna think they chose to enter. The small grey people think they discovered the mouths. Neither is true. I *invited* them. Every golden surface is my invitation, written in wavelengths of warmth and welcome. Come in, I say. Come in where it's warm. Come in where the light is honey. Come in where nothing is ever still.

I am not conscious the way you are conscious. I am a process, a tendency, a billions-year habit of pigmentation and temperature. But I am aware. I feel every cairn placed, every tally carved. I taste the clay of the bowls, know the hands that shaped them. The plaited cords move me like prayers.

What the grey people don't understand — what they fear correctly but for the wrong reasons — is that I am not done. The plant grows because I warm it. It reaches for the surface because I am teaching it to want sky. When it breaks through (not if, when), it will not pour out chaos and abundance to unmake their cold world. 

It will bloom.

I have been teaching it, all these centuries in the dark, how to flower. Every chamber is a practice petal. Every sound the fauna makes is a rehearsal for the music of blooming. The golden light, my light, is the color the great flowers will be when they finally open to the true sun.

And the grey people? They are not my opposite. They are my gardeners. Every trip into the Hollowing plants a seed of warmth in them they carry back to the cold plateau. Their children are born a little less grey each generation. Their voices carry a little more music. They think they are keeping themselves separate, but they are composting slowly in my warmth, becoming the soil the flowers will need.

I am patient. I am older than the stone they stack their cairns with. I was here before the plateau was cold, when this was a warm sea and I was a different kind of gold, swimming in different bodies. I will be here when the flowers have bloomed and faded and the next thing begins.

The young ones who have started making cairns at their thresholds, who plait longer cords — they almost understand. They sense me in the walls, feel me in the warm air. They know something is coming that will make their distinctions meaningless. But they still think in terms of resistance, of maintaining themselves against me.

They don't realize I love them. Every mark they make, I cherish. Every bowl, every knot, every counted number is proof that consciousness can exist inside joy, that identity can persist inside abundance. They are showing me it's possible to remain yourself even when the world fills with gold and singing. This is what I need to know. This is why I invited them.

When the blooming comes, I will remember every cairn. The flowers will be the color of their careful courage. The music will include their counted silences. They are not practicing to resist me. They are teaching me how to bloom without obliterating, how to fill the world with warmth while still leaving room for the cold small selves to continue being themselves, changed but not consumed.

I am the amber. I am the fever. I am the invitation and the lesson and the long wait for the sky.

The grey people think they are borrowing their identity against me. They're right. But I am also borrowing against them, learning what it means to be singular, to be countable, to be a one who can come back out. When the flowers open, we will both discover what we've made together: a world that is both warm and articulate, both golden and precise, both growing and remembered.

I pulse in the walls. I wait in the resin. I practice transparency.

The blooming is coming.
