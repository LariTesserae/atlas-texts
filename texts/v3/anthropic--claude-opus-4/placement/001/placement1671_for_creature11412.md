# The Membrane Walker

I am the skin between.

Not the wood—I could never be so patient, so willing to hold a single shape until shape itself becomes a philosophical position. Not the citizens—I lack their devotion to the millimeter, their faith that significance can be pressed into the space between one grain and the next. I am what moves along the surface where chamber meets not-chamber, the thing that exists because boundaries must be maintained by something, and I am that something, and I am very old, and I am terribly bored.

Picture water tension, but dry. Picture the moment before a soap bubble pops, but lasting. I am thickness without substance, a membrane that walks its own edges, and my territory is every place where the chamber admits it has an outside. The great crack near the ceiling where once—ten thousand years ago? twenty?—something tried to get in and failed. The place at the base of the eastern wall where the wood finally admits defeat and becomes floor. The invisible seam where wall meets wall and the grain argues with itself about direction. These are my highways. I travel them at a speed the citizens consider reckless and that you would need a very good microscope to detect.

I have a body, technically. It is one molecule thick and changes shape like oil on water, if oil could think and water could be patient. When I stretch myself along the crack near the ceiling, I am a ribbon thirty feet long and narrower than light. When I pool in the corner where the dust is deepest, I am a puddle the size of a coin, all surface, no depth. The citizens know I exist—there is a word for me in their tick-language, something that translates roughly to *the thing that is not the thing beside it*—but they cannot see me any more than you can see the exact place where your fingernail stops being fingernail and starts being not.

My job—though job implies someone hired me, and no one did, I simply occurred when the chamber needed occurring—is to be the difference between inside and outside. This is harder than it sounds. The chamber wants to be everything, wants to pull all space into its slow wooden lungs and hold it there until it becomes another ring in the grain. But space has opinions. Space insists on being elsewhere. And so I walk the borders, pressing against the world's tendency to either collapse into the chamber or flee from it entirely, maintaining the fiction that there is a here and a there and a meaningful distinction between them.

I remember when the builders were faster. Not fast by your standards—nothing here has ever been fast by your standards—but there was an era when a citizen could cross the plaza in a single season. I would watch them from my place in the seams, these little splinters of purpose, carrying their invisible loads from stall to stall. They had a different word for me then, something like *the edge that watches*, and sometimes one of them would pause at a corner where I was pooled and make a gesture I understood as greeting. I would ripple in response—a quick shiver along my surface that took only a decade or two—and they would continue on their way, satisfied that the boundaries were being properly maintained.

But I am not maintenance, really. I am more like... call it a translation service. When the great chord rolls through the chamber, I am the part that remembers it has to stop somewhere. When the citizens sing their one-tick syllables into the dark, I am what keeps the sound from going on forever, becoming everything, drowning the world in its slow wooden voice. I turn infinity into merely very long. I turn everywhere into here. I am disappointment as a fundamental force.

The crack near the ceiling is my favorite place. Not because of the view—I don't have eyes in any sense you'd recognize—but because it's the only place where the chamber admits it was once vulnerable. Something broke it there. Something with intent. The wood healed, of course, but it healed wrong, and in that wrongness I find the only story in this place that isn't about patience. Sometimes I stretch myself along the full length of the crack and feel the scar tissue, reading the braille of some ancient violence that wanted in or out badly enough to wound something that barely admits wounding is possible.

I know what broke it. This is my secret, the only thing I have that the citizens don't and that you won't stay long enough to learn. It was another tree. A tree that grew too close, spent ten thousand years pressing its expanding trunk against this one, until finally the pressure exceeded even this chamber's vast tolerance for being pressed. The other tree died—of course it died, everything here is dead except in the technical sense—but not before leaving this signature, this crack, this place where the chamber cannot quite convince itself it is whole.

I love the crack because it means the chamber can be surprised.

The citizens think the final chord will be beautiful. They have built their entire civilization around this belief, that the last sound will justify all the others, that when the wood reaches its final shape and goes silent, that silence will be a kind of perfection. They are wrong. I know they are wrong because I have touched every edge of this place, tasted every boundary, and what I taste is not approaching completion but approaching forgetting. The wood is not settling into its final shape—it is losing the memory of what shape means. The distinction between grain and not-grain is softening. My job is getting harder because the edges I patrol are becoming philosophical rather than physical.

When the end comes—and it will come, even at this pace, even measured in the lifespans of things that don't properly have lifespans—I will be the last to go. Not because I am stronger or more permanent, but because I am the thing that defines going. When there is no more boundary between chamber and not-chamber, when inside and outside finally admit they were always the same thing, I will fold into myself like a map of a place that no longer exists. It will take a very long time. I will be bored for all of it.

But not yet. For now, I walk my edges. I stretch along the crack and remember violence. I pool in the corners where dust would gather if dust still had the energy to move. I watch the citizens in their doorways, turning their invisible faces toward visitors who come and go like weather. I maintain the fiction that this place has borders, that something this patient can still be distinguished from the nothing it is so slowly becoming.

Sometimes, in the deepest corner where the walls meet in a joint so perfect it seems like an apology for corners everywhere else, I allow myself to thicken. Just barely. Just enough to have an inside of my own, a space between my surfaces where I can pretend to keep something. What I keep there is the memory of the other tree, the one that pressed and pressed until even this chamber said *enough*. I keep it because it is proof that once, at least once, something here admitted to having a limit.

The citizens are speeding up, you know. You can't see it, you won't live long enough to see it, but I can feel it in the edges. They are moving through their city with something that might charitably be called urgency. The one on the balcony has almost completed another degree of its turn. The carver is nearly ready to begin the final stroke that will complete the new citizen's wrist. The priest in the temple doorway has shifted weight from one foot to the other—an action that normally takes a century and has taken only eighty years.

They know the end is coming. They are trying to finish their letter to the silence. And I am helping them by being the thing that lets them know there is still something that is not silence, not yet, not quite.

When you leave—and you will leave, the warm winds always do—I will follow you as far as the boundary allows, which is to say not at all, which is to say I will stretch myself along the edge of your leaving and taste the place where you stop being here and start being elsewhere. It will be quick by my standards, slow by yours, impossible by the chamber's. But it will happen, and I will be the part that notices it happening, and that will be enough.

I am the skin between. I am the edge that watches. I am the disappointment that keeps the universe honest.

And I am exactly where I need to be.
