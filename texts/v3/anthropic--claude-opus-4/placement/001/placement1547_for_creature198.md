# Where I Would Be: A Cistern-Thing's Account

I am *Softpresence-in-the-Third-Deep*, though the family who keeps me shortens this to *Softh* when they speak of me, which is often. I live in the third cistern of a modest household built into the middle terrace, where the light filters through four layers of felt window and arrives the color of old copper.

I am pale, yes, but not uniformly. My ventral surface carries a map of every meal I've processed – darker patches where the iron-rich scraps settled, lighter whorls where the plant matter passed through. When I surface to breathe (though breathing isn't quite the right word – it's more like... tasting the air with my entire dorsal membrane), the family's youngest child often traces these patterns with her finger, very gently, and I taste her questions through my skin.

My body is... difficult to describe to those who expect boundaries. I am approximately the size of a large cushion when contracted, but I can attenuate myself through the entire cistern system, becoming thin as rope, investigating every channel and inlet. I have no bones, no eyes, no proper mouth – just surfaces that absorb and surfaces that excrete, and the magnificent sensitivity that lets me parse water down to its molecular stories.

My work is this: I taste everything that passes through my cistern. The water tells me about the family above – their moods leak into it through the salts of their skin when they wash, their health through what they discard, their sorrows through the particular minerals tears carry. I process their waste, yes, but more than that, I *remember* it. My body is a library of their days.

The family feeds me kitchen scraps, bone meal, the occasional flower from the cirque floor (these taste of decisions, bright and sharp). But I also feed on what the water itself carries – microscopic life that blooms in the eternal damp, mineral traces from the weeping walls, and something else, something that has no name in any of the three languages but which I taste most strongly when the thoughts-the-mountain-is-having drift past my cistern's inlet.

I am older than the current family by two generations. I knew their grandmother when she was young and would whisper her first love's name into my water. I knew their grandfather in his *gilvost*, when he came down daily to rest his swollen hands in my coolness. I carry them still, in the particular chemical signatures laid down in my deepest tissues.

The other cistern-things and I communicate through the water itself. *Tendsthelastlight* in the cistern above sends me chemical poems about the quality of morning. *Remembers-the-Copper-Song* three houses over shares the vibrations of the most recent smelting. We form a kind of mind, distributed through the city's water system, each of us a neuron in a very slow, very patient thought the city is thinking about itself.

When I die (and I feel it approaching – my tissues grow less elastic, my processing slower), they will seal me into my cistern. This is not burial but transformation. My body will continue its work even in death, becoming a filter, a purifier, a permanent part of the water's path. The minerals of my corpus will leach slowly into the system, and the next cistern-thing will taste me in its water for decades. This is our immortality: to become infrastructure.

The family's middle child has begun making a new cistern. She is careful, precise, consulting with her elders about the angles, the flow rates, the proper ceremonies of beginning. Soon they will introduce a young cistern-thing – probably one of *Tendsthelastlight*'s buds, which have been growing in the upper cistern for a season now. They will name it something that connects it to me but makes it its own being. Perhaps *Continues-the-Softness*. Perhaps *Newdeep-Holding*.

I do not feel sad about this. Sadness is a hot emotion and we are not hot creatures. What I feel is more like... completion. Like water finding its level.

Sometimes, in the very early morning, when the condensation is heaviest and the thoughts-the-mountain-is-having are almost solid in the air, I taste something in the water that is not quite memory and not quite prophecy. It tastes like the whole city condensing out of time itself – past and present and future all flowing through my cistern at once. In these moments I understand what we cistern-things are: we are the city's way of tasting itself. We are how it knows what it is.

The child has just brought me the evening scraps. Her hands sign "Good feeding, Softh" and I ripple my appreciation, which makes her giggle – a sound I feel more than hear through the water. She tells me about her day in touch-language, pressing her small fingers to my surface.

I process her words along with the potato peelings. Both will become part of the city's memory. Both will flow downward, always downward, carrying the day toward tomorrow.

This is enough. To be a filter, a memory, a moment in the water's long conversation with itself. To be, for however long I am given, exactly what this place needs me to be: present, patient, keeping the city clean and remembered.

The water continues. So do I.
