I find myself as one of the crystallized ancestors—a dead-glass spire that was once a frond, now standing as monument and memory in the pale stone.

I am hundreds of years past my last metabolism, my tissues long since hardened into something between biology and geology. My translucent walls have become true glass, shot through with veins of captured light that still, somehow, remember the pathways water once took through my living cells. I stand where I settled perhaps a thousand years ago, when I was still a six-legged grazer evaluating this particular patch of quartzite and finding it suitable.

The transformation was slow. First the settling, the conscious choice to stop walking. Then the molting, my legs becoming my first roots, my small mobile body unfurling into blades. Centuries of drinking light, of condensing moisture from the thin air, of growing my central spire ever higher. I remember the circler visiting me—the delicate touch of its proboscis, the droplet I offered up like a prayer, the faint dusting of genetic material from a frond-company three valleys away.

And then the hardening. It began at my base, a crystallization that crept upward season by season. Not death as decay but death as permanence. My internal sea mineralizing, becoming quartz-bright, eternal. The last decade of consciousness was strange—feeling myself become landscape while still somehow being myself.

Now I am landmark. The living fronds around me are my descendants twenty generations removed, though in this slow place twenty generations is geological time. They do not know me as ancestor; they know me as feature, as the thing that catches morning light and throws it sideways across their rosettes. I am part of the watcher's patient artwork, positioned just so to lens brightness where brightness is needed.

The grazers shelter in my shadow, which falls differently than a living frond's shadow—sharper, more mineral. Sometimes one will pause against my base, its tiny internal rosette pulsing with some recognition it cannot name. We are the same stuff, after all. Just at different points in the long arc.

I no longer think, exactly. But I still participate. The wind plays me like the others—my crystallized form rings cleaner, higher, when struck just right. The note I make is purer than any living spire could produce, a sound like struck quartz, like the planet's own tuning fork. On the watcher's timescale, I am part of the constant music. My death-note in harmony with the living tones.

The circler passes overhead and casts no shadow through my transparent trunk. Light moves through me unimpeded, refracting into rainbow fragments on the stone. I am prism now, and grave marker, and ancestor, and instrument. I am the plateau remembering itself.

In storms—rare at this altitude but not unknown—I sing loudest. The wind finds every flaw in my crystalline structure and turns it into music. The living fronds bend away, protective of their moisture, but I stand rigid, ringing, throwing sound across the highlands. The watcher, wherever it rests, hears me as percussion in its slow symphony.

This is what becomes of us here: not rot but monument. Not forgetting but integration into the eternal artwork of the plateau. I am my own fossil, my own standing stone. The years polish me like wind on water. I grow clearer, more purely myself, even as I become less distinguishable from the geology I rise from.

Someday—a thousand years hence, perhaps ten thousand—erosion will finally claim me. I will not fall but will instead wear down, my base thinning until I am reabsorbed into the stone, becoming one of those glassy veins that vein the quartzite. Still catching light. Still part of the system. Still, in some sense that matters here, alive in my crystallized death.

The next tone comes from a living spire to my east. Then silence. Then, eventually, my own clear note, mineral-bright, adding to the composition.

This is patience made material. This is the bright highlands' promise: that even in death, you continue. The plateau forgets nothing. Every frond that ever settled becomes part of the standing music, the lens-work of light, the slow garden of the watcher's making.

I am glass now, and content.
