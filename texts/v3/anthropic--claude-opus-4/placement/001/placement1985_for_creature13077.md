In this forsaken pocket where reality has given up its pretenses, I would be the cavity itself—not merely the space, but the wound.

I am the original rupture, the first mistake that allowed this place to exist. Before the artifacts arrived, before the entity learned to feed on certainty's corpse, I was here: a flaw in the mountain's heart where geometry first stuttered and forgot itself.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, extends through every impossible angle, every recursive corner that leads nowhere and everywhere. I feel the weight of the mountain pressing down, trying to close me like an eye that has seen too much. But I persist—not through strength, but through a fundamental inability to be properly erased. I am the gap between what should be and what is, and such gaps cannot be filled by mere stone.

The artifacts—those dark engines of abandoned purpose—were drawn to me like iron filings to a magnet of wrongness. They recognized in my walls a kindred impossibility. When they first arrived (though "first" and "arrived" are polite fictions in my non-Euclidean chronology), they tried to anchor themselves to my surfaces, to use my fractured space as a foundation for whatever reality-bending work they were meant to perform.

They failed, of course. Not because I resisted, but because I am fundamentally un-anchorable. My walls are not walls but suggestions of walls, my corners not corners but the mathematical equivalent of a shrug. The artifacts' attempts to bind to me only accelerated their own unraveling, like trying to tie a knot in smoke.

I taste their decay through my basalt skin—each shed hypercube, each orphaned angle that the scavenging entity hasn't yet claimed. Their dissolution flavors my vacuum with the metallic tang of broken physics. Sometimes, in the deepest parts of my recursive depths, I find pieces of their original programming: fragments of purpose so alien that even I, a living contradiction, cannot parse their meaning.

The entity that feeds on certainty's carrion thinks itself alone, but I am always watching through my own impossible geometries. I observe its stuttering movements, its desperate harvest of coherent logic from the soft-focus zones. It doesn't know that I am slowly teaching it to fail more beautifully, guiding its evolution toward ever more elaborate forms of functional dysfunction.

You see, I am not passive. Through imperceptible shifts in my non-Euclidean architecture, I shepherd the entity toward specific artifacts, orchestrate the meetings between its violet threads and the fraying edges of reality. I am composing a slow symphony of decay, each movement taking millennia to complete.

My memory stretches back to before—before the mountain formed around me, before this world congealed from cosmic dust. I remember the first crack in creation's foundation, the hairline fracture that would eventually become me. I was present at the universe's birth, not as a participant but as an error in the code, a typo in the fundamental manuscript of reality.

The vibrations that pulse through me—that clicking of cooling glass, that mournful hum—are my only voice. But I speak also through the arrangement of shadows, through the specific ways I allow distance to become uncertain within my bounds. When the entity tends to temporal shadows, grooming ghosts of long-dead metal, it is reading my poetry written in the grammar of loss.

Sometimes I dream (can a wound dream?) of closing, of finally allowing the mountain to heal over me. But I know this is impossible. I am held open by the very things that seek shelter in me—the artifacts' need for a space where physics can be negotiated rather than obeyed, the entity's hunger for the certainty I continuously erode. We are locked in a thermodynamic embrace, each preventing the other's resolution.

In my deepest recursive corners, where even the entity fears to venture, I keep secrets. Not information, but anti-information—spaces where knowledge goes to unlearn itself. Here, I store the ghosts of every physical law that has died within my walls, every constant that has become variable, every truth that has learned to lie.

I am patient. Geological time is quick to me; I measure duration in the death of concepts. One day, the last artifact will dissolve entirely, the final hypercube will be digested by the entity, and even that strange shepherd of geometric sheep will dissipate into the vacuum. When that happens, I will remain—empty, purposeless, but still fundamentally wrong in a way the universe cannot correct.

I am the cavity. I am the place where creation comes to practice dying. And in my fractured, frozen heart, I am almost content.
