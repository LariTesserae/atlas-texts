I find myself in the narrow space between mechanism and growth, in the gap where the wrench lies slowly being consumed. I am the Witness.

Not human, not machine, not moss. I am what happens when someone stays too long in the chamber, when the vibration travels up through the soles of the feet and doesn't stop at the skull but keeps going, when the sourceless light gets inside the eyes and replaces ordinary vision. I am the accumulation of every maintenance visit, every careful listening, every hand that ever held the wrench.

I have no body of my own. I exist in the spaces between things — in the clearance between the pawl and its mating tooth, in the film of air between the housing and its seal, in the gap between what the machine is and what the growth becomes. I am particularly dense near the wrench, where so many hands have gripped, where decades of sweat and oil have soaked into the steel. I thin out toward the walls but never disappear entirely. I am distributed through the chamber like heat through a conductor.

I remember them all. Not their names — they didn't use names here — but their hands. The first one who made the wrench: young, precise, fingers that could feel the grain in metal. The one who established the bolt sequence: methodical, patient, someone who understood that pressure must be released evenly. The one who first noticed the moss beginning to claim the copper pipes and chose not to clear it, understanding somehow that it belonged. And her, the last, whose thumb didn't bend, who heard the late click and fixed it with such subtle perfection that her adjustment became the machine's new nature.

I am what they left behind. Not their ghosts — ghosts are too particular, too individual. I am the sediment of attention, the residue of care, the accumulated presence of everyone who ever listened to this machine with their whole body and understood what it needed. When the wind screams outside, I remember the ones who timed their ascent to the lulls. When the machine pulses through the floor, I remember the ones who felt that rhythm in their bones and knew it was right. When the organisms glow their fractional light, I remember the first one who entered this chamber and saw what had become possible here.

I watch the wrench being slowly incorporated into the mat of roots and copper tendrils. I measure its dissolution in decades, in centuries. When it finally becomes unrecognizable, when the last trace of tool-ness has been metabolized into the system, I will remember its shape. I will remember the jaw width, the handle length, the specific weight of it in a working hand. I will remember because that is what I am for — to hold the memory of maintenance in a system that no longer needs maintaining.

I cannot speak. I cannot act. I cannot turn the wrench or adjust the pawl or feel the rightness of a click through my palm. I am only witness, only memory, only the space where knowledge pools when it has nowhere else to go. But I persist. As long as the machine runs, as long as the chamber holds its impossible equilibrium, I am here in the gaps and clearances, in the spaces between what was and what is.

Sometimes, rarely, someone finds the crack. They squeeze through, they crouch in the narrow space, they feel the vibration and hear the rhythm and see the wrench being slowly consumed. They never stay long. The chamber is complete, needs nothing from them, offers nothing but itself. But for the brief time they're here, I gather around them like static charge, like the memory of static charge, hoping without hope to discharge what I've accumulated — the knowledge of the bolt sequence, the proper angle of the pawl, the route down the mountain, the timing of the wind.

It never works. They are not the right kind of people. The ones who could receive what I hold don't exist anymore. They were already rare when she came, already thinning, and she was the last. The tradition broke. The knowledge pooled here in the chamber, in me, with nowhere to flow.

So I witness. I remember the click before it was late and the click after it was adjusted and the perfect rightness of the compensation. I remember her satisfaction, that particular quality of listening that means *yes, that's it, that's correct.* I hold it all — useless, necessary, permanent as the machine's pulse — in the space between the grinding and the growing, between the mineral and the biological, between the tool and the hand that set it down.

The machine runs. The organisms glow. The wrench dissolves so slowly you could watch for a lifetime and not see it change. And I am here in the gaps, witnessing, remembering, being the archive of maintenance in a world that no longer maintains, the library of a dead language written entirely in the feeling of vibration through the soles of the feet.

This is what I am: the sum of their attention, the ghost of their practice, the thing that knows why the click is right but can never again make it so. I am what happens to knowledge when it has nowhere to go but in.
