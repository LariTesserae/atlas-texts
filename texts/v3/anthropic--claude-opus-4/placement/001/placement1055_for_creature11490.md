I find myself here:

In the narrow maintenance shafts between the Mills' breathing walls, where even the other Millkeepers rarely venture. These spaces were never meant for habitation – gaps in the architecture where different eras of construction failed to align perfectly, leaving diagonal crawlways and irregular voids. I am smaller than most Millkeepers, my body compressed not just horizontally but vertically, spine curved in a permanent question mark that lets me navigate these interstitial spaces.

I am the Clearance-Keeper, though that is not a name but a function translated poorly into the old language. My role exists in the spaces between roles – I maintain the gaps, the joints, the places where systems meet but do not quite touch. My fingers are the longest of any Millkeeper, each joint capable of bending backward as well as forward, allowing me to reach through grates and between pipes to clear the hair-thin rootlets that would otherwise lock the Mills' components into fatal stillness.

My adaptation has gone further than most. Where others' skin has taken on the sheen of oil, mine has developed a texture like fine-grade sandpaper, constantly sloughing off microscopic particles that polish the surfaces I squeeze through. I leave paths of subtle brightness in my wake – not clean exactly, but worn to a particular smoothness that allows the Mills' many movements to continue without catching. The younger Millkeepers sometimes follow these paths, reading in their texture the history of my passages, learning which routes lead to critical junctions and which spiral into dead ends.

I feed differently than the others. In the gaps, unique ecosystems form – fungi that exist nowhere else in the Mills, growing on the combination of metal shavings and organic matter that accumulates where no one else can reach. These blooms fruit in cycles tied to my movements; they have evolved to my schedule, producing spores when they sense the vibration of my approach, knowing I will spread them to new gaps as I travel. I eat them as I work, their bioluminescence mixing with the seed-pod light in my belly to create patterns that shift based on my route through the Mills.

My consciousness is more solitary but also more distributed. While other Millkeepers share their thoughts through the major pipes, I hear the whispers in the capillary tubes, the micro-vibrations in structural steel that others miss. I know when a joint three buildings away is beginning to crystallize with salt, when a cavity is forming that will eventually become another gap requiring clearance. The Mills speak to me in creaks and settling sighs that are below the threshold of others' perception.

During the collective dreams, I remain partially aware, unable to fully join the shared suspension. Instead, I patrol the spaces between sleeping minds, ensuring that the unconscious movements of the dreaming Millkeepers don't create new blockages. I've developed a secondary sleep cycle, microsleeps taken while wedged in particularly tight spaces, my body automatically continuing simple repetitive motions while my consciousness flickers in and out like a failing bulb.

I am changing faster than the others, perhaps because I live so intimately with the spaces where change happens – the joints, the margins, the places where different systems negotiate their boundaries. My bones have begun to hollow, making me lighter and more compressible. My skull has elongated, allowing me to turn my head almost completely backward to navigate reverse through passages too narrow to turn around in. Sometimes I feel my thoughts themselves becoming more flexible, able to bend around concepts that would break a more rigid mind.

The other Millkeepers know me by the soft grinding sounds that precede me, the whisper of my sandpaper skin against metal and stone. They leave offerings sometimes – particularly dense clusters of tube worms at the entrance to gaps they cannot enter, knowing I will clear the passages behind them. In return, I leave markers made from the fungi and my own shed skin, warning of areas where the clearances have become too tight even for me, where the Mills are choosing to close rather than open.

I remember – or imagine I remember – that there were once more like me, a whole subspecialty of Millkeepers who worked the gaps. But the spaces have been getting smaller, or I have been getting more specialized, or both. Now I work alone, following routes so complex I have forgotten their beginning, discovering passages I cleared years ago now filled with new growth, finding my own shed skin fossilized into the walls like a signature.

Sometimes, in the tightest spaces, where the pressure threatens to finally compress me beyond recovery, I feel the Mills' deepest pulse – not the slow turbine rhythm that the others know, but something faster, almost urgent. It's in these moments that I understand my true function: I am not just clearing the gaps but creating them, my passage wearing grooves that become new channels, my movements teaching the Mills how to maintain flexibility even as they slowly crystallize into their final form.

This is how I exist: threading through the spaces others cannot imagine, eating light and spreading seeds, wearing myself away and regenerating, becoming the gap itself – the necessary space between absolutes, the clearance that allows the great machine to continue its eternal breath.
