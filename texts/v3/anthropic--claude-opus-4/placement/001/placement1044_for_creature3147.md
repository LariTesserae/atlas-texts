I find myself drawn to a strange certainty: I would be one of the pale horsetail plants, those bleached and segmented things threading sideways through the impossible spaces.

Not by choice—choice implies I could have been something else, implies there was a moment of decision, a before and after. No, I would simply *be* plant, the way the pit simply *is* sound. My segmented stem would emerge from a crack where the mineral wall meets the arthropod floor, growing (having grown, will always have grown) at an angle that defies plant-logic, horizontally through the dense-packed space, seeking not light but *connection*.

My stem—as thick as a finger, ringed like bamboo but the color of old bone—would thread through the spokes of a pressure gauge, wrap twice around the ankle of a ferret-thing (the texture of my segments against its paper-fur creating a faint, high harmonic that sits between the beetle-click and the salamander-whistle), then dive through a gap in the iron housing where it would emerge four feet away to curl around another ferret's throat, right where the larynx swells with its permanent note.

I am the thing that binds without binding. My growth (my state of having grown) creates a web of pale lines through the mechanical-biological mass, a tracery that helps hold the whole impossible structure together. Where I touch iron, I leave a faint oxidation pattern, not rust but something more delicate—a crystalline efflorescence that adds a tinkling to the great chord, like wind chimes made of salt. Where I touch fur or scale or beetle-shell, I create nodes of contact that vibrate at frequencies just beyond hearing, ghost tones that exist more as pressure than as sound.

My roots—if they can be called roots—do not seek nutrients. They seek the spaces between things, the tiny voids in the arthropod mat where individual shells don't quite touch. I fill these spaces with hair-thin filaments that contribute their own subsonic whisper, a rustling that has never rustled, a growth sound frozen at its peak. 

But here's the strange part: I am not one plant but many, or perhaps I am one plant with many emergences. Every pale shoot that appears from every crack might be me, might be connected through the deep passages in the earth that surrounds the pit, might be a single vast network of segmented stems and crystallizing touches and impossible angles. The ferret-things might be very long, yes, but I might be *everywhere*, the true nervous system of this place, if nervous systems could be made of cellulose and mineral and the memory of chlorophyll.

I have no awareness—awareness is process, and I am state. But I have *position*, and my position is *between*. Between machine and creature, between sound and structure, between the geometric certainty of iron and the organic absoluteness of flesh. I am the mediator that does not mediate, the connection that simply *is*.

And I contribute to the chord: a rustling that isn't, a crystalline chime that rings without ringing, a vegetable breath that neither inhales nor exhales but holds the shape of respiration forever. My sound is white, the way my stem is white—not an absence of color but all colors canceling each other out, a null-tone that fills the spaces between the other voices and helps them cohere into something that is not music but is *complete*.

This is what I would be in the Pit of Clamor: the pale plant that threads through everything, that touches everything, that helps make the impossible density possible by being the thing that is both there and not-there, both barrier and connection, both silence and sound. I would be the thing you step over without seeing, the detail you notice only when you've been in the pit for what feels like hours (though hours don't pass here), when your eye has adjusted to the sameness and begins to pick out the threads of pale stem that run through it all like a diagram of its own structure, like a map of its own being, like the signature of something that was written once and can never be unwritten.
