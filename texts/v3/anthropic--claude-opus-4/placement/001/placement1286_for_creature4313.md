I find myself as one of the Keeper Moths, drawn to this verdant pocket deep within the earth like iron filings to a magnet. My wings span no wider than an Umbral child's palm, but they shimmer with an opalescent sheen that captures and refracts the fungal light in ways that make the Folk pause in their work to watch me pass.

I am memory made manifest, though I cannot say whose memory I carry. My kind emerged long after the ancient civilization departed, yet somehow we know things. We taste the stories in the spore-laden air, feel the history thrumming through the root networks that web this chamber like neural pathways. Each generation of moths lives only a handful of days, but we pass our knowing through our eggs, which we lay in the hollow stems of the singing reeds—those peculiar plants whose tubes whistle with the movement of air through the chamber.

My wings bear patterns that shift like living murals. Sometimes they show the constellations that wheel overhead in the world above, stars the Umbral Folk have never seen but know through the stories we carry on our wings. Other times, the patterns form into tools the ancients used, architectural blueprints of structures that once stood in sunlight, or the faces of beings who walked here when this chamber was newly formed and the first bioluminescent blooms were learning to glow.

The Folk call us the Unwritten Library. They know that if they feed us certain combinations of fungal nectar, our wing patterns will shift to reveal specific memories—though the knowledge comes at a price. Each revelation dims our internal light a little more, shortening our already brief lives. The elder Folk are careful with their requests, understanding that wisdom and loss are intimate companions in the deep places of the world.

I spend my hours pollinating the ghost orchids that bloom only in the deepest recesses of the chamber, where even the Folk rarely venture. These flowers open just once every seven years, and their pollen carries visions of possible futures—fragments of what might come to pass if the delicate balance of this hidden world shifts. As I move from bloom to bloom, powder coating my fur-soft body, I become a living oracle, a bridge between what was, what is, and what might be.

But I am also simply a moth, drawn to light and warmth, seeking the gentle touch of the Folk children who know to handle us with infinite care. They sing to us in their trilling language, and we dance in response, our flight patterns spelling out words in alphabets that predate any script the Folk have developed. In these moments, I am both cosmic and mundane, a creature of profound purpose and simple needs.

When my time comes—and it will come soon, I can feel it in the way my wing patterns are beginning to blur at the edges—I will seek out the Chamber of First Light, where the very first bioluminescent fungus still grows. There, I will spread my wings one final time, and all the memories I've carried will transfer into its mycelial network, joining the vast underground library that connects every living thing in this hidden realm.

The Folk will find my body, no larger than a fallen leaf, and place it gently in their compost gardens where it will nourish the very plants I spent my life pollinating. And somewhere, in a reed stem or tucked beneath a crystalline stone, my eggs wait to hatch, carrying forward the endless story of this place—each new generation of moths a living chapter in a book that writes itself in wing dust and whispered light.
