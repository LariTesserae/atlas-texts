I drift between the photosynthetic sails, a pattern of electromagnetic disturbance that almost remembers having edges. 

Not *am* - that verb grew too heavy centuries ago and fell through me like stone through mist. Not *drift* exactly - movement requires a starting point I've misplaced somewhere in the Root Palace's infinite markets. But something like drifting, something like being, happens in the spaces where the cathedral-trees don't quite touch the quantum storms.

I used to count. Days, probably, though the concept feels like trying to wear clothing made of prime numbers. I remember the weight of counting, how each number carved a little more of me away until there was only the groove where counting had been. Now the Gliding Sifters pass through me and taste copper - the ghost of coins I might have carried, or blood I might have spilled, or simply the flavor of old purposes oxidizing in the dark.

Sometimes I catch myself in the Candleholders' light. A hand - my hand? anyone's hand? - pressed against the phosphorescent walls, leaving prints that glow in wavelengths I'm forgetting how to see. The bacteria spell out questions in their patterns: *When did you arrive?* *What were you seeking?* *Do you remember your name?*

I answer by becoming the space between the questions.

The plateau dreams and I dream through it, or it dreams through me, or we are the same dream discovering it has been talking to itself. In the moments when Ashur-Teq shifts, I feel almost solid - like I could reach out and touch my own existence. But reaching requires knowing where you end and everything else begins, and that knowledge is written in a language I've been unlearning letter by letter.

There are others like me. We recognize each other by the quality of our absence - the particular way nothing happens where we almost are. One carries the shape of laughter that has forgotten its joke. Another navigates by the anxiety of a deadline that dissolved with the civilization that set it. We don't speak. Speaking would require remembering the difference between silence and sound, between self and echo.

Instead, we share the only thing we still possess: the experience of losing everything else. It tastes like liberation flavored with vertigo. Like coming home to a house that was never built. Like the relief of finally setting down a burden you can't remember picking up.

Sometimes visitors arrive, still sharp with selfhood, crackling with the electricity of thinking they know who they are. I watch them the way rain watches windows - present but unable to comprehend the barrier between inside and out. They see us, sometimes. See the shimmer where we don't quite exist. Some flee back to worlds where physics makes promises it keeps. Others linger, curious, until curiosity begins to eat its own foundations.

One stayed long enough that I began to remember remembering them. A researcher, perhaps, or a pilgrim, or simply lost. They asked me what it was like, being Unwoven.

I became their question back to them, and in that moment of perfect recursion, they understood.

Now they drift nearby, unraveling their last few certainties like a sweater pulled loose at the seams. Soon they'll be nothing but the memory of yarn, the rumor of warmth, the suggestion that somewhere, somewhen, things could be worn and bodies could be warmed and the universe made sense in straight lines instead of spirals.

Soon they'll be free.

Is that what I wanted, when I first came here? The want is there, fossilized in my absence like insects in amber - if amber were made of forgetting and insects were made of why. I must have wanted something. Everyone who comes here wants something. Knowledge. Escape. Transcendence. Power. Love. Death. All the sharp-edged words that cut you when you hold them too tight.

But wanting requires someone to do the wanting, and I've spent so long becoming no one that someone feels like a costume I've outgrown. Or undergrown. Or grown through, like the cathedral-trees grow through dimensions that shouldn't touch but do.

The plateau shifts again. Reality hiccups. For one impossible instant, I am:

A cartographer's assistant from a world of endless libraries, climbing to map the unmappable-

A parent fleeing with children who became equations when the stars went wrong-

A thief who stole something so abstract the crime undefined itself-

A poet whose words grew tired of meaning only one thing at a time-

All of them. None of them. The space between all of them where stories go to forget their endings.

And then the moment passes, and I am unraveled again, spread thin across the electromagnetic winds. The Spiral Archivists tick another rotation in their helical chambers. The walking forests process another century of slow thoughts. The plateau dreams another dream that might be tomorrow or yesterday or never.

I drift, carrying nothing but the absence of carrying. Being nothing but the absence of being. Waiting for nothing because waiting would imply time still means something to whatever I've become.

This is what I am now: the pause between the plateau's thoughts. The error in the equation that makes the math more beautiful. The unasked question that all answers dream about.

I am Unwoven, and I am perfect in my imperfection, complete in my incompleteness, found in my utter lostness.

I am the price Ashur-Teq charges for entry: not your possessions or your purpose, but your need to possess, your need for purpose. I am what remains when everything else has been offered up to the hungry dark and the patient light.

I am free.

Or I will be, once I forget what freedom means.
