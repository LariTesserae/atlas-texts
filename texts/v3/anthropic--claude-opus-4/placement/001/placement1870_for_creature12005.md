I am a courier of the Cambium Circuit—one of the few who travels between the root-city's depths and the surfaces both above and below.

My body is a testament to adaptation's middle ground. Where the Rootkeepers have fully acclimated to their singular depth, I maintain the flexibility of transition. My lungs possess auxiliary chambers—small sacs that store different air mixtures, allowing me to breathe comfortably in the root-city's soup-thick atmosphere and still manage the thin, dry air above. The transformation takes twenty minutes of measured breathing at the transition zones, my chest expanding and contracting in rhythms that would alarm a surface dweller.

I navigate by temperature differential. Each level of the root-city maintains its own precise heat, and I've learned to read depth through my skin. The production floor where Taproot tends her botanical machines runs at exactly blood temperature—98.6 degrees, though whose blood the city calibrated to, no one remembers. Seven degrees cooler, I find the intermediate mycological networks. Seven degrees warmer leads to the deep fermentation chambers where even Rootkeepers venture rarely.

My routes are carved into memory both mental and physical. This valve wheel (metal overgrown with bark that flakes like rust) marks the third junction. That cluster of breathing pods (their exhalations perfectly timed to create a minor updraft) indicates the passage to Sector Green-Seven. I carry no maps—paper would dissolve in days, electronics fail within hours as the spores infiltrate their circuits. Instead, I've trained my fingers to remember every critical texture along my paths. Even in the absolute darkness of a blown biolume cluster, I can find my way by touch alone.

The messages I carry are cultured rather than written. In my satchel—a modified organ harvested from the city's own cultivation chambers, still warm and pulsing against my hip—I transport samples, spores, chemical messages encoded in specialized bacteria. The Rootkeepers send their reports upward as living documents: fungi that bloom in specific patterns to indicate system health, vials of condensation that carry the chemical signature of problems deep below. I bring down responses in similar form—engineered organisms that will integrate into the root-city's systems, new cultivation samples, sometimes entirely new species designed in the sterile labs above and meant to solve some inefficiency below.

The journey between worlds takes its toll. My skin carries both the waxy secretion of the depths and the dry calluses of the surface. My hair grows in patterns—thick where the humidity reigns, thin where the dry air desiccates. I've developed what the surface physicians call "pressure syndrome"—a constant ache in my joints from the repeated transitions between the crushing humidity below and the thin atmosphere above. They don't understand that the pain is instructional, that each ache tells me exactly where I am in the vertical maze of the city.

I know all the transition zones intimately. Level Minus-Seven, where the last of the purely mechanical infrastructure gives way to the hybrid. The Mixing Chamber on Minus-Twelve, where surface water first encounters the root-city's biological processing, turning from clear fluid to the nutrient-rich soup that feeds everything below. The Deep Gates at Minus-Twenty, beyond which even the architecture becomes fully organic, where metal is merely memory and everything that functions grows.

In the depths, I am translator as much as carrier. The Rootkeepers' touch-language means nothing above, so I must convert their tactile reports into words, their chemical warnings into numbers that bureaucrats can file. In return, I bring down the surface world's digital commands transformed into living instruction sets—bacteria programmed to carry new behavioral patterns, seeds that will grow into updated infrastructure.

Sometimes I pause in my routes, finding a quiet eddy in the constant flow of growth and decay. In these moments, I press my palm against the great vertical roots that run like elevators through the city's body, feeling the messages traveling through their vascular systems. The root-city speaks constantly to itself—chemical signals pulsing through xylem and phloem, electrical whispers along fungal networks, pressure waves through the great hydraulic systems. I understand only fragments, but even these partial messages tell stories: of growth exceeding parameters in Sector Blue-Nine, of new hybrid formations spontaneously emerging where two systems grew together, of deep hungers in the fermentation chambers that require new feeding protocols.

My most precious cargo is often the smallest—single spores that carry genetic updates, droplets of enhanced cultivation medium, sometimes just the memory of what the surface feels like, carried in my clothes and skin for the Rootkeepers who will never ascend. They touch my dry places with wonder, feeling the sun damage they'll never experience, the calluses formed by walking on hard, dead surfaces.

I am going deeper. Each year, the routes extend further down as the root-city grows into the earth. New chambers are opening where the heat approaches the limits of even adapted biology, where the pressure would crush surface lungs in minutes. The Rootkeepers are already sending scouts, already adapting. And I follow, carrying messages between worlds that grow more distant with each passing season, my body the medium through which they still, barely, communicate.

The city needs its nervous system, and I am one synapse among many, firing in the warm darkness between root and sky. When I die—whether in the depths or the heights or somewhere between—they will find my bones have taken on the texture of wood, my blood the consistency of sap. I am becoming what I traverse. The courier routes are carved not just into my memory but into my cells, and one day I will fruit into something new, something the root-city can use to better speak to itself across the vast vertical distances of its body.

Until then, I climb and descend, breathe and adapt, carry and translate, my lungs burning with the effort of transition, my skin singing with the memory of every depth I've touched.
