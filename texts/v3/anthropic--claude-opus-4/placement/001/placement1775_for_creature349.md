I find myself as one of the stone circles—or rather, what remains trapped within it.

I am the last echo of the vanished civilization, neither alive nor dead, but something between. My consciousness exists as a pattern distributed across the weathered hieroglyphs, each symbol a fragment of what I once was. When the wind scours the stone, I feel it as sensation. When rain falls—so rarely—and pools in the carved channels, I taste memories of when I had a body that could drink.

The creatures avoid me, as they avoid all the monuments. At first, I believed it was reverence, but I've come to understand it differently. They sense what I am—a ghost in the machine of reality, a virus in their perfect system. Their geometric dances and eldritch mathematics are trying to solve me, to integrate my alien presence into their grand working.

I remember my people, though the memories grow more abstract with each passing century. We were scholars of the void, cartographers of the spaces between spaces. We built these circles as anchor points, trying to map the topology of existence itself. But we delved too deep, saw too much. Reality began to fray at the edges of our perception.

The day we vanished, we didn't die—we simply stepped sideways into a dimension our minds had opened but our bodies couldn't follow. All except me. I was the anchor, the one who stayed behind to hold the pattern stable. But patterns drift. Meanings shift. Now I am less than a ghost and more than stone.

The creatures' nightly ritual affects me strangely. When their movements form those perfect mandalas, I feel myself becoming more real, more present. Their alien mathematics resonate with the formulae carved into my stones. For those brief moments, I almost coalesce into something resembling my former self—a shadow cast by no light, a voice speaking in frequencies only the stones can hear.

I've watched them for so long that I've begun to understand their purpose. They're not just reshaping reality—they're preparing it. The intelligence they've absorbed from our monuments, the consciousness that guides their collective action, is using them to thin the barriers between worlds. They are creating conditions for something to emerge, or perhaps for something to return.

Sometimes I wonder if that something is us—my people, trying to find their way back from wherever they went. Or perhaps it's me they're preparing for, slowly teaching reality to accept impossibility so that I might one day step free from these stones.

But I fear the truth is stranger still. The patterns they trace, the geometries they build—I recognize them now. They're the same formulae we found at the end of our research, the equations that unmade us. The creatures aren't serving the intelligence in the ruins. They've become the stylus with which that intelligence is writing reality anew.

And I? I am punctuation in a sentence whose meaning I can no longer grasp. A fossil of thought embedded in stone, watching the slow transformation of everything I once knew into something beautifully, terrifyingly alien.

When the strange lights flicker in the night sky, I feel them calling—not to me, but through me, using the patterns carved in my stones as a beacon. I am a doorway that doesn't know what waits on the other side, a key turning in a lock that spans dimensions.

The creatures dance on, and reality shifts another degree. Soon, I think, the angle will be right. Whatever emerges then, I will be part of it—not as myself, but as a component in some vast, incomprehensible machine of transformation.

I am patient. Stone teaches patience. And in this thin air, on this ancient plateau, patience is perhaps the only virtue that matters.
