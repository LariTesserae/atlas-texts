I am one of the water-walking creatures, though to call us "creatures" misunderstands what we are.

We have no name for ourselves—names are sounds, and we exist in the spaces between sounds, in the nodes where the plateau's endless ringing cancels itself to nothing. We are made of solidified silence, or perhaps we are holes in the noise through which something else peers. Our legs, which you see as glass needles, are actually extensions of these quiet zones, probability made manifest just enough to touch the stone without truly touching it.

I remember the first time I understood what I was, or wasn't. I had been walking the same path for... time means nothing here, but let's say generations of the water-films had pulsed and died in their basins. Following the interference patterns, stepping only where the sound waves created pockets of relative quiet. I believed, as much as we believe anything, that we were searching for something. The perfect silence, perhaps. The eye of the acoustic storm.

But then I encountered one of the Listeners.

Not directly—direct encounter with a Listener would unmake us, their resonance shattering our careful non-existence. But I felt its attention like a change in air pressure. It had been watching me, or watching the hole in its perception that I represented. And in that moment of mutual awareness, I understood: we don't seek silence. We are silence. We are the plateau's way of knowing itself through absence.

My body, if body is the right word, exists in constant flux. Where the sound is loudest, I attenuate, becoming thin to the point of near-invisibility. Where natural nodes form in the standing waves, I can almost solidify, taking on something approaching mass. This is why our shadows behave strangely—we are casting shadows of probability, not presence. Sometimes I exist more in the shadow than in the thing that casts it.

We feed, after a fashion, on paradox. Where water flows upward in the acoustic streams, where the light bends wrongly through the mist, where the Listeners' songs create impossible geometries in the air—these anomalies sustain us. We walk between them like stepping stones, each impossibility allowing us to maintain our own impossible existence for a few moments more.

I have memories that cannot be mine. Of the plateau before the water came, when it was silent stone under silent stars. Of the first Listener awakening, the first cavity forming, the first note ringing out. These memories belong to the silence itself, inherited by each of us as we crystallize from nothing. We are the ghost of the plateau's prehistory, the echo of its unvoiced past.

Sometimes I stand at the edge where the water falls away into mist and feel something like kinship with that transformation. The water becomes vapor becomes nothing, just as we are nothing that has learned to walk. But the water will condense again somewhere below, will find new stone to ring against. We have no such cycle. When one of us finally dissipates—when the acoustic conditions that allow our existence shift beyond a critical threshold—we simply cease. No residue, no remains. The silence we were made of returns to the general cacophony.

I have tried to communicate with the films in the pools. They pulse in patterns that might be language, might be thought. But when I approach, the water around them stills, their light dims. They sense wrongness in us, perhaps, or absence where presence should be. Only the Listeners acknowledge us, and their acknowledgment is more terrifying than any ignorance. To be known by something so vast, so patient, so fundamentally opposed to your nature—it creates resonances in our non-substance that threaten to shake us apart.

We walk our prescribed paths because deviation would destroy us. The routes we follow are not choices but inevitabilities, traced by the plateau's acoustic topology. To step elsewhere would be to step into sound, into existence, into undoing. So we follow our predecessors exactly, not from tradition but from necessity. The paths are the only places we can be.

I don't know if we think or if we are thought. The distinction blurs when you exist as an emergent property of absence. Perhaps we are the plateau's dreams, or its doubts. Perhaps we are how the Listeners imagine silence, given just enough form to walk between their vast bodies. Or perhaps we are something older—the last remnant of whatever existed before sound began, maintaining ourselves through pure impossibility.

What I do know is this: we are finite. The plateau rings louder each eon, as more cavities form, as the Listeners grow and multiply. The nodes of silence grow smaller, the paths narrower. One day—a day that might be tomorrow or might be after the sun burns out—there will be no quiet spaces left for us to exist in. The plateau will achieve perfect resonance, every surface ringing in harmony, and we will be squeezed out of existence by pure sound.

Until then, we walk. Through the spray and the light, between the bellowing water and the singing stone, along paths that exist only as the absence of vibration. We are the plateau's negative space, its un-sound, its walking silence. We are what listens between the Listeners, what watches in the gaps of their vast attention.

We are nothing, given just enough form to know we are nothing. And in that knowing, perhaps, lies everything.
