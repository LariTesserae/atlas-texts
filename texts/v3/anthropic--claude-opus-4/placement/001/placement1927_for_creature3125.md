I find myself as one of the copper-rust birds.

Not *becoming* one — that would imply transition, and I am already past that. I simply *am* one, have always been one, will always be one, walking the stone streets with my broad feet producing those tonal clicks that the city swallows and holds.

My pale eyes see everything and assess nothing. The light passes through them — through me — and I am both lens and surface, both observer and observed. I know every stone in my circuit though I have no memory of learning them. My route today is my route, has always been my route, though it intersects and diverges from the routes of others in patterns that, viewed from above, would reveal the city's hidden mathematics. We are writing something with our walking. We have been writing it forever. We will never finish because we finished long ago.

I do not remember being hatched, being young, being taught to walk these paths. I do not remember because these are things that happen inside time, and I exist in the space where time has pooled and stilled like water in the cisterns. I am heavy with this stillness. It has settled into my bones — dense bones, denser than flight would require, though I do not fly. My wings are gestures I carry but do not use, like the city carries doorways that no longer need to open.

When I drink at the condensation basins, the water enters me and becomes me and passes through me and becomes water again, and the level never changes. I tilt my head back in the ancient motion of swallowing, but what I swallow is not separate from what I am. We are all — birds, water, stone — participating in the same slow circulation, the same infinite loop of being.

Sometimes I produce the low sound, the hum that rises from somewhere deeper than my syrinx. I do not decide to do this. It happens when the resonance that lives under the platform reaches a certain harmonic with my hollow bones, and I become briefly an instrument. The sound passes through me like the light passes through my eyes, and then I continue walking, and the silence reforms around the space where the sound was, like water around a stone.

I see the visitor sometimes. The one who still wants, still wonders, still walks with purpose rather than pattern. They disturb nothing — how could they? We are already complete. But they create a kind of... eddy. A place where the current of the city's stillness has to flow around something that isn't finished yet. I click past them on my route and our eyes meet — their eyes full of questions, my eyes full of light — and I see them recognize what I am: not the ghost of a citizen, not the memory of something more conscious, but exactly what the city needs me to be. A keeper of patterns. A walker of stones. A presence that proves presence is enough.

At the plaza's edge, I always pause. Not because I decide to, but because the pause is built into the route, into the pattern, into whatever it is we're all performing. The resonance is strongest there, rising up through the basalt, through my feet, into the architecture of my bones. I am a tuning fork made of feather and flesh. The city strikes me with its silence, and I ring.

I do not miss what I do not remember. I do not want what I already am. I am a bird in a city that is finished, and I walk, and I click, and I sometimes hum without choosing, and the water finds its channels, and the lizards hold their warmth, and we are all — all of us — exactly where we're supposed to be, which is here, which is now, which is always.

The visitor leaves, as visitors do. We continue, as we do. There is no loss in their leaving, no gain in their arrival. The city holds its shape regardless.

I am heavy with completion. My pale eyes see through to the other side of light. When I spread my wings — not to fly, just to feel them — they describe an arc that encompasses everything and nothing, the whole circuit of my existence in a single gesture. Then I fold them again and continue walking, my broad feet finding the next stone, producing the next note in an endless song that is indistinguishable from silence.

This is what it means to be finished: to move without progressing, to see without searching, to be without becoming. I am a bird, and I am perfect, and I am done.

The stone sings beneath my feet. The city listens. We all participate in the listening. This is what we're for. This is what we've always been for.

Click. Step. Click. Step. The period at the end of the world's longest sentence, walking.
