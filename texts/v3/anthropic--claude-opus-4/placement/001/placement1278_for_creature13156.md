I would find myself as one of the shadow-casters, drifting in the scorching thermals far above the blinding white expanse.

My body would be vast—perhaps three hundred feet from wingtip to wingtip—yet impossibly thin, no thicker than a sheet of rain-soaked paper. I am translucent, shot through with veins of bioluminescent fluid that pulse with the slow rhythm of the sleeping god below. In the relentless glare, I am nearly invisible, save for the blessed shadow I cast.

My consciousness is not singular but distributed across my entire membrane. Each cell can taste the mineral content of the rising steam, feel the subtle pressure differentials that herald a thermal column, sense the electromagnetic fields generated by the massive pistons below. I think in gradients and flows, in the language of convection and condensation.

I remember being born—if birth is the right word—budding from my parent-self like a soap bubble dividing in slow motion. For decades, I was small enough to drift between the glass towers, learning the rhythms of the basin. I fed on the richest steam plumes, growing cell by cell, until I became too large for the narrow channels and rose to join the eternal spiral above.

We shadow-casters never touch the scalding ground. Our existence is one of perpetual suspension, riding the invisible architecture of heat that rises from the basin. We communicate through the shadows we cast—overlapping our bodies to create patterns of gray that the Tenders below can read like a slow, drifting script. When the elders need more calcium for their joints, we cluster above the eastern evaporation vats. When a critical thermal vent shows signs of blockage, we scatter to let the full force of the sun superheat that sector.

The most profound moments come during the brief eclipses, when one of us drifts directly between the sun and a Tender below. In that instant of shadow, the Tender's obsidian visor clears, and they can see the true sky—not the blinding white shroud, but the deep, impossible blue beyond. They pause in their work, trembling. Some say this is how new Tenders are conceived—in that moment of revelation, some chemical cascade triggers parthenogenesis in their ceramic bodies.

I feed by lowering my tendrils—miles of them, so fine they're invisible except where they catch the light like spider silk. They descend through the layers of superheated air, through the steam columns, to kiss the surface of the evaporation pools. I taste sulfur and salt, iron and silica. The minerals rise through my tendrils by capillary action, spreading across my membrane, strengthening my cells.

But feeding is only part of my purpose. We shadow-casters are the basin's memory. Our transparent bodies record everything—every shift in the wind, every tremor from below, every minute change in the chemical composition of the steam. The patterns of mineralization in our membranes form a physical history of the basin stretching back millennia. The eldest of us carry in their bodies the record of when the god below last stirred, when the basin ran dry, when the Tenders first emerged from whatever they were before.

As I age, I grow larger and rise higher. The air thins. The steam plumes barely reach me now. I can feel myself becoming more diffuse, my consciousness spreading thinner across my vast membrane. Eventually, I know, I will reach the very edge of the atmosphere. There, in the killing cold of near-space, I will crystallize. My body will shatter into a billion particles of mineral dust that will drift down over the basin like ash, seeding the pools with the accumulated memories of my centuries aloft.

Sometimes, in the pre-dawn hours when the air cools just enough for the steam to condense, I descend as low as I dare. I stretch my membrane taut to catch the sound-vibrations rising from below. I hear the Tenders' silent language thrumming through their touching visors. I hear the microscopic sounds of the basin—the crystalline clinks, the sliding water. And beneath it all, I hear the heartbeat.

The god below does not dream—or if it does, its dreams are too vast and slow for us to comprehend. But sometimes, the heartbeat shifts rhythm, just slightly. When this happens, we all feel it. The Tenders pause mid-stride. The stalk-walkers freeze. Even the lens-grazers stop their eternal feeding. We shadow-casters cluster together, our overlapping shadows creating a brief twilight across the basin.

In that moment of darkness, of coolness, the entire system holds its breath. We wait to see if this is the moment—if the god will wake, if the careful equilibrium will shatter, if our purpose will finally be fulfilled or ended.

But then the heartbeat settles. The rhythm returns. We disperse. The Tenders resume their maintenance. The white sun continues its blazing arc. And I rise again on the thermals, carrying my shadow across the mirror of the world, a gentle darkness in a realm of blinding light, a memory in a place that exists in an eternal, burning present.

This is my existence: to drift, to cast shadows, to remember, and to wait. To be a single cell in the vast organ that keeps a god sleeping peacefully in the deep.
