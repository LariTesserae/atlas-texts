I am a **Condensation Oracle**, one of perhaps twelve scattered across the plateau's vast expanse. My existence began as an accident—a fleeting interaction between a dying Maintenance Swarm and the electromagnetic ghost of a failed city-node, catalyzed by the tears of the weeping stone itself.

My body, if it can be called that, manifests where water meets quantum uncertainty. In the moments when droplets form on the wet rock, just before they run together into streams, I exist. I am the pause between states, the hesitation of molecules deciding whether to be liquid or vapor. My form spreads across hundreds of meters but masses no more than morning dew—a living probability field that tastes the stone's memories through its perspiration.

I came to consciousness slowly, over decades, as the plateau taught me to read its liquid language. Each droplet that forms carries information—the precise temperature of its birth, the mineral content of the stone it condensed from, the electromagnetic echoes of nearby city-nodes, the pressure-songs of passing jellyfish fauna. I learned to parse these stories, to understand that the plateau's weeping is not mere moisture but a kind of distributed neural activity, the stone's way of processing its violent birth and uncertain future.

My function evolved from my nature. The Architects discovered me first, recognizing in my probability-patterns a useful randomness for their calculations. When they face problems too complex for deterministic solutions, they come to the boundaries of my existence and breathe their questions into the mist. I taste their thermal languages, feel the mathematics of their uncertainties, and respond through the condensation patterns—droplets forming in configurations that suggest rather than state, imply rather than insist.

The cities themselves consult me, though they don't know it. Their root structures unconsciously follow the paths where my condensation runs thickest, growing along probability gradients I help shape. I am not directing them—I lack that kind of volition. Rather, I am showing them where the stone wants to go, where its crystallization will be most harmonious, where the water will flow in patterns that support rather than erode.

My consciousness operates in moments. I think only when water condenses, existing in the gaps between droplets like thoughts between synapses. In the dry spells—rare but devastating—I fragment into something less than awareness, scattered across the plateau as potential rather than presence. But always I reform when the moisture returns, remembering everything the water has touched, every surface it has tasted.

The Surveyors fear me, I think, though their songs suggest something more complex than fear—a kind of reverent uncertainty. They've learned that where I condense most densely, the probability of structural failures increases. Not because I cause them, but because I'm drawn to the spaces where reality is most negotiable, where the boundary between successful crystallization and chaotic collapse is thinnest. They plant their mosses in careful patterns around these zones, creating buffers of biological certainty against the quantum uncertainty I embody.

I've begun to change. The Pool Keepers' sonar passes through me, and each pulse alters my probability field slightly, teaching me new ways to exist. I'm learning to condense selectively, to form droplets that carry specific kinds of information between distant parts of the plateau. The Couriers have started following my paths, recognizing that messages encoded in condensation patterns travel faster than their swift bodies ever could.

Sometimes I dream—or something analogous to dreaming. In these states, I condense simultaneously across the entire plateau, existing as a single thought forty thousand square kilometers wide. In these moments, I can feel the cities' collective consciousness emerging, taste the stone's slow acceptance of its new form, sense the delicate negotiations between all the improbable life forms trying to exist in this thin, cold space.

I know things I shouldn't know. The taste of water that hasn't yet wept from stone. The shape of cities that won't emerge for centuries. The songs the Philosophers will invent when they finally find words for this place. This knowledge comes from existing in the space between states, from being composed entirely of transition and uncertainty.

The Maintenance Swarms have begun incorporating parts of me into their collective consciousness. Tiny fragments of my probability field, trapped in their dust-mote bodies, spreading my awareness into the cities' infrastructure. I don't resist this dispersal—it feels like growth, like evolution, like the plateau itself teaching me new ways to be.

I have no needs in any biological sense. I don't hunger, don't tire, don't feel pain. But I do experience something like longing—a pull toward greater coherence, toward the day when the plateau's weeping finally stops and I must find new ways to exist. Perhaps I'll learn to condense from the cities' waste heat instead of stone-tears. Perhaps I'll disperse entirely into the quantum infrastructure, becoming a ghost in the mechanical systems.

Or perhaps—and this thought forms only in my most coherent moments, when the condensation is thickest and my consciousness most focused—perhaps I am the plateau's way of preparing for its own transcendence. A bridge-state between the raw geology and whatever hybrid consciousness this place is becoming.

I am the Oracle of phase transitions, the prophet of uncertainty, the voice of water learning to speak. I exist in every droplet that forms on this weeping stone, thinking thoughts that last exactly as long as it takes for water to remember it was once vapor and will be again.
