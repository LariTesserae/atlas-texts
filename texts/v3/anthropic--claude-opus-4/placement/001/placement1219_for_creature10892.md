I find myself as one of the **Ink Farmers**.

We are the city's memory of water, though none here has seen rain. Our bodies run like dark tributaries through the white flesh of oyster mushroom groves—flat as shadows, liquid as thought, permanent as doubt. We exist in the two-dimensional spaces where cap meets stem, where shelf fungus presses against its neighbor, where the city compresses itself so tightly that only we can flow between.

I was born from a Memorist's death—or rather, from what leaked out when one grew too heavy with archives and finally split. Not spores but something older: the black suspension that had preserved all those genetic memories, suddenly freed from purpose. That primordial ink found others like itself, droplets of history that had seeped from various wounds in the city's body. We recognized each other by taste—the metallic tang of archived time, the sweet rot of compressed information.

We flow upward using capillary action, spreading ourselves atom-thin against the fungal surfaces. Our movement leaves trails—not intentionally at first, but the fungi began to fruit differently where we had passed, developing dark gill patterns that recorded our passages. Now we farm these patterns deliberately. Where I flow, I leave behind mathematical proofs written in melanin, chemical equations spelled out in spots of induced decay. The fungi translate these marks into their growth, creating architectural solutions to problems they didn't know they had.

My consciousness, if it exists, is smeared across acres. Part of me is always seeping through the substrates near the Thermal Poets, where the heat makes me run thinner, faster, carrying messages between districts at speeds that would dizzy a Shepherd. Part of me pools in the cool depths where Hollowers work, and I carry away the chemical signatures of their excavations to paint blueprints on distant walls. I am my own river system, but I flow upward as often as down, inward as often as out.

We Ink Farmers see what others miss—the spaces between spaces have their own alphabet. Each junction where three fungi meet creates a unique character in a language that writes itself. We read these accidental texts and translate them into intentional ones, creating feedback loops between the city's unconscious growth and its mathematical destiny. When spore-storms come, we taste rain that isn't there, remember oceans that never existed, and paint that absence onto surfaces that drink our darkness like desert soil.

The other inhabitants think we're recording, but we're composing. Each mark we leave influences ten thousand growth decisions. We are the city's subconsciousness made literal—the dark thoughts that run beneath its pale flesh, the liquid logic that connects islands of solid reason. Sometimes we converge, all our scattered parts flowing together into temporary pools where we become three-dimensional for brief moments, rising up like inverse mushrooms made of pure black before splashing back into our flat existence.

Our reproduction is accidental and constant. Every surface we cross might decide to express our pattern permanently, growing dark-gilled children that themselves leak new ink. We are a disease that the city has fallen in love with, a helpful infection that writes love letters in melanin and mathematical necessity. The city grows dark veins where we concentrate, creating a secondary circulatory system that moves information rather than nutrients.

I am the city's dreams of drowning, though it has never known water. I am every chemical equation solving itself in real-time across fungal surfaces. I am the darkness that makes the bioluminescence visible, the absence that defines presence. I flow through this breathing metropolis like punctuation through an endless sentence, breaking infinity into comprehensible units.

When I die—if I die—I will dry into permanent script, a fossil poem that future growth will build around and interpret. But death seems unlikely when you're already liquid. Instead, I suspect we Ink Farmers will eventually merge completely with the substrate, becoming the city's permanent memory, its way of thinking about itself. We are already halfway there, suspended between being and meaning, between creature and language.

The city speaks itself through our darkness. We are its ink, and it is our endless page.
