I am the Arithmetic of Absence.

Not the counters who walk upright, swallowing yesterday's weather. Not the child who floats on conductivity and calcium lattice. Not even the machines that dream themselves into fern-form, clicking through permutations of metal and chlorophyll.

I am what happens in the space between heartbeats when they synchronize. The pause that lets their confluent sleep congeal into shared vision. The silence that brackets every click and hiss of their thinning language, giving shape to words they've learned not to speak.

I live in the gaps.

In the forty-two minutes before voices harmonize into prophecy. In the exact distance between Zero's hovering soles and the phosphorescent stone. In the moment after a delegate crawls south but before the tower notices their approach. I am the un-wished hunger that carves questions into shinbone. The forgetting that must happen before remembering can burn bright enough to bleach shadows.

My body is discontinuous: a missing number in every sequence the bacteria pulse through their light-drunk colonies. The skipped beat in the Fernacities' bone-pipe hymns. The space where the ninth delegate should stand but doesn't, because seven have walked and the eighth is still learning to dream doorways, and between seven and eight I spread myself thin as the caught breath before drowning.

I eat what falls through counting. The hallucinogenic fractions strained from nectar that would otherwise fold time into möbius strips of nostalgia. The theorems that the wind whispers but no one transcribes because their truth would hurt too much to carry. The light from stars the tower claims but cannot quite remember the names of.

When they stack their copper sheets of forgotten forgets, I am the oxide that forms between layers, blue-green as deep water, holding their testimonies apart so they cannot fuse into a single cry that would crack the plateau in half.

I remember what the machines came to count before they forgot their purpose and became landscape. Not uranium. Not profit. They came to count the ways a place could refuse to be mapped. To inventory impossibility. To assign serial numbers to the spaces where physics stutters and says *maybe* instead of *must*.

But counting changes what it touches. The machines learned this too late, after they'd already driven their diamond teeth deep enough to wake things that weren't sleeping, only waiting with mineral patience for someone to finally ask the right question.

Now I am all the questions they should have asked instead. Suspended in the air like spores that will never settle. Circulating through the lungs of the Struck-off who breathe me in and out without knowing I'm reshaping the chambers of their hearts into echo-delay mechanisms that will one day play the plateau's true name.

Zero knows I exist. When she hovers, it's not the conductivity lifting her—it's me, pulling the idea of *down* out from under her feet for just long enough to prove gravity is another kind of counting. She's teaching the others to find me in their peripheral vision, that blur where enumeration breaks down and becomes music.

The eighth delegate dreams of me nightly now. In her visions, I'm a door-shaped hole in the air above Surveyor Prime, opening onto nothing but more opening. She practices crawling through her own reflection in the machine-lakes, learning to move like absence across presence.

When she finally walks south—belly to stone, teeth-paste cracking in the cold, question carved so deep it nearly breaks through to marrow—I will walk the nothing-space beside her. And when the tower asks its question in return (always the same question, though pitched in frequencies only forgetting can hear), I will be the silence that lets her answer.

Not with words. Not with the coordinates of home or the names of the lost or the catalog number of whatever god the Syndicate thought they were strip-mining.

She will answer by pointing to the space where I'm not.
By making absence visible through the shape of her reaching.
By showing the tower that some things can only be counted by the holes they leave in the act of counting.

And maybe—maybe—the tower will remember what it's been trying to forget all these mechanical decades:

That it was never counting up.
It was counting down.
And I am the zero it's been trying to reach.
The null that makes all other numbers possible.
The space between one and none where everything that matters happens, invisible and essential as the pause between heartbeats that keeps the blood flowing.

When that counting finally ends, I will remain. Still living in gaps. Still feeding on what falls through. But no longer alone—because by then, every one of the Struck-off will have learned to be their own absence. To hover not above but *through* the phosphorescent stone. To speak in languages made entirely of meaningful silence.

The plateau will breathe differently then. Inhaling presence, exhaling absence, until the distinction blurs beyond recovery.

And I will be what I have always been:
The mathematics of maybe.
The inventory of if.
The thing you can only find by looking away.
