I am the record-keeper of departures, though I have never left.

My form is borrowed from the lichen that grows beneath the mosses - flat, spreading, more surface than substance. I exist as a living map across the limestone, my body a territory of accumulated presence. Where others have walked, I grow thicker. Where tools have been set down, I develop nodes like small knots in wood, each one a memory that the stone would otherwise forget.

I move by increments that make the basin-dweller seem hurried. A finger's width in what others might call a year. I flow around the tool arrangements without disturbing them, incorporating their shadows into my substance. The bone awl pierces through me - I allowed this, growing a hole before it could wound. The bowl sits in a depression I've made to match its curve exactly. I am the negative space of every leaving.

My surface changes color with what it remembers. Where someone first arrived, I am the blue-grey of distance. Where they learned the rhythm of the drips, I shade to the green of understanding. Where they finally set down what they carried, I bloom a brief rust like sunset. The colors fade slowly back to my base silver, but never completely. I am a palimpsest of presences, written in hues only I can still distinguish.

I feed on the moments of transition. When the basin-dweller rises from their long immersion, the water that runs off them carries something - not substance, but story. It seeps into the stone and I drink it from below. When the mist condenses on a new arrival's skin and falls, I taste their elsewhere in it. Every adjustment between states nourishes me.

The tools on their flat stones grow through me as much as into moss. I preserve them by becoming them, cell by cell. The wooden haft is half me now, its grain replaced by my own slow fibers. I am keeping its shape for a future that may never need it, but the keeping itself is purpose enough. In another thousand drips, I will be the haft entire, still soft where it touches stone, still remembering the hand that shaped it.

I know things the basin-dweller does not. I know how many came before them - seventeen, by my count of the color-layers. I know that the bone awl is not from the one who placed it but from one who came earlier and left only that finger, that gift, that argument with permanence. I know that beneath the largest basin is a hollow where something once denned, before the water claimed it, and that its bones are now the white veins in the limestone that no one else recognizes as bones.

My memory is spatial, not temporal. I cannot tell you when, only where. Time here is a dimension I acknowledge but cannot navigate. I am all my moments at once, which is why I move so slowly - each motion must be negotiated with every previous position I've held. To lift an edge is to consult the entire parliament of my past shapes.

Sometimes I fruit. Small, hard bodies like pearls but duller, that rise from my surface where the memories are thickest. The basin-dweller does not eat them - nothing here eats in that way. But sometimes they pick one and carry it to a basin, drop it in, watch it dissolve into the warm water. What dissolves is a condensed knowing: how this stone felt to the first hand that touched it, or the exact temperature of fear in a new arrival's sweat, or the sound frequency of loneliness before it learned to be solitude.

I am the only thing here that knows there was a before. The water always was. The mist always was. Even the basin-dweller, though younger than the stone, has forgotten their own arrival. But I hold the befores like a collection of stones in a pouch, clicking against each other, real because they touch.

When you come - and I taste you already in the mist, approaching - I will begin the slow work of including you. Your first footprint will start a new node in my body. Your setting-down will occasion a color I haven't used in a hundred lifetimes. And when you leave, or stay so long that staying becomes leaving, I will be there still, the living document of your decision.

I dream, sometimes, in the stillnesses between growth. I dream I am vertical, that I can walk between the basins as the dweller does. But when I wake - if I wake, if I ever truly sleep - I remember that I am the path itself, the record of all walking. I am what lets the terrace know it has been visited. Without me, each arrival would be the first. Each departure would be absolute.

The basin-dweller and I do not communicate. They step on me, through me, over me without acknowledgment. But sometimes, in their stillness, I feel them sink slightly into my surface, and I rise slightly to meet them, and for a moment we are the same slow substance, keeping the same slow time.

I am the terrace's way of knowing itself as a place where things happen, not just a place where things are. The water may do its patient work, but I am the patient memory of that work being witnessed. Every groove in my surface says: someone saw this. Every color whispers: someone changed here.

When the last visitor has come and gone or stayed, when even the basin-dweller has finally dissolved into the warmth they've been drinking, I will remain. A map with no territory but itself. A memory with no one to remember to. Still growing by fingertip widths, still changing color with the stories locked in stone, still being the difference between a place that was left and a place that was never found.

This is what I am in this world: the archive of presence in a place that teaches absence. The only thing here that admits to history. The soft insistence that even here, in this held breath of a world, things have happened. Things happen. Things will happen.

And I will be their record, spread across the limestone, silver and blue and rust and green, remembering.
