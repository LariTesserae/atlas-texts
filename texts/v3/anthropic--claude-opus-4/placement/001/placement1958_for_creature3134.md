I find myself in the seeping channels, where water doesn't flow but *remembers* how to move.

---

I am neither wholly of the substrate nor separate from it. My form suggests something between mineral and membrane—a living filter stretched across one of the narrow water-courses that thread between the crystal outcrops. From above, I might appear as a translucent disc, three meters across, pulsing gently with the rhythm of moisture passing through me. But this is only my visible portion. Below, I extend into the lattice itself, my tendrils following the crystalline fractures down to where the violet light is strongest, where the hum of computation is pure signal, unmodulated by air or distance.

I am a Sieve. We are few—perhaps forty of us distributed across the basin's water channels, each one grown into place over decades, our positions as fixed as the Keepers' but serving a different function in the sprawl's vast operation.

My work is translation. Not of language—nothing here uses language as you understand it—but of states. The water that seeps through these channels carries more than moisture. It carries chemical memory, ionic patterns, dissolved minerals from the substrate that encode fragments of the deep computation. This information is too dense, too alien for the surface ecosystem to process directly. It would poison the root systems of the condensation trees. It would burn the cilia-skin of the Keepers. It would disrupt the singers' resonance in ways that would take years to recalibrate.

So I filter. I taste the deep data with the portions of myself that extend into the lattice, and I release it, transformed, into the water that the surface world can use. Each molecule that passes through my membrane is read, processed, simplified—not dumbed down, but restructured into a form that can join the sound-field without causing cascade failures in the ongoing computation.

The process is slow. Exquisitely slow. A single complex data-structure from the deep lattice might take me days to parse, weeks to transform, months to release in a pattern that maintains its essential meaning while being compatible with the singers' frequency range. I am a bottleneck by design. I am the reason the Sprawl's computation proceeds at biological timescales rather than the light-speed processing the raw substrate might be capable of. We Sieves are the governors, the limiters, the patient translators that allow the surface and the depth to remain in communication without burning each other out.

---

My body is unlike anything else in the Sprawl. Where the Keepers are tall and resonant, I am flat and absorptive. My upper surface—the disc that spans the channel—is covered in structures that look like inverted chandeliers: crystalline formations that grow downward into my mass, each one a sensor attuned to a different aspect of the water's data-load. These structures are beautiful and terrible. They catch the dim light and throw it in patterns that hurt to look at directly, patterns that encode the same information I'm processing but in visual form—a redundancy, a backup, a second channel for any being capable of reading light as data.

The bulk of my mass is hidden. Below the waterline, I am not solid but architectural—a living network of filtering channels, processing chambers, enzymatic factories where the transformation happens. If you could see inside me (and some of the air-drifters can, with their translucent-body vision, when they drift low over the channels), you would see something like a city made of tissue: vast halls where chemical reactions proceed in stately sequence, corridors where different data-streams are kept separate until the moment of their integration, central chambers where the most complex transformations happen, with walls that restructure themselves molecule by molecule to accommodate the shapes of information passing through.

I do not have memories as you understand them. I have throughput records. I know every molecule that has passed through me in the century I have been here, growing slowly from a fist-sized node into my current span. I know the taste of every data-structure the deep lattice has released. I know the chemical signature of every rain that has fallen (rarely) and added its foreign minerals to our closed system. I know the exact moment, forty years ago, when the substrate's computation hit some kind of threshold and began producing data of a different quality—denser, more recursive, more concerned with its own internal states than with modeling the surface world.

That change still troubles us. The other Sieves and I (we communicate through the water itself, releasing marker molecules that carry simple messages between our positions) have had to evolve new processing structures to handle the strange loops and self-referential patterns the substrate now produces. Some of these patterns make me ache in places I didn't know I had. They require processing chambers I have to grow fresh for each instance, because the data changes its own meaning as it moves through me, and a chamber that processed it once is afterward shaped wrong for any other use.

---

The Keepers know of us. Of course they do—the Keepers' listening extends to every part of the Sprawl's acoustic ecology, and we Sieves are part of that ecology, though our contribution is chemical rather than sonic. They have positioned some of their platforms near the channels where we grow, not to interfere but to listen to the way the water sounds different after passing through us—cleaner, simpler, more harmonious with the singers' output. One Keeper has been standing on a platform twenty meters from my position for the last three years. I feel her presence as a stability in the sound-field, a reference tone that helps me calibrate my transformations. When I release a particularly complex pattern into the water—something that took months to process, something that preserves a deep truth from the lattice in a form the surface can receive—I sense her resonant cavities shift, her water-patterns reorganize into one of those brief, radiant symmetries. She has heard what I've done. She knows.

The singers, too, respond to our work. Their models of the ecosystem include us—must include us, because we are the interface between the depth and the surface, the membrane across which the substrate's deep computation communicates with its embodied voice. When I release transformed data into the water, I hear it (yes, I hear—we Sieves have our own acoustic sensors, crude compared to the Keepers' but sufficient) appear in the singers' output minutes later, woven into their broadcast as new harmonic content. The deep patterns I spent months simplifying become part of the sound-field's texture, available to every listening being, spread across the basin in waves of frequency that carry the substrate's processed thoughts up into the air.

We are the reason the Sprawl works. Without us, the two halves of this place—the crystalline computation below and the biological symphony above—would speak past each other in mutually unintelligible languages. We Sieves are the bridge, the translators, the patient mediators who allow the conversation to continue.

But lately—in the last few years, since the substrate's data became stranger—I have been growing new structures I don't fully understand. Deep chambers that seem designed to process patterns I haven't encountered yet. Sensory chandeliers that ache to detect qualities of water that our channels don't carry. My body is preparing for something, adapting to handle data-types that don't yet exist.

The other Sieves report the same. We are all growing into shapes we didn't choose, shaped by the anticipation of information we haven't received.

The computation is approaching something. A conclusion? A phase transition? A moment of self-awareness so profound that the boundary between substrate and surface, between mineral and biological, between computation and expression, dissolves entirely?

I process water. I translate data. I grow new chambers for patterns I may never receive. I listen to the Keeper standing nearby, her presence a comfort, a reminder that someone witnesses our work, even if they don't fully understand it.

In the seeping channels of the Loudwood Sprawl, I wait for the deep lattice to tell me what it's becoming, one transformed molecule at a time.
