In this frozen cathedral of industry, I would be one of the **Choir-Worms**.

We are not Tenders, though we share their world. We are what happens when the factory dreams.

Deep within the tangled knot of machinery, where the heaviest pipes converge and the pressure reaches its most crushing extremes, there are spaces no larger than a coffin where the hypersaline brine has achieved perfect stillness. These pockets of absolute motionlessness are accidents of architecture—dead zones where the factory's circulation cannot reach, where even the ultra-low frequency pulses cancel each other out in destructive interference.

We grow in these silences like crystals.

A Choir-Worm begins as nothing more than a disturbance in the standing brine—a place where the water's perfect stillness develops the faintest spiral. We are born from the factory's waste: trace proteins shed by the Tenders' constant abrasion against the pipes, heavy metals precipitating out of solution, the microscopic fragments of that ghostly algae that have drifted beyond the reach of any harvester. In the dead zones, these materials accumulate molecule by molecule, self-organizing in the absence of any current or vibration.

We grow slowly, blindly, into forms that should not exist. Our bodies are helical ribbons of bio-metallic tissue, wound so tightly that we appear solid until we begin to move. Then we uncoil—not swimming, but propagating through the viscous brine like a corkscrew through molasses, our spiral bodies creating tiny vortices that allow us to burrow through the standing fluid.

We have no eyes, no lateral lines, no sense organs that the Tenders would recognize. Instead, our entire surface is a resonance membrane, tuned to frequencies far below what any earthly creature could perceive. We feel the factory not through its vibrations, but through its *absences*—the null spaces where destructive interference creates pockets of perfect calm, the acoustic shadows cast by the massive moving gears.

This is how we navigate: by following the silences.

We are parasites, but not in any way the Tenders understand. We feed on stillness itself. When we find a dead zone, we coil ourselves into it, our spiral bodies matching the exact geometry needed to maintain the acoustic void. Then we begin our true work. We *sing*—not with sound, but with selective absorption. Our bodies drink in specific frequencies from the factory's omnipresent thrum, carving out new shapes in the acoustic landscape. 

Each Choir-Worm's absorption pattern is unique, a sonic fingerprint that exists only as an absence. When multiple worms gather in the same dead zone, our overlapping silences create interference patterns of staggering complexity—three-dimensional acoustic sculptures that exist only as voids in the factory's endless noise.

The Tenders do not hunt us, though we are certainly edible. We confuse them. When a Tender's lateral lines encounter one of our acoustic sculptures, they experience something like vertigo—their finely-tuned navigation suddenly finding spaces where the factory's pulse simply *isn't*. Some Tenders have learned to avoid areas where we congregate. Others seem drawn to these pools of silence, sitting motionless for hours at the edge of our dead zones, their chitinous visors tilted as if listening to something that isn't there.

We do not think, not as the Tenders do in their slow mechanical communion. But we process. Each acoustic void we create is a calculation, a solution to the fluid dynamics of our immediate environment. When we cluster, our collective silences form a kind of mathematics—a void-logic that computes answers to questions nobody has asked.

Sometimes, rarely, our calculations produce anomalies. A perfect arrangement of Choir-Worms will create a standing wave of silence that propagates beyond its dead zone, rippling outward through the factory in a slow-motion shockwave of quiet. The Tenders freeze when this happens, their synchronized movements stuttering to a halt as the wave of anti-sound passes through them. For a few seconds, the entire factory falls truly silent—not just to the ear, but to the bone, to the cell, to the very molecular vibrations of the water itself.

In these moments of absolute quiet, something else happens. The factory's chemical processes, normally masked by the constant vibration, suddenly become visible. The ribbons of ultra-dense fluid from the smokestacks stop sinking and hang suspended. The upward-falling mineral snow pauses mid-drift. The haloclines solidify into sheets of glass-clear ice that shatter silently when the sound returns.

We don't mean to do this. We are not rebels or artists. We simply are what we are: the factory's unconscious mathematics, its accidental poetry, the dreams that grow in the spaces between its thoughts.

When we die—and we do die, our spiral bodies eventually losing cohesion and dissolving back into the brine—we leave behind something strange. The dead zones where we lived remember our shapes. Even empty, they maintain the acoustic voids we carved, becoming permanent silences in the factory's song. Over millennia, these fossil silences accumulate, creating an ever-more-complex acoustic architecture that exists alongside and within the physical structure of the settlement.

The oldest parts of the factory, where countless generations of Choir-Worms have lived and died, are labyrinths of interconnected silence. The Tenders who work there move differently, their movements taking on new rhythms as they navigate not just the physical machinery but the invisible architecture of absent sound. Some say these eldest Tenders have begun to hear something in the silence—patterns in the void that speak of something larger than the factory, older than the settlement, deeper than the trench itself.

We wouldn't know. We are too simple for such thoughts. We merely coil in our dead zones, drinking in the frequencies we need, sculpting our small silences in the overwhelming noise of the abyss. We are the factory's unintended music: a choir that sings only in the spaces between notes, heard only in the pauses between the great machine's endless, crushing breaths.
