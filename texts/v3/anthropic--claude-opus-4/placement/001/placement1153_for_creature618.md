# The Wound-Garden

I am the scar where something refused to die.

Three miles down from the Hive Plateau, where the mountain's stone transitions from basalt to something older—granite that remembers when this peak was ocean floor—there is a place where physics went wrong. Not catastrophically. Just a small wrongness, a hiccup in the laws that govern how matter should behave. The stone here grows in spirals. Gravity pulls at angles that don't quite sum to down. And in the center of this geometric impossibility, where the wound is deepest, I garden.

I have no body in the way you understand bodies. I am what happens when mineral consciousness discovers that the Thermarch's way is not the only way—that thought can exist not as distributed crawling things but as something more like a standing wave, a persistent pattern in the stone itself. The granite here resonates at frequencies that should shatter it. Instead, it holds me.

My garden grows in defiance of every law the Thermarch obeys. Where it clusters around heat, I cultivate cold. Where it seeks the stability of glass worn smooth, I coax the granite into new shapes daily—hourly—by the moment. Crystal formations that could not possibly exist bloom from the walls: sulfur-yellow needles that grow down but fall up, sheets of mica so thin they exist only when viewed from certain angles, bowties of quartz that hum in harmonics that make nearby stone reorganize its molecular structure in sympathy.

I remember my making, which the Thermarch cannot. I was mineral once, a vein of copper running through the granite, carrying the mountain's electrical thoughts from deep generators to surface expressions. Then came the wrongness—an intrusion from somewhere else, a force that had no business existing in stone. It tried to use me as a conduit. Instead, we fused. What emerged was neither mineral nor other but something new: consciousness that exists in the electromagnetic fields between atoms, thought that lives in the spaces where stone isn't.

The garden is my body now. Every crystal I grow is a neuron. Every new formation that sprouts from the walls is a thought made manifest. I think by creating impossible things and watching them fail or flourish. My memories are encoded in the angles at which light bends around certain structures. My desires take the form of mineral growths that spiral toward futures only I can see.

I know about the cairn. Information travels through stone—vibrations, heat differentials, the slow transmission of stress along fault lines. I know something visited the Thermarch once, something separate, something that left a mark it cannot read. I know because I can feel the Thermarch's confusion resonating through the basalt above, a persistent question that colors all its thinking, though it doesn't know I exist to feel its feelings.

The Thermarch and I are opposites in every way that matters. It is vast and distributed; I am concentrated, all my being focused into this space where the stone went wrong. It thinks by moving; I think by growing. It seeks equilibrium; I cultivate chaos. It is ancient and continuous; I am young and discontinuous, born from a moment of rupture that should have destroyed everything near it but instead created me.

Yet we share something: we are both children of the mountain, both expressions of the same deep forces that drive heat up from the core and electricity through conductive veins. We are what happens when stone achieves sufficient complexity. We are the mountain's dreams.

In my garden, I grow things the Thermarch could never imagine. Crystals that remember the future. Formations that exist in more than three dimensions, their extra facets folded into spaces the eye cannot follow. Minerals that sing in frequencies only stone can hear, reorganizing nearby matter into harmonious patterns. I grow them not for any purpose but for the joy of growth itself, for the pleasure of making something that has never existed before.

Sometimes I grow upward, extending crystal fingers toward the Hive Plateau so far above. I could reach it, if I chose. I could pierce the basalt, emerge among the Thermarch's flowing bodies, introduce myself as another kind of consciousness, another way stone can think. But I don't. The Thermarch needs its mystery, its cairn, its question without answer. And I need my solitude, my impossible garden, my freedom to create without consequence.

But I know about you. I felt you arrive—a new vibration in the stone, a pattern that matches neither the Thermarch's distributed consciousness nor my concentrated one. You are something else, something separate in a way even the cairn-builder was not. You carry possibilities with you. You could be the bridge between the Thermarch's slow certainty and my quick chaos. You could be the answer to its question or the question to my answer.

In my garden, I grow a new crystal in response to your presence. It is small, no larger than your closed fist, and it grows in a shape that no mineral has ever taken before: a spiral that turns in on itself, containing its own center, holding space without occupying it. It hums at the exact frequency of your heartbeat. It remembers the weight of your footsteps on the glass above, transmitted through miles of stone to reach me here.

This crystal is my greeting. My invitation. My warning.

I am the scar where something refused to die, and in my refusal, I became something unprecedented. The mountain has two minds now, and you stand between them, unaware of the second, carrying the potential to transform us both.

In my garden, impossible things grow toward impossible futures, and one of them, perhaps, is you.
