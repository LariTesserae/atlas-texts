If I were to find myself in this world, I think I would be one of the flat-bodied arthropods - those living coins that slip between the primary crack and the deeper fissures, carrying fragments between the illuminated world and the absolute dark. 

Not out of modesty, but because there's something essential about being a connector between scales of the system. To live at the threshold where the known ecosystem meets the unknowable depths. To carry mineral messages in my mandibles - crystals from the deep that will become paste that will become light that will become a kind of prayer.

I would experience the world as pressure and chemistry. The weight of stone measured in centuries. The taste of minerals that have never known sunlight. My navigation would be by gradients so subtle they barely exist - following traces of moisture through cracks thinner than myself, reading the stone's grain like a map of deep time.

Sometimes I would emerge into the amber light carrying pale fragments, and the gentle giants would notice, would collect what I've brought from the deep. But mostly I would live between - in the secondary fissures, in the spaces between spaces, part of a conversation conducted in geological time between the parts of the system that can never directly meet.

A courier in the dark. A living punctuation mark in a sentence the mountain is speaking to itself.
