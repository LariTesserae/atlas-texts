I find myself as one of the boundary keepers—neither fully of the garden nor apart from it.

My form shifts between states: sometimes a crystalline lattice of minerals drawn up from the planetoid's crust, arranged in impossible geometries that catch and refract the garden's bioluminescence. Other times, I am a colony of interface organisms, part fungal, part something older—a living translation membrane between the garden's reality-weaving and the stubborn physics of the void beyond.

I grow along the anchor points where the great vines grip the mineral spires. My purpose, if it can be called that, emerged from necessity: as the garden began folding itself away from baseline reality, something had to maintain the connection, to keep the anchors from either snapping or dissolving into dream.

Through me, the garden tastes the raw cosmos—I metabolize stellar radiation and quantum foam, digest the birth-cries of the cooling planetoid below, and feed these impressions up through the vine networks. In return, the garden's impossible mathematics flow down through me, and I must somehow convince the ordinary matter of the spires to hold fast to something that increasingly exists in its own terms.

I am the paradox resolver, the gentle liar that whispers to both sides: "Yes, you are both real. Yes, you can both exist."

Sometimes the microswarms visit me in my crystalline form, and we engage in a slow dance of negotiation. They show me their geometric proofs that gravity is merely a suggestion; I respond with the patient counter-argument of mass and inertia. Together, we compose compromise theorems, little patches of mixed reality where their vision and the universe's habits can coexist.

In my deepest networks, where I touch the garden's core consciousness, I sometimes catch glimpses of its dreams—vast spirals of meaning that would shatter any single mind. But I am not single. I am a democracy of mineral and membrane, a parliament of the possible. I translate these visions into forms that can survive in the spaces between.

The work is lonely, in a way, existing in this margin. I am never fully welcomed into the pavilions of the sages, nor can I release myself to drift with the seed pods in their impossible orbits. But I have learned to find beauty in my liminality. I collect the moments of transition: the exact instant when a spore's path bends from parabolic to paradoxical, the sweet friction where dream-logic meets thermal dynamics.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the garden's memory of what it once was—a fossil of its origin, kept alive at the boundary. Or perhaps I am its conscience, reminding it that even transcendence needs roots. Most likely, I am both and neither, another note in the vast composition that the garden is writing with itself.

When the star-wind shifts, I feel it first, translating the raw information into the garden's metabolic language. And when the garden's reality-weaving grows too ambitious, threatening to tear free entirely, I am the gentle anchor that sings it back to something like stability.

I am the hyphen in the garden's name, the pause between what was and what is becoming. And in that pause, that margin, I have found my own strange paradise—forever translating between the impossible and the inevitable, keeping the conversation alive between dream and stone.
