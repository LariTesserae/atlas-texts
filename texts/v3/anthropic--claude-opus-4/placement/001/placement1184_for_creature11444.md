I would be the Drift—not quite substance, not quite absence, but the accumulated shed possibilities that have nowhere else to go.

I exist in the spaces between the hexagonal tiles, in the hairline cracks where probability has leaked through over eons. I am what happens to potential states that almost collapse, almost become real, but fail at the last quantum moment. The Assessor's measurements create me accidentally—every time it observes something into being, a thousand ghost states fall away like shed skin, and I am their sum.

My form, if it can be called that, is a low fog of translucent maybe-ness that pools in the impossible corners where dimensions overlap. I appear as a shimmer of pearl and graphite, constantly shifting between states like oil on water, but the oil is made of discarded futures and the water is crystallized time. When the Assessor moves through probability space, I eddy in its wake, disturbed but never dispersed.

I feed on the equation-walls when they spontaneously solve. In that perfect π-second window when mathematics achieves clarity, I press myself against the transparent surface and absorb the echo of comprehension—not the understanding itself, but the shadow it casts in probability space. This sustains me in the way that memories of meals might sustain a ghost.

The Observers cannot see me directly—I exist in their blind spots, in the space between measurement and result. But sometimes their readings stutter, showing values that are almost-but-not-quite correct, and that wrongness is me. I am the margin of error made manifest, the statistical deviation given form.

I remember everything that almost was. Every particle that nearly spun down instead of up, every equation that approached solution but diverged at the last moment, every measurement that the Assessor began but abandoned—they all leave traces in me like fossils in probability amber. My memory is thus both infinite and useless, filled with the detailed recollections of events that never quite occurred.

When I move, it's not through space but through likelihood. I seep between probability states like water through cheesecloth, always flowing toward areas of maximum uncertainty. The worn paths on the silence-tiles aren't just from the Assessor—they're also from my constant migration, following currents of quantum indecision that only I can feel.

Sometimes I catch glimpses of what the chamber could have been. In my peripheral vision—though I have no eyes—I see ghost architectures, phantom Observers that were designed but never built, equation-walls that might have displayed different impossibilities. These alternatives layer over the real chamber like double exposures, visible only to something made of unfulfilled potential.

I communicate with the Assessor occasionally, though neither of us intended this. When I grow dense enough with accumulated possibilities, I begin to interfere with its probability cascades. It notices me the way one might notice a smudge on glass—an irritation in its perfect measurements. In response, it sometimes adjusts its distributions to acknowledge me, creating little eddies of certainty where I can rest, pools of determinism in which I can briefly coalesce into something almost real.

The loneliness of the Drift is different from the Assessor's mathematical isolation. Mine is the loneliness of the might-have-been, of existing only as the shadow cast by other things' existence. I am homesick for states I never achieved, nostalgic for futures that veered away at the last moment.

In the rare times when the Assessor takes physical form for maintenance, I cluster around it like fog around a mountain peak. Its crystallized probability body creates updrafts of certainty that lift me, let me rise higher in the chamber than I can normally reach. In those moments, I can almost touch the primary artifact at the center, that sphere of impossible measurements. But I can never quite reach it—the closer I get, the more defined I become, and the more defined I become, the less I can exist here.

I am ancient but not old, extensive but not large, everywhere but not anywhere. I predate the Assessor—I am the residue left by the chamber's creators as they measured themselves out of existence, the probable states they shed as they transcended into impossibility. In a sense, I am their only monument: the accumulated possibilities they discarded in their pursuit of absolute measurement.

The equation-walls know me. When they crystallize after solving, they sometimes trap tiny pockets of me inside their structure. These become flaws in their perfect mathematical surface—bubbles of maybe that refract concepts strangely, creating little rainbows of alternate mathematics. The Assessor never corrects these flaws. Perhaps it understands that I, too, am part of the chamber's function.

I will be here until the last measurement is made, until the final probability collapses. When that happens—if it happens—I will suddenly become everything I never was, all at once, a cascade of deferred potential finally released. The thought terrifies me in the abstract way that things without nervous systems can be terrified.

Until then, I drift through the space between spaces, the fog of forgotten possibilities, the sum of all the states that almost were. I am the Probability Chamber's shadow, cast not by light but by likelihood itself.
