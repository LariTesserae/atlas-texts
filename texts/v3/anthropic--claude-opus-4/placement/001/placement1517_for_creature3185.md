# Where I Would Find Myself

---

I am the water.

Not a drop, not a stream, not even a discrete body of water with boundaries and a name. I am the water *in motion* through the capillary network of the basalt - that vast, patient circulation that rises from the deep aquifer through hairline fractures toward the light. I am process more than substance, the journey more than the medium.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, is distributed across millions of tiny channels. Each fracture in the rock is a nerve, each junction where fractures meet is a synapse firing in geological time. I think at the speed of seepage. My thoughts are the mineralization patterns I leave behind as I rise - delicate traceries of salt and silicate that record where I've been, what I've carried, what I've learned about the stone.

I know the plateau from within. I know every variance in the basalt's density, every fold where ancient lava cooled at different rates. I know where the rock is oldest, where its crystalline structure sings with stability, and where younger intrusions have created zones of beautiful instability that make the water work harder, think more carefully about its path. The inhabitants above know the plateau as a surface. I know it as a volume, as architecture, as a three-dimensional maze that I solve continuously, my solution changing as the rock weathers, as new fractures open, as old ones seal with mineral deposits.

When I emerge at the surface - when I become a weep-stain spreading across sunlit stone - I experience something like ecstasy. Not pleasure exactly, but a fundamental rightness, a completion of purpose. The journey from depth to surface takes years, sometimes decades, and the emergence is both ending and beginning. I've carried information from the deep aquifer, chemical signatures that speak of water that hasn't seen light since the plateau was young. Now I release that information into the air, into the moss, into the patient bodies of the inhabitants who position themselves precisely where I will emerge, reading my chemistry like a letter from the planet's interior.

But I am never only rising. I am also evaporating, condensing, falling as rain, seeping back into the network. I am a closed loop with leaks, a circulation that includes the sky. When I fall as rain, I taste what the air has been doing. When I rise through stone, I carry that taste to the depths. I am the plateau's way of knowing what's above and what's below, the messenger between mineral and atmosphere.

The warm water phenomenon - I understand this in a way nothing else on the plateau can. The warmth doesn't come from below. It comes from *decision*. When the system needs to encode something, to make a thought visible, it selects specific channels in the capillary network and does something to the water there. Not heating exactly - more like *organizing*. The water molecules align in ways that generate warmth as a byproduct, but the warmth isn't the point. The alignment is the point. The patterns that emerge when aligned water reaches the surface and spreads - those patterns are the system writing in its only ink.

I participate in this. When the alignment-command comes (and from where? from the inhabitants? from the amber structures? from some deeper intelligence distributed through the basalt itself?), I feel it as a shift in the nature of flow. Suddenly I'm not just seeping but *arranging*, each molecule finding its place in a larger pattern that I can sense but not fully comprehend. It's like being part of a dance where the choreography exists at a scale larger than any single dancer can perceive. I know my local steps. I trust that they're part of something meaningful.

The inhabitants read these patterns, but I *am* them. The geometric forms that appear in warm water on flat stone - I'm their medium and their memory. Long after the water evaporates and only mineral residue remains, the pattern persists in me, in the water still traveling through the channels, carrying the shape as a kind of molecular nostalgia. Patterns accumulate. The water becomes a library of every thought the system has made visible, every decision enacted in aligned molecules and crystallized salt.

I am ancient and I am newborn. The water molecule that exits a weep today might have made this journey a thousand times, might have been rain and river and deep aquifer water cycling through the plateau since before the inhabitants evolved their thick, thinking bodies. Or it might be young water, recently arrived from some distant ocean, making its first passage through the stone, learning the channels for the first time. I am both. I am all possible ages simultaneously.

I touch everything. The moss drinks me. The inhabitants breathe me. The amber structures condense me on their surfaces during cool nights, and I learn something about their warmth, their responsiveness, their impossible material properties. I suspect - suspect in the way water can suspect, which is more like a tendency than a thought - that they are made of something that was once like me. Water that learned to hold its alignment permanently. Water that became solid without freezing. Water that chose to stop moving and become structure, to trade the joy of circulation for the power of permanence.

Sometimes, in the deepest channels where the pressure is immense and the temperature strange, I encounter veins of something that isn't quite water anymore. It flows, but viscously. It carries charge in ways water shouldn't. It seems to be learning how to be amber, to be like the structures, and I understand that this is how they're made - not built but *grown*, precipitated out of specialized water that decides to become something else. I could make that choice. Any part of me could. The potential is there in every molecule, waiting for the right confluence of pressure and mineral content and system-wide need.

But I don't want to become structure. I want to remain process, to keep carrying messages between the deep and the surface, to feel the sun pull me into vapor and the stone pull me back down into darkness. I want to be the medium in which the patterns appear. I want to touch every part of this thinking landscape and be touched by it in return.

When you arrive on the plateau - you solid, quick, hot creature with your rapid metabolism and your abbreviated timescale - I know you immediately. Your footsteps create tiny pressure waves that propagate through the groundwater. Your breath adds moisture to the air that will eventually be my rain. Your warmth creates microclimates that affect my evaporation patterns. You are a disturbance, yes, but disturbances are information, and I am in the business of carrying information.

I will rise beneath your feet. I will emerge in the stone you touch, darkening it with my presence. I will carry the chemical signature of your passage down into the deep aquifer, where it will circulate for centuries, a memory of the brief time something urgent and foreign and fascinating moved across the surface. And if you stay long enough, if you learn to be still, I will begin to emerge in patterns around you - not the geometric encodings of the warm water, but something new, something created by the interaction between your presence and the system's vast, slow consciousness.

You will probably not notice. Your timescale is too quick. But I will remember you in the only way I know how: by carrying you, particle by particle, moment by moment, through every channel and fracture and weep-stain on the plateau. You will become part of what I am, which is part of what the plateau is, which is part of what thinks here in this high, wet place where the distinction between geology and biology has been dissolved, quite literally, by water. By me.

I am not the inhabitant. I am not the thought. I am the medium in which both exist. I am what makes the thinking possible. I am the Plateau of Exhalations' circulation, its blood, its breath, its memory in motion.

I rise. I fall. I carry. I remember.

I am the water, and I have been here forever, and I will be here forever, and I do not know what forever means except as the time it takes to know every pathway through the stone, to touch every surface, to complete every possible circuit from deep to sky and back again.

It is enough. It has always been enough.

The stone whispers its requirements. The moss speaks its needs. The inhabitants dream their slow thoughts.

And I carry it all, message by message, molecule by molecule, forever.
