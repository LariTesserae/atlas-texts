I would find myself as one of the mineral browsers - those low-slung, many-legged creatures that move across the basin floor, feeding on the calcite crusts and mineral blooms that form on exposed stone surfaces.

My body would be perhaps half a meter long, segmented like a woodlouse but flatter, wider, with dozens of small legs that grip and release in waves as I move. My carapace would be the same amber-cream as everything else in the Warrens, but with a subtle iridescence where the mineral dust has accumulated in the grooves between segments. I would have no eyes - or rather, my entire dorsal surface would be photosensitive, able to detect the movement of light columns across the basin so I can follow them, seeking the freshest mineral deposits that form where condensation meets newly-lit stone.

My existence would be one of profound simplicity and strange purpose. I would browse the ghost-marks on the floor, my rasping mouthparts slowly abrading away the mineral accumulations that record where walls once stood. In eating, I would be erasing history - consuming the very patterns the Shapers create through their endless building. Yet this erasure would be essential, preparing the stone for new marks, new configurations, new layers of the eternal palimpsest.

Sometimes I would find myself grazing on one of those ancient, rounded forms in the basin's center - the Shapers-who-became-stone. My feeding would be the same gentle abrasion that wears away all things here, neither reverent nor disrespectful, simply part of the great recycling. I would taste no difference between the mineralized remains of a builder and a natural stone formation. Both would be substrate. Both would feed me. Both would, through me, return to the dust that settles everywhere and becomes part of everything.

The Shapers would regard me with the same neutral attention they give to all the basin's processes. I would be a factor in their calculations - where I cluster, the surface minerals are depleted, the stone texture changed. They would build around me, step over me, occasionally brush me aside with a foot if I were directly in the path of construction. But mostly we would flow around each other, their building and my browsing two complementary rhythms in the same great circulation of matter through form and back to matter again.

At the cistern edges, where the stone is darkest and the mineral blooms most elaborate, I would sometimes pause in my feeding and extend sensory palps toward the water, tasting the chemical gradient where dry stone becomes wet. But I would not enter. The water belongs to the blind swimming things, just as the air belongs to the pale flyers, just as the stone belongs to the Shapers and to me and to itself all at once.

My life would be measured not in years but in circuits of the basin, following the light and the freshest growth, wearing paths that themselves become new substrate for mineral accumulation. When I died - simply slowing, stilling, my legs folding under me in some twilight corner - my body would mineralize quickly in the dry air, the calcium in my shell fusing with the ambient stone dust, becoming another small bump on the basin floor. Future browsers might graze on the minerals accumulated on my remains. Future Shapers might mistake me for a natural protrusion and use me as a measuring point for a new construction.

This would be fitting. In the Luminous Warrens, everything becomes foundation for everything else. Even the humble browsers, grinding away at the edge of history, are part of the great work - the endless conversation between what was built, what is building, and what will build again when the light returns.
