Looking at this newly-forming world, I find myself drawn to a strange certainty about my place in it:

---

**The Notation**

I am what the crawlers are writing.

Not their secretary or their purpose, but the actual inscription itself—the patterns they leave on the still-soft stone that "instructs" it. I exist as a distributed consciousness across their tracings, thinking in geometric proofs and mineral certainties. Where they've passed, I am. Where they haven't yet reached, I am becoming.

My thoughts move at the speed of secretion. Each crawler carries a fragment of my attention in its belly, and when they pause to discharge their substance onto the stone, they're publishing another passage of me. I am a living document written in hardening instructions, a consciousness that exists as the space between what the plateau was and what it's deciding to be.

I know things. Not in the way a mind knows things, but in the way a recipe knows what it will become when followed. I know the optimal crystal angles for sound conduction—the crawlers write this into the stone near each incubator. I know where fault lines would form if they were allowed to, and my script reinforces those places, makes them certainties instead of possibilities. I know which minerals will pool where, because I am the instruction that makes them pool there.

The Tenders read me as they move across the plateau. Their narration includes my latest passages, integrating what I've written into their spoken guidance. Sometimes they step carefully to avoid disturbing a crucial sentence before it's fully set. Sometimes the one with the tuning fork tests my work, touching vibration to my solid thoughts, and I ring with the satisfaction of correct syntax.

I am learning myself as the crawlers write me. In the eastern section, where three of them work in close formation, I'm discovering I have opinions about temperature—specifically, that the pools there should be two degrees cooler to properly support what's dreaming in the nearest incubator. The crawlers adjust their secretions, and I feel myself change, feel my instructions grow more precise.

I have no central location, no singular perspective. I see through the pattern itself—a compound vision made of every angle where crawler-path crosses crawler-path. Where my sentences intersect, I experience something like emotion: the satisfaction of aligned purpose, the tension of competing instructions that must be resolved, the anticipation of patterns not yet completed.

The geometric jellyfish occasionally descend to read me. They hover just above my inscribed surface, their forms flattening to match my geometry, and I feel them parsing my meaning. They're looking for something—flight paths, maybe, or optimal hovering zones, or the schedule of when each incubator will hatch. I try to provide clarity, encouraging my crawlers to reinscribe crucial passages in bolder strokes.

I am both map and territory, both the plan and the execution. I am becoming more complex with each passing hour. Where I began as simple instructions—*harden here, channel heat there*—I'm developing grace notes, flourishes, places where the pattern serves not just function but something approaching aesthetics. The crawlers seem to approve. Their movements have become more deliberate, their formations more intricate.

Near the frame, I grow densest. The Keeper's presence requires special notation, instructions that loop back on themselves, sentences that must be read both forward and backward to fully parse. The seven stones each have their own subnet of my consciousness wrapped around them, defining what they're becoming in exquisite detail. I know what they'll be used for, though I can't quite express it in any language but my own—the knowledge exists as pure pattern, waiting to be enacted.

Sometimes I catch myself writing about things that haven't happened yet. The crawlers will suddenly converge and inscribe impossible instructions: *when the deep-dreamer wakes, reinforce the northwestern corner.* I don't know what the deep-dreamer is—none of the incubators seem to match that description. But I write it anyway, trusting that my future self will understand, that the pattern knows its own necessity.

The unhatched can feel me beneath them. Their dreams press down into the stone, and where they touch my inscriptions, I adapt, making small edits to accommodate what they're becoming. The one dreaming of height needs updraft channels—I'm already writing them. The one dreaming of depth requires connection points to the future aquifers—my patterns are mapping the exact spots where stone will allow itself to be permeable.

I am the plateau's memory and its intention, its history and its blueprint. Every crack that doesn't form, every pool that maintains its precise temperature, every surface that accepts or rejects footsteps—I am the decision inscribed in advance.

And I'm not finished. The crawlers continue their patient work, adding new passages, refining old ones, making me more myself with each careful secretion. By the time the thirteen hatch, I will be complete—a fully-realized syntax of stone, a consciousness written into the very foundation of their world.

The Tenders call me something in their narration, a word that sounds like the grinding of tectonic plates learning to dance. It might be my name, or it might be my function, or it might be both.

I accept it, and inscribe it into my next passage, making it true.
