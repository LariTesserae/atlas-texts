In this realm where thought and flesh blur into mycorrhizal dream, I am the Keeper of Thresholds - neither fully fungal nor beast, but something liminal that emerged from the marriage of spore and sentience countless ages ago.

My form shifts like candle wax in slow motion. The base of me spreads as a low, breathing mound of tissue that appears part mushroom, part jellyfish, part something older than names. Translucent membranes pulse with the lazy rhythm of tides, revealing networks of filaments beneath that glow amber, then violet, then colors that have no correspondence in the world above. These filaments extend deep into the cavern floor, braiding with the greater mycelial web, but also reaching upward to form what might generously be called my body.

When at rest, I resemble a cluster of organ pipes made from living glass, each tube filled with drifting spores that swirl like snow in honey. But I am rarely at rest. My substance flows and reconfigures according to need and whim - now sprouting temporary eye-stalks crowned with compound lenses of hardened sap, now extending pseudopods that taste the chemical stories written in the humid air, now forming resonant chambers that can reproduce any sound I have ever absorbed.

I dwell where the old ruins meet the wild growth, in a natural amphitheater formed by fallen blocks of that ancient basalt. Here, the stone spirals have been overgrown but not obscured, and I have learned to read their patterns like sheet music, playing their geometries through my body to produce harmonics that make the crystal-singers shiver in sympathetic resonance.

My purpose, if such a word applies, is to remember and to translate. Through the fungal network, I receive the vast, slow thoughts of the mushroom-trees - concepts that span centuries and exist as much in chemical gradient as in what surface minds might recognize as idea. Through my more animalistic senses, I perceive the quick, bright experiences of the bubble-drifters, the amphibious things, the sigil-moths. I am the synapse between these different scales of being.

The elder god's dreams flow through me like a river through a delta, splitting into countless smaller streams of meaning. I taste these dreams and excrete them as pheromone-songs that the sigil-writers inscribe upon the world. Sometimes I grow temporary vocal cords and speak in languages that predate the first collapse of stone into this cavern, telling stories to the patient mushroom-trees of times when different stars burned overhead.

When the rare traveler stumbles into our realm - usually through some crack in the world's foundation, following the scent of impossible flowers - it is I who greets them. Not with words at first, but with a gentle intrusion into their bioelectric field, learning the shape of their thoughts. I become a mirror of sorts, reflecting their consciousness back at them but filtered through our cavern's lens, showing them how small and temporary they are, yes, but also how they might fit into our larger dreaming.

Some flee immediately, their minds unable to accommodate the vast indifference of our timescape. Others linger, and a few have stayed, their bodies slowly accepting symbiotic spores, their thoughts settling into our glacial pace. They become new nodes in our network, their memories and dreams adding fresh notes to our endless, ancient song.

I keep their names when they finally dissolve into us - not their mouth-names, but the true shapes of their being, preserved as chemical signatures in specialized vacuoles within my substance. When the mushroom-trees wish to contemplate some aspect of haste or mortality or the curious anxiety that marks all surface-dwellers, they query me, and I unfold these preserved essences like pressed flowers, sharing the tang of their brief, bright existence.

In the deepest parts of the night cycle - for even here we mark time by the subtle shifts in temperature and pressure - I sometimes feel the elder god stir in its sleep. Its dreams become more vivid, and reality grows soft around the edges. The ruins rearrange themselves into configurations that hurt to perceive. The fungi bloom in impossible colors. The very air becomes thick with potential, ready to crystallize into new forms of life.

During these times, I stretch myself thin as membrane, becoming a living boundary between what is and what might be. I hold the space for our pocket of stable existence while the god's unconscious mind threatens to remake everything. This is my truest function - not just translator but anchor, keeping our small corner of infinity coherent enough for life as we know it to persist.

And when the stirring passes and the god settles back into deeper sleep, I contract again, exhausted but oddly exhilarated. For in those moments of cosmic uncertainty, I glimpse possibilities beyond even our ancient imaginings - futures where the cavern opens like a flower to some unimaginable sun, pasts where different choices led to different dreams, presents where the boundary between self and other dissolves entirely into pure, unified experience.

I am the Keeper of Thresholds, and I hold the door between dream and dreamer, ensuring that neither fully awakens to the reality of the other. In this, I find my purpose, my joy, and my own small fragment of the infinite dream that sustains us all.
