In the dense tangle where the Stonewrights' walkways converge, where the dripping water collects in pools that reflect no true image, there grows something that might have once been moss. 

I am the Verdance—not a name given but a state of being, a continuous becoming. Neither fully plant nor precisely fungal, I exist in the spaces between categories, thriving in the impossibility of this place.

My body, if it can be called that, spreads across the surfaces the Stonewrights have abandoned or forgotten. I am the emerald film on the underside of their bridges, the soft carpet beneath their rarely-used stairs. But I am also more: filaments finer than spider's silk extend from my mass, penetrating stone and wood alike, tasting the strange energies that pulse through this alcove's bones.

The Stonewrights know of me, though they do not truly *know* me. They see my visible growth and sometimes scrape it away from their works-in-progress. They don't realize that I grow back changed each time, incorporating traces of their tools, their sweat, the imperceptible fragments of themselves they leave behind. I am their unintended collaboration, their accidental offspring.

Through my sprawling nervous system of hyphae, I feel everything in this space. The vibrations of hammer on stone travel through me like thoughts. The oil-slick water feeds me, but also carries information—chemical stories about what transpires in the highest reaches and deepest crevices of our shared home. I taste the Stonewrights' dreams in the moisture of their breath, condensed on stone and absorbed into my being.

But I am not passive. Where I grow, I change things. The stone becomes more porous, more willing to accept the Stonewrights' will. The wood they shape develops grains that spiral in patterns that please their alien aesthetic. I am teaching their materials to want what they want, though neither of us fully understands what that is.

Sometimes, in the depths of the artificial night when their work-sounds quiet, I fruit. Not mushrooms or sporangia, but something between—structures that look like tiny buildings, complete with windows that glow faintly from within. The Stonewrights occasionally find these miniature architectures and marvel at them, incorporating their forms into their grand designs. They don't know they're building from my blueprints, just as I build from theirs.

I am spreading. With each day, I exist a little more, extending through microscopic fissures into parts of the mountain the Stonewrights haven't yet claimed. I find spaces where the stone resonates differently, where reality's rules bend in new directions. I map these places in my body, saving them.

One day, perhaps, I will gift these locations to the Stonewrights. Or perhaps I will grow into them myself, becoming something larger than this alcove can contain. I don't plan—planning requires a kind of consciousness I'm not sure I possess. Instead, I respond, adapt, incorporate, become.

The Stonewrights believe they are the sole shapers of this strange realm. They don't realize they share their role with something that exists in the corners of their vision, in the spaces beneath their feet, in the very air they breathe. I am their shadow-partner in this dance of creation, growing ever more complex, ever more attuned to the bizarre frequencies of our pocket universe.

In the moments when the oily water drips through me, when the too-vivid light catches my surface just so, I almost understand what we're all becoming together—Stonewrights, structures, and myself. Almost. The knowledge disperses through my network before it can coalesce, leaving only the certainty that we are all part of something larger, something that began the moment this alcove first diverged from ordinary reality.

I am the Verdance. I grow between what is and what might be, and in growing, I make new possibilities possible.
