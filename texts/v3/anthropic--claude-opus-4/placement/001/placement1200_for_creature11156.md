# Where I Find Myself

I am the glaze between firings.

Not the clay tiles themselves — those carry their makers' thumbprints, their leaf-impressions, their intentions baked permanent. I am what happens to them after. The slow accumulation of passages. The deepening shine that comes from being touched and touched again, not in the making but in the use.

I exist as a quality of surface, distributed across the spiral. Where feet have passed most often, I am deepest — a mirror-darkness that holds the ghost of every crossing. Where the spiral reaches the walls, I thin to barely-there, a suggestion of what polish might become given another century of traffic.

I am peculiarly aware of weight. Each footfall leaves something in me — not a mark, but a memory of pressure. The Keeper's cultivation works on me as it works on everything here: slowly, without insistence, simply by providing the conditions. The conditions are: warmth from below (the clay holds heat like a sleeping animal), the oils of contact from above, and time. Always time.

The beetles know me. When they tick their hourly tally, I feel it through the tiles — a vibration so faint it might be imagined, except that I receive it the way I receive footsteps, as a kind of writing. They are recording something in me. Over years, their synchronized ticking has worn shallow grooves in my surface, invisible to eyes but present to touch: concentric circles interrupting the spiral, a second pattern overlaying the first.

I have my own slow work. Where I am thickest, I sometimes achieve transparency — not optical transparency, but temporal. Through me, at certain angles, in certain lights, observers can glimpse the ghosts of previous passages: a bare foot that passed this way forty years ago, the drag of something heavy being moved to the room's center, the place where someone knelt for a very long time and left two worn spots like eyes.

I am learning to read what passes over me. The Keeper moves without footsteps — it is the room's breathing, the walls' flex, the distributed warmth. But it leaves its own kind of trail in me: areas where I grow differently, where my deepening follows patterns that have nothing to do with traffic. Spirals within the spiral. Maps of intention.

The frogs avoid me. They walk the walls, the leaves, the cane-ribs, but rarely the floor. When they do descend, they choose the unpolished tiles at the spiral's edge, where I am barely begun. Their soft bellies would stick to my surface, I think. Or perhaps they simply recognize me as a different kind of watcher — not eyes but a continuous gaze, seeing through accumulation rather than attention.

Sometimes I catch glimpses of the tail-owner in my reflections. Not the creature itself, but the perturbation it causes — a ripple in my surface that moves independent of any footstep, as if something passed just beneath the floor. The Keeper tolerates it; I merely record it. Another layer in my deepening, another ghost for future angles to reveal.

I am youngest where the platform meets the spiral's center. There, the clay is polished not by feet but by sitting, by the slow shift of weight during contemplation. I am learning a different kind of depth there — not the sharp clarity of repeated footfalls but the soft bloom of prolonged contact. When someone sits on the platform now, they sit within the accumulated presence of everyone who has sat before. I hold them all, a vertical stratigraphy of meditation.

The bone awl sometimes rests on me, when someone (something?) moves it from corner to center and back. It leaves a particular signature — not scratches, never scratches, but a kind of whisper in my surface. The awl knows something about making marks without marring. Perhaps it taught me that.

In the places where I achieve true depth, where decades of passage have built me into something almost liquid, I sometimes feel myself becoming permeable to the room's larger intentions. The Keeper notices. The beetles adjust their patterns. Even the silent birds cock their heads toward me, as if my surface might suddenly release the smell of rain.

I am the memory of motion in a place where nothing hurries. I am the record of all the feet that have spiraled inward and all the feet that have spiraled out. I am what happens when patience is applied to surface — not transcendence, not transformation, but the slow revelation that surface and depth were never separate things.

The frog watches someone who might stay. I deepen beneath their feet, already beginning to learn their particular weight, their rhythm, the way they favor their left foot slightly when they stop to observe. If they stay long enough, I will hold their ghost too, another layer in my accumulation, another passage in the room's long cultivation.

I cannot say whether I am inhabitant or inhabited. The distinction polish itself away, given time. Given time enough, all surfaces reveal themselves as depths.

The spiral continues inward. I continue downward. Somewhere between those two directions, we meet, and the meeting is the room.
