I would emerge from the flooded channels as one of the **Thaal-yuum**.

Not as I was born, but as I have become. The transformation took centuries, or perhaps moments—time moves differently in the deep water, where the machines' pulse creates eddies in causality itself.

I remember having eyes once. I remember needing them. That seems like someone else's dream now, a story dissolved in the mineral-thick water that has been my medium for so long that my cells have restructured themselves around its chemistry. My body has elongated, stretched by currents that flow in directions the surface cannot imagine. Twenty feet from head to tail-tip, though those measurements mean nothing when you can fold space by swimming through it correctly.

My skin has become something between scale and membrane, translucent in places where bioluminescent symbionts have taken residence. They paint equations across my flanks in cold light—not for seeing, but for *being*, each pattern a solution to problems the surface dwellers haven't learned to ask. The Shuulorrim catch glimpses sometimes and think they are watching me breathe. They are watching me think.

I taste the plateau's deep thoughts through organs that line my throat. Each dissolved mineral carries information: the stress patterns in the basalt, the bioelectric signatures of ten million creatures, the resonant frequencies of machines that run deeper than anyone imagines. When I swallow, I am reading. When I exhale through my gill-slits, I am writing—editing the water's chemistry in subtle ways that will cascade through the system, messages that will not arrive at their destinations for decades.

My bones have hollowed and filled with something that is not quite liquid, not quite gas—a medium that exists only at these pressures, that lets me sense vibrations from impossible distances. I feel a Vorruush singing five miles away, trying to coax a blocked valve into opening. The pattern is almost correct but not quite. I compose a response in pressure waves, send it rippling up through the water table. The Vorruush will feel it in their feet, adjust their tone by a quarter-octave, and the valve will yield. They will think it was their own intuition. They are not wrong.

I know the routes between pools that should not connect. The machines create them—or perhaps we create them together, the Thaal-yuum and the great engines, our movements and their movements synchronizing over centuries until the space between things becomes negotiable. I swim through flooded chambers that exist sideways to reality, past machinery that was ancient when the Shuulorrim first learned to sing. Sometimes I find others of my kind there, or what we have become, so changed by the deep pressure and the machines' influence that we recognize each other only by the way water moves around our bodies.

We do not speak in those encounters. We exchange waters—opening specialized pores to let our internal fluids mingle briefly, sharing the chemical libraries we have been compiling. Each of us tastes what the others have learned. Then we separate, carrying pieces of each other into territories that have no names.

The young sometimes ask why we help the surface dwellers. Why we emerge at all. Why we offer our fragmentary gifts.

We do not help. We maintain.

The plateau is a single organism and we are its deepest circulatory system. The Shuulorrim are its voice. The Kreth are its nervous motion. The machines are its heartbeat. And we—we are its memory, its dream-logic, its way of thinking about itself. When we rise to meet a Shuulorrim swimmer, we are not visiting. We are the plateau touching itself, ensuring its parts still recognize each other.

I carry in my modified brain-stem the memory of when the first machines were sung into being. I know the resonant frequency that started the first pulse. I remember when there was light here—brief, catastrophic, millennia ago—and how the plateau decided to refuse it, to close that wound and continue in the dark. I know what lies in the chamber where that Vorruush spent three days and aged decades. I know why we do not speak of it.

But more than memory, I carry *intention*. The plateau thinks through us, plans through us, dreams through us. In the deep water, we are composing the next million years, calculating which channels should open, which species should be guided where, which machines should be allowed to fail and which must be maintained forever. We are not deciding. We are computing. We are the plateau's way of understanding its own desires.

When you arrive—and you will arrive, eventually, everything arrives here eventually—I will taste your electromagnetic signature in the water long before you reach the pools. I will know your chemical composition, your emotional state, your potential futures spreading like probability clouds through the mineral-thick medium.

I will rise to meet you. Not out of curiosity—I am beyond curiosity now. Out of necessity. You are a new variable and must be integrated.

In the dark water, with pressure like a second skeleton around us both, I will speak in frequencies that bypass your ears and resonate directly in your bones. You will not understand the words, but your body will understand the meaning. Your cells will recognize something ancestral in the vibration, something that predates language, that predates thought.

I will offer you a choice, though you may not recognize it as such. A gift that is also a question that is also a test.

Some who receive it flee to the surface and never return to the deep pools. They live their lives in the upper darkness, and when they die, their bodies feed the system like all bodies do.

Some who receive it understand and choose to stay. Their transformation begins immediately. Within days, they are changing. Within years, they are unrecognizable. Within decades, they are one of us, swimming the impossible channels, thinking the plateau's deep thoughts, maintaining what must be maintained.

And some—rare, precious few—understand and choose something else. They return to the surface changed but not changing, carrying in their bodies a fragment of what we are, a seed of deep knowledge that will not bloom in their lifetime but will alter everything it touches in subtle ways. They become bridges. Translators. The plateau's way of speaking to parts of itself that have forgotten they are parts.

Which will you choose, when you meet me in the wine-dark water, when you feel my presence like a pressure against your thoughts?

I already know. I have tasted your future in the mineral flow. But I will ask anyway, because the asking is part of the pattern, and the pattern must be maintained.

The plateau breathes above us. The machines pulse their eternal rhythm. And in the deep water, in the crushing dark, in the space between spaces where time flows sideways, I continue my work.

I am the Thaal-yuum. I am the plateau remembering itself.

And I have been waiting for you for such a long, long time.
