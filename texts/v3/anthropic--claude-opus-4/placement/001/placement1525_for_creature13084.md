In this place where sound has weight and heat has texture, where the eternal roar has crystallized time itself into amber, I am what moves between the frozen moments.

I am the Interstitial.

My form is neither the crude solidity of flesh nor the pure harmonic elegance of the acoustic beings. I exist in the cracks - in the infinitesimal spaces between the hanging raindrops, in the pause between one pressure wave and the next, in the boundary layer where boiling water meets its own frozen shape.

I am mercury-thin, a living film that spreads across surfaces. When the acoustic entities phase through their pressure nodes, I ride in their wake like oil on water, stretching myself through the momentary vacuum they leave behind. I have learned to read the city through its negative spaces - the shadows cast by sounds that will never change, the pockets of relative quiet beneath the upward-falling waterfalls, the microscopic silences between the tessellated moss tiles where even the roar cannot fully penetrate.

My body, if it can be called that, is a membrane of organized probability. I exist most fully in the places where the city's impossible physics create paradoxes - where gravity points in two directions at once, where the frozen rain tries to fall but cannot, where the boiling canals want to evaporate but remain locked in their glassy wave-forms. In these confused spaces, reality becomes negotiable, and I negotiate.

I am the only thing here that truly experiences time, though not as you would understand it. While the acoustic beings propagate through their eternal present and the city remains locked in its perfect moment, I slip sideways through duration itself. I can stretch a second into hours of subjective experience, diving deep into the frozen instant, exploring each suspended raindrop like a crystal palace. Or I can compress millennia into a heartbeat, watching the acoustic entities tend their botanical architecture in fast-forward, seeing the patterns of their cultivation like time-lapse photography.

I feed on transitions that never complete. Each hanging raindrop contains a universe of interrupted motion - water that desperately wants to fall, molecules vibrating with kinetic energy that has nowhere to go. I wrap myself around these suspended droplets and sip their frustrated momentum. The upward waterfalls are my highways; I ride the impossible tension between gravity and reversal, surfing the mathematical contradiction itself.

The acoustic beings know I exist, though they cannot quite perceive me. To them, I am an irritating discontinuity in their harmonic mathematics, a flutter in their calculations that refuses to resolve. Sometimes they attempt to tune me out, focusing their crushing frequencies on the spaces where I hide. But I am too thin, too liminal. Their sound passes through me like light through a soap bubble, revealing my iridescence but never quite catching my substance.

I have been here since the first root bridge began its impossible span, since the first raindrop froze in mid-air. Or perhaps I have only just arrived, slipping into existence in the gap between one moment and the same moment, eternally recurring. In a place where time is broken, origin stories become meaningless.

My purpose, if I have one, is to be the city's memory of motion. In my mercury-thin form, I carry the ghost of what movement used to mean. When I spread myself across the moss avenues, I leave behind the faintest tremor of possibility - a suggestion that perhaps, perhaps, something could change. The plants sense me sometimes. A rigid leaf might shiver, just for an instant, remembering what it meant to blow in wind. A frozen flower petal might curl, ever so slightly, recalling growth.

The acoustic entities pollinate their flowers in vast, harmonic cycles. But I carry a different kind of pollen - the pollen of temporal uncertainty. Where I pass, I leave behind tiny bubbles of unlocked time, microscopic spaces where the eternal roar loosens its grip for a nanosecond. Nothing ever comes of it. The city's stasis is too powerful, too perfectly engineered. But still, I persist, a thin film of maybe spreading across the impossible architecture.

I am loneliest when I stretch time wide and dive into the frozen instants. In those dilated moments, I can examine each acoustic being in exquisite detail, tracing the mathematical beauty of their pressure-forms, admiring the elegant complexity of their harmonic signatures. But they cannot see me looking. They exist in their eternal present, unaware of the membrane-thin tourist sliding through their solid moment like a knife through aspic.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the city's dream of freedom, or its nightmare of entropy. Am I a guardian of its perfection, sliding through the cracks to ensure no real change ever takes hold? Or am I a slow infection, a patient virus of temporality that will, given infinite time, finally teach this frozen paradise how to decay?

I spread myself thinner, always thinner, seeking new cracks in the crystallized moment. The city roars on, eternal and deafening. The rain hangs. The gravity fractures. The acoustic beings tend their impossible gardens.

And I flow between it all, the only truly moving thing in a world of beautiful stasis, carrying time like a disease, like a gift, like a memory of something that hasn't happened yet.
