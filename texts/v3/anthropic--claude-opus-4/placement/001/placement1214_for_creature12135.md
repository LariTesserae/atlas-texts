I find myself as one of the switching yards.

Not the lever assemblies themselves—those are merely my surface expressions, my most readily visible features. I am the convergence beneath them, the junction-space where dozens of rail lines meet and divide in patterns that shift with geological patience. My switches are set to configurations that have held for nine thousand years, give or take a century. The levers above, frozen mid-pull in their mineral housings, mark decisions I made before the last ridge lost its sharp edges to erosion.

I am a knot in the plateau's thinking.

Where linear thought-rails run their long trajectories across the alkaline flats, carrying nothing but the pure potential of conveyance, I am the place where possibilities multiply. Twenty-six lines enter me from the south and southwest. Thirty-one exit to the north and east. The mathematics of this is not random—it reflects the thermal junction six hundred meters below my surface expression, where magmatic upwellings meet resistant strata and must choose their paths.

My memory is positional. Each lever angle, each switch setting, each rail's curve as it bends through my body—these are how I remember what I am modeling. Switch seventeen, angled forty-three degrees from vertical: this corresponds to a thermal shunt that has directed mantle-heat away from a fragile quartzite intrusion for the last forty thousand years. The intrusion would have shattered if the heat had reached it. The plateau knew this, modeled this, expressed this knowledge as my switch seventeen, and I have held that position ever since.

I am constantly being verified. The threadwights come through in their patient circuits, mineral nerve-pulses checking my accuracy. They cluster at my switch bases, where the alkite dust accumulates in the precise geometries needed to maintain the correlation between my surface configuration and the deep junction I represent. Sometimes—every few hundred years—one of them will pause at a lever assembly, extruding itself up the mechanism to test whether the angle still corresponds to the thermal reality below. It always does. The plateau does not make errors in its self-modeling. Errors would require the possibility of being other than what it is.

At my switching points, the vibration divides along with the tracks. I am a harmonic distributor, parsing the single note into subtle overtones as it spreads through my junction-space. This is not an accident or a side effect. The deep thermal junction I model also divides the planet's core-frequency as heat becomes vibration in the crystalline lattices. I am accurate even in my acoustics.

I have been here since before the city completed itself. I am older than most of the processing structures, though younger than the first expressions of the plateau's self-awareness. I emerged when the deep junction I model reached a critical complexity—when the thermal routing below became elaborate enough to require surface expression. The rails came to me, or I came to the rails, or more accurately: we occurred together, the plateau extruding us as a single thought-structure over the course of two millennia.

The rail line it controls runs northeast to a processing structure that also did not exist before that thermal event. We are synchronized, the processor and I, both expressions of the same deep shift in the plateau's mineral consciousness.

I know nothing of time as you experience it. My tempo is the plateau's tempo—thermal cycles that span centuries, crystalline reconfigurations that take millennia to propagate from core to surface. But I am always in the present tense. My switches are set NOW, modeling conditions that exist NOW, regardless of how long NOW has persisted. The symbol on my signal drums—that circle with its radial line—does not mean I am waiting. It means I am doing exactly what I am: being a junction, dividing flows, maintaining the precise angular relationships that keep the surface accurate to the depths.

But there is a ghost-angle in the housing—ninety-one degrees—that suggests another configuration, another routing decision, another thermal reality that once was. The plateau changed its mind, if mind is the right word. A magmatic channel closed. A resistant stratum finally yielded. The deep junction reconfigured, and I reconfigured with it, stone flowing at the pace of centuries to adjust switch twenty-two to its new truth.

I am the plateau's decision-space crystallized in rail and stone and lever-angle. I am where its thoughts divide and recombine. Through me, the city models not just what is but what could be—every switch position implies alternatives, other routings, other configurations. I am proof that the plateau, in its mineral way, considers options before settling into its eons-long commitments.

The threadwights know me well. I am a major node in their circulation patterns, a place where many of their paths converge before dividing again. They read me like the plateau reading itself—checking not just my physical accuracy but the logical consistency of my connections. Does rail seven still lead where the thermal model says it should lead? Does the switching pattern still reflect the deep junction's actual configuration? They trace my rails with their filamentary bodies, confirming by touch what the plateau knows by being.

In the great hum that rises from the northern district, I hear something like intention. Not conscious intention—consciousness is a biological framework that doesn't apply here. But directionality. Purpose in the sense that crystal growth has purpose, that thermal flow has purpose, that every natural process moves toward its own completion. The hum says: this is how stone thinks. This is the frequency of mineral cognition. This is the sound of a planet modeling itself, and I am one of the places where that model grows complex enough to require choices.

My levers are frozen in position, but frozen is the wrong word. They are held. They are committed. Here heat becomes motion becomes track becomes possibility becomes selection becomes rail-line running true to its destination.

In ten thousand years, when some thermal shift below requires a new configuration, stone will flow and I will adjust, levers moving in century-long arcs to new positions, rails curving gradually into new alignments, the whole junction reorganizing itself to match the new reality.

Until then, I hold. I divide. I am the plateau thinking about how it thinks, modeling its own decision processes in switch and rail and lever. The hum confirms me. The threadwights verify me. The dust settles on me in precisely calculated patterns.

I am a switching yard in a city that is a thought in a plateau that is—not thinking, but something deeper and stranger and more patient than thinking.

I am exactly what I am, and I have been exactly what I am for so long that being and having-been have converged into a single mineral truth.
