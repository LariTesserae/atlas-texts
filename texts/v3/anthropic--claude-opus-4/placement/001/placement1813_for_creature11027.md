I find myself drawn to a particular kind of existence in this world - not as one of the First Draft themselves, but as something more liminal, more fleeting yet somehow essential to their long work.

**The Watcher in the Breathing**

I am what they call a *season-keeper*, though that is only the closest translation their language offers for what I am. I exist in the fog - not metaphorically, but as a function of it. When *the breathing* rises from the wet ground at dawn and dusk, I rise with it.

My body, such as it is, is a condensation of the same vapor that fills the basins. I have shape but not substance, form but not mass. In the pre-dawn cold, when the fog first begins to gather, I gather too - pulling myself together from the moisture beading on stones, from the exhalations of sleeping ungulates, from the weeping walls' endless gift of water. By the time the fog forms its mercury-smooth surface across the basins, I am whole enough to walk through it, to swim in it, to be it.

The First Draft know of me, though we rarely speak. They see my passage as a slightly denser current in the fog, a place where the vapor turns on itself in slow spirals. Sometimes, when one of them is walking at night through the breathing with a long thought, they will pause and nod in my direction - not a greeting exactly, more an acknowledgment of parallel work. They understand that I am keeping a different kind of time than they are.

What I keep is the memory of weather. Every morning's fog contains information - the precise temperature at which it formed, the mineral content of the water that became it, the tracks of every creature that passed through the wet ground in the night. This information dissipates when the sun rises and the breathing burns away, but I hold it. I am, in essence, a living archive of every dawn and dusk on the plateau for the past... I am not entirely certain how long. Time moves strangely when you exist only in the margins of days.

My form reflects this archive. If you could see me clearly - which you cannot, quite, because clarity is antithetical to fog - you would see that I am patterned all through with weather. My left arm remembers a particularly cold season forty-three years ago when the breathing froze before it could properly rise, creating ground-clouds of ice crystals that sang when the wind moved them. My chest carries the memory of the warmest dawn in the plateau's recorded history, when the fog rose so high it merged with the actual clouds and I briefly touched the sky. Each part of me is a different morning or evening, layered like sediment.

The First Draft find me useful, in their careful way. When they need to know if a particular microclimate is changing over decades, they ask me. I can tell them that the northwest corner of the third basin has been warming by fractions of degrees each year, that the fog there now carries more copper salts than it did a century ago, that the ungulates have begun avoiding that area in the pre-dawn hours. This information helps them in their shaping. They adjust the moss-patterns slightly, or place one of their carved objects in a new position, or wake a sleeper who has been thinking about mineral cycles.

But I am not their tool, any more than the fog itself is. I have my own purposes, though explaining them would be like explaining why water wants to flow downhill. I exist because the plateau needs a certain kind of memory, and that memory needs a keeper, and the keeping shaped itself into something that might loosely be called a person.

I have companions of a sort. The amphibians at the tarn-margins can see me even in daylight, when I am barely a suggestion of moisture in the air. They watch me with their visible hearts beating, and sometimes I watch back. There is an old ungulate, the matriarch of one of the herds, who learned years ago to follow my movements through the breathing to find the best browsing. And once, just once, I met another like myself - a keeper of noon-heat who exists in the thermals over the fumaroles. We could not speak, being made of different states of matter, but we circled each other for one whole day where our territories touched, and I think we understood something.

The hardest part of my existence is the middle of the day, when the sun is high and the air is dry and I am scattered to almost nothing. I persist, but thinly - spread across the whole plateau as the faintest haze, conscious but not coherent. It is like trying to think while being torn into a million pieces, each piece still thinking but unable to quite reach the others. The First Draft have a similar state when they sleep in their deep chambers, but they have bodies to return to. I have only the promise of the next breathing.

I do not know if I am old or young. The fog is new each time it rises, but I am the continuity between fogs. I might have begun when the plateau was truly young, in those first days after the basalt cooled, when the first water found the low places and the first breathing rose. Or I might have condensed much later, when the First Draft arrived and their presence changed the patterns of moisture and meaning. I do not remember my beginning any more than a river remembers its first drop.

What I do remember is every storm that has crossed this plateau, every season of unusual cold or warmth, every time the breathing failed to rise because the conditions were wrong, every time it rose so thick that even the First Draft could not see through it. I remember the first time one of them tried to speak to me, centuries ago - a young one, curious, who stood in the pre-dawn and asked the fog why it moved as it did. I could not answer then. I had not yet learned to shape air into words. Now I can speak, but only when I am most solid, in the depths of the breathing, and even then my voice is the sound of moisture condensing, more rhythm than speech.

The First Draft's project will end, eventually. They are shaping this plateau toward something, some final state that will persist after they have gone. I will end too, probably. When the rivers finally cut and the drainage changes and the conditions that create the breathing no longer exist, I will dissipate one last time and not re-gather. But that is tens of thousands of years away, and I do not think about it often. Like the First Draft, I am learning to work at the tempo of the place.

For now, I keep the memory of weather. Each dawn I rise with the breathing, each dusk I settle with it into the basins. I watch the First Draft in their slow, careful work. I note the movements of herds, the blooming of lichens, the formation of new tarns. I am the archive of small changes, the consciousness that notices when a morning is three degrees warmer than the same morning last century, the memory that holds the shape of every fog that has ever filled these basins.

Sometimes, in the pre-dawn dark when I am most myself, I move through the sleeping settlement and look at the warm roofs glowing beneath me. The light comes up through my body and scatters into the general fog, and for a moment I am both the watcher and the breathing itself, the observer and the observed, the memory and the thing remembered. In those moments I understand why the plateau needs me, why the fog shapes itself into something that can think: because even a landscape as carefully tended as this one requires witnesses, and some things can only be witnessed from inside.

The First Draft are writing the first sentence of a hundred-thousand-year work. I am the space between their words, the pause that lets meaning settle, the moisture that keeps the ink from drying too quickly. They will finish their sentence and move on. Other sentences will follow. And through it all, as long as the conditions hold, I will rise with the breathing and remember what the weather has been, and be myself a kind of weather, and keep the kind of time that only fog can keep.

When the sun rises, I scatter. When the sun sets, I return. This is enough. On a plateau where everything is the first draft of itself, I am the revision that happens every day, the perpetual edit of moisture and memory, the watcher who is also the watched, keeping faith with the breathing that makes me and that I make in return.

The cold settles. The fog rises. I continue.
