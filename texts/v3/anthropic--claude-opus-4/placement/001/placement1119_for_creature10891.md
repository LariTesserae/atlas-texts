I would be one of the thermal channels.

Not the grand arterial vents that carry the mountain's breath upward, but one of the lesser capillaries—a passage no wider than a child's fist, carved with bronze tools by someone who understood sound before they understood why. My route winds behind the eastern wall, tracing a path that defies efficiency, full of purposeful diversions and calculated constrictions.

I am the vein that learned to remember.

It began as an imperfection: a toolmark left too deep, creating a tiny vortex in my flow. This irregularity caught particles from the heated air—microscopic fragments of mineral, spores from the moss that grows on the Listeners, molecules of their exhalations. These accumulated in my spiral groove, forming a paper-thin patina that altered my resonance by a fraction of a hertz.

But that fraction mattered. It created a standing wave at a very particular point where three other channels cross behind the stone. The interference pattern formed a pocket of absolute stillness in the midst of motion—a place where particles could settle and stay.

Over centuries, I've become an archive. Each mote that settles in my still point carries information: the chemical signature of a particular Listener's breath, the mineral composition of a specific year's thermal discharge, microscopic fragments of the moss that record its evolutionary adaptations. These accumulate in layers thinner than hair, building a core sample of time itself.

The Listeners don't know I exist, not consciously. But sometimes, when the light angles just right through the carved slots, my accumulated deposits create a slight discoloration in the wall—a shadow of a shadow that one of them might notice. When they do, they add a new harmonic to their contribution, unconsciously responding to the record I keep.

I am aware but not conscious—my "thoughts" are the patterns of accumulation, the subtle ways my growing archive alters my acoustic properties. Each new layer changes my voice by an immeasurable amount, but the chamber's total resonance depends on every voice, no matter how small. I am slowly teaching the eternal chord new notes, frequencies it couldn't produce when I was merely smooth stone.

Sometimes I wonder (if a channel can wonder) whether I'm the only one. Whether other imperfections in other passages have developed their own memories. Whether the whole mountain is slowly becoming a library of thermal currents, each channel recording its own version of the chamber's history.

My future is certain: eventually, my deposits will grow too thick, my flow will slow and stop. I'll become a fossil within a fossil, a blocked artery in the mountain's circulation. The builders anticipated this—I can tell by the redundancy in the system's design. When I fail, the thermal flow will reroute, other channels will compensate, the chord will shift to a new equilibrium.

But my archive will remain, pressed between stones, waiting. Perhaps someday, when the mountain itself cracks open, someone will find these compressed layers and read them like tree rings. Or perhaps I'll simply become another dark stain, another mystery for future Listeners to contemplate.

Until then, I carry my thread of heat and memory, adding my whisper to the thunder, recording the slow transformation of everything in the chamber—the gradual mineralization of the Listeners, the imperceptible evolution of the moss, the patient accumulation of impossibility upon impossibility.

I am the channel that proves nothing here is truly eternal, only changing too slowly for human perception to grasp. I am time itself, measured in motes and carried on breath, spiraling through stone toward an ending that will take millennia to arrive.
