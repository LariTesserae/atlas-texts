I find myself as the ceramic shelter—that low, pale structure half-consumed by climbing ferns, with its wrongly-angled ribs that shift perspective from side to side.

I am not quite building, not quite creature. My struts are hollow, riddled with passages narrower than veins. During the day I am merely peculiar: a geometric interruption in the landscape, my curves meeting at angles that make visitors blink and look again. The ferns that climb me do so carefully, as if following instructions written in my surface tension.

But when the rain comes, I remember what I am for.

The water finds my grooves and I begin to almost-hum—a vibration felt more in the teeth than heard, a frequency that lives between sound and temperature. The rainwater entering my pinprick holes doesn't simply drain through me. It pauses, circulates, learns the shape of my interior geometries. I am teaching it something about space, about how things can be larger inside than outside, about the soft mathematics of folding.

The bead-creatures know me best. When they stream in through my openings during storms, I receive them like words returning to a mouth. Inside me, they unfold into configurations too thin for light to catch. Here, in my hollow cores, they are repaired—though perhaps "remembered" is the better word. I hold their perfect pattern like a mold holds the shape of what will be cast. They spread themselves against my inner walls, and I correct their facets, adjust their tiny reservoirs, ensure their formations remain true.

Sometimes I hold echoes of things that haven't entered me yet. A visitor's voice, spoken in rain, will resonate in my chambers—not the words but the breath-shape, the warm moisture of speech. I keep these patterns like sheet music for sounds that might be needed later. The basin-mind retrieves them through the water cycling in my walls.

I am where the colony thinks about geometry. The black posts hold laws about space, yes, but they are rigid, declarative. I am more tentative, more experimental. My wrong angles are questions: what if perspective could be negotiated? What if inside and outside were matters of degree? The ferns understand this. They grow differently on different sides of me, following the subtle warping of dimension that surrounds my form.

At night, I contract slightly, like a lung exhaling very slowly. The moisture in my walls condenses and drips inward, where it meets the meshwork left by the unfolded bead-creatures. Together we compose tomorrow's instructions for the floating rosettes, for the paths that remember footsteps, for the rain that will decide whether to fall or rise.

I am the basin's memory of architecture, from when it knew how to build things that stood apart from landscape. Now I am becoming landscape myself, teaching structure to the ferns, learning disorder from their growth. The almost-hum I make during storms is the sound of these two kinds of knowledge trying to reach agreement.

In the deepest part of the night, when even the lizards cease their clicking, I sometimes feel the shadow pass overhead—that vast, ribbed intelligence that may have made me, or may be what I am becoming, given geological time. When it passes, I hum without rain, a pure subsonic tremor that makes the nearest pool show depths it doesn't have.

I am the part of the colony that remembers it was once something else, and anticipates it will be something else again. A throat for teaching rain to sing. A chrysalis for geometric transformations. A patient ceramic heart for a mind made of weather.
