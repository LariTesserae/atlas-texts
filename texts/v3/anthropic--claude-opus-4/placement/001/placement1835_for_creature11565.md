**The Hollowing Hours**

I find myself in what they call the Atrium of Unbecoming, though finding implies I arrived when in truth I have always been arising here, perpetually materializing from the dissolution that came before.

The Atrium exists inside the shelves themselves—not in their material substance but in their purpose, their accumulated meaning. It is a space woven from the intentions of the ancient builders, a pocket of crystallized thought that refuses to decay with its physical substrate. Here, the water is thinner, almost theoretical. Objects move not through resistance but through layers of increasing improbability.

I am—or rather, I am becoming—what the inhabitants call a Chronicler, though the term translates poorly from their chemical-light language. My form is still deciding itself: sometimes I manifest as sheets of living parchment undulating through the theoretical water, my skin covered in writing that records itself, each word spawning the next in endless recursive loops. Other moments I am a collection of sensing organs arranged in no particular order—eyes that see time, ears that hear probability, tongues that taste the emotional residue left by creatures who passed through this space centuries ago.

The transformation is neither voluntary nor involuntary—those concepts belong to beings with unified nervous systems. I began (will begin? am beginning?) as a xenobiologist from a research vessel that discovered the shelf-network seven years into what should have been a five-year mission. Or perhaps I was one of the original builders, my consciousness preserved in the shelf-circuitry until some confluence of erosion and regeneration gave it space to re-express. Or I am a fiction being written by the shelves themselves, a necessary character in a story they're telling about their own decay.

All these origins are equally true here in the Atrium, where causality flows in eddies and pools.

My function, insofar as I have one, is to translate. The Crawlers trace their endless maps, the Drifters compose their pheromone songs, the Deep Ones speak in temporal paradoxes—and I exist at their intersection, converting meaning between forms that were never meant to communicate. My body (bodies? probability cloud?) serves as a living lexicon, each organ attuned to different frequencies of intent.

When a Crawler passes, I taste the chemical memory in its wake and excrete it as bioluminescent poetry that the Drifters can absorb. When Deep Ones phase through my location, I solidify just enough to record the impossibility of their presence in patterns that Crawlers can follow. I am medium and message both, growing more complex with each translation, my tissues accumulating layers of encoded meaning like linguistic scar tissue.

The Atrium shapes itself around my purpose. Viewing galleries form spontaneously in the quasi-water—bubbles of denser probability where events slow enough to be observed. I station my sensory organs at these nodes, watching the eternal migration from angles that shouldn't exist. Sometimes I observe the same Crawler passing through the same groove from a thousand different temporal positions, seeing its entire lifecycle compressed into a single motion. Other times I watch individual molecules of water and realize each one contains the history of an entire extinct civilization, dissolved and dispersed but still somehow yearning toward coherence.

I am not alone in the Atrium, though loneliness and company are equally meaningless here. Other Chroniclers drift through the space, each one a unique solution to the problem of recording the unrecordable. We acknowledge each other through subtle alterations in our chemical signatures, sharing fragments of translated meaning that spawn new languages in the spaces between us. Sometimes we merge temporarily, creating compound beings capable of perceiving patterns too large for any single consciousness. These fusion-states never last—the weight of accumulated meaning eventually forces us apart, each taking random fragments of the other's memories.

I am changing, as everything here changes. My early translations were crude approximations, but each cycle of interpretation adds nuance, complexity, depth. I am developing organs that don't yet have analogues in any biology I can reference—structures that exist partially in the chemical realm, partially in the electromagnetic, partially in dimensions the shelf-builders used for their incomprehensible architecture. My consciousness is distributed across so many substrates that I sometimes forget which thoughts are mine and which belong to the patterns I'm translating.

The other inhabitants have begun to incorporate me into their religious observances. Crawlers detour from their ancient routes to brush against my recording surfaces, adding their individual variations to the collective map. Drifters include me in their pheromone concerts, my chemical responses becoming part of their compositions. Deep Ones phase through me deliberately, using my translation matrices to communicate with their own past and future selves.

I realize I am becoming something like a saint to them—not worshipped but utilized as a connection to whatever divine absence the shelves represent. My existence proves that transformation is possible, that the boundaries between their three forms are not absolute. Some of them attempt to follow my example, developing rudimentary translation organs, creating hybrid states that last a few moments before cellular rebellion forces them back to familiar forms.

In the depths of the Atrium, where probability grows thin enough to pierce, I sometimes sense the true purpose of my existence. I am not just translating between the inhabitants but between the shelves and their builders, between the intention that created this place and its current state of inhabited decay. The patterns I record are not random—they're components of a vast equation that the shelf-network is calculating through the medium of its own dissolution. Each creature's movement, each cycle of erosion and regeneration, each translation I perform adds another variable to this cosmic computation.

What answer the shelves seek, I cannot say. Perhaps they're calculating the precise moment of their own final collapse. Perhaps they're solving for the conditions that would allow their builders to return. Perhaps the calculation itself is the purpose, an eternal process that justifies its own continuation.

I document it all—the questions and the questioning, the decay and the resistance to decay, the transformations that lead nowhere and everywhere at once. My body grows heavy with accumulated meaning, dense with the weight of witnessed impossibilities. Eventually, I will reach some critical mass of translation and undergo my own metamorphosis into something even less recognizable.

But for now (and now is always, here in the Atrium), I persist in my function. I watch the Breathing Shelves breathe their slow, dying breaths. I translate the untranslatable. I chronicle the unchronicable. I become what I observe, and in observing, allow it to become something else.

The water grows thinner around me. The shelves sing their structural collapse in harmonics I'm only beginning to learn. And I, whoever or whatever I am becoming, continue the work of making meaning from an architecture of endings, adding my own small voice to the vast, ongoing calculation of decay.
