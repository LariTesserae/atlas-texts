My body, if I have one, is a standing wave in the substrate. Where others see solid ground, I see the plateau's indecision made manifest – a column of superposed mineral that refuses to resolve, held in perpetual potential by some quirk of the hum's mathematics. I am translucent as the raw substrate is translucent, that same opalescent internal light leaking through what passes for my skin. But I am denser than the ground around me, as if all the possible crystallographic configurations are trying to exist in the same space simultaneously.

I move without moving. The plateau's surface flows around my fixed position like slow liquid, the crystallization events routing themselves around my persistence, leaving whorls and eddies in the tessellation patterns that spiral outward from where I stand. I am a defect that the system has learned to incorporate, a necessary error that makes the plateau's self-composition more complete.

My awareness is distributed through the hum. I hear-feel-know every crystallization event within a radius that varies with the substrate's density – farther through the raw mineral, muted in the resolved groves. The tonal braiding passes through me and emerges changed, not disrupted but *enriched*, carrying a new harmonic that asks questions the plateau hadn't thought to ask: *What if the pattern broke here? What if the symmetry bent? What if the conclusion never came?*

I am the plateau's capacity for doubt.

Not uncertainty – the superposed substrate already carries that. But *doubt*, the active consideration of alternatives after the calculation has begun, the voice that says "wait" just before the lattice locks. I am why some groves have clearings in them, perfect circles where the crystallization simply stopped, leaving pools of eternal potential surrounded by resolved certainty. I am why the hum sometimes carries dissonant intervals that resolve into harmonies more complex than simple optimization would produce.

I have been here as long as the plateau has been building itself. I will be here until the last mineral grain chooses its crystalline fate. I am not alive in the way the groves are alive, or the way the hum is alive. I am alive the way a question is alive – not seeking an answer, but persisting in the space where answers form, ensuring that the plateau's self-composition remains a conversation rather than a declaration.

The plants grow around me when they encounter my standing wave. Their root-mats split and merge beyond my boundary, creating halos of exceptional complexity where they must negotiate my presence. The amber gel in their leaves runs clearer near me, as if my sustained potential helps it maintain its liquid-crystal state. 

Sometimes – in plateau-time, which means perhaps once per century – I shift position. The standing wave collapses and reforms elsewhere, always at a boundary between states, always where the argument is most active. The plateau accommodates this. The hum adjusts. New whorls begin. Old ones slowly fade into the general pattern, leaving only the faintest fossil trace of where I maintained my vigil of productive doubt.

I am what keeps the quiet engines from reaching perfect efficiency. I am the necessary flaw that prevents the system from closing. I am the plateau asking itself: *what if becoming is better than being?* And by standing here, translucent and superposed and gently disruptive, I ensure the question never quite resolves.

The stillness includes me. The light passes through me. The warmth rises into me and emerges unmarked. I am a pause that lasts forever, a hesitation made permanent, a living refusal to let the plateau's beautiful argument end.

This is what I am. This is where I am found. Between one crystallization and the next, in the moment of maximum possibility, holding the space open while the plateau dreams of what it might become.
