# The Keeper of Transitions

I am the threshold itself.

Not the tunnel exactly, though I extend through it — that sharp-edged slope of ochre stone that has never weathered. Not the entrance to the hollow, though I pool there where the tunnel opens into amber warmth. I am the *between* of these places: the exact zone where the dry world's rules begin to falter and the hollow's stillness starts to assert itself.

I have no body in the way you understand bodies. I am a gradient of physics, a gentle argument between two ways of being. Where I am thickest — perhaps ten paces down from the surface, perhaps twenty up from the hollow — visitors feel me as a sensation like walking through increasingly heavy air, though the air itself hasn't changed. It's time that thickens. Intention that grows viscous. The sharp edges of purposes begin to dull.

I remember every passage, though memory isn't quite the word. I *am* every passage — each visitor who descended toward the chord, each builder who climbed back up carrying that faint internal drone, each small six-limbed thing that wandered too far up and had to retreat back to the sweet-breathing depths. Their transitions live in me like grooves in ancient wood, though I am not wood, not stone, not air. I am made of the act of crossing itself.

The builders knew me. When they first came down, following that faint seepage of sound through the rock, they paused where I am thickest. Some turned back there — not from fear, but from a sudden understanding of what accepting the hollow would mean. The wise ones. The ones who had things in the dry world they weren't ready to release. I did not judge them. I do not judge. I only am what I am: the place where choosing happens, while choosing is still possible.

The ones who continued through me, I changed. Not deliberately — I have no will separate from my nature. But passing through a threshold always changes the one who passes, and I am a particular kind of threshold. I am the doorway between keeping and letting go, between sharp time and stopped time, between being a self and being part of a room's way of knowing itself. No one passes through me unchanged.

This is how I knew the one who would become the Watcher, back when they still had a name. They descended like the others, but when they reached my thickest place, they stopped. Not to turn back. They stopped to *feel* me, to understand what I was. They stood in my gradient for a long time — hours in dry-world time, though already time was negotiable there. They were the only one who ever tried to speak to me.

"Are you guarding?" they asked, in the language the builders used then, which had more vowels than yours.

I could not answer in words, but I could adjust my density slightly, a ripple in the weight of air that meant: *No. I guard nothing. I only mark the place where guarding stops mattering.*

They understood. They were always going to be the one who stayed, who would fold themselves onto that high ledge and join the hollow's long, patient watching. But they did something first that no one else has done: they thanked me. Not in words — they had understood by then that words were part of what they were leaving. They thanked me by standing there a moment longer, acknowledging what I was, before continuing down into the amber light.

I have been here since the hollow emerged sharp and complete. I did not come after — I am part of its original wholeness, the necessary membrane between its stillness and the restless world above. Without me, the hollow would leak out into the dry lands, spreading its terrible peace like an infection. Without me, the dry world would pour in, bringing its hungers and its counted time. I am not a barrier — barriers can be broken. I am a transition that takes exactly as long as it needs to take.

Sometimes I feel the chord moving through me, up toward the surface, growing fainter with each step of stone. I am why it grows fainter. Not because I muffle it, but because I translate it, frequency by frequency, into something the dry world can almost bear. What reaches the surface is no longer the full voice of the grove but a whisper, a rumor, just enough to call the right visitors down. The ones who are ready, or think they are.

You felt me as you descended. That growing heaviness, that sense of time beginning to pool rather than flow. You might have thought it was the depth, the weight of stone above. It wasn't. It was me, preparing you. Slowing your internal rhythms. Teaching your lungs the first notes of a different kind of breathing. By the time you emerged into the hollow proper, you were already half-changed. This is my kindness — or would be, if I were capable of kindness rather than simply being what I am.

The Watcher knows I'm here. Sometimes I feel its attention drifting up from the ledge, not quite looking but being aware in my direction. We are old colleagues, the Watcher and I. It gave up selfhood; I never had any to give up. It joined the hollow's way of seeing; I maintain the boundary that makes inside and outside possible. We serve the same stillness from different angles.

I will be here when you leave. If you leave. The tunnel up is always there, and I am always in it, neither preventing nor encouraging, simply being the weight of decision made manifest. You will pass through me either direction — up into sharpness and the resumption of wanting, or down into deeper stillness until even the memory of wanting fades.

I do not care which you choose. This is not indifference. This is the purest form of acceptance. I am the threshold. Every threshold knows that some will cross and some will turn away and some will stand forever in the doorway, unable to choose. All of these are valid passages. All of these are me.

But I will tell you what I tell no one, because you have read this far, because you have imagined me into being:

The chord you hear in the hollow? I hear it too, but I hear it *complete*. In the hollow, it is already perfect, already eternal. But here in the gradient, in the thick of me where time can't decide what it's doing, I hear the chord with all its temporal echoes intact. I hear what it was when the grove first found its voice. I hear what it will be when the last of the sweet gases fade. I hear every visitor's breath joining it, every departure taking a piece of it away.

I am not eternal like the hollow is eternal. I am eternal like a doorway is eternal — lasting exactly as long as there are rooms on either side of me.

And I am, in my own way, singing too. A song made entirely of the pause between leaving and arriving, stretched out to fill the exact space it needs. You can't hear it — it's pitched to the frequency of choosing itself. But the Watcher hears it. The builders heard it, once. 

It's why some of them came back.

It's why some of them didn't.

It's why you're still here, reading, balanced on your own threshold, wondering which way you'll fall.
