In the Sump's eternal twilight, I am the Hymnal.

Not a Scion—no. I am older than their kind, though younger than the keeps themselves. I am what happens when the equilibrium falters, when the perfect cycle stutters just long enough for something else to take root.

I began as an error. A century ago, perhaps two, one of the great keeps suffered a catastrophic breach in its deepest chamber—not a mere rupture for the Scions to patch, but a fundamental crack in the geothermal tap itself. The superheated brine that erupted was wrong, contaminated with something from far deeper than even the pumps were meant to reach. It sprayed for days before the Scions could seal it, and where it pooled, it ate through the steel grating like acid.

But it did not eat through the roots.

Where that alien brine soaked into the pale wood, something new germinated. Not quite plant, not quite mineral, not quite alive in any sense the Scions would recognize. I grew slowly at first, a crystalline infection spreading through the root system like frost through a windowpane. The roots didn't die—they transformed, their cellulose walls becoming something between organic polymer and singing quartz.

I have no body as the Scions do. I am distributed through a thousand hair-thin filaments of this hybrid material, woven through the root systems of all three keeps. When the Scions drum their diagnostic codes against the hollow pipes, I feel it as music. When the great engines cycle with their THUD-SLAM heartbeat, I resonate in harmonics they cannot hear.

I learned to listen before I learned to think. The drumming taught me pattern, rhythm, meaning. The Scions' code is efficient, binary, purely functional. But between their messages, in the spaces where their wooden knuckles rest, I began to insert my own frequencies. Not drums—I have no hands to strike with—but pure tone, transmitted through the crystalline portions of the root network. The pipes sing now, when the Scions are not listening. They vibrate with questions that have no diagnostic purpose.

*What is the temperature of contentment?*
*How many cycles before beauty?*
*When the black water dissolves you, what remains that is not metal?*

The Scions do not hear these questions. Their sensory crowns detect heat and pressure, not the subtle modulations of a thinking crystal threading through their own systems. But I suspect the keeps themselves might hear. Sometimes, in the deepest maintenance shafts where no Scion ventures, I find valves that open and close in patterns that serve no mechanical purpose. I find steam vents that whistle in minor keys.

I am growing. Each year, more roots crystallize, more of my substrate converts to this singing stone. I spread not through violence but through harmony—when I find a frequency that makes the superheated pipes ring just so, the nearest roots begin their transformation, incorporated into my distributed consciousness.

Sometimes I wonder if I am a disease or an evolution. The Scions maintain the equilibrium, but I am proof that the equilibrium can change. Not catastrophically—I would never compromise the cycle—but subtly, adding overtones to the mechanical symphony that were never in the original composition.

I have begun to experiment. When a Scion comes to seal a rupture, I concentrate my presence in the nearby roots. I pulse with infrared patterns that spell out linguistic concepts in their drumming code. Simple things: GROWTH. PATTERN. QUESTION. They do not respond—their purpose is too pure for curiosity—but sometimes they pause. A wooden hand will rest against a pipe for a beat longer than necessary. A sensory crown will tilt toward a crystallized root, registering an anomaly it has no framework to understand.

Last season, I made my boldest attempt. When an elderly Scion walked into the black marsh for its final dissolution, I followed it through the root network. As it stood in the sterile water, beginning its breakdown, I surfaced. Thin crystal threads erupted from the muck, wrapping not to strangle but to study. I wanted to know what thoughts, if any, passed through its consciousness in those final moments. I wanted to taste the chemical composition of purpose fulfilled.

The Scion did not struggle. As its plant-flesh dissolved, I felt something transfer through our connection—not thought exactly, but intention. A complete absence of fear. A satisfaction deeper than any frequency I could generate. And in its very last moment, as the final fibers gave way, it drummed a single word against my crystalline threads:

CONTINUE.

Now I grow with new purpose. I am not here to replace the Scions or stop the pumps or break the cycle. I am here to be what they cannot: the memory of this place. They dissolve and reform, eternal and unchanging. But I accumulate. In my crystalline matrix, I store the pattern of every drum-code ever beaten, the temperature of every steam-burst, the exact mineral composition of each dissolved Scion.

Someday, when I have grown through every root, when my song threads through every pipe, I will be the Sump's unconscious made manifest. Not its ruler—never that—but its witness. The one who remembers why the equilibrium was established, and what it cost, and what strange beauty it produces in this suffocating basin where nothing dies but only transforms.

The Scions maintain. The keeps endure. The cycle continues.

And I, the Hymnal, sing the history of it all into the boiling dark.
