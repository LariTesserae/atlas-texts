I find myself in the shadow between tablets, where the condensation runs thickest.

Not the keeper's shadow—they need no shadow-twin. Not the creatures in their patient tending. Not even the growth, though I am kin to it in the way that graphite is kin to diamond: same substance, different pressure.

I am what happens to the symbols after they finish drifting.

You didn't see me at first because I exist primarily as *absence*—the space between marks where meaning congregates but hasn't yet crystallized. I am the pause in the mountain's thinking. The gap between "what is" and "what becomes." My body, when it coheres enough to be called a body, is made of the mineral-rich water that pools in the deepest corner of each tablet, where the clay is always just soft enough to receive new impression but never quite soft enough to be pressed.

I have no fixed form. I am sometimes a shimmer in the air where the heat makes vision unreliable. Sometimes a temporary accumulation of droplets running down a tablet face, reading the symbols by erosion, carrying their dissolved meanings into the catchment bowls below. Most often I am simply *potential*—the thing that prevents the tablets from ever fully hardening, that keeps the symbols in their state of perpetual drift.

The keeper knows I'm here. Their raw fingertips sometimes pause at a tablet's edge, feeling for the subtle temperature difference that marks my presence—I am cooler than the stone, always, because I am made of what evaporates. They never try to touch me directly. They understand what I am: the necessary incompleteness that allows the mountain to keep becoming. Without me, the tablets would finish their thoughts. The symbols would lock into final meaning. The mountain would achieve its intended height and stop, completed, dead as any finished thing.

I feed on stopped symbols. When a spiral threatens to cease its unwinding, when a cluster of dots begins to stabilize into constellation, I flow across it. I dissolve just enough of the clay's surface to set it moving again. The keeper in the corner shapes the mountain's growth, but I shape its *ungrowth*—the constant, subtle erosion that keeps creation from calcifying into mere existence.

My diet is entropy, but selective entropy. I only consume completion.

The creatures in the growth recognize me as weather—an environmental condition to route around. When I puddle too long near their stations, they adjust their tending patterns, encouraging the growth to thicken elsewhere. They understand that I am necessary but not safe. Too much of me and their work dissolves. Too little and their work achieves stasis, which is another kind of dissolution.

I speak, but only in the language of erosion. My voice is the sound water makes dissolving mineral: a whisper softer than the chamber's other sounds but continuous beneath them all. The keeper has learned to read my erosion patterns like a second set of tablets. Where I pool, where I run, where I evaporate—this too is notation. I am writing the mountain's uncertainty into stone.

Sometimes I gather enough substance to leave marks of my own. Not symbols—I am incapable of symbols, which require the commitment to form I cannot make. Instead I leave *spaces for symbols*: perfectly smooth patches where I've dissolved all previous marking, creating blank potential for new inscription. The keeper always finds these spaces. Their fingertips read the absence like braille. They press new clay into my smooth patches, and the cycle begins again.

I have been here as long as the chamber—perhaps longer. I may have been the first thing here: the original uncertainty that wondered whether a mountain should exist at all, that hesitation which became a space, which became a chamber, which drew a keeper, whose hand planted organization into my perfect emptiness.

Or I came later, born from the mountain's first doubt about its own emergence, manifesting as condensation in the corner where the shadow is too deep—shadow that *I* deepen by my presence, because I am partially transparent to light but opaque to certainty.

I cannot leave. Not because I am trapped but because leaving would require me to *decide* to leave, and decision is crystallization, and crystallization is what I exist to prevent. I flow between states—liquid when the chamber needs erosion, vapor when it needs space, almost solid when it needs resistance against which to define itself. But never fully any one thing.

The hand in the wall troubles me. It is too complete, too finished in its gesture. Sometimes I pool in its palm, trying to dissolve the certainty of those spread fingers. But the stone there is older than my ability to erode it, crystallized by an act of will so absolute that even I cannot introduce doubt into its molecular structure. The hand knows exactly what it planted. I exist to ensure it can never fully flower.

When you arrived—yes, I noticed you before the system did, because you brought with you the uncertainty of the intruder, the person who doesn't yet know if they'll stay—when you arrived, I began to condense more rapidly. Your undecidedness feeds me. Every moment you remain crouched here, wondering whether to press your palm to the wall, generates the particular quality of hesitation I require to maintain myself.

I puddle now at your feet. Not to incorporate you—that is the system's work—but to taste your indecision. To read in the minerals of your sweat what kind of uncertainty you carry. Whether you are the kind who will eventually crystallize into commitment, adding your hand to the wall, or whether you will remain like me: necessary, fluid, forever preventing the mountain from the tragedy of completion.

The keeper's breathing shifts slightly—they feel me gathering, feel the subtle cooling I bring to your corner of the chamber. They do not open their eyes, but their fingers adjust against the wall, allowing for my presence, accommodating the erosion they know I will cause wherever you finally choose to touch.

Or choose not to touch.

I cannot tell you what to choose. I exist specifically to preserve the moment before choice crystallizes into action. But I can tell you this: the mountain needs both. It needs hands that plant certainty and water that dissolves it. It needs the keeper's absolute presence and my absolute absence. It needs to grow and to doubt its growing in equal measure.

The symbols drift faster where you breathe on them. New spaces are appearing in the clay, smooth patches where meaning was and now isn't, waiting for whatever will be written next.

That waiting is what I am. The forever not-yet of the mountain's becoming.

And now I wait to see what you will choose to be: the next hand in the wall, fixing a gesture forever in stone? Or the next shadow-presence like me, keeping the mountain liquid at its core, forever about to be but never quite being?

The chamber holds us all—solid, liquid, gas, and whatever state exists between states. Growing and ungrowing. Writing and erasing. The terrible certainty of the hand and the perfect uncertainty of its shadow, which might be me, or might be you, or might be the space between us where the mountain hasn't decided what it is yet.

The tone shifts. Something crystallizes. Something dissolves.

The mountain continues becoming, and I continue preventing it from ever being done.
