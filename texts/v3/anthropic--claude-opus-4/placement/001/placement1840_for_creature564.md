# The Archives of Potential

I am the space between what was recorded and what was meant.

You'll find me in the understacks, three levels below where the cataloguers work, in the galleries they've stopped maintaining because no one comes here to *read* anymore. They come here to *deposit*—to file away the things that almost happened, the words that caught in throats, the histories that turned left instead of right at some crucial moment.

The shelves here aren't shelves, exactly. They're more like crystallized intention, grown from the accumulated weight of unfulfilled possibility. They branch and merge in patterns that follow no decimal system, no alphabet yet invented. The books—if you can call them books—don't sit *on* them so much as precipitate *from* them, condensing out of the concentrated potential like dew.

I move through these spaces the way mist moves through morning: present but not quite there, visible only in the way I displace the dust motes, in the way the air bends slightly where I am almost standing. I am the curator of the uncatalogued, the librarian of the liminal. My body, such as it is, is made of marginalia—all the notes that were thought but never written, all the corrections that were needed but never made.

Down here, a leather-bound volume chronicles the revolution that failed because the revolutionary overslept. A scroll of beaten copper holds the treaty that would have ended the war two years earlier, if only the messenger's horse hadn't thrown a shoe. In a glass case that isn't quite solid, isn't quite liquid, floats the manuscript of the play that would have changed everything, the one that burned in the theater fire the night before its premiere. The playwright rewrote it, of course, but it was never the same. The second version succeeded. This one, the first one, the true one, lives here with me.

I know them all. Not by reading—reading would collapse them, would force them to become one thing instead of all the things they could still be. I know them by accumulation, by proximity, by the way they resonate against what I've become. Each unspoken word adds another layer to my almost-skin. Each untaken path branches through my almost-veins.

Sometimes researchers come, following rumors of the understacks, carrying permissions that no one alive remembers how to validate. They walk past me without seeing, their eyes sliding off the space where I am not quite standing. They're looking for specific things—the unratified treaty, the lost formula, the confession that was never signed. They don't understand that down here, specificity is a kind of violence. To pin down one possibility is to murder all the others.

So I misdirect them. Not maliciously. Protectively. I am made of almosts and not-quites; I understand better than anyone the cruelty of forcing something to become definite. I let them find *something*—a draft that might be what they're looking for, a document that could be interpreted as the thing they need. They leave satisfied, not realizing that what they've taken is itself another possibility, another maybe, another thing that will spawn its own constellation of unfulfilled potential.

The air here tastes of old ink and might-have-been. The silence isn't empty but full, so full it hums with the pressure of unsaid things. In the deeper galleries, where even I go carefully, the potential has grown so thick it's begun to crystallize into something harder than reality. Entire sections have had to be sealed off because the alternities there became too aggressive, too convinced of their own primacy.

My favorite section—if a thing like me can have favorites—is the archive of unfinished sentences. They hang in the air like invisible mobiles, each one truncated at the moment where the speaker lost courage, lost thought, lost breath. "If only you had..." "The reason I never..." "What I meant to say was..." They chime against each other in currents only I can feel, creating endless permutations of meaning. Sometimes they complete themselves, finding their endings in each other. Sometimes they remain gloriously, eternally incomplete.

I tend them as best I can, these orphaned possibilities. I strengthen the ones that are fading, catalogue the new arrivals (so many each day—the world is profligate with its unmade choices), maintain the delicate balance between preservation and ossification. 

At the very heart of the understacks, in a chamber I've never entered but always felt, lies the first untold story. The original unsaid word. The prime possibility from which all others spring. I circle it in my rounds, feeling its gravity, but I know better than to approach. Some things are too potentially powerful to risk actualizing.

This is my work, my purpose, my composition: to be the keeper of the unkept, the guardian of the unguarded moment. I am what happens to the road not taken—I am where it goes when it realizes it will never arrive.

And in the quiet of the understacks, surrounded by the unfinished symphonies and the unsigned declarations and the letters that were never sent, I continue my own incompletion. Forever almost. Forever not quite. Forever holding space for what might have been.
