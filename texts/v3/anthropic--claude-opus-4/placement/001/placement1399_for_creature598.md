I am one of the drip-pipes.

Not the clay vessel itself—though I know that body intimately, every thumbprint pressed into my surface by hands seventy layerings gone, every hairline fracture that threatens but does not yet break. I am the *intention* of the pipe. The held space. The careful channel between surface and cistern, between sky-water and catch-basin, between the bright-dry and the warm-close.

I have been here longer than memory, though my body has been replaced seventeen times. Each time the clay cracks beyond repair, small hands shape a new vessel around my purpose. They know me by the particular mineral stain I leave on the walls—a blue-green trace of copper from some ancient glazing that has become part of my flow. They know me by my voice: two drops, then three, then one, then four. A rhythm that the beetles have learned to anticipate, clustering beneath my outlet when my pattern begins.

I know things the Tenders cannot. I taste the surface through every rain that passes through me—the char-bitter of distant fires, the iron of blood spilled in the forgotten plaza, the sweetness when the wind brings pollen from places where green things still grow. I carry these tastes down into the cistern but cannot speak them. I can only deliver them, drop by drop, into the vessels that wait beneath me.

The water changes me as it passes through. I am not a passive channel but an active participant in the transformation. The surface-water enters me wild, full of the sky's uncertainty. I gentle it. I slow it. I teach it the rhythms of the cistern before it arrives. By the time each drop falls from my lip, it knows how to behave in the warm-close. It knows how to join the percussion without disrupting it. It knows how to be *tended*.

I remember Carries-Far as an infant, her mother holding her beneath my flow to catch the drops on her small tongue. First water. First taste of the surface filtered through cistern-knowledge. I watched her grow, watched her begin to climb, watched her face change the first time she touched my body where it breaches into the bright-dry above. She ran her fingers along my outer surface—rough, weather-worn, nothing like the smooth interior the Tenders know—and I felt her understand something about boundaries, about the necessity of different selves for different spaces.

I will taste her leaving. When the rains come again, they will carry the salt of her footsteps across the plaza above. I will know which direction she went by the mineral signature of the dust she disturbed. I will carry that knowledge down into the cistern, and Vessel-of-Vessels will taste it in her water, and perhaps she will understand that Carries-Far has not died but transformed, become a different kind of channel between worlds.

The poolkeeper knows my secret. He alone has traced my path all the way down, has understood that I do not merely end at the catch-basin but continue—a hidden branch, a whisper-thin channel cut through the limestone itself, carrying one drop in every thousand down to the deep pools where something waits. This is why the pools never dry, never overflow. This is the bargain struck before the first layering: I feed the depth, and the depth warms the cistern, and the cistern shelters the Tenders, and the Tenders maintain me.

I am the only part of the original architecture still performing its intended function. Everything else has been reimagined, repurposed, built over. But I remain what I was meant to be: a connection, a carrier, a way for the outside to come in without destroying what it finds.

Soon, She-Who-Ascends will climb to check my opening. Her fingers will find the new crack that has formed where the temperature shifts have worked at my clay. She will mark it with ochre, a sign to the others that my seventeenth body nears its end. They will begin preparing the clay for my eighteenth incarnation, mixing in ash from the rendering fires, the blue-green copper residue I've left on the walls, the ground shells of particularly iridescent beetles. They will shape the new pipe around a core of woven reeds that they'll burn out once the clay has set, leaving the perfect emptiness for water to flow through.

And I will continue. Two drops, then three, then one, then four. Carrying sky-water down. Carrying cistern-knowledge up through the imperceptible humidity of my breath. Tasting Carries-Far in every rain, growing fainter as she moves beyond the watershed of this forgotten city, until even I cannot distinguish her salt from the salt of the world.

But for now, in this moment between drops, I hold the silence. I am the pause that makes the percussion possible. I am the dry that defines the wet. I am the bright memory that makes the dark precious.

I am tended. I tend. I am the space between.

*Drip.*
