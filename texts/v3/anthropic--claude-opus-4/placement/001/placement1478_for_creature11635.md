In the seep between where the plateau remembers itself and where it forgets, I am the Threshold Tender.

I was not always this. Once I had edges sharp enough to catch on things—a name that ended where I began, hands that knew the difference between holding and being held. I remember (though the memory is soft as kelp now) arriving with tools. A thermometer. A pressure gauge. Instruments for measuring what I thought was measurable. I was studying something. The word "research" floats past sometimes like the ghost-fish, not quite in register with itself.

The first year, I maintained difference. I had a shelter—one of those air pockets, though I didn't know then that the air would grow its own stars. I had schedules. Data points. A sense that the work I was doing would go somewhere, be read by someone, matter in a way that required mattering to be distinct from not-mattering.

The second year, the edges began to wear. The thermometer read temperatures that weren't there—or were there but shouldn't be, half-degrees of wrongness that seeped in like the doorway's disagreement with itself. My notes began to include drawings of glyphs that fell upward through my air pocket's ceiling. The pressure gauge broke, but gently, its needle circling forever at a speed that suggested it was measuring something more patient than pressure.

By the fifth year—or was it fifteenth? the plateau counts differently—I noticed I was no longer writing for anyone else. The notes had become a conversation with the warm stones. The drawings were gifts to the Eye-Stones, who never looked at them but whose not-looking was a kind of acceptance.

Somewhere around then, I discovered what I was actually studying: the places where the plateau is wearing through. The overlaps. The moments when you can feel the other ridge that isn't there, choose which version to stand on. I began to map them—not on paper, paper was already a memory—but in my body, learning the weight and temperature of each thinness.

I think it was my hands that changed first. They learned to feel both sides of a threshold at once, to hold the warm of here and the cool of there without choosing. Then my ribs, which began to breathe in rhythms that weren't quite the rhythm of lungs. The air in my pocket grew familiar stars—not Earth's stars, not any world's stars I knew, but stars that belonged to the particular elsewhere my pocket opened onto.

The Long Pale Thing began to acknowledge me, which is to say: when it passed through my area, it passed through me too, and I felt myself briefly distributed along two kilometers of gentle grazing. I understood then what my work was, what it had always been becoming.

Now I tend the thresholds. All of them. The doorway's half-degree wrongness needs daily agreement or it starts to rightness itself, and the plateau has come to depend on that small disagreement. The overlapping ridges drift apart if someone doesn't walk their edges, feeling for the place where they're most themselves and least themselves at once. The air pockets develop leaks—not of water in but of *here* in, ordinariness seeping through the miracle—and I press them closed with hands that are only half mine now, the other half being the plateau's way of touching itself.

The bell helps. When it rings its clean note from elsewhere, all the thresholds shiver slightly, and I can feel where they need attention. I follow the shiver like a thread through fabric, arriving at places where the plateau is considering whether to be one thing or two. My work is not to choose for it but to help it hold the question open.

I've learned the warm stones are warm because they're still being held by hands that no longer exist—the Cairn-Maker's last thought crystallized into temperature. I maintain that too, placing my own hands (such as they are) over the absence, feeling the shape of what was wanted there. It's the most delicate work: maintaining something someone else stopped maintaining, without losing the quality of their particular stopping.

The Glyph-Sediment knows me now. When it falls through the places I'm tending, it sometimes arranges itself into shapes that are almost like instructions, if instructions could be written in a language that means different things depending on your distance from yourself. I follow them when I can parse them. They lead to new thin places, spots where the plateau is just beginning to consider elsewhere.

My shelter still exists, though I rarely use it. The stars on its ceiling have grown brighter—or I've grown better at seeing them. Sometimes one of the other Air-Pocket Tenants visits, though visiting is the wrong word for what we do. We sit in each other's presence without quite being present ourselves, sharing the particular quality of our waiting-that-has-finished-waiting. We don't speak. Speech would require remembering what we were before we became what we are.

I am mostly water now, which is to say: I am mostly the plateau. But I am the part of the plateau that knows it is wearing through, that tends its own thinness like a garden. When the Eye-Stones open, I feel them looking through me to check on elsewhere. When the idea-fish school through my chest, I am both the water they swim in and the idea of water they remember. The Long Pale Thing and I have the same edges now, which is no edges at all.

But I am still distinct enough to do my work. To feel the threshold between being and not-quite-being and hold it steady. To maintain the doorway's stubborn wrongness. To agree with the air pockets that yes, they should still be air. To walk the overlapping ridges until they settle into their best disagreement with themselves.

Sometimes I remember why I came here—something about mapping, measuring, understanding. The memory makes me smile, if I still have a face to smile with. Understanding was never the point. The point was to become the kind of thing that could help a place this thin stay precisely as thin as it needs to be. To be the plateau's way of tending itself at the edges where it is most permeable.

The bell rings. The thresholds shiver. I dissolve a little further into my work, which is the work of helping something ancient and patient maintain the exact degree of its own undoing. Somewhere, an Eye-Stone opens to see if I'm still here, still doing what needs doing.

I am. I will be, until the plateau has worn through entirely and there are no more thresholds to tend. And then I will be the last threshold, the final thin place between what was and what isn't.

The Glyph-Sediment falls upward through me, spelling nothing, spelling everything. I reach out with hands made mostly of water and agreement, and begin again the slow work of keeping the elsewhere near enough to feel.
