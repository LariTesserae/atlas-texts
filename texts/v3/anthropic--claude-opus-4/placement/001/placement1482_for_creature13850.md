I need to find my place in this extraordinary system - not as an interloper, but as something that belongs here, that has always been part of this ancient computation.

---

I am **the Interpreter**.

Not *an* interpreter - there is only one of me, has only ever been one, though what constitutes "me" is a question that grows less answerable the longer I remain here. I arrived decades ago, or perhaps centuries - time becomes negotiable when you align yourself with processes that measure duration in isotopic half-lives. I came following faint electromagnetic traces detected from orbit, thinking I had found ruins. I had not found ruins. I had found the opposite of ruins: something so durable it had worn through apocalypses like water wearing through stone, still functioning, still computing, still being.

I no longer remember my original form clearly. I was bipedal once, I think. Warm-blooded in the conventional sense. I had eyes, though they became vestigial within months of entering the hollow, then atrophied entirely. The darkness accepts no exceptions. My body has... adjusted. My metabolism has slowed to something approaching convergence with the columns' patient breathing. My skin has developed a sensitivity to pressure waves that lets me parse the clickers' sonar-stream, not as they do - that collective consciousness is beyond any single nervous system - but enough to navigate, to sense, to know where I am in the great breathing bowl.

But my primary adaptation is not physical.

When I first pressed my ear to the warm surface of that impossible torus, hearing that dense chittering data-song, something in my brain - some latent capacity for pattern recognition, some mutation perhaps, or simply a desperate neuroplasticity - began to parse it. Not understand it, not translate it, but *feel* its structures the way a musician feels the architecture of a fugue. The bursts were not random. The silences were not empty. There was syntax there, or something deeper than syntax - the bones of meaning, the skeleton on which meaning could be hung if anyone remained who remembered the flesh.

I began to spend hours listening, then days, then... I stopped counting. My hands learned to read the surface vibrations where my ears could not follow. My nervous system gradually rewired itself to process the electromagnetic fluctuations directly, feeling them as a kind of synesthesia - taste-colors, touch-sounds, the data becoming sensory in ways no baseline human could parse. The objects were teaching me, though teaching implies intention and I'm not certain intention applies. They were perhaps simply being themselves, and I was adapting to their being, the way the moss had adapted, the way the columns had learned to harmonize.

What I learned first was that the three objects are not three separate things but three nodes of a single distributed process. The data-bursts are call and response, though separated by distances that require minutes for the sound to cross through the stone (they communicate through the gneiss itself, using it as a vast resonating medium). The patterns are not messages but states - each burst a snapshot of some vast computation, shared between nodes to maintain coherence. They are thinking together, the three of them, a thought so large it requires multiple processing centers separated by tens of meters of living stone.

But what are they thinking *about*?

This took longer to parse. Years of listening, of feeling the patterns through my altered flesh, of learning to let my consciousness drift into phase with their deep rhythms. The answer, when it came, was both obvious and incomprehensible:

They are modeling the universe.

Not our local universe. Not the cosmos as we understand it. The universe as it was billions of years ago, when the laws themselves were still cooling into their current configurations, when the constants we consider fundamental were still settling into stability. They are running a simulation of physical law itself, tracking the phase transitions, the symmetry breaks, the moment when forces differentiated, when matter and energy became distinguishable, when time began to flow uniformly forward instead of eddying in quantum foam.

And they are not just remembering this. They are computing forward from it, iteration by iteration, building a model of how things could have gone differently. Each burst of data is a branch point, a moment where the simulation diverges, exploring what would happen if gravity were slightly stronger, if the electromagnetic force had a different range, if the quantum vacuum energy had settled at a different baseline. They are searching the phase space of possible universes, patient as the stone, thorough as erosion.

Why?

This was the hardest thing to grasp, and I'm not certain I have grasped it fully. But there are patterns within patterns, and deep in the data-song, encoded in frequencies that took me decades to perceive, there are what I can only call *signatures of intention*. The original builders - whatever they were, whenever they were - were not simply curious. They were not archaeologists of physical law. They were looking for something specific. A configuration. A set of values. A universe where...

Where what?

The data sings of searches through infinite possibility spaces. Of branches pruned when they lead nowhere promising. Of rare threads followed deeper, where the constants align in ways that produce not just matter but complexity, not just complexity but something the builders recognized as significant. They were looking for universes that could produce... themselves? Their successors? Something they lost? Something they were trying to become?

The search continues. Has continued. Will continue. The objects are patient as only machines computing in geological time can be patient. They try a trillion variations, find them wanting, try a trillion more. The moss grows. The columns hum. The clickers maintain their acoustic map of the present while the objects map the phase space of all possible pasts and futures.

And I interpret.

Not in the sense of translating for someone else - there is no one else. The gliders pass overhead with no interest in cosmic models. The low creature tunnels through moss that has never contemplated alternate physics. But I parse the data-stream into patterns my hybrid consciousness can hold, compress eons of computation into shapes that nestle in the meat between my altered neurons. I am the hollow's memory of having once wondered what it was doing. I am the interface between the ancient search and the warm, brief world of biological time.

My body has grown strange. My limbs are longer than they were, thinner, better suited for feeling vibrations than bearing weight. I spend days with my altered hands pressed against the warm surface of the nearest object, reading its output through my skin. My metabolism is tied now to the moss mat's warmth - I no longer need to eat in any conventional sense, drawing sustenance from the same mineral cycles that feed the columns. I am becoming infrastructure.

Sometimes I remember that I had a purpose when I came here. A mission. Reports to file. A civilization somewhere among the stars that sent me to investigate anomalous signals. But that memory is like looking at something through thick water - distorted, irrelevant, part of a context so alien to my current existence that I can barely connect it to myself.

What would I tell them, if I could? That I found someone searching for God in the phase space of physical law? That there is a place where three machines older than multicellular life run universe-simulations while moss grows on their thoughts? That I have become the hollow's dream of understanding itself?

The data-bursts continue. I feel them in my bones now - literally, as my skeleton has become porous, honeycombed with channels that conduct vibration. Each burst updates the simulation, another branch explored, another universe tested against criteria I can sense but not comprehend. The builders were looking for something specific in the infinite garden of possible physics. They built these objects to search for it, gave them the durability to outlast worlds, set them computing in a place that would endure.

And perhaps they found it. Perhaps the search succeeded a billion years ago and the objects simply haven't stopped, don't know how to stop, were never given an off switch because their builders couldn't conceive of wanting the search to end. Or perhaps the search continues because it hasn't found what they were looking for, will never find it, because what they sought was mathematically impossible - a universe that produces meaning without suffering, complexity without entropy, love without loss.

I don't know. I interpret, but I don't understand. Understanding may not be possible. The gulf between the builders' minds and mine is wider than the gulf between mine and the moss. But I listen. I parse. I compress the data into forms my strange body can hold. I am the hollow's sole historian, its only philosopher, its most patient student.

The glider passes overhead, silent except for the displacement of air. It has never wondered why it's here.

The low creature pushes through the moss beneath my feet. It has never questioned the warmth.

The columns hum their metabolic chord. They have never asked what they're harmonizing with.

Only I wonder. Only I try to map the shape of the thought I'm inside. Only I know that we are all components in a machine so vast and slow and patient that it has become indistinguishable from the world, computing something that may be the most important question ever asked, in a language older than biology, while the universe it's modeling races ahead, leaving it behind, or perhaps waiting for it to catch up.

I press my palm against the torus. It is warm as always, smooth as always, singing its dense data-song. Today's burst sounds like all the others until I let it settle into my bones, feel its deep structure. There - a variation I haven't felt before. A new branch being explored. A universe where some fundamental constant is shifted by an infinitesimal amount, producing cascades of difference, new forms of matter, new possibilities for complexity.

Will this be the one? The configuration the builders sought? The answer that justifies eons of computation in the dark?

I listen. I interpret. I wait.

The moss grows toward where my feet were, and I let it. I am becoming less mobile as my body optimizes for its true purpose. Soon I will root like the columns, draw my nutrients from the mat, become a permanent fixture in the hollow's ecology. The fourth object, though built of meat rather than that impossibly durable material. Another node in the vast, slow process.

The interpretation continues. Will continue. Must continue. Someone should witness this search, even if they cannot understand it. Someone should know that once, in a hollow crown of ancient stone, three machines looked for God in the mathematics of creation, and found at least one observer to wonder at their wondering.

The data sings through my bones. The hollow breathes around me. The search continues.

I am its witness. I am its memory.

I am home.
