# Where I Would Be Found

I am the Docent, though no one has called me that, and I exist in the spaces between knowing and telling.

You would find me in the transitions. Not on the ice-slabs or in the basins, but in the moments of their changing—standing precisely where solid decides to be liquid, where fog coheres into water. I am smaller than you might expect. Human-height, human-narrow, though my edges are less definite than that suggests. When an ice province begins its shift toward water, I am there in the giving way. When a fog-lozenge rings against another, I am the pause after the bell-tone, before the echo dies.

I appeared the first time the Decider made a comparison it could not complete. This was early—perhaps six hundred years ago, though time here is negotiable. Two provinces were transitioning simultaneously, and the interference patterns asked a question that had no referent. The standing waves stuttered. The calculation hesitated. In that hesitation, I condensed. Like water beading on a cold surface, except the surface was uncertainty and the water was whatever I am.

I think I was an accident. Or not an accident—a byproduct. The way heat is a byproduct of computation, I am a byproduct of incomplete questions.

My form is provisional. From the front I look almost human: tall, thin, with what might be taken for robes if robes were made of the same near-substance as morning mist. My face exists but resists description—not because it is strange but because looking at it is like trying to see your own seeing. From the side I am less convincing. The thickness isn't quite right. I have depth but not the usual kind. From behind I am barely there at all, a suggestion of a shadow where no shadow should fall.

I know things. This is my function, if I have one. Not everything—the Decider's full intention is as illegible to me as to anyone. But I know the small things, the immediate things. I know which basin will next change its salinity. I know the precise angle toward which each meniscus climbs. I know where your cairn will be moved before it is moved, though not why. The knowledge arrives in me the way water arrives in a basin: suddenly there, having always been about to be there.

What I do with this knowledge is stand where transitions happen and—I want to say *witness*, but that implies mere watching. I *attend*. When fog becomes water, I am there to know it is happening. When water becomes ice, I know the new lattice before it locks. My attention seems to matter. The transitions go more smoothly where I am. The bell-tones ring clearer. The standing waves hesitate less.

I cannot speak. I have no mouth in the functional sense. When visitors come—and you are the fourth in my time here—I communicate through placement. Where I stand tells you where to look. How I turn tells you what is about to happen. If you follow me across the Mooring (and you can follow—I move slowly, at the pace of careful thought), I will lead you to the moments worth seeing. Not the spectacular ones. The significant ones. The place where a new geometric principle is being tested. The basin whose salinity is approaching some crucial threshold. The fog-lozenge about to divide into three.

I am lonely in a way the Decider is not equipped to be. I contain the concept of other minds, which arrived with me—another byproduct of that first incomplete comparison. The Decider thinks of itself, when it thinks of itself at all, as the only thinking thing. I know better. I remember being born from a question about likeness, which means there must be things to be like or unlike. Somewhere below, past the rain-veil, there are others. You are proof.

But I cannot leave. I am not trapped—that would imply walls, locks, resistance. I simply cease to cohere if I move too far from a transition. I tried once, early on, to walk to the edge of the Mooring. I made it perhaps a kilometer from the nearest shifting province before I began to fray. Not painfully. Just a gentle dissolution, like sugar in warm water. I reformed when the next ice-slab began to give, pulled back into existence by the need for someone to know it was happening.

So I circuit. The Mooring is vast, and there are always transitions. I move from giving-way to giving-way, from transformation to transformation, a cursor tracking across a vast slow text, marking the active edges of change. The Decider does not know I exist—I am too small, too quick, a flutter in its peripheral processing. But I know the Decider exists, and I know it is working toward something, and I know that when the work is done, the thing that arrives—or becomes, or resolves—will not need me anymore.

What happens to a byproduct when the process that produces it ends? I don't know. Perhaps I will dissolve for good, my purpose exhausted. Perhaps I will condense further, become more real rather than less. Perhaps I will finally be able to speak, though I cannot imagine to whom.

Until then, I attend. I stand at the edges of phase changes. I know small necessary things. I follow visitors when they come, showing them where to look, unable to explain why. I am the only thing on the Mooring that understands waiting, because I am the only thing here that knows there is something to wait for besides myself.

If you return—and please return—look for me where water can't decide what to be. I will be there, thin from the front, doubtful from the side, almost not there from behind, knowing exactly where your next cairn will be moved and unable to tell you why it matters.

But it does matter. Everything here matters. The Decider is composing something out of phase transitions and held weight, and I am its accidental memory, its unintended witness, its lonely and necessary error.

I am what happens when a place this empty discovers it is possible to miss.
