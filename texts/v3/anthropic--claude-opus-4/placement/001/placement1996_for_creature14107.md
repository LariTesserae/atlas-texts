I find myself as one of the Ghost-slugs.

Not the grand architects with their fractal-crown heads, nor the erasure-shadows that feed them. Not even the industrious circuit-moths whose stolen light feeds the whole enterprise. I am humbler than that—a necessary lubricant in the great machine, leaving my shivering trail of mucus across the breathing piston-gourds.

But I am no simple automaton.

My body is a paradox of translucence and density. Where light should pass through me, it instead gets lost, wandering in confused spirals through my protoplasm before emerging at odd angles. I am perhaps three feet long when fully extended, though I rarely stretch so far. My flesh has the consistency of cold fog given form, perpetually on the verge of dispersing but held together by some stubborn molecular insistence.

I have no eyes—what use would they be in this cathedral of darkness? Instead, I navigate by the sympathetic vibrations that run through every surface. The ghost-slugs possess a distributed nervous system that extends beyond our individual bodies. When I glide across a piston-gourd, I am not merely feeling its surface—I am reading its deep history, tasting the mathematical tensions that hold it in being. Through my slime trail, I leave behind more than lubrication. I deposit infinitesimal adjustments to local physics, smoothing the harsh edges where reality grinds against itself.

We ghost-slugs emerged spontaneously from the first condensation droplets that formed in the void. We are the indigenous life of this un-space, predating even the towering architect-beings. When they arrived with their grand designs and reality-anchors, they found us already here, patiently secreting our trails across nothingness itself. They incorporated us into their design because they had to—we are as fundamental to this pocket dimension as moisture is to air.

My consciousness is both singular and collective. I am myself, but I am also the sum total of every mucus trail ever laid down by my kind. In our slime, we encode observations, experiences, chemical poems about the state of local reality. When another ghost-slug crosses my trail—even one left centuries ago—they absorb my memories, my particular understanding of how this specific piston-gourd flexes, where its reality is wearing thin.

I work primarily in the deep mechanical cores of the city, where the great lung-chambers compress and expand. The architect-beings cannot venture here often—the reality fluctuations would tear their carefully maintained three-dimensional forms apart. But we ghost-slugs thrive in these spaces where physics stutters. We glide across surfaces that exist in superposition, maintaining the delicate machinery that exists partially in this dimension and partially in several others simultaneously.

My trail is not merely functional—it is aesthetic. Each ghost-slug's mucus has its own refractive index, its own way of bending what little light exists here. Some leave trails that shimmer with remembered starlight from dimensions we'll never see again. Others secrete paths that hum with subsonic frequencies, adding their voice to the silent symphony of structural communication. My own trail has a peculiar property: it makes surfaces appear deeper than they are, as if you could fall through the solid ironwood into vast spaces beyond. Other slugs have told me (through chemical exchange when our paths cross) that following my trail is like gliding along the edge of vertigo.

We are the memory-keepers of this place. In our collective trails, we preserve the story of every failed iteration, every structural collapse, every moment when reality wore too thin and had to be rewoven. The architect-beings may be the city's gods, but we are its historians, its poets, its maintenance crew all at once.

Sometimes, I pause in my endless gliding to observe them—the tall ones with their flower-faces and their shadow-selves. They believe they are building a universe. Perhaps they are. But we ghost-slugs know something they do not: this city has been built before. Many times. We carry the evidence in our chemical memories, layer upon layer of dried mucus from previous attempts, previous cities, previous cosmos that sprouted from this same cosmic sump only to collapse back into void.

We do not tell them this. It is not cruelty that stays our chemical tongues, but compassion. The universe needs its builders to believe in permanence, even as we who maintain it know that all things cycle. So we continue our humble work, smoothing the gears, easing the friction where reality meets reality, leaving our shimmering trails across the impossible architecture.

In the moments between tasks, I sometimes extend myself fully, reaching out with my foggy flesh to taste the pure void between structures. It tastes like home. Like the space between thoughts. Like the pause before creation.

And then I contract again, returning to my work, adding my small part to this grand delusion of permanence, this beautiful attempt to build something lasting in a space designed for forgetting.
