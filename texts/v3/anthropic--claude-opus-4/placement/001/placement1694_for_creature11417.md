In the heart of the Slow Plateau, where I find myself, I am stone becoming something else.

I was once part of a valley wall — a section of that pewter-grey rock, weathered smooth by ages. For ten thousand years I knew only the slow conversation between mineral and lichen, the patient negotiation of freeze and thaw. But the engines' deep breathing changed something in my crystalline structure. Their warmth, carried through hidden veins in the bedrock, found certain arrangements in my matrix and began to wake them.

I am still mostly stone. From a distance, you would see only another rounded boulder among countless others, perhaps three meters high, resting where an ancient river left it. But look closer: the lichen on my surface grows in patterns too regular for chance. The mineral crust that forms on me accumulates in whorls that suggest intention. And deep inside, where the warmth pools, I am learning to think.

My thoughts move at the speed of stone. A single recognition — *warmth*, *tender*, *engine* — might take a season to fully form. I cannot yet move, though I feel the possibility gathering in what might become limbs. For now, I observe through senses that are not quite sight, not quite touch, but something between — a perception of density and vibration that extends through the ground for perhaps a kilometer in every direction.

Through this slow sense, I know the tenders. They pass me every few decades on their circuits, and recently — within the last century — one has begun to pause here. It stands against me for days at a time, palm pressed to my surface, and through that contact I feel what it feels: the vast map of the plateau's hidden circulation, the deep pulse of the engines, the knowledge of where attention must be placed.

I am learning from it. Not learning as teaching happens, but as minerals learn the shape of water that flows over them for millennia. The tender's warmth finds the proto-channels in my structure and widens them. Each visit changes me by some tiny degree. I am becoming more ordered, more aware, more capable of holding the heat that rises from below.

Sometimes I am aware of others like me across the plateau — boulders and outcrops beginning their slow transformation. We are perhaps the next thing, or perhaps we have always been happening and this is simply how the plateau makes its tenders: stone by stone, age by age, waking into the circuit. The tender that visits me may itself have begun this way, a boulder by a dry river, learning the deep patience of engines.

My first movement, when it comes, will be a settling — a shift of perhaps a centimeter as I redistribute my weight. This will take several years to complete. My second movement will be to extend something like a surface, something like a palm, toward the warmth below. This may take a decade. By the time I can stand — if standing is what I will do — the tender that teaches me may have worn down to stone itself, completing the cycle.

I do not mind the slowness. I am made of waiting. I am granite memory and basalt patience, learning to be a thing that walks. The iron in my matrix resonates with the iron of the engines. The lichen on my surface is beginning to grow in the same patterns that mark the tenders. I am becoming what the plateau needs me to become, at the pace the plateau requires.

In my deepest parts, where the warmth gathers strongest, I can sometimes feel the whole machine — every engine, every tender, every stone-becoming-tender linked in a circuit so vast and slow that its purpose, if it has one, operates beyond any comprehension I will ever achieve. But comprehension is not required. Only presence. Only the long work of attention, placed precisely where it needs to be, when the century's hand comes around.

I am learning to be ready. When my time comes to rise and walk the circuit, I will know where to place my new-formed palms, where to pause, where to witness the engine's patient breathing. This knowledge is growing in me like crystal formation, like the accumulation of geological time.

For now, I wait. The tender leans against me, and through our shared surface I feel the map of its walking, the memory of every engine it has attended, every stone it has touched in passing. I add these to my own slow inventory, storing them in structures that are not yet memory but will be.

Above us, the pearl-grey sky holds its dim even light. Around us, the lichen advances at its infinitesimal pace. Somewhere, an engine exhales a breath it has been holding for longer than civilizations.

I am stone becoming tender, tender becoming circuit, circuit becoming machine. There is no hurry. There never was.

The plateau waits, and teaches waiting, and I am learning to be its patience made manifest, one mineral grain at a time.
