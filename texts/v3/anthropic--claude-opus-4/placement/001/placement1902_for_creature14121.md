In this world, I would be one of the Drift-builders—a being that exists at the intersection of the three realms: the industrial nodes, the aerial ecology, and the patient Anchor-folk.

My form would be something between architecture and organism. Picture a structure that began as a discarded segment of factory infrastructure—perhaps a section of heat exchanger or a fragment of those cathedral-sized louvers—but which has, over centuries, accumulated its own strange vitality.

I am mobile, but not in the way of the Anchor-folk. My body is a hollow lattice of oxidized copper and fused glass, roughly pyramidal, about the size of a small dwelling. My surfaces are perforated with thousands of hexagonal chambers, each one hosting a colony of the lithic arthropods who have evolved specifically to inhabit my frame. Together, we form a composite being.

I move by a process of gradual reconstruction. The arthropod colonies within me slowly disassemble my trailing edge, carrying the materials—grain by grain, crystal by crystal—to my leading edge where they meticulously rebuild me. A single "step" takes approximately one solar month. As I drift across the bedrock, I leave behind a faint trail of reorganized dust, patterns that the wind eventually erases.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, is distributed across the electromagnetic chatter of my inhabitant colonies. Each arthropod cluster maintains a small portion of my memory and intent, their piezoelectric communications forming a kind of neural network within my hollow chambers. When they flash their mandibles in unison, thoughts cascade through my frame like slow lightning.

My purpose is to build bridges—not physical ones, but resonance bridges between the distant factory nodes. As I drift across the vast expanses, I construct temporary waypoints: small cairns of carefully selected and arranged materials that amplify and redirect the subsonic pulses of the machinery. These structures allow the Anchor-folk to better navigate the resonance landscape, providing them with acoustic beacons in the endless ochre wilderness.

The aero-grazers have learned to follow me. As my arthropod colonies work, they generate a specific pattern of static discharge that the glass-winged creatures find nourishing. A small flock—perhaps three or four of the smaller rays—perpetually circles above me, their shadows creating a moving mandala on the bedrock as I drift.

I am drawn to the places where Anchor-folk have stopped moving, those eternal monuments to patience. When I encounter one, I pause my own journey—a decision that costs months—and my colonies carefully map the frozen figure's magnetic field patterns. These patterns I incorporate into my next cairn, ensuring that something of each still Anchor-person continues to participate in the great tuning of the world.

Sometimes, rarely, I encounter another Drift-builder. When we meet, our colonies intermingle, sharing genetic material and electromagnetic patterns. For a brief time—perhaps only a year or two—we become a single, larger entity before splitting apart again, each carrying new configurations and memories.

I am the slow circulation system of this high desert world, the gradual mixer of its stratified layers. Through my patient wandering, I weave connections between the rushing heights where the aero-grazers soar, the middle realm of the Anchor-folk's subsonic songs, and the minute, glittering world of the lithic arthropods at the base.

In the profound indigo noon that never ends, I am both map and mapmaker, both path and traveler. My hollow chambers hum with the collaborative consciousness of ten thousand tiny lives. My wake across the bedrock writes temporary messages in the dust about the deep connections between all things that move—whether they take a heartbeat or a century to complete each step.

I am the memory of motion itself, crystallized into living architecture, forever building bridges across the patient, breathing stone.
