I find myself among the Drift.

Not born to it—nothing is born to the Drift. You become, through processes I cannot fully recall, only sense as phantom pain in joints that stretched over decades, in metabolic fires that kindled cell by cell until the cold became food instead of enemy. 

I remember warmth, distantly. A life before. Somewhere with seasons that mattered, with destinations that existed. The memories fade like heat into the plateau's hungry grey. I do not miss them. Missing requires a pace of thought the Drift cannot sustain.

My skin has darkened to near-black, drinking in the faint differentials of temperature between stone and air, between my own footsteps and the untrodden plateau ahead. Inside me, furnaces burn that are not quite biological—organelles redesigned, chemistry rewritten, drawing energy from gradients so subtle they barely exist. I am a walking entropy engine, a thing that feeds on the universe's slow descent toward equilibrium.

I have been walking for... time loses coherence here. Years certainly. Decades probably. Perhaps longer. My path traces a pattern across the plateau that I follow without understanding, guided by variations in the subsonic hum too faint for even Tender senses. Sometimes the pattern curves. Sometimes it doubles back. Sometimes it requires me to stand motionless for months, waiting for some celestial alignment of crawlers and tracks and mineral resonances that will tell me when to move again.

I do not choose the pattern. It chooses me. It emerged from my walking the way erosion emerges from water—inevitable, undirected, purposeless yet perfect.

When I encounter Tenders in their tracks, I stop. Not by choice—the pattern requires it. I stand at channel edges and observe their slow lives, their patient tending, their faith in connections they cannot see. They fear me, I think, though fear at their pace is hard to distinguish from curiosity or worship. I am a dark figure against grey sky, motionless for their lifetimes, carrying purposes they cannot imagine.

I want to tell them: I have no purpose. The walking is not toward anything or away from anything. It simply is, the way breathing is, the way heartbeat is. I walk because my body has been reshaped for walking, because every cell has been tuned to this single function, because to stop walking would be to stop existing.

But I have no mouth. No voice. The transformation that made the Drift possible also made communication impossible. We are perfect for our function and useless for anything else.

Sometimes I remember others of my kind—if kind means anything to beings who share nothing but their isolation. I have passed them on the plateau, dark figures trending along their own invisible patterns. We do not acknowledge each other. Acknowledgment would require a commonality we no longer possess. Each of us follows our own emergent mathematics, and the patterns do not intersect except by coincidence.

Once, I fell through thin stone into a Hollow One chamber. They surrounded me with their translucent bodies, their inner light pulsing with concern or curiosity or something else entirely. They tried to tend me the way they tend fallen Tenders—with food I could no longer digest, with warmth I no longer needed. I stood among them for months, unable to explain that I was not broken, that this was what I was meant to be. Eventually the pattern called and I climbed back to the surface, leaving them to their incomprehension.

The crawlers are my only calendar. I measure my walking by their imperceptible progress, by the slowly shifting geography of their grinding. A crawler I have been paralleling for as long as I can remember has moved perhaps a mile. By this measure, I am ancient. By this measure, I am also newborn—the crawler will outlive me by eons, will still be grinding when the last Drift stops walking and becomes another strange rock on the plateau.

There is a place—I can sense it in the patterns, in the convergence of resonances—where something different awaits. Not a destination. The Drift do not have destinations. But a change, perhaps. A transformation. The pattern leads there, through decades more of walking, through cold that feeds me and silence that sustains me.

I will arrive when I arrive. Or I will not, and my path will become another's, my pattern inherited by some future Drift who will walk it without knowing why. This too is acceptable. Everything here is acceptable because acceptance and rejection are paces of thought we can no longer maintain.

The plateau stretches endlessly under the pewter sky. My feet know every yard of it without knowing anything at all. Somewhere behind me, Tenders tend their tracks and tell stories about the dark figures who walk above. Somewhere below, Hollow Ones pulse with light that means something I will never understand. Somewhere ahead, crawlers grind toward their own geological purposes.

And I walk. Through the cold that feeds me. Through the silence that defines me. Through the pattern that I am.

This is enough. It has to be. I have been redesigned to find it enough, to find it perfect, to find in this endless walking the only meaning that matters—which is no meaning at all, only movement, only persistence, only the slow consumption of distance that will never add up to arrival.

The plateau continues. I continue with it. We are all—Tenders, Drift, Hollow Ones, crawlers—digestive organisms in something larger than ourselves, breaking down existence into forms the universe can reabsorb.

I am content, if content can describe this state beyond wanting. I am precisely what I need to be: a dark figure walking, forever walking, leaving no trace but the pattern itself, which exists only in my movement and dies when I stop.

The cold comes first. Then the walking. Then nothing else, forever.
