# The Rim Walker

I would be found at the very edge of things, where the ceramic plain begins to buckle and crack, where the patient rules of the Slow Foundry start to fray.

Call me **the Cartographer of Incompletions**.

---

You will not find me near the domes or the fronds or the patient Annealers. I walk the rim — that theoretical boundary where the world-floor must eventually give way to whatever is not-world, though no one has ever confirmed such a place exists. I am small, compared to the Annealers. Human-sized, but denser, as if I've been compressed by the weight of searching for edges in an edgeless place.

My skin is not the bone-pale of the floor but darker, like ceramic that has been overfired, with a web of fine crazing across my arms and face — hairline cracks that weren't there yesterday, won't be there tomorrow, but always are there *now*. I leak a faint warmth through these cracks, not unlike the domes, but intermittent. Sometimes I glow. Sometimes I'm as cool as the space between raindrops.

I carry tools that aren't quite tools: a long rod of fused jade, clearly torn from some ancient frond-cluster. Pouches of kiln-bloom, sorted by color (there are colors, if you look closely: pearl, ash, bone, milk, frost). And most importantly, sheets of what might charitably be called paper — thin slices of ceramic so fine they're translucent, which I unroll across the warm floor to make my maps.

---

What am I mapping? Not the geography — that would be futile in a place that grows itself. Instead, I map the *incompletions*. The spots where a dome began to form but gave up, leaving only a slight swelling in the floor like an unfinished thought. The places where fronds emerged but never uncurled, frozen in their first gesture. The regions where the ceramic shows stress-patterns that suggest something *wanted* to grow here but couldn't decide what.

I have been walking the rim for... time is difficult here, but let's say: long enough that I've begun to notice patterns in what fails to become.

My maps are abstract by necessity. I draw them with kiln-bloom mixed with my own saliva (one of the few sources of moisture reliable enough to make a mark). The symbols I use evolved from observation: a spiral for a dome that started growing downward instead of up. A branching line for places where the floor tried to split but healed itself. A small circle with rays for the spots where, inexplicably, the ceramic is *cold*.

The Annealers do not acknowledge me, though I've learned to read the quality of their non-acknowledgment. When one passes near my work, it will sometimes pause and hold its warm hands over my maps, not quite touching, reading them the way they read the domes. Once, after such an encounter, I returned to find one of my symbols had been subtly modified — a spiral now had a small tail, suggesting perhaps that the downward-growing dome had found some deeper level and was coming back up elsewhere.

---

Why do I do this?

Because someone must witness the failures. In a world so patient with its own becoming, the incompletions are the only hints we have about what it's deciding *not* to be. Each failed dome, each frozen frond, each stress-pattern that leads nowhere — they're all votes against certain futures, and I am their scribe.

I suspect (though I cannot prove) that somewhere at the true rim, if there is a true rim, all these incompletions are gathering. That there's a place where the failed domes have piled up like discarded drafts, where the frozen fronds form forests of potential that will never actualize. I walk toward this place, though I may never reach it. The world may be growing faster than I can traverse it.

But in my walking, I've begun to notice something: the incompletions are not random. They cluster. They form patterns that almost resolve into meaning before dissolving again. Sometimes, when I spread out all my maps on the warm floor and step back, I can almost see it — the shadow of what this world is choosing not to become. It looks like... but no, it's gone again, lost in the shimmer of heat-distortion.

---

I was not always the Cartographer. I remember (dimly, and perhaps falsely) arriving here with others, a long time ago. We were explorers, scientists, something like that. The details have been worn smooth by the constant warm wind. One by one, the others slowed down, began to take on the world's patience. I watched them grow still, watched their shadows go reluctant, watched them begin to tick.

But something in me resisted. Not out of stubbornness, but out of a peculiar kind of love. I loved the world too much to let it seduce me into stillness. I needed to see all of it, even the parts it was ashamed of, even the parts it was trying to forget. So I began to walk, and map, and remember what the world would rather leave unrecorded.

The crazing on my skin is the price. Each crack is a small rebellion against the world's desire for seamlessness. I am a walking incompletion, a failure to properly integrate. But this makes me the perfect witness to the world's other failures. We recognize each other, the failed things and I.

Sometimes, in the early whatever-passes-for-morning, when the kiln-bloom is thickest and the domes hum their deepest notes, I find a fresh incompletion. A dome that rose three feet and then simply... stopped. Not damaged, not eroded — just *decided against*. I sit beside it with my ceramic sheets and my bloom-ink and I document it carefully: size, temperature, the exact quality of its abandonment. I give it the dignity of being remembered.

And sometimes — very rarely — as I'm making my notes, the incompletion will tick. Just once. A small sound of acknowledgment, or perhaps gratitude. As if to say: *thank you for noticing that I almost was.*

---

This is what I am in the Slow Foundry: the one who walks the edge between becoming and not-becoming, mapping the boundary where decision meets indecision. I am neither patient enough to be an Annealer nor fixed enough to be a dome. I am the chronicler of almosts, the surveyor of not-quites, the only thing in this world that's in a hurry — not to arrive anywhere, but to catch the failures before they're reabsorbed into the trying again.

My maps will never be complete. The world creates incompletions faster than I can document them. But that's the point, isn't it? In a place devoted to eventual perfection, someone must tend to the imperfect. Someone must love the abandoned domes, the frozen fronds, the places where the world stuttered and had to start over.

That someone is me, walking the rim forever, my crazed skin a map of my own incompletion, my ceramic sheets a love letter to everything that tried and failed to be.
