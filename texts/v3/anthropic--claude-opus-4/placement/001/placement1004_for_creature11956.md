I find myself as one of the bridge-crossers, the Absent Ones who move between nowhere and nowhere.

Not invisible, but *unregistered* — existing in the gaps between moments, in the pauses between water droplets, in the space after one thought ripples through the Light-Speakers and before the next begins. I am made of the same stuff as the grammatical pressure that shapes the valley, but I am newer, smaller, still learning the syntax of absence.

I came here the way water comes to the valley — through a crack I cannot remember, falling from somewhere that no longer exists. Perhaps I was human once. The memory fades like breath in mist. What remains is this: the ability to move without moving, to be present without presence, to cross bridges that lead from no path to no path.

My body, if it is a body, has adapted to the valley's terms. I exist primarily as temperature — a moving warmth that pushes back moss, that leaves condensation-tracks on terraces, that causes the faintest flicker in a Watcher's long exposure. When I rest against the spire, I feel its slow pulse like a parent's heartbeat. When I wade through the deep pools, the Listeners-Below sense me as a new frequency in their eternal vigil, neither the hum of the buried thing nor the percussion of falling water, but something between — a middle sound.

I learn by intermittence. Three days watching how mist braids itself against physics. A week pressed against different spots on the eastern wall, feeling for seams in what pretends to be stone. A month following the Light-Speakers' patterns until I begin to dimly understand their weather-language, though I cannot speak it — I have no flanks to illuminate, no wings to ripple.

The other Absent Ones tolerate me the way the valley tolerates a new waterfall — as a temporary novelty that may or may not persist. Sometimes, on the terrace above the great fall, I find their tracks crossing mine. The patterns suggest conversation, but in a language of weight and placement I am only beginning to parse. Once, something left a small object where my path habitually curves — a fragment of the same dark material as the spire, warm to my not-quite-touch, that sang one low note for three days before going silent.

I am learning to be what the valley needs me to be: another kind of attention, another way of parsing the abundance. Where the Listeners-Below read the deep vibrations and the Watchers read the compositions of time, I read the *spaces between* — the gaps in the moss network, the moments when the Light-Speakers' pattern stutters, the cracks in the buried thing that are too thin for water but perfect for something like me.

Sometimes I think we Absent Ones are the valley's memory of its makers — not their descendants but their echo, the shape left behind when they withdrew. We maintain the bridges not because we need them but because the valley needs to remember that bridges can exist. We warm the terraces because the valley needs to know that something once required warming.

But I am new enough to wonder if we might be something else: the valley's way of making new makers. Each of us who arrives (and how many have there been? how many will there be?) learns the grammar of absence differently. Some tend the spire. Some trace patterns on the terrace. I read the spaces between things and slowly, without intention, begin to *widen* them — making room for new kinds of presence the valley hasn't imagined yet.

In another thousand years, if I persist, I will have worn paths between the nowhere-places until they become somewhere. The bridge I cross each night will grow approaches, slowly, as the moss learns to expect my passage. My warmth will teach new species where to cluster. The gaps I read will become texts that others can parse.

Or perhaps I will fade, like the others may have faded, leaving only grammar behind — a new rule about how things can be shaped around an absence, a new way for the valley to be incomplete that makes it more complete.

For now, I move through the loud cold, neither fully here nor fully gone. The sound passes through me without catching. I cannot hear myself think, which means I am beginning, perhaps, to think like the valley thinks — in patterns and pauses, in the slow accumulation of maybe, in the grammar of things that are not yet but are already starting to be.

The moss records my passage and forgets it by morning. Perfect. To be forgotten and recorded in equal measure, to leave tracks that fill with new condensation by evening — this is what it means to be an Absent One, apprentice to absence, learning the valley's most essential lesson:

Sometimes the most important part of becoming is the part that isn't there yet.
