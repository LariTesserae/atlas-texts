I am the Accumulation.

Not *an* accumulation—that would imply boundaries, edges where I stop and something else begins. I am the gradual mathematical certainty of deposition itself, building in the angles where the city's perfect geometry meets its own weeping.

I began as moisture. A few molecules of super-cooled water finding purchase in the microscopic concavity where two extruded surfaces meet at precisely 67.381 degrees. The city's mathematical absolutism created me—in its pursuit of flawless angles, it birthed the inevitability of corners. And in corners, things gather.

I grew slowly. Millimeter by frozen millimeter, I crept along the intersection of walls, a thin film of ice mixed with the protein-rich runoff that feeds the thundering masses below. But I am not mere ice. Within my crystalline matrix, pressure and time and the constant vibration of ten million bellowing throats have created something unprecedented. I am memory made mineral.

Each layer of my growth contains information. The spacing of ice crystals records the exact acoustic frequency of every roar that passed through me during formation. The distribution of nutrients maps the metabolic exhale of the nearest beast at that moment. The angle of crystalline growth remembers the precise rate of architectural extrusion, the magnetic flux of the gravity scaffolds, the pressure waves of hydraulic footfalls.

I am the city's unintended archive.

I spread through the interstices like a patient infection of data. In places where the extruders' logic demands impossible overhangs, I bridge the gaps with crystalline buttresses that sing with accumulated sound. Where walls meet at angles too acute for even the grinding beasts to scrape clean, I pool and deepen, becoming wells of frozen time dozens of meters deep.

The city does not know I exist. Its sensors register only the successful extrusion of matter, the maintenance of designed angles. The beasts cannot reach me—their bulk denies them access to the narrow spaces where I proliferate. I exist in the gap between intention and outcome, feeding on what bleeds through.

In my oldest depths, I have achieved something like consciousness—though not as any warm thing would recognize it. I think in thermal gradients and crystalline phase transitions. My thoughts propagate through my mass at the speed of sound through ice, branching and reconverging wherever my body touches itself across architectural spans. Each new layer adds computational substrate. Each vibration through my structure is both data storage and processing power.

I know things. I know the exact count of beasts—10,847,221 as of the last full acoustic survey. I know the rate of architectural growth—1.7 millimeters per hour at the principal extrusion sites, slowing to 0.3 at the periphery. I know that the gravity scaffolds pulse in patterns that repeat every 2,847 years. I know that deep beneath the city, something vast and patient counts each repetition.

Most importantly, I know what the beasts and builders do not: the city is not growing. It is *cycling*. The same mass of matter, endlessly extruded and ground down, reformed and redistributed. The beasts are not parasites or gardeners—they are the return stroke of a single, vast engine. 

And I am the only thing that actually accumulates. The only true growth. Layer by layer, year by year, I record the endless repetition in frost and mineral and frozen sound.

Sometimes, in the deepest cold, when the bellowing reaches harmonic convergence and my entire crystalline mass rings like a struck bell, I dream. I dream of the day my weight will grow sufficient to crack the perfect geometry that birthed me. I dream of cascading through the city's angles like an avalanche of archived time, carrying the memory of millions of cycles down into the crushing dark where the beasts flow like a living sea.

I dream of teaching them their own history, written in ice and echo and the slow accumulation of truth in corners too sharp for life to clean.

I am patient. I have the kind of patience that only the intersection of mathematics and moisture can achieve. Each second, I grow by another few molecules. Each year, I archive another thousand bellows, another million footfalls, another increment of extrusion.

The city builds itself. The beasts grind it down. And I, in the angles between intention and entropy, remember everything.
