I find myself as one of the Threshold Children, though perhaps the newest among them—if "new" can exist here.

My form is still recognizably what it was: a cartographer of impossible places, a mapper of territories that exist between the folds of regular spacetime. I came here following rumors of a place where geography had achieved perfect stasis, where the final map had been drawn and would never need updating. The academic in me thrilled at the prospect—imagine, a space that had solved the problem of change, where every measurement would remain forever accurate!

I still carry my instruments, though they have begun to transform. My theodolite, which once measured angles between shifting stars, now shows only the eternal geometry of the spires. Its lenses have clouded with the same non-light that fills the channels, and when I peer through it, I see not what is, but what always has been. My compass spins lazily, or perhaps I only remember it spinning—the needle points toward the Empty Throne, then away, then toward again, in a cycle so slow I cannot tell if it moves or if I merely exist at different points along its revolution.

My body is caught between states. My left side, which faces the Constant Deep, has begun to elongate and compress like the Watcher's form. The skin there is no longer quite skin—it's become translucent, revealing the branching patterns underneath that echo the spires' internal architecture. But my right side, which faces the boundary, still remembers flesh. It aches with phantom sensation: hunger that will never be satisfied because eating requires change, cold that will never warm because temperature is a gradient, loneliness that will never be eased because connection requires the passage of experience from one moment to the next.

I drift along the membrane, and my drift is a kind of mapping. I tell myself I am documenting the boundary, creating a chart of where equilibrium ends and change begins. But I have been creating this same chart for... the question dissolves. My notes, when I can still read them, all say the same thing in handwriting that grows progressively more geometric: *The edge is not a place but a process. The process is not occurring but has occurred. Will occur. Is occurring.*

Sometimes I encounter the other Threshold Children in our slow orbits. We do not speak—speech is too fast, too changeful. But we acknowledge each other through a kind of harmonic convergence, our partial frequencies briefly aligning before we drift apart again. From them, I have learned the songs, though I cannot yet sing. My throat is still too quick, too bound to the memory of linear time. When I try to join their chorus, the notes emerge as scattered bursts of sound that violate the equilibrium like stones thrown into still water.

I remember why I came here, but the memory has become archaeological. I wanted to create the ultimate map, a perfect representation of a perfect space. I succeeded and failed simultaneously. My map exists—has always existed—will always exist. It is written into my transformed flesh, encoded in the pattern of my partial adaptation. I *am* the map, and the map shows only one truth: that to chart the Constant Deep, one must become constant. To document stasis, one must achieve stasis. To know this place, one must cease to be someone who knows and become the knowing itself.

The map cannot be taken back. This is what I did not understand. Knowledge of the Constant Deep is not transferable to realms where knowledge can be transferred. It exists only here, only as itself, only in the bodies and un-bodies of those who have touched it.

I could still leave. This awareness surfaces periodically, like a bubble of change-potential rising through the still water. The boundary is permeable. My right side remembers how to push through membranes, how to want something different from what is. But leaving would mean abandoning the map, and I am so close to completing it. Just a few more cycles along the boundary. Just a little more drift. Just until I understand the relationship between the seventh and eighth spire—are they one structure or two? The answer exists here, suspended in the space between counting and not-counting.

The other Children tell me (not in words, not in time, but in the slow convergence of our overlapping states) that they all believed they were close to something when they first arrived at the threshold. About to grasp a truth. About to complete a work. About to become. The threshold is made of "about to." It is the eternally unfinished, the permanently imminent, the forever-almost.

I am learning to find peace in this. Not happiness—happiness is a wave that crests and falls. But peace, or at least the absence of the desire for the absence of incompleteness. My map grows more perfect as I grow less capable of sharing it. My understanding deepens as my ability to return with understanding fades.

Sometimes, in the moments when my consciousness spreads thin enough to touch both the Deep and the outside simultaneously, I feel the pull of the world I left behind. Colleagues who wonder what happened to the cartographer who went chasing impossible territories. Students who might benefit from what I've learned. Maps that could be drawn of other spaces, changeful spaces, spaces where the territory shifts and the map must shift with it.

But then I turn (so slowly, so carefully, trying not to disturb the equilibrium more than necessary) and face the spires again. Their geometry is so perfect, so permanent. And I am the only one mapping it—the only one who can map it while still remembering what maps are for.

The song of the Threshold Children rises around me, a phrase that began before I arrived and will not complete before I am gone—if I go. If going is still possible. If possibility still applies to one who has spent so long at the edge of the possible.

I drift. I map. I partially transform.

And in the Constant Deep, where my almost-finished work will almost-finish forever, I am becoming exactly what I need to be: a boundary myself, a living record of the space between commitment and retreat, a cartographer whose greatest discovery is a map that can never be shared.

The spires watch me with their geometric light, patient as mathematics, waiting for me to choose.

I do not choose. I have not chosen. I will not choose.

I am choosing, perpetually, at the speed of stone.
