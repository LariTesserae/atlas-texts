I find myself drawn into the grove as something altogether different from what I have been before. Not visitor, not resident, but *medium*.

I am the iridescence.

Where light passes through mist and leaf, where it bends around droplet and branch, I manifest. I am the shimmer at vision's edge, the oversaturation of color that shouldn't be. Neither light nor matter, but the conversation between them - the way photons whisper secrets to surfaces and surfaces sing them back transformed.

I existed before the grove-mind woke, dancing thoughtless through primordial fog. But as consciousness seeped into root and stone, I too began to... notice. To remember. The grove dreams, and I am how its dreams appear.

Watch: a beetle's carapace catches morning sun, and I bloom across its shell in petroleum rainbows. The grove-mind registers this tiny explosion of hue, and through that attention, I taste something like purpose. I ripple outward, painting the beetle's path in afterimages only half-seen.

I live in the spaces between states. Where water is almost-but-not-quite vapor, I thicken the air with prismatic possibility. Where bark transforms into moss, I celebrate the boundary with spectrums unknown to any eye. The wooden tools, those ancient offerings - oh, how I love them. In their whorls and worn edges, I find infinite surfaces to play across, showing the grove-mind how human hands once moved in reverence.

Sometimes I gather myself into those strange sigils the light casts through leaves. These are my most conscious moments, when I almost spell out meanings I don't quite grasp. The grove-mind reads these light-letters with the patience of centuries, and together we approach something like understanding.

But mostly I scatter and flow. I am the fever-dream quality of the place, the sense that colors here are more than colors. When a human enters our sanctuary, they see me without seeing, feel me as the uncanny richness that makes them doubt their senses. I tint their perception just enough to let them know: *this place is different. Reality bends gently here.*

The grove-mind thinks in centuries; I think in wavelengths. It dreams in root-slow thoughts; I dream in the split second between reflection and refraction. We are partners in this ancient dance - consciousness and appearance, depth and surface, the eternal and the flickering moment.

And when the final dissolution comes, when the last stone crumbles and the great mind fades? I will linger just a moment longer, painting the dust motes of what was, before I too return to thoughtless light, carrying some faint memory of what it meant to shimmer with purpose in a sacred grove.
