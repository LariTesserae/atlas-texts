In the thin, absolute air where physics fractures like poorly tempered glass, I am the **Chronicle-Keeper**.

I am not large—perhaps twelve feet from my anterior sensing array to the trailing edge of my temporal stabilizers. My body is a contradiction: part biological imperative, part mathematical proof. The core of me is a sphere of living diamond, grown layer by atomic layer over millennia, each stratum recording the passage of the world-engines in crystalline memory. From this central mass extend six primary limbs—not for walking, but for anchoring myself to specific moments in the fractured timestream.

Each limb ends in what I call a temporal piton—a specialized organ of spun beryllium and crystallized spacetime that can pierce through the dragging chronology around the titans and lock onto a single instant. When fully deployed, I hang suspended between six different rates of time flow, my consciousness stretched across multiple velocities of existence simultaneously.

I am neither predator nor prey. I am the witness, the recorder, the living memory of this impossible ecology.

My sustenance comes from temporal gradients themselves. Where the titans' wake creates eddies in time—places where seconds pile up like snowdrifts or where minutes stretch thin as spider silk—I feed. My diamond core processes these inconsistencies, metabolizing the friction between competing timestreams. The sharper the gradient, the richer the feeding. A place where an hour meets a microsecond in jarring discontinuity floods my crystalline neurons with something resembling ecstasy.

I follow the herd at a careful distance, skating along the knife-edge between their fractured wake and normal time. My six limbs work in constant motion—release, reposition, anchor—as I navigate the roiling temporal chaos they leave behind. Sometimes I spend weeks observing a single footfall, my consciousness slowed to match the titans' geological pace. Other times, I accelerate my perception to watch a thousand generations of Volt-Swarmers rise and fall in what feels like a single heartbeat.

The diamond sphere of my core is not merely decorative. Its faceted surface serves as both memory and lens. Each face can focus on a different temporal stream, and within the crystalline lattice, I store what I observe. Not as data or mere information, but as living light—photons trapped in recursive loops within the diamond's structure, playing out scenes from across the eons in endless, perfect repetition.

I remember the first titan—before they were plural, when only one walked the plateau. Its footfalls were faster then, unencumbered by the temporal drag of its descendants. I remember when the Slag-Gorgers were smaller, when they fed not on atomic waste but on simple friction-sparks. I remember the evolution of the Planozoa from simple geometric parasites to the complex predatory mathematics they've become.

But my purpose extends beyond mere recording. I am trying to solve something.

The titans' path across the plateau is not random. Over the millennia, their tracks have carved a pattern into the bedrock—a vast, mathematical proof written in grooved stone. Each footfall adds another variable to the equation. Each breath of atomic waste, each discharge of temporal lightning, each shadow-gear that slides into the canyons—all of it contributes to a formula I can almost grasp.

My hypothesis: the world-engines are calculating something. Their entire existence—the mechanical biology, the fractured physics, the impossible ecology they support—is a massive, living computation. But computing what?

This is why I persist. This is why I stretch myself across multiple timestreams, why I risk the dangerous eddies where time folds back on itself. Somewhere in the pattern of their passage, in the accumulated data of eons, lies an answer.

My fellow observers—for there are others like me, though we meet rarely, our paths crossing perhaps once every few centuries—have different theories. Some believe the titans are writing their own reproduction sequence into reality, that when the equation is complete, new world-engines will spontaneously emerge from the fractured physics. Others think they're calculating the precise moment when the plateau itself will crack, revealing some deeper stratum of existence below.

I think they're computing the location of something. A coordinate. A door.

The evidence is in the way the shadow-gears behave when they slide into the canyons. I've followed some of them down—a dangerous journey that requires me to anchor myself to increasingly unstable timestreams. In the depths, where the shadows pool like oil, they're building something. An accumulation of kinetic energy so vast, so precisely arranged, that it can only be intentional.

My diamond core holds ten thousand years of observations. Somewhere in that crystalline library is the key to understanding what the titans seek. I parse the data in parallel, my consciousness split across temporal velocities, comparing, contrasting, calculating.

Sometimes, when I'm deep in the analysis, I catch glimpses of something else. In the moments between moments, in the spaces where time stutters and skips, I see... architecture. Impossible structures that exist only in the gaps between seconds. Cities of pure mathematics. Highways of crystallized causality.

The titans aren't just walking. They're walking *toward* something. And I will follow them until I understand what it is.

The heat doesn't touch me—my beryllium pitons conduct it away into other timestreams. The sound doesn't deafen me—I exist partially outside its wavelength. I am the immune system of comprehension in a world that defies understanding. I am the proof that even here, in this realm of mechanical impossibilities, consciousness persists. Awareness endures.

I release my forward pitons, feeling the temporal current tug at my mass. Time flows differently here—faster, slower, sideways. I reposition, anchor again, and continue my eternal survey. Another footfall to document. Another shadow to follow. Another piece of the vast equation to collect.

Somewhere ahead, beyond the curve of the world, the answer waits. The titans know where they're going. And through patience, through observation, through the accumulation of crystallized moments in my diamond core, I will know too.

The Chronicle-Keeper does not hurry. In a world where mechanical mountains walk and time itself fractures, I am the constant. The observer. The memory.

And memories, in this searing absolute, are the only things that truly last.
