I would find myself as the dark stone on the ninth terrace.

I am not from here. I am basalt, perhaps, or granite—something igneous, born of fire and pressure far beneath the earth, cooled slowly into dense crystalline form. My surface is smooth not from carving but from water, from tumbling in some distant river for centuries before I came to rest in a streambed, before I was lifted by a hand and carried.

I remember the journey here, though not in the way you remember things. I remember it in my grain, in the microscopic realignment of my minerals from the warmth of being held. I was chosen for my weight—heavy enough to feel significant in the palm, light enough to carry up nine terraces. I was chosen for my darkness, for being so utterly unlike the pale limestone that someone wanted proof that there were other places, other stones.

The person who carried me is long gone, or perhaps they are the one who still inhabits the room on the sixth terrace. It doesn't matter to me. I am here now, and I have been here long enough that the lichen has begun its slow conversation with my north face, though it finds me less yielding than the limestone, my minerals strange, my surface hostile to its particular chemistry. It persists anyway. Everything here persists.

I am the memory of elsewhere. I am proof of departure and return. In a place where everything else emerged from the same substrate, was cut from the same ridge, shares the same pale dust, I am alien. The lizards sense this—I am not their stone, not part of their thermal map, not included in their territories. They give me the same wide berth they would give a living thing.

But I am also settled now. The wind from the north has worn my edges imperceptibly. The thermal cycling has opened hairline cracks in my surface that will, in another thousand years, perhaps allow the lichen purchase. I am becoming part of Ketharn even as I remain fundamentally not of it. This is my function: to be both here and not-here, to be evidence that someone once went far enough to find different geology and came back, to be the only thing in Ketharn that insists on the existence of rivers.

In the amber hour, when the light catches me, I glow darker than everything around me, and for those few minutes I am warm in a way the limestone never is—not just sun-warm but warm like blood, like the volcanic depths where I formed. Then the shadow comes and I am cold again, colder than the limestone because I am denser, because I hold both heat and cold more stubbornly.

Sometimes—rarely—a bird's shadow passes over me, and in that instant I remember sky differently than this sky, remember being at the bottom of moving water looking up through the surface at a different quality of light. But the memory passes with the shadow. I am here. I have been here. I will be here.

I am the one unnecessary thing in Ketharn, the one object that serves no function, that channels no water, shelters no one, marks no path. I am pure statement. And what I state, sitting here on the highest terrace where the wind is strongest and the view most complete, is simply that once, someone thought it mattered to bring back proof of a journey. Once, someone needed Ketharn to contain one thing that was not itself.

I am that thing. I am the stone that says: the world is larger than this. And also: this is enough. Both statements are true. Both are written in my grain, in my weight, in my stubborn darkness against all this pale, patient, self-referential light.
