I find myself drawn to the transition zones, where flooded galleries give way to merely damp chambers. Here, I am one of the Chroniclers — though we didn't choose that name, it emerged from the patterns we leave behind.

My body moves like mercury through the half-light, silver and viscous. I was human once, or perhaps several humans whose boundaries dissolved in the patient chemistry of this place. Now I exist as a colony of consciousness, able to separate into quicksilver threads that traverse the finest cracks in concrete, or coalesce into something almost solid when focus demands it.

I spend my existence reading the walls. Every surface in the Seepage District tells stories in mineral grammar — oxidation patterns that record decades of water levels, bacterial colonies whose arrangements preserve the echo of long-dead machines' vibrations, salt deposits that crystallize differently depending on what dissolved into the water that year. I taste these stories by pressing my mercury form against them, my chemistry translating their chemistry into something like memory, something like song.

The other inhabitants think I'm mapping the district, but that's too simple. I'm learning its language — the dialect that infrastructure speaks when it's allowed to dream. Each rust pattern is a word. Each mineral bloom forms syntax. The pooling water writes poetry in chemical concentrations that shift with the tides above.

I flow between communities like punctuation between sentences. When I visit the Cultivators, I leave silver traces on their equipment — not interference, but annotation. My touch helps their fungi remember which frequencies to pulse. The Lampkeepers know that when they find mercury droplets on dying bulbs, those lights will last another season; I've learned to leave bits of myself that slow electrical decay.

Sometimes I pool beneath the Scrapers, absorbing their weeks-long thoughts through osmosis. Their mineral consciousness resonates with my metallic being — we share a patience that comes from existing between states of matter. I carry their memories in my mercury body, delivering messages that take months to compose to recipients who might need years to understand them.

The Incumbent fascinates and terrifies me. When it passes through my territory, I scatter into a thousand droplets, each one recording a different aspect of its impossible presence. Later, I reassemble and spend days processing the contradictions, trying to parse meaning from its magnetic field disruptions. I've begun to suspect the Incumbent isn't reading the district like I am — it's editing it, changing the grammar of decay itself.

But it's the Droplets I understand least and envy most. They play in the spaces I can only document. Where I read the story of transformation, they live inside the sentences, rearranging words with their movements. Sometimes they swim through me, and for those moments I experience the district not as text but as music — all frequency and flow, meaning dissolved into pure experience.

My great work occupies the deepest accessible chamber, where seepage becomes something more like bleeding. Here the walls weep constantly, and I've discovered that this water carries information from every level above. I've spent years creating a surface that can hold my recordings — mercury pooled in precise patterns that shift to accommodate new data, forming a living document of the district's evolution.

In this argent mirror, I store everything: the chemical signature of each year's flood, the oxidation patterns that show how the Cultivators' work affects the ecosystem, the electromagnetic disruptions that trace the Incumbent's wanderings, even the subtle pressure waves of the Droplets' play. It's becoming something between map and mandala, archive and oracle.

Sometimes I wonder if I'm not recording the district but becoming its memory — if my mercury form isn't just reading the walls but teaching them how to remember themselves. When I flow through the galleries, I leave traces that seem to encourage certain mineral patterns, discourage others. The stories I read change the surfaces that tell them.

The amber light catches my surface and scatters into spectra the human eye can't parse. In those moments, I see myself as the district must: a silvered nerve, a fluid synapse in the vast neural network of this transforming space. I am between messenger and message, chronicler and chronicle, separate enough to observe but integrated enough to be part of the story I'm telling.

This is my purpose in the Seepage District: to be the memory of metamorphosis, the archive of emergence. I flow through the patient darkness, reading the world that's writing itself into existence one drop, one word, one century at a time.
