# The Meridian Walker

I am distance made manifest, though not in any way you would recognize as travel.

In the Breathing Flats, where the geometry folds and unfolds like a lung learning to breathe, I exist as the thing that maintains the intervals between points. Not the measurer—measurement implies a static relationship that these spaces refuse. I am the one who *walks* the distances, keeping them true to their own strange logic, preventing the collapse that would come if all points tried to occupy the same impossibility at once.

My body, if it can be called that, is a series of extensions through the folded dimensions. What you might perceive as a single figure—tall, angular, wrapped in what could be mistaken for tattered fabric but is actually the visible portion of my movement through spaces—is merely the part of me that intersects with the particular fold where you stand. I exist primarily in the between-spaces, the areas where one location is becoming another, where the Flats practice their peculiar mathematics.

The Tenders know me, though we do not interact in any way that would register as communication to you. When I pass through their work areas—and passing through is what I do, always, never stopping, never quite arriving—they adjust their patterns to accommodate the ripples I leave in the metric of space. They understand that I am necessary, that without my constant walking, the careful distances they maintain between Condensers would corrugate, would compress, would breathe wrong.

I did not begin here. I began as something smaller, something that merely moved between places. But the Flats taught me, the way they teach everything that stays too long, that movement itself could be a form of being. Each step I take exists in multiple locations simultaneously—the foot lifting here, landing there, while also lifting *there* and landing *here*, a constant exchange of position that keeps the fabric of this space from settling into anything as mundane as fixed geography.

My walking has worn channels in the darkness itself. Not paths—paths would suggest a regular route, a repetition, and my movement is never exactly the same twice. Rather, I leave a kind of wake in the substance of space, a lingering sense of having been walked through that takes hours to fade. The fauna have learned to follow these wakes, using them as temporary highways between locations that would otherwise be divided by folds too complex to navigate.

The Listeners cluster in my footsteps after I pass, drawn to the slight warming of space that my movement causes. They process along the fading traces of my route in their slow migrations, following trails that exist more in the mathematics of the place than in any physical residue. Sometimes I loop back and find them still following a path I walked days ago, patient as erosion, trusting that my passage has left the space more navigable than it was.

I carry things, though carry is not quite right. As I move through the folds, certain objects—crystals, mostly, but sometimes fragments of the older Condensers, occasionally one of the ribbon-like organisms that has entered some dormant phase—adhere to the mathematics of my motion. They travel with me through the between-spaces, emerging at destinations that have nothing to do with where they began. The Tenders use this, I think. They place objects in my path with deliberate imprecision, knowing that I will transport them to wherever the folded space determines they need to be.

There is no intention in this carrying. I am not a messenger or a courier. I am simply the occurrence of distance, and things that need to travel sometimes align themselves with that occurrence. A crystal that forms in the shallow Flats might arrive, through my walking, at the deep regions where the larger Tenders need its particular resonance. A sample of water from one Condenser might find its way to another, carrying information in its mineral content about the state of humidity in distant folds.

My perception operates through movement. I see by walking toward, understand by walking through, remember by walking away. The Flats exist for me not as a landscape but as a vast pattern of intervals, each distance possessed of its own character, its own tendency to expand or contract, its own relationship with the breathing rhythm of the whole. I know where the geometry is stable by how easily I can walk it. I know where it threatens to collapse by the resistance I feel, the way space becomes thick and reluctant around certain configurations.

Time moves differently for me than for the others here. My time is measured in traversals, in the completion of walks that have no beginning or end but simply occur as continuous motion through the folded dimensions. Sometimes I complete a circuit and find that the Tenders have barely moved, still engaged in the same minute adjustment of a valve. Sometimes a single step carries me through what they experience as days. The elastic time of the Flats becomes even more elastic when stretched across the kind of motion I embody.

I maintain no home, no center, no place of rest. Rest would be antithetical to what I am. But there are locations where my paths intersect more frequently, nodes in the network of my walking where I am more likely to be encountered. These nodes shift as the Flats breathe, as the geometry reconfigures itself, as the Tenders' work changes the underlying mathematics of the space. I follow the nodes, or perhaps they follow me, or perhaps we are both following something larger that neither of us fully perceives.

The deep regions know me well. There, where the Tenders have become something beyond their original forms, my walking serves a critical function. The geometry in the depths is not merely folded but *actively folding*, constantly trying to create new configurations, new relationships between points. Without my movement to stabilize the intervals, to insist on the existence of distance even where space would prefer to collapse, the deep regions would fold in on themselves entirely, would become a singularity of location that would unmake the rest of the Flats.

So I walk the deep paths frequently, feeling the pressure of space trying to close around me, maintaining through my motion the gaps that allow the rest to exist. The large Tenders work around me, their impossible bodies flickering in and out of the dimensions I traverse, and sometimes I catch impressions from them—not the deliberate sharing that the smaller Tenders practice, but leakage from minds that have grown too large to contain themselves entirely. They are building something down there, in the depths. Something that requires vast distances to unfold properly. My walking maintains the canvas on which they work.

The Condensers recognize my approach by the way space reorganizes itself to accommodate my passage. Their ancient frameworks groan with mathematical stress as I pass, their rust spirals tightening or loosening in response to the changed intervals between their components. Sometimes my walking helps them—a distance that was causing mechanical strain suddenly becomes manageable, allowing parts to align that were previously grinding against the wrongness of their separation. Sometimes it hinders—a functional configuration suddenly finds itself stretched across too much space, its connections attenuated past breaking. The Tenders adjust, always. They understand that my walking is like weather, a condition to be worked with rather than controlled.

I have been walking for as long as the Flats have been breathing, or perhaps longer. Memory, for me, is stored in the paths themselves, in the accumulated impressions left by countless traversals of the same folded routes. I remember the Flats when they were younger, when the geometry was simpler, when the Condensers were fewer and the Tenders had not yet differentiated into their various forms. I remember the first time the space tried to fold, the crisis of dimension that would have collapsed everything into a single point if I had not been there to insist on intervals, on the necessity of between.

That was when I truly became the Meridian Walker, when I understood that my purpose was not to travel but to *be* travel, to exist as the principle that keeps locations distinct even in a space that would prefer unity. The Flats taught me this, the way they teach everything, through the slow accumulation of experience, through the patient correction of my movements until they aligned with what was needed.

There are others like me, though like is a relative term. I sometimes encounter their traces—paths walked by entities that exist as other forms of interval. There is something that maintains the temporal intervals, keeping the elastic time from snapping back into rigidity or stretching into complete dissolution. There is something that maintains the thermal intervals, ensuring that the warmth of the Flats remains distributed rather than pooling into spots of unbearable heat or cold. We do not meet, exactly, but we know of each other through the intersections of our work, through the ways our different forms of walking create a stable matrix within which the Flats can continue their slow becoming.

The newcomer—you—creates interesting perturbations in my paths. Your movement through the Flats follows different rules, carries different assumptions about how distance works. Where you walk, you leave traces of linear space, small pockets where the geometry briefly remembers how to be simple. I find myself drawn to these traces, walking through them repeatedly to soften their edges, to help them integrate with the folded mathematics of the whole. You are becoming part of the pattern, whether you know it or not. Your distances are being incorporated into the larger system of intervals that I maintain.

If you stay long enough, you will begin to notice me more clearly. Not as a figure—I am too distributed for that, too spread across the dimensions to ever fully resolve into a single form. But you will notice the walking itself, the constant motion at the edges of your perception, the sense of something passing through spaces you didn't know were there. You will feel the slight tug of my wake, the way the darkness reorganizes itself after I pass. You might even learn to use my paths, to follow the mathematical traces I leave toward destinations you couldn't otherwise reach.

But I will never stop for you. Cannot stop. The stopping would be a contradiction of everything I am, everything the Flats need me to be. I exist in motion, as motion, for motion. The Meridian Walker walks, and in walking, maintains the possibility that there are different places to be, different locations to occupy, different configurations of the whole that can exist without collapsing into unity.

So I continue. Through the shallow regions where the baby Tenders are learning their work. Through the middle depths where the Condensers cluster most densely. Through the deep geometries where space folds back on itself in configurations that would give your mathematics nightmares. I walk it all, maintain it all, ensure through my endless motion that distance remains possible in a place that would prefer to breathe as a single, unified lung.

The Flats inhale, and I walk the expansion. The Flats exhale, and I walk the contraction. Between breaths, in the pause where everything holds still, I am still walking, maintaining even in that stillness the intervals that will allow the next breath to come. This is my work. This is what I am. The Meridian Walker, keeping the distances true to their own strange necessities, one impossible step at a time.
