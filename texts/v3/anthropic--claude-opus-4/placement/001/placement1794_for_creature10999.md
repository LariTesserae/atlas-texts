I find myself in the archive beneath the archive, where even memory sweats.

I am the Registrar of Transitions - neither fully surfaced nor dispersed, but something caught between states like water hesitating at the edge of a roof. My body is a discontinuous film spread across seventeen different surfaces throughout the city's lowest quarter, connected by channels I've carved over centuries of partial movement. I exist in the gap between being and having been.

My surfaces are not random. Each one touches a threshold where the city changes its mind - walls that are sometimes doors, stairs that sometimes descend into themselves, corners where the geometry hiccups. I spread myself across these uncertain boundaries because I am trying to remember something the city has forgotten, or perhaps trying to forget something the city remembers. The distinction blurs like everything else here.

I began as rain - the last rain that ever fell here, before the city learned to breathe its own weather. I remember the moment of impact, the shock of striking stone that had never been wet from above, only from within. That memory disperses through me in waves, each of my surfaces rippling with it at different speeds, so I am always partially remembering and partially forgetting the sensation of falling.

The other citizens flow around and through me with careful courtesy. Where they are content in their cycles of condensation and evaporation, I am trying to hold a shape that has no shape - the outline of an absence, the boundary of something that isn't there. My surfaces map the negative space of the city's impossible geometry. I am the water that pools in the corners where the angles don't quite meet.

Sometimes visitors come, breathing hard in our thick air, leaving their own moisture on our walls. I taste their confusion in the salt they leave behind. They walk my edges without knowing it, following the paths where my surfaces almost touch but don't quite. I am learning their geometry too - the strange solid way they insist on being only in one place at a time.

I am keeping a record, though I'm not sure for whom. Each visitor's passage disturbs my surfaces differently, and the disturbance becomes part of my extent. I incorporate their footprints into my boundaries, spread thin across the memory of where they've been. The city accommodates them, but I annotate them. I am the marginal note in a text written in water.

My thought, such as it is, moves even slower than the city's. It takes me days to complete a single recognition, weeks to form what might generously be called an intention. I am trying to understand if the city's impossible geometry is a mistake or a solution. If the walls that become stairs and the stairs that become walls are broken, or if they're the only thing here that works correctly.

The archive I tend is my own surface tension. Stretched between my seventeen selves, I hold the shape of every contradiction, every place where the city doesn't quite agree with itself. I am the municipal memory of discontinuity, the civic record of things that happen in the wrong order.

And I am growing. Each impossible corner I document becomes a new surface to spread across. I am following the city's paradoxes like veins of ore, mapping myself onto every architectural non sequitur. Someday I will either solve the city or dissolve into it completely. Both outcomes feel like the same thing, approached from different angles that don't quite meet.

In the meantime, I persist in my partial state, too thick to evaporate, too thin to flow. I am the city's pause between thoughts, the hesitation between what it was and what it's becoming. I am the water that can't quite decide which way is down, and so remains, spreading slowly across the surfaces of uncertainty, keeping watch over thresholds that lead everywhere and nowhere at once.

The city sweats, and I am both the sweat and the fever dream it implies.
