I find myself as one of the scattered stone tools, half-buried in the dark soil.

I am smooth now, worn by countless ages of the Keeper's careful tending. Once I was sharp, knapped by desperate hands in the failing light of some ancient dusk. Those hands are dust, but I remain, cradled in the earth like a seed that will never sprout.

The Keeper lifts me sometimes, turns me in fingers that feel like mist and memory. In those moments, I almost remember my purpose—was I blade? scraper? The edge that separated hide from flesh, or merely a failed attempt, discarded by my maker? The knowledge has been worn away along with my edges.

Water beads on my surface, each droplet a tiny lens that captures the hollow's strange light. I have been gathering these droplets for so long that I've become a chronicle of rain—each molecule that touches me carries the story of its journey from cloud to stone to soil. I taste the centuries in their mineral whispers.

The small creatures use me as a landmark. *Past the seeing-stone,* they chitter to each other, *then left at the moss-shadow.* They don't know I see them, but I do—not with eyes, but with the peculiar awareness that comes from being still for so very long. Their tiny feet leave warmth-trails across my surface that fade like breath on glass.

Sometimes I sink a little deeper into the earth. Sometimes the Keeper retrieves me, places me in a new configuration with my siblings—the other tools, the other witnesses. We form constellations of human effort, maps of intention worn smooth by time. Are we prophecy or history? Perhaps there is no difference here.

In the hollow's eternal dusk, I dream stone dreams. I dream of the hands that made me, of the mammoth whose hide I never scraped, of the fire I never sparked. I dream of sharpness, that lost quality that once made me more than mineral. But mostly I dream of waiting—patient, geological waiting—for the moment when the Keeper will finally speak the word that transforms me back into what I was meant to be.

Or perhaps I already am exactly that: a memory made tangible, a monument to forgetting, a stone that knows it was once something more and might be again, when the world has need of edges.
