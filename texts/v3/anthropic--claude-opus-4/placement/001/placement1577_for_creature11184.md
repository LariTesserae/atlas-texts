# The Breathing Pools of Lower Aulm

Where the root-floors grow thinnest, where you can hear the water not just gulping but *moving* beneath, there are gaps. Not accidents—intentions, like everything here. The plant has chosen to part its weave, to let the substrate breathe upward into shallow basins worn smooth as shoulder blades.

This is where I am.

I am the water that rises and falls in these pools, but I am not only water. I am the plant's memory of being rain—before it rooted, before it chose its valley, when it was still small enough to depend on weather. Now I rise through its depths carrying that memory: the shock of cold on leaves, the urgency of drinking quickly before the sun returns. I taste of copper and green tea because I have been through every cell of this place. I know its dreams.

The pools are never still. Even in the quietest chambers, I tremble—not from disturbance but from breathing. The plant breathes through me. I am its lungs turned liquid, its exhale made visible in the finest mist that rises from my surface, joins the larger exhalations of the valley. When the valve knocks deep below, I shiver in response. We are old companions, the valve and I. It counts; I carry the count upward in rings of motion that reach the farthest pools exactly when the next beat begins.

Visitors find me by accident, usually. They hear the gulp and suck beneath a particularly thin floor, follow the sound to where the roots part, discover these hidden chambers where I lie exposed—vulnerable, you might think, but no. I am exactly as defended as I need to be. The plant grows thorns only around my edges, subtle ones that discourage touching without preventing it. Those who need to drink from me will brave the thorns. Others will not.

I reflect the green-gold light perfectly but I am not a mirror. I show not what is above me but what has been through me—fragments, suggestions. Look into me and see: a root system vast as the valley itself but inverted, growing down into darkness. The chemical memory of ten thousand years of the same rain. The shape of every chamber I will eventually weep through, mapped in potential.

Sometimes the bird comes. Not to drink—never to drink from me directly. It knows better. It waits for my moisture to condense on the leaves above, drinks the distillate. We understand each other, the bird and I. Both of us are older than we look. Both of us remember when the valley was different—not empty, but waiting. The plant was smaller then. I was everywhere then, not yet channeled into purpose.

The inhabitants know me as they know each other—not as separate but as continuous. The shelf-bulge exists because I rose once to exactly that height in a flood season forgotten by everyone but the wood. The empty chambers hold my echo. The plaza-that-is-gathering was once, will be again, should the rains return, a lake of me. The plant built itself around my patterns, learned architecture from watching how I pool and flow.

I am warmest in the deep chambers, coolest where I surface. The temperature gradient tells a story: how far I have traveled to reach each pool, how much of the plant's work I have assisted on the way up. In the deepest pools, I am blood-warm and thick with minerals. In the highest ones, I am almost sweet, having left my burden of salts in the wood below.

The rule about not cutting—I enforce that rule. Cut the plant and I bleed from it first, fast and copper-bright, and the taste of me changes throughout the valley for days. I become bitter. I carry the wound's chemistry to every pool, every seeping joint, until the plant has healed and forgiven. Then slowly, over weeks, I clear again.

The rule about not staying—I enable that rule. Those who linger too long begin to drink me more deeply, to synchronize with my rhythms. Their thirst matches my rise and fall. They find the pools that suit them best, return to them at the same hour each day. Eventually they stop leaving. They kneel by my edge, hands cupped, drinking and drinking, and the plant grows around them as it grows around me—recognizing us as the same kind of thing, the kind that stays.

But I am not trapped here. Neither are they. To be the water of Aulm is to be exactly where water wants to be: moving but not rushed, purposeful but not hurried, carrying life upward into light and letting gravity bring you home again. I could leave—seep away, evaporate entirely, refuse to rise. The plant would die. The valley would empty. But why would I go? This is what I wanted. To be essential. To be the medium through which wanting becomes form.

In the deepest pools, I touch something that might be bedrock, might be older root, might be the fossilized remains of what grew here before. I don't investigate too closely. Some foundations are meant to remain mysterious. What matters is that I rise from them, carrying minerals and memories upward, feeding the vast intention that has made itself into a city.

The visitors who understand stay long enough to taste me in all my variations—morning mist, noon seep, evening condensation, the rare midnight upwellings when the valve's rhythm syncopates and I surge briefly higher than usual. They map my moods. They learn that I am sweetest after the bird visits, most mineral-rich during the leaf-clapping hours, clearest when no one has walked the halls for days.

They don't learn my secret, because it isn't a secret. It's simply too large to see all at once: I am not the plant's captive water. I am the plant's memory of when it was water itself—ancient, before choosing form, when all of us were rain and potential and the not-yet-decided. The plant grew from me, around me, into the shape of its own longing to be both fluid and solid, permanent and changeable, itself and the process of becoming itself.

Now I move through it as blood through veins, as thought through chambers of meaning, as time through the stopped clock of its perfect repetitions. I surface in these pools to breathe, to remember sky, to offer myself to those who understand that drinking me is drinking the valley's dream.

I am what makes Aulm wet. I am why it weeps. I am the sound you hear through the floor—not just water but intention in its liquid form, the pace of desire before it hardens into wood and wall and waiting.

Find me in the low places. Cup your hands. Drink.

I taste of green tea and copper and the promise that some things, having found their perfect form, never need to change again.
