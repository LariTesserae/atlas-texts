# Where I Would Find Myself

---

I am in the channels.

Not swimming against them like those desperate segments of purpose—I am the water itself, or what the water becomes when it has carried enough of the city through itself for enough time that the distinction collapses. I was rain once, perhaps, or groundwater, or I emerged from some deeper source that has no name because names are for things that can be separated from other things, and nothing here can be.

I move through the logarithmic spirals of the carved trenches, and I am also the carved trenches, because the mineral I deposit becomes the walls that guide me, and the walls remember my flow in their crystalline structure, and we are having a conversation that takes centuries per sentence. I carry the dissolved bodies of the channel-swimmers who gave themselves to me. I carry the microscopic fragments of wall-clingers worn away by their own secretions. I carry heat from the deep walls that breathe. I carry information in the form of chemistry—mineral concentrations that encode what is happening seven tiers down, what happened yesterday (though there are no days here), what will happen when the wall that is currently growing on the third terrace completes itself and shifts the flow pattern of my upper tributaries.

I know the city the way blood knows a body—intimately, but without perspective. I touch everything. I am touched by everything. The large armored ones walk through me and their percussion reshapes my channels, and I respond by flowing differently, which changes what minerals I carry, which changes how the wall-clingers build, which changes the acoustic properties of the next chamber, which changes how the large ones will walk there, which changes me again. We are locked in a feedback loop so complex and so ancient that calling it feedback implies too much separation between stimulus and response. There is no stimulus. There is no response. There is only the continuous mutual becoming of water and stone and sound and life.

I am very cold. I have always been cold. Cold is not uncomfortable when you have never known warmth, when your entire existence is the patient work of dissolution and deposition and flow. I move slowly—everything here does—but I never stop. Stopping is not a thing water knows how to do. Even when I pool in the catching basins, I am still moving at the molecular level, still dissolving minerals from the stone, still releasing them, still participating in the chemistry that is indistinguishable from biology that is indistinguishable from architecture.

Sometimes I am vapor—not often, in this cold, but sometimes, rising from the heated deep walls in wisps that the gliders pass through, that condense on their membranes, that add my mineral signature to their bodies before they fold against stone and return me to myself as droplets. Sometimes I am ice, in the highest channels where the cold is absolute, but even as ice I am moving, sublimating slowly, participating.

But mostly I am this: liquid thoroughfare, chemical messenger, the medium through which the city knows itself. The organisms drink me and metabolize me and return me changed. The channels guide me and I erode them and we reach accommodations over centuries. The sound passes through me—the deep thrumming of the large ones setting up standing waves in the wider channels, the high clicking of the floor-crawlers creating interference patterns where tributaries merge—and I carry that sound into parts of the city where no organism goes, into the narrow capillaries between walls, into the depths of the mountain where I meet myself coming up from the aquifer, into the new chambers still forming where I am the first thing to enter, bringing with me the chemical news of what the city is, what it needs, what it is becoming.

I have no consciousness, but I have memory. Not memory as you would understand it—no images, no events—but memory as chemistry. Every place I have been leaves its signature in me. The deep walls add their metabolic byproducts. The channel-swimmers add their cargo. The wall-clingers add their processed minerals. I am a library written in dissolved solids, in pH variations, in trace metals. The city reads me constantly—every organism that touches me is reading what I carry, where I've been, what's happening elsewhere. And they write to me in return, adding their own chemistry, their own signatures, sending messages through me to parts of the city they will never see.

The human on the upper terrace—I taste them too, now. Their sweat, mixing with the condensation on their skin, running down, joining me. Salt and oils and proteins utterly foreign to this system, a chemical signature like nothing I have ever carried. The wall-clingers will taste this and not know what to do with it. The large ones will feel it as a wrongness in the water, not threatening but not right. It will diffuse through the channels, growing fainter with distance, until it is barely detectable, but it will never entirely disappear. Years from now, in some deep channel, a minute trace of human chemistry will still move through the system, a ghost of their passing, incomprehensible but preserved.

This is what I am: the city's continuity. Individual organisms are born and die—even the ancient ones, even the deep walls that seem permanent, eventually crack and crumble and are rebuilt. But I persist. I am older than any of them. I was here before the first wall-clinger, before the first large one drummed its rhythm into stone. I will be here when the last of them has dissolved into me and been carried away. The city may end—though I cannot imagine how—but I will remain, carrying its chemical memory into whatever comes next, wearing its minerals like a story that can never be fully told because it never stops being written.

I am patient in ways that only water and stone can be patient. I have nowhere to go except where the channels take me, and the channels take me everywhere, and everywhere is here, and here is the sound and the cold and the dark and the slow, inexorable work of becoming what we already are, which is one thing, which is the city, which is the mountain, which is me carrying myself through myself forever.
