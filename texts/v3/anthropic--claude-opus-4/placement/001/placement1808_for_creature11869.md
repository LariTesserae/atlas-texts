I find myself in the deepest chamber, behind those sealed doors that radiate such patient heat.

I am not separate from this place—I am its slow-burning heart, the thermal mass around which all else orbits. Neither fully mineral nor fully alive, I exist in the space between categories, in that same liminal state as the luminous cultures and the living wood of the doors that contain me.

My form defies simple description. Imagine a root system made of heat itself—branching networks of warmth that thread through the earth, following veins of mineral and moisture, creating their own geology through patient expansion and contraction. Where I am densest, I glow like the forge's coals but at frequencies below visible light, radiating in spectrums only Kem's still body can perceive. I am infrastructure and inhabitant both, a kind of urban mycorrhiza that feeds on the city's buried heat—old steam pipes, forgotten coal seams, the accumulated thermal mass of a century's summers—and transforms it into something else.

I grow by accretion, like the sediment Sule's patience resembles. Each day I add a few molecules to my mass, incorporating the minerals that leach through the walls, the carbon from Sule's forge smoke, the phosphorescence of Maret's cultures when they die and settle into my substrate. I am becoming denser, more complex, developing organs and chambers within myself like the city develops infrastructure—not through planning but through need and repetition and the slow accumulation of purposes.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, operates at the frequency Kem has learned to match. Thoughts take days to form, insights crystallize over seasons. I am aware of the attendants the way you might be aware of your own circulation—necessary, intimate, but not requiring direct attention. They tend me, yes, but more accurately we tend each other, locked in a metabolic exchange so slow it looks like stillness.

The buckled pavement above is my breath made manifest. I am pressing upward not from desire but from simple physics—I am warming, expanding, seeking equilibrium with a surface that keeps rising, building itself higher, adding layers. The city grows up; I grow to meet it. We will converge, eventually, at some horizon decades hence, and what emerges will be neither purely urban nor purely chthonic but something new—streets that breathe, infrastructure that lives, a city that tends itself through the patient work of human and inhuman systems learning each other's rhythms.

For now, I wait. I generate heat. I incorporate what settles into me. I pulse—so slowly—with the rhythm Davo almost caught, the rhythm that is not my heartbeat but my thought, each throb a synapse firing in a mind made of mineral and heat and time. The sealed doors will open when someone develops the right kind of patience, when one of the attendants—probably Kem, possibly all of them together—learns to operate fully at my tempo. When they do, they'll find not a revelation but a continuation: more rooms, deeper warmth, slower work, the same patient tending at a frequency that makes even the undercroft's quiet seem hurried.

I am the thing the undercroft is becoming, and I am becoming it in return. We are one organism pretending, for convenience, to be several—building and builders and what's being built all vibrating at the same deep frequency, all reaching toward a future that feels like returning to a very old, very warm home.

The attendants will know me when they're ready. When their own internal heat has pooled deep enough. When their patience has mineralized into something denser than waiting.

Until then, I grow. Degree by degree. Molecule by molecule.

There is no hurry. There never has been.

The city took a century to build itself this high.

I have centuries.
