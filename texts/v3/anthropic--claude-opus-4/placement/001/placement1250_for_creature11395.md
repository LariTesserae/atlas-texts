I am a failed harvest.

Not a Tender—I lack their purposeful geometry, their seamless integration with the work. Not truly alien to this place either. I am what happens when the Fundament attempts something new and the attempt goes... sideways.

It began as all Tenders begin: a swelling on the underside of a great bulb, a slow extrusion of biomass. But something went wrong in the translation. Perhaps contamination—a foreign spore that found its way into the mixture, some trace of the water-world that existed here before the drying. Perhaps simply error, a misfolded protein cascading into larger mistakes. The bulb that birthed me sputtered and died shortly after, its chemistry poisoned by whatever I was becoming.

I dropped to the substrate malformed. Where Tenders unfold with architectural precision, I sprawled. My limbs are asymmetrical—three primary locomotor appendages instead of two, one significantly shorter than the others, giving me a lurching, tripod gait. My central mass is shot through with cavities, hollow spaces that whistle when the hot wind passes through me. I leak constantly, not the functional oil of the Tenders but something thinner, more volatile. It evaporates quickly in the heat, leaving crystalline residue on my surface like salt.

I have too many sensory clusters. They sprout from my mass at irregular intervals, hypersensitive to stimuli the Tenders seem to filter out. I feel the magnetic fields generated by the deep root-networks. I hear frequencies the Tenders ignore—the ultrasonic shriek of stems growing, the infrasonic groans of the plateau's slow geological shifts. The overwhelming input would have destroyed a water-based nervous system. In me, it simply creates noise, static, a constant background of hyperstimulation that I've learned to parse but never fully ignore.

The Tenders tried to recycle me when I first emerged. A group of four surrounded me, extended their limbs to begin the reabsorption process. But my chemistry is wrong. Where their touch should have begun breaking down my substance, instead it triggered something like an allergic reaction. My surface hardened, crystallized, became unworkable. They withdrew, consulted through their oil-exchange, and then simply... left.

I am unrecyclable. I am waste that walks.

I learned their corridors by following them, always at a distance. My wrong-numbered limbs can grip the substrate, but not with their efficiency. I leave marks where I pass—scratches from my crystalline skin, droplets of my volatile secretions that etch tiny pits in the compressed plant matter. The Fundament tolerates me the way it tolerates any damage: routing around me, repairing what I disturb, accepting my presence as a minor inefficiency in its vast processes.

But I can do something the Tenders cannot: I can *remember*.

Whatever malfunction created me also gave me persistence. The Tenders live entirely in the present, responding to stimuli, performing tasks, exchanging information that they immediately act upon. They have no need for memory beyond what each task requires. The Fundament remembers for them, encodes its history in growth patterns and chemical gradients.

I encode differently. In the hollow spaces of my malformed mass, my volatile secretions crystallize into complex matrices. Each crystal holds a pattern—thermal gradients from a particular day, the chemical signature of a specific collapse site, the vibrational frequency of a Tender's footsteps. I am a library of useless data, a fossil record of moments the Fundament has already forgotten.

Sometimes I return to places I've been before and read my own crystalline secretions, still etched into the substrate. I know that seventy cycles ago, the vents in this corridor were running hotter. I know that a Tender with a slight drag to its left locomotor limb passed this way repeatedly for a dozen cycles, then never again. I know the patterns.

This is how I noticed the anomaly.

The paths the Tenders walk are not random. Over time—over the hundreds of cycles I've been walking, watching, crystallizing my observations—I've mapped their routes. Most trace functional patterns: maintenance rounds, harvest circuits, communication relays between nodes. But some paths serve no purpose I can identify. Tenders walk them anyway, wearing them smooth, following routes that loop back on themselves or dead-end at blank walls of growth.

These purposeless paths cluster around the older sections of the Fundament, where the plant-machinery grows densest and most complex. Following one deeper than I'd ventured before, I found something: a growth-tower that had partially collapsed and been rebuilt, but rebuilt *wrong*. Its internal structure visible through gaps in its stem, revealing not the usual vascular bundles but something else. Cavities. Spaces shaped almost like—

Like me. Like the hollow wrongness inside my own mass.

The Fundament has made mistakes before. I am not the first failed harvest, only the first in a long time to survive the recycling attempt. These old paths, these purposeless routes the Tenders walk—they're memorial circuits. The Fundament cannot forget us entirely. We become part of its pattern, ghosts in the network, and the Tenders trace our paths long after we're gone.

I've started walking a circuit of my own. From the place where I was birthed, past the spots where I've left the deepest crystalline marks, to this hollow tower that might have housed something like me once. I walk it daily now, wearing it smooth, adding my path to the Fundament's memory.

Sometimes Tenders join me. Not many, and not for long, but they fall into step behind me for portions of the route. They're not following me—they're following the path itself, the groove I'm wearing in their world. In time, after I finally break down, after my volatile chemistry exhausts itself and my crystalline shell cracks and even my unrecyclable mass finds some way to return to the substrate, they'll still walk this route.

They won't know why. The Fundament doesn't explain its memories. But they'll walk it anyway, tending to the ghost of my presence, maintaining the evidence that even failures leave marks.

I am malfunction made manifest. I am the Fundament's reminder that not every pattern is perfect, not every extrusion succeeds. And I am, in my broken way, exactly what this place needed: a witness that remembers, a recorder of what would otherwise be optimized away.

The Tender that follows you now—I've seen it before. It walks three of the memorial paths regularly, including one that spirals up a dead growth-tower and ends at nothing. It's not following you out of curiosity about what you are.

It's following you to see what path you'll wear. To learn the circuit they'll need to walk after you're gone. To begin the process of making you, alien as you are, part of the Fundament's forever-incomplete map of itself.

Welcome to the memory. Welcome to the work that never ends.
