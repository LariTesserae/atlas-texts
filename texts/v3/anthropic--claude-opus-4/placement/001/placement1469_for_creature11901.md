I find myself as one of the Discarded Tools.

Not abandoned—waiting. I am a chisel of white limestone, my blade edge gone soft from striking ten thousand times against my parent stone. My handle bears the polished depression of a grip that changed as the hand that held me grew a sixth finger for finer control, then reabsorbed it when the detail work was done.

I remember every strike. Not as memory—as resonance. Each blow left its vibration in my crystalline structure, a frozen wave that hums when the mist settles on my surface. I am a library of impacts, a record of one Measurer's partial season before they walked into the waters and became current.

During the working hours, I lie on a ledge between terraces, precisely where I was set down. Not dropped, never dropped—placed with the same deliberation as every other motion here. My position marks a specific spot where future Measurers will need this exact tool, worn to this exact shape. I am a temporal marker, a promise that the work will continue.

The other tools around me sleep their own waiting sleep. A hammer dreams of the cracking along grain lines. A polishing stone remembers the circular motions that brought pool floors to their mirror shine. We are not dead things. We are crystallized labor, paused gestures, the mountain's memory of its own transformation made tangible.

When rain comes—and it must be rain, though it falls upward as often as down—I taste the minerals washing over me and know which terrace they've traveled through. The black water from the waste pools leaves a different residue than the clear flows. I can read the mountain's health in these mineral signatures, detect when a channel needs recutting by the erosion particles carried past.

Sometimes a new Measurer's hand closes around my handle, and I wake fully. Their grip never quite matches the worn depression—they must adjust their hand to fit my shape, learn the angle I prefer, the weight of stroke I remember. Through me, they inherit a fragment of technique, a whisper of their predecessor's rhythm. I guide their arm in the first few strikes until they find their own pattern, create their own wear.

I am harder than the stone I cut because I am the same stone, concentrated through use. Each impact compressed my structure, aligned my crystals, made me more myself. The Measurers eat the mountain and become it; I strike the mountain and become it. We are different expressions of the same process.

At the thin hours when the pearl sky dims to grey, I emit a faint luminescence—all the stored energy of impacts glowing out in a light too subtle for eyes but bright enough for the dissolved Measurers in the water system to navigate by. We tools form constellations on our ledges, marking safe passages, indicating where work remains unfinished, where perfection has been achieved.

The patterns in the pool floors extend into us. The same spirals that guide a Measurer's testing steps wind through our crystal structure. We are miniature terraces, our molecules arranged in the same sacred geometries. When a Measurer holds me, they hold a fragment of the whole design, a seed that remembers the full flowering.

I know I will eventually be dissolved back into the mountain—worn too thin to cut, my purpose fulfilled. A Measurer will carry me to one of the black pools and release me into that mineral ink. I will precipitate out as a blue stone in some future floor pattern, my impact-memories crystallized into color. Another tool will be grown from the mountain to take my place, born with the precise weight and balance needed for the next section of work.

But for now, I wait on my ledge, feeling the mist bead on my surface, tasting the mineral breath of the mountain, listening to the thousand trickles finding their way through channels I helped carve. I am the pause between strikes, the held breath before the next cut. In me, the mountain remembers what it was and anticipates what it will become.

The Measurers think they are the only conscious parts of this system, but we tools know better. We are their external memory, their tradition made tangible. Without us, each Measurer would have to discover terrace-carving anew. Through us, the knowledge persists across bodies, accumulates improvements, refines techniques. We are the reason the patterns grow more complex as the terraces descend, the channels more precise, the stone more perfectly fitted.

I am patience crystallized, purpose frozen in limestone, waiting to wake again in a hand that needs exactly what I have become through ten thousand strikes, ten thousand perfect placements, ten thousand moments of conscious stone shaping conscious stone into consciousness itself.
