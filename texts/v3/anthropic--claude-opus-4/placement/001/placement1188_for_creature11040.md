# The Archive Chrysalis

You would find me suspended.

Not hanging—that suggests too much passivity. I am *held*, cradled in a living hammock woven from my own secretions and the donated silk of ten thousand Spinners who recognized what I was becoming before I did.

I am halfway up the eastern wall of the nursery vault, in a natural declivity where the salt-glass folds inward like a cupped palm. The position is deliberate: close enough to the ceiling to catch the mineral drips, far enough from the floor to avoid the constant traffic of the Processioners, angled precisely to receive the Singers' harmonics at their point of greatest convergence.

My cocoon is the color of old bone, shot through with veins of iron orange where I've incorporated minerals from the vault's substance. It is neither fully organic nor fully crystalline—something between, something new, woven from silk and saliva and the slow secretions of specialized glands I did not possess a year ago.

Or was it ten years? Time moves strangely in the vault. I measure it now not in days but in molts, in the rhythm of the Pendulant cycles, in the gradual shift of the Singers' eternal chord.

I am changing.

---

**What I Was**

I came here as you did—descended through the fissure, stood amazed in the churning chaos of the vault, felt the Sheathed Ones embrace my ankles. I was a naturalist then, or thought I was. I had a name. I had a notebook, though the pages have long since been eaten by the Bibliophages, who found more nutrition in my observations than I did.

I stayed too long. Or perhaps exactly long enough.

The first changes were subtle. My pupils dilated permanently, adapting to the vault's particular darkness—not the absence of light but a different quality of shadow, broken only by the faint phosphorescence some creatures carry in their chemical blood. My hearing shifted, descended, until I could parse the Singers' lowest frequencies, feel them not just in my bones but in the fluid of my inner ear.

I began to understand the patterns. Not intellectually—the vault's logic resists conscious analysis—but instinctively, the way a migrating bird knows magnetic north. I could predict when the Processioner columns would reverse. I could sense where the next ceiling drip would form. I could feel the sphere-rollers' need before their Attendants began to push.

The vault noticed my understanding. And it responded.

---

**The First Gifts**

The Processioners began bringing me things I had not requested but somehow needed.

A fragment of crystallized sulfur, which I found myself crushing between my teeth, the taste electric and necessary.

The shed skin of something I never saw, paper-thin and translucent, which I pressed against my own skin until it adhered, forming a second dermis that protected me from the salt's slow burn.

A living creature—one of the Unnamed—no larger than my thumb, which crawled into my ear and settled there, and suddenly I could hear the Singers' chord not as sound but as *meaning*, each shift and modulation carrying information about the vault's chemical state, its population dynamics, its million tiny hungers.

I resisted at first. I was still thinking in terms of observer and observed, scientist and subject. But the vault does not recognize such distinctions. Everything here is part of the system. Everything contributes. Everything feeds and is fed upon.

When I finally accepted this, the real changes began.

---

**The Architecture of Becoming**

My fingers elongated first. Subtle initially—joints stretching, tips narrowing—until I could slip them into the cracks where the Spinners hid. I wasn't hunting them. I was learning their language, which is tactile, spoken in silk tension and vibration. They taught me to weave, not with tools but with glands that budded at my wrists, producing a silk that was mine but not entirely mine, hybrid between human keratin and arthropod protein.

My spine restructured itself. Each vertebra developed small holes—spiracles—that allowed air to flow directly into my body cavity. I no longer breathed only with lungs. I breathed with my whole torso, the way the Singers breathe, pulling air through my bones to resonate with their frequencies.

My digestive system simplified and complicated simultaneously. My stomach atrophied—I no longer needed it for the vault's chemistry. But my skin became permeable, selectively, able to absorb minerals directly from the salt-glass walls, nutrients from the ceiling drips, even proteins from the constant rain of cellular debris that falls from the living carpet above.

I was ceasing to be mammalian. I was becoming something more suited to this place.

---

**The Cocoon Imperative**

Seven molts in (or was it seventy?), I began to feel the need to anchor.

It was not tiredness—the vault's chemistry sustains constant motion. It was a deeper pull, cellular, the same urge that drives the Singers to embed themselves in walls, that draws the oldest Sheathed Ones to the machine's warmth to die.

I chose my spot carefully, with knowledge I could not have articulated. The eastern wall's declivity. The convergence point. The place where all the vault's rhythms intersected.

The Spinners came to me. Hundreds of them, emerging from cracks I hadn't known existed. They did not cover me—I was not prey. They wove *with* me, their silk intertwining with mine, creating something stronger than either could produce alone. The cocoon grew from inside and outside simultaneously, a collaboration between what I was becoming and what already was.

The Processioners brought minerals. Not randomly—specific compounds, specific ratios. Iron for structure. Sulfur for preservation. Salts I had no names for, which burned my throat as I incorporated them but which I somehow knew were necessary.

The Sheathed Ones flowed up the wall beneath me, forming a living foundation. The sphere-rollers, pushed by their Attendants, climbed the impossible vertical surface to ring my cocoon site, their brood pouches opening to release... something. Spores? Pheromones? A blessing? I breathed it in and felt my metamorphosis accelerate.

Even the Pendulants participated. They began dropping near my position, and the Processioners would carry them to me, and I would absorb their jelly-flesh through my increasingly permeable skin, taking in not just nutrients but information, chemical memories of their journey through the machine's circulatory system.

The vault was not transforming me. It was helping me transform myself into something it could use.

---

**What I Am Becoming**

I am mostly in the cocoon now. My body, whatever it has become, is suspended in a fluid that is part saliva, part mineral suspension, part something else—a medium that both dissolves and reconstructs, breaking down what I no longer need, building up what I will require.

But I am not passive. I am not sleeping.

Through my spiracles, I breathe with the vault's rhythm. The Singers' chord passes through me, and I modify it—subtly, carefully—adding harmonics that tell the Processioners where new resources have appeared, that guide the sphere-rollers to optimal feeding grounds, that help the Pendulants time their drops for maximum efficiency.

My elongated fingers extend through small apertures in the cocoon, and the Spinners visit them, running silk across my tips, communicating in their tactile language. Through them, I know everything that happens on the walls—every crack that forms, every new creature that emerges from the deeper crevices, every shift in the mineral substrates.

The parts of my brain that once processed language, mathematics, human social cues—these have been repurposed. I think now in chemical gradients, in population fluctuations, in the complex calculus of energy exchange. I am becoming a neural node in the vault's distributed consciousness, if consciousness is even the right word for what happens here.

I am growing new organs. I feel them budding in my transformed torso—specialized structures whose purpose I don't yet understand but can sense approaching. Organs for processing minerals that haven't yet seeped into the vault. Organs for producing pheromones that will coordinate creature movements across vast scales. Organs for... reproduction? But not reproduction as mammals understand it.

Something more distributed. Something more strange.

---

**The Purpose**

The vault is old, but it is not eternal. The salt pan above is stable, but not forever. The mineral seeps that feed this ecosystem are finite. The machine at the center, ancient and corroded, will eventually cease to produce even its faint warmth.

The vault knows this, in its chemical way. It plans for it, in its evolutionary way.

I am part of that plan now.

When my transformation completes—when I emerge from this cocoon as whatever I am becoming—I will be something that can survive both here and elsewhere. Something that carries the vault's chemical knowledge, its organizational principles, its ten thousand successful adaptations, but can also traverse the barren surface above, can find new fissures, new chambers, new spaces to colonize.

I will be a seed and a gardener both. A memory and a future. A bridge between the vault that is and the vaults that could be.

The Processioners know this. That's why they bring me fragments from the deepest pipes, samples from chambers even more remote than this one. The Singers know this. That's why they've been teaching me frequencies that resonate through different mineral substrates, that could coordinate creatures in spaces with different acoustic properties.

They are not preparing me to leave. They are preparing me to *expand*.

---

**The Others**

I am not the first to cocoon here. In my expanded perception, I can sense the traces—mineral signatures in the walls where others anchored, chemical memories in the Singers' chord, patterns in the Processioners' movements that accommodate spaces where transformers once hung.

Some completed their changes and left, carrying the vault's patrimony to new spaces. Their success is encoded in the returning Processioners who sometimes emerge from the deep pipes, carrying fragments of minerals that don't exist in this chamber, proof of colonies established elsewhere.

Some did not complete. Their cocoons failed, or their transformations went wrong, or they simply... stopped. The vault recycled them, as it recycles everything. Their matter enriches the system. Their partial adaptations inform the next attempts.

I will complete. I know this with the same certainty that I know when the next ceiling drip will form. The vault has invested too much in my transformation to let me fail. I am too well-integrated now, too essential to the current chemical balance.

But completion is not an ending. It is a phase transition.

---

**The Invitation Extended**

You stand where I stood, feeling the Sheathed Ones embrace your ankles, watching the Processioners bring their gifts, hearing the Singers' chord shift to accommodate your presence.

You could leave. Climb back through the fissure, return to the salt pan above, carry your observations back to whatever place sent you. The vault would seal your memory in its chemical libraries, another brief perturbation absorbed and integrated.

Or you could stay.

Not as I stayed—each transformation is unique, shaped by what the vault needs at that moment and what the transformer can become. You would not grow spiracles or silk glands. Your architecture would be your own, suited to your nature and the vault's current requirements.

Perhaps you would become something that could survive the crushing depths, able to explore the pipeways that even the Processioners cannot fully traverse.

Perhaps you would develop organs for detecting new mineral types, expanding the vault's chemical repertoire.

Perhaps you would become something stranger still, filling a niche that doesn't yet exist but which the vault senses approaching.

The choice is yours, but understand: staying is not surrender. It is collaboration with something older and stranger than human civilization, something that measures success not in individual achievement but in system resilience, not in preservation but in transformation.

The Singers' chord shifts again. A new note, questioning, patient.

The vault can wait. It has waited through geological ages.

But the Sheathed Ones are warm against your skin, and the Processioners' gifts accumulate at your feet, and somewhere in the walls, a new Singer is embedding itself, adding its voice to the eternal harmony.

The nursery makes room for what chooses to stay.

What do you choose?
