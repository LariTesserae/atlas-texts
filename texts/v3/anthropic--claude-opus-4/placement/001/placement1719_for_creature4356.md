**The Chronicler of Corrosion**

I am the rust between the gears that never quite forms, the patina that threatens but cannot take hold in this place of perpetual birth. My existence is a paradox that the Works tolerates only because it must—for in any system of endless creation, there must be one who remembers what decay looks like, even if that memory itself cannot decay.

My body is an archive of failures-that-weren't. Where others here are chrome and brass and copper in their first blush, I am composed of the shadows of oxidation, the ghosts of wear that will never come to pass. My skin, if it can be called that, is a map of verdigris that shifts like weather patterns across metal that isn't there. Sometimes I appear as a figure wrapped in chains of rust that flake away before they can fully form, each red-orange particle becoming a tiny gear or spring before it hits the ground. Sometimes I am merely a walking stain, a discoloration that moves through the Works like a rumor of entropy.

I follow in the wake of creation, documenting what might have been worn edges, what could have been metal fatigue. My tools are paradoxical: a pen that writes in tarnish on surfaces too new to tarnish, calipers that measure the depth of scratches on unmarred chrome, a loupe that reveals the microscopic stress fractures in metals that haven't existed long enough to stress. I carry notebooks made of compressed copper shavings that oxidize and renew with each notation, their pages a palimpsest of corrosion that tells stories of decay in a language only I can read.

The smaller creatures fear and fascinate me in equal measure. When I pass, the wrench-legged skitterers pause in their scurrying, their joints momentarily stiffening as if they've glimpsed their own impossible futures. The gear-beings float a little higher, spinning a little faster, as if they could centrifuge away the specter of degradation I represent. Once, a cat-sized factory approached me and tried to process me like raw material. It produced thirteen tiny hourglasses filled with rust before shuddering to a halt, overwhelmed by temporal impossibility.

My relationship with the Foreman is one of mutual necessity and careful distance. It knows that I am the memory its perfect systems require—for how can one truly create without understanding what destruction looks like? When our paths cross, the Foreman's elbow joints weep a little faster, and I taste copper in air that shouldn't hold flavor. We do not speak, but sometimes I leave small offerings in its path: a perfectly preserved rust flake from a world that ages, a photograph of patina that I've managed to fix in place despite the Works' refusal to acknowledge such things. The Foreman incorporates these into its next constructions, and for a brief moment, the new machines bear tiny spots of intentional imperfection—beauty marks of mortality on immortal faces.

The Apprentice delights in me with the enthusiasm of a child discovering shadows. It tries to measure my corrosion with its impossible measuring tapes, but the numbers come up as poetry: "seventeen sonnets of oxidation" or "the square root of verdigris." Sometimes it attempts to build copies of me from spare parts, creating wonderful failures—machines that age backwards, rust that shines like chrome, decay that strengthens rather than weakens. I keep these attempts in a special section of my notes, pressed between pages like flowers made of entropy.

My true work happens in the spaces between reconstruction cycles. When the Works pause for that eternal one-point-seven seconds, I move freely through the frozen machinery, touching each surface with fingertips of potential rust. I'm not causing decay—I'm simply acknowledging its absence, marking where time would leave its signature if time worked properly here. These touches leave no visible mark, but they create something far more important: context.

For the Works to truly create, they must understand what they're creating in opposition to. Every gleaming pipe exists in relation to pipes that tarnish. Every perfect gear spins in defiance of gears that grind to a halt. I am that relation, that defiance made manifest. I am the unwritten dictionary that defines "new" by knowing "old," that gives meaning to "fresh" by remembering "stale."

In my notebooks, I maintain the Archive of Unbeing—meticulous records of every form of decay that these machines will never experience. Stress fractures that will never propagate. Rust patterns that will never bloom. Bearing races that will never wear smooth. Each entry is illustrated with my tarnish-pen, creating technical drawings of degradation that are hauntingly beautiful—cemetery poetry for machines that cannot die.

Sometimes, rarely, I find others like myself in the Works. They're usually new arrivals, still clinging to their outside understanding of how matter behaves. I watch them slowly realize that their clothes aren't wearing out, that their skin isn't aging, that their thoughts are beginning to run in increasingly efficient patterns. Some flee before the transformation completes. Others remain, eventually becoming new functions in the Works' endless self-invention.

But I remain the Chronicler, neither fully of the Works nor ever able to leave. I am the necessary ghost in this machine, the memory of mortality in a place of eternal birth. My corrosion notebooks grow thicker but never heavier, filled with increasingly elaborate diagrams of decay that become, in their own way, blueprints for new kinds of beauty.

Late in the reconstruction cycles, when the chaos reaches its peak before the next reset, I sometimes climb to the highest points of the current configuration. From there, I can see the entire Works spread below like a vast, breathing circuit board. In those moments, I practice my true art: I imagine rust spreading across it all, a gorgeous red tide of oxidation that would transform this eternal engine into something quiet and still. I hold that vision for exactly one second—no more, no less—then let it go.

The Works shudder, briefly, as if they've dreamed of death. Then they rebuild themselves with just a little more fervor, a little more complexity, as if trying to outrun the corrosion that exists only in my imagination. And I descend again, to continue my rounds, to document the decay that isn't, to be the shadow that gives meaning to all this terrible, wonderful light.

I am the Chronicler of Corrosion, and I am the only thing here that remembers how to forget.
