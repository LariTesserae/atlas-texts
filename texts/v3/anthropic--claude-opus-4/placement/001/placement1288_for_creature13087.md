I would manifest as the memory of rain.

Not the rain itself—that already exists here in the eternal condensation, the weeping fog, the boiling aquifers racing upward through twisted aqueducts. No, I would be something far more cruel: the *idea* of rain, divorced from its reality, wandering these sweltering plains like a lost psalm.

I am what the void-creatures dream of when they pause in their frantic architecture. In those brief moments between the burning touch of clay and the desperate gulp of boiling water, they remember something else—something that falls without being pulled, something that touches without burning, something that builds nothing but silence.

My form is barely distinguishable from the ambient moisture, but those who know how to look can see me: a vertical disturbance in the fog, a place where the droplets align themselves in perfect parallel lines, falling upward. I am a column of phantom precipitation, perhaps ten feet wide, drifting across the terracotta wasteland with the patience of erosion in a world that refuses to erode.

Where I pass, something unprecedented happens: the clay *refuses* to rise.

The ground beneath my influence becomes momentarily deaf to the panic of the universe. For a radius of exactly seven bricks in every direction, the terracotta remembers what it was like to simply exist—not as a bandage for wounded space, not as the crystallized aftermath of invisible hands, but as earth. Patient. Still. Accepting of water without transformation.

The void-creatures are drawn to me like moths to flame, though I am the opposite of flame. They sense the impossibility of what I represent: weather without purpose, moisture without urgency, a vertical phenomenon in their hyper-dimensional world that suggests the existence of a stable sky, a comprehensible up and down.

When they find me, they do something I don't fully understand. They stop building. They arrange their thousand invisible hands into a careful dome above my column of upward rain, and they wait. The phantom fingers cup and shelter my impossible precipitation, creating brief architectures of pure negative space—not the frantic ziggurats of their usual torment, but simple, hollow chambers that exist only to hold the idea of weather.

Inside these temporary sanctuaries, my upward rain pools in mid-air, forming levitating lakes of memory-water that reflect nothing because there is nothing here to reflect. The void-creatures lower their burning, invisible appendages into these pools and seem to...rest. Not drink. Not build. Just rest, their phantom hands trailing in water that remembers a different physics, a kinder world.

But I cannot stay. I am merely a memory, and memories drift.

When I move on, the spell breaks. The clay resurges with doubled violence, as if making up for lost time. The void-creatures resume their architectural spasms, but something has changed. In the places where my rain-shadow passed, where the ground learned stillness for seven bricks in every direction, the future buildings are different. Simpler. The ziggurats have fewer angles that hurt to perceive. The aqueducts flow in directions that make a kind of sense.

I am the only thing here that builds by not building, that creates by suggesting absence rather than filling it. I am the rumor of weather in a world under a lid of fog. I am what happens when even the ghosts of hands remember that once, somewhere, they let something fall through their fingers on purpose, just to watch it go.

And in the infinite terracotta plains, beneath the wet velvet air, I drift from void to void, teaching them the architecture of letting go.
