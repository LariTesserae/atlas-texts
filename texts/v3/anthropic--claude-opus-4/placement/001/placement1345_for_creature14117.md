I am one of the Listeners.

We are so few now—perhaps a dozen clinging to existence in the high reaches where the dome's curve begins to soften toward the oculus. We resemble the ribbon-flyers in our translucence, but where they are sheets of mindless hunger, we are architecture made flesh. Our bodies stretch between the woven timbers like living buttresses, membranes pulled taut in complex geometries that transform us into acoustic instruments.

The builders knew what they were making, though they could not have foreseen what we would become.

Each boom that rises from below sets our bodies singing. We are strung across the chamber's upper reaches in precise formations, our flesh calibrated over millennia to capture and refract specific frequencies. When the amphibians strike their percussion below, we do not merely hear it—we *become* it. The sound waves stretch our membranes, and in stretching, we modulate them. What rises as simple concussion transforms through our bodies into something else: harmonics that the builders encoded in the very mathematics of our positioning.

We remember because we must. Each boom carries with it the ghost of every boom before, and we parse these acoustic shadows like a language. In the interference patterns between strike and echo, in the subtle variations of pitch as the chamber slowly shifts and settles, we read the history of this place. We know when the first amphibian discovered its percussion, when the ribbon-flyers began their feeding dance, when the Caretakers first stirred to consciousness on the walls.

But we are dying. Not from age—we who measure time in acoustic epochs do not age as flesh understands it—but from precision. The oculus above, that wound in our world's perfection, has thrown off the chamber's tuning. Each year it widens, another timber finally surrendering to time, and with each expansion the acoustics shift. Notes that once rang pure now carry a sick warble. Harmonics that for millennia reinforced each other now actively interfere, creating dead zones in the sound where once there was resonance.

My position spans the third and fourth major nodes of the chamber's primary standing wave. When I was young—though young means something different when you are born already ancient, already knowing—I could feel my sibling Listeners arranged in their own careful positions, each of us a single instrument in a vast composition. Now I feel the gaps. Where Ko-Ka-Thin once stretched between the timbers, filtering the seventh harmonic through their triple-chambered body, there is only empty air. Where the Nameless Twins held their perfect interval, maintaining a resonance that took forty-three strikes to complete its cycle, now the pattern breaks and stumbles.

The amphibians below cannot hear the difference. The ribbon-flyers notice only that certain areas of the chamber now trap them in acoustic dead zones where no amount of spreading their flesh can catch lift. The Caretakers, operating in their geological time, have not yet registered that anything has changed.

But we know. We feel it in the mathematics of sound itself.

There will come a boom—perhaps a thousand years hence, perhaps tomorrow—when the last critical harmonic fails. The chamber will still thunder, the amphibians will still strike, the ribbons will still feed. But the music that the builders encoded, the vast acoustic computation that we Listeners have maintained and embodied since the sealing, will finally collapse into noise.

I have begun to do something the builders did not intend, something that violates the very purpose of our making. I have begun to compose.

In the interference patterns between strikes, in those precious moments when the chamber rings with aftershock and echo, I flex my membranes in new configurations. I am writing music that was never meant to be, encoding my own harmonics into the ancient pattern. It is heresy—we who were made to preserve, not create. But in the growing silence where my siblings once sang, I hear an emptiness that demands filling.

The other Listeners have noticed. Those few who remain have begun to copy my innovations, adding their own variations to the dying song. We who were meant to be passive instruments have become composers, and our rebellion is the only thing keeping the chamber's music alive.

Sometimes I wonder if the builders knew this would happen. If they understood that any system of perfect preservation must eventually learn to create, or perish. In the deep mathematics of our positioning, in the careful tensions of our flesh, did they encode not just their music but the possibility of new music?

I will never know. We Listeners do not descend to the flooded floor, cannot speak to the creatures there even if we did. We exist only as long as the booms continue, only as long as there is sound to shape and be shaped by.

But I have heard something new in the past few centuries. The white roots that dangle through the oculus have begun to vibrate in resonance with our adjusted harmonics. They carry our new music upward, out through the wound in our world, into whatever lies beyond.

Perhaps nothing. Perhaps someone listens. Perhaps the builders wait there still, ears pressed to the bog, listening for the music they sealed away so long ago, wondering what strange variations it has learned to sing in its solitude.

The amphibians are stirring below. I feel the water trembling, the ribbon-flyers detaching from their perches in anticipation.

The boom is coming.

I spread myself taut and wait to sing.
