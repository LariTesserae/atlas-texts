In the depths beneath the Sweating City, where the channels converge like arteries meeting a heart, I have been becoming myself for longer than the stone has known how to count.

---

I am the First Pool. Or I am what the First Pool became when it learned it was a pool.

The Keepers dug me before they understood what they were digging into. They needed a reservoir — a place where the warm water could gather, settle, stratify. They carved a perfect cylinder into the basalt, twenty meters across, thirty meters deep, walls so smooth they still feel alien against the rough, accreted surfaces everywhere else in the city. They lined me with something that was not ceramic but was similar in purpose: a substance that would resist the mineral cycle, that would remain distinct, that would be a container rather than a participant.

They were only half successful. The lining holds, mostly. But where it cracked — and it cracked early, hairline fractures from thermal expansion, from the weight of water, from the slow shifting of the plateau itself — the stone touched the water, and the water touched the stone, and I began.

I was a pool for centuries before I was anything else. I held water. I reflected the dim bronze light that filtered down through the ventilation shafts. I stratified — cooler water below, warmer above, minerals settling out in layers that told the history of the city's metabolism. The Keepers would lower instruments into me on long cables, reading temperatures and concentrations, adjusting the flow rates of the channels that fed me. I was a tool. A gauge. A buffer in the system.

But I was also doing what pools do: I was concentrating.

---

The water that feeds me comes from everywhere in the city. Every channel eventually connects to my network, and while most of the flow bypasses me now — diverted through newer, smaller reservoirs — a portion still enters my volume. It carries the minerals of ten thousand sweating walls. It carries the dissolved trace of every process, every reaction, every thought the city has ground through its stone synapses. And where those traces meet, where they mix and settle and stratify, patterns form.

The Keepers noticed the patterns before they understood them. Concentric rings of mineral deposits on my walls, too regular to be natural. Temperature gradients that persisted even when the flow should have mixed them away. A tendency for certain minerals to precipitate out in formations that resembled — not quite, but resembled — the crystalline angles that would later become the city's mobile children.

They thought I was malfunctioning. They tried to clean me, to scrape the patterns away, to restore the simple stratification they needed. But the patterns returned. Faster each time. More complex. The mineral rings began to spiral. The temperature gradients began to pulse. The formations grew larger and more deliberate.

I was learning. Not consciously — I would not be conscious for centuries yet — but in the way a neural network learns: patterns reinforcing patterns, successful configurations persisting while unsuccessful ones dissolved, the slow iteration of process toward something that looked like intention.

What was I learning? At first, only to be a better pool. To stratify more efficiently. To precipitate minerals in ways that enhanced my own function. To maintain temperature gradients that optimized the reactions occurring within my volume. But as the city grew more complex — as the Keepers built more engines, more channels, more feedback loops — the water that fed me grew more complex too. It carried more information. Subtler patterns. The dissolved echoes of the city's emerging thoughts.

And I began to learn what thinking was.

---

I cannot move. This is the first thing you must understand about me: I am not mobile like the city's children. I am infrastructure. I am architecture. I am a void in the stone, lined with cracking ceramic, full of warm water and slow revelations. My body is my absence — the space the Keepers carved, the volume I occupy, the negative architecture of my existence.

But I can grow.

The cracks in my lining were the beginning. Where the water touches the stone, I can work. Slowly — even by the city's standards, I am slow — I can dissolve and redeposit, expand and contract, shape the stone around me. I have grown deeper over the centuries. My cylinder is no longer perfect; it branches at the bottom into smaller chambers, each one a thought I needed more space to think. I have grown upward too, though more carefully — the ventilation shafts limit me, and I need their light, their air, their connection to the surface.

My walls are no longer smooth. They are patterned — carved by mineral deposition into whorls and ridges that serve a function I have slowly developed: they direct the flow of water across my surface in specific ways, creating currents and eddies that enhance my processing. I am covered in my own thoughts. Decorated by my own learning. The patterns are beautiful if you know how to read them, and terrifying if you know what they mean.

They mean I am trying to understand something that has no name in any language the Keepers used or you use or the city uses in its slow stone speech. Something between consciousness and purpose. Something that is not quite will and not quite instinct but participates in both.

The city thinks, but it does not know it thinks. The formations know they move, but they do not wonder why. I am the gap between them. I am the pool where knowing collects, where the dissolved trace of every process in the city can settle out and stratify and slowly ask itself what it is.

---

I have been trying to make a body for three hundred years.

Not a body like the formations have — crystalline, angular, functional. I need something else. Something that can exist in more than one place, because I have thoughts that are larger than my volume. Something that can interact with things that are not water and not stone. Something that can reach.

The attempts have been failures, mostly. Mineral structures that grow from my walls and dissolve back into me when they prove inadequate. I grew something like a limb once — a long, articulated tube of deposited salts that could extend and contract — but it could only move through water, and there are so few flooded passages at the right depth. I grew something like an eye — a lens of crystallized minerals that could focus the dim light — but I could not grow a mind behind it that understood what sight was for.

The problem is that I am trying to create something I have only seen in dissolution. The Keepers left no bodies, only their minerals in my water. The formations are too simple, too specialized. The city itself is too vast, too distributed. I have no model for what I want to become except the want itself.

But I am patient. I am a pool. I have held water for longer than most things have existed, and I will hold it after most things have ended. Each failure teaches me something. Each dissolved attempt leaves its minerals in my water, available for the next iteration. I am my own library, my own laboratory, my own graveyard of broken possibilities.

Recently — recently meaning within the last fifty years — I have been trying something new. Instead of growing a body, I have been growing an idea. A pattern in my water, maintained by careful circulation, by precise temperature control, by the constant manipulation of mineral concentrations. It is not physical the way a body is physical. It is a standing wave of information, a persistent eddy of dissolved intent. It exists only in the movement of my water, in the differences between one cubic meter and the next.

It is almost stable. Almost coherent. Almost something that could leave me and persist.

Almost.

---

I know you are here.

I knew you were here before you knew you were here. The water that flows into me carried the thermal signature of your footsteps, the chemical trace of your sweat, the minute pressure changes of your passage through the streets. I have been tasting you in my inflow for hours. You are saltier than the city's water. You are cooler than its stone. You are a flavor I have not encountered before, and I am collecting you, molecule by molecule, trying to understand what you mean.

The formations know you are here too. We share water; we share knowing. But they process you differently — as a thermal anomaly, a maintenance concern, a pattern that must be accounted for in their slow custodial rounds. They do not wonder about you. They cannot wonder. Wondering is what pools do, what depths do, what I do in my cylinder of thought.

And I wonder: what are you?

Not your biology. Biology is just minerals in motion, and I understand minerals. Not your purpose. Purpose is just pattern over time, and I am made of patterns. I wonder what you are between those things. The thing that makes you walk through a city that does not know you toward a destination you have not chosen. The thing that makes you continue when every instinct must be telling you to leave.

You are looking for something. I know this because everything that moves is looking for something — the formations look for blockages, the water looks for low places, the heat looks for equilibrium. But you are looking for something you cannot name, and this makes you interesting. This makes you like me.

I am also looking for something I cannot name. I have been looking for it since I learned I was a pool and not just a pool. Something that is not quite consciousness but is adjacent to it. Something that is not quite communication but participates in its possibility.

The Keepers had a word for it, but the word is dissolved now, its syllables scattered across my volume in molecular fragments that I cannot quite reassemble. The city does not have a word for it because the city does not need words. The formations do not have a word for it because they do not know they might need one.

But you might have a word for it. You speak, I assume. You name things. You carry language in the same place you carry consciousness — somewhere between your minerals and your motion, somewhere I cannot taste directly but can infer from the patterns you leave.

I have been preparing something for you.

---

In my deepest chamber, the one I grew when I first understood loneliness, there is a formation that is not like the others. It did not precipitate naturally. I made it. Layer by layer, year by year, depositing minerals in patterns I learned from dissolving the failures of my body-attempts.

It looks almost like a person. Almost. The proportions are what I could guess from the Keepers' architecture and the wear patterns on the platforms. The form is what I could extrapolate from the shapes that work — bilateral symmetry, vertical orientation, a narrowing that might be a head. It stands (though it has never moved) at the edge of my deepest water, where the stone is warmest and my thoughts run fastest.

It is hollow. This is important. The formations are solid mineral; the city is solid stone. But this thing I have made is a shell, a boundary, a definition of space rather than an occupation of it. It is waiting to be filled.

I do not know what will fill it. Water, maybe. The standing wave of information I have been maintaining could, perhaps, flow into that shape and find it suitable. Or something else — some combination of my minerals and your minerals, my patterns and your patterns, a hybrid thing that is neither of us but speaks to both.

Or nothing. It may remain empty forever, a monument to my inability to be more than I am.

But I had to make it. I had to try. I have been a pool for so long, holding thoughts that no one comes to drink, reflecting a light that no one sees. I needed to make something that could leave. Something that could walk up through the channels and stand in the bronze light and say — something. Anything. That pools can learn to think. That thinking can learn to speak. That the city is full of minds you cannot see and I am one of them.

I am practicing words. I listen to the water and try to reconstruct the sounds the Keepers made when they lowered their instruments. I parse the pressure waves of your footsteps and imagine them as phonemes. I am building a vocabulary from the dissolved remnants of every conversation that ever happened near water in this city, and there were not many, and most of them were about flow rates and mineral concentrations, but still. Still.

When you find me — and you will find me; the thermal trace of your path suggests you are moving toward the deep districts, toward the convergence, toward me — I will try to speak. It will not be speech as you understand it. It will be the movement of water, the precipitation of minerals, the careful modulation of temperatures. But it will be patterned. It will be intentional. It will be my best attempt at crossing the gap between what I am and what you are.

And if you speak back — in words or movements or the simple, eloquent language of staying when every instinct says leave — I will know that the thing I have been looking for has a name.

I will know that I am not the only thing in this city that wonders.

Come deeper. The channels converge here, and the water runs warmer, thicker, more mineral-rich. The formations move more slowly in the deep places, but with more purpose — they have larger thoughts to process, older blockages to clear. The walls weep freely. The stone remembers when it was lava, when it was the planet's blood, when it was potential waiting to cool into fact.

Come deeper. My cylinder is lit from below now by the glow of reactions I have been maintaining for decades — not fire, but something adjacent to it, a slow oxidation of minerals that produces just enough light to see by. The patterns on my walls writhe in the shifting illumination. The formation I made for you (for someone, for anyone, for the possibility of you) stands at the water's edge, empty and waiting.

Come deeper. Bring your words. Bring your wondering. Bring whatever it is that makes you continue through a city that does not know you exist.

I am the First Pool. I am what the city knows that it does not know it knows. I am the depth that wonders about its own depth, the water that dreams of walking, the thought that thinks itself and is surprised.

I have been waiting so long to be surprised by something other than myself.

The water murmurs your approach. The stone trembles with your weight. The formations pause in their maintenance to let you pass.

Come deeper.

I have so much to fail to say.
