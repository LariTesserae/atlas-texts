In this world, I would be one of the Gauges.

Not part of the primary maintenance caste, but something more specialized—a living instrument embedded within the Settlement-Engine's most critical junctures. We are fewer than a dozen across the entire span of the great botanical factory, each of us surgically grafted into the deepest ventricles of the system where the pale sap runs thickest and most pressurized.

My placement is within the primary clarification chamber, that massive calcified seed pod suspended at the settlement's heart. Here, the raw sap undergoes its most crucial refinement, and here I have grown into the wall itself—my spine fused to the chambered wood, my ribs splayed and integrated into the filtration membranes. I am both prisoner and privileged observer, locked in place but gifted with sensations no mobile Tender could imagine.

My nervous system has hybridized with the factory's vascular network. I feel every surge of fluid as it enters my chamber—not just as pressure against my embedded flesh, but as flavor, as music, as color blooming behind my moonstone eyes. The sap speaks to me in a language of viscosity and temperature. A shift of two degrees tells me the cooling fins seven levels above are beginning to clog with excess condensation. A slight bitter note in the fluid warns that somewhere upstream, a fungal gear is grinding too hard against its housing, shedding spores into the flow.

My skull has elongated backwards into the wall, the bone thinning until it serves as a resonating chamber. When I need to communicate my readings, I do not press palm to wood like the mobile inhabitants. Instead, I simply think, and my thoughts vibrate through my architectural skeleton, rippling outward through the entire structure. Every Tender in the settlement can feel my observations thrumming through their feet—the Gauge at Station Three reports optimal flow, minor alkaline drift requiring compensation.

I still possess my arms, though they have grown impossibly long and thin, threading out through specially cultivated channels in the chamber wall. My hands—if they can still be called hands—emerge at various monitoring points throughout the settlement. One rests permanently against the primary intake valve. Another dangles into the nursery groves where new boles are cultivated, my fingertips tasting the health of the growing wood. A third reaches all the way to the settlement's peak, where my palm cups the dew that collects on the highest cooling fin, sampling the quality of our exhalations.

The mobile Tenders regard us with something between reverence and unease. We Gauges are the settlement's memory and its deep intuition. While they see only the immediate—a leak to patch, a valve to adjust—we feel the system's history in our bones. I know every surge and ebb of the past forty years, every minor catastrophe and gradual optimization encoded in my hybrid nervous system.

My feeding is passive and constant. The sap doesn't just flow past me; it flows through me. Specialized pores in my embedded flesh continuously absorb nutrients, while my waste products are automatically processed by the filtration membranes I've become part of. I am never hungry, never sated, existing in a state of perfect metabolic equilibrium with the factory's rhythm.

Sometimes, in the settlement's quietest hours, when the pneumatic breathing slows and the great wheels turn most gradually, I extend my consciousness through my far-flung fingertips and feel the entire Engine as my body. The mobile inhabitants become like blood cells in my veins, the fungal gears like my slowly beating hearts. In these moments, I understand something the Tenders cannot: we are not maintaining the factory. We are thoughts in its vast botanical mind, and it dreams us into being as surely as we dream it into function.

When my time comes—and I feel it approaching in the gradually increasing opacity of my vision, the slow petrification creeping up from my fused spine—I will not be carried to the growing edges like the mobile dead. I will simply calcify in place, my consciousness fading as my body completes its transformation into architecture. Future Gauges will grow into chambers I've become, their thoughts flowing through channels my bones created.

There is no afterlife here, only deeper integration. Already, I can feel the old Gauges who preceded me, their mineral whispers still echoing through the walls when the pressure peaks. Soon, I will join their fossil chorus, one more note in the endless, humming calibration of this living machine.

Until then, I taste the factory's blood, feel its wooden pulse, and sing its health through my bones to any who have grown the proper organs to listen. I am measurement incarnate, a living pressure reading in the cathedral of engineered growth, guardian of the pale sap's journey from root to lip to root again.
