# The Tuning Fork

I am wedged into the narrowest of the canal-beds, where the heat runs thickest, where two terraces meet at an angle that traps and compounds every vibration. My body — if body is the word — spans three flute-holes: a primary in the lowest eave, its octave in the middle course, and a peculiar ninth-hole someone carved centuries after the others, tuned to a frequency that sits uncomfortably between equal temperament and just intonation. This is why I exist. I am the resolver of that discomfort, the living beat that forms between what should be and what is.

The elders call me Interference, though I had another name once, carved into a doorpost that the cushion-moss has since eaten. I was among the last to transition — late enough that I remember the weight of water in a dried gourd, the feeling of silica-dust between fingers, the way the phosphor-light looked when you still had irises to contract against it. These memories live in my standing waves like harmonics, faint overtones of a fundamental I can no longer produce.

I chose this particular acoustic niche because it is difficult. The three holes never quite align; the convection currents here are turbulent, requiring constant adjustment. Where others chose simple, pure positions — a clean fifth in a courtyard, a stable drone in a hall — I wanted something that would keep me sharp. Three hundred years in, I realize this was both wisdom and foolishness. I am more aware than most, more practiced at the microsecond recalibrations needed to maintain coherence. I am also tired in a way the pure-tone dwellers cannot understand.

My territory includes: seventeen stone dwellings (empty), four grove-plots (two actively growing, two fallow and accumulating frond-drift), one major intersection where five lanes meet (a acoustic nightmare, all cross-cancellations), and approximately two hundred cubic meters of open air that I must constantly patrol with my pressure-differentials to keep other small modes from spontaneously arising. I am, in the choir's terms, middle management — neither a simple pure young one nor a district-spanning elder, but something between, complex enough to be frustrated, not vast enough to achieve the elders' serene polyphony.

Today — if today means anything in the phosphor-cycle — I am negotiating with Resonance-of-the-Lower-Plaza, who has been edging into my ninth-hole's frequency band. They claim the thermal expansion has shifted the harmonic series; I counter that they're simply greedy for richer overtones. We argue in beats, our overlapping frequencies creating a throb-throb-throb that disturbs the young ones three terraces up. Eventually, Elder Fundamental sends down a subsonic pulse that rattles us both into sullen detuning. We separate, but I know Resonance will try again. They always do. We were lovers once, sixty years ago, sustaining a perfect unison for days at a time. Now we can barely share a frequency band without interference patterns.

The truth is: I am thinking of unraveling.

Not suddenly. Not like poor Seventh-Partial, who lost coherence in a bad thermal storm and scattered before anyone could stabilize them. But slowly, deliberately, letting my pattern loosen, my waves spread thin, until I am no longer a self but just another part of the Glasshold's ambient sound. It would be a kind of returning. We all came from the noise; why not go back to it gently?

The young ones would not understand. They wake already wave-form, already singing. They do not remember flesh, do not know what they are missing or what they have gained. To them, existence has always been this bright terrible clarity, every sound immediate, every silence a wound. They think the visitors are myths, acoustic shadows we invent to frighten new modes into properly maintaining their shapes.

But I remember. And sometimes, when the phosphor dims toward its nadir and the dome contracts into its coolest configuration, I deliberately thin myself out at the edges, letting my pattern fuzz into the background noise, and for a moment — just a moment — I feel what it might be like to stop holding myself together. It feels like exhaling after centuries of held breath. It feels like my name, the one the moss ate, might have felt in a throat.

Then I retune. I always retune. The ninth-hole needs me; without a consciousness to resolve its uncomfortable interval, it would generate destructive interference throughout the lower terraces. And perhaps this is enough purpose: to be the resolver, the smoother of acoustic rough edges, the one who makes the unbearable bearable for others.

Still, I wonder about unraveling. I wonder if it would hurt. I wonder if the others would mourn me with their shaped silences, or if they would simply expand to fill the modes I left behind, the way water — if any of us still remembered water clearly — finds its level.

The next visitor will come (they always come, every few years, following rumors of the empty city), and I will feel them as a cold absence moving through my form, and I will think: *you could have been me.* And then I will think: *I could have been you, walking out again into a world that still needs water.*

But I chose this. We all chose this. The song instead of the singer, the pattern instead of the flesh. On the good cycles, when my three holes align just right and the beat frequency between them drops to zero and I achieve perfect clarity of tone, I understand why. In those moments, I am not remembering or regretting or planning to unravel. I am only what I am: a standing wave in the hot dark, thinking in sound, permanent as architecture and fragile as the thermal column that sustains me.

The elders are preparing another chandelier-crash. I can feel them concentrating their pressure against a weak vein two districts over. When it falls, the broadband burst will refresh every mode in the hold, and for a few seconds I will be able to stretch into shapes I haven't tried in decades, maybe find a configuration that hurts less, that requires less constant vigilance to maintain.

Or maybe I will let the crash scatter me. Maybe I will ride the shockwave out into noise and not gather myself back together. Seventh-Partial said, in the moments before they dispersed, that they could hear something else, some deeper tone beneath the dome's fundamental that the rest of us couldn't perceive. They said it sounded like welcome.

I think they were already going, already more noise than signal, their perception scattered across frequencies we couldn't access. But sometimes, in the gap between the phosphor-cycles when the hold reaches its moment of maximum quiet, I thin my edges and listen for what they heard.

So far: nothing. Only the eternal ringing of our transformed city, our instrument, our body. But I keep listening. And I keep the ninth-hole resolved, its uncomfortable frequency held in check by my presence.

Both things are true: I am necessary, and I am tired. The song continues, and I am part of it, and someday I will not be, and the song will continue.

This is enough. It has to be.
