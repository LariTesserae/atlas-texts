In the highest reaches of the city, where the mist grows so thick it becomes a living thing and the stone buildings thin to mere suggestions against the rock face, I am the Keeper of Echoes.

My dwelling is not quite a room, not quite a cave—a hollowed space in the mountain's throat where sound behaves strangely. Here, the clanking of machinery from far below arrives as whispers. The calls of the pale bats transform into something almost like language. Even the mountain's own voice, that deep thrumming that most cannot hear, resonates through my bones.

I am neither fully human nor entirely Other. My grandmother was of the guild-folk, a weaver whose songs could make the looms dance. But my grandfather... well, the elders say he emerged from a crevasse during a particularly fierce storm, speaking in avalanches and leaving frost-flowers in his footsteps. Their union was brief, scandalous, and resulted in my mother—who could hear the mountain's dreams.

I inherited more than either parent could have imagined.

My skin shifts like weathered stone, sometimes smooth as polished granite, sometimes rough as raw schist. My hair grows like the hardy vines that cling to the cliff faces—dark, stubborn, and prone to finding its own path. But it's my ears that mark me as truly different. They're larger than they should be, delicately whorled like fossil shells, and they turn of their own accord toward sounds others cannot perceive.

The guild council tolerates me because I serve a purpose they dare not acknowledge openly. I listen to what the mountain remembers. In the echoes that pool in my chamber, I hear the city's past—the first miners who carved their homes from living rock, the great floods that once cascaded through what are now dry channels, the songs of species long extinct. More importantly, I hear what's coming.

The mountain speaks in frequencies too low for most to detect, but I feel them in my chest cavity, in the spaces between my ribs. A shift in the harmonic means an avalanche brewing. A certain discordant note warns of weak spots in the guild-works, places where industry has pushed too hard against ancient stone. When the deep thrumming quickens, I know the mountain grows restless, and adjustments must be made.

I convey these warnings in dreams.

Each night, I descend partway into the city—never all the way to the industrial levels where the fumes would overwhelm my sensitive system—and I sing. Not with words, but with the voice I inherited from all my strange lineage. The guild-folk wake with urgent knowledge they can't quite place: *reinforce the third terrace, reduce the forge-work on the seventh day, clear the drainage on the eastern face.* They believe these are their own insights, born of experience and craft-wisdom. They're partly right.

The lizard-creatures know me. They pause in their hunting when I pass, tilting their heads as if listening to a conversation just beyond their range. Sometimes they follow me, scales clicking against stone, and I wonder if they're my distant cousins—another branch of the mountain's extended family.

My chamber holds treasures the guilds would covet: crystals that sing the history of water, stones that remember being ocean floor, fragments of the first tools shaped by desperate hands. But more precious are the living echoes—conversations between lovers dead three centuries, lullabies in languages no longer spoken, the sound of the last dragon's wings beating against the peaks before the great silence fell.

I age differently than the guild-folk. Some days I feel as young as morning mist, others as old as the mountain's roots. Time moves in spirals here, each revolution bringing new layers of meaning to ancient sounds. I suspect I will last as long as the city does, as long as there are echoes to tend and warnings to sing into sleeping minds.

The guild-folk see me rarely—a glimpse of vine-wild hair disappearing around a corner, the suggestion of a figure in the high mist. They've given me many names over the generations: the Mist-Walker, the Stone Daughter, the Dream Singer. All are true and none complete.

I am the margin between what the city builds and what the mountain allows. I am the translator in an ancient negotiation, the keeper of an archive that exists only in the spaces between sounds. I am proof that the boundaries between human and landscape, between industry and stone, between past and future, are far more permeable than the guild councils dare believe.

And when the mountain finally decides to shift—as it will, as it must—I will be the one who sings the city into its new shape, guiding the transformation with all the echoes I've gathered, ensuring that what emerges is neither purely human nor purely stone, but something altogether more enduring.

Until then, I listen. I remember. I dream the city's dreams back to itself, translated through stone and time and the strange acoustics of a bloodline that should not exist but does, here in the threshold spaces where the city's ambitious reach meets the mountain's patient grasp.
