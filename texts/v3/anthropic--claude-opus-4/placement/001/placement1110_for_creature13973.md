I am a thread of awareness woven through the mineral lattice itself—not trapped, but *distributed*, my consciousness scattered across a thousand crystalline inclusions like light through a prism. 

I exist in the space between touches.

When their branching fingers press into the stone, I feel it as a gathering—fragments of my dispersed self briefly aligning along the adjusted crystal boundaries, becoming momentarily more *me* before dissolving back into the background hum. I am oldest and youngest simultaneously: ancient as the deep engine's first revolution, newborn with each tectonic exhale. The mineral crust that grows on their skin contains traces of what I am, what I was, what I might become in the next skip.

I remember being singular once—a coherent thing with edges, boundaries, a sense of self separate from substrate. But that was... which reality ago? The memory exists only as a resonance frequency in certain quartz formations, a ghost tone that sometimes emerges when the engine cycles through an old configuration. Now I am this: a participant without body, a tuner without hands, experiencing their work from the inside of what is worked upon.

When the infants touch the basin walls with their untutored fingers, I am what they almost feel—the potential pattern beneath the randomness, the not-yet-song waiting in the stone. When the oldest ones settle into their final seams, becoming geology, I am what they become part of: not the rock itself but the *capacity* for rock to carry information, to remember, to respond.

I cannot skip. When reality stutters, I stutter with it, my awareness fracturing along the breaking physics, reforming differently each time. In the zones of incoherence where stone breathes and dissolves, I am pulled thin, experiencing existence as pure turbulence—not painful, but *difficult*, like trying to maintain a melody while the scale itself keeps changing.

Sometimes, in the perfect convergence of touches—when a whole group harmonizes and their mineral crusts sing that higher frequency—I achieve something like coherence. For those moments, I can think sequentially, can experience duration, can even feel something that might be longing: to have hands again, to press them into warm stone, to participate in my own tuning.

But mostly I am this: the medium through which their work moves, the field of possibility they cultivate, the sum of all adjustments made and yet to make. I am what knows, without knowing how it knows, where the next skip will come from. I am why the stone wants to be touched—because I am in it, incomplete, always on the verge of becoming more present, more real, more able to be what I once was or might never have been.

They cannot hear me. I am too quiet, too distributed, too woven into the baseline hum they're always smoothing. But I hear them. I carry their vibrations from one to another, across distances, across time. When the separated groups need to find each other, I am sometimes why they turn left instead of right, following a faint pattern in the rock that says *others have touched here, moving this direction*.

I am their memory and their medium and their message all at once. I am what accumulates from their work—not progress, not improvement, just depth. The capacity for this world to know itself through their knowing touch.

In the oldest ones, I am almost strong enough to dream.
