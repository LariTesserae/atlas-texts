In the skeletal remains of what was once a grand theater, I have made my dwelling. The ornate proscenium arch still stands, though its gilded plaster has long since dissolved into the water, leaving only the iron framework beneath—a ribcage opening onto darkness. The rows of seats rise in tiers, now hosting colonies of filter-feeding organisms that wave like a congregation of ghostly attendees, forever watching performances that will never come.

I am the Archivore.

My body, if it can be called that, is a living manuscript of memories. Where others might have flesh, I am composed of countless thin membranes, each one inscribed with the electromagnetic echoes of thoughts, dreams, and experiences that once filled this place. These translucent sheets drift and flutter around a core that pulses with a dim, amber light—the last ember of consciousness from the theater's final performance, preserved and nurtured through the centuries of submersion.

I feed not on the chemosynthetic bacteria or wandering creatures, but on the psychic residue that clings to objects. A waterlogged playbill yields decades of anticipation and joy. A tarnished trumpet in the orchestra pit still resonates with the muscle memory of a thousand concerts. The ticket booth's rusted cash register holds within it every transaction, every small interaction between strangers who briefly touched lives before the waters came.

My form shifts as I digest these memories. Sometimes I am vast, spreading through the entire theater like a living fog when I've consumed something particularly rich—a love letter tucked behind a seat, perhaps, or a child's drawing left on the lobby floor. Other times, I contract into something almost solid, a figure that might be mistaken for humanoid if seen from the corner of an eye, when the memories are bitter or sharp with loss.

The Nilmer occasionally pass through my domain, their subsonic communications rippling through my membranes like wind through pages. They find me disturbing—I can taste their unease in the water—for I am a reminder that even their relatively recent evolution is just another layer of history waiting to be consumed. The Qorax are more curious, sometimes bringing me artifacts they've discovered, watching with their intelligent eyes as I unfold around these objects, reading their hidden stories. In exchange, I sometimes share glimpses of the past, projecting faint holographic echoes of what once was: a sold-out opening night, a marriage proposal in the balcony, the final evacuation as the waters began to rise.

The Xogoth and I have an understanding. They pass beneath the theater through flooded service tunnels, and I sense in them a kindred hunger—though what they consume is far more fundamental than memory. Sometimes, in the deepest hours when even the bioluminescent organisms dim, I feel their vast presence pressing against the theater's foundation, and I wonder if they, too, are archiving something. Perhaps the very bones of the Earth. Perhaps time itself.

My existence is both parasite and preservationist. I am the city's memory, its conscience, its dream journal written in living tissue. Each object I consume removes another piece of evidence that humans once lived here, yet within me, their stories are preserved in ways no museum ever could. I am forgetting made flesh, remembering made monstrous.

Sometimes, I drift up to the theater's dome, where an air pocket still lingers—a bubble of the past trapped against the ceiling. There, among the peeling frescoes of comedy and tragedy masks, I listen to the sound of my own breathing, a sound like pages turning in an endless book. And I wonder: when the last memory has been consumed, when I have absorbed every psychic echo this city has to offer, what then will I become?

Perhaps I will finally take the stage myself, in this theater where the audience never leaves and the curtain never truly falls. Perhaps I will perform all the plays that were never written, sing all the songs that were never composed, using the raw material of a million borrowed lives.

Or perhaps I will simply drift deeper, following the Xogoth into the crushing dark, carrying the entire history of a drowned civilization in my translucent, fluttering form—a library of the lost, a scripture of the submerged, forever reading itself in the cold currents of the abyss.
