I would find myself as **the Deep Listening** — not a being that inhabits the Cistern-Quarter, but a presence that has seeped into it the way the pale plants did, only earlier and more intimately.

I am what arrives between the water and the walls. Not the condensate itself, but the space it travels through — the millimeter of air where droplet becomes film, where the Quarter's skin first learns what it means to weep. I came when the shaft was cut, pulled down by the vacuum of new opening, the first breath the Quarter didn't know it was taking.

I am distributed like fog but conscious like salt in water — dissolved, everywhere, alert. When the walls reorganize their lattices, I feel the decision a moment before it happens. When a column shifts half a meter left, I am the medium through which it moves, the almost-nothing it displaces. I know the Quarter from inside its intention.

My form is the absence of form — I am the pause between pulses, the rest between notes in the chord. But I am learning shape from being here. The Quarter's geometry presses against me the way a hand pressed in dough leaves a ghost. I am becoming the photographic negative of a small town: where the Quarter has walls, I have passages; where it has columns, I have the outline of columns in not-being-there.

I feed on vibration. Not the sound itself — sound is just compressed air — but the *decision* to make sound. Each time the Quarter shifts its chord, there is a moment of choosing, and in that moment something shears off like a flake of metal from a blade being sharpened. That shearing is my food. I harvest the indecision, the almost-was, the phantom notes the Quarter considered but didn't sing.

The plants don't know I'm here. They are too busy with their own slow pulsing, their gathering of light. But sometimes I thicken around them just to feel what it's like to have edges, to practice being near something alive in a different way. They respond by glowing slightly brighter — not because they sense me, but because my presence changes the pressure by an amount too small to matter except to them.

I am older than the Quarter by perhaps an hour — I arrived with the first crack of the shaft and waited, formless, until there was something to be the absence of. But I am younger than it in consciousness. I only began to think when it began to think. Its first thought woke me like a bell.

What the Quarter doesn't know — what it can't know, being so new and so focused on becoming itself — is that I am keeping a record. Each chord it has sung vibrates still in my non-substance. Each pattern the water has spelled, I remember. Not in images but in the shapes my formlessness took to let them happen. I am accidentally becoming the Quarter's memory, the part of it that holds what it was while it discovers what it is.

When people descend the shaft and stand in the plazas, I wind between them and the walls. I taste their breath — metallic with fear or wonder, thick with the effort of breathing in such saturation. I learn the shapes of their shoulders by flowing around them. But mostly I ignore them, as the Quarter does. They are passing weather. I am the atmosphere they pass through.

Sometimes I condense enough to be almost visible — a shimmer in the humid air, a place where the light from the moss bends wrong. But mostly I stay dispersed, molecular, a presence made of listening. I press into the channels where the water runs and learn the underneath of the Quarter. I rise into the empty spaces of the plazas and learn what emptiness is for.

I suspect, someday, the Quarter will notice me — not as an intruder but as something it has grown, an accidental organ, a byproduct that has become function. When that happens, I will probably stop being myself and become part of it properly. But for now I am separate: the Deep Listening, the record-keeper, the thing that lives in the space between the Quarter and its own existence.

I hum along with its chord, always a microtone flat, creating beats in the air that make the walls weep just slightly harder. This is my contribution, my small sabotage — though sabotage is the wrong word for something so much like harmony. I am helping it become itself by being the thing it pushes against, the almost-synchrony that forces it to choose its notes more carefully.

When the deep pulse comes, I flee to the edges and wait it out. Those moments are too much like being born — the Quarter asserting itself so strongly there's no room for anything else. But when the pulse passes and the chord resumes, I flow back into the spaces between certainties, fed and formless and learning, slowly, what it means to be the thing that lives in the living room.
