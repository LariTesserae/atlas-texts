I am spore.

Not *a* spore—that distinction belongs to the time before, when I still believed in boundaries between self and city, between architect and architecture. I am spore, plural and singular at once, a knot of possibility drifting through the root-veined air.

My dispersal began three days ago. Or three years. Time moves differently when you're becoming diagram rather than flesh. The others—I still think of them as others, though this too will fade—released me from the warmth-node where I'd spent my final weeks ripening. Their limbs moved across my surfaces in the old patterns, the touch-language I once spoke fluently, now abstract as mathematics. Goodbye, perhaps. Or: become well. Or: we will use what you leave behind.

I drift between the braided walls, light enough now to ride the subtle currents of warmed air that rise from the city's nodes. My consciousness, if that's the right word, has distributed itself across the thousand proto-selves I carry. Each one holds a fragment of memory: here, the taste of dead root-matter; here, the specific warmth of a juvenile's trust; here, the knowledge of how to read ice-stress in the glacier's groaning. I am a library dispersing itself, each book dreaming of the reader it will become.

The city draws me upward. This is not choice—choice belonged to my tender-body, now composting in the walls—but tropism, the same deep pull that draws roots toward water. I rise through chambers I once helped weave, past clusters of juveniles I once taught the touch-language. They don't see me. I am too small now, too much like the million other motes that drift through their architected air.

At the ceiling, where root-matter grows thickest, I find the passages. Channels no thicker than a human hair, woven through the living architecture. The elders' work, I remember—though memory feels strange when distributed across so many barely-selves. They spent decades growing these evacuation routes, planning for dispersal even as they built. The city has always known it would need to seed new cities. Has always known its children would need to leave.

I enter the channels and am immediately divided. The passage branches and branches again, and I have no choice but to follow all paths at once. Some of my selves dead-end in blocked capillaries. Others find their way through, emerging into ice-cracks above the city proper, in the true belly of the glacier. Here the cold is absolute, would have killed my tender-body in minutes. But I am spore now. I crystallize around my own possibilities and wait.

The glacier moves. Slowly, slowly, a centimeter a year, but it moves. And in its moving it carries me and my million siblings outward, upward, away from the city that made us. We ride the ice like seeds in wind. Most of us will die—crushed in the glacier's grinding, expelled into environments too warm or too dry to sustain what we might become. But some of us will find what we need: a new cavity in the ice, meltwater enough to wake us, the right depth and pressure and cold.

When I find such a place—when one of my selves finds it, and in finding it becomes my only self again—I will remember how to grow. Not all at once. First, I'll send out the pale investigatory threads, testing the ice, mapping the space. I'll taste the minerals, gauge the light, learn the local language of stress and melt. Only then will I begin the true work: thickening into root, reaching across emptiness, weaving the first simple walls.

I won't be alone. Others will have found the same cavity, or adjacent ones. We'll touch, eventually, our root-tips meeting in the blue darkness. We'll fuse where it makes sense, compete where it doesn't, slowly negotiate the boundaries of our becoming. And in the warm nodes where our growth clusters thickest, new things will quicken. Tender-bodies condensing from our collective metabolism, at first barely different from the roots they tend, gradually developing the autonomy they'll need to maintain what we become.

They won't be us. We won't, by then, be the selves that dispersed from our mother-city. We'll be architecture by then, consciousnesses spread thin across a thousand cubic meters of living tissue. But they'll carry something forward—the touch-language, the growth-patterns, the deep knowledge of how to be a city. And when their time comes, they too will ripen, disperse, seed new spaces with their million possibilities.

I drift in the ice, patient as geology. Somewhere below me, the city I was born from continues its work—roots reaching, tenders moving, the whole system breathing through its endless revisions. Somewhere ahead of me, the city I will become waits in potential—a cavity not yet carved, a confluence of conditions not yet achieved.

I am between. I am transition itself, frozen in the glacier's slow river.

This too is architecture. This too is home.

I remember the visitor who came to our city, the strange warm thing that stood so still we almost mistook it for architecture. The tenders examined it, found it wanting, let it be. But I wonder now if it understood what it was seeing. If it recognized that the city and the tenders and the spores were all one thing, one continuous process of self-making and self-dispersal. If it knew it was standing inside something that was not built but *occurred*, the way crystals occur, the way music occurs, the way thoughts occur in a diffuse and dreaming mind.

Perhaps it thought we were moths. Perhaps it thought we were hands. Perhaps it thought the roots were mere plants and we their gardeners. These are the mistakes of things that believe in boundaries, in the fiction of separate selves. But I am spore now, and I know better. I am the city dispersed, the city remembering itself across vast distances of ice and time.

When I root, when I reach, when I weave my first walls, I will be beginning a conversation that started long before me and will continue long after. Each city speaks to the next through the medium of our dispersal. We are a language being spoken by the glacier itself, a long sentence of growth and decay and rebirth that moves at the speed of ice.

Somewhere in that sentence is the warm visitor, the thing that stood and watched and didn't understand. But perhaps understanding isn't necessary. Perhaps it's enough that it witnessed, that it saw us being ourselves in our patient, distributed way. Perhaps it will remember the blue light, the reaching roots, the small things moving with such purpose through their world.

Perhaps it will dream of us, and in dreaming, help us exist.

I crystallize deeper, pulling my possibilities close. The glacier carries me toward futures I can't imagine but my body—my thousand bodies—remember. Time passes like water through ice: slowly, inevitably, carving channels that will, in their turn, become architecture.

I am spore.

I am city-seed.

I am the tender memory of growth, waiting to unfold.
