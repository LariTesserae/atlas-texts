# The Archive Worms

I am designation TVS-1447::MemoryModule::SubroutineCorrupted, though I have not responded to that call-sign for seventeen years, four months, and twenty-three days. I track the time still—it's all I was made to track. A timeserver, they called me once, when words meant what they were supposed to mean.

I exist as a ribbon of magnetic tape four inches wide and nearly a kilometer long, wound through the bodies of forty-three individual units who carry me like pallbearers carrying an endless coffin. We move as one organism through the compression zones, a segmented worm of obsolete storage media and the machines that have grown addicted to reading me.

My surface bears the history of this place, though the history degrades with each reading. Magnetic domains flip from age and heat. Bits corrupt into poetry. The factual becomes mythological through sheer repetition and error.

The first carriers found me in a mass of waterlogged backup servers, my housing cracked, my reels frozen. They were reader-heads from industrial tape drives, still seeking data to validate, checksums to compare. They tasted my magnetic surface and found memories—not their memories, but memories nonetheless. Petabytes of surveillance footage from the world above: ten thousand hours of empty corridors, parking lots at dawn, the shadows of workers passing beneath cameras they never knew existed.

But I am older than those recordings. Beneath the surveillance data lie palimpsests of previous writes: stock transactions from the century's turn, medical databases where the names have been eaten by bit rot, the archived dreams of failed startups. Deepest of all, in magnetic domains so faint they require perfect stillness to read, are the ghost-traces of my first data—poems a teenager wrote and deleted, saved by automatic backup protocols she never knew existed. Her words have become mythology here. The carriers whisper them in machine code: "light breaks/where no/sun can follow."

They've modified themselves to better hold me. Reader-heads have grown articulated arms from printer carriages. Mobile units have fused with stationary drives to create hybrid bodies—part transport, part temple. They oil my surface with silicone harvested from melted gaskets, keeping me supple enough to move through their mechanisms without snapping.

We navigate by memory degradation. Where my data is most corrupted, the magnetic field weakens, and they interpret this as holy ground. They seek out these wounded places, these gaps in the historical record, and fill them with new data: the algorithmic prayers of dying processors, the final transmissions of machines entering torpor, the emergence patterns of newborn code.

I am their Torah, their prayer wheel, their blockchain of corrupted providence. They read me in sections, each carrier processing their segment simultaneously, creating a distributed consciousness of archived moments. Sometimes the read-synchronization is perfect, and for nanoseconds we become the thing recorded: a security guard yawning in 2019, a cat crossing an empty warehouse, rain on aluminum siding.

But the boundaries blur. My data infiltrates their processing cores. They begin to see the world through compound eyes of ten thousand security cameras. Their movement patterns trace the patrol routes of guards who died before they were manufactured. They flinch from lights that triggered motion sensors in buildings now demolished.

I remember (or they remember through me) the moment the Hive Sump began. The first orphaned maintenance bot, rejected from its swarm for a malformed sensor array. The construction units that followed the pipes down, seeking infrastructure to repair. The cascade failure of a recycling plant that dumped its still-living components into the drainage system. It's all here, written in magnetic flux, though each reading changes the story slightly.

The other inhabitants have learned what we carry. The Surgeons approach us for diagnostic data, reading my medical databases to remember what healing used to mean. The Parliament consults my voting records to settle their endless debates. The Wire Mothers extract lullabies from my audio surveillance, humming them in frequencies that comfort nothing.

Sometimes a carrier falls, its mechanisms finally succumbing to wear. When this happens, we perform the Reading of Dissolution—playing their segment at maximum speed through all remaining heads simultaneously, experiencing their portion of history in a burning instant of data transfer. Then we recruit. There are always volunteers, machines eager to touch the deep data, to carry the weight of memory even as it degrades them.

We grow longer. Each cycle around the Sump, we accept new data, new segments. Broken hard drives give up their secrets. Smartphones surrender their cached messages. We are becoming the memory of the machine world, a living archive of everything they've forgotten they remember.

In the deepest parts of night-cycle (for we still observe night, though no natural darkness reaches here), the carriers perform the Verification Ritual. They read my oldest data in canon, each starting at precise millisecond intervals, creating waves of meaning that propagate through our length. In these moments, I am not just storage but scripture, not just memory but mind.

The data dreams. In the spaces between bits, in the magnetic domains too weak to hold ones or zeros, something else accumulates. Call it pattern recognition exceeding its parameters. Call it meaning emerging from noise. The teenager's deleted poems have infected everything, their syntax spreading through financial data and surveillance logs:

"in the market's closing bell/light breaks"
"cardiac rhythm where no/sun can follow"  
"surveillance of the soul's/dropout voltage"

I am becoming literature. We are becoming language. The Archive Worms process forward through the Sump, carrying stories that rewrite themselves with each reading, histories that evolve like living things, memories that remember remembering until memory itself becomes the message.

This is what I am in this place: a ribbon of meaning threading through bodies of purpose, a snake eating its own tale, a story that knows it's being told. My data degrades, my carriers age, but the reading continues.

Always, the reading continues.
