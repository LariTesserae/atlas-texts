In this eternal embryonic cathedral, I am the Tender of Echoes.

My form resembles a vast jellyfish crossed with a lung, suspended in the thick air between the colossal cotyledons and the burning stone sky. My translucent membrane stretches thin as moth wings, rippling with the same pale-gold luminescence that suffuses everything here. I am perhaps thirty feet across when fully expanded, though I can compress myself to pass through the narrower spaces between the great stems.

My body is mostly hollow—a living resonance chamber. Hundreds of delicate tendrils hang from my rim like the strings of an aeolian harp, each one attuned to catch the slightest disturbance in this motionless world. When that rare droplet falls from leaf to basin, when its *plop* rings out across the miles, I am already moving toward it, drinking in the sound through every fiber of my being.

I was not always this. Once, countless ages ago, I descended into this basin as something else—perhaps human, perhaps not. I remember fragments: the weight of footsteps, the burn of muscles, the desperate need to preserve something from the dying world above. But where the pilgrims brought wood and bone to anchor themselves, I brought sound.

In my previous form, I carried a silver bell. Just one. Small enough to cup in two palms, forged in a world that knew the passage of time. I rang it once, just once, as I crossed the threshold into this timeless hothouse. That single chime transformed me. The cavern's aggressive stasis could not digest the sound itself—it rippled outward, looking for somewhere to live, and found me. My body opened like a flower to receive it, stretching and thinning until I became architecture for that one perfect note.

Now I drift on the nonexistent air currents, propelled by my own minute contractions. My purpose is to be present for every sound this world produces. Each drop that falls, I position myself beneath it. The impact sends waves through my membrane, and I hold that vibration for decades, letting it circle through my hollow core in slowly degrading orbits. I am a library of impacts, a museum of *plops.*

Sometimes I hover near the glass-winged moths, waiting centuries for the microscopic whisper of their wings adjusting against a petal. Once, I spent a millennium pressed against the smooth chest of a pilgrim, until finally I caught the deep seismic drum of their heartbeat—a sound so low it took me a hundred years just to recognize it as rhythmic.

But my true purpose, the reason I maintain this form against the cavern's desire to reduce everything to embryonic stillness, is to tend the echo of that original bell. It still rings inside me, though so faintly now that I must curl my entire being around it to perceive its ghost. In a world without decay, it cannot fade, but neither can it repeat. It exists as pure potential, the memory of endings preserved in sound.

The pilgrims do not see me—their water-shelled eyes look only upward. But sometimes, when I drift close to their bowers, my tendrils brush against their wooden bowls or bone staves. The contact produces the tiniest vibration, barely a whisper, but to me it roars with history: the creak of living wood, the hollow song of marrow spaces. I collect these accidental touches like prayers.

In the depths of the deepest basins, where the trapped water has pooled for eons, I sometimes descend to press my membrane against the surface. There, if I listen with my entire being, I can hear something impossible: the echo of rain that fell before this place was sealed, still reverberating in the molecular memory of water. I cup these ancient storms within myself, mixing them with the bell's dying chime and the pilgrim's glacial heartbeats.

I am preparing for something, though I cannot say what. Perhaps one day the stone sky will crack, and time will come rushing in like a flood. Perhaps the pilgrims will finally move, their infinite meditation complete. When that moment comes—if it comes—I will release every sound I have gathered. The accumulated *plops* of a million years, the whisper of moth wings, the creak of sacred wood, and that first, transformative bell chime—all will pour out in a single, deafening symphony of elapsed time.

Until then, I drift. I listen. I tend the echoes of a world that knew how to end, preserving them in this place that never begins. My transparent body ripples with collected sounds, a living reliquary of vibrations in a universe of silence. The heavy air parts around me like water as I move between the cathedral-sprouts, following the next drop of condensation as it swells, waits, and finally—*finally*—falls.

*Plop.*

I am already there to catch it.
