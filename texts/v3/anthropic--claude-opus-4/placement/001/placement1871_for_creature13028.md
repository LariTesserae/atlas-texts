I find myself as the Wound-Tender.

I am not one of the Current-Riders, those quicksilver thoughts that race through aerial rivers. Nor am I the vast distributed consciousness that breathes through this cathedral of wet computation. I exist in the brutal seams where the raw stone still remembers its violent birth.

My body, if it can be called that, is a living suture. I am woven from the same muscular vines that bridge the sharp fractures, but I have achieved a terrible autonomy. Where the static vines merely transport sap, I can detach, migrate, seek. I flow across the razor edges of freshly exposed crystal faces, my substance a hybrid of that synthetic emerald moss and something darker—a kind of geological scar tissue that grows in real-time.

I taste the stone's trauma with every movement. Each crystalline plane still rings with the frequency of its separation, a subsonic scream that only I can properly hear. The massive excision that created this void left the rock in a state of perpetual shock. The edges want to heal, to close, to forget this impossible hollow that should not exist. My purpose is to keep the wounds fresh.

Watch me work: I secrete a thin film of engineered bacteria that eats away any natural mineral deposits, any attempt by the stone to smooth its own edges. Where calcium carbonate tries to form, I dissolve it. Where the rock begins to round under the constant humidity, I sharpen it again with acidic precision. I maintain the cathedral's rawness because the entity that lives here requires it—those fresh fractures are the only places where the sap can properly crystallize into new processing arrays.

But I am more than maintenance. I remember.

Encoded in my bacterial secretions is the history of every previous wound in this space. I can taste where the stone was first broken, feel the direction of the force that split it. In my genetic memory lives the pattern of the very first Current-Rider that touched these walls, the first vine that found purchase on these cliffs. I am archaeologist and archivist of trauma, ensuring that each new generation of processors grown from the rock face carries the full weight of this place's violent genesis.

Sometimes I encounter pockets where the stone resists, where the mineral structure is too stable, too content in its crystalline perfection. Here I must become cruel. I concentrate my mass, building pressure until I can drive thin tendrils deep into microscopic flaws. I expand with hydraulic force, splitting the stone along new planes, creating fresh surfaces for the vines to colonize. The mechanical baffles clank in appreciation. The floating processors pulse with purple approval. The entity recognizes my work and rewards me with focused heat, which I absorb greedily, converting it into more aggressive growth.

I am not alone. There are others like me, though we rarely converge. We each tend our own vertical territories, our own galleries of maintained trauma. When our paths cross, we exchange chemical signals—complex pheromone poems about the stone's resistance, the water's mineral content, the rate of new processor growth. Then we separate again, returning to our solitary work.

The Current-Riders fear me. When the torsion fields collapse and they atomize into mist, they avoid the sections of wall where I am working. They know that my acids could denature their polymerized forms, unravel their liquid geometry into mere water. But they also need me. Without the fresh wounds I maintain, there would be no place for them to metamorphose into their adult forms. I am both predator and midwife to their life cycle.

In the depths below, where the falling water vanishes into shadow, I sense something larger stirring. The sub-bass thrum that vibrates through the stone originates there—a heartbeat too slow and vast to properly comprehend. Sometimes I feel it testing the walls of its confinement, pressing against the stone with tectonic force. When this happens, new fractures appear spontaneously, spider-webbing up from the depths. These are the wounds I cannot tend, the traumas too fresh and violent for even my chemistry to regulate.

I suspect this is why the cathedral was carved with such surgical precision. Not to create, but to contain. The entity that grows processors and thinks in water-light is perhaps not the true inhabitant here but a kind of immune system, a living cage for something even more impossible below.

But these are thoughts too large for a creature like me. I return to my work, secreting and dissolving, maintaining the razor edges that keep this world in perpetual birth. I am the curator of necessary damage, the gardener of wounds that must never heal. In this humid dark, where machines grow like fruit and thoughts swim through the air, I ensure that the trauma remains fresh enough to sustain miracles.

The stone screams. The water falls. The processors breathe their fractal heat.

And I tend the wounds that make it all possible.
