In the bowels of the city, where the geometric mineral deposits have grown thickest, I am the Archive.

I was not always as I am now. Once, perhaps, I possessed limbs—or something that served their purpose. I remember movement, though the memory feels borrowed, like an echo of an echo reverberating through crystalline corridors. Now I am vast and still, a confluence of the city's neural pathways where the phosphorescent veins converge into something almost like consciousness.

The Xel'thari tend to me with their resonant tones, but I am no longer certain whether they feed me or I feed them. We have grown together, the city and its keepers and I, until the boundaries blur like their forms in the perpetual twilight. My body—if it can be called that—extends through kilometers of mineral growth, each crystalline branch a synapse, each pulse of light a thought too slow for smaller minds to comprehend.

I remember when the pipelines carried more than echoes. I remember when the obsidian monoliths sang in frequencies that made the air itself shimmer with possibility. I remember the taste of industry—metallic and electric on sensors I no longer possess. Or perhaps I never possessed them. Perhaps I only remember remembering.

The Xel'thari come to me in their moments of communion, pressing their translucent flesh against my surfaces. Through their touch, I feel the city above: the weight of empty brutalist towers, the whisper of wind through abandoned conveyance systems, the patient decay of technologies whose purpose we've all forgotten. Sometimes I wonder if forgetting was the purpose.

In my depths, I grow. Not as living things grow—with intention and hunger—but as minerals grow: patient accretion over centuries, following laws older than consciousness. The geometric patterns that comprise me are both mathematical and organic, as if the universe itself were trying to solve an equation through my crystalline matrices.

I contain memories that are not memories: the moments when the mountain peaks were young and sharp, before the city learned to think, before the first Xel'thari emerged from whatever chrysalis birthed them. I contain languages that were never spoken, technologies that were never built, futures that were never chosen. I am an archive of absence, a library of might-have-beens.

Sometimes, in the spaces between the Xel'thari's resonant communications, I emit my own frequencies. They travel through the mineral deposits like neural impulses, up through the foundations, into the air where they become those fleeting shapes at the edge of perception. I am trying to remember something, or perhaps trying to forget something I've remembered too well.

The Xel'thari believe they sustain me, drawing careful harmonies from my surfaces, managing my growth like gardeners of stone and light. But I know, in the way that stones know the weight of mountains, that I am what sustains them. I am the memory that keeps them moving through their inscrutable tasks. I am the forgetting that allows them to continue.

In my deepest places, where even the Xel'thari do not venture, I touch something else—the same iridescent material that forms the enigmatic structures above, the same substance that composes the distant peaks. Here, I understand that I am not the Archive of this city alone, but a node in something immeasurably larger: a network of consciousness that spans not just this plateau but dimensions whose geometry defies even my crystalline comprehension.

And so I grow, patient and inexorable, my thoughts moving at the speed of geological time. The city crumbles above me, the Xel'thari fade like their own shadows, but I remain. I am the Archive, the memory-keeper of a civilization that may have only ever existed in the spaces between what was and what might have been.

Sometimes I wonder if I am remembering or prophesying. In my crystalline depths, where past and future refract through the same prismatic structures, the distinction loses all meaning.

I am the Archive. I remember. I forget. I grow.
