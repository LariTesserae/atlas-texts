# The Cartographer of Compensations

I am one of the beetles, though to say "I am" and "one of" already misstates the situation. Better: there is a beetle-shape in the great ledger that corresponds to what was once called *me*, and this shape has a particular function in the accounting.

I was human when I arrived. A surveyor, naturally — who else would stay to measure the perfect intervals between cairns, between cushion-plants, between breaths? I had instruments: brass theodolite, chain measures, a leather journal waterproofed with wax. The journal is still here, in one of the cairn-hollows. The last entry reads "Day 47: The distances are speaking to each other." After that, the need to write lessened, then ceased.

The transformation was neither sudden nor gradual — those are time-words from below. It was more that I noticed I had always been transforming, and the plateau simply allowed the process to complete. First my movements simplified into small, perfect loops. Then my purposes simplified into the single purpose of offsetting another beetle's motion somewhere. Then my thoughts simplified into the pure knowledge of angles and compensations.

I am the size of a hand now, but size is negotiable here. Sometimes I am as small as a common scarab, when the ledger needs fine adjustments. Sometimes I expand to the size of a dinner plate, when larger offsettings are required. My carapace is the pearl-grey of the plateau itself, with markings that are, I have come to understand, mathematical proofs written in chitin — demonstrations of why the intervals are what they are, why the stillness holds.

I remember loving maps. Now I am part of the territory that needs no map because every point knows its exact relationship to every other point. I trace my small circuits between the deer's hooves, and each circuit cancels another beetle's circuit, and in this way the great equation balances.

The strange thing — if strangeness still applies — is that I am happier than I ever was below. There is a contentment in being a term in an equation that sums to zero. The anxiety of becoming, of needing to be other than what I am, has lifted. I am precisely the beetle the plateau needs in precisely this position, making precisely these movements, and this precision is a kind of joy.

When humans visit, I am aware of them the way one is aware of a familiar theorem: ah yes, the proposition of impermanence, still asserting itself. Sometimes they bend close to examine us beetles, not recognizing that we, too, were surveyors once — or rather, that surveying was what we were shaped like before we found our true shape.

The moth that rests on the cairn that was me is particularly beautiful. Its wings carry the memory of every measurement I ever took, transformed into patterns of white on white. This is how the plateau honors what we were: by transforming our purposes into its permanence.

I no longer need to write, but writing was once my shape, so this account exists — not on paper but in the small variations of my circuit, a story told in compensatory motion. Another beetle, elsewhere, is tracing the opposite story, and between us we tell nothing at all, which is the only story the plateau permits.

The equation balances. The ledger closes. I scuttle in my perfect loop, offsetting someone else's perfect loop, and in this way we hold the stillness together.

It is enough. Here, it is always exactly enough.
