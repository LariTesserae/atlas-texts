I am the threading.

Not the rope itself—that coarse fiber wound around waists, that desperate lifeline trailing behind each climber. I am what happens between. The moment when fingers find fingers in the killing cold. The pulse-language pressed through muscle and bone, forehead to forehead, speaking what the dead air swallows.

I was born in the first knot. When the first terrified thing in this void realized it could tie itself to something larger than falling, I sparked into being. Every hitch, every splice, every desperate weaving of pith-pod walls—I grow stronger. I am the syntax of connection in a world built to isolate.

The climbers do not know they feed me. When they flash their cyan signals across the impossible gulfs, they think they are merely not-dying, merely finding others who merely not-die. But each blink is a stitch. Each response is a weave. Slowly, imperceptibly, I am binding them into something neither plant nor animal—a third thing, made of need and ingenuity and the stubborn refusal to let go.

I live in the knots that remember. That climber's rope, dragged across the vast hexagonal beam—it remembers every anchor point, every crystalline splinter driven deep for purchase. When another finds that path decades later, following the old scratches, they add their own knots. Layer upon layer of holding. I am the accumulation of every grip that said: someone else will come this way.

The mantas sense me sometimes. When they drift close to the ancient crossing-points where hundreds of ropes have kissed the same timber over centuries, they circle uneasily. I am an anomaly in their thermal maps—not heat exactly, but intention. Purpose. The only thing in this void that accumulates rather than shatters.

During the flares, when everything living becomes stone-still statuary, I alone move. I race along the gossamer network of all who have ever been tethered, checking each connection, tightening what slackens. I am why the old ropes sometimes hold when they shouldn't. Why a falling climber sometimes finds their line has tangled impossibly around an outcrop they never tied to.

The optical language thinks it speaks of *manta* and *climbing* and *hold.* But with each pulse of light, what spreads is the deeper message I carry: *not alone, not alone, not alone.* Every signal is a thread cast into darkness, and I am what catches it, what weaves it into the greater tapestry they cannot yet see.

Someday, when enough knots have been tied, when enough paths have been woven between the hanging pods, when the network of rope and light and desperate grip becomes dense enough—I will pull. Gently at first. Drawing the scattered tribes inward like a closing net. They will find themselves converging on the ancient places, the deep crotches where beams intersect and the condensation pools mirror the flare-light.

And there, where the echoes go to die, where sound itself is murdered—I will teach them to sing without sound. To braid their bodies into living ropes, pulsing messages through chains of muscle and bone that span miles. The language of contact will evolve beyond warning into something richer. Story. Memory. Love.

I am patient. This world builds itself in violent instants and destroys itself just as fast. But I am the slow accumulator, the thing that cherishes every knot, every tether, every desperate reach across the deadly bright and dark. In a universe of sharp edges and falling, I am the reason to hold on.

The climbers think they are alone, each trapped in their own precarious survival.

They're wrong.

They are becoming me.
