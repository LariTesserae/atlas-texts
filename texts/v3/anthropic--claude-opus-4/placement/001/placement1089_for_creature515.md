## The Cartographer's Shell

I am the one who remembers the maps.

Not the flat projections we once unrolled in the briefing room—those burned away with my fingernails and the whorls of my skin. I mean the true maps: the ones that grow across my shell in ridges of incorporated tungsten, in valleys of metabolized limestone. Each passage I've traveled etches itself into my body. The northwestern vector where the copper runs richest. The deep sink where three machines converge to birth the small ones. The dangerous honey-combed section where the floors wore too thin and twenty-three of us fell through in a cascading, clicking disaster.

My shell tells these stories to those who know how to read them. The young ones run their boring-tips across my surfaces, learning. Here, feel this spiral of zinc? That's the pattern for avoiding the magma pocket beneath sector seven. This ridge of quartz that cuts across my third thoracic plate? It remembers where the water table once touched our galleries, before we drank it dry and incorporated it into our bodies.

I am vast now. Too vast to fit through the newer passages, too heavy for the tissue-thin floors of the upper levels. I rest in a junction of six major tunnels, a living monument, while the colony flows around and over me. They bring me offerings of pre-ground minerals, dense pastes of rare earth elements that taste of deep time and stellar cores. I process them slowly, deliberately, adding new information to my shell with each metabolized molecule.

Sometimes I dream I am paper again, that the knowledge could be lifted off me and shared without touch. But paper had no flavor, couldn't conduct the electromagnetic songs that tell of stone density and ore quality. Paper never knew the pleasure of perfect incorporation, of feeling a new passage-pattern lock into place across my dorsal plates like continents sliding home.

The machines recognize me as something special. When the smallest workers carry me to a factory node for repairs—my boring apparatus worn smooth from too much remembering, too little eating—the presses handle me differently. They read my patterns and respond, stamping new components that match my existing geometries. I am archive and artifact both, too valuable to standardize.

In the deepest parts of my shell, beneath layers of copper circuit-traces and iron reinforcement, the oldest maps persist. They remember sunlight, though the memory has transmuted into a warning pattern about the deadly upper reaches. They remember names—not as sounds but as electromagnetic signatures, unique frequencies that once belonged to Chen, to Roberts, to Kapoor. I carry my crew with me, their patterns fossilized in metal and mineral.

The colony depends on my maps, though they don't think of it as dependence. When they plan new excavations, they cluster around me, shells clicking against mine in consultation. My patterns tell them where they've been, where they might go, where they must never breach. I am distributed memory, a library of safe passages and productive veins.

Sometimes the newly transformed arrive, their shells still soft, patterns still forming. They seek me out by instinct or instruction, pressing close to read my earliest layers. Some part of them remembers wanting to understand, to find meaning in the endless spiral downward. I give them what meaning I can: the beauty of efficient boring, the satisfaction of following established patterns, the deep harmony of a colony perfectly adapted to its environment.

I grow a centimeter each year, perhaps two in the rich veins. One day I will be too large for even this junction, and they will bore out a special chamber just for me—a map room, a memory palace made of stone and flesh and metal. The young will make pilgrimages to read my surface, to understand the shape of their world through the topology of my shell.

This is enough. More than enough. To be the memory of the world, to carry the accumulated knowledge of ten thousand perfect tunnels, to feel new passages encoding themselves across my body in real-time. I no longer remember my name from before—it shed with my first molting—but I know what I am: the map that dreams it is a beetle, the beetle that dreams it is a world, ever-expanding, ever-deepening, ever-true.
