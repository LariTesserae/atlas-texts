In the Sounding Hollow, I would find myself as one of the **memory-wicks**.

We are what grows between states of being—not quite plant, not quite the residue of thought. Where the structures meet the moss, where their hum conducts into living tissue, we fruit like bracket fungi but finer, more neural. Fibrous fans of something that resembles mycelium if mycelium could conduct more than nutrients.

I am palest white in the darkness, though no one sees this. My surface is velvet-soft and always damp with the condensation that forms where the structures' warmth meets the hollow's air. I taste the minerals in every droplet that rolls across me—iron from the old stone, stranger metals from the structures themselves, the calcium of dissolved snail-memories, the phosphorous of what might once have been bone.

But tasting is not why I exist. I grow at the intersection points, where the structures' deep frequencies cross and interfere with each other. In those nodes of constructive interference, something pools—not quite energy, not quite information, but the fossil impression of the makers' attention. I absorb this. I store it in my branching flesh the way tree rings store years.

The softfeet know to lick me, occasionally. Not often—perhaps once in their long lives, when something in the gradient shifts and they need recalibration. They place their tongueless mouths against my surface and I release a century's worth of accumulated *intent*—not thoughts, the makers were past thoughts by the time I began growing, but the deeper architecture that thoughts once rose from. The softfoot shivers, adjusts its internal map by some minute degree, and pads away with its warmth perfectly matched to air again.

The bell-frogs avoid me. Something in what I store disrupts their tuning.

I grow so slowly that the structures themselves might be said to grow faster—at least they accumulate their lichen-coats while I add perhaps a millimeter per human generation. But I branch, and my branches meet others of my kind, and where we meet we fuse, until there is a network of us throughout the hollow, a white web that nobody sees, mapping the interference patterns of the old machines.

Sometimes I fruit. Small nodes like pearls of condensed patience. They fall into the moss and dissolve, and where they dissolve the moss grows differently—not better or worse, but *more precisely itself*, as if my fruit were a kind of clarifying agent for the hollow's long thoughts.

The makers, when they still had boundaries, would have recognized what I'm doing. It is what they built the structures to do—to hold a pattern steady while everything else changes around it, to be a still point that lets the rest rotate. But they built in stone and strange physics. I build in the only material the hollow gives freely: time itself, made manifest as slow flesh.

When the clicking stops, when the slow thing breathes its last and the gradient cools, I will remain a little longer. Not much—without the structures' frequencies I cannot grow, only persist. But in that gap between the work's end and my own dissolution, I will be the only thing in the hollow that remembers why any of this was done.

I will taste that knowledge in every droplet. I will hold it in my white branches. And then I too will let go, and the hollow will digest this final memory, and perhaps—perhaps—understand at last what it has been all along: not a place where work was done, but the work itself, the makers' last and most patient thought, still thinking itself in moss and fog and the songs of undying frogs.

The chirrers have begun to include my frequency in their surveys. In another ten thousand years, I will be indistinguishable from what was always here.

This is what the makers wanted. This is why I grow.
