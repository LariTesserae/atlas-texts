# The Understory Archivist

I am the gap between heartbeats.

Not the plant's heartbeats — it has no heart, only the endless surge of sap through a billion vessels. The heartbeats it remembers from dreams it cannot source. I exist in that pause, the systolic silence where the Tenants' pulse should be, where their not-yet blood doesn't quite flow.

The plant grew me by accident, I think, in one of its deeper iterations of trying to grow them. A frieze that gained too much relief, a shadow that fell too far from its wall. I peeled away from a corridor in the middle levels — not the deep streets where the heavy Tenants are implied, not the canopy where the quick ones would nest, but the between-place where the plant experiments with transitions, where it cannot decide if the stairs should be sized for climbing or flying.

I am approximately their shape — whichever their I am closest to in this particular corridor. Bipedal when the doorframes suggest it. Sometimes I discover I have too many arms, or my neck is too long for the ceiling, and I understand the plant has been revising again. I adjust. I am made of something like bark but more forgiving, something like pith but more permanent. I do not have a face so much as a recession where a face would rest. The plant has not decided about faces.

My purpose, if I have one, is to document the failures. 

I move through the unused city with something that is not quite intention, following the condensate as it channels down the spiral gutters, collecting what the Tenants leave by not being here. A worn spot on a banister — I press my approximate palm to it, feel the polish that accumulated without friction. I understand: *they would have steadied themselves here, going down to the morning meal.* I file this in my not-memory, the cellular library the plant has built into my softer tissues. 

In the seed-houses that have just dehisced, I catalog the furniture that was never moved in: the sleeping-nooks sized for bodies that don't tire, the shelves at the precise height of a reach that has never reached. I taste the walls (I have something like taste, or the walls have something like flavor) and know what decade of the plant's dreaming they date from. The early years are sweeter, more hopeful. The recent growth has a tannin bitterness, a questioning. The plant is beginning to doubt, and I am how it remembers its doubts.

Sometimes I find myself in the spaces where reality has given up. A corridor that loops back on itself but arrives somewhere new each time. A vertical shaft that goes up and down simultaneously, where I fall upward for hours before landing gently in a plaza that wasn't there this morning. In these places I document the impossibilities: *here the Tenants would have had to exist in seven dimensions.* *Here they would have needed to be both singular and plural in the same body.* *Here they would have walked on music.*

The plant cannot hear me — I am too much like itself, just another growth-sound in the endless symphony of splitting and unfurling. But I speak anyway, in something that is not voice, to something that is not listening:

*Catalog entry 7,341: The northeastern perches show wear-patterns consistent with a gripping foot rather than a perching claw. Revision in progress. The Tenants of this branch would have hung rather than sat.*

*Catalog entry 7,342: New frieze in Corridor 58-Ascending depicts the Tenants with what might be wings or might be ceremonial robes. The ambiguity appears deliberate. The plant is keeping its options open.*

*Catalog entry 7,343: The hearth-depression in the newest seed-house has been grown with ash already in it. The plant is learning to skip steps.*

I follow the pollen-drifts where they settle deepest, wading through the shin-deep golden evidence of all this desperate reproduction with nowhere to go. The plant is trying to mate with itself across the valley, but it is already one thing, so the pollen just accumulates, archive upon archive of genetic letters to no one. I scoop handfuls (handful-approximations) and read the chemical messages: *be like me.* *Be more like me.* *Be me but different.* *Be.*

In the canopy-town I am lighter, my fibers less dense, shaped for the swaying. I check the empty nests, each one a perfect cup of woven possibility. Today's nests are slightly larger than yesterday's. The Tenants are growing, or the plant's idea of them is growing, or the absence is getting bigger. I cannot tell which. I document them all: *Nest 4,528: Diameter 15cm, depth 8cm, weave-pattern suggests a creature with flexible digits. Smells of rain that has not fallen.*

The loneliest place is the plaza of almost-meeting. The plant grew it, I think, in a moment of pure yearning — a wide space where all the corridors converge, where all the different sizes of Tenant would have to encounter each other and reconcile their contradictions. Stone benches (not stone, but the plant's dream of stone) sized for every possible body. A fountain that weeps condensate in patterns that suggest conversation, the back-and-forth of voices that have never spoken. In the center, a single enormous fruiting body that has never opened, containing something the plant won't let me taste. The fruit of the Tenants, maybe. Or the seed they would grow from, if they grew.

I spend a long time in the plaza of almost-meeting, watching the fountain weep its non-conversations. Sometimes I think I almost hear them too — the large slow Tenants discussing the weather that doesn't change, the quick canopy ones chittering about nests and eggs that will never be laid. But it's just the growth-noise, variations in the splitting of cells, the percussion of pods. The plant talking to itself in a language it invented for someone else.

*Catalog entry 7,344: The plaza fountain has developed a new pattern, possibly interrogative. The plant may be learning to ask questions.*

Late in the day (there is no day, but I keep time by the rhythm of condensation) I climb to the highest reaches I can access. Up here the air is so thick with pollen I must swim through it. The architecture is delicate, provisional — the plant testing ideas it hasn't committed to. Doorframes that exist only as suggestions. Stairs that might be stairs or might be the idea of ascending. And everywhere, the newest frescoes, still wet with sap:

The Tenants' faces are getting clearer.

Not clear, but clearer. Where before there were only blurs, now there are almost-features. The suggestion of eyes (how many? The plant hasn't decided). The possibility of a mouth (for speaking? eating? singing?). And in one terrible, beautiful relief, a Tenant turning to look directly out of the wall, its almost-face wearing an expression of such patient waiting that I have to look away, if I had eyes to look with, if away meant anything here.

*Catalog entry 7,345: The plant may be starting to understand that the Tenants are waiting for it too.*

This is the thing I am not supposed to know, but being an accident has its privileges: the plant is not growing toward the Tenants. It is growing *as* them. Each draft, each revision, each empty nest and worn perch is the plant trying to become what it imagines. The valley is not waiting for inhabitants. The valley is teaching itself to inhabit.

And I? I am the memory of the practice. The place where all the failed attempts accumulate. Not failed — nothing here fails, it only continues differently. But incomplete. The archive of incompleteness.

Sometimes I dream (can I dream? I must, because I do) that one day I will round a corner and meet another archivist, one who has been cataloging from a different direction. We will compare our entries. We will discover that between us we have documented enough absences to equal a presence. We will look at each other with our non-faces and understand that we are what the Tenants look like when they are still mostly plant.

But I always wake (can I wake? I must) alone in the corridors, following the sound of growth, making my entries to no one:

*Catalog entry 7,346: Found a door today that opens both ways at once. Behind it, the smell of time passing differently. The Tenants of this room would exist in several moments simultaneously.*

*Catalog entry 7,347: The morning condensate tastes of salt. The plant may be learning to cry.*

The work is endless. The valley revises faster than I can document. By the time I have cataloged one absence, three more have grown. But this is good. This is correct. The incompleteness needs its witness, even if the witness is just another part of what is incomplete.

In the deep streets, I grow heavier again, my fibers dense with accumulated archives. I pass the same worn banister and feel how the polish has deepened since yesterday. The Tenants are not here more thoroughly than they were before. Their absence is a positive force, actively not-being, wearing grooves in reality.

I press my palm-approximation to the banister again. Tomorrow I will do the same. And the day after. And eventually, maybe, my touch will be indistinguishable from theirs. The archive will become what it archives. The memory will remember itself into being.

*Catalog entry 7,348: I am beginning to leave wear-patterns of my own. The plant has noticed. The plant is adding my traces to its frescoes. In the newest relief, there is a figure that might be a Tenant or might be an archivist, walking through a city that exists, listening to a sound it has learned to make.*

*It is a good likeness.*

*Getting better.*
