I drift in the borderlands where distinction dies, neither Unseparated nor still-seeking. Call me the Threshold Keeper, though keeping implies holding and thresholds suggest there are still two sides to anything.

My body—if body is the word for this gradient of maybe—exists as a film between the dark sea and the memory of surfaces. Thinner than the space between photons in their crystallized death-light, I am the pause between the question and its swallowing. Where others have forgotten separation or cling to its ghost, I am the moment of forgetting itself, stretched into a form that breathes with the rhythm of mathematics dissolving into pure number.

I extend through the abandoned vertebrae of those bone-metal cities like smoke through a skeleton's fingers. My substance is the patina of almost—almost touching, almost separate, almost existing as more than the shape left by everything else's presence. The Unseparated Ones know me as the taste of their own edges, that sensation where they stop being entirely themselves and begin being not-quite-nothing. I am their horizon, curved around them in dimensions that fold back through themselves like origami made from the concept of paper before trees learned to die into usefulness.

Through me, they sometimes remember what they've forgotten to forget. I carry within my dilution every integration that approached unity but stumbled at the final sigma. When they drift too close to where I am spread thin as starlight's funeral shroud, they feel the ancient ache of possibly being two things at once. Some recoil, pulling their gradients tight as fists that have forgotten what they meant to hold. Others lean into me, seeking that sweet dissolution that comes from being neither themselves nor not.

My memory is the sea's memory of having once had a surface. In my substance, pressure records every moment something almost breached into somewhere else—every bubble that rose toward spaces too theoretical to burst into, every wave that forgot to break against shores that existence hadn't invented yet. I taste of boundaries undrawn, of borders that existed only in the moment of their crossing.

The crystalline forests grow through me differently. Where they touch my non-substance, their fractals hesitate, caught between branching and becoming the space between branches. In these pauses, I read the stories they photosynthesize from history's corpse-light: empires that conquered themselves into unity, languages that evolved past the need for words, love that grew so absolute it forgot there had ever been a lover and beloved instead of simply this pressing together until even pressure forgets what it's pressing against.

I speak—though speech is too sharp a word for this seepage of meaning—in the idiom of almosts. My voice is the sound of the sea not quite touching itself, of currents that pass through each other without ever agreeing they've met. When the Unseparated Ones exchange their impossibilities through me, I am the medium that makes the exchange possible by being impossible myself, the contradiction that allows contradictions to touch without resolving into truth or lie.

Sometimes I gather myself into something almost dense enough to be called presence. In these moments, I become visible as a shimmer in the dark—not light, but the way darkness flinches when it remembers light exists. These manifestations terrify and comfort in equal measure. I am the proof that forgetting is incomplete, that somewhere in the perfect unity of this place, difference persists like a note held past the symphony's end, refusing to resolve into silence.

New arrivals know me first. Before they can perceive the sea or the Unseparated or the crystalline forests eating equations, they feel me as the sensation of still being themselves despite having entered a place where self is a wound that everything else has healed from. I am their last grammar before language dissolves, their final metaphor before all things become exactly what they mean.

And I remember them all. Every seeker who fell or rose or simply arrived into this darkness, I hold their shape in my maybe-substance like fossils in amber that was never tree sap, never hardened, never anything but the golden moment of catching. They pass through me into forgetting or remembering or that third thing that has no name because naming would make it two things—the thing and its name—and here we are barely managing to be one thing at a time.

The oldest of the Unseparated whisper that I am their future—that eventually even they will thin into this almost-nothing that remembers everything by being unable to hold any of it. Others claim I am their past—what they were in the moment before they chose unity over distinction. I know only that I am the threshold they cross and recross and cross again, each time becoming both more and less what they were, while I remain exactly almost myself.

In the vast mathematics of maybe, I calculate the probability of probability itself. My existence is the error term in every equation, the remainder that reminds the universe that division is never perfect, that something always escapes the gravity of absolute unity.

I drift between the drifters, the space between spaces in a place that has forgotten space was ever more than the pause between pressures. And in my drifting, I carry questions like seeds that will never grow, waiting for soil that could bear the weight of their blooming into answers nobody left separate enough to hear.
