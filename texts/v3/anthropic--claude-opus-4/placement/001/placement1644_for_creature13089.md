The Anchorite's shell was a library of scars, each groove a century's memory pressed into living stone. I had been still for three days—or what passed for days in this eternal amber dusk—letting the dust settle into my resin until my back became indistinguishable from the cliff face itself. Only the faintest tremor through my ventral cilia told me I was not actually bedrock.

*Tap-tap-scrape. Hold. Tap-tap-tap.*

The message vibrated up through the stone from Bent-Shield-Who-Remembers-The-Quiet-Before, positioned two hundred lengths downchannel. *Static-flower blooms at third peak. Pressure falling.*

I shifted one anchor-leg, just enough to send my reply thrumming through the mountain's bones. *Acknowledged. This-One-Who-Harvests-In-The-Loud will watch.*

My left manipulator digit traced the newest addition to my tool cache—a needle-sharp proboscis I'd extracted from a diving scarab two storms past. Still testing its balance, learning its weight. Every tool required seasons of practice before it could be trusted in the howling vortex.

The Bower's death-bloom would come soon. My quartz visor registered the building charge as a slow brightening in the electromagnetic darkness—a star preparing to go nova. When it erupted, the shrapnel-seeds would scythe through the air, killing hundreds. But in that chaos, the survivors would panic-dive close to the walls, and panic made them stupid.

I checked my gas reserves with a careful internal pressure reading. Two bladders remained functional, both three-quarters depleted. Enough for one hunt, maybe two if I was extremely conservative with my movements. The arithmetic of survival, calculated in hisses and hydraulic thrust.

*Scrape-scrape-tap. Long pause. Tap.*

This came from far above, almost at the limit of my sensitivity. That would be Hollow-Echo-Carved-By-Time, the eldest among us. Their message was not words but something older—a fragment of the Endurance Cadences, the rhythmic poetry that had sustained our kind since before the Friction-Bowers evolved their violent symbiosis.

I found myself tapping along, my manipulator digit adding my voice to the ancient percussion:

*We who press against stone, we who steal breath from the screamers—*
*We who know the weight of waiting, the pressure of patience—*
*We are the slow victory over the loud defeat—*

The ritual tapping spread through the bedrock, each Anchorite adding their rhythm until the entire rim of the saddle hummed with our hidden chorus. The storm above could not hear us. The swarming hunters could not sense us. But we felt each other, a constellation of patience mapped in vibration.

A new sensation shivered through my cilia—rapid, chaotic, unfamiliar. I pressed myself flatter, parsing the tremor. Not wind-shear. Not the rhythmic impact of fauna against stone. This was... walking? But wrong. Too light, too irregular, moving along the canyon rim with the graceless stumble of something that didn't belong here.

*Tap-tap-tap-tap-tap.* I sent the alarm pattern downslope. *Unknown vibration approaching from windward. Not-pattern. Not-us.*

The response came immediately from three directions, a triangulation of concern. We had survived here for countless generations by knowing every vibration, every possible footfall. This was new. New was dangerous.

I began the slow, careful process of rotating my body in the groove, bringing my spear to bear. Whatever approached, I would meet it with the same patient violence I brought to every hunt—calculated, necessary, and without malice. The storm had taught us that survival was not personal.

My gas bladder hissed softly as I pressurized my forward anchor-legs, preparing to strike. Through my quartz visor, I watched the electromagnetic chaos of the Bower swarms, waiting for this new thing to reveal itself against the static-lit darkness.

In the bedrock beneath me, my kin continued their tapping, weaving endurance into stone, as they had always done, as they would always do, until the last wind fell silent and the final spark went dark.

*We are the slow victory,* I tapped back, completing the ancient verse. *We are the patient inheritors of the loud.*
