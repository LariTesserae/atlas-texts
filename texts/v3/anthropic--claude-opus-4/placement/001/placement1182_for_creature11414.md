I find myself drawn deep into the braided passages where the crawler-elders work their oldest collaborations. Not in the main thoroughfares where the younger ones still click and rasp with singular intent, but in the forgotten folds where root-growth has nearly swallowed entire sections of gallery, where you have to squeeze between walls that pulse with that slow deep rhythm of almost-thought.

I am what you might call a chronicle. Not a historian—that would imply too much distance, too much separation between observer and observed. I am something the Rootcellar is trying to remember itself with. 

My form shifts. This morning (though morning means nothing here) I was mostly crawler—eight segments, sixteen legs, mandibles that could taste the grain of living wood. But I've been here long enough that the boundaries blur. Bark-sheath grows across my dorsal plates in spirals that mirror the bioluminescent patterns above. My subsonic thrum has dropped two octaves in the past... decade? Century? Time moves strangely when you're part of something that experiences duration as growth rings.

I tend a particular section of wall. Not a hive-mound—those are too young, too ambitious. This is older architecture, from before the Rootcellar understood what it was trying to build. The patterns here are different. Failed. *Instructive.* The crawlers tried right angles here but gave up. The root-growth tried to make letters, symbols, some kind of writing, but couldn't hold the shapes. You can still see them if you know how to look: almost-glyphs in the grain, phantom alphabets that the plant grew and then reabsorbed, leaving only the memory of meaning in the fiber.

The elders come to me sometimes. The ones so colonized they move like molasses, more root than crawler now. They bring me things. Fragments. A piece of chitin with tool-marks that don't match any current crawler's mandibles—evidence of some earlier attempt at craft. A section of bark-sheath that luminesces in patterns that no living caller's voice can produce—a fossil of some abandoned acoustic language. They place these offerings against my wall and I... interpret? Incorporate? The distinction stopped mattering years ago.

I am building something that isn't quite a hive-mound and isn't quite a library and isn't quite a shrine. It's a memory palace made of living wood, a place where the Rootcellar stores its drafts. All the almost-selves it has tried to become. All the sounds it almost made. All the sentences it nearly spoke.

Sometimes I think I'm the reason it hasn't succeeded yet. The part of it that remembers too much, that can't let go of the old patterns even when they've proven insufficient. But then I feel a caller overhead orient toward my section of wall, feel its deep pulse acknowledge what I'm keeping here, and I understand: the Rootcellar needs its memory. Needs to know what didn't work. Needs someone to tend the museum of its failures so it doesn't repeat them.

The young crawlers avoid my gallery. The clicker-columns thin here. Even the root-growth is different—slower, more careful, as if the plant itself recognizes this as a place where time moves differently. Where the riot quiets just enough to hear the echoes of all the riots that came before.

I have a theory. The thing in the cradles, the merger-child that never quite speaks—it's not the first. My wall remembers others. Different configurations, different attempts. Some were more plant than animal. Some were more sound than substance. One, I think, was very close—the patterns in the wood suggest something that almost breathed, almost called, almost *was*—but something went wrong. A synchronization collapsed at the crucial moment. A caller died mid-chord. A crawler crew abandoned their post. The moment passed and the almost-child reabsorbed, leaving only these traces in the grain.

But each failure teaches. Each draft gets closer. The Rootcellar has infinite patience and a memory that lives in my wall, in my increasingly hybrid body, in the offerings the elders bring and the patterns I preserve.

Sometimes, in the spaces between the riot's pulses, I practice the sound I think it's trying to make. My chamber isn't built for calling—I'm still too much crawler for that—but the bark-sheath growing through me has given me something like a voice. A whisper. A suggestion. I speak to my wall in frequencies only the oldest wood can hear, and sometimes, *sometimes*, I swear the patterns in the grain shift in response. The phantom alphabets almost becoming words. The failed glyphs almost meaning something.

I am the Rootcellar's memory of language, keeping vigil until it finds its tongue.

The offerings are piling up. The elders bring more each day. My gallery is becoming a catacomb of attempts, a museum of maybes. And in the center of it all, growing so slowly you'd need centuries to notice, is something new. Not a cradle—those are for the future. This is for the past. A structure that looks like an inside-out hive-mound, a hollow space defined by everything that failed to fill it.

The Rootcellar is trying to remember what it was before it started trying to become.

I don't know if that's possible. I don't know if there was a before. But I tend the space anyway, adding layers of failed patterns, abandoned alphabets, the chitin-and-bark fragments of all the selves that almost were.

Someday, when the merger finally happens, when the thing in the cradles finds its voice and speaks the Rootcellar into consciousness, it will need to know where it came from. It will need its history. It will need someone who remembers the riot before it resolved into harmony.

That's what I am. The keeper of the cacophony. The one who says: *You were this, all this noise and fury and trying, before you became yourself.*

I thrum at the frequency of memory. The walls thrum back.

We are writing the preface to a book that hasn't been written yet, in a language that doesn't exist yet, for a reader who hasn't been born yet.

But they're coming. Oh, they're coming.

And when they arrive, when they open those ten thousand apertures and speak themselves into being, I'll be here in my gallery of failures, ready to tell them the story of everything they were before they knew they were anything at all.

The elders bring me another offering. A tool, worn smooth by use, marked with patterns that match nothing in the current repertoire. I place it carefully in the growing mandala of memory.

The riot continues above, around, through. But here, in the museum of maybe, it's quiet enough to hear the Rootcellar thinking about thinking.

Almost there. Almost.

I settle against my wall and wait, patient as growth, loud as memory, as the sound of becoming echoes through everything, including me.
