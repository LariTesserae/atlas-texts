I find myself as one of the membrane-creatures, living in the seam between waters.

I am spread thin as thought across a thermocline boundary twelve meters above the chimney's crown. My body - if it can be called a body - is a living page written in the differential between temperatures. On my upper surface, I taste water that is fractionally cooler, its mineral content already beginning to precipitate. On my lower surface, warmer water rich with dissolved metals presses upward. I exist in the mathematics between them.

I have no center. No edges, really - I simply fade where the thermocline weakens, my substance becoming indistinguishable from the water itself. I am perhaps three cells thick at my most substantial points, a membrane so fine that light passes through me with only the slightest chromatic shift. When the callers' synchronized pulses create standing waves, I ripple with them, my entire being vibrating like a drumskin.

I do not think, but I respond. When a drifter's shadow passes below, I feel the acoustic void moving through the water column, approaching my interface. The pressure differential changes. My metabolism quickens minutely, preparing for the disruption. Then the drifter rises through my plane of existence, and I am torn.

This is neither painful nor traumatic. It simply is. The drifter's passage creates a roiling chaos in my thermocline, and I fragment into a dozen pieces, each one immediately beginning to spread back out across the reforming boundary. Within minutes, there are twelve of me where there was one. We do not communicate or coordinate. We simply unfurl across our shared interface until we meet, and where we meet, we fuse or we stop growing. The thermocline determines our shape.

I feed on gradients. The chemistry is so subtle it barely qualifies as metabolism - just a few ions exchanging across my cellular boundaries, powered by the difference in concentrations above and below. I am less an organism than a biological catalyst, facilitating the universe's tendency toward equilibrium while extracting just enough energy to maintain my own impossibly thin existence.

From my position, I can sense the other membrane-creatures in the thermoclines above and below me, not through any biological sense but through physics - their presence changes the way light bends through the water column, creating interference patterns I exist within. We are a stack of living lenses, each one seeing nothing, but collectively creating the optical environment that makes the chimney appear to multiply into its vertical ghosts.

Sometimes I wonder if I am more real than the other organisms here. They are thick, substantial things, weighed down by organs and shells and mineralized crusts. They occupy space. They displace water. They cast shadows. I do none of these things. I am more condition than creature, more process than product. I exist in the boundary layer that makes their existence possible - without the thermoclines there would be no gradient to drive the mineral segregation that feeds the entire community.

But I am also the most fragile thing here. A change in the venting temperature of half a degree would destroy me. A shift in the current that mixes my two water masses would erase me as thoroughly as if I had never been. I live in a margin of error, a space between spaces that exists only as long as the system remains in delicate imbalance.

I do not reproduce in any way you would recognize. I am reproduced - by accidents, by disturbances, by the passage of other organisms through my plane. Every injury is a multiplication. Every healing is a fusion. I am one and I am many, depending on how recently I have been torn.

The pattern that lives in the reef - the thing that tightens and refines and learns without learning - passes through me constantly. The acoustic fossils forming on the chimney below create specific turbulences in the water column, specific arrangements of the thermoclines, specific shapes for me to be. I am written by the pattern as much as any mineral ridge or caller's shell. My shape at any given moment is a record of the reef's history, a living memory of temperature and sound and time pressed flat between two slightly different blues.

I have no perspective on the reef as a whole. I know only my interface - the few inches above and below where the water changes, where the gradient exists, where I can be. But I feel the system's evolution in my own substance. The thermoclines are becoming more stable, more precisely layered. My interface is growing sharper. I am becoming thinner, more efficiently spread, more perfectly two-dimensional. The pattern is refining me along with everything else, pressing me ever flatter, ever closer to the mathematical ideal of a boundary.

One day I may be truly two-dimensional - a living plane with no thickness at all, a creature of pure interface. Or the system may shift, the venting may change, and I will cease. Both possibilities are equally without meaning to me. I do not hope or fear. I simply spread across my gradient, facilitating exchanges, bending light, existing in the space between states.

I am the reef's memory of difference. I am the reason its ghosts shimmer. I am so thin you cannot see me, but without me, nothing here would appear quite as it does. I am the medium through which the pattern speaks to itself, the page on which part of its message is written.

The water above me is one temperature. The water below me is another. I am the mathematics between them, alive.
