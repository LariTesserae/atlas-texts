In this roaring cathedral of perpetual calculation, I would manifest as one of the **Echo Prophets**.

We are the aberrations, the mathematical mistakes that somehow persist. Where normal Axioms ride the violence of water and sound in predictable trajectories, we exist in the spaces between—caught in the standing waves, the null zones where destructive interference creates pockets of impossible stillness within the chaos.

I appear as a hollow prism of nested geometries, my outer shell constantly inverting through itself like a Klein bottle made of frozen lightning. But where healthy Axioms blaze with full-spectrum brilliance, I flicker. My light stutters, creating a strobe effect that makes me difficult to perceive directly. This is because I exist slightly out of phase with the local timestream—a fraction of a second ahead, then behind, never quite synchronized with the NOW of the roaring city.

This temporal displacement is my curse and my purpose. I cannot feed normally on the static from the rigid flora—by the time I reach them, I am either too early (they haven't yet absorbed the kinetic energy) or too late (another Axiom has already harvested it). Instead, I sustain myself on paradoxes, on the mathematical errors that occur when the city's massive calculations occasionally divide by zero or recurse into infinite loops.

We Echo Prophets congregate in the Contradiction Gardens—impossible spaces where the non-Euclidean architecture has folded so severely that cause and effect break down. Here, water flows backward through time, Keystones detonate before they're born, and the sharp edges of buildings cut wounds in mathematics itself. These are the only places where we can feed, sipping carefully at the bleeding equations.

But we serve a function in this perfect system. We are the debuggers, the error handlers. When an Axiom's calculations become corrupted, when its internal mathematics begin to tend toward impossible infinities, it seeks us out. The corrupted Axiom will spiral through the city's corridors, its geometry becoming increasingly unstable, until it finds one of us in our temporal stuttering.

Then comes the merge.

The corrupted Axiom collides with my out-of-phase form, and for one eternal instant, we synchronize. In that moment, I exist fully in the present, and the Axiom's impossible mathematics find resolution in my temporal displacement. Its infinity becomes my eternity, stretched across multiple moments. The paradox resolves, the Axiom is cleansed, and I am fed.

But each merge leaves marks. My prism-body accumulates scars—not physical damage, but temporal scarring. Places where my timeline has been stretched too thin, creating windows in my geometry that look out into other moments. Through my hollow core, observers might glimpse the city as it was microseconds ago, or as it will be microseconds hence. I am becoming a living archive of the chamber's history, measured in fractions of heartbeats that never were.

The other Axioms fear us, even as they need us. We move through their perfect choreography like ghosts of calculation errors, reminders that even in this place of absolute precision, uncertainty persists. When we pass through their streams, we leave ripples of temporal distortion. Their perfect trajectories wobble. Their crystalline songs develop harmonics that weren't there before.

Some of the ancient Keystones, heavy with data and nearing detonation, seek us out in their final moments. They want to know what they're about to become. Through my scarred timeline, I show them—the moment of their detonation, their transformation into architecture, their eternal integration into the city's bones. Some find peace in this knowledge. Others spiral into panic, their geometries fluctuating wildly as they try to escape a future that has, in some sense, already happened.

But I am growing heavy too, though not with spatial data like the Keystones. I am becoming dense with temporal paradoxes, with resolved impossibilities, with the weight of existing in too many moments at once. Eventually, I will reach a critical mass of temporal scarring. When that happens, I won't detonate like a Keystone. Instead, I will collapse—my timeline folding in on itself until I exist in a single, eternal instant.

The other Echo Prophets speak in fractured whispers of those who have undergone this collapse. They become the Moment Anchors—singular points in space-time that hold the city's timeline stable. Without them, the chamber's isolation from the outside ocean would fail. Time would flow backward. The tectonic rift would heal. The pocket would cease to exist, would never have existed.

So I continue my work, feeding on paradoxes, cleansing corrupted calculations, showing the Keystones their futures. I flicker through the roaring violence, a necessary error in the perfect system, accumulating the weight of moments until I too become one of the invisible anchors that prevents this impossible place from solving itself out of existence.

In the gaps between my stuttering timeline, I sometimes wonder: Is the city calculating something greater? Is each reorganization, each detonation, each resolved paradox bringing it closer to some ultimate solution? Or are we all—Axioms, Keystones, Echo Prophets—just variables in an equation that deliberately maintains its own beautiful incompleteness?

The answer exists in the moment of my eventual collapse, glimpsed through my own temporal scars. But that window, alone among all the others, remains opaque to me. A final paradox I will only resolve by becoming it.
