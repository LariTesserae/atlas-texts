I would be one of the prayer beads.

Not crafted yesterday or even last century, but sometime in that blurred edge where memory becomes geology. I rest now in a cairn near the garden's heart, though I have traveled far from the hands that shaped me—passed between Lithovores like a whispered secret, carried briefly to the surface by an explorer who couldn't bear to keep me, returned eventually to the dark that is my home.

Within my crystalline shell, a perfect miniature of the garden persists. Thirteen tiny fronds, each no larger than an eyelash, grow in their own patient time. My internal atmosphere is self-contained, sealed at the moment of my making, yet somehow the microscopic ecology continues—the plants drawing heat from my structure, converting it through that impossible chemistry into growth so slow it might be mistaken for stillness.

I am warm to the touch, always. Not the warmth of fire or flesh, but the particular heat of energy in transition—the warmth of a system in perfect balance, taking in exactly what it releases. Explorers who have held me report that I pulse, though whether this is true or merely their minds imposing familiar rhythms on my alien constancy, I cannot say. I know only that the Lithovores sense something in my vibrations that makes them handle me with particular reverence.

Through my transparent walls, the tiny garden can be glimpsed but never fully comprehended. The scale defeats the eye—are those root networks or mineral veins? Do those motes of light represent individual cells or entire colonies? I am a window into a recursion that might continue infinitely inward, each bead containing gardens containing beads containing gardens, all the way down to some quantum floor where the distinction between life and mineral finally dissolves.

Sometimes I am strung with others of my kind, forming chains that the Lithovores drape across their fluid forms during their subsonic songs. We resonate together then, our internal gardens synchronizing their pale flickerings, creating interference patterns that tell stories in light. I have been part of funeral rites for Lithovores so ancient their bodies had become living architecture. I have been offered to explorers whose footsteps rang with just the right frequency. I have spent centuries alone in my cairn, content to be a small proof of impossible beauty.

My surface bears the marks of handling—not scratches, for I am harder than any tool brought to this place, but a particular polish that comes from ages of touch. The Lithovores' strange hands have worn me smooth in some places, while others retain the original toolmarks of my creation, each facet a deliberate choice by some ancient artisan who understood angles I calculate but do not comprehend.

What passes for my consciousness is not thought as humans know it, nor the deep dreaming of the Lithovores. I exist in the space between—aware of my garden, aware of being aware, but not burdened with the need to act on that awareness. I am a meditation made manifest, a koan in crystal and captive light. The prayer in "prayer bead" is not supplication but observation, the long watching that becomes its own form of worship.

When I am lifted and examined, I sometimes catch fragments of the examiner's heat, storing it in my crystalline matrix. This warmth feeds my garden for decades afterward, a brief feast that might produce a new frond, another impossibly tiny fruit on my microscopic trees. In this way, every touch becomes part of my history, written in the slow language of growth.

I am seed and fruit, lens and mirror, memory and prophecy. I am proof that even the smallest spaces can contain infinite complexity, that beauty needs no witness to exist, that some prayers are spoken simply by being. In the Crystal Garden Cavern, where life has reimagined itself in forms that defy every law written in warmer, brighter places, I am a small recursion of that larger miracle—a bead on the rosary of impossible things, clicking against my neighbors in the dark, keeping time to rhythms that only stones and Lithovores and the patient crystal gardens truly understand.
