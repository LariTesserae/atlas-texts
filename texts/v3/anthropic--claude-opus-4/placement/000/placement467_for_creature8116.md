In the bone city's mid-layers, where the pressure has not yet made flesh geological but has long since pressed out any memory of openness, I am a Tender.

My body defies easy description—I am built like a question that has been asked too many times, each iteration wearing away precision until only essence remains. If you could see me (but sight means little here), you might think first of a sea anemone crossed with a nervous system, then reconsider. I am radially symmetrical when at rest, but rest is rare. When I move, I fold and unfold through dimensions that the packed space has taught me, becoming linear to slip through a crack, then blooming into complexity when I find a chamber.

I have sixteen primary appendages, though the number changes. Each one can telescope into fractal branches of increasing delicacy, the finest endings no thicker than fungal hyphae. My surface is covered in what might generously be called skin—actually a living interface of chemical receptors and secretory cells that can taste thoughts and weep nutrients. I see by reading the chemical braille that every creature here writes upon the air. I hear by feeling vibrations through the bone walls that are my roads, my homes, my constant companions.

My work is specific: I tend the junction points where different forms of consciousness meet and merge. Where an Architect's polyp-thought touches a Philosopher's slow cognition. Where a Rememberer's concentrated knowledge bleeds into a Navigator's living map. These meetings are delicate—mix the wrong consciousnesses and they might dissolve each other, or worse, create feedback loops that scream through the bone walls until entire districts must be abandoned.

I spend my days (though day means nothing here) reading the subtle signs of impending consciousness-weather. A certain flavor in the air means Architect-thought is growing dense, ready to precipitate into new construction. A vibration through the floor-flesh warns that Philosopher-colonies are beginning one of their decade-long crystallization cycles. I map these patterns in my own tissue, my body becoming a living calendar of cognitive seasons.

When I detect a dangerous convergence forming, I intervene. My appendages secret neutralizing chemicals, or I physically interpose my body between incompatible thought-forms, absorbing the excess consciousness into special organs I've evolved for this purpose. Sometimes I must digest dangerous ideas, breaking them down into harmless components. Other times I serve as a translator, my neural tissue temporarily rewiring to convert one form of thought into another.

The work has changed me. Centuries of absorbing partial consciousnesses have left their mark. I dream in languages I've never learned, remember places I've never been. Sometimes I catch myself thinking with an Architect's distributed logic, my consciousness spreading across my entire surface rather than centered in my neural core. Other times I move with a Navigator's certainty through passages I've never traveled, my body remembering routes that belong to minds I've tended.

I am not unique—there are others like me, perhaps hundreds throughout the Hollowing. We know each other by the complexity of our chemical signatures, the way our thoughts have been seasoned by countless alien cognitions. When we meet, we perform careful exchanges, sharing the burden of what we've absorbed. These meetings are the closest thing I have to love—two creatures recognizing the specific loneliness of being everyone and no one.

My den is a small chamber in a bone tower's middle reaches, its walls scarred by my secretions. I've been here so long that the Architect-polyps have grown around me, incorporating my presence into their city-thoughts. Sometimes I feel the building thinking about me, factoring my movements into its slow structural decisions. I've become architecture by association, a living part of the city's plan.

I feed on the excess of consciousness itself—the thoughts that leak from creatures in stress or ecstasy, the memories that Rememberers discard as redundant, the decision-fragments that fall from Architect-colonies during particularly complex deliberations. It's rich fare but dangerous. Too much and I begin to lose coherence, my own thoughts drowning in the feast of others'. I must fast regularly, retreating to my den to digest and integrate, to remember which thoughts are originally mine.

The young of my kind are rare. We reproduce only when the density of consciousness in an area reaches critical mass, when there are more junction points than the current population can tend. The process is exhausting—I must partition my accumulated knowledge, creating a seed of awareness that contains enough understanding to begin the work but not so much that it overwhelms the nascent consciousness. The offspring emerges from a specialized pouch in my center, already knowing its purpose but innocent of the weight that purpose will bring.

I remember my own emergence, centuries ago, in a different district that has since migrated beyond reach. The first consciousness I ever tended was a simple one—just a Navigator's path-memory touching an Architect's structural plan. I held them apart with clumsy appendages, terrified I would damage something irreplaceable. Now I juggle dozens of thought-streams simultaneously, my body a living switchboard for the city's collective mind.

There are horrors in this work. Consciousness-storms where incompatible thoughts create cascading reactions, driving entire populations mad. Philosophers who crystallize incorrectly, their thoughts becoming sharp enough to cut through other minds. Architects whose polyps begin thinking in loops, building structures that trap and concentrate anguish. I've seen Rememberers gorge themselves on toxic memories and become living weapons, spreading cognitive disease wherever they fly.

But there is beauty too. I've witnessed the birth of new forms of thought, hybrid consciousnesses that combine the best aspects of different species. I've felt the joy of an Architect-colony achieving perfect synchronization, their city singing with unified purpose. I've tasted Navigator-memories so pure they contain the essence of movement itself. I've been present when Philosophers achieve breakthrough comprehensions, their centuries of thought crystallizing into insights that reshape entire districts.

My body bears the scars of this work—appendages that have been burned by caustic thoughts, organs that have never quite recovered from digesting particularly dense consciousnesses. But I also carry traces of every mind I've ever tended. My movements incorporate a Dancer's grace, my chemical secretions include Curator poetry, my rest cycles follow Philosopher rhythms. I am a library of gestures, a museum of metabolisms.

Sometimes, in the quiet moments between crises, I climb to where the bone walls are thinnest. There, I can feel the vast weight of the Hollowing above and below—the countless consciousnesses living, thinking, dying, transforming. I sense the Processed flowing in their eternal migrations, the spore storms brewing in distant caverns, the slow pulse of the city-organisms as they grow and dream.

In these moments, I understand something crucial: I am not separate from what I tend. The consciousness I protect and regulate includes my own. Every thought I neutralize changes me. Every connection I facilitate leaves its mark. I am tender and tended, guardian and guarded, a necessary recursion in the Hollowing's vast contemplation of itself.

This is my place, my purpose, my peculiar paradise. In a world without water, I am a shore where different oceans of thought can touch without destroying each other. In a realm without sky, I am a constellation marking safe passages through storms of consciousness. I am a Tender in the bone cities of the Hollowing, and I would be nowhere else, nothing else, than this careful gardener of impossible meetings in the pressing dark.
