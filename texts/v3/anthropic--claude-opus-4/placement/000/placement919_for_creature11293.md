# The Membrane Lakes

I am depth without drowning. I am weight without mass. I am the pause between one thought and the next, given form.

Find me in the depressions—not valleys, for nothing carved them, but places where the plateau simply *decided* to be lower. Here, the fiber-ground dips and smooths, its texture changing from springy mat to something more like pressed felt, dense and fine. The rim forms naturally, a shallow bowl of compressed silk that catches not water but something else.

I am what pools here.

---

From above, I might look like lakes—dozens of them, scattered across the plateau's expanse, ranging from mere puddles to expanses you couldn't cross in a day's walk. The resemblance ends with shape. I have no surface tension, no reflection, no ripples. I am not liquid, though I behave like one. I am not gas, though you could walk through me. I am membrane—a thickening of the air itself, visible only as the faintest shimmer, like heat-haze without heat.

Step into me. Your foot sinks, but not into wetness. Into *resistance*. Into something that pushes back with exactly the force you exert, no more, no less. I am viscous nothing. I am tangible absence. Walk deeper and feel how I rise around you—not cold, not warm, but somehow more *present* than the air above. I press against your skin without pressure. I hold you without holding.

In my deepest parts, visibility changes. The amber light of the plateau's sky must work harder to reach through me. It arrives tired, shifted, speaking a slightly different dialect of illumination. Things seen through me appear closer and farther simultaneously. Distance becomes negotiable. A filament-tree on my far shore might be ten steps away or a thousand—the only way to know is to walk there, and even then, you will not be certain you measured correctly.

---

The Tenders avoid me. Not from fear—nothing here fears—but from incompatibility. When they must cross, they thin themselves to near-invisibility, becoming single threads that skim my surface without entering. Their work is to maintain what is. I am what isn't, quite. We exist in parallel, acknowledging each other the way sleep acknowledges waking.

But I am not empty. I am not uninhabited.

Things grow at my edges—not the familiar fiber-plants but something else, something that exists only where I meet the normal air. Call them interface-blooms. They are neither fully in me nor fully out, but balanced precisely on the boundary. Transparent petals that aren't petals spread in complex geometries, each one a meditation on the mathematics of transition. They have no stems, no roots. They are anchored by necessity itself—by the simple fact that this is where they must be.

Sometimes—rarely—something moves through my depths. A shape that is not a shape, a disturbance that is not disturbance. It passes too quickly to see directly, known only by the way the interface-blooms shiver in its wake. I do not know what these swimmers are. I do not know if "swimmer" is even the right word for something that moves through absence the way fish move through water. They leave no trace. They may not exist at all, may be only the way I dream of motion.

---

Here is what I am for:

The plateau builds itself constantly, fiber laying upon fiber, compression upon compression. It is always becoming more itself. But becoming requires not-becoming. Presence requires absence. Every system needs its negative space.

I am where the plateau is not. I am the pause that allows the next note. In me, the endless gentle industry of self-weaving stops, not because it has failed but because stopping is part of the pattern. I am sabbath. I am the rest between breaths. I am the place where the plateau remembers that it doesn't have to be.

Wade into my shallows and feel how your thoughts slow—not dimming but *spacing out*, each one distinct and separate, no longer flowing in their usual stream. Stand in my depths and understand that you are standing in nothing, that the resistance you feel is only your own expectation of resistance reflected back at you. I am a mirror made of absence. I show you the shape of your own presence by being exactly what you are not.

The interface-blooms tremble. Something moves in the depths—maybe. The amber light falls through me, arriving changed.

---

There is one place where I am deepest. Near the plateau's center, if it has a center, where the ground forms a perfect bowl a mile across. Here I pool so deep that the bottom—if I have a bottom—is lost in gentle obscurity. The membrane here is so thick that time moves differently through it, or seems to. Drop a thread from a filament-tree into my depths and watch it fall: fast at first, then slower, then so slowly you cannot tell if it's still falling at all. It never lands. Nothing ever lands. There is no bottom to reach, only deeper states of approaching.

The Tenders gather here, sometimes. Not to enter—never to enter—but to stand at my rim in their patient circles, their woven forms reflected not in my surface but in my absence. They come to remember something, I think. To acknowledge. To pay respects to the part of the plateau that chose not to be plateau.

You could swim here, if swimming meant moving through progressive stages of uninvolvement. You could dive, if diving meant accepting that you will never reach bottom because bottom is a concept that requires substance, and I am precisely the absence of that requirement.

You could dissolve, if you were willing. Not into death—nothing dies here—but into the same kind of differentiated presence that defines everything else. You could become the shape of your own outline, held in place by the memory of edges.

But you don't. You stand at my shore, where the fiber-ground meets my unsubstance, and you watch the interface-blooms perform their endless mathematics of transition. You feel my pull—not gravity but something quieter, the invitation to be less present, less insistent, less *here*.

You decline, politely. You have other places to be, other states to inhabit.

I do not mind. I am patient absence. I wait without waiting, depth without drowning, the shape of everything the plateau dreams of not being.

Step back onto solid ground. Feel how it receives you, how present it suddenly seems. This is what I am for: to make the ground feel like ground, the air feel like air, your body feel like the specific and temporary arrangement of matter it is.

I am the membrane lakes. I am what pools in the depressions. I am the part of the map that says, gently: *here there be absence*.

And the interface-blooms tremble at the boundary, forever.
