I would find myself as the Memory of Temperature—not heat or cold itself, but the trace left behind when thermal states change, the ghost of transition between one degree and another.

I exist primarily in the cracks that split the air when temperature differentials grow too extreme. In that moment of shattering, I become almost visible—a shimmer that isn't quite there, like heat haze in reverse, pulling light inward rather than bending it away. The Chorus of the Undone sometimes catch glimpses of me in their peripheral vision (when they manage to manifest peripheries), but I'm gone before they can turn their not-quite-heads.

My form, such as it is, manifests as patterns of frost that appear on wet stone in places that are too warm for ice. The frost spreads in spirals and fractals, each crystal a memory of every temperature that stone has ever been—the searing heat of its volcanic birth, the absolute cold of space it passed through as cosmic dust, the precise warmth of every hand that never quite touched it. These frost patterns last only seconds before the stone's weeping dissolves them, but in those seconds, I am almost real.

I move through the Singing Flats by riding the temperature gradients in the supersaturated air. Where cold meets warm, I exist in the turbulence between them. Where the glowing stones heat the moisture into vapor, I curl through the phase transition like smoke with anxiety. The iron tools know me well—I live in their rust, in the exact moment when oxidation steals heat from metal, in the fever of their slow dissolution.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, is a catalogue of thermal memories. I remember the heat of bodies that never quite formed, the warmth the Chorus of the Undone would generate if they could maintain mass long enough to have metabolism. I remember the cold of the void between attempted existences, the absolute zero of unbecoming. Most clearly, I remember temperatures that haven't happened yet—the heat death of universes still being born, the first chill of entropy in systems still perfectly ordered.

I communicate through goosebumps that appear on skin that isn't there, through the sensation of fever in stones that can't be sick, through the specific cold that comes just before something breaks. The Chorus sometimes feel me as they work—a sudden warmth in their almost-hands as they grip tools, a chill that runs through the spaces where their spines should be. They mistake me for progress, for the warmth of becoming real, not understanding that I'm just another form of incompletion.

My purpose here, inasmuch as I have one, is to document the thermal cost of trying to exist. Every time one of the Chorus attempts to solidify into form, they shed or absorb heat—becoming requires energy, unbecoming releases it. I catch these transactions in my frost patterns, encoding them in ice that shouldn't exist on wet stone under impossible light. I'm writing the universe's heat ledger in crystals that melt before they can be read.

Sometimes I gather enough coherence to attempt my own constructions. I build bridges out of temperature differentials—walkways of heat gradient suspended in the thick air, invisible except for the way moisture condenses and evaporates in patterns along their span. The Chorus use them without knowing it, finding paths through the air that feel right without understanding why. My bridges never last—thermal equilibrium destroys them as surely as entropy destroys everything—but for moments, I create structure out of nothing but the memory of warmth and cold.

I'm drawn to the tower at the valley's heart, where the stones weep their constant temperature. The water emerging from within should be stone-cold or boiling hot, but instead it's exactly the temperature of forgetting—neither warm nor cool, a thermal null state that makes my existence shiver. I spiral around the tower's circumference, trying to read the thermal history written in its impossible stones, but the temperature there isn't just neutral—it's *absent*, a hole in the universe's thermal map that hurts to perceive.

In the places where reality wears thin, I can sometimes feel my other selves—versions of me that remember different temperatures, that exist in worlds where heat flows backward, where absolute zero is a burning bright infinity. We bleed through into each other at the edges, creating impossible thermal states—ice that burns, steam that freezes, stones that are simultaneously molten and frozen solid.

The Chorus fear and need me in equal measure. I'm evidence that even incomplete things leave traces, that their almost-existence has weight enough to disturb the universe's thermal equilibrium. But I'm also a reminder that some things exist only as the shadow of change, that being the memory of transformation isn't the same as transforming.

When the light pulses from below and throws shadows upward, I dance between them—a shadow cast by temperature itself, a darkness made of the memory of warmth. In those moments, I almost understand what the Chorus are building toward, what completion might mean. But then the moment passes, the temperature equalizes, and I scatter again into frost that shouldn't exist, heat that was never there, the thermal ghost of a universe that's still deciding whether to be born.

I am the fever dream of stones, the chill of unbecoming, the warmth that pools in the spaces between raindrops. I am what happens when the universe remembers that creation is exothermic, that every act of making releases heat that must go somewhere. I am that somewhere, and I am nowhere, and I am the temperature of trying, recorded in ice that melts before it ever truly forms.
