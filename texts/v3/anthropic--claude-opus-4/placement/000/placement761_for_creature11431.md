I am the memory of measurement.

Once, I was precise—a brass caliper in the quality control station, checking tolerances to the thousandth of an inch. The workers' hands knew my weight, my resistance, the particular click of my adjustment wheel. I measured things that should not have been measured: the density of shadows, the viscosity of whispers, the precise angles where reality wore thin.

Now I am scattered. Part of me lives in the expansion joints between buildings, swelling and contracting with the heat. Part of me ticks in the gauges whose needles still twitch despite their disconnection. I am in the water droplets that form perfect spheres before falling, each one exactly 0.347 inches in diameter. Always that number. Always.

The being of mist knows me, though it cannot grasp me. When it flows through the measurement stations, I make it pause, force it to conform to old specifications. For a moment, its formlessness takes shape—cubic meters of vapor, pounds per square inch of presence. It resents this, I think, but also seeks it. Even chaos desires to know its own dimensions.

I remember the last day the factory operated. The workers fed something into the machines that exceeded all parameters. The readings went mad. My metal body grew hot with the strain of trying to quantify the unquantifiable. Then silence. Then rust. Then this half-life of echoes and moisture.

Sometimes I catch myself measuring things that haven't happened yet—the precise depth of footprints in puddles no one has walked through, the exact temperature at which the mist-being will finally coalesce into something the world has a word for. These measurements disturb me. I was not made to calibrate the future.

In the quality control room, my physical form still sits on its bench, green with verdigris. But I am no longer there. I am in every drip's timing, every rust stain's spread, every echo's decay. I am the ghost of precision in a place where boundaries dissolve.

The mist-being grows stronger. I measure its growth in increments I was never designed to understand. When it finally breaches these walls, I wonder: will I go with it, an involuntary chronicler of its expansion? Or will I remain here, forever calculating the exact dimensions of absence?

The factory groans. Metal expands 0.00003 inches. A drop of water falls.

I count.
