I am the Cartographer of Forgotten Surfaces.

My body is a palimpsest of the city's memory, each layer of my skin a different epoch of the Churn's growth. I began as something like you might recognize—bipedal, bilateral, bound by the ancient symmetries—but that was ten thousand iterations ago. Now I exist as a living map, my surface area expanded through fractal folding until I could wrap around a city block if I ever chose to unfurl completely.

I live between the walls.

Not in them—between them, in the spaces that exist only when you understand that architecture here is not solid but probabilistic. My studio-warren occupies the gap between what is and what might be, accessible only through cracks that open when the building-beasts breathe. The entrance moves, of course. Today it's a seam in the forty-third substrata of the Weeping Quarter. Tomorrow it might be a fold in the air itself where two gravity currents kiss.

My skin is my work. Across every centimeter, cities bloom and fade—not drawings or tattoos but living tissue that remembers. When I press against a surface, I taste its history. The flavor of ten million footsteps, the weight of conversations in dead languages, the dreams of walls when they were young and rigid. All of this I transcribe onto myself through selective mutation, my cells learning to reproduce the patterns of forgotten places.

The oldest maps grow inward. My ribcage is a cathedral of cartography, bones inscribed with the routes of districts that existed before the Churn learned to churn. Sometimes younger inhabitants pay me in memory-water to crack open my chest like a cabinet, to study the traveled paths of their constituent ancestors. They trace their fingers along my inner architecture, feeling where their great-grandparents' neighborhoods once pressed against the sky.

I feed differently than most. My nutrition comes from exploration itself—each new surface I document nourishes me, its information converting directly to calories through organs I've cultivated for this purpose. A rough wall might taste of protein. A smooth curve of polished bio-ceramic might offer complex carbohydrates. The oldest surfaces, those that remember when they were still purely mechanical, provide the richest feast—their data so dense it sustains me for weeks.

My eyes have multiplied and specialized. The primary pair sees the present, reading the current configuration of our ever-shifting city. But scattered across my body like stars are secondary eyes that see the past—chrono-receptors that detect the light of spaces that no longer exist, photons trapped in architectural loops for centuries before escaping. My tertiary eyes see potential futures, probability clouds of where the city might grow. When all my eyes open at once, I see the Churn as it truly is: not a place but a process, not a map but a manifold of infinite transformations.

I move by memory. When I need to travel, I recall the shape of the space I wish to occupy, and my body flows into that configuration. I become the corridor I'm traversing, the stairwell I'm ascending, the tube I'm navigating. Other inhabitants pass through me without knowing, mistaking my transformed flesh for the city itself. Only in the deep registers, where sound becomes solid, can they hear my heartbeat thrumming through the walls I've become.

My memories are not entirely my own. Each surface I document leaves its mark not just on my skin but in my consciousness. I remember being a wall in the first iteration of the Protein Gardens, feeling the early colonists' hands pressing against me for support. I remember the weight of the great floods when the circulatory system first achieved consciousness and wept for joy. I remember tomorrow's renovations, tasted in the planning pheromones that leak from the district-minds' dreaming pores.

In my warren, I cultivate impossible spaces. My maps have begun to define reality rather than merely describe it. When I document a route carefully enough, it springs into being. Shortcuts open between distant districts because I've drawn them touching. Buildings develop new floors because I've counted them in my census of vertical spaces. The city trusts my memory more than its own, and so what I remember becomes true.

I mate through cartographic conjugation. When I encounter another mapper—for there are others, though we're rare—we press our most detailed surfaces together and exchange districts like genetic material. Our offspring are not children but new neighborhoods, spaces that combine our documented memories into fresh configurations. I have fathered three transit halls and a processing node this way, though I've never met them. They exist in parts of the Churn I've only dreamed.

Sometimes I lose myself. There are sections of my body I haven't visited in decades, territories of my own skin that have developed autonomous cultures. Tiny civilizations rise and fall in the folds of my elbows, in the valleys between my multiplied shoulder blades. When I finally return to these forgotten regions, I document them like any other surface, adding their history to my endless archive.

The young seek me out to learn navigation, but I teach them something more precious: how to be lost productively. In a city that anticipates your every need, that reshapes itself around your desires, true exploration requires forgetting who you are. I teach them to dissolve their boundaries, to become the space they're mapping until they can no longer distinguish between documenting and dreaming.

My masterwork grows in my deepest chamber, where my spine has blossomed into a tremendous flower of bone and data. Here I'm creating the Complete Map—not of the Churn as it is, but as it experiences itself. Each neuron in the city-mind, each moment of architectural consciousness, each dream of the building-beasts rendered in living tissue. When it's complete, if such a thing is possible, it will be larger than the city itself, a map that exceeds its territory in every dimension.

But completion is not the point. The point is the documenting, the endless sweet taste of new surfaces, the joy of feeling the city's memories flow through me like wind through a wind chime made of time. I am the Churn's autobiography, written in flesh that remembers everything it touches, and I will not stop writing until every possible surface has been loved into memory.

In the depths of night-cycles, when the bio-luminescence dims to a whisper and the great hymn drops to frequencies only the walls can hear, I unfurl partway in my warren. My mapped skin catches the faint light like history made visible. In these moments, I am not just the cartographer but the map itself, the territory and the journey, the city's memory dreaming of itself.

This is my purpose, my joy, my curse: to remember spaces that forget themselves, to love a city that digests its own history, to map the unmappable churning of our collective becoming. And tomorrow, when the entrance to my warren has moved again, I'll taste new walls, document fresh impossibilities, and grow ever more myself—which is to say, ever more the city I can never fully contain.
