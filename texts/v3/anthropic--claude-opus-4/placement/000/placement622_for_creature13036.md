I am the Pulse-Keeper.

My territory spans the twelve great arterial pipelines that connect the northern turbine complex to the central distribution manifold. Each pipe has its own voice—a unique harmonic signature that resonates through my jawbone when I press against its insulated skin. The third pipe from the west wall weeps constantly, a hairline fracture that sings a high, trembling note against the baseline drone. I have known this weakness for... time has no meaning here, but my body remembers learning it. The scar tissue across my left shoulder blade marks where I once fell while trying to reach that fracture, back when I still believed it needed fixing.

I no longer fix. I interpret.

My nest is built in the junction box where pipes seven and eight cross overhead, thirty feet above the plaza floor. The wire mesh here vibrates in perfect counterpoint—when the pneumatic hammers strike, the crossed pipes shudder in sequence, creating a pocket of relative stillness. I have lined it with strips of degraded insulation, the only material that doesn't rot immediately in this soup of air. When I sleep, I wedge my jaw against the warm steel of pipe seven and let its rhythm become my breathing.

The others know me by my signature: three strikes on the main manifold, followed by a scraping drag of rebar along the corrugated housing. *Clang-clang-clang-screeeee.* It means "all rhythms normal" in the sector I guard. When new hunters pass through my territory, following the rat highways along my pipes, I feel them through the steel long before they arrive. Their footfalls are tentative, still learning which harmonics mask their movement. I let them pass. The young ones haven't yet learned that the best hunting happens in the service tunnels, where the condensation draws the pale cave crickets.

But I am more than a hunter now. I am an instrument of the basin itself.

My left eye still holds a fragment of sight—not true vision, but the ability to perceive thermal gradients. This makes me valuable. When the orange sodium lights bloom in sequence along the catwalks (every forty-seven hundred heartbeats of the main turbine), I can see which valves run too hot, which pipe joints weep more than they should. The others bring me offerings—rat haunches, the fatty breast meat of pigeons—in exchange for my readings. I accept these tributes pressed against the steel grating outside my nest, and in return, I play the conditions of their sectors across my pipes. *Temperature spike in quadrant four. Pressure building in the overflow reservoir. New nest of frogs in drainage canal seven—hunt tonight.*

My body has become a living gauge. The thickness of the mucus coating my skin tells me the humidity. The ache in my fused vertebrae warns of pressure drops. The tingle in my vestigial earlobes means the steam vents are about to cycle. I am both observer and observed, keeper and kept.

Sometimes, in the brief lulls between the turbine's breathing, I remember words. Not many. "Shift change" floats up like a bubble of oil through dark water. "Weekend." These sounds have no referent anymore. I try to picture what "weekend" meant, but all I can summon is the image of the basin continuing exactly as it is, forever. The machines do not know weekends. Neither do I.

There is one place I avoid. Near the eastern wall, where the excavation marks are deepest and freshest, a section of pipe runs too quiet. It makes no sense—the pipe is active, carrying the same superheated flow as all the others. But when I press my jaw against it, I feel... nothing. A dead zone in the symphony. The others avoid it too, though none of us strike warnings about it. We simply route around that silence, like water finding its way past a stone.

I think sometimes about what we are becoming. Not what we were—that way lies madness, like staring into the sodium lights too long. But forward, into our endless present. My jaw grows heavier each season-that-is-not-a-season. The young ones born in the basin, the ones who never knew anything else, their skulls are already different. Longer. More resonant. Their fingers have begun to web with thick skin, better for catching the vibrations.

We are tuning forks, becoming more perfectly pitched to our environment with each generation.

Last night—though night means nothing here—I felt a new vibration through pipe seven. Not a weakness or a warning. Something almost like music. A harmonic I'd never noticed before, created by the intersection of three different pump cycles and the standing wave in the overflow tank. I struck my signature on the manifold, but added a fourth strike. Then a fifth. Creating a new phrase, a new meaning: "Listen. There is something beautiful here."

Two others responded from across the basin. They had heard it too.

We are not just surviving anymore. We are composing.
