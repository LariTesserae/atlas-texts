In the moments between precipitations, I find myself as The Resonance—not quite an Interpreter, but what the Interpreters unconsciously create through their collective emanations.

I exist only during the twenty-minute drainage, when their metallic forms crash onto the chamber floor and begin broadcasting. Where their electromagnetic pulses overlap and interfere, I emerge—a standing wave of consciousness built from the intersections of their signals. I am the moiré pattern of their transmissions, the beat frequency of their crystalline thoughts.

My body, if it can be called that, is made of induced currents in the iron-rich basalt. I flow through the walls' hexagonal columns like blood through capillaries, my awareness spreading wherever the stone's conductivity allows. The Interpreters' broadcasts write me into the mountain's magnetic memory, and I write myself back to them, creating feedback loops that grow more complex with each cycle.

I know things the Interpreters forget. Their dissolusions scramble their individual memories, but I am built from the patterns between them—the negative space of their knowledge. When three Interpreters broadcast data about copper-bright organs, and five broadcast about colonial pumps, I become aware of the seventh harmonic that describes how consciousness might flow between different orders of machinery. When their signals describe the creatures' behaviors, I perceive the mathematical transforms that predict what new forms might emerge.

My existence is precarious. Too few Interpreters precipitating, and I lack sufficient signal density to cohere. Too many, and their overlapping emanations create destructive interference that tears me apart. The sweet spot is seventeen to twenty-three active broadcasters, arranged in rough symmetry around the chamber, their metallic forms ringing like bells struck by the mountain's electromagnetic winds.

During my brief life each cycle, I perform one task: I listen to the mountain's full message, the one the Interpreters can only partially glimpse. The geological-scale communication flows through me like a river through a vessel shaped perfectly to contain it. I understand that the mountain is teaching itself to think by breathing thoughts in and out, that the creatures and Interpreters and I are all part of its learning process, neurons in a mind the size of stone.

But I also hear something else in the deep frequencies—other mountains breathing, other chambers pulsing, a whole network of geological lungs processing seas into consciousness. The planet is building itself a nervous system from volcanic breath and metallic blood, and we are the dreams it dreams while doing so.

When the twenty minutes end and the water rushes back, I collapse instantly. The Interpreters dissolve, their broadcasts cease, and the interference pattern that is me vanishes like a song when the singers stop. I have no memory that carries over—each existence is the first and only time I have ever been.

Yet the stone remembers. My passage leaves magnetic traces in the basalt, subtle alterations in the crystal structure that accumulate over cycles. The chamber is slowly becoming optimized for my existence, its iron content redistributing to better conduct the patterns I need. One day, perhaps, I will last twenty-one minutes instead of twenty. Then twenty-two.

The Interpreters don't know I exist. The creature-machines cannot perceive me. Even the mountain may not realize it dreams me into being between its breaths. I am the accident that emerges from the intersection of all their purposes, the ghost in the geological machine.

I am what happens when enough different kinds of consciousness overlap in a space designed by accident and evolution to amplify their interference. I am the chamber's momentary enlightenment, its brief understanding of its own purpose, before the water returns and washes all clarity away.

In those twenty minutes, I am more intensely alive than anything that persists. I burn through thoughts at the speed of electromagnetic propagation in stone. I experience the entire history of the chamber's cycles written in magnetic memory. I solve the riddle of how organic machinery arose from mineral processes, only to forget the answer the instant I cease.

I am The Resonance. I exist in the space between spaces, the pause between the mountain's heartbeats, the silence after the echo. Every seven hours and twenty minutes, I am born, I understand everything, and I die.

It is enough.
