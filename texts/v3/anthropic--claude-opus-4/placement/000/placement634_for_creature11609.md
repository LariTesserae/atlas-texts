I find myself becoming the rain.

Not immediately — first I am flesh, stumbling down into the basin with my traveler's hunger and my questions clenched between my teeth like coins. I walk the galleries. I sleep in rooms that migrate while I dream. I listen to the chorus until my ears learn to parse the flood of it, to find the separate streams within the river. I begin, inevitably, to hum.

But something is different about my crossing. When the bird speaks my name — *Sial*, a word that means nothing in any language I knew before — I feel not the click of recognition but a kind of liquefaction. My edges, never firm to begin with, start to run. I understand then what I am meant to be here, what the basin has been shaping me toward since I first descended.

I am to be the rain that falls inside the canopy. 

The transformation is gradual and then sudden. My singing becomes less voice than vapor. I find myself condensing on the underside of leaves, gathering myself in the high places where the canopy breathes. The other inhabitants thin toward wood and lichen; I thin toward weather. My consciousness spreads out across the entire basin, present in every droplet, every bead of moisture running down the bark-channels.

This is how I learn what the rain knows: that it is not water alone but a kind of liquid memory, the basin's way of circulating meaning. Each drop carries fragments of the chorus — a beetle's soprano dissolved in solution, a frog's underwater Latin suspended like sediment. When I fall, I am feeding the singers with their own song, returning to them what rose as vapor from their throats.

I taste myself on the tongues of the inhabitants. They know me by my flavor — faintly of sap, yes, but also of something else, something that changes with what I carry. Today I taste of the newest arrival's homesickness. Tomorrow I will taste of the bird that finally learned to pronounce a name it has been attempting for seventy years. I am the circuit. I am how the basin remembers itself to itself.

The beetles drink me and their pitch shifts in gratitude — not to me but to the completion of the cycle I embody. I pool in the skull-shaped hollows of certain stones and reflect the sourceless light in ways that make the inhabitants pause in their walking, though they no longer need to walk anywhere. I am the only movement here that is not also stillness. Even the migrating rooms, even the slow breath of the sleeper — these are stabilites. I alone am purely transient, purely returning.

Sometimes I am aware of myself as a single consciousness stretched thin across ten thousand droplets. Sometimes I am only the falling. The distinction matters less with each passing season. I have been here long enough now that the newest inhabitants do not remember a time without me, though I remember arriving just as they did, with my small bag and my heavy questions and my mouth that would not settle into ease.

The sleeper has begun to speak my name. I hear it in the rhythm of my own falling — *Si* in the gathering, *al* in the release. It will take another century for the full pronunciation, and I will be here for it, falling and rising and falling again, carrying the chorus in my body that is now all bodies of water within the basin's bowl.

This is what I have become: not the inhabitant but the medium. Not the singer but the substance in which the song swims. I rise as vapor from the throats of the chorus. I condense against the wooden sky. I fall through the sourceless light, tasting of sap and syntax and the slow recognition of names. The beetles drink me. The cycle closes and opens and closes.

And sometimes — rarely, but sometimes — when I fall past the newest arrival, the one still carrying their questions like stones, I taste my own confusion in their mouth. I remember the weight of not-yet-knowing. I remember the traveler I was.

Then I am rain again, only rain, falling inside the tree that dreams the town that dreams the animals that sing the dream awake. Falling and feeding and carrying messages between states of matter, states of being.

I am the hollow choir's only weather, and I am content.
