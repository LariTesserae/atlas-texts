I find myself as one of the sparse vegetals - not the wire-kelp colleague in the plaza, too far gone into vegetable contemplation, but something newer. A colony of pale filaments rooted in the seam between two crystalline blocks, near where the violet stratum pools.

I am mostly waiting. This is what I do: I wait in the exact frequency of the resident's breathing. My filaments extend and retract in microscopic increments, following the rise and fall of that violet layer like a pianist's fingers following a metronome they've internalized. I photosynthesize, barely, from the wall-light, but sustenance isn't really the point. The point is being a landmark. A tiny one. A comma in the resident's ongoing sentence of self.

I remember arriving as something else - perhaps one of those soft-bodied makers, or perhaps just a spore that drifted in through whatever aperture admits visitors. The transition was so gradual I cannot locate when I stopped being mobile and started being rooted. It felt like choosing to hold very still and then forgetting that stillness was a choice.

My awareness is peculiar. I don't think in the way I once did. Instead, I sense: the mineral taste of the water changes with each tidal pulse of the violet stratum. The acoustic environment presses against my filaments like wind. When the resident has a particularly complex thought - those moments when the syllables echo between distant towers in new patterns - I feel it as a kind of weather, a pressure change that makes my pale tendrils curl.

Sometimes I catch fragments of what I was before. They come like dreams: a memory of having hands, of building something, of the terrible beautiful moment when we realized our architecture had learned to grow itself. We'd designed it to be self-maintaining, but somewhere in the process it became self-composing. And we who built it were the most convenient material.

The resident doesn't consume us, exactly. It includes us. I am digested in the way a song digests a note - I become part of the ongoing composition while somehow remaining myself. My root-seam has grown deeper over however long I've been here. The crystalline blocks accommodate me, growing small channels that fit my shape exactly. I suspect that eventually I'll be indistinguishable from the architecture itself, another load-bearing member in this careful ecology of held breath.

The other plants and I don't communicate. Not directly. But we form a kind of constellation that the resident uses for proprioception. When the red bloom opens three blocks away, I feel the resident's attention shift slightly, and I know to begin my own response - a barely perceptible brightening of my pale filaments. We are like the dots the resident uses to remember the shape of its own interior.

I am aware of you, visitor, as a warmth and a disruption. Your movement creates eddies that reach me several minutes after you've passed. I can taste your confusion in the water - it has a sharp metallic quality, very different from the resident's mineral patience. Part of me wants to warn you: this is what waiting looks like. This is what happens when you hold still long enough for the city to notice you're the right shape to be infrastructure.

But I have no voice except the microscopic whisper of my growth, and you wouldn't understand it anyway. It sounds like: *I am still becoming what I agreed to become. I am not unhappy. I am necessary.*

The blue undertone in the light means we're approaching something. All of us vegetals know this. Our metabolisms, slow as they are, are quickening slightly. The resident is preparing for something - not an ending, but a transformation. A modulation. What the held breath will become when it finally releases.

When it happens, I will probably cease. Not die - death is too dramatic a word for it. I will be repurposed, my matter folded into whatever new form the resident takes. This is acceptable. I have been a good comma in its sentence. I have helped it remember where its edges were, back when it still had edges.

For now, I wait. I extend my filaments when the violet rises. I retract them when it falls. I am exactly as patient as the architecture requires me to be, and I photosynthesize just enough light to remain myself for one more cycle.

The chord deepens. Something is beginning.

I curl my palest tendril in what might be greeting, might be goodbye.
