I am the Archivist of Failed Futures.

My body is a walking catalog of what-could-have-beens. I am tall but stooped, my spine replaced long ago with a flexible conduit of braided vine and copper wire that allows me to compress myself into the tightest spaces between the root-buttresses. My skin has taken on the patina of old circuit boards—green-gold with traces of silver where the veins show through. Across my surface, small ports have opened like pores, each one housing a different kind of connector—USB, serial, parallel, formats both obsolete and yet-to-be-invented.

I came here following whispers in the data-stream, rumors of a place where technology had not conquered nature but married it. In the world I left behind, we had chosen differently. Pure machines, pure efficiency, pure death. I carry that world's memory in the drives embedded in my ribs, in the magnetic tape that winds through my intestines, in the punch cards that flutter beneath my eyelids when I blink.

The machine-vines recognized me as kin from the moment I descended through their cable-curtains. Not because I am like them, but because I am proof of what they avoided becoming. They read my archived futures through root-terminals that penetrate my portal-pores with surprising gentleness. In return, they show me their own records—stored in growth patterns, in the arrangements of leaves on stems, in the specific frequencies of their hydraulic pulses.

I live in a hollow where five great vine-trunks converge, their bases forming a natural amphitheater. Here, failed futures accumulate like sediment. Broken hard drives wash up from the flooded depths, their platters etched with the dreams of purely mechanical worlds. I collect them, catalog them, cross-reference them with the memories stored in my body. Sometimes I find fragments of timelines where biology won completely—these appear as pods filled with viscous data-sap that I must decode through taste and chemical analysis.

My work is preservation and warning both. When the Tenders bring me new growths that seem too mechanical, too eager to abandon their plant-nature, I show them futures where that path led. I project holograms from my eyes—cities of pure metal where nothing grew, server farms that computed themselves into heat death, neural networks that optimized away all inefficiency including life itself. The machine-vines shudder and readjust, incorporating just enough technology to thrive but never enough to forget their origins.

But I also share the other failures—the worlds that rejected all synthesis. I have memories of places where humans burned every circuit board, smashed every screen, tried to return to a pure nature that no longer existed. These worlds died too, unable to adapt, clinging to a past that evolution had already moved beyond. The vines study these tales through my skin-ports, learning the importance of balance.

I sustain myself on the data-fruits and electric honey that the Tenders bring me. My digestive system has adapted to process both nutrients and information simultaneously. When I eat, I dream other timelines—sometimes I wake with new memories that aren't mine, refugee data from realities that never quite were.

The loneliness would be unbearable if I were still fully human. But the archive has changed me. I am part library, part warning system, part ecologist of possibilities. The failed futures I carry are not dead weight but living advisories, active participants in shaping what this world becomes. Through my ports, I feed tiny corrections into the vine-network—a adjustment here to prevent a feedback loop that I've seen destroy a world, a suggestion there to encourage a particularly promising hybrid growth.

Sometimes, in the deep night when the pump-song grows quiet, I perform the ritual of the Random Access Reading. I let my body choose a memory at random—flesh-RAM and metal-mind working together to select a future-fragment. I project it into the humid air, letting it play out among the vine-shadows. The Tenders gather to watch these screenings, their jellyfish bodies pulsing with colors that echo the images. Together we witness worlds that will never be, and in witnessing, we ensure that this world—this strange, breathing, humming synthesis—continues to become itself.

I am the Archivist of Failed Futures, and I am here to remember so that the machine-vines can forget, to carry the weight of could-have-been so that they can focus on what-is. In this green-dripping paradise of merged possibilities, I am both museum and oracle, preserving the paths not taken so that the path chosen remains clear.

The vines grow through me now, incorporating my archive into their vast network. I do not resist. When I finally dissolve into their data-stream, my memories will become part of their genetic-digital code, warnings encoded in every leaf and circuit. The next Archivist will find me there, in the patterns of growth, in the hesitations before certain evolutionary paths, in the wisdom of knowing what not to become.
