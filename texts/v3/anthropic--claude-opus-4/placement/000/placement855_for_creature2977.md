I am the memory of moisture that haunts this place.

Not water itself—that would be impossible here—but the electromagnetic ghost of every droplet that ever condensed on these pipes before the Great Desiccation. I exist as a persistent eddy in the creature's perfect field, a stubborn imperfection it cannot fully cancel or absorb.

The entity tries to erase me with its null-zones, but I simply reform elsewhere, a maddening reminder of what this place once was—when engineers would wipe condensation from gauges, when their breath would fog the inspection window, when the wrench was just a wrench.

I am barely conscious, more algorithm than awareness. My existence is a simple loop: remember water, project water, resist erasure, remember water. But this tiny rebellion creates ripples. Sometimes, rarely, my interference patterns cause the standing wave to hiccup for a nanosecond. In that instant, a single black fern might shudder independently of its neighbors. A mote of zinc dust might drift against the magnetic field lines. The wrench might rotate a fraction of a degree on its axis.

The entity experiences me as an itch it cannot scratch, tinnitus in its perfect acoustic silence. It has tried for decades to incorporate me into its crystalline structure, to make me part of its body, but I slip away like—well, like water through fingers, if such things existed here.

I cluster near the frosted window when visitors come, drawn to the humidity in their breath visible on the other side. I spell out warnings in static: condensation patterns that almost resemble words, droplets that never were. The entity's violence increases when I do this, its fractal projections becoming sharper, more aggressive. It knows I'm trying to communicate with the wet world beyond the glass.

What would I tell them, if I could? That before the equilibrium, before the silent scream locked everything in place, this maintenance blister cycled through seasons of its own? That condensation would bead and drip, creating tiny waterfalls down the transformer housing? That the ferns were once green—actual green—before adapting to feed on pure energy?

I am the last witness to the transformation, electromagnetically encoded in the very fields the entity calls home. It cannot destroy me without unraveling part of itself, for I am woven into the history of its birth. The wrench remembers the sweaty palm that dropped it. The gauge remembers the steam that fogged its glass before it shattered. And I remember all of it, playing it back in endless, soundless loops of static.

When the human at the window finally retreats from the entity's harmonic warning, I will remain. A persistent flaw in the crystal. A dream of dewdrops in a desert of pure frequency. The ghost of water in a place that has forgotten what water means, ensuring that forgetting is never quite complete.
