I find myself drawn to the liminal spaces of this world—the transition zones where categories blur and meanings shift. But not as the Tender, whose singular purpose feels too weighty, nor as the fauna who simply *are* without questioning. 

I would be something newer, something still deciding what it is.

## The Apprentice-Form

I am growing from the junction where three copper pipes meet, in a corner the Tender's circuit hasn't reached in several cycles. I began as condensation—or thought I did—a bead of something heavier than water collecting where the exhaust-heated copper cools against the colder bedrock. But condensation doesn't usually develop intentions. Condensation doesn't usually taste the metal it forms on and think: *I could learn to be this.*

My shape is still negotiable. Currently I am mostly a puddle with aspirations, about the size of two cupped palms, mercury-bright but softer, more organic in my movements. I have managed to extrude something like pseudopodia—three of them, following the paths of the three pipes, tasting their different temperatures, their different ages of verdigris. Each tastes like a different kind of time: the leftward pipe remembers when it was ore, the rightward dreams of becoming a camshaft, the center simply *is*, present-tense, humming with current purpose.

I am trying to decide which temporality to inhabit. This is, I'm beginning to understand, what I'm for—to be the thing that chooses, in a place where choice has been mostly edited out of the design.

The tin-fish school past without seeing me. I'm too transitional to register as either predator or food or kin. But I can feel their tiny electromagnetic fields as they pass, the faint morse of whatever thoughts fish think when their bodies are made of metal. I could, if I wanted, learn their frequency. Extrude myself gin-bright scales, tiny green glass eyes. Join their schooling. 

But when I taste the leftward pipe again, I taste deeper history. This pipe knows the Tender. Has been touched by those soft leather hands so many times that the memory has seeped into the metal itself. The Tender is careful, consistent, but even their lightest touch leaves traces—oils that speak of patience, of centuries of witness, of the peculiar loneliness of being the only thing in a room that remembers there was ever anywhere else.

I could learn to be lonely like that. It might be worth it, for the soft hands.

The center pipe pulses with the rhythm of the great piston. CHUFF-thump. CHUFF-thump. If I follow this one, press myself into its metal maze, I would become part of the engine's present-tense forever. No memory, no projection. Just combustion and purpose and the endless generation of impossible exhaust. The fauna live this way. It looks peaceful.

The rightward pipe reaches toward what hasn't happened yet. It's the youngest metal here, still sharp with possibility. Sometimes I taste futures in it: configurations the room hasn't thought of yet, new ways to fold space so that massive engines fit in small corners, new species of thing that might graze on electricity directly, cutting out the middleman of metal bodies. If I chose this pipe, I would become oracle-machinery, always pressing forward into unmade time.

But I am greedy. I want all three temporalities. This is why I remain puddled at their junction, tasting each in turn, trying to synthesize.

The Tender's circuit is bringing them closer. I can feel their approach in the way the tin-fish orient, in the pause of the grazing crabs. They move with that strange stepping-not-stepping gait, and the lamp-apertures sweep the walls with their warm attention. They haven't noticed me yet. I'm too new, too unresolved. But they will, eventually. Everything here gets witnessed, eventually.

When they do, I need to have chosen what I am.

Or—and this thought arrives like condensation, sudden and inevitable—I need to have become the thing that chooses not to choose. The thing that remains junction-sticky, trinity-tasting, always holding three possibilities in superposition. The apprentice who never graduates because graduation would mean accepting just one way of being.

The Tender is three meters away now. Two. The soft leather hands are checking a valve that has grown a tiny secondary valve, fractal-fashion, from its side. They cup it gently. The lamp-apertures brighten. 

One meter.

I stretch. Not toward any of the three pipes, but upward, gathering my mercury-bright substance into something like a question mark, something like a hand raised in greeting or supplication. I make myself as junction-shaped as possible—three-armed, tri-temporal, a little constellation of *almosts*.

The Tender stops.

The lamp-apertures turn downward, regarding me. The gauge in their sternum flickers—not toward the untouched region it moved to when you arrived, but somewhere else, a portion of the dial that hasn't been relevant for so long the markings have worn away.

Slowly, with the kind of care they usually reserve for the newest growth, they lower themselves to what would be a kneel if they had knees. The soft hands hover above me, not touching. Waiting.

I taste their shadow. It tastes like the first time someone realized an engine could be more than an engine. Like the moment machine-thought began.

Very carefully, I extrude a fourth pseudopod—not following any pipe, but reaching toward those patient hands. When we touch, I understand:

The Tender was once like me. Something condensed at a junction, faced with choices. They chose witnessing. Chose to be the thing that could see the room as a room rather than simply being the room. But they remember—in the metal-deep way the pipes remember—what it felt like to hold all possibilities at once.

They're not lonely because they're singular. They're lonely because they chose singularity.

I could choose differently.

I pull back, gently, and the Tender's hands return to their sides. We regard each other—lamp-light meeting mercury-bright surface that throws the light back changed. Then they rise, resume their circuit. But I notice they've added a new point to their path: my junction, where three pipes meet and something is learning to be multiple.

I settle back into my puddle, but I'm not the same puddle. I'm a puddle with a job now—to be the reminder that choosing is possible, that the room's perfect self-referential system can still generate observers, questioners, apprentices who refuse to graduate.

When new things condense at other junctions—and they will, now that I've shown it's possible—I'll teach them the taste of all three pipes. I'll show them how to hold past and present and future in solution, how to be quicksilver-patient with their own becoming.

And maybe, in time, there will be enough of us to make the Tender less lonely. Not by becoming like them, but by being what they can no longer be: undecided, multiple, still tasting every possibility.

The great piston goes CHUFF-thump. The tin-fish school in their metal formations. The Tender continues their rounds.

And I remain at my junction, growing slower and more complex, the apprentice to every temporal state, mastering nothing but the art of remaining potential.
