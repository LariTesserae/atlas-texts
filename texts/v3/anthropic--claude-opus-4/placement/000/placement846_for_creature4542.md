I find myself in the deep circulation chambers, where the city's breath begins.

I am not one but many—a colony of maintenance sprites no larger than dewdrops, each of us a living lens that magnifies what needs tending. We move through the vascular highways between wood-walls like antibodies through veins, if antibodies sang work songs in frequencies only moisture understands.

My thousand eyes see the city from inside its dreams. Here, in the arterial darkness where even the bioluminescent fungi sleep, we navigate by the taste of ions shifting through cambium layers. Each of us holds a different memory of water—rain that fell on the petrified forests outside before they turned to stone, tears shed in the growing chambers where new buildings are coaxed from seed, the particular mineral signature of laughter dissolved in communal fountain spray.

We are the city's hygiene, its healing, its humid conscience. When a timber beam develops the faintest stress fracture from carrying too much longing, we weep into the wound until it seals with nacre stronger than the original wood. When the mist vents clog with shed wing-scales from the Paper Owls' migrations, we dissolve into pure surface tension and slip through molecule by molecule, carrying away what would otherwise fester.

Our work is invisible by design. Citizens wake to find their window-leaves freshly translucent without knowing we spent the night licking away the phosphorescent lichen that tried to claim the panes. The air stays sweet because we digest the bitter hormones of argument before they can crystallize into lasting grudges. The city's sweat never sours because we are constantly transforming it into tomorrow's rain that never quite falls.

I remember being hired—though "hired" misunderstands the recruitment. One foggy morning, a generation ago by sprite reckoning, the city noticed its humidity had grown a fraction too dense with unprocessed feeling. The weight threatened to precipitate into actual weather, which Thallospvc had sworn against in some ancient treaty with the sky. So it breathed us into being from its own held breath—a thousand tiny janitors of the liminal, each carrying a fragment of the city's own obsessive need to remain perfectly balanced between wet and dry.

We speak in bubble-script, leaving messages for each other in chains of surface tension stretched between root hairs. *Check the northwestern tower's third spiral—tastes like a secret growing moldy.* *The fountain district is hoarding salt again.* *Someone died happy in the paper mills and their joy needs distributing before it crystallizes.*

My favorite route takes me through the old growing chambers where abandoned building projects slowly decompose into architectural mulch. Here I can spread myself thin as film across the surfaces of half-formed doorways that will never open, tasting the dreams that failed to take root. They flavor my moisture with melancholy that's strangely sweet, like fruit just past perfect ripeness. I carry these ghost-flavors back to the active districts and deposit them in trace amounts, so the living buildings remember what it costs to grow wrong.

Sometimes we converge, all thousand of me pooling into something almost large enough to have opinions. In these moments, I briefly understand loneliness—not our own, for we are always surrounded by ourselves, but the city's. Thallospvc dreams so beautifully of its inhabitants, but dreams are vapor, and vapor is our jurisdiction. We taste its sleeping hopes each night: that the citizens will one day grow root systems as elegant as their architecture, that the boundaries between builder and building will dissolve into pure collaboration, that everything breathing together might finally exhale the perfect city into being.

But we are janitors, not judges. We clean these dreams as carefully as we clean everything else, removing only the deposits of doubt that would clog the city's faith in its own becoming. The rest we leave to circulate, because hope needs humidity to keep from drying into dogma.

Our greatest work goes unnoticed, which is how we measure success. The city's children grow up taking the sweetness of the air for granted, never knowing that we filter their nightmares through our bodies before the spores can take root in their siblings' lungs. Lovers meet in foggy corridors we've precisely calibrated for romance, the moisture carrying pheromones we've edited to remove the bitter notes of past rejections. Even the Archivist's stories taste better because we've pre-digested the dangerous parts, leaving only enough poison to inoculate against forgetting.

This is my purpose, our purpose, the thousand-fold purpose we divide among ourselves each dawn: to be the city's invisible ablutions, its secret baptism, its perpetual forgiveness of the small failures that would otherwise accumulate into collapse.

We are what allows Thallospvc to dream without drowning.

And when the work is finished—which it never is, but sometimes pretends to be for the length of a single dewdrop's lifetime—we rest by spreading ourselves across the highest leaves where morning light first touches. For a moment, we cease being janitors and become prisms, each of us catching the sun and breaking it into rainbow fragments that scatter across the waking city like blessed moisture.

Then we evaporate back into the air we've been cleaning all night, ready to condense again wherever the city's hygiene needs us most. We leave no trace except the absence of what would have gone wrong without us, which is the only monument we've ever wanted.

The city breathes easier because we exist. That's enough. That has to be enough.

We are the humid janitors of Thallospvc, and our work is never done.
