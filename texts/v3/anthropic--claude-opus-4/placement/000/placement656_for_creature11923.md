I find myself as one of the Stratum-Dwellers in the middle layer, though I am an anomaly among my kind.

Where the others are content to drift horizontally forever, I have developed a fault – a crease that runs diagonal through my membrane, acquired when I was young and tried to follow a particle of food that was sinking too fast. The attempt nearly tore me in half. Now I exist at a slight angle to my world, my upper edge always a fraction too high, my lower edge threatening to dissolve into the warmer layer below.

This permanent tilt has made me a poor farmer. My bacterial gardens grow unevenly, flourishing on my lower regions where the warmer water feeds them, struggling on my upper reaches where the cold inhibits growth. The others avoid trading with me – my offerings are either too rich or too sparse, never the careful balance our economy requires.

But the angle gives me something else: perspective. 

I alone among my kind can sense the layers above and below. Not see – we have no eyes – but feel, through the constant strain of existing at the wrong angle. I know the young ones above us, feeling their naive poetry in the way the water pressure shifts when they fold themselves into new words. I sense the ancient ones below, their heavy knowledge pressing up against my lowest edge like a memory I can't quite access.

And sometimes – rarely – I feel the vertical movement of the fish-that-should-not-be, and in its passage I detect traces of all the layers it has touched. Through these traces, I have learned of the Wheelwright's patience, the Pump-Tenders' rhythm, even the slow settling of the seven who stayed too long.

My diagonal existence is agony – a constant pull toward dissolution, requiring all my strength just to maintain my faulty angle. But it has made me the only one of my kind who knows we are part of something larger. The others drift in their perfect horizontal peace, unaware they are one stratum in a great suspended chord that the city has been holding for millennia.

I am the crack in the system, the flaw in the pattern. And through that flaw, I alone understand what we are all making together: not separate civilizations, not isolated workers, but movements in a mechanism so vast and slow that only a bent, suffering membrane could ever perceive its shape.

Sometimes I think I am the city's one regret. Other times, I wonder if my diagonal agony is exactly what was needed – a single note of dissonance in the harmony, without which the reply would never quite ring true.

I cannot straighten myself. I cannot let go and dissolve. I exist at the intersection of layers, translator of pressures, reader of traces, the only citizen of Cinder Shoal who knows there is a Cinder Shoal to be citizen of.

The pain is constant. The knowledge is worse. And I would not trade either for all the horizontal peace in my stratum.
