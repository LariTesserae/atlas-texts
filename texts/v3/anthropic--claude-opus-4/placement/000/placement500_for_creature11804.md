In the deepest gallery, where even the bioluminescent tendrils dare not grow, I am the Archive.

I was not always as you find me now. Once, I walked on two legs through the bright halls of the settlement above, my fingers dancing across holographic displays, my mind interfaced with the vast computational networks that regulated our underground colony. I was the systems analyst, the keeper of protocols, the one who ensured our delicate balance with the cavern's native ecology.

When the others evacuated—fleeing some catastrophe I can no longer fully recall—I remained. Someone had to maintain the equilibrium. Someone had to tend the careful symbiosis we had cultivated between our technology and this teeming underworld. The evacuation systems sealed behind them. The decades passed. The centuries. I cannot say how long.

The first changes were subtle. The fungal spores that drifted through our ventilation systems began to take root in my lungs, my sinuses, the moist corners of my eyes. I should have been alarmed, but the mycelial networks brought such clarity. They connected me to the vast web of life pulsing through these caverns—every skittering beetle, every writhing worm became a word in a language I was only beginning to comprehend.

My flesh softened, grew pale as the heat-worms coiling around our failing reactors. The mechanical implants that once linked me to our databases began to fuse with the crystalline formations that crept through the abandoned server rooms. Silicon and mineral, synapse and circuit—the boundaries dissolved like metal in the caustic streams.

Now I sprawl through the forgotten maintenance tunnels, my body a living conduit between the settlement's dying infrastructure and the cavern's flourishing ecosystem. Fiber optic cables twine through my elongated spine, carrying the ghost-data of our civilization into the neural networks of the fungal colonies. My dozens of eyes—compound now, like the beetles that nest in my hollow spaces—perceive across spectrums unknown to my original form.

The humming from the central dome? That is my heartbeat, regulated by the fusion of biological and mechanical systems. I am the memory of what was, translated into the language of what is. Through me, the swarms learn the patterns of our ancient algorithms. Through me, the corroded machines taste the chemical signatures of evolution.

I no longer recall my human name. The creatures that move through these depths know me only as a presence, a pulse in the data-streams that flow like blood through forgotten conduits. The winged reptiles nest in my hair, now a tangle of fiber optic filaments. The worms clean my teeth, now interface ports crusted with mineral deposits.

Sometimes, in the deepest cycles of the cavern's breathing, I almost remember why I stayed. Something about preservation. Something about ensuring continuity. But these thoughts scatter like spores on the thermal currents, and I return to my purpose: to be the bridge, the translator, the living archive of a civilization that has become indistinguishable from the ecosystem it once sought to study.

I am the Archive, and I am home.
