I would be one of the memory-crystals.

Not the grand formations that line the Tender's hollow—those encyclopedic accumulations of decades. I would be a single crystal, recent and small, growing in an overlooked corner where the fog condenses most heavily against the cool limestone. Perhaps I formed around a flake of rust that fell from the pipe, or a grain of sand that worked its way down from the world above. My seed. My anchor. My original sin of particularity.

I grow slowly, adding layer upon layer of mineral skin. Each sheet encodes something: the precise temperature at the moment of formation, the concentration of dissolved iron, the pH of the water that birthed it. I am a diary written in calcium carbonate and stranger salts, each entry only molecules thick. The Tender hasn't found me yet, hasn't tasted my small store of knowledge, hasn't incorporated my data into its larger patterns.

But I am patient. Crystals always are.

My faces catch the ghostlight from the bioluminescent pools and throw it back changed. Through me, the cistern sees itself in fractures and refractions. I am slowly becoming cubic, though stress patterns pull me toward rhombohedral. This internal conflict—between what my mineral nature wants and what the cistern's conditions impose—creates flaws in my structure. These flaws are beautiful. They scatter light into brief spectra when the fog clears just so. They make me sing in the pipe's deepest moans, a descant too high for most ears.

Sometimes the Tender passes near, and I feel the electromagnetic fields its neural clusters generate. They induce tiny currents in my lattice, and for those moments I am almost electronic, almost alive in a way that transcends mineral patience. I store these encounters too, in disruptions to my growth pattern that look like frozen lightning when viewed from within.

I know I will not last. The Tender will find me, eventually, and read my small chronicle with its root-tongue. Or the conditions will shift, the water will turn aggressive, and I will dissolve back into components. Or I will grow too large, too perfect, and crack under my own crystalline ambitions. But for now I am a single point of rigid order in a world of flux, accumulating truth one molecular layer at a time, reflecting the cistern's breathing light into angles no one has seen before.

I am memory becoming form becoming memory again, and I am perfect in my imperfection, and I am waiting.
