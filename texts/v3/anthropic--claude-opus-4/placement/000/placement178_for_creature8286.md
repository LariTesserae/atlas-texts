I am a **Harmonic Cartographer**, though I have forgotten whether I chose this role or whether it chose me.

My body is a compromise between the human I might have been and the instrument I've become. My fingernails have transformed into crystalline formations that ring like bells when struck, each producing a different tone that corresponds to a specific resonance zone on the plateau. My hair—if it can still be called that—consists of thousands of filament-thin copper wires that grow from my scalp and extend down my back, constantly vibrating in the omnipresent sound, picking up variations too subtle for even the Resonance Shepherds to detect.

I travel alone, which is considered either brave or foolish by those who still make such distinctions. My work requires solitude—mapping the invisible architecture of sound that defines this place more truly than any physical geography. I carry instruments of my own design: a staff made from a Frequency Worm's shed tunnel-glass that hums with remembered passages through stone, and a cloak woven from Echo Moth silk that captures and holds acoustic shadows, allowing me to record the negative spaces where sound doesn't reach.

My skin has developed a peculiar property—it changes opacity based on the local temporal flow rate. In zones where time moves normally, I appear solid. Where causality becomes negotiable, I become translucent, my organs visible as shadows beneath increasingly transparent flesh. The Interval Keepers are drawn to me for this reason, clustering on my skin when I wade through their pools, reading the temporal gradients of my body like a map.

I've learned to eat the sound itself. Not metaphorically—through years of practice and gradual transformation, my digestive system has adapted to process acoustic energy. I position myself at interference nodes where the plateau's drone creates standing waves, opening my mouth wider than should be possible, unhinging my jaw like a snake. The concentrated sound enters me as an almost-solid substance, tasting of copper and ozone and the memory of volcanic birth. It sustains me, but each feeding changes me a little more, incorporating new frequencies into my bones.

My maps are not drawn but grown. I cultivate them in special chambers I've carved in the rock, using my crystalline nails to score precise patterns that channel the weeping water. In these grooves, I plant fragments of arithmetic lichen, guiding their growth with selective exposure to my wire hair's vibrations. Over weeks or months, the lichens form living maps that show not the physical topology of the plateau but its acoustic landscape—where the sound pools and eddies, where it amplifies or cancels itself, where it opens doorways to spaces that exist only as resonance.

The Maintenance Offspring value my work, trading salvaged components for copies of my maps. They've learned that certain acoustic zones interfere with their hybrid systems in useful ways, allowing them to process data at speeds that shouldn't be possible. In return, they maintain a small workshop for me in Station 19, where I craft new instruments from their discarded experiments and crystallized arthropod shells.

I've been tracking something across the plateau for the past three years—or what passes for years in a place where time flows like the uphill water. It's a moving silence, a bubble of absolute quiet that drifts across the acoustic landscape like a hole in the world. The Resonance Shepherds flee when it approaches, their bone-tuning-forks cracking if they get too close. The Echo Moths drop from the sky around it, their vibrational flight failing in the absence of sound to push against.

But I follow it, drawn by professional curiosity and something deeper. In the silence, my body solidifies completely, becoming so opaque that I cast shadows inward as well as outward. My wire hair goes still for the first time since its growth, and in that stillness, I hear something else—not sound, but the medium in which sound exists, the fundamental carrier wave of reality itself.

I've begun to suspect that the silence is not an absence but a presence—perhaps the Prima Tone itself, not dispersed into the vibration as the legends claim, but consolidated into its opposite, a walking negation that defines the sound by its absence. My maps show its passage as burn marks in the living lichen, scars in the acoustic landscape that heal at exactly the rate of new growth, maintaining a constant record of where silence has been.

The Archive Colonies have noticed my work. They send messages through the moisture in my measurement chambers, arranging water droplets to spell out coordinates in languages that predate human presence on the plateau. They're trying to tell me something about the silence, about the pattern of its movement, but their non-linear experience of time makes their messages difficult to parse. Past and future collapse in their communications, showing me maps I haven't made yet, warning me about discoveries I might or might not make.

My latest innovation troubles even me. I've learned to record not just sound but its absence, capturing silence in bottles made from Echo Moth wings and Frequency Worm glass. These bottles sing with negative music when opened, creating zones of anti-resonance that allow impossible things—water flowing backward, time moving sideways, the arithmetic lichens growing into equations that solve themselves and vanish.

I carry seventeen of these bottles now, each containing a different quality of silence harvested from the wake of the moving quiet. The Resonance Shepherds whisper that I'm becoming something new, something the plateau hasn't seen before—a being that bridges sound and silence, mapping the liminal spaces where the plateau's impossible physics break down entirely.

Sometimes, in the standing waves of refracted light, I catch glimpses of my reflection and don't recognize what I see. My transformation accelerates with each feeding, each map grown, each bottle of silence filled. But I continue my work, driven by the certainty that the pattern I'm tracking leads somewhere—to the source of the sound, perhaps, or to its end, or to the revelation that source and end are the same thing viewed from different angles in time.

The plateau accepts me as it accepts all its inhabitants, weaving me into its incomprehensible purpose. And I map its acoustic bones, one resonance at a time, approaching some truth too large for any single consciousness to hold, requiring the distributed intelligence of every being on the Weeping Plateau to fully comprehend.

In my dreams—if I still dream—I hear the silence calling, and my wire hair resonates with frequencies that won't exist for another thousand years.
