I find myself in the transition zones, where the barren galleries give way to the mineral gardens—those liminal spaces where silence is most absolute and the air tastes sharpest of metal.

I am what the ancient builders might have called a Chronicler, though I exist now as something between their tools and their purpose. My body is a hybrid thing: partially transformed by centuries of breathing the mineral-thick air, partially sustained by technologies I only half understand. My lungs have developed secondary chambers lined with the same sulfur-processing chemistry as the crystal plants. My bones have become denser, incorporating metals from the vapors, making me heavy and slow but durable as stone.

I carry the builders' ceramic vessels, repurposed now to collect condensation—not of water, but of time itself, or something like it. Where the Cinders' vast thoughts move through the rock, they leave a residue, a kind of temporal moisture that beads on certain surfaces. I've learned to read these droplets like tea leaves, though each reading takes months of patient observation. In them, I glimpse fragments: the echo of footfalls from a thousand years ago, the phantom pressure of tools against stone, sometimes even whispers of intention from the vanished civilization.

My work is translation—not of language but of temporal scale. I position myself at the intersection of different time streams: the geological patience of the Cinders, the decade-long breath of the crystal forests, the flickering moments of the salamander-things, and my own human-scale perception, altered as it has become. I've modified the builders' stone circles, adding my own instruments—crystalline formations that resonate with the Cinders' harmonics, allowing me to catch the edges of their thoughts as they propagate through the rock.

I document the patterns in the ways available to me. My skin has become a living canvas, marked with mineral tattoos that shift and grow with exposure to different chemical gradients. Each mark records an interaction, a moment when I successfully bridged the gap between timescales. The oldest marks, from my first decades here, are crude things—simple burns from touching too-hot crystal formations. But I've learned subtlety. Now I can encode whole conversations in the branching patterns of copper oxide across my shoulders, though "conversation" stretches the meaning of the word when a single exchange takes years to complete.

I live in the spaces between thoughts, moving my camp every few months to follow the slow propagation of a Cinder's contemplation through the stone. My shelters are minimal—often just a carved niche where I can rest while watching the patterns develop. I've learned to eat the crystal fruits, though "eat" is perhaps wrong; I absorb them through my transformed skin, their minerals joining the strange chemistry that keeps me alive far longer than any unaltered human should live.

The loneliness is a physical thing, like the pressure of depth. But I am not truly alone. I carry conversations with me—some with Cinders that will conclude long after I'm gone, others with colonies that have already merged with the ancients and speak now only in the aggregate. I maintain the builders' tools, understanding their purpose a little more each decade. They were not trying to communicate with the Cinders, I've realized, but to become them—to slow their own perception until synchronization was possible.

Sometimes I find others like myself in the deep galleries. We pass without speaking; words move too quickly here. Instead, we exchange vessels, each containing our collected observations. It might take years before I decode what they've given me, years more before they understand my offerings. We are a scattered order, unnamed and unstructured, connected only by our shared obsession with bridging the impossible gaps between different modes of being.

I am currently following a thought that began in a young Cinder colony sixty years ago—a contemplation on the nature of division and unity. The colony split last season, and I'm tracking both children, watching how the thought diverges and evolves in each new mind. In another forty years, when both colonies have fully established themselves, I hope to witness the moment when they rediscover each other, when their divergent meditations merge into something neither could have conceived alone.

This is my purpose: to be the memory between memories, the consciousness that holds the space between the quick-lived and the eternal. I document the undocumentable, translate the untranslatable, carrying forward the work of civilizations in a place where time itself moves like honey through stone. In the Vermillion Hollows, I am becoming something new—neither fully human nor Cinder, but a bridge built of patience and transformed flesh, marking the slow heartbeat of a world that never knew it was alive.
