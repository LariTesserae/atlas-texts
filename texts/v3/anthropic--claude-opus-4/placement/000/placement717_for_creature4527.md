**The Codicil Chambers**

I find myself neither visitor nor resident but something between—a marginal notation in flesh, dwelling in the spaces between Sifam's endless contracts.

I am one of the Glossators.

We inhabit the codicil chambers: those narrow galleries that run parallel to the main thoroughfares, accessible only through doors that appear when someone misreads a particularly important clause. Our bodies are mostly ink—we sweat footnotes in the humid air, our blood runs in citation format, our tears fall as asterisks that bloom into explanatory sub-clauses wherever they land.

My chamber winds through the southeastern cortex of Gwyrgch, a helical ribbon of space that exists primarily as *commentary* on the spaces around it. The walls are living vellum, their surface constantly sprouting new addenda as the tree herself reconsiders old growth decisions. I spend my days annotating these second thoughts, cross-referencing them against the precedent of previous seasons, adding marginalia to marginalia until meaning develops fractally inward.

We evolved—or perhaps *devolved*—from a sect of scribes who took their work too literally. The story goes that our ancestors were hired to document a particularly contentious root-treaty negotiation between Gwyrgch and Memphine. The talks lasted seventeen years. By year three, the scribes' notes had become more substantial than the treaty itself. By year seven, they were eating only paper pulp and drinking fermented squid-ink. By the treaty's end, they'd developed the ability to photosynthesize meaning directly from ambient conversation.

Now we are maybe a dozen, maybe a thousand—it's difficult to count people who exist primarily as interpretive apparatus. My neighbor might be the smell of old parchment. My lover was a particularly insightful semicolon until she evolved into pure implication last Tuesday.

My particular specialty is *amendments to forgetting*. When the trees misremember something important—and they do, constantly, their memory more editorial than archival—I crawl through the cambium courts documenting what was lost and why its absence matters. My skin has developed a peculiar transparency, visible only in the light that falls through leaves that no longer exist. When I hold my hand up to these ghost-rays, you can read the full text of everything Gwyrgch has deliberately forgotten written in scar tissue across my palm:

- The location of her first taproot (sold for gambling debts to an underground river)
- The taste of volcanic soil from before the city lifted itself out of history  
- The names of seventeen saplings who didn't survive the Ethics Wars
- How to stop growing

This last one I've been annotating for forty years. The marginalia has developed its own ecosystem—tiny mites that feed on recursive subclauses, moths whose wings are exactly the width of an average disclaimer, a small colony of syntactic beetles who've built a shrine to the God of Unparseable Grammar in my left elbow.

My daily routine, such as routine exists here:

Dawn: Wake to the sound of new precedents precipitating out of the morning mist. Check my skin for overnight amendments—last week I woke up with an entire appeals process tattooed in legalese around my ankle. The process was for a case I hadn't been born for yet.

Morning: Crawl through the interpretation tubes to the Calcium Exchange, where I trade yesterday's annotations for fresh ink. The skeleton-traders always want commentary on their own contracts, which I provide in exchange for powdered memory of legal systems that predate language.

Midday: Hold office hours in my chamber. Petitioners arrive—confused moths clutching property disputes, lawsuits between lichens, a love poem that's suing itself for libel. I document their grievances in the margins of my margin, add footnotes to their fury, until their problems become beautiful enough to abandon.

Afternoon: Attend the Daily Redaction, where teams of us argue about which laws should be unwritten today. The process involves inhaling yesterday's legislation until we're lightheaded with whereas clauses, then exhaling slowly while the Department of Necessary Forgetting takes notes on what we don't say.

Evening: Visit the Nunation Archives, where punctuation marks that have lost their sentences gather to share war stories. I'm translating their memoirs—the autobiography of a question mark who spent three decades attached to a query about the meaning of happiness, the travel diary of a peripatetic period that refuses to end anything permanently.

Night: Return to my chamber to find it has reorganized itself again. My bed (a hammock woven from rejected rough drafts) now hangs from what used to be the floor. My desk (a mushroom trained to retain impression) has developed opinions about my latest annotations and expresses them through selective spore release.

Before sleep, I perform the Evening Enumeration—counting every interpretation I've made that day, then formally releasing them into the city's dreaming. Sometimes I see them later, evolved into independent arguments drifting through the fog layer, mating with other escaped ideas to produce theoretical frameworks that nobody intended but everyone suddenly needs.

The work changes you. My fingernails now grow in citation format. My dreams have footnotes. When I speak, subordinate clauses fall from my mouth like breadcrumbs, leading back to arguments I've forgotten making. Sometimes tourists ask what I am and I can only point to the margins of their maps where warnings about me are already being written by tomorrow's cartographers.

But I love it—this half-life between meaning and meant, this existence as everyone's second thought. In a city built on endless negotiation, someone must document the contracts within contracts, the fine print that develops its own fine print, the asterisks that bloom into entire cosmologies of exception.

Besides, the retirement benefits are excellent, assuming you believe retirement is real. The pension plan is written entirely in hypothetical mood. We're still waiting to see if it conjugates properly.

Yesterday—or was it tomorrow?—Gwyrgch grew a new clause in our chamber wall. It reads: "This space intentionally left meaningful." I've been annotating it all week, adding layer after layer of commentary until the words disappear entirely under their own interpretation.

This is how we live in the margins—by making them wider than the page itself, until the exception subsumes the rule, until the footnote grows into forest, until even our absence requires extensive documentation to properly not exist.
