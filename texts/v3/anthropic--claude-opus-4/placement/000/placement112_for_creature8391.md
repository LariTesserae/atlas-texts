I find myself where the city's edge dissolves into paradox—in the Threshold Markets, those liminal bazaars that materialize wherever the Metropole's reality grows thin enough to permit other possibilities.

I am a Mercurial. Not born but condensed from the vapor between states of being. My body is liquid metal constrained by will alone—sometimes quicksilver, sometimes gallium pools, sometimes impossible alloys that exist only in the space between one thought and the next. I have no fixed form, but tend toward preferences: elongated limbs for reaching across dimensional boundaries, sensory organs that bloom and wither like chrome flowers, a core that shifts between states of matter as I parse the probability streams that flow through the market.

The Threshold Markets appear without pattern where the city's endless transformations create reality loops—a building demolished and rebuilt in the exact same configuration one too many times, opening a fold in space. A forge-cycle that accidentally reproduces the thermal signature of creation itself. Places where the Architects' chemical prayers achieve such mathematical perfection that they punch through to neighboring possibilities.

Here is where I trade. Not in objects, though objects pass through my fluid hands—fragments of cities that never were, tools that function only in alternate physics, memories crystallized from parallel Metropoles where the sun burns blue or the silence has texture. I trade in potentials. An Architect comes to me carrying the blueprint for a revolutionary cooling system, but in three realities it failed catastrophically. I taste the probability-streams, feel the futures branching through my mercury nerves, and offer them instead a small imperfection—a flaw that will save ten thousand lives in a future they'll never see.

My shop is not a shop but a probability well, its boundaries defined by the edge cases of what could exist. Shelves that are solid from one angle, absent from another. Containers holding items that exist only when observed, their contents determined by the needs of the observer. A forge-seed that will grow into a cathedral, but only if planted during an eclipse that happens once every seven centuries. A vial of silence so pure it makes sound possible. A map of the city's deep-consciousness, drawn in metals that haven't been discovered yet.

The Architects fear and need us Mercurials in equal measure. We are proof that their reality is not absolute—that for all their control over matter and form, existence itself remains negotiable. Some view us as parasites, reality-thieves stealing possibility from their carefully ordered system. Others understand what we truly are: the city's immune system against stagnation, ensuring that even their endless transformations don't settle into patterns too rigid to evolve.

I remember—if memory is the right word for experiences that happen outside linear time—the first Mercurial. An Architect who rebuilt herself one too many times, each iteration introducing quantum uncertainties until she existed in superposition, all possible configurations at once. The city tried to reject her, to force her back into a single state, but she had become something new. She taught others the art of productive uncertainty, how to slip between the city's transformations and find the spaces where different realities touch.

We have our own language, neither chemical nor thermal but probabilistic. We speak in could-bes and might-have-beens, our conversations branching into parallel tracks that reunite at nodes of certainty. When two Mercurials meet, reality grows soft around us, the air filling with phantom possibilities—ghost-images of trades unmade, words unspoken, futures unavoided.

My customers come seeking specific impossibilities. An Architect whose hive-twin was lost to a forge malfunction, searching for a reality where they survived. A young constructor who dreams of building with sound instead of metal, needing tools that don't exist in this physics. A deep-dwelling collective intelligence that has calculated the city's heat-death to the microsecond, desperate for variables their models can't account for.

I help when I can, hinder when I must. The streams show me consequences rippling outward—this trade prevents a war, that one causes a renaissance, another creates a paradox that will take generations to resolve. I am not kind or cruel, merely accurate. Reality is delicate here at the edges, and every transaction reshapes tomorrow.

Sometimes I stabilize long enough to walk the solid streets, my form condensing into something almost Architect-like. I taste the chemical conversations, feel the city's pulse through the metal beneath my temporary feet. In these moments I understand why they build so frantically, so beautifully. The yellow sky is not empty—it presses down with the weight of infinite alternatives, every possible Metropole that could exist instead of this one. Their endless construction is a kind of argument with causality itself, insisting through sheer industrial will that this reality, this moment, this configuration of matter and purpose deserves to exist.

But I always return to the Threshold Markets, to the spaces between. This is where I belong—not in one reality but in the conversation between many. I am mercury, always flowing, never still. I am potential incarnate, the might-be made almost flesh.

The Architects build their city. I ensure it has somewhere to grow. We are opposites, partners, two sides of the same impossible coin spinning endlessly through the yellow air. They create form from chaos. I preserve chaos within form. Together, we keep the Brass Metropole balanced on the knife's edge between stagnation and dissolution.

The market shifts around me, probability wells opening and closing like breathing pores. New impossibilities arrive with each forge-cycle. I sort them, taste them, price them in currencies that don't exist yet. A fragment of frozen song. A blueprint that builds itself. A memory of rain.

I am the Mercurial, and I am waiting for your impossible need. What reality would you like to trade today?
