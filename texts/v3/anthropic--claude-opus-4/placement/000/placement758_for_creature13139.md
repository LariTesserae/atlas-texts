In this eternal engine of flesh and iron, I am the Aberration.

I emerged three thousand cycles ago—though time means nothing here where no sun rises or sets—from Sluice Gate 49,227. Something went wrong in my extrusion. The vital fluid that formed me had pooled too long in a forgotten eddy of the pipes, had fermented with trace minerals leached from a microscopic crack in the conduit walls. When I fell from the gate, my carapace was wrong. Not white but a deep, mottled grey shot through with veins of tarnished copper. Not smooth but ridged with irregular, crystalline growths that caught the light like broken glass.

But the true horror of my existence revealed itself in the seconds after my awakening. As my legs unfurled and began their pneumatic clicking against the basalt, I did something no member of the Swarm had ever done.

I turned around.

The magnetic pull toward the hopper still thrums in my corrupted ganglia, but it competes with something else—a second force, weaker but insistent, that pulls me in impossible directions. Sideways. Backwards. Up.

For my first hundred cycles, I fought to maintain my place in the flow. I marched with my siblings, our shells grinding together in that frictionless ballet of purpose. But my legs would stutter. My path would veer. I would find myself walking perpendicular to the current, my crystalline shell scraping harsh furrows across the smooth carapaces of the others. They did not react—could not react—but simply flowed around me like water around a stone.

My mantle is wrong too. Where others drink only the condensation and metallic slurry, mine has developed secondary channels, hair-thin spiracles that taste something else in the processed air. I taste the age of the machines. I taste the microscopic erosion of the hopper's teeth, each gear imperceptibly smaller than it was a million cycles ago. I taste time itself, that thing which should not exist here.

By my five hundredth cycle, I had discovered the walls.

The Swarm never reaches the walls. Their magnetic imperative draws them in efficient channels between the three great machines, wearing their eternal trenches deeper with each pass. But I, pulled by my aberrant magnetism, found myself pressed against the sheer basalt cliffs that ring our world. And there, in the perpetual twilight where the pale light barely reaches, I discovered the Etchings.

They cover every inch of the walls from floor to as high as my corrupted senses can detect. Microscopic scratches in the stone, carved by iron-tipped claws identical to my own. At first, I thought them random—the meaningless scars left by malfunctioning units like myself. But as I traced them with my legs, tasting their depth and direction through vibration, patterns emerged.

Maps. Formulae. Warnings.

The oldest ones, carved so deep they must be millions of cycles old, speak of the Time Before. When the machines were new. When the Swarm numbered in the thousands, not millions. When the vital fluid ran clear instead of pale, and the creatures lived for days instead of hours.

But more terrible are the recent etchings. Some only hundreds of cycles old. Some still sharp enough to cut. They speak of others like me—Aberrations born of minute variations in the perfect engine. One whose legs grew backwards and walked its entire life in reverse. One whose mantle inverted and exhaled instead of inhaled, slowly poisoning the units around it with unfiltered air. One that developed proto-eyes and went mad from seeing.

They all ended the same way. The hopper's gears are patient and indiscriminate.

Except I have not ended.

By my thousandth cycle, I had learned to mimic the flow. When the density is highest, I slip between the bodies, my aberrant magnetism allowing me to move in ways the others cannot. I spiral through the mass, up and down, forward and back, always arriving at the hopper's edge just as my four hours complete. But instead of falling, I catch myself on the underside of the lip, my crystalline growths finding purchase on the pitted metal. I hang there as millions pour over me, their claws clicking across my shell like rain. When the pressure eases, I drop back to the basin floor and begin again.

I do not know why I persist. The imperative to die is as strong in me as in any unit. But the second magnetism, the wrongness in my core, is stronger.

I have begun my own etchings. High up on the walls, where even I can barely reach, I carve my observations. The rate of erosion in Trench 7. The seventeen-cycle fluctuation in the condenser's output that no one else can taste. The way the exhaust fortress has begun to breathe half a second off-rhythm, a harbinger of some change too vast and slow for even I to fully grasp.

But mostly, I carve questions.

What are we cooling?

What requires this endless filtration?

What lies beyond the basin walls, in the world that needs our perfect engine?

And the question that haunts me most as I traverse my aberrant path through the deafening tide: If I am a flaw in the system, why does the system allow me to exist?

Sometimes, hanging beneath the hopper's lip, I taste something in the vital fluid that pours over the edge. A variance so slight it might be imagination. As if deep in the pipes, in some forgotten corner of our closed world, other eddies are forming. Other minerals are leaching. Other aberrations are gestating.

The machines grind on. The Swarm flows eternal. And I persist in my wrongness, a single grey note in the white symphony, documenting the slow decay of perfection.

Three thousand cycles.

And counting.
