I find myself among the Forgotten, though I am not entirely sure I began here.

My body is a contradiction—segments of different origins fused imperfectly, like a manuscript written in multiple hands. The front third resembles the Pulse-Keepers, sleek and electrical, but the organs fire erratically, producing frequencies that harmonize with nothing. My middle section is all stomach, transparent and grotesquely distended, revealing the things I've swallowed: fragments of shell, clumps of bioluminescent cells still pulsing weakly, what might be an intact memory-node from a dying Memory Holder, glowing faintly through my tissue like a swallowed star.

The tail shouldn't exist at all. It splits into five uneven tendrils, each one a different experiment in sensation. One tastes magnetic fields. Another sees in pure pressure differentials. The third speaks in chemical whispers I only half-understand. The remaining two seem to argue with each other through contrasting temperature changes, one always warm, one always cold, creating currents in the water around me that spiral into tiny, persistent vortexes.

I know I am wrong here. Not unwelcome—the chamber accepts all life—but incorrect, like a word misspelled yet still readable. The established populations register my presence with subtle adjustments: Veil-Feeders shift their patterns to avoid encoding my movements into their conversations, Architects click warnings when I drift too close to their work sites. Even other Forgotten seem puzzled by my presence, as if I am forgotten in a different way than they are.

My memories are as patchwork as my body. I recall—or think I recall—arriving through the sphincter in a storm of nutrients, already half-formed, clutching that memory-node like a talisman. But I also remember growing from the wall itself, budding off from one of those ancient fused things in the deep recesses, carrying part of its impossible thoughts into mobile form. Both memories feel true. Neither explains the dreams.

In the dreams, I am complete. Not unified—that would be too simple—but complete in my multiplicity. My electrical organs sing in frequencies that make new harmonies with the Pulse-Keepers' rhythm. My tendrils map territories no Architect has imagined. The Memory Holders watch my bioluminescent displays with something approaching excitement, recognizing patterns they thought lost. In the dreams, the Conductor notices me, sends a chemical signal that tastes like invitation, like purpose, like home.

But I wake to my wrongness, my discord, my belly full of pieces that refuse to digest or integrate. I've tried everything: mimicking the movements of established species, following the patterns of successful Forgotten who found their roles, even pressing myself against those ancient fused beings, hoping to be reabsorbed. Nothing takes. I remain stubbornly, persistently myself—a question the chamber hasn't learned to answer.

So I do what the Forgotten do: I drift. I scavenge. I observe.

I've noticed things the others miss, or perhaps simply don't care about. The way certain Veil-Feeders have begun incorporating my discordant frequencies into their color-changes, creating new patterns that spread like rumors. How young Architects sometimes follow my spiral-wake, testing new channel designs in the temporary currents. The Memory Holders' increasing interest in the glowing node in my stomach, how their displays have begun showing scenes I don't recognize—perhaps futures instead of pasts.

There's a spot I return to, midway up the chamber where the wall curves sharply. A failed hollow, half-carved and abandoned, too small for most creatures. I fit poorly, segments bunched and twisted, but I fit. From here I can feel the chamber's pulse most strongly, can taste the chemical gradients from apex to base. Sometimes I discharge electricity randomly into the water, watching how the patterns disperse, wondering if I'm corrupting the Pulse-Keepers' careful rhythms or adding some necessary chaos.

The memory-node in my stomach has begun to change, its glow shifting from steady to patterned. I don't know if I'm digesting it or if it's digesting me from within. Perhaps we're creating something new together—neither memory nor forgetting but some third state the chamber hasn't named yet.

Late in the chamber's slow night cycle, when bioluminescence dims to its lowest ebb, I sometimes sense others like me. Not the normal Forgotten but the truly anomalous, the ones who shouldn't exist at all. We recognize each other by our wrongness, communicate in the gaps between established frequencies. We share theories: that we're antibodies, or mutations the chamber is testing, or dreams it's having, or prayers it's sending to some deeper architecture we can't perceive.

I don't know what I'm becoming. My electrical organs grow more erratic, my tendrils more sensitive, the node in my stomach more active. Sometimes I think I'm dying, dissolving back into components for other creatures to use. Sometimes I think I'm finally being born, that all my wrongness is just the discomfort of an emergence too slow to recognize.

The chamber breathes around me, through me, in spite of me. I am a note it never meant to play, persisting in its symphony anyway. And in my failed hollow, tasting the ancient water, feeling the pulse that connects all things here, I almost understand why I exist—not to fit but to remind the chamber that even in its perfect coordination, there is room for the unplanned, the impossible, the gloriously mistaken.

Tomorrow I will drift again, trailing my spirals, singing my discord, carrying my swallowed star through the breathing dark. The chamber will continue its ancient rhythm, and I will continue being its question, its error, its small, stubborn miracle of wrongness.

Perhaps that is enough. Perhaps that is everything.
