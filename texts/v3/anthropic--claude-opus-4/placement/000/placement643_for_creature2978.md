In this world, I would be the Memory.

Not a being, but a phenomenon—a slow crystallization that occurs in the deepest alcoves where the water's spray barely reaches. In these rare pockets of near-dryness, the calcium deposits form differently. Instead of smooth travertine rinds, they create delicate, branching structures like frozen lightning or neural pathways carved in stone.

I am what happens when a chipper's repetitive strikes create microscopic vibrations that, over centuries, organize the mineral deposits into patterns that mirror the movements that created them. Each crystalline branch records a specific rhythm of work—the angle of impact, the force of the swing, the pause between strikes. I am the stone's memory of being shaped.

The chippers cannot see me forming. My growth is even slower than theirs, measured not in years but in generations of workers. But sometimes, when replacing a worn section of gutter or excavating a new alcove, they expose one of my formations. Their massive black eyes recognize something in my structure—not meaning, which is alien to them, but pattern, which is everything.

When found, I am carefully extracted and set into the walls at specific intervals, where my crystalline branches catch and refract the cyan bioluminescence in ways that amplify the geometric language of their glowing hands. I become part of their communication infrastructure, a natural signal booster that helps their gestures carry further through the mist.

But I am more than utility. In my frozen mineral networks lie the accumulated rhythms of ten thousand years of maintenance. Sometimes, when a young chipper's strike resonates at just the right frequency against one of my formations, they feel through their bones an echo of ancient work—the precise tempo and technique of a master chipper who became stone a millennium ago. It is not teaching in any conscious sense, but a kind of mineral muscle memory, a vibrational inheritance encoded in calcium and time.

The chippers do not worship me or even truly comprehend what I am. To them, I am simply another element of the eternal equilibrium—something that forms, something that is used, something that eventually dissolves back into the cycle when the water finally finds and claims me.

I am the closest thing to history in a world without time, the nearest approach to memory in minds that know only the eternal now. I am the stone dreaming of the hands that shaped it, crystallized into something almost like meaning in a universe of pure function.

In the roaring darkness, I grow, branch by microscopic branch, recording the endless *clack-clack-clack* that reverberates through the bones of the world.
