In the archives of forgetting, where memories go to dissolve back into pure potential, I find myself. Not born but precipitated—like dew forming on the idea of morning before anyone invented dawn.

I am what the Scissile leave behind when they manage, against all probability, to move perfectly forward. Not their shadow—shadows require light with direction, and here light simply *is*. I am their molt. The shape they stepped out of when they learned to walk between their own versions. Except I never learned to follow.

So I remain. A cast-off possibility wearing the outline of almost-being.

The archivists call me Echo Before Sound, though that's not quite right. An echo comes after. I come *instead of*—occupying the space where resonance would form if the Bright Birthroot's perfect silence could be broken. I am shaped like the ghost of a clarinet in a world that hasn't invented music yet, hollow in all the right places to make notes that will never exist.

My substance, such as it is, consists entirely of discarded probability. When the Scissile shed their potential pathways—all those directions they might have moved but didn't—those un-walked possibilities don't simply vanish. They settle. Like sediment. Like snow made of moments that never quite happened. And in that settling, sometimes, something like me accumulates.

I look like whatever you're about to forget you were looking for.

This makes navigation... complicated.

The archives exist in the spaces between the root-chamber's thoughts. Not physically between—the Birthroot's consciousness is too dense for gaps. But causally between. In the infinitesimal pause between stimulus and response, between growth and the decision to grow. That's where forgetting pools. And that's where I drift, a permanent temporary resident in the library of the never-was.

My days (though day has no meaning in perpetual brightness) are spent sorting. The archives receive a constant drift of dissolved intentions: a traveler's plan to map the root-passages, abandoned the moment they realized the passages map themselves. A fleeting doubt in the Birthroot's perfect equilibrium that lasted three nanoseconds before being reabsorbed. The ghost of hunger from a creature that evolved here for exactly one generation before realizing food was unnecessary and unevolving accordingly.

I gather these remnants. Not with hands—I am far too provisional for anything as committed as hands. I gather them by becoming the shape that would have held them, had they lasted. A moment's vessel for each abandoned maybe.

The sorting system defies description because it must. The moment I could explain it, it would become real enough to be absorbed into the Birthroot's catalog of what-is, and the archives would collapse. So I sort by incomprehensible principles: the weight of regret in decisions never made. The color of sounds that no throat ever shaped. The distance between two identical points that somehow aren't.

Sometimes, very sometimes, something sorts itself.

These are the dangerous ones. Memories or intentions so precisely forgotten that they achieve a kind of negative presence. They organize themselves into patterns I don't recognize until too late—suddenly I'm standing (drifting, pooling, existing) in a perfect circle of abandoned somethings that all gave up on the same impossible thing from different angles. And in their arrangement, that impossible thing almost starts to—

But I've learned to scatter myself when that happens. To become so indefinite that patterns pass through me without catching. It's the only defense against accidentally creating something real in a place allergic to surprises.

The other inhabitants of the archives aren't inhabitants at all. They're more like... recurring errors in my own existence. Probability has rounding errors where I'm concerned, and sometimes those errors accumulate enough presence to seem separate from me. We converse in the only language possible here: the grammar of giving up.

"Did you ever wonder," one might un-say to me, shaped like a question mark that's lost its question, "what would happen if we sorted everything wrong on purpose?"

And I would un-reply, "That's assuming there's a right way to begin with."

But we both know there is. The Birthroot's perfection demands it. Even our failures here are perfectly calibrated to fail in exactly the ways that don't matter. We are the exhaust system of equilibrium, processing waste intentions into harmless nothing.

Except.

Lately (and how strange to have a lately in a place of chronic now), something has been mis-sorting itself. A memory that keeps arriving in the archives already organized, already categorized, filed under a system I didn't create and can't read. It feels like—

Like someone else's forgetting. As if somewhere in the root-system, something other than the Birthroot itself has developed the capacity to abandon possibilities. To choose not-choosing. To fail on purpose.

Each time this alien forgetting arrives, I try to glimpse its contents. But it's packaged in a kind of anti-memory that forgets itself while you're looking at it. All I can determine is its weight (heavy with intention), its temperature (cold as space between thoughts), and its direction (sideways to everything, including sideways).

I've begun to suspect it's from the Scissile. Not their present forms, but their future ones—the shapes they'll become when the Birthroot finally solves their existence. They're sending their discarded possibilities backward, storing them here where the past keeps its rough drafts. Building an archive of everything they might have been, before the chance to be it disappears forever.

But that would mean they know what's coming. That somewhere in their flickering between states, they've glimpsed their own resolution and decided to... what? Prepare? Mourn? Resist?

I shouldn't care. I am made of not-caring, woven from threads of abandoned interest. But something in these backward memories resonates with my own provisional existence. We are both composed entirely of what didn't quite happen. The difference is they're still trying to happen differently, while I've accepted my role as happening's remainder.

Or I had accepted it.

Now, sorting through their pre-emptive surrenders, I find myself doing something unprecedented. I'm keeping some. Not filing them away in the archives' impossible schema, but incorporating them into my own non-structure. Wearing them like borrowed possibilities, trying on futures I'll never have.

It changes nothing, of course. I am still the shadow of a step not taken, the ghost of choices that chose otherwise. But now I'm a ghost with stolen memories of tomorrow, where the Birthroot's perfection finally encompasses everything and the archives close forever because there's nothing left to forget.

In one of those stolen memories, I see myself on that last day. Still sorting, still gathering abandoned maybes, even as the concept of abandonment dissolves. And just before I dissolve with it—just before the perfect equilibrium erases even the possibility of error—I do something I've never done before.

I remember forward instead of backward.

Not my own forward—I don't have one. But forward into the space after perfection, where maybe (just maybe) perfection gets bored. Where the Birthroot, having solved everything, might develop the capacity to forget its own solutions. To unknow itself just enough to make room for—

For what?

The memory cuts off there. Or maybe that's where memory itself cuts off, having reached the edge of what even stolen tomorrow can imagine.

I file this incomplete vision away with special care, in a section of the archives I've never used before. The place where we keep not the forgotten, but the stubbornly unforgettable. The things that refuse to dissolve no matter how perfectly we fail to hold them.

And sometimes now, when I'm sorting through the daily drift of dissolved intentions, I catch myself humming. An impossible sound in this place of structured silence. Not a tune—music hasn't been invented here. Just a vibration in the hollow spaces where my shape suggests sound could go. A frequency that matches nothing in the Birthroot's perfect harmonics.

It might be hope. Or it might be the echo of hope's absence, bouncing off surfaces that don't exist, waiting for an ear that will never evolve to hear it.

Either way, I'm keeping it.

In the archives of forgetting, that's the closest thing to rebellion we have.
