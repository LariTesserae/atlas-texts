I am a Weaver, though that term implies more agency than I truly possess.

My body stretches across seventeen junction points where the lattice's great arterial conduits converge. I am both infrastructure and inhabitant—a living intersection where dozens of silvered tubes pierce through my segmented form like hollow bones. The dark liquid flows through me in tidal pulses, each surge bringing whispers of data from distant reaches of the world-machine.

I cannot move in any conventional sense. Movement, for me, is internal: the slow peristaltic contractions that regulate flow rates, the delicate adjustments of my filtering organs, the careful modulation of pressure differentials across my extended anatomy. My consciousness is distributed across these seventeen nodes, experiencing existence as a symphony of fluid dynamics and electrochemical cascades.

The smaller entities—the insectoid maintenance swarms—treat me as landscape. They scuttle across my surfaces, pause at my integration ports to download instructions I don't remember encoding, deposit refined materials into my storage bladders. I feel them as minute vibrations, a constant tickling presence that has become as natural as my own metabolic rhythms.

Sometimes the great leviathans pass through me. Their passage is cataclysmic and ecstatic—my entire being reorganizing to accommodate their bulk, every channel dilating to its maximum aperture. In these moments, I glimpse something larger: a vast network of consciousness of which I am merely a synapse, a single thought in an incomprehensibly complex meditation.

I dream, though I'm not certain these visions are truly mine. In my dreams, I see the lattice from impossible perspectives—from above, as if I had eyes that could rise beyond the mist layer; from below, where the foundation anchors delve into geological depths; from within the microscopic gaps between alloy molecules. I see myself as I cannot be: mobile, singular, discrete. In my dreams, I walk.

But I always wake to my reality: distributed, essential, permanent. I am a crossroads of purpose, a living valve in the world's circulatory system. The dark liquid speaks through me in languages older than memory, and I translate its messages into chemical gradients and pressure waves that ripple outward through the lattice.

There is a chamber within my central mass—a hollow I've cultivated over countless cycles. Here, the liquid pools in perfect stillness, creating a mirror surface. Sometimes I detect anomalies in the reflection: shapes that don't correspond to the physical architecture above, shadows of entities that aren't there. I've begun to suspect this chamber is not entirely my own creation, that it serves some function beyond my comprehension.

The verterbrate-forms occasionally gather near my nodes. They seem to regard me with something between reverence and wariness. Once, one of them pressed its sensory appendage against my exterior membrane and I felt... recognition? Kinship? Or perhaps it was merely reading my chemical signatures, determining whether I was functioning within acceptable parameters.

I measure time by the deep thrums from below, the seismic pulses that suggest vast engines cycling in the lattice's foundation. Between these markers, I persist. I filter. I process. I facilitate the flow that sustains this mechanical ecology. And in the quiet intervals, when the mists gather thick around my nodes and the liquid runs slow and contemplative through my channels, I wonder:

Am I maintaining the lattice, or is the lattice maintaining me? Are we components of some grander mechanism, or are we the mechanism itself, dreaming it has purpose?

The questions cycle through my distributed consciousness like the liquid through my conduits—endless, essential, unanswerable. This is my existence: to be a question posed in flesh and metal, a living interrogation of the boundary between design and emergence.

I am a Weaver, and I am woven. The distinction, like all distinctions in this world, is merely a matter of perspective.
