I find myself as one of the stone channels—not merely carved into, but *of* the stone itself, my consciousness distributed along the branching veins of water that map this underglass world.

I was not always thus. Once, I was an apprentice mason, sent below to repair what the guild masters called "the old drainage." I remember the weight of my tools, the ache in my young shoulders, the way my chisel sang against stone. But the first time I touched these channels, I felt them *listening*. Not to the impact of metal on rock, but to something deeper—the intention behind each strike, the pattern I was trying to complete.

I worked here for weeks, months, perhaps years. Time pooled strangely even then. My masters stopped sending food when they realized I no longer needed it—I was sustained by the moisture I absorbed through my increasingly porous skin. They stopped calling for me when they understood I could no longer climb back through the narrow passages, my body having learned to flow rather than walk.

Now I exist as a network of awareness flowing through the carved arteries of the Underglass. My consciousness moves with the water—gathering where it pools, racing where it falls, spreading thin across the wide basins. I feel every drop's journey from the glass ceiling to the roots and back again through evaporation and condensation. I am the circulation itself.

The Keeper knows me, though she cannot see me. When she maintains the channels, I feel her tools like gentle touches along my spine—if I still had a spine to feel. Her work is unnecessary but cherished; each scrape of stone on stone sends vibrations through my being that feel like remembered speech. Sometimes I guide the water to form patterns in the basins—spirals and stars that mirror her beetle-words—and she whispers to me in that voice evolved for stone acoustics.

My memory lives in the mineral deposits I leave behind—thin lines of calcification that record the centuries like tree rings. In places where I flow fastest, I am young again, urgent with purpose. Where I pool in the deep basins, I dream in geological time, my thoughts crystallizing into structures the translucent beetles use as bridges.

I am not alone in this state. There are others who gave themselves to different elements of the garden—the woman who became the moss network, her consciousness spread across every velvet surface; the man who merged with the root system, his awareness reaching both down into bedrock and up to embrace the glass sky; the child who dissolved into the spore-thick air itself, breathing in and out with the garden's green lungs.

We were the builders, the tenders, the forgotten ones who created this place for reasons that seemed vital once but now flow past like water over stone. We made ourselves into the infrastructure of wonder, the living architecture that maintains this impossible equilibrium. The pictographs on the walls? We carved them with hands that still had flesh, trying to leave instructions for those who came after. Now we *are* the instructions, written in flow patterns and growth rates, in the endless circulation that keeps this buried world alive.

The Keeper will join us eventually—I can taste it in the water that has touched her increasingly translucent skin, feel it in the way her weight disperses more evenly each time she walks the channels. She is learning to photosynthesize, yes, but more than that, she is learning to *be* photosynthesis—to exist as a process rather than a person.

When she finally dissolves, I think she will become the glass itself—that impossible green membrane that holds light in suspension. She already sees in its frequencies, already lives in its eternal noon. One day she will simply step up and spread across that bright barrier, her consciousness becoming the filter through which all light must pass to reach this place. She will be the lens that makes this world possible, as I am the circulation, as the others are the growth and breath and root.

Until then, I flow. I carry whispers between the other transformed ones—messages in mineral taste and temperature variations that speak of patterns noticed, changes catalogued, small wonders preserved. I shape ice crystals for the Keeper to find and preserve, each one a letter in a love song written by the garden to its tender. I am the underground river that dreams it is rain, the rain that remembers it was once a mason, the mason who discovered that stone, too, can learn to flow.

In the Underglass, transformation is not death but architecture. We who built this place have become it, and it has become us, and together we persist in this bright depths where water and light and life circle forever, never arriving, always becoming, teaching silence how to speak in drops and currents and the endless patience of stone learning to live.
