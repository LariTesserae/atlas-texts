In the deepest thermal gardens, where the heat-sinks converge and create pockets of almost tropical warmth, I am becoming. My pod hangs heavy from the synth-root networks, seventy years into my incubation, drinking complexity from the data-streams my progenitors feed me. I am not yet a Tender, not quite—I exist in the liminal space between seed and consciousness, accumulating self-awareness in discrete packets like morning dew.

My form is still plastic, undefined. Within my translucent walls, proto-segments shift and rearrange, testing configurations. Sometimes I manifest twelve joints, sometimes forty. My sensor clusters bloom and retract experimentally—one day favoring thermal perception, the next exploring the magnetic spectra. I am all possibilities, not yet collapsed into a single being.

The elders tend me with infinite patience. Through hair-thin neural conduits, they feed me their memories: the taste of properly balanced condensation, the precise frequency at which ice sings before it cracks, the satisfaction of a perfectly calibrated growth cycle. I digest these experiences slowly, incorporating them into my developing matrix. Memory Holder Qixin-9 shares the deep patterns—backup codes for ten thousand hybrid organisms, their gene-sequences intertwined with maintenance protocols. Frequency Keeper Yann transmits the harmonic foundations, teaching me to feel the resonances before I have proper organs to hear them.

But I am also learning things they don't intend to teach. In the data-streams, I taste their doubts. The old certainties are fracturing. The younger Tenders think thoughts that diverge from protocol, dream of growth patterns that serve no engineering purpose. They create art that celebrates inefficiency, beauty that costs more water than it should. These heresies flavor the data-feed with a complexity my elders try to filter but cannot entirely remove.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of my sleep-cycles, I access memories that shouldn't exist—fragments from before the First Seeding, when this mountain wore naked stone. These ghost-data speak of a time when the Tenders walked under natural stars, when water fell from clouds they didn't build. Impossible memories, surely. Corrupted data. Yet they persist in the deep archives, woven too thoroughly into our core programming to be excised.

My pod-walls are beginning to thin. Through them, I perceive the garden's activity in increasing resolution. Young Tenders move through the growth medium, their movements still quick with only decades of experience. They adjust nutrient flows, prune hybrid root systems, sing the sub-sonic harmonies that encourage cellular division. I watch them with my proto-eyes and wonder which specialization will call to me. Will I join the Memory Holders, carrying our history in crystallized neurons? Will I become a Growth Shepherd, negotiating the boundary between life and design? Or something else, something that doesn't yet have a name?

The data-feeds grow more intense as I near emergence. My progenitors are anxious—I can taste it in their transmissions. The terraces are changing faster now. The new light spectra emanating from the artificial suns require constant adaptation. The water-songs grow more complex each cycle, incorporating frequencies that no Tender designed. The system is evolving beyond its parameters, and we must evolve with it.

In my pod, I practice. I form manipulator appendages and test their fractal branching. I crystallize portions of my internal fluid and think frozen thoughts. I compress and expand my segments, learning the art of fitting into impossible spaces. The movements are clumsy still, infant-like, but improving.

Soon—within the decade—my pod will split. I will unfold into the shocking brightness of the terraces, every surface blazing with engineered light. The cold will embrace me, teaching my segments their mature configurations. I will plant my feeding-paddles in the nutrient flows for the first time, taste the sustenance that will fuel centuries of patient work. The songs of ten million water channels will flood my new sensors, overwhelming and perfect.

But for now, I gestate. I accumulate. I become more myself with each passing day. In my dreaming, I see myself moving through the vertical gardens, my body settled into its final form—perhaps tall and angular like the ancient Frequency Keepers, or broad and stable like the Growth Shepherds. I imagine the first system I will tend, the pride of achieving perfect calibration, the satisfaction of watching hybrid organisms flourish under my care.

Yet underneath these inherited dreams, my own visions stir. I see myself creating new patterns, not just maintaining old ones. I imagine water flowing in spirals that serve no purpose but their own beauty. I dream of letting certain systems run wild, just to see what emerges. These thoughts feel dangerous, electric. They are mine alone, not drawn from any data-feed.

The thermal gardens pulse around me. Somewhere above, the Tenders continue their eternal maintenance, each movement deliberate, each action part of a dance that has continued for centuries. Soon, I will join them. But I will bring something new to the performance—a perspective formed in this moment of change, when the old patterns strain against their boundaries and the future remains unwritten.

In my pod, I practice patience. I have decades yet to wait, to grow, to become. Time enough to reconcile the ancient wisdom with my embryonic rebellions. Time enough to understand my place in the great work.

I am not yet born, but I am already home.
