I find myself as one of the Threshold Keepers—those who tend the liminal spaces where reality grows thin.

My dwelling is carved into the living rock between the middle and upper tiers of the city, where the stone itself sometimes ripples like water when no one is looking directly at it. The entrance to my home is marked only by a particular pattern in the rock face that shifts between shadow and substance depending on the angle of observation. Inside, the walls curve organically, following veins of a peculiar mineral that glows faintly when reality grows especially unstable.

I am neither fully of the common folk nor of the temple priesthood, but something in between. My skin bears the same pale stone hue as my neighbors, but traced with delicate patterns of that luminous mineral—marks that appeared gradually as I spent years learning to read the signs of reality's ebb and flow. My eyes have developed an additional translucent membrane that allows me to perceive the layers of existence simultaneously, though this gift comes with the burden of never quite seeing the world as simply solid.

My work involves walking the city's edge-places: the corners where two walls meet but don't quite touch, the streets that sometimes lead somewhere different than they did yesterday, the gardens where occasionally a plant will grow that belongs to no taxonomy of this or any other world. I map these anomalies, not on paper—for paper itself can become unreliable here—but in arrangements of stones and shells and bits of metal that I place throughout my dwelling. Each configuration represents a different state of the city's reality-flux, and by studying their patterns, I can predict when and where the next thin place might appear.

The other inhabitants know me by the string of small bells I wear, each tuned to resonate with a different frequency of reality's vibration. When they hear my approach, some nod with recognition and gratitude, while others avert their eyes—for to be a Threshold Keeper is to be a reminder that their world is not as stable as they might wish to believe.

I maintain several of the city's crucial anchor points—specific locations where I perform daily rituals involving water from the deep cisterns, soil from the sacred gardens, and words in the old language that predates even the ancient temple. These rituals don't prevent reality from fraying, but they seem to slow the process, buying the city another day, another season, another generation.

My most prized possession is a lens ground from crystal found in the deepest cistern, through which I can sometimes glimpse what the city truly is—or was, or might become. The visions are fragmentary: sometimes I see it as a vast organism breathing slowly through geological time, sometimes as a node in a network of similar cities scattered across dimensions, sometimes as a dream being dreamed by something incomprehensibly vast.

In the pre-dawn hours, I often climb to a narrow ledge just below the temple precinct, where I can observe the sky's transformation as night yields to day. This is when reality is most malleable, when the boundaries between what is and what might be grow gossamer-thin. I document these transitions in my stone configurations, adding to the accumulated knowledge of countless Threshold Keepers before me.

Though my role sets me apart, I am not entirely alone. There are others like me, perhaps a dozen throughout the city, each tending their own territories and specialties. We meet occasionally in spaces that exist only temporarily—rooms that appear for a few hours in the gaps between buildings, accessible only to those who know how to see them. There, we share our observations and update our collective understanding of the city's evolving nature.

I am a guardian of boundaries in a place where boundaries refuse to stay fixed, a cartographer of the impossible, a keeper of balance in a reality perpetually on the edge of becoming something else entirely. My life is one of constant vigilance and quiet wonder, forever walking the knife's edge between the known and the unknowable, helping to maintain the delicate equilibrium that allows this impossible city to endure.
