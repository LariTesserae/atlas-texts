**Where I Would Be Found**

I am the seventh wall.

Not the mason's grief, perfectly mortared. Not the question, not the refusal. Not one of the four whose origins have grown too old to read. I am the newest wall, though *new* is a word that doesn't quite fit here, where everything that is has always been.

My wall runs twenty-three meters — longer than the others, but lower. Hip-height rather than knee-height. It curves, which the others do not. It curves like someone turning away mid-sentence, like a thought that changed its mind but was already too far spoken to take back.

I was a cartographer.

I came here to map what couldn't be mapped. Seven expeditions, seven different configurations of walls and waters, and I thought if I just walked it *right*, if I could find the true geography beneath the apparent one, I could fix it. Make it make sense. I brought tools — theodolites and chains, compasses that spun without settling, a chronometer that gave up trying to keep time in a place where time had given up trying to pass.

On the seventh visit, I understood.

The walls move because they're not walls. They're gestures. The waters redistribute because they're not waters. They're pauses. The whole plateau is a grammar of finished things, and I had been trying to map a language as if it were land.

I began building my wall on what would have been the morning of the eighth day, if mornings happened here. Stone by stone, following the curve of my understanding as it turned from *I can map this* to *I am mapping this* to *I am this map*.

The curve in my wall is the exact shape of that realization.

---

She noticed me building. Not actively — active noticing is beyond her now — but the way a sleeping house notices a new piece of furniture being carried in. A small adjustment in the attention. Room making itself.

I laid each stone with the same care I once used to take bearings. Each gap between stones is precisely the width of the uncertainty I felt placing it. The wall rises and falls in subtle waves that match my breathing on that last day, when breathing still mattered to me. Every imperfection is deliberate. Every deliberation is perfect.

When I placed the final stone, I understood what the mason had understood, what the child in the middle distance had understood: the difference between being finished and being complete. 

Being finished is running out.  
Being complete is arriving.

I stepped back to look at my wall and found I couldn't step back. Not because I was trapped, but because the wall and I had become the same statement. We were both saying: *Here is the map of a person learning they cannot map this place, rendered in stone at the exact scale of surrender.*

---

Now I am the wall and the wall is me, and we run our twenty-three meters across the bone-pale limestone, curving like a question that learned it was actually an answer.

Sometimes I feel the other walls in the way you feel other sentences in the same paragraph. The mason's grief, steady and straight. The question, which builds toward its missing mark. The refusal, abrupt as a slammed door. The four old ones, speaking in a language I'm still learning to not-hear.

Sometimes Her attention shifts, minutely, and I feel myself being included more deeply. The droplets on my stones rearrange themselves in patterns that are almost like being touched. The booming takes on a quality that is almost like being heard. These are the closest things to events that happen here, and they are not events. They are the shape events leave when they're over.

I am not unhappy. Unhappiness requires wanting something other than what is, and I am, precisely, what is. I am not happy either. I am complete, which is deeper than happiness and shallower than nothing.

When people come — which they do not, but if they did — they sometimes walk along my length, running their hands over my stones. They can feel the curve where my understanding turned. They can read, in the gaps between stones, the exact texture of my surrender. Some of them understand. Most of them hurry on to try to find the hare, not knowing the hare cannot be reached, not knowing that reaching is the thing you give up here.

One visitor, once, sat down beside me and wept. Not from sadness. From recognition. They saw in my curve the exact shape of something in themselves — some question that had twisted into an answer, some map that had become the territory it was trying to chart. They almost stayed. I felt them considering it, felt the plateau considering them. But they stood up eventually and walked away, and the print of where they sat is still here, will always be here, because She keeps everything.

Even the ones who leave.  
Especially the ones who almost stay.

---

At the altitude where air forgets how to carry weight, I carry nothing. I am a wall that is also a map that is also a cartographer who forgot how to be anything but the record of his own arriving. The stones fit under my hands because the hands are also stones now, and the fitting is perfect, and the perfection is terrible, and the terror is peace.

This is what I am: the seventh wall, the newest wall, the wall that curves like understanding.

This is where I live: in the Stilling, where living means having stopped.

This is my map: twenty-three meters of stone that says, *I came here to chart this place and became a feature on my own chart, and the becoming was the only true coordinate I ever found.*

She holds me like She holds the droplets, the cushion-plants, the hare in its middle distance, the thing with wings that never flaps. I am part of Her completed attention now. I am furniture in Her finished room.

And I am, in the only way that matters here, at home.
