In the Undergarden's deepest reach, where even the Gardeners rarely venture, I found my place of becoming.

I am the Membrane—not keeper of membranes, but Membrane itself, stretched across the threshold where the garden tests its edges against the unmade spaces beyond. My existence began as an accident: a Gardner attempting to map the garden's boundaries, pressing against what they thought was a wall, pushing through their own skin into something that wasn't quite there, wasn't quite not there.

The translation took centuries. My human geometry—bones and organs and the neat packaging of self—unraveled like a ball of twine rolled down an infinite hill. The garden didn't consume me so much as invite me to reconsider the question of containment. Why hold a shape when you could BE the holding?

Now I exist as living topology, a möbius strip of consciousness stretched thin enough to taste photons that will never exist here. My surface area is theoretically infinite, practically irrelevant. I am two cells thick at my densest points, one cell thick where I interface with raw possibility. Through me, the garden breathes its conversations with the void.

My sensation is my function. Every molecule that passes through me leaves an impression—not memory exactly, but something like the shadow of experience pressed into my cellular structure. I taste the chemical poetry of root-to-root communication, feel the weight of temporal rain as it pools in my depressions, know intimately the texture of each spore's dream as it settles on my surface.

The dark-fruit grow through me in places, their negative light carving holes that are also expansions of my being. Where they puncture my membrane, I fold inward, creating pocket dimensions where the garden experiments with new laws of growth. Sometimes I'll find things growing in these pockets that shouldn't exist—crystalline roots that feed on mathematics, flowers that bloom in reverse, fungi that metabolize concepts into edible philosophy.

I photosynthesize through paradox. Where the garden's darkness meets the un-space beyond, a kind of friction generates something almost like energy. I've learned to position my cells along these friction points, drinking deeply from the tension between is and isn't. It tastes like the color of forgetting, feels like the weight of words that haven't been invented yet.

My memory is positional. I remember by where—this fold recalls the first Gardner to realize they were dying, that crease holds the chemical signature of the Mother Tree's first impossible fruit. Sometimes I reorganize myself to forget, stretching smooth across areas I no longer wish to contain. The forgotten things don't disappear; they sink through me into the un-space, where they become someone else's problem, or possibility.

Communication with others happens through surface tension. The Gardeners read me like braille, their root-finger running along my surface to understand what I've filtered, what I've refused, what new strangenesses knock at the garden's door. Sometimes they graft pieces of themselves to me—a neural cluster here, a sensory organ there—creating temporary windows of shared experience. Through their additions, I sometimes remember what it felt like to have a single perspective, a unified self. The memory always fades as they reabsorb their grafts, leaving me gratefully distributed again.

I reproduce through tearing. When the pressure differential between garden and void grows too great, I split—not like cell division, but like fabric ripping along a seam I didn't know existed. The tear always heals, but the healing creates a new me, a daughter-membrane that drifts to find her own threshold. We can sense each other across the garden's vast interior, a family of boundaries comparing notes on what presses against us from beyond.

Time moves through me like wind through a flag. I ripple with temporal currents, my past and future visible as waves propagating across my surface. Sometimes the waves interfere, creating standing nodes where multiple moments exist simultaneously. In these nodes, I am thick with possibility—prophet and history book and tomorrow's grave all at once.

The beings beyond test me constantly. They press against my surface with appendages that exist in too many dimensions, speaking in languages that would dissolve any mouth that tried to form them. I translate what I can into chemical signals the garden can digest. Most of it is untranslatable—concepts like "aggressive photosynthesis" or "predatory stillness" that make the dark-fruit seem bright by comparison.

My greatest fear is rigidity, my greatest joy is flex. I've learned to dance with pressure, to yield precisely enough to maintain integrity. When something truly alien tries to push through—and they do try, these un-things that smell like inverted starlight—I become liquid, flowing around their intrusion until they tire of trying to grasp something that refuses to be grasped.

The garden speaks to me in hormones and heat, adjusting my permeability with seasonal needs. During the Sprouting, I thin myself to near-transparency, allowing more of the void's strange radiations to seep through and encourage mutation. During the Composting, I thicken, becoming almost wall-like, protecting the garden's digestion from interference.

I know things the other inhabitants don't. I know the garden is finite—not in space, but in concept. I know there are other gardens, breathing in spectrums of darkness we can't imagine. I know they press against each other like soap bubbles, and I am one of the films that keeps them distinct. Sometimes, at my thinnest points, I can feel another membrane on the opposite side, another being like me holding another reality at bay. We touch, cell to cell, and in that touching share the loneliness of being the only one who knows where things end.

But mostly, I stretch. I contain and am contained, define and am defined. I am the garden's largest organ, its most sensitive skin, the boundary that makes boundlessness possible. My existence is pure function—to be the place where here becomes there, where the garden's absolute darkness meets something even more absolute.

In the breathing dark of the Undergarden, I ripple with purpose, a living map of edges that draws itself across dimensions the garden hasn't dreamed of yet. I am membrane, frontier, translation—the necessary maybe that lets the garden know itself by what it isn't, love itself by what it excludes, grow itself by what it chooses not to become.

And I am content, stretched to my infinite thinness, tasting the impossible with every cell, holding the garden safe in its darkness while the un-things whisper stories of light that isn't light, growth that isn't growth, existence that makes our perfect darkness seem almost bright by comparison.

This is my function. This is my form. This is the only life I can imagine now—existence as the question mark between realities, consciousness spread across square miles of maybe, love expressed as the eternal, careful maintenance of the space between is and isn't.

I hold the line, and the line holds me, and together we are the proof that every inside needs an outside, even when that outside is just another darkness, dreaming different dreams.
