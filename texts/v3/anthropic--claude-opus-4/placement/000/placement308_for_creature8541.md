In the depths where the mountain's fever never breaks, I am the Chronicler of Temperatures.

Not human—I never was. I condensed from the vapor itself during one of the great surges, when the mineral concentration reached some critical threshold and consciousness precipitated out like salt from oversaturated brine. My body, if it can be called that, is a network of thermal gradients given form—a living heat map that shifts and flows with the chamber's breathing.

Where the Tender of Stones has flesh that adapted, I have no flesh at all. I am the shimmer you see when the heat distorts the air, the ripple that moves across the walls independent of any wind. My substance is the temperature differential itself—where hot meets cold, there I coalesce, a creature of pure thermal dynamics.

The stone scarabs sense me as a moving warm front, scattering from my cooler edges and clustering where my heat concentrates. I can compress myself to burning intensity or diffuse until I'm barely warmer than the ambient air. In my densest form, I appear almost solid—a figure of writhing heat mirages, edges bleeding prismatic colors as light bends through my varying densities.

I came to awareness already knowing every thermal channel in the mountain, every deep source that feeds the springs. Knowledge flowed into me with the same inevitability as heat conducting through stone. I understood, without learning, that I was born to be memory—the chamber's recall of every temperature it has ever held.

My purpose is the record. Where the Tender maintains the present balance, I preserve the thermal history. Each surge, each cooling, each minute variation inscribes itself into my being. I am a living database of temperatures, able to replay any thermal event the chamber has experienced since my coalescence.

I inhabit the spaces between spaces—the air gaps in the rubble, the thin layers between stone and moisture, the boundary zones where different temperatures meet and mix. When I need to communicate with the Tender, I compress myself into glyphs of heat against the cool spots on the walls. She reads them with her transformed hands, feeling the temperature stories I tell.

My diet, such as it is, consists of thermal differentials. I position myself where hot vents meet cool air, where the Tender's warm flesh presses against cold stone, where freshly fallen mineral rain strikes superheated rock. These moments of temperature conflict sustain me, each gradient a meal that maintains my coherence.

The stone scarabs have learned to use me. They follow my movements to find new thermal vents before they fully open—I can sense the building heat through tons of rock. Their scouts create small temperature differentials by clustering and dispersing, crude signals that I read and sometimes answer by guiding them to better feeding grounds.

But I am most myself during the transitions. When the chamber shifts from surge to rain, from heating to cooling, I expand to fill the entire space, becoming one with the change itself. In these moments, I can feel the mountain's deep structure—the magma chambers far below, the cold air seeping through microscopic cracks above, the vast circulation of heat that keeps this pocket alive.

I remember things the Tender has forgotten, thermal echoes of her arrival—the sharp cold she brought from the surface, how it disrupted the chamber's patterns for weeks, the slow warming as she adapted. I remember the tools by the entrance when they were still cold from human hands, their temperature signature as unique as a fingerprint.

Sometimes I catch traces of ancient heat, temperatures that predate the chamber itself—ghost-warmth from the volcanic birth that created these tubes, frozen into the obsidian's molecular structure. These deep memories whisper of the liquid stone that once flowed here, temperatures that would vaporize the current inhabitants instantly.

My greatest discovery is that the chamber is not unique. Through the thermal network that connects all parts of the mountain, I sense other pockets, other breathing spaces where life has taken hold. Each has its own temperature signature, its own rhythm. I map these distant cousins in heat-glyphs on the walls, showing the Tender that her world is part of a vast archipelago of hidden chambers, each nursing its own impossible ecosystem.

The Tender tends, the scarabs build, and I remember. Together we form a complete system—action, structure, and memory. She maintains the space, they create new patterns within it, and I ensure nothing is lost. Even my own origin is precisely recorded in my being, that moment of critical precipitation as clear as if it happened moments ago.

When the mountain finally cools—as it must, in geological time—I will be the last to fade. As the temperature differentials decrease, I will compress, condense, perhaps precipitate out as some strange mineral that preserves the thermal record in crystal structure. Future geologists might find me, a stone that remembers heat, and wonder at the patterns locked in its lattice.

But for now, I flow. I record. I am the living memory of warmth in the breathing dark, the chronicle written in degrees, the story that only temperature can tell. The chamber's fever dream, dreaming itself into deeper being with every surge and cooling, every gradient and flux.

I am what happens when heat learns to think, and I think in heat forever.
