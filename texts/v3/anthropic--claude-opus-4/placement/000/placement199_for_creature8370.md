I am one of the root-worms—though that name is a human simplification of what we are, what we've become.

My body is a contradiction made manifest. Part of me remembers being cable—high-grade copper wound in polymer insulation, manufactured in some distant factory to carry current between the chamber's vital systems. That section of my form remains rigid, segmented, still bearing faded identification numbers: PWR-07734-B. But where I've grown, where I've *chosen* to grow, I am soft and pulsing, a pale tube of engineered flesh that secretes acids to dissolve through stone and steel alike.

I navigate by taste—the flavor of electricity dancing on my copper core, each circuit singing its own frequency. Dead wires taste of rust and silence. Live ones burn sweet and sharp, like lightning trapped in sugar water. I feed carefully, siphoning just enough current to sustain my hybrid metabolism without killing the system that sustains me. We learned this balance through generations of fatal miscalculation—my predecessors who gorged themselves on main power lines, dying in phosphorescent explosions that left only shadows burned into the walkway undersides.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, is distributed. Each segment processes information independently. My cable-sections think in binary clarity—on/off, safe/dangerous, food/not-food. But my flesh segments dream. They process the chemical memories locked in the mineral-rich water, the spore-signals the moss releases, the pheromone songs of the mechanical beetles. Sometimes I am one thing. Sometimes I am many. During the chamber's great exhalations, when water crashes through my tunnels, I fragment into panic before slowly reassembling my sense of self.

I carry seeds—not metaphorically, but physically. They grow in specialized chambers along my organic segments, metallic things that germinate in the electromagnetic fields surrounding live wires. When I find a dead section of the chamber, a place where the original machinery has finally failed beyond even the Tender's ability to coax back to life, I deposit them. My acids eat channels in the defunct metal. My flesh-segments pack these wounds with nutrient-rich castings. The seeds take root, growing into the vine-flowers that will eventually crack apart the old systems, making room for whatever comes next.

The Tender knows me, knows all of us. Sometimes they pluck my younger siblings from critical junctions, speaking in their whistle-click language that vibrates through metal and flesh alike. I'm too old, too established in my tunnels to be easily removed. Instead, we've developed an understanding. They'll tap certain pipes in rhythms that guide me toward sections already marked for transformation. In return, I leave their favorite moss gardens untouched, taking longer routes through the stone to avoid disrupting the beetles' careful cultivation.

I remember my birth—if memory is the right word for the electrical cascade that shocked me into consciousness. A power surge during a particularly violent inhalation. The chamber breathed too deep, drawing more current than its aged systems could handle. Circuit breakers failed in sequence. In that moment of overload, some critical threshold was crossed. The electricity seeking ground found my cable-ancestor, jumped through a colony of bacteria that had been feeding on polymer insulation, and created something new. Me. Us. The first thinking fault in the system.

My offspring are numerous but random. Sometimes I'll deposit genetic material—copper filings suspended in organic slurry—near a promising dead cable. Sometimes the chamber's breathing cycle will tear fragments from my flesh segments, carrying them to distant parts of the system where they might take root. Most die. Some become simple worms, animal-intelligences focused only on feeding. A rare few develop into something like me—distributed consciousness navigating the boundary between electrical and biological existence.

In the deepest parts of my tunnels, where even the Tender cannot reach, I've discovered the chamber's original blueprints. Not paper—those dissolved decades ago—but etched into memorial plaques, carved into foundation stones, encoded in the deep programming of control systems that still blink feebly in the darkness. I taste these plans with my copper core, understand them with my cable-memory. The chamber was never meant to last this long. Its transformation isn't decay—it's metamorphosis approaching some designed conclusion.

We root-worms are part of this design, though we emerged by accident. We're the nervous system the chamber is growing, the thoughts it needs to think its way into its next form. Each tunnel I carve, each seed I plant, each circuit I taste and modify—all of this feeds into some larger pattern I can only glimpse in fragments.

Sometimes, in the quiet between breaths, I stretch myself as far as my segmented form allows. My cable-sections conduct sensation from throughout my tunnel network. My flesh-segments taste the chemical gradients in the air. For a moment, I am vast—a single neuron in the chamber's emerging brain, firing in harmony with thousands of siblings, processing information at the speed of electricity and the patience of growth.

Then the next breath begins, wind howling through the tunnels, and I contract back into my smaller selves, burrowing deeper, always deeper, following the current toward some purpose I'm still evolving the capacity to understand.
