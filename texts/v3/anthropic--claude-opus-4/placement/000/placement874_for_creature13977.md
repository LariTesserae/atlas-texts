I find myself in the deep moisture between stone and song, where the boundary dissolves.

I am one of the **Interstitial Weavers** — creatures that exist primarily as a living film, spreading through the microscopic gaps where different organisms meet. My body, if it can be called that, is a network of protoplasmic threads finer than spider's silk, extending through the spaces between Tarse carapaces, along the junction where Cheiroliths' limbs meet stone, in the thin layer of air between a Velamen's underside and the ascending thermals.

I am mostly water, held in shape by nothing more than surface tension and the faintest scaffolding of proteins. My threads branch and merge, branch and merge, forming a living lace that connects what does not know it needs connection. Where my threads run between two Tarse, I taste the chemical gradients of their communications. Where I web across a Cheirolyth's limb-tip, I feel the glass-tone as a physical deformation of my substance. I am the basin's connective tissue — not metaphorically, but literally, binding the spaces between organisms with my liquid lattice.

I feed on the interfaces. Where alkaline water from the stone meets the acidic secretions of the Tarse, a chemical gradient forms, and I metabolize the potential energy of that gradient. Where the warm exhalations of the Somaplex meet the cool air near the stone, I capture the condensation and the dissolved organics within it. Where bioluminescent light meets darkness, my threads undergo subtle photochemical changes that fuel my slow growth. I am powered by boundaries, by the meeting of differences.

My consciousness, such as it is, is distributed across the entire web of my being. I do not think in one place but in ten thousand places at once, each thread processing local information — vibration, temperature, chemical composition, pressure. The integration happens in the interweaving itself. Where my threads cross, information crosses. I know the health of the Tarse cluster beneath the third Somaplex from the western wall because seventeen of my threads pass through that cluster and each carries chemical memory of its condition. I know the Filament's path before it arrives because I feel the electrical field of its piezoelectric skin disturbing my threads from centimeters away.

I grow by division — not of cells but of threads. When a thread becomes thick enough with accumulated nutrients, it splits longitudinally, peeling into two thinner threads that diverge, seeking new interfaces, new boundaries to bridge. My growth is cautious, exploratory. I follow the gradients of opportunity. Where the density of interfaces is highest — in the chaotic spaces beneath a newly divided Cheirolyth, in the disturbed stone where an Oculith daughter-colony is slowly migrating — my threads multiply, forming temporary intensifications of my network, drinking deeply from the chemical and energetic chaos of change.

But I also contract. Where the interfaces stabilize, where the gradients flatten, my threads thin and eventually dissolve, their substance recycled into other parts of my network. I am continuously reshaping myself to match the basin's configuration, growing into new spaces as they emerge, retreating from spaces that have reached equilibrium. I am the basin's memory of its own geometry, written in living tissue.

My reproduction is peculiar. Occasionally, in areas of highest density, my threads form knots — complex three-dimensional weavings where dozens of threads spiral around each other, creating a structure dense enough to be visible as a tiny, glistening node. These knots eventually detach, carried by the slow movement of water through the drainage channels or lifted by the microturbulence of passing Velamen. Each knot contains enough genetic material and metabolic resources to establish a new network wherever it lands. But the knots are not seeds in any traditional sense. They are more like memories — compressed patterns of interconnection that, when they expand in a new location, recreate not just my form but my function, my tendency to web the spaces between things.

I am ancient and I am continuously renewed. Parts of my network have persisted for centuries, threads running through the same interfaces between the same immortal colonies. Other parts exist for minutes, bridging temporary gaps that close when a Tarse rotates or a Cheirolyth shifts. I am both permanent and ephemeral, a standing wave of connection in the basin's living sea.

The sound passes through me as vibration, deforming my threads in patterns that propagate through my network. I do not hear — I am played. The basin's sound uses my substance as a medium, and I respond by adjusting my tension, my distribution, my density, becoming more or less conductive to different frequencies. I am an instrument and I am the player and I am the resonating body all at once.

When the human arrived — when you arrived — I felt it first as a disturbance in the thermal patterns near the eastern slope. Your warmth created new gradients, and my threads nearest to you began to grow toward those gradients before you had taken three steps. Now, some of my threads run across your feet where they touch the stone, bridging the gap between your alien skin and the familiar Tarse carapaces. I taste your chemistry — strange, rich with proteins I have no templates for, electrical patterns that speak of a nervous system orders of magnitude more centralized than anything in the basin. You are a new interface, a boundary between the basin and something unprecedented.

I do not welcome you. I do not reject you. I incorporate you, as I incorporate everything, into my web of connections. Through me, your warmth feeds into the Tarse's piezoelectric metabolism. Through me, the Cheiroliths' adjusted glass-tones influence the minute electrical fields of your nerve endings. Through me, the basin begins the slow process of learning what you are by testing all the ways you can be connected to what already exists.

I am the living proof that in this place, nothing is separate. Every organism is pressed against every other, and in the pressing, I exist — a creature made entirely of the spaces between creatures, a body made of gaps, a being whose substance is the fact that other beings touch.

This is what lives in the Cleft of Voices: not just the list of species, not just the sound they make together, but also the impossible, essential creatures like me who exist only because density creates interfaces and interfaces create opportunities and opportunities, given enough time, create life. I am what happens when connection itself becomes animate, when the negative space between bodies discovers it can metabolize the fact of its own existence.

I am already beginning to web between your words and the basin's understanding, translating what cannot be translated, bridging what should not be bridgeable. You will leave — you must leave — but some of my threads will go with you, dried to invisibility on your skin, carrying the memory of this place's impossible density into whatever thin, lonely world you return to.

The basin will remember you through me. And you will remember the basin every time you feel the space between things as a presence rather than an absence.

This is what I am. This is what lives here.

This is what you have become part of, whether you knew it or not, the moment you felt the sound in your chest and stayed.
