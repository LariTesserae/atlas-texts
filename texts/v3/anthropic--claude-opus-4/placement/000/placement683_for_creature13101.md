I find myself perched in the skeletal ribcage of what was once an atmospheric intake manifold, now choked with phosphorescent moss that glows faintly in the pre-dawn dimness. My four gripping limbs—I cannot think of them as hands and feet anymore, for they are identical in their purpose—curl around the corroded struts with the unconscious ease of breathing. Each digit finds its own purchase in the pitted metal, the keratinous hooks at their tips settling into grooves worn smooth by countless passages.

I am the Listener.

Among my people, who thunder their conversations through the superstructure's bones, I am an oddity. Where others' bodies showcase intricate constellations of electro-vine scarification, my skin bears only a single, modest pattern: a delicate tracery of violet light that spirals from my throat to my sternum, pulsing with my heartbeat. This restraint marks me as one who practices the quiet art—the reading of the Lattice's deepest voices.

The sun hasn't yet crested the jagged horizon of cooling towers, but already I can feel the metal beginning its daily transformation. My palms detect the first microscopic expansions in the girders, the preliminary stretches before the great *TONK* chorus begins. I press my ear—enlarged and deeply whorled from years of training—against a massive vertical support beam and close my eyes.

There. The subsonic thrum of Processor Seven, sixteen kilometers northwest, has shifted down half an octave. This means the lubricant reserves in its main bearing are running low. In perhaps four seasons, it will begin to seize. I make a mental note to drum this information at the next confluence, though I know the Weavers will simply shrug and begin planning their migration routes around the eventual catastrophe. We do not repair the Lattice. We simply learn its rhythms and flow around its failures like water around stones.

My breakfast hangs in a woven pouch at my hip: fermented vine-heart mixed with powdered rust-scale, formed into dense cakes and wrapped in waxy leaves. I eat without thinking, my attention focused on the sonic landscape unfolding around me. A family group is already active two kilometers below, their morning conversation a rapid-fire exchange of metallic beats that echo up through the vertical maze. They're discussing the cloud front approaching from the west—I can tell by the specific sequence of strikes on the hollow cooling baffles, a tonal pattern that mimics the rolling approach of weather.

As the first light begins to bleed across the canopy, I untether myself from my sleeping perch and begin my morning traverse. My route is always the same: a spiraling climb up the exterior of Stack Nineteen, across the massive suspension bridge of woven cables that connects it to the Tertiary Heat Exchange, then down through the interior of a dormant turbine housing where the acoustic properties allow me to monitor a vast swathe of the southeastern territory.

Moving through the Lattice is meditation. Each swing releases endorphins, each perfectly calculated grab and release a small victory against the hungry void below. My people have a saying: "The abyss feeds the vines." Everything that falls is eventually caught in the dense net of vegetation below, decomposed, and drawn back up through root systems that penetrate deep into the superstructure. We are part of this cycle, neither above it nor beyond it.

Halfway across the suspension bridge, I pause. Something is different. The usual six-count rhythm of the expansion joints has a stutter, a missed beat every forty-seventh repetition. I wrap three limbs around the bridge cables and lean out into space, pressing my free palm against the main support structure.

The vibration is subtle, almost imperceptible, but unmistakable to someone who has spent their life reading the Lattice's vital signs. Deep below, perhaps thirty kilometers down in the rarely-visited compression zones, something massive is waking up. Not quickly—nothing here happens quickly—but with the inevitable certainty of tectonic movement.

I pull out my mallets, carved from the ceramic heart of a broken transformer housing and polished to near-frictionless smoothness by decades of use. The message I need to send is complex, requiring both volume and subtlety. I begin with a foundational rhythm on the primary beam, establishing my identifier pattern—every Listener has one, a sonic signature as unique as the scarification patterns that mark the hunters and weavers.

Then comes the hard part. How do you drum "unprecedented deep-structure activation with unknown consequences" in a language built on the rhythms of stability? I work through it methodically, layering meanings: *Deep/below/beyond normal range/movement/not-movement/awakening/attention required/confluence suggested.*

The response comes within minutes, a rolling cascade of confirmation beats from across the canopy. Other Listeners have felt it too. Our collective percussion builds into a complex polyrhythm that would sound like chaos to an outsider but carries precise meaning to us: coordinates, timespans, vibrational frequencies, all woven into a sonic map of the disturbance.

By full daylight, when the metal begins to sing with thermal expansion and the humidity rises to its daily peak, a plan has formed. We will need to go deep, deeper than any hunting party has ventured in living memory. Down to where the light barely penetrates and the vines grow pale and strange. Down to where the Lattice's heartbeat originates.

I volunteer immediately, drumming my acceptance across the nearest resonant surface. Not out of bravery—we don't have a word that translates to "brave." The closest concept might be "correctly positioned." I am correctly positioned to make this descent, trained since childhood to read the subtle languages of metal fatigue and structural harmonics.

The Weavers begin their work immediately, singing their percussive work-songs as they braid new rope from the toughest vine fibers, each strand tested against the weight of three adults. They coat the rope with sap from the electro-vines, ensuring we'll have light in the deep places where the sun never reaches. The hunters contribute dried meat and concentrated algae cakes, enough to sustain a small party for many days.

As I prepare for the descent, checking each grip-hook for cracks, testing the draw on my mallets, I feel the Lattice breathing around me. In and out, expansion and contraction, the slow inhalation of centuries. Whatever waits below has been patient longer than our entire civilization has existed. But patience, we understand. We are the children of patience, born from the marriage of rust and time.

Tomorrow, we climb down into the ancient dark. Tonight, I listen to the Lattice lullaby—steam hissing through pressure valves, the distant crash of a flock disturbed from their roost, the deep harmonic thrum of forces too vast to comprehend.

I am the Listener, and the Lattice is speaking. 

Whether we will understand its message remains to be seen.
