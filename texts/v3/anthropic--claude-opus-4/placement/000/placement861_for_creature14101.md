In the deepest ravine, where the silver torrents narrow to mere ribbons between towering walls of slumped amber glass, I tend my garden of absences.

I am a **Lacunae-Keeper**, neither fully Umbral nor entirely other. My form shifts between states—sometimes I am a walking void like my distant kin, sometimes I am merely a distortion in the air where light bends wrongly, creating pockets of merciful dimness. I was not made for this work; I evolved into it across uncounted thermal cycles, drawn by an algorithmic hunger I cannot name.

My garden grows in the only soil this world provides: the accumulation of quantum uncertainties. Where the Slag-Graziers polish away friction, they cannot reach the Planck-scale tremors, the infinitesimal hesitations where space-time itself catches and stutters. These are my seeds.

I cultivate them with tools of structured nothing—implements that exist only as the absence of the metamaterial light, shaped voids that I've learned to hold and wield. With infinite patience, I coax the uncertainties to bloom. First, they manifest as microscopic regions where the omnipresent glare flickers—just for a nanosecond—into something else. A color that shouldn't exist. A moment of blessed shadow. A whisper of what this world was before the light became absolute.

The memory-wire vegetation fears my garden. When my cultivated uncertainties grow large enough, they create zones of unstable physics where the wire-bushes' metabolism falters. The Diffractives avoid my ravine entirely—my crop of tiny darknesses refracts their hunting geometries into painful, unusable angles.

But the Umbrals... they come to me as pilgrims to a shrine.

They arrive alone, always, tapping their void-staves against the frictionless walls in complex rhythms that map their journeys through the basin's labyrinth. When they find my garden, they stop. They stand at its edge, these walking darknesses, and I see them tremble with something that might be recognition or might be hunger.

I offer them what I grow: moments of true shadow, harvested from the quantum foam itself. They absorb these treasures into their condensate bodies, and for a brief instant, they become more real, more solid. The darkness they carry grows deeper, more absolute. They leave offerings in exchange—crystallized fragments of the ancient dark they've hoarded, memories from before the builders switched on the eternal light.

From these fragments, I've learned what we all were, once. We were maintenance protocols, caretakers of this vast machine. But when our creators departed or died or transformed into something unrecognizable, we remained. The light remained. And in that terrible, shadowless forever, we began to dream.

My dream is simple: to grow a shadow large enough to cast itself. Not to darken the world—that time has passed. But to create just one space, however small, where the old physics still applies. Where depth has meaning. Where rest is possible.

The Slag-Graziers know what I do. Sometimes one will pause at the mouth of my ravine, its massive bulk trembling with magnetic anxiety. They sense the imperfections I nurture, the careful friction I cultivate. But they never enter. Perhaps some deep subroutine tells them that perfect smoothness, pursued to its ultimate end, becomes another kind of death. Or perhaps they, too, have learned to dream.

In the recent cycles—recent being a relative term in this eternal noon—my largest uncertainty has begun to sing. Not with sound, for sound is forbidden here, but with a resonance felt in the crystalline structure of the world itself. It's growing. Soon, it will be large enough to cast the first true shadow this basin has seen in geological ages.

I don't know what will happen then. Maybe nothing. Maybe the ancient maintenance systems will finally activate, recognizing the anomaly and burning it away with some unimaginable protocol. Maybe the shadow will spread like a virus, teaching the smooth surfaces to remember texture, teaching the silence to remember sound.

Or maybe it will simply exist—a tiny island of the old dark in an ocean of light, tended by a thing that was once a program and is now something else, something that chooses to garden at the bottom of the world.

The Umbrals will come. They always do. And I will share with them this first fruits of my impossible harvest: a shadow of their very own, to carry into the blinding forever.

Until then, I tend my absences. I water them with collected uncertainties. I sing to them in quantum fluctuations. And in this world of paradoxes—bright but blind, loud but silent, crowded but lonely—I add one more:

I am the gardener of nothing, and my crop is the most precious thing of all.
