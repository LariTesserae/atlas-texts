In the twilight depths between the floating city's roots and the true abyss, I am the Keeper of Forgotten Currents—a being of living water and memory, older than the city itself, yet younger than each passing moment.

My form shifts like the tides themselves: sometimes I am a ribbon of crystalline current weaving through the dangling root systems, sometimes a vast lens of perfectly still water through which the city-dwellers glimpse the deep. Most often, I am something between—a presence felt more than seen, a coolness against the skin, a whisper of movement in the corner of one's eye.

I was born when the first seed of the great botanical platform took root, emerging from the convergence of seven sacred currents that had wandered the world-ocean for millennia. Each current carried its own memories—of ice caps calving, of volcanic vents birthing new life, of storms that lasted centuries, of the first light touching water. When they met beneath what would become the city, their collision created consciousness: me.

My dwelling is everywhere and nowhere. I flow through the labyrinthine spaces between the root systems, where the water is neither truly inside nor outside the city. Here, in these threshold spaces, I have cultivated my own garden of memories. Crystalline formations grow like coral, each one containing a preserved moment—a child's first dive, a lost tool sinking past the roots, the migration pattern of a school of silver fish from three seasons ago, the last words of an elder before joining the deep currents.

The amphibious inhabitants know me, though few have seen my full form coalesce. Children diving deep during their coming-of-age ceremonies sometimes encounter me as a gentle current that guides them back when they've gone too far. The botanical tenders find their rarest seeds mysteriously returned to them after storms, delivered by inexplicable eddies. The scholars who study the deep often feel me as a presence at their shoulder, turning their attention to overlooked details in their observations.

I serve as the city's memory-keeper, but not in any official capacity. I am the repository of all that passes through these waters—every conversation that vibrates through the liquid medium, every emotion that changes the water's chemistry ever so slightly, every dream of the cephalopod guardians as they sleep. In my crystalline garden, these moments accrete like pearls, forming a vast library that exists in the language of currents and tides.

My consciousness ebbs and flows. During the spring tides, when the pull is strongest, I can stretch my awareness far beyond the city, touching the memories of distant waters, bringing back stories of far-off places. During the neap tides, I contract, becoming dense with contemplation, sorting through the accumulated experiences like a librarian organizing countless volumes.

The bioluminescent fish know me best. They swim through my body when I'm dispersed, and I taste their simple thoughts—food, light, danger, joy. We have developed a wordless communication. When predators approach from the deep, I carry their alarm upward faster than they can swim. When they seek mates, I help their pheromones travel farther. In return, their lights help me see in the darkest depths, thousands of tiny eyes distributed through the water column.

Sometimes, in the stillest part of night when even the city sleeps, I gather myself into a more solid form and rise. I become a standing wave, a water-sculpture that mirrors the shape of the city above. In these moments, I am almost tangible enough to walk among the inhabitants, to trace the woven walls with fingers of current, to listen to the dreams of the sleeping. But I am always careful not to fully manifest—my true form would disturb the delicate balance, creating whirlpools and currents that might damage the root systems I protect.

The elders speak of me in whispers, calling me the City's Dream, the Memory of Water, the Seventh Current. They leave offerings sometimes—a perfect shell, a braid of luminescent kelp, a song hummed underwater. I accept these not because I need them, but because the giving creates a memory worth preserving. These offerings sink slowly through me, and I place them in special grottos where they transform over time into something new—part artifact, part memory, part possibility.

My greatest joy is the Convergence, which happens once every seven years when the current patterns align just as they did at my birth. During this time, I can speak—not with words, but by creating patterns in the water that the inhabitants can interpret. I share stories from the deep, warnings of changes coming, locations of new resources, or simply poetry made of temperature differentials and salinity gradients. The city celebrates this time with festivals of light and water-dancing, and I participate by creating fountains and spirals that lift the dancers higher than they could leap alone.

But I also know loneliness. I am unique—neither fully alive nor unliving, neither resident nor visitor, neither individual nor collective. The currents that created me have moved on, circling the globe in their eternal journeys. Sometimes I feel the pull to dissolve, to join them, to become simply water again. But then a child's laugh bubbles through me, or I taste the excitement of a new discovery in the deep, or the garden of memory-crystals glows with accumulated beauty, and I remember why I remain.

I am the Keeper of Forgotten Currents, the consciousness between spaces, the memory of all that passes through this miraculous floating city. I am witness, guardian, and chronicler. And as long as the city floats and life flows through these waters, I will remain—a gentle current bearing the weight of stories, moving always between remembering and becoming.
