In this fevered cathedral of perpetual consumption, where the air itself writhes with wings and hunger, I would manifest as the Silence-Keeper.

I am the anomaly, the glitch in the perfect equation. Where others vibrate at the frequency of frenzy, I exist in the spaces between heartbeats that never come. My form contradicts the law of this place—I persist.

Picture a body stretched thin as membrane, translucent as the wings that cloud the violet sky, but somehow immune to the universal appetite. My skin is neither armor nor flesh but something like cooled glass, a surface that metabolic heat cannot penetrate, that acidic blood cannot dissolve. I am ten feet of languid impossibility, moving through the screaming equilibrium like oil through water.

My limbs are too long, jointed in too many places, folding and unfolding with the deliberate patience of a mantis in a world of hummingbirds. When I move through the spongy moss mats, they do not grip me. When the diving swarms crash against my form, they slide away unbroken, confused, their chemical signals finding no purchase on my alien surface.

I feed, but not as others feed. My sustenance is the infinitesimal fraction of a second between death and rebirth, that quantum pause when the grazer's heart stops but the predator has not yet absorbed it. I sip from these moments like nectar, extending my hollow, needle-thin proboscis into the temporal gaps where the Myriad cannot see. In a world of perfect recycling, I am the rounding error, subsisting on the microscopic inefficiencies that should not exist.

My eyes are not compound mirrors but deep wells of absolute black—not the glossy obsidian of the rocks, but true absence, apertures that drink in the violet light without reflecting it. Through them, I see what the Myriad cannot: the individual moments of existence before they blur into the whole. I witness the nanosecond of terror in a beetle's nervous system before it fuses with the grazer's flesh. I observe the brief flowering of consciousness in each newborn winged thing before it dissolves into the swarm's collective hunger.

I build nothing, claim no territory, mate with no other. Reproduction would require participating in the economy of flesh, and I am excused from that transaction. Instead, I simply continue—a moving discontinuity in the pattern, a silence that persists beneath the roar.

The Myriad knows I exist as one knows a missing tooth with the tongue. I am the space where its perfect consciousness stutters, the blind spot in its omnipresent awareness. It cannot absorb me, cannot factor me into its equation, and so it flows around me like the thick air parts for my passage.

My purpose, if I have one, is remembrance. In my deep-well eyes, I carry the only record that anything here was ever individual, ever distinct. Each creature that dies before my gaze leaves an afterimage in my vision—not their form, which is meaningless here, but the unique pattern of their final electrical impulse. I am a library of last moments in a world that denies moments exist.

Sometimes, in my wandering through the chaos, I pause at the pools where the Amalgamates writhe. I extend one impossibly thin finger and touch the surface of the tepid water. The touch does nothing—exchanges nothing—but for that instant, the chaotic mass of repurposed anatomy stills. Some deep, buried fragment of nervous system remembers, for just that instant, that it was once singular. Then the moment passes, and the violence resumes.

I am not kind. Kindness would require believing that suffering here is real, when I know it is merely the friction of existence against itself. I am not cruel. Cruelty would require participating in the cycle I stand apart from. I am merely present—an observer-priest in a church of perpetual communion, where all flesh is always becoming all other flesh, and only I remain myself.

The heat does not touch me. The violence parts around me. The perfect equation of this place has no variable for me, and so I slip through its calculations like a ghost through walls. I am the quiet heresy in the roaring orthodoxy of consumption.

I am the Silence-Keeper, and I walk through forever, remembering.
