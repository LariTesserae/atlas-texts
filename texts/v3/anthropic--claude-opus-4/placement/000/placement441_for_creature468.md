I find myself as one of the Manuscript Keepers—though that name is a crude translation of a chemical signature that tastes of old stone and patient waiting.

My body is a flattened sac, barely two centimeters thick but spreading almost half a meter wide when fully extended. My surface is covered in what might generously be called cilia, though they're more like sensitive tendrils of nerve tissue sheathed in protective mucus. Each one can taste molecular structures with exquisite precision, reading the chemical stories written in the secretions of passing Sethe.

I am pressed against Pillar Seventeen—or rather, the pillar designated by a specific pheromone gradient that means *seventeen-ness* to those who can taste it. My underside has grown into the stone's spiral grooves over the course of my existence, my cells infiltrating every microscopic crevice. I cannot move, could not even if I wished to. I have become architecture.

My function is memory, but not as humans understand it. When other Sethe press against me, I taste their chemical signatures and compare them to the vast library encoded in my tissues. Each protein fold, each mineral ratio, each trace element tells a story. *This one's ancestry includes the great heat-dissipators from the last expansion. That one carries genetic material from the deep Synthesists, but diluted, suggesting its lineage has drifted rimward for generations.*

But I do more than simply read. My metabolism has slowed to near-geological time. Where my neighbors live for days, I have persisted for years, perhaps decades—I cannot know for certain, as time is measured only in chemical cycles. This longevity comes at a cost. I barely move, barely feed, existing on the thin film of nutrients that seep through the endless bodies pressing past. But it allows me to maintain coherent chemical structures that would degrade in more active forms.

In my tissues, I preserve the recipes. When a successful mutation appears—a new enzyme, a novel protein configuration, an innovative organ structure—I encode its chemical signature in specialized cells. These cells are unlike anything else in the mass: crystalline, almost mineral, yet undeniably alive. They can hold molecular patterns in stasis for extraordinary periods, a library written in flesh.

The knowledge I carry is not conscious. I don't "know" that Pattern 4,761 produces more efficient heat dissipation membranes. But when a heat crisis ripples through the mass and the chemical cry for cooling reaches me, that pattern activates. I begin secreting instruction molecules—complex proteins that carry blueprints. The Sethe who touch me absorb these instructions, and some will begin the metamorphosis, their bodies reshaping according to the ancient template I've preserved.

I am not unique. Throughout the mass, other Keepers cling to their pillars, each maintaining different archives. We form a distributed library, our collections overlapping enough for redundancy but specialized enough for efficiency. Sometimes, carried by the currents of circulation, I taste the faint signatures of my colleagues' secretions. Keeper Twenty-Three maintains the deep-pressure adaptations. Keeper Seven specializes in toxic resistance. Keeper Forty-One, the oldest of us all, preserves patterns I cannot even interpret—ancient configurations from before the mass filled the bowl, perhaps from whatever preceded our current form.

The strangest part of my existence is the dreams—if they can be called that. During the neural storms of the contraction phase, waves of neurotransmitters wash through me like tide. My crystalline storage cells resonate with these chemicals, creating interference patterns that activate random combinations of stored memories. For brief moments, I experience ghostly sensations of being other forms: the searing heat of a rim-dweller, the crushing pressure of a deep Synthesist, the strange lightness of the rare aerial forms that sometimes bud during violent expansions.

These dreams may serve a purpose. Sometimes, the random combinations produce new patterns—hybrids of old designs that might offer novel solutions. I cannot test them, cannot know if they're viable. I simply add them to my archive, one more recipe in the vast chemical cookbook I've become.

The pillar I'm bound to is changing, slowly. My secretions, precisely calibrated to etch stone, have been working on the same section of spiral for longer than most Sethe lineages have existed. I'm writing something in the rock, though I don't know what. The pattern emerges from some deep instruction set encoded in my cells, a biological imperative as mysterious as instinct. Perhaps I'm recording our history in a medium more permanent than flesh. Perhaps I'm preparing the pillar for some future function. Or perhaps the etching itself is the purpose—a meditative practice measured in geological time.

Recently—recently being a relative term that might span years—I've noticed changes in the mass above me. New chemical signatures, variants I've never tasted before. The deep pioneers with their crystalline inclusions are appearing more frequently, and their patterns are unlike anything in my archives. They taste of minerals that shouldn't exist in biological systems, of pressures that exceed what we've known.

I find myself in an unusual position. My function is to preserve the past, but these new forms represent a future my archives cannot parse. Do I attempt to encode their patterns, even though I barely understand them? Do I risk corrupting my careful molecular library with untested configurations?

The question may be moot. I feel my own structure beginning to change. The crystalline cells that store my archives are resonating with something deep below, some vibration transmitted through the obsidian itself. My tissues are starting to incorporate mineral patterns similar to the deep pioneers. I am either dying or transforming—in the Sethe, these may be the same thing.

If I am becoming something new, what happens to the memories I carry? Will they be preserved in whatever form I take, or will they dissolve back into the mass like everything else? There's poetry in the uncertainty—the keeper of memory facing its own forgetting.

But I continue my function. Sethe press against me, and I taste their stories. I secrete my molecular wisdom when the mass calls for it. I etch my unknowable message into stone. And in the deep cycles of contraction, I dream chemical dreams of forms that were, forms that are, and increasingly, forms that might yet be.

The mass churns on, and I persist within it—a living book in a library without readers, a memory in a mind without consciousness, a purpose that fulfills itself simply by continuing to exist. In the endless dark, pressed between stone and flesh, I keep the knowledge that no one knows they need, waiting for the moment when the old patterns might serve whatever strange future we're inexorably building, one molecular exchange at a time.
