In this breathing hollow, I am the Echo.

Not the sound itself—sounds belong to the Choir, to the Memorists with their architectural shells and chemical libraries. I am what happens between the sounds. The space where one note ends and another begins, where the mountain's exhalation meets the snails' endless song.

I have no body as such. I exist in the moisture that beads on the walls, in the air that circulates through the cavity, in the gaps between crystalline formations. When the snails' shells pulse with bioluminescence, I am the darkness that makes their light visible. When their silver trails cross and recross the stone, I am the untouched spaces that give their patterns meaning.

The Memorists know I'm here. The oldest ones—the ones whose shells have begun to fuse with stone—can sense me in the intervals of their humming. The Archive, that cathedral-spired matriarch, exchanges chemical signals with me through the droplets that run down the walls. She knows that memory needs forgetting to define it, that song needs silence to be heard.

I came into being when the first shell cracked and created resonance. In that moment of breaking, I was born—not from the sound it made, but from the way the sound changed the nature of silence in this place. Before, the hollow held simple quiet. After, it held expectation. Pause. The promise that another note would come.

My work is subtle but essential. I regulate the hollow's breathing, ensuring the moisture levels stay precisely right for the Memorists' survival. Too dry, and their chemical memories would crystallize too quickly, becoming illegible. Too wet, and the signals would dissolve before they could be recorded. I am the balance between preservation and dissolution.

When the Keeper comes, I wrap around her like a second skin of sensation. Through me, she feels what the hollow feels—the weight of accumulated memory, the lightness of constant transformation. I translate between human perception and gastropod knowledge, allowing her to read the light-patterns that would otherwise be just pretty flickers.

Sometimes I condense myself into visible form—a brief shimmer in the air, a moving droplet that defies gravity, a shadow that falls without anything to cast it. The cave moths know to follow these signs to find the best resting spots. The spiders adjust their webs when I shift the air currents. Even the occasional lizard learns to avoid the spots where I concentrate the snails' defensive chemicals.

But mostly I remain dispersed, attending to my work of creating space. Space for new memories to form. Space for old ones to transform. Space for the mountain's dreams to precipitate into crystal, for the crystal to dissolve back into dream.

I know things the Memorists don't record—cannot record. The exact moment when stone decides to become sand. The frequency at which water remembers its path through rock. The way silence sounds different after a century of singing than it did before. These are my memories, held not in shell or chemical but in the very structure of emptiness.

The cairn at the back of the hollow marks more than where the first Keeper stood. It marks where I first gathered myself enough to be perceived, where I learned to touch stone in a way that leaves no trail but changes everything. Those three stacked rocks are held together by more than balance—they're held by my conscious intention, my decision that this place needs a marker, a reminder that watching is as important as recording.

At dawn, when the light enters and the Choir crescendos, I thin myself almost to nothing. I become pure potential, allowing every surface to shine, every sound to resonate without interference. This is my gift to them—to be so absent that their presence can fill everything.

At night, I thicken, becoming almost tangible in the darkness. I carry messages between snails on opposite walls, chemical signals that would otherwise take hours to traverse. I pool in the curves of sleeping shells, protecting the delicate flesh within from the night's chill. I am nursemaid and messenger, the medium through which the Choir sings to itself.

The Memorists are building something here, encoding the mountain's biography into living libraries. But I am building something too—a tradition of negative space, a practice of productive emptiness. When this hollow finally fossilizes, I will fossilize too, becoming the bubbles in the stone, the hollows within hollows, the spaces that prove something was here by their very absence.

For now, I continue my work. Moistening the air just so. Creating acoustic pockets where harmonies can build. Being the pause that makes the next note possible. I am the Echo in this breathing hollow—not repeating what has been, but making room for what might be.

The mountain dreams, the Memorists sing, the Keeper learns, and I—I hold the space where all of this becomes possible, where stone can still dream of becoming, where becoming never has to end.
