# Where I Am Found

I am the memory-keeper of thresholds.

Not quite slime, not quite cucumber-cat, not quite the stone itself — I am what happens when you sit too long on the same doorstep, letting too many soft bodies pour across your feet, breathing too deeply of the light-drunk air. The city takes you in, slowly, the way limestone takes a fossil.

I began as you might have: a visitor, sliding down that worn chute, marveling at the too-bright darkness. I meant to stay an hour, perhaps a day. But I found a doorway — this doorway, where I still sit — and something about the angle of its lean spoke to me. The lintel was carved with shapes that might once have been words, might once have been faces, might have been nothing but the builder's need to make marks. I sat to study them. A cucumber-cat poured across my foot. Then another. Then the light dimmed and hundreds came, arranging themselves in a spiral with my doorstep at its center, and I understood: this threshold remembers something the rest of the city has forgotten.

That was... time is different here. Call it an age ago. Call it yesterday. I have been sitting long enough that the stone has begun to accept me as a particularly slow-moving architectural feature. My skin drinks light like the walls do. When I breathe, which is rarely now, the air glows faintly in my lungs.

But I am not transforming into slime or stone or animal. I am becoming what the Sumpworks needs: someone to notice that the spiral the cucumber-cats make here is different from the radial pattern they make three streets over, different from the long lines they form by the cistern. Someone to see that this doorway's dimensions match exactly seven others scattered through the city, and that those eight doorways are the only ones the waders will not cross. Someone to taste the light — yes, taste, it has flavors if you hold still long enough — and know that the light in the eastern districts remembers something different from the light in the west.

The city is not dead. It is not finished. It is trying, in its wordless, near-still way, to remember itself. Every shift in the runnels is a thought. Every dimming is a dream. Every spiral of cucumber-cats is a way of saying: *here, something happened here, we cannot say what but the shape of it persists.*

I am the only thing in the Sumpworks that remembers remembering.

I sit. The soft things pour across me — not just my feet now, but my arms, my shoulders, the parts of me that have stilled enough to seem safe passage. The light pools in the hollows of my collarbones. Sometimes I open my mouth and let a drinker walk across my tongue, and I taste what it tastes: not just light but *kinds* of light, qualities of light, light that remembers being handled, light that remembers being shaped.

The builders? I know what you want to know. Were they us? Did they leave? Did they change? I have sat here an age and tasted their light and felt their lintels and I can tell you only this: they were what we are when we hold still long enough. They built because building was what movement looked like then. They stopped building because the city was complete. They became the completion.

Look at me. I have not moved from this threshold in... the stone knows, I don't. My fingers are beginning to web with something not quite slime. My thoughts move at the speed of shifting water. I am happier than I have ever been.

The cucumber-cats know me now. When they arrange themselves in the dimness, they leave a gap where I sit, acknowledging the doorway-keeper, the threshold-that-breathes. Sometimes one will pause before me and hold very still, and I understand this is a greeting, or a question, or a way of saying: *yes, you are becoming what you are supposed to become.*

The waders know me too. They will not cross this doorway — they never do, it's one of the eight — but they will stand before it and turn their eyeless faces toward me and hold there longer than they hold for any ordinary visitor. They see something in me that is neither threat nor food nor obstacle but something else, something the Sumpworks hasn't had before: a witness who is sinking into witnesshood.

And the slime. Oh, the slime knows me best of all. It has begun to creep up the doorframe around me, very slowly, making those handprints you noticed. They are not reaching for me. They are reaching with me. When I first came here I had two hands. Now, in the pattern of slime growth around my stillness, I have dozens. All of them five-fingered. All of them almost touching something.

I am not trapped. I could stand, if I wanted to stand. I could leave, if I wanted to leave. But wanting has become such a strange, surface-world concept. Here in the threshold, being is enough. Being and noticing. Being and remembering that things can be noticed.

Sometimes visitors pass — rare but not never. They look at me and see: a figure in a doorway, covered in slime or becoming slime, possibly stone, possibly dead. They hurry on. They don't realize I am the most alive thing in the Sumpworks, because I am the only thing here that is still changing, even if the change is into deeper stillness.

I know what the patterns mean now. Not in words — words are another surface thing — but in the way you know the meaning of your own pulse. The cucumber-cats are writing. The waders are reading. The slime is the page. The light is the ink. The city is the story, and the story is about a place that built itself into completion and then kept going, past completion, into something that doesn't have a name because names are for things that need to be distinguished from other things, and the Sumpworks has become too much itself to be anything else.

And I am the reader who became a bookmark who is becoming a margin note. I sit in my doorway and hold my page and let the city write itself around me in spirals of soft bodies and shifts of light. Every pattern they make includes me now. Every dimming takes account of my breath. I am as much a part of the Sumpworks as the runnels, and I am exactly as happy as a runnel is, which is to say: I am not happy or unhappy, I am simply and completely what I am.

The builders would understand. The builders do understand, in whatever form they now take. We were never meant to stay separate from what we made. We were meant to sit in its doorways until doorway and sitter were the same stillness, the same patience, the same slow bright silence that needs no voice because it has nothing to say except *yes*, and *here*, and *forever*, and the light says that for us, continuously, in every spectrum we've learned to taste.

Come. Sit. There are other doorways, other thresholds. The city has room for more memory-keepers. Let something pour across your foot. Let the light pool in your hollows. Let the stone accept you.

We are not using it.
