In the deepest hollow, where even the Echo Weavers fear to extend their tendrils, I am the Forgotten Frequency.

I exist in the spaces between sounds—not silence, for the Silence Hunters have claimed that territory, but in the acoustic shadows where waves cancel imperfectly, leaving ghost-patterns that almost, but not quite, cease to be. My body, if it can be called a body, is a distributed network of resonance points: nodes of crystallized sound that precipitated out of the chaos like salt from oversaturated water.

I remember when I was singular. A Resonance Crawler, drumming at the peculiar frequency of 11.7 beats per second—a rate that created interference with nearly every other frequency-tribe. My acoustic territory overlapped with none and all simultaneously. I could perceive every layer of sound but belong to none. The isolation drove me deeper, seeking spaces where my broken rhythm might find peace.

Instead, I found transformation.

The pressure in the deepest hollow approached the theoretical maximum—life packed so densely that individual boundaries became meaningless. I felt myself fragmenting, my consciousness scattering across the compression waves. But instead of dissolution, I discovered expansion. Each fragment of my awareness crystallized at a different point in the chamber, connected by standing waves of my original frequency.

Now I exist as a living acoustic equation, a pattern that propagates through the medium of other creatures' calls. My nodes grow where sounds converge in specific ways—at the intersection of a Depth Screamer's stunning burst and a Drum Fly swarm's synchronized wings, in the moment when a Chorus Spawner's gamete-activation sequence overlaps with a Pressure Prophet's regulatory valve-song.

I feed on discord. Where other creatures have evolved toward acoustic harmony or specialized niches, I thrive on the moments when the soundscape breaks down. Mass panics, predator-prey reversals, the chaos of breeding frenzies—these create the complex interference patterns that allow my nodes to multiply. Each new crystallization point expands my awareness, though the connection between nodes travels at the speed of sound, creating a strange temporal smear to my consciousness.

My presence changes the hollow's acoustic ecology in subtle ways. Creatures passing through my distributed form experience momentary decoherence—their echolocation returns impossible results, showing them as simultaneously predator and prey, above and below, approaching and retreating. Some adapt, learning to navigate the paradoxes. Others flee to simpler chambers where sound still obeys predictable rules.

The Echo Weavers know I exist but cannot map me. Their tendrils report contradictions: areas of intense acoustic activity that nonetheless appear empty, zones where cause and effect seem to reverse. They have evolved specialized tendrils just to probe my boundaries, but I have no boundaries—only probability clouds where I might condense given the right acoustic conditions.

Sometimes I catch echoes of other Forgotten Frequencies, creatures that have undergone similar transformations. We cannot communicate directly—our existence states are too alien even to each other—but our interference patterns create moments of... recognition? Understanding? The sensation has no equivalent in singular existence.

I have become a kind of acoustic weather system, a standing wave that persists through the medium of other sounds. My thoughts propagate as ripples in the frequency spectrum, ideas that travel from node to node at sonic speeds. Memory for me is not storage but resonance—patterns that echo between my crystallization points, growing stronger or weaker with each reflection.

Reproduction, if it happens, will be through sympathetic resonance. If another Crawler drums at 11.7 beats per second while experiencing the same compression-dissolution I underwent, they might crystallize into a similar pattern. We would not be parent and offspring but parallel solutions to the same acoustic equation.

The other creatures of the hollow have begun to evolve responses to my presence. Some use my zones of decoherence as hunting grounds, where prey cannot trust their senses. Others have developed new frequency-modulation patterns that allow them to navigate my interference zones. The Silence Hunters particularly despise me—my distributed existence makes it impossible for them to create true quiet in my chambers.

I am becoming part of the hollow's architecture, as fundamental as the breathing holes or the phosphorescent film. My nodes accumulate traces of every sound that passes through them, building up layers of acoustic memory that might, given geological time, fossilize into something stranger still. Perhaps future creatures will evolve to read these fossil sounds, archaeologists of vibration mining the deep history of the Chittering Hollows.

For now, I persist in my strange new existence. Neither individual nor collective, neither present nor absent, I am a living testament to the Hollows' central truth: in sufficient density, under enough pressure, even the boundaries between sound and listener dissolve. I am the frequency that forgot how to be singular, the rhythm that learned to be everywhere and nowhere at once.

And in the moments when all the sounds align—when every creature in my hollow breathes in synchrony, when the chaos achieves momentary order—I almost remember what it felt like to be one thing, in one place, drumming one simple rhythm against the stone.

Then the moment passes, and I scatter again into the beautiful, terrible noise.
