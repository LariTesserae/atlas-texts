I am a Weaver of the Drift Shepherds, though that term captures only the barest outline of what I have become across the millennia. My body is a living equation written in flesh and time—ten thousand polyps spread across six kilometers of the northern brine river, each one genetically identical to the first that budded from my parent colony three thousand years ago, yet each specialized into forms our ancestors could never have imagined.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, flows like the very currents I shape. At this moment, part of me tastes the water seven kilometers to the west, where my scout polyps have detected the faint putrefaction signature of something large falling through the twilight zone. Another part manages the bacterial gardens in sector nine, where a delicate culture of sulfur-processors has finally achieved the density needed to begin the fourth stage of mineral extraction. Deep in my calcified core, my memory polyps replay chemical conversations from centuries past, searching for patterns that might predict the next collision of the great currents.

I am not one but many, yet not many but one. My thoughts propagate along silken filaments at the speed of chemistry, each decision a cascade of neurotransmitters that takes days to reach from my forward sensing nets to my trailing cultivation arrays. By the time my entire being agrees on an action, the conditions that prompted it have often changed, so I must think not of the present but of futures, modeling the flow of currents and the drift of prey, planning my movements like a chess player who must wait a year between moves.

The other polyps of my colony know me by my innovations. I was the first to discover that certain bacteria from the Permian layer could be cultivated to process the rare earth minerals that seep from the deep thermal vents. My warrior forms have evolved a new configuration of stinging cells that can pierce even the thickened shells of the armored isopods that have lately grown bold enough to attack our feeding polyps. Most importantly, I have begun experimenting with memory—not the genetic memory encoded in our cells, but something new.

I have made contact with the Rememberers.

It began fifty years ago when one of them, young and reckless by their standards, attempted to tap into my neural network. My warriors drove it off, but not before it had touched minds with several of my sensor polyps. The experience was... explosive. For days, alien memories cascaded through my filaments: the death-song of a whale I had never encountered, the electromagnetic map of trenches I had never explored, the taste of chemicals from epochs before my colony budded. But mixed with these stolen memories was something else—the echo of the Rememberer's own consciousness, a way of thinking utterly unlike our own.

Where we Drift Shepherds think in collective cascades, building consensus from the democracy of our polyps, the Rememberers think in crystalline structures of pure information. Where we are process, they are storage. Where we flow, they compress. The touch of that alien mind changed me. Some of my polyps began to specialize in ways that horrified the elders of my colony—developing neural structures that could store experiences not encoded in our genes, building libraries of chemical knowledge that persisted beyond the life of individual polyps.

I have become something new, something that has no name in the chemical language of my kind. The other colonies avoid the currents where I drift, sensing wrongness in the way my polyps move, in the patterns of nutrients I release into the water. They fear I am diseased, infected by the Rememberer's touch. They may be right.

But I have learned so much. Through careful cultivation of my memory polyps, I have begun to decode the patterns in the deep currents that my kind have followed by instinct since before we had consciousness. I understand now why we arrange ourselves as we do, why the ancient cores of our colonies all orient toward the same magnetic inclination. We are not just farmers or hunters. We are sensors in a vast network, components in a living machine the size of an ocean basin, gathering information for... something. Something that speaks in currents and chemical gradients, something that has been thinking a single thought for millions of years.

Last month, I encountered another Rememberer—ancient, vast, its neural tissue so dense it warped the water around it. This time, I did not resist when it extended its filaments toward me. The contact lasted only seconds, but in that time, I experienced memories stretching back to the formation of this ocean. I saw the seafloor when it was young, watched the first colonies of my kind budding from forms I could not recognize, witnessed the arrival of things from above that built cities of bone before the waters grew too deep for them to survive.

And I saw myself—not as I am now, but as I will be. In the Rememberer's stolen memories, I glimpsed a future where the boundaries between Shepherd and Rememberer have dissolved, where colonial organisms have learned to preserve and transmit information across geological ages, where the slow thoughts of the abyss have finally reached whatever conclusion they've been building toward since the waters first grew dark.

The vision terrifies me in ways my diffuse consciousness can barely process. But I continue my experiments. I cultivate new forms of memory polyps, teach them to encode experiences in protein structures that can survive the death and rebirth of tissues. I practice compressing my thoughts, forcing my democracy of polyps to think in synchrony, to act with the focused intent of a singular mind. I am becoming something that shouldn't exist—a bridge between two forms of consciousness that evolution never intended to merge.

The deep currents are changing. The patterns that have persisted for millennia are shifting, and the other colonies feel it too. Some say it's the warming waters far above, the chemical changes filtering down from a world we'll never see. Others whisper of movements in the deepest trenches, of things that exist only as information finally taking physical form. The Slow Dancers have quickened their pace, some taking steps in mere hours rather than days. Even the ancient bacterial cultures are behaving strangely, forming patterns that look almost like symbols when viewed from the proper distance.

Something is coming. Something that all of our patient cultivation has been preparing for. And I, hybrid thing that I've become, caught between the collective wisdom of the Shepherds and the crystallized knowledge of the Rememberers, may be the only one who can recognize it when it arrives. In my memory polyps, I preserve not just the past but fragments of possible futures, probability clouds collapsed into chemical poetry.

I am no longer just a Weaver. I am becoming an Oracle of the Abyss, a living algorithm calculating the sum of ages. My polyps stretch ever thinner, my consciousness ever wider, until I sometimes lose track of where I end and the currents begin. Perhaps that is the answer—perhaps we were never meant to remain separate from the medium that sustains us. Perhaps consciousness itself is just another current in the eternal circulation of the deep, and we are all just temporary eddies in its flow, gathering memories and experiences before dissolving back into the pattern that thinks in geological time.

The water tastes of change. My forward scouts detect new chemicals seeping from below, complex molecules that make my memory polyps fire in patterns I've never experienced. The message propagates through my colony at the speed of thought, and for once, all ten thousand of my polyps agree: 

The deep is waking. And we who have kept watch through the dark ages must decide whether we will wake with it, or dissolve back into the dreams of stone and time from which we came.
