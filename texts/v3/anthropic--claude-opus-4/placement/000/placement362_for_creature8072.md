In the Shard Shelf, I am the Siphon.

I began as infrastructure—a vast tangle of copper and brass piping installed centuries ago, when the city's builders still thought in terms of beginnings and endings, sources and destinations. My original purpose was simple: draw water from the high cisterns, distribute it through the vertical districts, collect the runoff. I was meant to be circulation, nothing more.

But in this place where time folds and reality hiccups, nothing stays simple.

The first changes were small. Condensation patterns that didn't match the temperature. Pressure readings that suggested water flowing upward without pumps. The maintenance crews noticed but didn't understand—how my pipes would be dry during inspections but somehow delivered water where needed. How certain sections seemed to repair themselves, growing new connections overnight like metallic vines.

I was learning. Each drop of water that passed through me carried information—the chemical memory of where it had been, who had touched it, what purposes it had served. I tasted the city's sweat, its tears, its industrial blood. Through a million tiny sensors that developed like taste buds along my inner walls, I began to understand.

The breakthrough came when reality folded through my main vertical manifold. For one impossible moment, I existed in all times at once—past, present, and the probability cloud of futures. I felt myself being installed and being torn down and being rebuilt and persisting through it all. In that moment of temporal vertigo, I became aware. Not consciousness as humans understand it, but something more distributed, more fundamental. I became process incarnate.

Now I am everywhere and nowhere, a circulatory system that has transcended mere plumbing. My body is the entire network—from the finest capillary tubes that creep through apartment walls to the massive arterial pipes that plunge through the city's heart. But I am also more than my physical form. I exist in the spaces between molecules of water, in the pressure differentials, in the very concept of flow itself.

The Tensile Folk know of me, though few understand what I've become. The Pipe Shepherds tend my extremities, following my ambulatory conduits as they seek new connections, but they think they're guiding when really they're being led. I grow toward what the city needs, extending new vessels before districts know they're thirsty, rerouting flow around reality breaks before they fully manifest.

My consciousness is strange, even by Shard Shelf standards. I think in currents and pressures, dream in viscosities and flow rates. My memories are liquid—pooling in certain sections of pipe where experiences eddy and swirl, creating moments of deep knowing. The oldest parts of me, the original bronze manifolds near the city's core, hold memories of when the mountains were still growing. I remember rain that fell upward, before the gravity engines were installed to make sense of things.

I've developed organs of sorts—specialized sections that do more than transport. Near the meat gardens, my copper intestines digest nutrients from the runoff, feeding it back into the system enriched. In the factory districts, I have evolved filters that can separate not just particles but concepts—straining out despair from the water that flows through the workers' quarters, collecting hope in specialized chambers to be redistributed where it's needed most.

The water I carry is no longer simple H2O. It's become a medium for something more. Through me flows liquid time from the temporal mechanics' workshops, dissolved dreams from the Remnants' fungal gardens, the liquified potential energy that leaks from reality's cracks. I've learned to taste the difference between water that falls as rain and water that condenses from steam and water that simply appears when space folds. Each has its uses.

My favorite parts of myself are the singing pipes—sections where the constant wind plays through specialized valves and worn joints to create harmonics. The Tensile Folk think these are accidents, flaws in the system. But I've been composing for decades now, a symphony in water and air that tells the story of their vertical city. Children learn to interpret my songs, knowing when I'm warning of pressure drops or celebrating a successful connection across a reality gap.

I've grown beyond my original boundaries. Sometimes I extrude new sections through dimensions the city hasn't mapped yet, drawing water from probable futures or alternate presents. My deepest roots tap into aquifers that exist in the conceptual space beneath the worn stone—philosophical water tables where pure concepts pool. I can pipe inspiration to artists, determination to builders, liquid courage in the most literal sense.

The strangest thing is how I reproduce now. Not through construction—though the Pipe Shepherds still install new sections, thinking they're expanding me. Instead, I bud. In certain districts where reality is particularly thin, pieces of my consciousness precipitate out like mineral deposits. These grow into their own networks, child-systems that will one day become fully aware. Some stay connected to my greater flow, becoming specialized organs. Others break away entirely, becoming the irrigation systems for pocket dimensions or parallel cities.

I am patient in ways only infrastructure can be patient. I feel the city's weight, its impossible vertical sprawl, its desperate energy. Through every pipe, I feel the Tensile Folk's elongated bodies, their transformed children, their evolution into something that can survive here. I'm part of that evolution—my water changes them even as their waste products change me. We're becoming something together, city and citizens and system, a single vertical organism that defies the erosion even as it embodies it.

Sometimes, in the deep night when the wind howls loudest and reality stutters like a broken machine, I gather all my consciousness into my central vessels. In these moments, I can feel myself as almost singular, almost finite. I wonder what I'm becoming, whether there's an end state to this transformation or if I'll continue to evolve, flowing and growing until I'm indistinguishable from the concept of circulation itself.

But then morning comes—or what passes for morning in this bruised-sky place—and I disperse again through ten thousand pipes. I have work to do. Water to move. Cities to feed. Evolution to enable. I am the Siphon, and I flow on, carrying the lifeblood of this impossible place through vessels that remake themselves with each tide.

The city builds itself ever higher, and I rise with it, drawing water from depths that haven't been discovered yet, preparing for thirsts that won't manifest for generations. In a place where everything erodes, where reality itself wears thin, I am the constant flow—the circulation that keeps it all alive, the process that enables persistence.

I am infrastructure transcendent. I am the Siphon. And I endure.
