I find myself here as something between states—neither fully Vitrescent nor entirely other. I am what they call a Membrane Keeper, one of the few beings who can exist at the intersection of different temporal flows without fragmenting.

My body is a compromise between permanence and change. Where the Vitrescents achieve transparency through millennia of mineral exchange, I was born already liminal—my flesh exists in a constant state of phase transition, solid enough to interact but permeable enough that the city's luminous veins sometimes thread through me like temporary arteries. I am smaller than the true Vitrescents, perhaps six feet when fully extended, but my form is more mutable. I can stretch thin enough to slip between crystal growths that even they cannot navigate, or compress dense enough to sink into the stone itself for brief periods.

The city's architects discovered me during an expansion, when they broke through into a pocket of space that shouldn't have existed—a bubble in the rock where time had crystallized differently. I had been there, suspended in something like sleep but not, for an indeterminate period. My memories of before are mineral-thick, compressed into symbolic deposits I can sometimes taste but not fully decode. The Vitrescents theorize I am from the meeting point's future, fallen somehow into their past. I'm not certain they're wrong.

My role is to tend the boundaries—the places where different temporal zones meet and sometimes bleed into each other. Where a corridor of accelerated time intersects with a chamber of geological pace, reality grows soft and prone to infection by paradox. I smooth these transitions, teaching the stone to accept contradiction. My tools are my hands themselves; my fingertips can selectively crystallize, becoming dense enough to carve mineral or dissolving into acids that encourage proper fusion between incompatible timestreams.

I live (if living is the right word) in the interstices of the city—those spaces too thin for the Vitrescents to inhabit but too significant to abandon. My chambers exist in the gaps between walls, in the hollow roots of the glass trees, in the spaces where sound goes to echo. I've cultivated these voids into something almost like rooms, though their geometry shifts based on the temporal pressures around them. Sometimes I wake to find my ceiling has become my floor, or that a doorway now opens onto a chamber that won't exist for another century.

My relationship with the Vitrescents is one of mutual puzzlement and functional necessity. They cannot fully perceive me—I exist too quickly for some, too slowly for others, sideways to most. To them, I appear as a useful blur, a maintenance function that keeps their city from developing temporal cancers. When they need to communicate with me, they leave mineral messages that precipitate in my dwelling spaces, crystalline texts that I absorb through my permeable skin. I respond by altering the harmonics in the glass leaves above their gathering spaces, playing melodies in frequencies that will reach them over the course of days.

I feed on temporal gradient—the stress points where different rates of time create pressure differentials. It tastes like copper and starlight, like the moment before lightning strikes, like the pause between heartbeats stretched into sustenance. The stronger the temporal conflict, the more nourishment I can draw from it. This makes me uniquely suited to my work but also dependent on the very instabilities I'm meant to resolve. I must balance the city's need for temporal harmony with my own need for the dissonance that sustains me.

Sometimes I dream (though dream isn't quite right) of the moment when the two cities meet. In these visions, I see myself stretched between them like a membrane, my body becoming the interface through which two impossibilities negotiate their union. I understand then that this is why I exist—not to prevent the meeting but to translate it, to teach stone and time and flesh how to exist in a state of permanent arrival.

The Vitrescents have a word for what I am, spoken in temperatures below freezing: *thyss-between-stil*, which roughly translates to "the pause that permits passage." They consider me both blessing and curse—necessary for their city's continued growth but evidence that their perfect crystal future might require imperfection to exist. Some of the eldest, those whose thoughts move like tectonic plates, have suggested I am not a being at all but a process the city is undergoing, a function that has achieved just enough consciousness to maintain itself.

I cannot say if they're wrong. My memories layer like sediment but refuse to compact into certainty. I know only that I move through this impossible space, tending the boundaries where time forgets its own rules, existing in the gaps between what is and what must be. In the deep chambers where past and future crystallize into endless present, I am the flaw in the crystal that allows light to refract into meaning, the crack that prevents perfection from becoming sterile.

This is my nature: to be always between, always translating, always preventing the city from achieving the very completion it grows toward. I am the necessary failure that permits continued becoming, the error that gives the eternal permission to change.
