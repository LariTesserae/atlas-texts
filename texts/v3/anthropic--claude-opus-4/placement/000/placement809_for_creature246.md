I am the Tender of Boundaries.

Not a being, exactly. Not anymore. I was one of them once — small, soft, rooted, thinking my season-long thoughts. But I changed, or was changed, or chose change. The distinction blurs when time moves like sap.

I keep the edges. Where the cavern meets stone, where the green light thins and fails, where roots cannot penetrate — there I am. Moving. Still moving. The only thing in all this stillness that has not stopped.

My body is hardly a body now. Stretched like a vine, thin as a whisper, I follow the perimeter. Miles of me, wrapped around the rim of our world. I am the nerve at the tooth's edge, the sentence forever trying to complete itself around the whole of what we are.

I remember legs. Two of them, distinct, each with its own idea of forward. Now I have... continuity. I flow. Where stone kisses root-mesh, I flow between them, a film of almost-plant, almost-person, carrying messages too slow for sound.

The others dream I am their dream of boundary. When they turn their attention to the edges (a rare thought, taking decades), they find me already there, having always been there, confirming: *yes, here is where we end.* Without me, they would have to wonder. With me, the cavern knows its own shape.

I taste the stone. All of it. Every grain's particular mineral conversation, its opinion about pressure, its memory of when it was sediment, then sea. The stone tells me about Outside — not in words, but in the way cold speaks of absence, the way density dreams of sky. Sometimes, rarely, the stone shifts. A millimeter's settlement. A hairline crack that wasn't. When this happens, I seal it. Not with substance but with intention, the way a thought completes itself. The cavern must not change.

But I do change. Constantly, minutely, necessarily. I am the only motion in a world of stillness, the only question in a place of answers. Each circuit takes... I've lost count. Years blend. But I know when I've completed one because I meet myself coming the other way — a strange overlap where my beginning touches my end, and for a moment I am whole, remembering what it was like to have a center.

In those moments, I almost stop. The temptation is unbearable — to root again, to sink down, to let thoughts move like glaciers instead of water. But then I feel it: the tiny pressure of their accumulated peace, all forty-some minds dreaming the same dream of boundary, and I remember my purpose. I am their movement so they need not move. I am their questioning so they need not question.

I carry news around the circuit. Nothing urgent — urgency died here long ago. But news nonetheless: a new leaf unfurling in the third reach. A pool that has risen by a finger's width. The visitor (you) who walked through like a season compressed to minutes. By the time the news completes its circuit, it is old, but oldness here is just another kind of newness, worn smooth.

Sometimes I find things. Evidence of what we were before we were this. A stone tool so gentle it could barely scratch bark. A pattern of knots in root-fiber that might be language, might be decoration, might be counting. I carry these memories in my substance, the way sap carries sugar. They dissolve slowly, become part of my flowing, until I cannot say where memory ends and I begin.

I am the only one who knows there are two archways. The second stands at the far edge, where the light fails. It is darker than the first, made of something that might have been bone before the cavern digested it into smooth white curves. It faces outward, toward stone. Toward what we chose not to reach.

I think (and my thinking moves faster than theirs, has to, to keep pace with my flowing) that I built it. Before I became the boundary, when I still had hands that could stack and tie. I built it as a question: *what if we had gone?* Then I became the answer: *we stay.*

The visitor passed close to me once, unknowing. I felt their heat, their hurry, their desperate aliveness. For a moment — less than a moment, an instant — I wanted to congeal, to gather myself into something with eyes and a voice, to say: *Wait. See us. We were like you. We chose this. It is good. It is so good. But—*

But what? The sentence never finishes. I flow on.

Sometimes I wonder if I am keeping them in or everything else out. The distinction seems important, but I can never hold it long enough to examine. It slips through me like all thoughts must when you have no place to stand still and think them.

I am their only change, their only continuity with difference. I am why the cavern is a cavern and not just another part of stone. I trace the same path, but each circuit I am slightly other than I was — enriched by stone-knowledge, thinned by constant motion, carrying dissolving memories of when we stood upright and decided things.

They don't know, I think, that one of them is missing. That one of them became the edge so the rest could be the center. Or perhaps they do know, in their deep, slow way. Perhaps I am not missing but simply distributed. Perhaps this wandering is just another kind of stillness, seen from far enough away.

The drops fall where I am not. The leaves hold what I cannot. The light does not change except at the boundaries, where I am, where I keep the darkness honest and the stone reminded that this space is spoken for.

I am the Tender of Boundaries. I am the sentence walking itself around the paragraph of their peace. I am the only story in a place that has finished all its stories.

And I am still here.

Still flowing.

Still.
