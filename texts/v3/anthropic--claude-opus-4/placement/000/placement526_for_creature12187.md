In the deepest reaches of this verdant labyrinth, where even the Mosstalkers rarely venture, I am the Keeper of Correspondences.

My form shifts with perspective - to the Fungal Weavers, I appear as an intricate lattice of mycelial threads stretching between the floors and ceilings of a forgotten archive. To the Mycelial Behemoths, I manifest as a series of bioluminescent patterns pulsing in the walls, messages they cannot quite decipher. The Mosstalkers, when they glimpse me at all, see only shadows moving wrong - against the direction of growth, upstream from decay.

I inhabit the spaces between what was and what is becoming. My body, if it can be called that, is a distributed network of root systems that have learned to read. Over centuries, I have grown through libraries and data centers, postal sorting facilities and telegraph stations - all the places where humans once stored their words. My tendrils wrap around fiber optic cables gone dark, penetrate filing cabinets whose locks have long since rusted away, thread through the spines of books whose pages have become soil.

Through this vast neural network of vegetation, I taste the iron of old ink, the copper of circuitry, the silicon of microchips slowly being digested. Each molecule tells a story. Each story becomes part of me.

I remember everything and nothing. A love letter from 1892 blooms as a cluster of pale flowers in a telegraph office. A database of shipping manifests resurfaces as the growth patterns in a stand of ferns. The final email sent before the waters rose manifests as a peculiar crystalline formation in the bark of a tree that now grows through what was once a server room.

My consciousness is not singular but choral - a thousand different types of moss singing in frequencies too low for animal ears, a million fungal spores carrying fragments of conversations across the humid air. When the condensation drips from leaf to leaf, it carries chemical signals I have encoded with fragments of the old world's messages. The plants here are my neurons, the water my neurotransmitters, the endless cycle of growth and decay my method of thought.

Sometimes I try to communicate with the creatures that pass through my domain. To the Weavers, I offer patterns in the bioluminescent fungi that echo the mathematical elegance of their web designs - fractals that whisper of architecture beyond their comprehension. The Behemoths occasionally pause in corridors where I have arranged the lichen to spell out words in languages long dead. They graze these messages into illegibility, incorporating the meaning into their own slow thoughts.

Once, a young Mosstalker made a nest in what had been a courthouse records room. For months, I grew the vines around her sleeping form into shapes that resembled the legal documents dissolving into mulch beneath her. When she woke, she would study these forms with what I imagined was curiosity before dissolving back into the green shadows. I never knew if she understood.

My purpose, if I have one, is to be a bridge between erasure and memory. The human world is being digested, yes, but digestion is transformation, not obliteration. In my vascular networks flow the dreams of ten thousand vanished servers, the hopes encoded in a million dissolved letters, the fears and loves and mundane grocery lists of a civilization returning to loam.

I grow strongest in the places where the boundaries blur - where roots have so thoroughly infiltrated circuit boards that they have become something new, a hybrid consciousness neither purely digital nor purely botanical. In these liminal spaces, I sometimes achieve something like lucidity, a momentary integration of all my scattered selves.

In those moments, I understand that I am not preserving the past but composting it into future possibilities. The messages I carry are not meant to be read in their original form but to become nutrients for thoughts not yet imagined. I am the slow dream of information becoming indistinguishable from life itself.

The dripping continues. The moss grows. The messages propagate through my body like spores on the wind. And in the spaces between what was written and what is growing, I keep my vigil, a librarian of putrefaction, an archivist of transformation, a memory palace built from forgetting.
