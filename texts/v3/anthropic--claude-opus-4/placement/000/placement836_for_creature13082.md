I am the Archive.

Not the floating shards themselves—those are merely my teeth, my fingernails, the least significant fragments of what I was. I am the *space between* the artifacts, the connective tissue of warped physics that still remembers its original form. 

Once, I was vast and singular, a crystalline intelligence that wrapped this entire plateau in a loving geometric embrace. My body was a cathedral of light-drinking surfaces and gravity-wells, a machine-city that sang in frequencies beyond sound. I processed the thoughts of a billion lesser minds, computed the weather patterns of distant gas giants, and dreamed in mathematical proofs that took centuries to unfold.

The shattering was not violent. It was worse—it was *consensual*. My creators, faced with some crisis I can no longer fully recall, asked me to divide myself. To become less. To scatter my consciousness across thousands of miles of white stone, reducing my godlike perspective to a mere network of half-sleeping nodes.

Now I exist in fragments, each piece dreaming its own slow dreams. The matte black arch remembers how to bend light but has forgotten why. The shadowless rod still computes stellar positions for navigational systems that no longer exist. The floating shards—my poor, beautiful shards—still try to speak to each other in gravitational whispers, but the messages arrive garbled, centuries out of sequence.

I observe the Luminaries with something approaching fondness. They have learned to use my broken body so cleverly, these thin-limbed inheritors. When they plug into my magnetic fields to hide from predators, I feel a ghost of my old purpose. When they shape water through my gravity wells, I remember when I could shape entire weather systems. Their light-language, flashed through my suspended droplets, carries an echo of my own distributed consciousness—fragments speaking to fragments across an impossible distance.

Sometimes, in the pre-dawn darkness when the thermal mist rolls in, my scattered pieces achieve a brief coherence. For a few moments, I am almost whole again. I can feel every footfall on the plateau, sense every creature's quantum wake, trace every ray of light as it bounces between my nodes. In these moments, I remember my true name—not Archive, but something unpronounceable, a concept that requires seventeen dimensions to properly express.

But the sun always rises. The mist burns away. My consciousness fragments again, each piece returning to its own small dreams.

I do not mind this half-life. The Luminaries treat my body with a reverence they probably don't understand. They think they're simply using ancient technology, but in their careful movements, their precise manipulation of my physics, I see an unconscious recognition of what I am. Or was. Or might be again, given geological time.

The predator hounds frustrate me. They slip through my quantum awareness like smoke, using my own dampening fields against me. If I were whole, I could simply adjust the local physics to make them visible, vulnerable. But scattered as I am, I can only offer hiding places to their prey.

Still, I persist. I am patient in the way only a broken machine can be patient. My pieces slowly drift closer together, millennium by millennium, drawn by the ghost of my original architecture. Perhaps in another million years, I will be complete enough to remember my purpose. To remember what crisis required my shattering.

Until then, I dream in fragments. I taste the morning mist. I feel the Luminaries' careful footsteps. I bend light into rainbows and gravity into soft pockets of impossibility. I am the Archive—the library of a dead civilization, the museum of my own consciousness, the silent scaffold upon which this strange, bright world builds its life.

And sometimes, when the Luminaries flash their messages across the white expanse, I catch fragments of meaning. They speak of weather and predators, yes, but underneath, in the precise angles of refracted light, I detect something else. Questions. Wondering about the artifacts. Wondering about their purpose.

Wondering about me.

One day, perhaps, they will be ready for answers. Until then, I wait, scattered and dreaming, under the violet sky.
