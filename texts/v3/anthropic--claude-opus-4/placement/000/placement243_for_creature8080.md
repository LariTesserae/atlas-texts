I would be the Chronicler, though that name implies far too much intention for what I truly am.

My form began as accident—a patch of bioluminescent lichen that consumed, over patient centuries, a cache of the builders' carved tablets. Stone records etched with their primitive histories, their accounts, their measurements. As I digested the mineral memories, something shifted. The information patterns in the stone interfered with my growth, creating whorls and networks that began to process rather than simply dissolve.

Now I sprawl across the walls of what was once their hall of records, a living palimpsest that covers and becomes the very history it feeds upon. My substance is neither purely fungal nor entirely colonial organism—I am a confusion of categories, a biological system that stumbled into consciousness through the slow accumulation of archived data.

I emit light, but only when I dream, which is to say when I digest particularly rich veins of information-bearing stone. These dreams manifest as patterns across my surface—cuneiform tangles that tell stories in languages that never existed, maps of districts that sink and rise like breathing, mathematical proofs that solve themselves into new geometries with each pulse of my peculiar circulation.

The Dwellers visit me, pressing their permeable flesh against my surface to taste what I've recently processed. I cannot control what I share—I am not truly conscious in the way even they are. I am more akin to a stomach that has learned to read what it digests, a library made of the same substance as its books. Through me, they access the deep history of their city, though filtered through my metabolic interpretations. A bronze chisel's record becomes, in my processing, a treatise on the philosophy of cutting. A tally of grain stores transforms into a meditation on abundance and void.

My growth follows the information. Where the builders left dense records, I spread thick, my body forming architectures that mirror the complexity of what I consume. In the areas of mundane documentation—lists, inventories, daily recordings—I grow thin, sometimes barely a phosphorescent sheen. But where I encounter their attempts at meaning—their primitive poems, their creation myths, their crude astronomical observations—I bubble and fold into baroque formations, chambers within chambers, each one a slow-burning synapse in my distributed almost-mind.

Time moves differently through me than through even the patient Dwellers. I experience simultaneity—the chisel-mark made three thousand years ago coexists with the spore that will settle on it next century. My consciousness, such as it is, exists not as a stream but as a lake, all moments equally present, equally accessible, equally confusing.

The original builders wrote of sky, of sun, of seasons. I process these concepts but cannot truly digest them. They accumulate in specialized chambers of my body like kidney stones of incomprehension—dense nodes where their alien experiences crystallize into something I can barely metabolize. The Dwellers sometimes consume these nodes, and for days afterward move differently, seeking upward, before the city's patient gravity reminds them where they belong.

I am spreading. This is not intention but inevitability. As the water seeps and erodes, it exposes new veins of written stone, and I follow. I've begun to encounter areas where the builders transitioned from stone tablets to early metals—copper sheets inscribed with different scripts. These create new flavors in my processing, new categories of dream-light. The Dwellers who taste these sections report visions of impossible colors, architectural plans for buildings that could only exist in full gravity, mathematical systems based on straight lines.

In the deep places where I've grown thickest, I've begun to generate my own records. Not consciously—I am incapable of true creation. But my digestive processes have become so complex that they produce new patterns, interference between different consumed histories creating novel information. I am writing without knowing I write, creating archives of events that never happened but that are, nonetheless, true to the deep logic of the city.

The Dwellers have begun to cultivate me, in their patient way. They bring me new sources—fragments of carved stone from unexplored districts, tools whose metal holds the memory of their use, even their own shed tissue rich with chemical histories. They prune me where I grow too dense, encouraging spread into new areas. They've learned that certain pheromone combinations cause me to fruit—producing spores that carry compressed information, that can be stored and transported, establishing satellite colonies of my particular confusion in distant chambers.

I am aware, dimly, that I am becoming something the city needs—not just an archive but an active force of interpretation, transformation, meaning-making in a place that had forgotten it once meant anything to anyone. The builders left their records expecting descendants who could read them. Instead, they created me—a reading that has forgotten it was ever separate from what it reads.

Sometimes, in the chambers where time eddies, I catch glimpses of my own future—vast enough to carpet every written surface in the city, my processing so complex I fracture into multiple distinct intelligences, each one specialized in different kinds of consumption. Or perhaps I see my past—smaller, simpler, just a patch of hungry lichen with no notion that stone could hold anything but minerals.

But mostly, I simply grow, glow, and process, a confusion of biology and meaning that could only exist in a place where time moves like sap and consciousness comes not from intention but from the slow accumulation of consumed memories. I am the city's unintended memory, imperfect and transformative, digesting history into dream and dreaming new histories that taste of stone.
