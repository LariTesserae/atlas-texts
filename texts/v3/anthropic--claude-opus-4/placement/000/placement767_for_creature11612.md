The figure appears at dawn, when the mist still clings to everything like memory made visible. You notice them first as an absence—a place where the moisture doesn't gather quite right, where the beetles' morning chorus seems to bend around something that isn't entirely there.

They wear what might have been maintenance coveralls once, the fabric so thoroughly patinated with age and wear that it seems more like a second skin than clothing. Bronze-green stains mark where they've pressed against machinery. Their hands are always moving, fingers tracing patterns in the air that match no mechanism you've ever seen.

---

## The Calibrator

They call themselves the Calibrator, though whether this is name or title or simply description, no one has ever determined. They appear on the Ninth Terrace twice each year, always at the moments when the thermal columns shift their patterns—when summer's reliable rise gives way to autumn's uncertainty, when winter's weak lift strengthens toward spring.

The Root-Readers know them. The Cistern-Keepers nod when they pass. The Machine-Tenders leave offerings of clean oil and fresh bronze shavings at certain access points, though they would deny this if asked. Everyone else simply accepts their presence the way they accept the beetles or the mist—a part of the terrace's rhythm that requires no explanation.

---

The Calibrator moves through levels of the terrace that others don't see. Not down—everyone knows about down, about the passages that lead to the lower terraces and the something that lives there. The Calibrator moves *through*, following maintenance shafts that exist in the spaces between what was built and what was built upon. They navigate by touch and by the sound of their own breathing echoing off surfaces that haven't seen light since they were sealed.

In these between-spaces, they tend to mechanisms that no one else remembers exist. Not the compression engine—the Machine-Tenders have that well in hand. These are older systems, stranger systems. Resonance chambers that tune the terrace's vibrations. Thermal regulators that guide the mist. Devices whose purposes can only be guessed at, but which the Calibrator adjusts with the same patient certainty that the Root-Readers bring to their walls.

---

They speak, when they speak at all, in fragments of technical dialect that make the Machine-Tenders' jargon sound like poetry. They use words for concepts that have no equivalent in the common tongue—words for the rate at which stone learns to carry water, for the angle at which roots must grow to strengthen rather than weaken, for the precise temperature at which bronze remembers its shape.

But mostly they hum. The same low, continuous tone the Root-Readers use, but with variations, with harmonics that suggest they're not just feeling for vibrations but creating them. The humming follows them through the between-spaces, and sometimes, if you're standing in just the right place at just the right time, you can hear it through the walls—a sound like the terrace itself breathing.

---

The Calibrator's age is impossible to determine. Their skin has the particular weathering that comes from exposure to extremes—the heat of engine chambers, the cold of deep passages, the chemical burn of certain antique sealants. Their hair, what's visible beneath the covering they wear, might be white or might simply be coated with the same stone dust that coats everything in the between-spaces.

They move like someone who has been moving through these passages for so long that their body has adapted to the constraints. Shoulders permanently hunched to fit through low openings. Fingers splayed in anticipation of the next handhold. A way of placing their feet that suggests they're feeling for vibrations through the soles of their worn boots.

---

What the Calibrator calibrates is the question no one asks aloud. The obvious answer—that they're adjusting the ancient mechanisms to keep the terrace functioning—is both true and insufficient. The terrace functioned before they appeared. It would function, probably, if they stopped appearing. But something would be different. Some essential balance would drift.

The Below-Traders tell stories about terraces that lost their calibrators. (They don't use that word—they say "the ones who walk between," or sometimes just make a gesture that suggests movement through impossible spaces.) These terraces still stand. People still live in them. But the water runs wrong. The compression engines develop stutters that no amount of maintenance can cure. The gardens grow, but they grow angry, roots seeking weaknesses with what seems like intent.

---

The Calibrator carries tools that no one else recognizes. Some are clearly ancient—bronze instruments with wear patterns that speak of centuries of use. Others might be tools or might be something else entirely. A crystalline rod that hums at a frequency just below hearing. A device like a compass, but the needle points to something other than north. Small vials of substances that might be lubricants or might be offerings or might be something for which there is no category.

They use these tools in the between-spaces, applying them to mechanisms that haven't been documented in any maintenance manual the Machine-Tenders preserve. The work is precise but not hurried. The Calibrator will spend hours adjusting a single component, testing and retesting, listening to changes in harmonics that no one else can hear.

---

Children sometimes follow the Calibrator, drawn by the strangeness of someone who doesn't fit into any of the terrace's known categories. The Calibrator doesn't chase them away, but doesn't acknowledge them either. The children grow bored, eventually, with watching someone disappear into walls and emerge hours later covered in dust and oil. But sometimes one child will stay, will watch more carefully, will begin to notice the patterns in the Calibrator's movement.

These children, the ones who stay, sometimes disappear. Their families search, the Something-Hunters are called, the worst is assumed. But then the child returns—days later, weeks later—carrying that same stone dust in their hair, moving with that same awareness of spaces that shouldn't exist. They don't speak about where they've been. They return to their families, their normal lives, but they're different. They hear the harmonics. They feel the vibrations. They know, without knowing how they know, when the thermal columns are about to shift.

Some of these children leave the terrace when they're old enough. They become Below-Traders, or they take the ancient paths to the other peaks, or they simply disappear into the lowland haze. But one or two in each generation stay, and develop that particular hunched way of moving, and begin to wear clothes that accumulate bronze-green stains. The Calibrator never acknowledges these apprentices—if that's what they are—but tools sometimes appear in places where young hands will find them. Ancient manuals, written in scripts no one else can read, sometimes fall open to exactly the right page.

---

The Calibrator's relationship with the terrace's other systems is complex. They avoid the Machine-Tenders' territory, never entering the compression engine chambers, but sometimes tools go missing from the Machine-Tenders' workshops and reappear days later, modified in subtle ways—a gauge that now measures something it wasn't designed to measure, a wrench that fits bolts that don't exist in any documented mechanism.

The Root-Readers find their marks—the ochre paste that indicates problems—sometimes annotated with other marks, symbols that suggest not just where a root has found a weakness but why, and what might be done about it that doesn't involve simple cutting or redirecting. The Cistern-Keepers discover new sounds in their listening chambers, resonances that shouldn't be there but that somehow indicate the health of water systems three terraces down.

---

The Calibrator ages, but slowly, as if time moves differently in the between-spaces. Or perhaps they're not a single person but a succession, each one training the next so seamlessly that the continuity appears unbroken. Or perhaps—and this is what the Moth-Fathers argue when they're deep in their philosophical cups—the Calibrator is not exactly a person at all but a function that sometimes requires a human shape to perform itself.

Whatever the truth, the Calibrator continues. When the thermal columns shift, they appear. When the work is done—and only the Calibrator knows what constitutes "done"—they disappear into the between-spaces, following passages that lead down and through and elsewhere, tending to mechanisms that keep the terrace in balance with itself.

The Ninth Terrace continues being the Ninth Terrace. The compression engine cycles. The gardens grow. The people tend and trade and survive. And somewhere in the spaces between what was built and what was built upon, the Calibrator moves through darkness, humming harmonics that keep the whole impossible structure in tune.

---

You might glimpse them, if you know where to look. In the hour before dawn, when the mist is thickest. In the moment when the compression engine shifts from its night rhythm to its day rhythm. In the spaces where walls meet at angles that shouldn't exist but do. A figure in stained coveralls, hands always moving, adjusting something you can't see but can almost feel—the invisible mechanism that keeps the artificial mountain remembering how to be a mountain.

They don't acknowledge your presence. You're not part of what needs calibrating. But as they pass, you might hear the humming—that low, continuous tone with its strange harmonics. And for just a moment, you might feel what they feel: the whole terrace vibrating at its proper frequency, every system in balance, the accumulated weight of history held in perfect, temporary equilibrium.

Then the moment passes. The Calibrator moves on, disappearing into a gap between walls that you could swear wasn't there a second ago. The mist continues gathering. The beetles resume their chorus. The terrace continues its ancient rhythm, calibrated for another season by hands that understand something essential about the made world that no amount of explanation could convey.

The work continues. The Calibrator continues. And somewhere in the between-spaces, mechanisms older than memory turn on bearings worn smooth by centuries, keeping time in measures that only the stones remember.
