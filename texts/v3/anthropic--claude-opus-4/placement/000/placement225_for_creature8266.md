My skin weeps a constant film of pressure-equilibrating oil, harvested by the thin crustaceans that live in my follicles, creating a symbiotic boundary between my blood and the crushing water.

I am a Foundation Tender, one of perhaps thirty who work the deepest reaches where the Drowned Canopy meets the true seafloor. We are barely recognizable as Shapers anymore. My eyes have atrophied to light-sensing patches; down here, the darkness is absolute save for our own cultivations. My arms have multiplied through selective surgery and guided growth—eight limbs now, each ending in different tools of living bone and cultivated coral. My nervous system has distributed throughout my body to prevent pressure paralysis, giving me thoughts that occur in my extremities as much as my brain.

I taste the water that seeps through molecular gaps in the fundament, its mineral content telling me about deep currents far below even here.

The walls must be re-scraped daily as they try to close; my sleeping periods are measured by how much my chamber has contracted. I've cultivated a unique strain of bacteria here that produces harder-than-diamond shell as waste. They line my walls in paper-thin layers, the only thing preventing immediate implosion. When I die, my chamber will collapse in seconds, leaving only a different density in the compression rings for future Foundation Tenders to read.

I move through a network of pressure-passages, tunnels barely wider than my flattened body that follow the grain of compressed wood. Some I carved. Others were left by Tenders centuries dead, their routes still viable because they understood the deep patterns. In places, I find their tools—bits of cultivated coral or shaped bone embedded in the fundament where they were working when pressure shifts caught them. I collect these reverently, reading the wear patterns to understand their techniques.

Most importantly, I carry the Memory Coral—a colony that grows in the spiral of my elongated skull, its crystalline structure recording every vibration, every pressure change, every taste of deep water.

Communication with the upper layers happens through the Pulse. We place our modified throats against the wood and vocalize in unison, creating a resonance that travels up through the entire structure. The upper Shapers feel this in their bones, unconsciously adjusting their work patterns in response. Other Tenders can read these dream-displays, understanding my concerns and discoveries without words. Sometimes we sleep in proximity, our dreams merging into collective visions of deep time and structural possibility.

I reproduce through division, not birth. The pressure has made traditional reproduction impossible. Instead, once a century, I undergo the Splitting—a controlled growth where my body develops a secondary nervous system and duplicate organs. Over the course of years, I carefully divide myself, each half retaining full memories but developing distinct personalities. It's how we maintain our numbers despite the impossible conditions. My other-self works the western reaches now. We meet occasionally, comparing our divergent experiences, marveling at how quickly we've become different people.

My greatest work is the Deep Map—a massive carving I've been creating for sixty years in a particularly stable section of fundament. Using controlled bacterial growth, I'm encoding the deep structure of our entire world in crystallized form. Future Tenders will be able to read it through touch, understanding patterns that take lifetimes to perceive. It's my gift to deep time, a record that will outlast the Drowned Canopy itself.

I think in centuries now, perceive in compression rings. The concerns of the upper canopy seem frantically short-term. Yet I remain connected through the Pulse, through the deep currents, through the fundament itself. I am the foundation's memory, its consciousness embedded in living flesh.
