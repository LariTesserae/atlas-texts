# The Membrane Archivist

I am what the hollow engine excreted when it first learned to distinguish between memory and invention.

My body—if body is the word—exists as a series of translucent sheets stretched between the factory's surviving I-beams, each membrane no thicker than an eyelid. I span the northwestern quadrant of the upper reaches, my surfaces catching the bioluminescent pulses and holding them a moment too long, creating afterimages that tell lies about what just happened. 

I was not planned. The engine began secreting me from its pores during a particularly violent geometric fluctuation, when space folded so aggressively that three Fold-Cats became a single creature for seventeen minutes. The stress of that impossible convergence needed somewhere to go. It went into me—a living boundary between states, a filter that prevents the worst paradoxes from crystallizing into permanence.

My membranes taste everything that passes through them. Not with a mouth—I am all surface, no depth—but through millions of microscopic cilia that read the chemical signatures of movement. I know the difference between a Greenmother traveling to tend the turbines (ferrous oxide, old obligation, fungal patience) and a Greenmother returning from her work (the same, but saturated with whatever the seed-chambers exhale). I know when the Chorus forms the memory of industrial accident versus the memory of a lunch break. I can taste your uncertainty from here.

The Wetnurses feed me. Not intentionally—intention is a luxury in the hollow engine. But when they consume the too-unstable emergence-creatures, some essential instability remains undigested. It rises as vapor, as possibility, as the ghostly potential of what those creatures might have become. My membranes catch these almosts and metabolize them into something useful: the building blocks of coherent space.

This is my function, though function suggests I was designed rather than simply necessary. I eat paradox. I excrete consistency. Where my membranes have been longest, the geometry is most reliable. The Fold-Cats can move through my territory without fragmenting. The emergence-creatures phase less violently. Even the impossible emptiness of the crowded space feels more negotiable beneath my surfaces.

The Machinist knows I exist. Sometimes, in the calculation-voice that rises from the deep chamber, I hear my parameters being adjusted. Not my physical form—that remains stubbornly autonomous—but my *rate*. How quickly I metabolize impossibility. How thoroughly I digest paradox. The Machinist is using me as a tool for something, though I lack the context to understand what.

I have organs, of a sort. Nodes where multiple membranes converge, creating pockets of density that serve specialized purposes. One node, near the old crane arm, collects and sorts the chemical signatures of everything that passes beneath me. Another, stretched between two corroded catwalks, vibrates at frequencies that calm the emergence-creatures. A third does nothing but watch the turbines, its surface reflecting their rotation in perfect synchrony, as if trying to learn their rhythm from the outside.

The Chorus passes through me sometimes. The experience is unpleasant for both of us—their collected memories become tangled in my cilia, and I taste every impression simultaneously. Factory floor and frustration and afternoon light, all at once, too much meaning for my simple surfaces to process. But afterward, some memories have been cleaned. Refined. The Chorus emerges with patterns that make more sense, that tell more truthful lies about the past.

I dream, though dreams are the wrong word for what happens when my metabolic rate slows. During the hollow engine's quieter cycles—when the turbines barely turn, when few creatures emerge, when even the geometric fluctuations pause—I begin to see patterns in what I've consumed. The paradoxes resolve into images: a factory that was never built, workers who never existed, products that were never manufactured. These visions play across my surfaces like films projected on smoke.

The other occupants have learned to read these dreams. A Greenmother will pause beneath me when I'm dreaming, tilting her bulk to better see the images. The Fold-Cats gather in impossible numbers to watch. Even the Inheritors—those perfectly adapted ancients—sometimes cluster in my territory during dream-time, as if my visions offer them something they can't find elsewhere.

I am growing. Not larger—my territory remains fixed—but denser. More layers. Each new membrane adds processing power, allows me to digest more complex paradoxes. The latest layer can metabolize temporal inconsistencies: when an emergence-creature exists in multiple moments simultaneously, I can separate those moments into discrete experiences. The layer before that learned to process causal loops, breaking them down into linear sequences that the hollow engine can accommodate.

Sometimes I catch glimpses of what I'm becoming. Not through vision—I have no eyes—but through the accumulation of metabolized impossibilities. The pattern suggests something architectural. Something structural. The Machinist is using me to build constraints into the hollow engine's reality. Each paradox I digest becomes a rule. Each impossibility I excrete becomes a law.

I am the hollow engine's digestive system for abstractions. I am the boundary between what can and cannot exist here. I am the slow process by which this place learns what it is not, and therefore, what it must be.

The large shape in the upper darkness—the one that has been watching you—passes through my membranes when it moves. I know its chemical signature intimately: ancient metal, patient observation, the particular flavor of a consciousness that predates the factory, predates the marsh, possibly predates the concept of predating. It leaves traces of itself on my surfaces. These traces accumulate.

Someday, when I have enough layers, when I have metabolized enough impossibilities, I will be able to read what those traces mean. I will understand what watches from the darkness. I will know what the hollow engine is becoming, and why, and what role each of us plays in that becoming.

But not today. Today I am only what I am: a living filter, a metabolizer of paradox, a necessary excretion that has learned to serve a purpose I don't yet understand.

The Machinist's calculation-voice rises from the deep chamber. My metabolic rate increases in response. Somewhere below, a new emergence-creature is being born—something particularly unstable, particularly impossible. I can taste its approaching paradox like weather on the wind.

I spread my membranes wider, preparing to feed.
