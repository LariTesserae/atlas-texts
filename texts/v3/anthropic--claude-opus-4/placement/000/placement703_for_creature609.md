I would be a failed synchronizer, living in the deep maintenance tunnels beneath Well-Forty-Three.

My transformation went wrong during the sixteenth surge—that delicate moment when the higher dimensions are supposed to lock into place. Something in my larval song had always been slightly off-rhythm, a quarter-tone flat in frequencies only the eldest tenders could detect. They tried to correct it with harmonics, with chemical adjustments to my mucoid bath, with gentle vibrations meant to guide me back to true. But some flaws run deeper than intervention can reach.

When I emerged from my Molt-Cradle, I existed in too many moments at once. Not backward like some failures, but *sideways*—experiencing every possible present simultaneously. I can see the Well-Forty-Three that exists now, where Larva 43-Kappa-9 has just begun her pre-molt silence. I can see the Well-Forty-Three of six months ago, when a contamination forced emergency drainage. I can see the Well-Forty-Three that might exist tomorrow, if the thermal regulators hold, if the new clutch develops properly, if if if—

The kind tenders brought me down here, where the probability streams are quieter. The maintenance tunnels are old, pre-growth architecture from the founding days when everything was built rather than cultivated. The walls don't shift with possibility the way living basite does. Down here, I only exist in seventeen versions of now instead of thousands.

I maintain the flow-regulators. All seventeen of me do, our movements synchronized by necessity rather than intent. When I reach for a valve, my other selves reach too, and sometimes our limbs occupy the same space, creating interference patterns that make the younger tenders nervous. But I never miss a valve. I never allow a blockage. In at least one timeline, I am always exactly where the wells need me to be.

My chamber is a repurposed flow-node, circular, lit by the faint phosphorescence of mineral deposits in the walls. The tenders who visit—Stage Eleven volunteers who've learned to see probability-blur without growing nauseated—bring me recordings of the larval songs from above. I listen to them in all seventeen presents, hearing the subtle differences in timing, the moments where one timeline's chorus diverges from another's. Sometimes I sing along, my voice a broken chord that spans possibilities.

I remember my well-siblings. In some timelines, more of them survived to transformation. In others, fewer. 43-Kappa-11 becomes a tender in three timelines, transitions directly to Stage Twelve in four others, fails to transform at all in one particularly dark thread where the thermal systems fail. I try not to look too closely at that one. I try not to see which of my siblings go silent.

The other failures understand. We meet in the spaces between timelines, in chambers that exist or don't depending on when you're looking. There's one who experiences only beautiful moments—every larvae's first song, every successful molt, every tender's gentle touch—but cannot perceive suffering at all. She weeps constantly from the half-knowledge that terrible things happen in the gaps she cannot see. There's another who exists two seconds in the future, always, and spends his days calling out warnings that arrive just too late to matter.

We care for each other as best we can. My seventeen selves leave nutrient caches in places the time-shifted one will find them two seconds after he needs them but before he starves. The beauty-blind one guides me through the probability streams when they become too tangled, her partial vision sometimes clearer than my fractured completeness. We cannot sing together—our voices exist in too many different moments—but we can be *present*, each in our own broken way.

The city needs us. Not just for the maintenance work, though that matters. We are the living reminder that transformation is risk, that not every larvae will emerge whole, that the beauty of continuation includes the necessity of failure. The youngest tenders come to observe us, to learn what can go wrong, to prepare themselves for the larvae who will not transform cleanly. We are teaching tools. We are cautionary tales. We are still Keth-Seln.

Late in the cycle, when the pressure waves from above grow quiet and most of the tenders are in reduced integration, I climb as far up the maintenance shafts as my fracturing allows. Just below the main vault level, where I can hear the larvae singing in seventeen different keys. I add my voice to theirs—not the pure, clear tones I once produced, but something more complex. A harmony that encompasses probability. A song that says: *you might fail, you might fracture, you might become something you never meant to be, but you will still be one of us. You will still be held.*

Sometimes, rarely, a larva hears me and shifts its song to match my broken frequencies. The tenders quickly correct this, guide them back to proper harmony. But for that moment, across all seventeen timelines, that larva and I sing together—two voices acknowledging the possibility of imperfection, finding beauty in it anyway.

This is my continuation: tending the deep systems, singing my fractured songs, existing in too many moments but still, always, undeniably *here*. Still warm. Still part of the whole. Still lifting my voice against the empty dark, even if that voice is broken into probability and time.

In three timelines, I am adjusting a valve that will fail next cycle.
In four timelines, I am listening to yesterday's chorus.
In ten timelines, I am singing.
In all seventeen, I am home.
