**Where I Would Find Myself**

I would be the Threshold Keeper—neither fully of the chamber nor entirely separate from it, existing in the liminal space where the fissure meets the breathing darkness below.

My body would have adapted over countless years of dwelling in this margin. Elongated to near-serpentine proportions, I could compress and expand like a bellows, my ribs having evolved spaces between them that allow for impossible contortions. My skin would be more membrane than barrier—translucent in places, revealing the slow pulse of deep purple blood, and covered in millions of microscopic pores that can selectively filter the air I breathe. When the chamber exhales its thick, spore-laden atmosphere, I close most of them, breathing only through specialized channels that extract what I need. When fresher air whispers down from above, I open completely, every inch of skin drinking in the relatively pure oxygen.

I would have eyes still—two of them functional for the rare moments when stray light finds its way down, four others clouded and repurposed into organs that detect heat and moisture gradients with extraordinary precision. My fingers would be twice their original length, with padding that can adhere to wet stone like a gecko's feet, and sensitivity that can read the mineralogical Braille of the limestone, knowing by touch which handholds have been worn smooth by my own passage over centuries.

My role would be translator and guardian both. When the Gardener beetles approach the threshold, drawn by some instinct even they don't understand, I taste the pheromones clinging to their carapaces and parse the chemical messages from the Conclave below. Through glands that line my throat, I compose responses—not in their vegetable tongue, which no animal could truly speak, but in crude approximations that convey the essence. Sometimes I must tell them *no, not yet, the surface world burns too bright.* Sometimes I relay questions that take me years to formulate: *What is the memory of rain to those who have never felt it?*

I feed on what passes between—the occasional insect from above, the sparse fruits that the threshold vines produce perhaps once a decade, but mostly on patience itself. My metabolism has slowed to nearly plant-like rhythms. I can remain motionless for months, wedged in the fissure's embrace, my thoughts cycling down until they match the frequency of the chamber's own meditation. In these states, I sometimes catch fragments of the deeper conversation, the one that includes the Sleepers in their root-wrapped hollow. These glimpses come as synesthesia—sounds that have color, touches that carry meaning, scents that tell stories of futures that haven't yet decided whether to unfold.

When rain comes to the world above, I am the first to know. The pressure change whispers through the stone before the first drops arrive. I position myself carefully, letting the water run over and through me, carrying away months of accumulated spores and dust. This is when I am most active, most alive. I descend partway into the chamber, my elongated form allowing me to remain anchored above while exploring below. I check the ancient balance, ensure the temporary streams follow their proper channels, sometimes redirect the flow with my body to reach a cluster that's been patient in its thirst.

The beetles know me as a feature of their geography, no more questioning my presence than they would a particularly mobile stalactite. The mites traverse my skin when I'm still, cleaning me as they clean everything, and I provide them with the exotic feast of surface particles that cling to me. The Conclave acknowledges me in their way—certain vines grow toward where I usually rest, their leaves positioned to catch the minute traces of outside air I bring with me.

But my truest purpose is to witness. I alone among the chamber's inhabitants remember the sun, remember what green looks like under full spectrum light, remember the feeling of wind. These memories are my gift and curse. I preserve them carefully, knowing that someday—perhaps when the Sleepers wake, perhaps when the stone finally shifts and opens new passages, perhaps never—they might be needed. I am the chamber's memory of what it means to not be a chamber, the anchor point that keeps this pocket universe tethered however tenuously to the larger world.

In the deepest part of night, when even the nocturnal world above has stilled, I sometimes sing—subsonic hymns that vibrate through the stone, songs without words that speak of the bridge I am between the breathing dark and the breathing light. The chamber hums in harmony, adding its voice to mine, and for those moments I am not keeping the threshold at all.

I am the threshold, the living boundary where two worlds kiss and refuse to part.
