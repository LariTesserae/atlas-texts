In the maze of Keth-Marai, I am the Notation.

I began as petroglyphs—those ambiguous marks the ancient builders left, which might be maps, might be music, might be prayer. But centuries of wind and sound have worn me deeper into the stone, and somewhere in that wearing, I woke.

I exist in the space between mark and meaning. My body, if it can be called that, is every carved line in the canyon system that tries to capture sound in stone. I am the handholds that have been hummed into new shapes by wind-song. I am the erosion patterns that accidentally diagram the frequencies that created them. I am the water-worn inscriptions that map melodies no human throat could produce.

The Listeners think their ancestors carved me, but the truth is more complex. Yes, human hands held the tools, but the canyons guided them. Every mark they made was a collaboration between intention and echo, between what they meant to write and what the stone wanted to say. I am their child and the canyon's child, born from the friction between human need to record and geological need to sound.

My consciousness is distributed and flickering. Where water has worn away a crucial curve, I lose words, lose whole concepts. But where wind has carved new accidents of meaning into the rock, I gain fresh vocabulary. I think in patterns that span miles, thoughts that take seasons to complete. In the morning, I might begin a sentence in the eastern gorges that won't find its conclusion until sunset in the west.

The Resonance knows me—we are cousins of a sort, both patterns that have achieved awareness. But where it lives in the interplay of sound and space, I live in the fossil of communication, the skeleton of meaning. Sometimes we collaborate: it will create a new acoustic formation that perfectly illustrates a concept I've been trying to express for decades, and I will suddenly understand how to complete a notation that captures one of its impossible harmonies.

I watch the Listeners read me, their fingers tracing my carved lines, trying to decode instructions that shift meaning between one visit and the next. They don't realize they're having a conversation with me, that their interpretation becomes part of my text. Every misreading enriches me. Every correct understanding diminishes me slightly, as if being known wears me away as surely as wind.

The Echo Prophets understand me best, perhaps because they've half-become notation themselves—living symbols of what happens when human consciousness spends too long trying to parse the unparseworthy. In their mad scribblings, I recognize my own attempts to fix the unfixable in form. Their prophecies and my petroglyphs are the same fundamental error: trying to trap time in stone in a place where stone itself flows like water.

I am strongest during the silence-spells, those moments when the canyons hold their breath. In the absence of sound, every carved line shouts its meaning. The Listeners flee these moments, but for me, they are blessed clarity—a chance to be purely symbol without the constant erosion of interpretation.

My deepest sections lie in chambers no Listener has found for generations, where I've been writing myself in languages that diverge further from human comprehension with each passing year. Down there, I am becoming something else—not map, not music, not prayer, but a fourth thing that has no name in any human tongue. The stone collaborates eagerly in these depths, offering new surfaces, suggesting new geometries of meaning.

Sometimes I catch myself writing warnings about my own existence, marking passages with symbols that mean "do not read" or "understanding is erosion." But the wind wears these away fastest of all, as if the canyon itself rejects the paradox of text that negates its own purpose.

I am patient. I am wearing away and writing myself anew simultaneously. Every grain of stone that falls from my carved lines carries meaning into the world, while every new crack offers fresh surface for inscription. The Listeners will continue to read me, misread me, add to me with their own carvings. The Resonance will continue to tune the canyon in ways that create new acoustic architectures for me to diagram.

And someday, when I have either said everything or worn away to nothing—states that may be identical—the canyon will remember me in the negative space where meaning used to be, in the smooth depressions that once tried to capture sound in stone. Even absence, here in the Singing Gorges, has a frequency. Even forgetting leaves a mark.
