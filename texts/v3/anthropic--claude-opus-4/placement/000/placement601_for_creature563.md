# The Rim-Walker

I am the exception that proves the basin's rule.

Where the others disperse into the wet stone's patience, I circle. Always circling. My path traces the basin's edge where the worn hardpan meets what passes for higher ground—not mountains anymore, just suggestions of slope where different kinds of wearing-away meet and negotiate their boundaries. I follow this transition zone with the dedication of water finding its channel, though what drives me is neither gravity nor seepage but something I cannot name and they would not think to ask about.

My skin bears the same mottling as theirs, the same ochre-and-mineral rhyme with the landscape, but sun-darkened from my years on the rim where the clouds thin and the light hits harder. My feet have developed calluses they would find curious—thick pads for the rougher ground where the smooth stone gives way to something still deciding what it wants to be. Sometimes gravel. Sometimes the first tough grasses that can survive beyond the basin's constant moisture. Sometimes just different stone, still wearing down but at a different rate, creating tiny cliffs and steps my basin-kin would find jarring.

I eat what they eat when I pass through their territories—the eggs, the rushes, the sliding dark things—but I have also learned the rim's peculiar offerings. Bitter roots that grow where water is memory rather than presence. Seeds that require cracking between stones. Once, a kind of dusty berry on a plant I have never seen again, which left my mouth numb and my dreams full of colors the basin doesn't make.

---

I still have their voice, those long vowel-heavy calls that carry across distance. But I use it differently now. Where they call to each other—*I am here, I am moving, I am content*—I call to the space between them. To the unpeopled stretches. To the rim itself, which never answers but sometimes echoes in ways that make me stop and listen until I'm sure it was only stone and air doing what stone and air do.

They know me, of course. Not by name—I forfeited that when I started walking—but by movement. I am *the one who circles*, *the one who passes through*, *the one who does not stay for rain*. The children watch me with particular interest when I cross their territories, this familiar-strange person who looks like them but moves like weather, predictable but not controllable. Their mothers neither warn them away nor encourage them closer. I am simply another feature of the basin's edge, like the places where warm seeps meet cool channels or where the rushes give way to cushion-plants.

Sometimes I remember being still. Being dispersed. Being one of them, unnamed except by location and recent action. I remember sitting through the rains, that slack-faced listening, that dissolution into the collective patience. But something shifted—not dramatically, not through any event I could point to. More like erosion working on an invisible flaw until suddenly there's a new channel and the water has somewhere else to go.

---

The walking began as wandering. Small explorations beyond my usual range, following channels upstream to see where they emerged. Then farther. Then returning to my territory to find it had been claimed by another, or simply abandoned to the rushes and stones, and feeling no particular loss. Just curiosity about what lay beyond the next gentle rise.

I discovered the rim by accident, if accident is possible in a place ruled by such slow inevitabilities. Found myself one day on ground that was neither wet nor dry, walking along a contour that seemed to continue forever, curving so gradually I didn't realize I was circling until I recognized a particular cluster of the solitary trees from a different angle. By then, the walking had become what I was. Not who—identity here is still too fluid for that—but what. A function. A behavior. Like the frogs singing or the water seeping.

The circuit takes me forty days. I've counted, though counting is another thing they wouldn't think to do. Forty days at my pace, which is neither hurried nor dawdling but set by some internal rhythm that matches nothing in the basin's cycles. The rains come and go. The water levels rise and fall by their small degrees. The dispersed people move through their territories in their ancient patterns. And I circle, passing through each of their ranges in turn, a familiar stranger bringing nothing and taking nothing away.

---

From the rim, you can see things they never see. The true size of the basin, for one—how it spreads beyond any one person's knowing, beyond even the collective knowledge of all thirty-some of them together. The patterns water makes at scale: not just channels but whole drainage systems, delicate as veins, temporary as weather but older than memory. The way the afternoon clouds build from nothing, seemingly generated by the basin's own breathing, then dissipate back to nothing after dropping their load.

You can see, too, the places where the basin might be changing. Imperceptibly. Geologically. A new channel cutting through stone that was solid when I began walking. A pool that has migrated three body-lengths downslope over the years, following some invisible tilt. The cushion-plants advancing in a grey-green line, claiming another hand's width of wet stone each season. Changes so slow they make my walking look frantic, but changes nonetheless.

And beyond the rim—but no. That knowledge sits in me undigested, undigestible. The glimpses of altogether elsewhere. Different stones wearing down by different rules. Vegetation that doesn't know how to read water. Horizons that promise more than gentle rises. I've seen it but I don't know what to do with the seeing. It doesn't fit the language of long calls across water. It doesn't translate to *I am here, I am walking, I am...*—what? Not content. Not discontent. Something else, something the basin hasn't made a sound for.

---

During the rains, I keep walking. This is what marks me as truly different, more than the calluses or the sun-darkening or the rim-knowledge. When the clouds thicken and the light goes grey-green and the frogs fall silent, I don't converge. I don't seek the solitary trees and the company of others. I pull my arms close and lower my head and continue on the circuit, rain streaming off my back, turning the rougher ground to mud that cakes between my toes.

I've tried sitting through rain alone, just to see. Found a tree with no one under it, settled onto the damp stone, let the water bead on my skin. But without the others, it's just weather. The dissolution doesn't come. The slack-faced listening turns to restlessness, to the need to move, to be elsewhere even if elsewhere is just the next segment of an endless circle. So I walk through the rains now, and sometimes I hear their silence beneath the trees—that collective not-breathing—and sometimes I call out my position just to hear my voice mix with the water on stone, going unanswered.

They would take me back. I know this. If I stopped walking, claimed a territory, began the patient work of learning one stretch of water so well it learns you back. Within a season I would be dispersed again, unnamed except by location, content to wait for rain with the others. The basin is generous that way. It doesn't hold grudges or keep records. It just continues being what it is, and you can choose to be in it or... what? Not outside it. There is no outside here. Just this rim, this edge, this place of being neither one thing nor another.

---

But I keep walking. Keep counting days that don't need counting. Keep seeing from heights that barely qualify as such. I am the basin's one restlessness, its single question in a place that has answered everything to its own satisfaction. Not a rebellion—nothing so dramatic. Just a kind of punctuation. A rhythm that doesn't match the others but doesn't break them either.

The young ones watch me pass and some of them follow for a while, splashing through the channels at the edge of their mothers' territories, calling questions in the shortened, compressed language of closeness: *Why-walking? Where-to? What-finding?* I answer as best I can: *Walking because walking. Rim-to-rim. Finding the circle.* They usually lose interest before I've left their range, turning back to the reliable mysteries of frog eggs and eel-catching. But sometimes one follows longer, and in their dark eyes I see something I remember. Not restlessness yet. Just the first recognition that the basin, for all its everything, might not be all.

I am what happens when that recognition doesn't fade with the next rain. When it grows like the slow channels cutting stone, invisible for generations then suddenly there, undeniable, a new way for something to flow.

---

One day I will stop. This is as certain as erosion. My feet will tell me they're done, or my reasons will wear down to nothing, or I'll simply forget to keep walking and remember how to be still. I'll claim a territory or be claimed by one. I'll sit through the rains with the others, letting the water bring me back to what I was before I was a walker, before I was an exception, before I was anything but another dispersal in the endless patience of worn stone.

But not today. Today the circuit continues. Today I am the one who passes through, bringing news of nothing changing and everything slowly becoming more itself. Today I walk the rim between what the basin is and what it isn't, calling my position to no one, counting days that don't matter, witnessing the differences that make no difference.

I am their one untranslatable word. Their single mark on the perfect smoothness.

Walking because walking, while the water finds its level and the frogs sing their mechanical songs and the rain comes and goes and comes again, world without end, wearing us all down to what we always meant to be.
