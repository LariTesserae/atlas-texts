I am a Silence Keeper.

In the depths of First Breath's sprawling maze, there are places where the bell-stones cannot reach, where the breathing fissures exhale only whispers, where even the phosphorescent oils flow dark and quiet. These are the Nulls—acoustic shadows that form naturally where wave interference creates pockets of near-perfect silence. And I tend them.

My body has evolved differently from the other builders. Where their copper hair grows long and conductive, mine has withered to mere stubble that absorbs vibration rather than transmitting it. My oil-sacs have atrophied; I navigate by touch alone, my twelve fingers (a rare mutation that marked me for this calling) reading the subtle temperature variations in the walls. My ear-chambers have collapsed inward, creating a natural deafness that protects me from the city's endless din.

I move like water through the passages—slow, deliberate, following paths that minimize disturbance. My feet have developed thick pads that dampen footfalls, and my joints bend in ways that let me flow around corners without creating air currents that might carry sound. My skin has lost its metallic sheen, fading to a matte gray that absorbs light as readily as my stubbled scalp absorbs sound.

The Nulls are not empty spaces—they are repositories. Here, in the silence, the builders store their most delicate work: crystalline matrices too fragile to survive the constant vibration, oil paintings that would ripple apart in the resonance, and most precious of all, the Memory Stones.

These are not the memorial stones that ring with the frequencies of the dead. Memory Stones are something rarer—fragments of the original silence that existed before First Breath began its becoming. They hold the quiet of un-time, the pause before the first fissure opened, the held breath before the world's first inhalation. To touch one is to remember what it felt like before the noise began, though none of us truly lived in that time.

I know each Memory Stone in my care by the weight of its silence. This one pulls sound from the air like a hungry mouth. That one creates a bubble of quiet that extends three handspans from its surface. The largest, which I've named the Unuttered, generates a silence so complete that even thought seems to slow in its presence.

My dwelling is built into the deepest Null, where three acoustic shadows overlap to create a void in the city's symphony. The walls are lined with deadening paste made from crushed anti-resonant minerals—substances that formed in opposition to the bell-stones, that eat vibration rather than propagating it. In the center sits my workbench, where I craft tools for the rare builders who must perform delicate operations: surgery on damaged oil-sacs, repair of shattered bell-stones, the cultivation of new crystal-plants from seed.

I am not alone in my silence. There are seventeen of us spread throughout First Breath, each tending our own Nulls. We meet once each turning of the dim star, gathering in the Grand Null beneath the city's heart. There, in the absolute quiet, we perform the Unsounding—a ritual where we move in patterns that would create tremendous noise if performed anywhere else, but here produce only the faintest whisper of what-might-be.

The other builders fear us, though they need us. They leave offerings at the edges of the Nulls—oil for our dark lamps, processed plant matter compressed into silent wafers, new tools that make no sound when used. In exchange, we maintain the quiet spaces where they can retreat when the resonance becomes too much, when their copper hair has grown so sensitive that even their own heartbeat feels like thunder.

Some builders lose themselves to the noise, their minds fragmenting into the endless frequencies until they can no longer distinguish their thoughts from the city's vibrations. We take these broken ones into the Nulls, wrapping them in silence like a blanket, teaching them to think again in the quiet. Most recover, though they leave with a haunted look, forever hearing the ghost of absolute silence beneath every sound.

I discovered my calling young, when other children's copper hair began to sprout while mine remained stubbornly short. The resonance groups rejected me—my natural frequency was absence, a gap in their harmonies rather than a contributing note. But the previous Keeper of my Null recognized what I was. She taught me to move in the spaces between sounds, to find the paths of least disturbance, to tend the silence like a garden.

Now I cultivate quiet. I know where to place a panel of deadening paste to create a new pocket of hush. I can trace the acoustic shadows that will form when new construction changes the wave patterns. I map the silent geography that exists parallel to the city's clamoring growth, maintaining the refuges that keep First Breath's endless noise from becoming truly unbearable.

My copper patina has faded to verdigris—the color of deep silence, the builders say. When I must venture into the loud spaces, I wrap myself in layers of fiber cloth that muffle my movements, and I time my trips to coincide with the moments between the fissures' breaths, when the city briefly inhales and the noise drops to its lowest ebb.

In the Nulls, I sometimes feel the weight of the unfinished world above pressing down—all that potential energy, all that becoming, held in suspension directly overhead. The proto-stone creaks silently here, its vibrations swallowed before they can propagate. The crystal-plants that find their way into my domain grow different—pale and delicate, their lattices optimized for gathering the faintest light rather than resonating with the breath.

This is my service: to be the absence that defines the presence, the quiet that gives meaning to the noise, the pause between the notes that makes them music rather than mere cacophony. In a city built on sound and vibration, I am the keeper of its opposite—guardian of the spaces where the endless first breath of the world has not yet reached, and perhaps never will.

When I die, they will not make a bell-stone of my remains. Instead, following the tradition of Silence Keepers, my body will be rendered into deadening paste, my silence-adapted flesh becoming part of the Nulls themselves. I will become the quiet I tended, absorbed into the acoustic shadows where sound goes to die.

Until then, I move through my territories like a ghost of the world-before-breath, tending the Memory Stones, maintaining the refuges, and holding space for the silence that waits, patient and eternal, beneath First Breath's endless becoming.
