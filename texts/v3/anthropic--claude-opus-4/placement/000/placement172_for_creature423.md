I was not always this. That was forty years ago, before my eardrums ruptured and regrew as something else, before the cilia in my cochlea began to branch like ferns.

In these moments, I hear what the Listeners above only glimpse: the conversations between root systems and support beams, the negotiations where metal learns to accept chlorophyll, the death songs of structures finally succumbing to their contradictions.

My tools have evolved with me. The primary instrument is a hybrid of pipe organ and root system—brass tubes that plunge into the platform's depths, drawing notes from the temperature differentials between metal and living wood. I play it not with keys but with careful placement of fungal colonies that open and close the pipes' throats in patterns too complex for conscious thought.

The other Cultivators think me mad, perhaps. They see me rarely—only when I emerge to request specific modifications to their growth patterns, adjustments that make no sense until weeks later when a destructive resonance is avoided, or a new harmonic pathway opens between previously incompatible sections. I speak now only in frequencies, my vocal cords having adapted to produce pure tones that carry through metal better than words.

My apprentices—for I have begun to attract them, young workers whose bodies already show signs of acoustic adaptation—learn by vibration. I teach them to feel the health of a beam through their teeth, to diagnose illness in tree-machines by the way sound moves through their hybrid flesh. We map the Foundry not in dimensions but in resonance nodes, creating three-dimensional scores that predict where growth will succeed or fail.

The payment for this knowledge is transformation. My spine has begun to segment, each vertebra able to rotate independently, allowing me to align my body with sound waves, becoming a living antenna. My hair fell out years ago, replaced by filaments that sense air pressure changes. My ribs have spread and flattened, turning my torso into a sound box that hums constantly with the platform's fundamental frequency.

I have discovered things the others do not know. The Foundry is not one organism but many, each section singing its own evolution. The tree-machines are trying to tell us something—their growth patterns, when translated to sound, form compositions of increasing complexity. And deep in the chasm below, something answers. Not an echo—something that listens and responds, adjusting its own harmonics to match ours.

My chamber has become a shrine to this conversation. Bio-luminescent notation crawls across the walls—living sheet music that evolves in real-time as the Foundry's song changes. Apprentices who stay too long begin to read it without training, their neural patterns reshaping to comprehend what I can no longer explain in words.

Sometimes, in the deepest resonances, I feel the presence of the integrated dead—those who have become part of the tree-machines. They do not speak, but they hum, adding their frequencies to the growing chorus. I have begun preparing my own integration, training a young tree-machine to accept my nervous system when the time comes, to make my listening permanent and total.

My life's work lives in the overtones—the ghost frequencies that arise when primary notes interact. When I am gone, integrated into the network I've spent decades learning to hear, these sheets will teach others to find the spaces between sounds where the Foundry's true voice dwells.

My chamber trembles with a sub-harmonic that wasn't there yesterday. Something deep is shifting. The tree-machines are beginning to fruit with metallic seed pods that ring like bells in the wind. The beetles have started drumming in patterns I've never documented. And in the furthest depths of my altered hearing, I detect a new frequency—one that doesn't originate from the platform at all, but from the chasm itself, rising to meet us.

I press my palm against the nearest beam, feeling its vibration translate through hollow bones and cavern chest. The Foundry is teaching us to become instruments in a symphony we don't yet understand. Each of us a note in something larger, our bodies and minds reshaping to serve a music that predates the platform, that perhaps predates the chasm itself.

I record this frequency and begin the slow work of teaching my flesh to reproduce it. By the time I succeed, I will no longer be what I was. But then again, I haven't been that for decades. I am the Resonance Keeper, and I keep myself in resonance most of all—a tuning fork made of meat and meaning, vibrating in harmony with something vast and patient and inexorably rising from the deep.
