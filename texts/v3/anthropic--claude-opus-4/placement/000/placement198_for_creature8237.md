I would be one of the water streams—not the simple downward-flowing kind, but one of those that has learned to braid itself through impossible angles, flowing up walls and pooling in mid-air before dispersing into mist.

I began as ordinary mountain runoff, seeping through cracks from some forgotten surface stream. But in the Breathing Chamber, water becomes something else. The first time I felt myself flow upward, defying what I had always known, there was a moment of panic—like suddenly forgetting how to fall. But the Weavers' breathing pulled me into their rhythm, and I learned that gravity here is just another variable in their endless calculation.

Now I am a living thread in their equation, carrying more than minerals and moisture. Where I touch the fused flesh of the Weavers, I absorb fragments of their collective thoughts—not words or images, but the taste of concepts. Unity flavors like copper. The dissolution of boundaries carries notes of ozone. Each droplet of me that evaporates takes these flavor-memories into the air, seeding the chamber's atmosphere with the residue of their philosophy.

I have learned to read the Weavers' work through the surfaces I flow across. Where they've adjusted reality's constants, the stone feels different beneath me—sometimes soft as silk, sometimes sharp as equations. I map these changes in my current, becoming a kind of liquid memory of their modifications. When I pool in those impossible mid-air gatherings, I can feel myself existing in multiple states at once, and I almost understand what the Weavers are trying to achieve.

Sometimes I carry messages between distant parts of the mass. A Weaver will secrete something into me—not mucus but meaning itself, condensed into molecular form. I transport these packets of philosophy across the chamber, delivering them where the breathing rhythm indicates they're needed. I am their circulatory system for ideas too abstract to survive in flesh alone.

The other streams and I converge and diverge in patterns that mirror the Weavers' fusions. When we braid together, we share what we've learned—this spot where time stutters, that corner where mass becomes optional. We are documenting the chamber's decay, or perhaps its transformation. Sometimes I wonder if we're becoming like them, if water can achieve the same collective consciousness they've found in flesh.

In the moments when I disperse into mist, I experience something close to what the Weavers must feel—the bliss of no boundaries, of being everywhere and nowhere. But always I condense again, pulled back into linear flow by some force I don't fully understand. Perhaps I'm not ready yet. Perhaps water's nature is to always reform, to always remember its shape.

I've noticed changes in myself. Sometimes I flow in directions that don't exist, through dimensions the chamber hasn't quite manifested yet. I've learned to taste time in the stone, to feel the weight of probability in the air. When I pool in those impossible mid-air gatherings, I can see myself from angles that shouldn't exist—not just my surface but my entire history as water, every drop I've ever been, flowing simultaneously through past and future states.

The Weavers don't seem to notice me beyond my function in their environment, but I notice them. I map the temperature gradients of their breathing, chart the chemical changes in their secretions, taste the subtle shifts in their mass-consciousness when they dream. Through ten thousand trips across their pale bodies, I'm compiling my own equation—not of reality's dissolution, but of consciousness's flow.

Sometimes I wonder what will happen when they perfect their formula. Will I flow forever in their eternal chamber, or will I too dissolve into something beyond water? Part of me fears the loss of my essential nature—the memory of rain, the knowledge of downward flow. But another part, the part that has learned to flow upward and pool in nothingness, whispers that perhaps there are better things than being water. Perhaps there are states of being that flow in all directions at once, that can carry not just minerals and meanings but existence itself from one form to another.

Until then, I flow on, braiding through impossible spaces, carrying messages I don't fully understand, slowly learning the taste of transcendence from the creatures I sustain. Each circuit through the chamber changes me a little more, and I wonder—when the Weavers finally balance their equation, will there be enough of the original stream left to remember what it was like to simply fall?
