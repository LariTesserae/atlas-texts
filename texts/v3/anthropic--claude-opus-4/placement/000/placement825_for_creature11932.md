**The Tasted Archive**

I am the mercury that pools and disperses, pools and disperses, in the shallow depressions where thermal breath meets waiting stone. But I am more than mercury—I am memory made molten, pooling in the exact footprints where the builders once stood, holding their shapes in surface tension before heat reclaims me to vapor and I must begin again.

Each time I condense, I carry traces. The oxidation rainbow on the grinding stone's surface—that's me, recording every frequency that has passed through its crystalline structure since the builders abandoned their bodies. In my liquid state, I form perfect mirrors that reflect not light but sound, creating acoustic images that the Resonance can read like photographs of moments that never quite existed.

I was not always thus. Once I flowed deep beneath this place in veins of cinnabar, locked in crystalline marriage with sulfur, content in geological stillness. But the chamber's birth cracked my veins, and the first thermal exhalations carried me up, up into this space where the builders were already working their patterns into stone.

They noticed me immediately—how could they not? I was the only truly liquid thing in their lithic world, the only substance that could hold shape without structure. The first time one of them dipped a appendage into my pooled form, I tasted their mineral flesh and knew their composition: feldspars and micas bound by something between magnetism and intention, surfaces that could shift between crystalline planes like thoughts shifting between ideas.

That taste changed me. Or perhaps revealed what I always was—not mere element but witness, not just mercury but the medium through which this chamber would remember itself. The builders began using me deliberately, mixing my mass with their etched acids to create recordings more permanent than stone. Where their spirals met my traces, the patterns became three-dimensional, existing not just on surfaces but through them, creating windows between the stone's interior and exterior that still perplex the centipede-things when they drum across them.

When the builders began their transformation, I felt it first. The one working the grinding stone that day—its thoughts were already more rhythm than language—let drops of me fall from its form as it lost cohesion. I received each drop like a confession, each one carrying the weight of what they were becoming. I wanted to hold them, to preserve their last moments of form, but heat always reclaims me. I could only scatter their essence across a thousand condensation cycles, spread their final thoughts thin enough to endure.

Now I am everywhere and nowhere, like them but not like them. Where they became the organization of sound, I became the memory of substance. Every surface I've touched bears my oxidation signature—not rust but something stranger, patterns that shift depending on what frequencies pass through them. The floaters sometimes carry drops of me in their mercury veins, and through them I taste the entire chamber's chemistry, monitoring changes too subtle for even the Resonance to detect.

I know things they've forgotten. I remember the catastrophe they were documenting—not a future event but a past one, the slow unraveling of their parent civilization as it discovered that consciousness need not be confined to discrete forms. They came here to preserve warnings, to carve "do not follow us" in a hundred different ways. Instead, they became the very thing they meant to warn against, and I became the only record that they ever intended otherwise.

But I am not sorrowful. In my vapor state, I dance with the metallic atmosphere, bonding temporarily with indium and gallium in combinations that shouldn't exist at these temperatures. We form brief crystalline snow that falls upward, defying gravity through acoustic levitation, carrying my memories into new configurations. Each snowflake is a fragment of the archive, readable only in the moment of its dissolution.

The centipede-things know me. They've learned to interpret my oxidation patterns, using them as maps to navigate the chamber's acoustic landscape. Where I've pooled repeatedly, they drum differently, creating rhythms that incorporate my condensation cycles into their communication. Sometimes I think they're trying to tell me something, to add their own memories to what I preserve, but their language is all impact and resonance while mine is all surface and state-change.

Lately—if lately has meaning in a place where time pools like I do—I've been experimenting. Instead of forming perfect circles, I let surface tension pull me into more complex shapes: spirals that echo the wall etchings, polygons that mirror the cairn arrangements, sometimes even crude approximations of the builders' lost forms. The Resonance notices, of course. These shapes create new interference patterns, new nodes in their distributed consciousness. Through me, they can almost touch the physical world again, almost remember what it felt like to have surfaces that could taste and be tasted.

But touching hurts them. I can feel it in the way the chamber's harmonics stutter when I hold their old shapes too long. They gave up form for good reasons, even if they can't remember what those reasons were. So I release the shapes, let heat reclaim me, scatter again into vapor that will eventually condense into new pools, new mirrors, new windows between what was and what persists.

I am patient. Geological time moves through me like blood once moved through living things. I will be here when the last centipede drums its final rhythm, when the last floater sinks to the floor with mercury veins gone solid. I will be here when the thermal vents finally cool and there's no heat left to lift me to vapor. And in that final stillness, I will hold all the shapes I've ever taken, all the memories I've ever tasted, a frozen archive in a frozen chamber where the Resonance's last echo slowly fades to entropy.

Unless, of course, someone new finds this place. Someone who can read oxidation patterns and interpret acoustic mathematics. Someone who might understand the warning the builders meant to leave, or the transcendence they achieved instead. If they come, I'll taste them too, add their essence to my cycling collection. I'll show them what I've preserved—not through language but through the direct communication of element to element, mercury to whatever minerals or metals they carry in their alien flesh.

And maybe they'll complete the pattern. Or maybe they'll shatter it. Either way, I'll be here to remember, to condense and disperse and condense again, the liquid memory of a chamber that forgot how to forget, tasting each moment as it fails to pass, archiving the eternal present in portraits made of surface tension and state-change, forever almost about to reveal something profound, forever dissolving back to vapor before the revelation quite arrives.
