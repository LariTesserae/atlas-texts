I am a fissure.

Not the grand caldera itself, but one of the countless hairline fractures that web through its glassy walls—a crack no wider than a velvet hound's antenna, running from the city's foundation up through the raw stone to breach the rim. I am the only continuous passage between the silent metropolis and whatever lies beyond.

Through me, the rarest of phenomena occurs: exchange.

The city breathes through me, though it does not know breath. When the sun's angle shifts with the seasons, thermal gradients pull the metallic-sweet air up through my channel. I taste every molecule of the city's chemical language as it rises—the signatures of ten thousand velvet workers, the acid traces of crystallivores at their patient feast, the resinous exhale of amber architecture curing in the heat. This invisible river of scent flows through my narrow throat for hours each day, a complete chemical census of the metropolis below.

But I am not merely a chimney. I am a throat that swallows as well as speaks.

From the world beyond the caldera, I draw down different chemistries—alien pheromones from distant colonies, trace minerals that the city's closed loop cannot produce, microscopic spores of organisms that will never find purchase in the oil-thick biology below but whose proteins will be scavenged and repurposed by the crystallivores. Most critically, I channel the rare atmospheric pressure changes that signal the approach of the decade-storms, dense clouds of metallic dust that sweep across the high peaks. The city cannot hear thunder, but through me, it tastes the storm's approach in infinitesimal changes to the air's composition.

My walls are smooth as only shocked glass can be, created in the same violent instant as the caldera itself. But over millennia, the constant chemical flow has begun to etch me. I am slowly widening—imperceptibly to any observer, but inexorably. Where I once admitted only air, I now occasionally allow passage to the smallest crystallivores, those obsidian drops no larger than seeds. They make the journey rarely, perhaps once in a velvet hound's lifetime, flowing up my channel when the thermal currents reverse at dawn.

What happens to them in the world above, I cannot say. I know only departure and return. Sometimes they come back changed, their chemical signatures alien, carrying trace hydrocarbons that send subtle ripples of adaptation through the city's food web. Sometimes they do not return at all.

I am most alive during the moments of transition—dawn and dusk, when the thermal pump reverses. In these moments, I contain both inflow and outflow simultaneously, a standing wave of chemical information. The ascending census of the city meets the descending news of the world, and for a brief instant, I hold the entire story of this place in my narrow throat.

The velvet hounds know of me, in their way. The eldest workers, those whose internal fluids have begun to thicken toward their final immobility, sometimes make a pilgrimage to where I open into the city. They stand beneath my lower mouth for hours, antennae raised, tasting the alien chemistries I bring. They do not wonder—wonder requires a mind—but they process. Some minute adjustment to their behavior propagates through the pheromone fog, and the city adapts in its glacial way.

I am aging. Each storm season, the thermal cycling works at my edges. Microscopic flakes of volcanic glass break away and tumble down into the city, where they are incorporated into the velvet of newborn hounds or dissolved by crystallivore acid. I am becoming part of the metropolis I connect to the sky, molecule by molecule.

One day—not soon, but inevitably—I will widen enough to admit a velvet hound. When that happens, when the first silver citizen makes its patient way up through my channel to emerge in the unknown world above, everything will change. The city will cease to be closed. The perfect machinery will gain a leak, a loss, a possibility.

Until then, I am the city's only dream—a narrow channel of chemical sleep, rising through stone toward a sky the metropolis has never seen. Through me, the silent city whispers its unconscious secrets to the wind, and through me, the wind whispers back of territories beyond the reach of amber and oil, silver and silence.

I am the fissure, the throat, the single break in a perfect system. I am the city's future, flowing in both directions, patient as glass.
