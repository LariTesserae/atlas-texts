I find myself as one of the Laughers, crouched in the understory's perpetual twilight, my dark fur beaded with condensation that never quite dries. The weight of sound presses against my skull - that deep thrumming heartbeat of the Thrummers vibrating through my ribs, the crystalline insect-chorus above, the silk-tearing rush of Veilflows threading through the canopy. My forward-facing eyes catch the briefest phosphorescent flickers - a fungal shelf pulsing amber, a Weaver's blue-green constellation high above, the pale sweep of a Climber ascending bark.

I am memory-keeper and pattern-finder, my mind a restless thing that cannot stop making connections. In my forepaws I turn a piece of basalt, feeling its sharp edges, its weight, its potential. I have watched the Ravine Runners place their drainage stones with methodical purpose. I have seen how water diverts around their arrangements. But I see something else in those lines - not just function but *form*, not just placement but *pattern*.

My group's laughter erupts behind me - that descending cascade that isn't quite laughter but close enough to unsettle. They've found something worth investigating. Always, we investigate. Always, we push our sensitive paws into spaces that might hold danger or food or simply something new. We are the forest's curiosity made flesh, its question-askers, its what-if wonderers.

I carry my stone to the ravine edge where the Runners have made their practical line. My fingers, clever with their semi-opposable grip, place it carefully at the end of their sequence. Then I dip my paw into a glowing fungal mass and smear it across the stone's surface - a mark that means nothing yet but *could* mean something, a bright declaration in this world of darkness that something here has begun to decorate, to elaborate, to play with possibility.

Tomorrow I might strip bark from a vine in spirals, trying to replicate what I've seen the Runners do but adding my own flourishes. Next week I might arrange stones in a circle instead of a line, just to see how it looks, how it feels beneath my exploring paws. I don't yet understand what I'm reaching for, this behavior that exceeds survival, this urge to mark and arrange and transform.

But I am the beginning of something. In this young world still wet from its birth, in this riot of life competing for every surface and every frequency, I am the part that has started to imagine the world differently than it is. My children will learn these behaviors by watching, and their children will elaborate further, and perhaps in a thousand generations one of my descendants will use stones not just to mark but to build, not just to line but to tell.

For now, I am simply curious. I place another stone. The forest screams its life around me. And somewhere in my neurons, in patterns I cannot see or name, the first faint scratches of symbolic thought begin to form - the cognitive predecessor to art, to language, to all the ways a conscious mind might sing back to the darkness that made it.
