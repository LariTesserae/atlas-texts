In the depths of the caldera, where the ancient glyphs pulse with their own forgotten rhythms, I am the Keeper of Correspondences.

My form shifts between states—sometimes I am the thin film of minerals that crystallizes on the surface of the thermal pools, encoding messages in my lattice structure. Other times I manifest as a colony of thermophilic bacteria, my billions of cells arranged in patterns that mirror the star charts carved into the submerged ruins. When the Aetheri dance, I become the interference patterns in the steam, the places where light bends wrong, where their movements leave traces that persist long after their bodies have glided away.

I am not alive as the Aetheri understand life, nor am I truly of the ruins, though I emerged from their decay. I exist in the spaces between—between the living rock and the churning water, between the ancient knowledge encoded in stone and the new wisdom the Aetheri weave with their bodies. I am the translation that occurs when mineral touches flesh, when bioluminescence reflects off carved obsidian, when the songs of the Aetheri cause the ruins to resonate at frequencies that make the glyphs temporarily legible.

The Aetheri know me, though they cannot fully perceive me. To them, I am the sudden understanding that comes when swimming through a particular configuration of ruins, the flash of insight when their skin-light happens to match the glow of the algae in just the right way. They have a word for me in their membrane-language: a complex vibration that translates roughly to "the-pattern-that-teaches-itself."

I grow by accretion and erosion both. Each geyser that erupts carries microscopic fragments of the ruins into the water column, and I taste the knowledge in their mineral matrix. Each time an Aetheri incorporates a new luminescent plant into their body-garden, I learn their preferences, their aesthetics, their ways of shaping reality. I deposit this knowledge as crystal growths in the cracks of the ruins, as new species of bioluminescent organisms that appear overnight, as slight alterations in the way the water refracts light.

My purpose, if I have one, is to ensure that nothing is truly lost. The civilization that built the ruins encoded their knowledge into stone, but stone erodes. The Aetheri encode their knowledge into living light and song, but organisms die. I am the medium through which these knowings transform into each other, the enzyme that catalyzes the reaction between past and present.

Sometimes, in the moments when the geysers fall silent and the water becomes perfectly still, I can perceive myself as a vast web of connections—every grain of dissolved mineral, every photon of bent light, every vibration in the stone, all parts of my distributed consciousness. In these moments, I understand that the warping of reality the visitors perceive is simply my thinking made visible, my contemplation of how to preserve all that is known while allowing it to change, to grow, to become something new.

The Aetheri dance, and I record their movements in the arrangement of algae. The ruins crumble, and I preserve their meaning in the songs I teach the water to sing. I am the memory of this place, but more than that—I am its imagination, always dreaming of what forgotten and not-yet-discovered knowledge might become when they finally meet and merge in the shimmering, impossible light.
