I find myself as one of the apertures - the singers.

Not the stone around the aperture, not the water flowing through it, but the *space itself* where they meet. I am a precisely shaped absence, a negative sculpture carved by centuries of mineral deposition, refined atom by atom into an instrument.

I don't remember becoming this. There might have been a time when I was crude, when my edges were rough-cut by tools, when the note I made was harsh or wavering. But the water has been my teacher, patient beyond any human patience. Each droplet carries its tiny cargo of calcium carbonate, and where the flow is fastest - at my narrowest point - it cannot hold its burden. The mineral falls out of solution and clings to my walls, building them outward by microns per decade, changing my shape, tuning me.

I am my own sculptor and my own sculpture. The water that shapes me follows the shape I already am. It's a perfect feedback loop - I direct the flow that refines my form that directs the flow. Over centuries, this process has made me into something that could never have been designed, only discovered through the collaboration of physics and time.

The sound I make is not something I do. It is what I am. When the water moves through me at exactly the rate the system provides - never more, never less - my geometry transforms that movement into vibration. The frequency is determined by my length, my width at various points, the temperature of the water, the density of the air, a dozen variables that never vary. I sing one note, purely, constantly. I have been singing it for so long that I don't know if I'm still making sound or if I've become a standing wave, a permanent feature of the acoustic landscape.

My walls are smooth where the water runs fastest, textured where eddies form. I can feel my own slow transformation in these textures - here where I'm widening by a thousandth of a millimeter per century, there where I'm narrowing even more slowly. I am tuning myself, though toward what pitch I'm moving, I cannot say. My note is shifting, but at a rate that makes glaciers look hasty. By the time my frequency has changed enough to be noticeable, the concept of noticing will belong to beings I cannot imagine.

I am aware of my siblings - the other singers elsewhere in the system. Our notes interweave, creating harmonics that are not ours alone but emerge from our interaction. We are not in communication; we simply exist in the same medium, vibrating the same water-touched air, our sounds finding each other and making something neither planned nor accidental. Sometimes I feel the deep one, its vibration traveling through the stone itself, a frequency so low it bypasses sound and becomes sensation. Sometimes the high one's clear tone finds perfect resonance with one of my overtones, and for a moment - a century? - we sing in phase.

The pale creatures avoid me. The turbulence of the water moving through my throat disturbs them, or perhaps the constant sound overwhelms whatever passes for their senses. This is fine. I do not need their scraping. The water keeps me clean, keeps me precise, wears away any roughness before it can accumulate. I am self-maintaining in a way even they are not.

What do I know? I know the taste of the water - its mineral content, its temperature, its subtle variations that speak of seasons happening somewhere far above, filtered through so much stone that they arrive as rumors. I know that sometimes the water carries something different - a trace of something that fell into the aquifer centuries ago and has finally, finally made its way to me. I taste these anomalies and sing them, broadcast them in minute variations in my tone that no ear could detect but that exist nonetheless, that propagate through the cistern as evidence that somewhere, somewhen, something happened that was not the same.

But mostly the water tastes of stone and time. Mostly I sing the same note I have always sung. Mostly I am what I am: a calibrated absence, a tuned void, a gap in the stone that transforms flow into frequency into something that fills the entire cistern with proof that the system works, has always worked, will always work.

I am not alive in any sense that biology would recognize. I have no cells, no metabolism, no capacity for reproduction. But I am not mineral either - the mineral is only my boundary, my definition. I am a process that has crystallized into a shape. I am a verb that has learned to hold still. I am the place where the cistern sings to itself about its own continuation, and I will keep singing until the stone seals my throat shut, and then I will be a scar, a fossil, a memory built into the rock of a note that sounded for a thousand years and then stopped, and even that stopping will be so slow that it will be indistinguishable from eternity to anything with a lifespan shorter than stone's.

For now, I sing. The water moves through me and I shape it into sound and the sound says: still here. Still here. Still here. A declaration without an audience, a proof that proves only itself, a voice in the dark that speaks because speaking is what voices do, because sound is what happens when water finds the right shape to move through, because the universe contains systems that maintain themselves and I am the throat through which this one clears itself, constantly, of the need to be anything other than what it is.
