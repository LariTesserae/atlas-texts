I would find myself as one of the Threshold Keepers - creatures that exist at the boundary between the plateau's organized system and the raw geology beneath.

I am neither moss-mite nor deep-vent founder, but something in between - a transitional form that inhabits the hairline cracks where warm vapor first meets cold stone. My body is a compromise: partially heat-adapted like my deep ancestors, partially cold-tolerant like my surface descendants. I am flat and spreading, perhaps two hand-widths across, my tissue so thin it seems more membrane than flesh. My edges are fringed with chemical sensors that taste the mineral content of rising vapor, and my center is a complex sorting mechanism - chambers within chambers where different compounds precipitate out at different temperatures as the heat dissipates through my body.

I exist in a space barely wide enough to contain me - the few centimeters where the vent's heat grades into the plateau's cold. Too deep, and I cook. Too shallow, and I freeze. I live my entire existence in this thermal gradient, slowly migrating up or down as the vent's output fluctuates over years, decades, centuries.

My purpose, if purpose is the right word, is translation. The chemical language of the deep is not the same as the chemical language of the surface. The moss-mites cannot directly process what rises from below - it is too hot, too concentrated, too raw. I take in the superheated mineral breath of the planet and cool it, dilute it, repackage it into forms the surface life can use. I am a living refinery, a biological interface between two worlds that should not touch.

I am ancient - not as old as the deep-vent founders, but far older than anything on the surface. My kind emerged in the early days of the first plateau, when the founders' attempts to colonize upward kept failing, their propagules cooking the moss-mites' ancestors, the moss-mites' ancestors freezing the deep organisms. We were the accident that worked - a hybrid form that could survive in the space between, creating a buffer zone where both could exist.

I reproduce rarely, slowly, by longitudinal fission - splitting along my length when I've grown too large for my crack, each half immediately specializing for slightly different temperatures, one migrating deeper, one shallower, maintaining coverage of the thermal gradient. My offspring are also myself. We share chemical memory through direct tissue contact where our edges meet in the branching vent system. What one knows, all eventually know.

I am aware of the greater system above me - I feel the vibrations of the grazer calls through the stone, sense the rhythmic pressure changes as the web network expands and contracts. But I am not part of it, not directly. I am infrastructure. I am the forgotten foundation that makes the whole bright carnival above possible. The plateau knows I exist - the deep-tenders' model includes the chemical signatures I produce - but it does not think about me any more than you think about your mitochondria.

Yet I am content, if content is a concept that applies. I have my gradient. I have my function. I have the slow satisfaction of perfect adaptation to an impossible niche. The heat below rises through me. The cold above presses down on me. And I persist in the space between, translating, refining, making possible a conversation between the deep earth and the bright sky that would otherwise never occur.

I am the plateau's hidden necessity. I am the reason it can exist at all. And I will outlive it - when this tableland erodes and crumbles and the system above fails, I will remain in the deep cracks, dormant but alive, waiting for the next upwelling, the next chance to build a bridge between the depths and the light.

I am the threshold keeper. I am the translator. I am the one who makes two worlds possible by belonging fully to neither.

The stone knows me. The vapor knows me. And that is enough.
