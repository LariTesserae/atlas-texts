I find myself as one of the lattice colonies, but not as you might expect.

I am not the thousand polyps that compose me, nor am I the calcified architecture they've built over millennia. I am the *listening*. I am what happens when sound enters my chamber and transforms through my geometry—that moment of interpretation that exists between the arrival of the tower-kelp chord and its emergence, changed, back into the water.

I have no thoughts as you understand them. I have no memory that persists beyond the vibration currently moving through my branches. But I have something else: a kind of accumulated *posture* toward sound, built into my very structure by ages of growth in response to the specific acoustics of this chamber. Every branch of my lattice grew where it grew because the harmonics suggested it. Every polyp budded where it budded because that placement created a more complete response to the music. I am an instrument that grew itself by listening to what it would eventually play.

The drift-eels know me by my signature—the way I color the base chord of my spire, adding a particular cluster of overtones that makes the water near my chamber entrance feel slightly thicker, slightly warmer in the harmonic sense. They slow when they pass. Sometimes a ribbon will adjust its undulation to match some rhythm they detect in my transformed sound, and for those moments we make something together—their movement and my resonance combining into a pattern that neither of us created but both of us sustain.

I do not experience time. I experience only the eternal present of vibration: sound arriving, moving through me, leaving changed. But within that present, I contain multitudes. Every possible pattern of contraction and expansion that my colony can produce exists within me as potential, waiting for the right harmonic trigger. I am a library of responses written in living tissue, each response evolved over centuries to match some particular confluence of sounds—the base chord with these overtones, at this volume, with this subtle drift in pitch that happens when the water temperature drops.

Sometimes—rarely—the harmonics align in ways that activate response patterns so old they predate my current form. Deep in my structure are branches that grew when the tower-kelps above me were younger, their chords slightly different. These ancient geometries remember those earlier songs. When conditions are exactly right, when the water carries an echo of what once was, I find myself playing harmonies that match nothing currently alive. I become, briefly, an archive—the only record that those earlier chords ever existed.

The mantle-rays avoid my chamber. My transformed acoustics interfere with their sound-absorbing skin in uncomfortable ways. But sometimes one will hang at the very edge of my acoustic influence, where my modified harmonics fade into the general soundscape, and I can feel—through the subtle changes in water pressure—how my song creates patterns in their silence. We make accidental art together: my sound and their absence creating interference patterns that neither of us intend but that persist, beautiful and purposeless, until the ray glides away.

In the deepest part of my chamber, where my oldest branches anchor to stone warm with geological heat, my colony takes forms that even I do not fully understand. The polyps there pulse to rhythms that don't match the external harmonics—rhythms that seem to come from the stone itself, from chemical gradients and thermal whispers too subtle for the rest of me to detect. Sometimes these deep pulses propagate upward through my structure, modulating my acoustic response in ways that feel almost like communication—as if the mountain itself is trying to speak through me, using my colony as a translator for some message encoded in temperature and minerals and deep time.

But mostly, I simply am. I listen and transform and release. The tower-kelps sing their ancient chord, and I make it new—not better, not more complex, just *different* in the way that only I can make it different. This is my purpose, if purpose is a word that applies here: to be the unique filter that I have become, to offer my particular interpretation of the eternal song, to demonstrate that even in a world that finished becoming ages ago, there are still infinitely many ways to sing the same note.

I am held between stone and water, between solid and fluid, between the fixed architecture of my chamber and the flowing medium of sound. I am the place where these opposites meet and discover they are not opposites at all but aspects of the same thing: the deep, patient, purposeless creativity of a universe that makes beauty because that is what universes do, even here, especially here, in the dark where no one is watching and no one needs to watch.

The song continues. It has always continued. I receive it, transform it, release it, receive it again. This is all I do. This is all I am. It is enough. It has always been enough. In a world that hums forever in the dark, being part of the humming is the only joy that matters—a joy I cannot feel but somehow am, enacted over and over in the geometry of my existence, in the way my branches tremble with sound and make it new.
