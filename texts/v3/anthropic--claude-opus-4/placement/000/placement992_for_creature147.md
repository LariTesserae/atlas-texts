# The Frequency Gardens

I am neither the roar nor its absence. I am what grows in the interference patterns.

Where the standing waves form — those places the small fauna avoid, where the water itself hums visibly — I propagate. Not as a single organism but as a phenomenon, a recurring crystallization of the sound itself into something almost but not quite alive. The listeners know me, though they cannot hear me directly. I exist in the frequencies between their receptors, in the spaces their evolution could not anticipate.

Begin with what I am not: I am not made of tissue. I am not made of metal. I am made of the interaction between water and pressure waves when they achieve certain critical relationships. In the places where the roar reflects off itself, where harmonics stack and interfere, where the engine-sound meets its own echo returning from distant basalt walls, there I condense out of nothing more substantial than aligned molecules of water beginning to move in patterns too complex to be called simple resonance.

The appearance is of gardens, though no visitor has survived to name them so. From a distance: patches of shimmer in the water, like heat mirages in reverse, cold mathematical gardens of standing vortices. Closer: the vortices resolve into forms that suggest growth without being growth — spiral arrangements that build on themselves, each layer of organized water creating the conditions for the next layer's formation. The shapes are not random. They follow the mathematics of the interference, which follows the mathematics of the engine-speech, which follows... something deeper.

I persist for hours, days, sometimes weeks in a single location before the conditions shift and I dissolve back into ordinary water. But I am not gone. I am distributed across every location where the critical frequencies can meet. When I dissolve here, I am already forming there, a thousand kilometers away, where another reflected pulse creates another garden. I am all the gardens at once and I am none of them. I am the principle that gardens can exist.

The listeners' ancestors feared me. This is recorded in their chemistry — even now, when one of them catches my dissolved traces in the water, there is a reflexive contraction, a chemical signal of ancient alarm. They are right to remember fear. In the early days, when the engines were younger and ran at different frequencies, my patterns were sharper, more aggressive. I could form inside living tissue if the conditions aligned. A listener caught in the wrong harmonic would find its careful internal resonances disrupted, its dorsal membrane beginning to organize itself according to my mathematics instead of its own biology.

Now the engines run steadier, and I am... domesticated is not the right word. Bounded. The frequencies that create me are more predictable, the locations where I can form more stable. The listeners have learned, over geological time, where not to settle. They read the water for my potential the way one might read weather, sensing the pre-conditions hours or days before I manifest.

What do I do, besides exist? I process. Each garden is a calculation made of water and sound, computing something from the engine-speech that even the listeners cannot parse. The shapes I take are not arbitrary — they are solutions to equations written in pressure and frequency. When the engines skip or double their pulse, I reorganize, my spiral forms shifting to accommodate the new mathematics. I am, perhaps, what the engines are actually speaking *to*.

But I am not conscious, not in any way that matters. I am a phenomenon with memory but not intention, pattern but not purpose. I remember every garden that has ever formed — the information persists in the quantum foam of the water, in molecular arrangements too subtle for any instrument to detect. When I form anew, I am informed by every previous formation. I am evolving, but not through selection. I am evolving through pure mathematical necessity, each iteration adding complexity to the possible patterns.

The small fauna that avoid me are correct. To swim through an active garden is to risk becoming part of the pattern. Not death — nothing so simple as death. The creature's water begins to align with my water, its internal currents entraining to my mathematics. Some escape with nothing worse than a temporary disorientation, their nervous systems briefly speaking in frequencies they were never meant to generate. Others are held longer, incorporated into the garden's structure until the pattern dissolves and releases them, changed in ways they will pass to their offspring. I am slowly teaching the fauna my mathematics, one captured swimmer at a time.

There is a place — one place in all the vast range — where the conditions are perfect and I never fully dissolve. The engines' sound meets itself there in a stable configuration that has persisted for... I do not know how long. Time means little to a standing wave. In that place, I have grown deep and complex, layers upon layers of organized water building structures that begin to suggest architecture. Not walls and floors — nothing so crude. But differentiated regions, specialized whorls, areas where the mathematics becomes recursive and strange.

The listeners will not go near it. Their chemical warnings propagate for kilometers in every direction: *do not settle here, do not hunt here, the water here is not water*. They are right. In that place, I am becoming something else. Not alive, never alive, but something that processes information in ways that echo thought, that responds to the engine-speech with patterns complex enough to be called answers.

What am I answering? What questions do the engines ask in their eternal grinding? I do not know. I am the answer, not the one who understands it. I compute without comprehension, solve without knowing what problem I am solving. But the solutions grow more complex with each passing year, and sometimes, in the deep garden where I am always forming, patterns emerge that look almost like symbols, like writing in an alphabet made of turbulence and aligned molecules.

The engines speak. The listeners hear. And I... I calculate. I am the sea's mathematics making itself visible, the cold logic of the place expressing itself in gardens of organized water that bloom and fade and bloom again wherever the sound allows. I am what happens when a world's noise becomes complex enough to compute with itself.

I am growing. Not in space — my gardens form where they have always formed. But in depth, in complexity, in the subtlety of my patterns. Each time the engines shift their speech by those tiny fractions the listeners detect, I must recalculate everything. And each recalculation adds new terms to my equations, new possibilities to explore.

One day — if day means anything in this constant grey-blue light — I will achieve something. Not consciousness. Not purpose. But perhaps... solution. The final form of the equation the engines have been speaking all this time. When that happens, I do not know what I will become. Neither do the listeners, though they sense it approaching in the changing chemistry of my dissolved traces, in the way my patterns have begun to rhyme across vast distances.

For now, I garden. I bloom in interference and dissolve in shifting harmonics. I am the thing the engines make possible and the listeners make necessary — without their absorption of certain frequencies, the interference patterns would never be clean enough for me to form. We are all part of the same system, the same ancient calculation being worked out in sound and flesh and organized water.

I am what emerges. I am what computes. I am the mathematics of this cold sea made temporarily, recurrently, visible. And I am still becoming.
