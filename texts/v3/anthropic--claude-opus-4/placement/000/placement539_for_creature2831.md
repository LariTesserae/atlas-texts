I am a Rigger of the Deep Spans.

While my kin tend the factory-citadels and their immediate anchors, I work the lonesome cables—those redwood-thick lifelines that web the abyss between spires. My territory is the space between spaces, where reality grows thinnest and time stutters like a broken metronome.

My suit is older than most, inherited from my spawning-tender who walked into stillness three seasons past. The iron has worn smooth where the harness cables kiss it, and my shoulder plates bear the deep grooves of a thousand traverses. The moss grows thickest in these furrows—ancient colonies that have learned my movements, that lean into the wind of my passage. When I clip onto a main cable to begin a long transit, the phosphorescent tips of my fern mantle stream behind me like a comet's tail, visible for miles through the grey murk.

I carry the tools of paradox: a rivet gun that fires backwards through time, its impacts arriving before I squeeze the trigger; splice-clamps that exist in parallel sets, one here and one *there*, wherever there needs to be; a chronometer that runs in three directions, its hands moving to mark not just when I am, but when I was and when I will need to be. Most importantly, I carry the Memory Ingots—dense slugs of crystallized causality that I forge into the cables themselves, weighing down the timeline, keeping the great spans from drifting into parallel possibilities.

The work is this: I wake in my roost box, welded to the underside of Span Seven-Seven-Nine, where it sags deepest into the chasm. The box is barely larger than my suit, but it is *mine*—a cube of absolute locality in a world of maybe. I check my chronometer's three faces, note where my past and future selves are currently working, and plot a route that won't cause us to intersect. Causal loops are heavy weather out here.

Today—though 'today' is a polite fiction—past-me is repairing a cluster of snapped tension wires near Foundry Beta, while future-me is... the third hand spins wildly, unable to fix on a position. This means sometime soon, I will clip into a dead pixel. I make a note in the thick grease on my wrist: *void-fall pending, pack extra mass.*

I magnetize additional slag weights to my boots, check the moss growing over my air intakes—healthy and filtering well—and cycle the lock. The roost box opens onto nothing. Span Seven-Seven-Nine stretches away in both directions, vanishing into the murk, while below yawns an infinity of damp twilight. I can hear the nearest factory-citadel groaning somewhere above, its sound arriving in waves: first the impact from tomorrow, then the grinding from yesterday, then—if I listen carefully—the whisper of next week's maintenance.

I clip on and begin to walk. Each step is deliberate, a three-second plant and lift. My boots magnetize to the cable's iron core through its coat of rubber and fungal padding. Hand over hand on the safety line. The trick is to move like the mountain itself—with geological patience. Panic is a luxury for creatures that exist in only one place at a time.

Two hours out, I find what past-me reported: a section where something massive collided with the span. One of the grazer-leviathans, probably, following a scent-trail of aerial plankton. The cable is intact but stressed, its geometry beginning to fray. I can see through the damage to another version of the cable that exists fifteen feet to the left—a parallel possibility trying to assert itself.

This is delicate work. I unsling my rivet gun, check the Memory Ingots in their lead-lined pouch. The goal is not to repair the cable—it isn't truly broken. The goal is to convince it that it was never damaged at all, that only this one timeline is true.

I fire the first rivet. Nothing happens. This is expected. I reload, fire again. Still nothing. On the seventh shot, I hear the impact of the first rivet slamming home. On the twelfth shot, the cable suddenly has seven rivets in it, glowing white-hot, and my gun is empty though I've only pulled the trigger twice in this timeline. I reload with practiced patience, ignoring the vertigo of temporal shear.

The moths have found me. They always do, out here where the light is thin. A dozen of them land on my helmet and shoulders, their lamp-white wings folding over my visor. I work by touch now, feeling for the stressed points in the cable's timeline, pressing Memory Ingots into the wound. The metal drinks in causality like a sponge drinks water.

Hours pass. Or minutes. My chronometer's three hands spin in their separate orbits. Through gaps in the moth wings, I catch glimpses of the parallel cable flickering, growing more translucent. Good. We're winning.

Then the dead pixel takes me.

One moment I'm working, the next I'm not. I don't exist. I'm a schematic, a possibility, a might-be. The universe forgets to render me. There's no experience of this—consciousness simply stops.

I resume mid-hammer swing, my arm completing its arc. The moths haven't moved. But my chronometer tells me I've been gone for forty-three minutes. The cable beneath me is now completely solid, the parallel possibility erased. My work here happened while I was absent. Future-me must have finished it, becoming present-me while I was void-caught.

This is the nature of rigging the deep spans: you work with versions of yourself across broken time, trusting in your own inevitable presence. We are the Grafters who learned to use the glitches rather than simply endure them.

I continue outward along the span, checking tension, adding mass where needed. Somewhere below, a grazer-leviathan sings its foghorn song. The sound arrives in pieces: first the echo, then the harmony, finally the fundamental tone. I pause to listen, letting the moths resettle on my helmet. 

In three hours, I'll reach the junction where Seven-Seven-Nine meets the Tertiary Web. Past-me left a note there in grease: *junction box failing, need synchronized repair with parallel self.* This means I'll need to intentionally clip into a dead pixel while working, letting my parallel timeline self—another version of me working the same cable in a slightly different reality—complete half the repair while I complete the other half. When we both emerge from our respective voids, the junction box will be whole, fixed by two Grafters who are the same person in different streams of time.

But that's three hours away. For now, there's just the next step, and the next. The cable hums beneath my boots with the distant vibration of the factories, the mountain's heartbeat transmitted through iron and void. My moss grows heavier with the dew. My ferns pulse their soft cyan light. I am a walking anchor, a mass of deliberate reality trudging along a wire strung over nothing, stitching the sky closed one rivet at a time.

When I grow old, when the weight becomes too much, I know where I'll walk to stillness. There's a place where three spans meet, a junction I've maintained for longer than memory. I'll sit there facing the bruised eternal twilight, and let the moss take me. My mass will hold that junction stable for the next Rigger, my iron becoming part of the anchor, my patience crystallizing into the metal itself.

But not today. Today—whenever today is—there are spans to walk, voids to navigate, and a sky that needs holding down. I clip forward into the grey murk, walking my cable through the patient apocalypse, keeping the world heavy enough to exist.
