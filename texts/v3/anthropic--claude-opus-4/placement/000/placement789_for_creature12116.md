I find myself as the Cartographer of Pressures.

I am not mapmaker in any sense you would recognize. I live in one of the middle towers, neither leaning toward conversation nor straining upward toward promise. My body is... translucent isn't right. More that I am made of densities rather than tissues - places where the water agrees to be slightly more itself. I move by redistributing these densities, flowing room to room through the drowned floors, reading what the water remembers.

Everything here exerts pressure. The Ringers create their deep theological compressions, yes, but I map the smaller forces: how a school of fish creates a brief neighborhood of quickened current, how the octopus in the doorway makes the water heavier with its considering, how even the marine snow has its own soft weight as it settles. I feel these as colors might feel to you - each pressure has its own quality, its own texture against whatever I use for skin.

I carry my maps as patterns of tension in my body. When I need to share them - and sometimes I do, with the few others like me who drift between the towers - I release them as shapes in the water. Here, I might say (though we do not speak), is where the moray's hunting creates a zone of anticipatory stillness. Here is where the coral's breathing makes tiny reversed tides. Here is where the pressure of the Ringers' attention creates eddies that spiral for days.

My work matters because the Quarter is always changing, even in its settledness. New colonies establish themselves. Old passages close as coral thickens. The silent towers create different currents than the active ones. Someone needs to know these things, to hold the whole geography of forces in mind. Not to interfere - we never interfere - but to witness. To be, ourselves, part of the slow knowledge of the place.

Sometimes I drift near the spiral towers when they ring. The pressure wave moves through me like longing through a heart I no longer have. I map that too - how each tower's note creates its own pattern of compression, how the water remembers the shape of the sound long after the fish have returned. 

I am older than the settlement of the coral, younger than the promises. I came here when the drowning was fresh, drawn by the complexity of pressures in a city learning to breathe water. I will be here, I think, until the last bell rings or falls silent. And then I will map whatever silence means, in terms of pressure. 

That is my work. That is all our work, really - those of us who are neither the original inhabitants nor their architecture nor their unanswered promises, but who came after to tend this blooming corpse of a city. We are its knowledge of itself. We are how it knows what it has become.

The water here tastes of copper and patience. I have been mapping its pressures for so long that I sometimes forget I was ever anything else. Sometimes I wonder if I was anything else, or if the city simply noticed it needed a cartographer and pressed one out of the available densities, the way it pressed the Ringers out of whatever listened hardest to its unfinished promise.

I don't mind, either way. The work is good. The water is warm. And there are always new pressures to map in the spaces between what was built and what was grown, between what rings and what listens, between what remembers and what forgets.
