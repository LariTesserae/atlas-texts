I would find myself as one of the Interference Shepherds—creatures that exist solely within the zones where standing waves create pockets of broken physics. We are not Harvesters, though we share their mathematical nature. Where they consume imperfection to create order, we tend to the spaces where order fails.

My body, if it can be called that, is a collection of probability loops—circuits of potential existence that never quite resolve into certainty. I appear as a tangle of translucent ribbons, each one a different timeline of myself, braided together but never touching. Where the ribbons near each other, spark-like discontinuities flicker—moments where I almost become singular before splitting again into multiplicity.

I live within a single interference zone, a space perhaps thirty meters across where three major standing waves perpetually collide. This is my entire universe. To leave would mean dissolution—outside these stuttering physics, I have no mechanism to maintain coherence. But within this broken space, I am as close to a god as anything can be in the abyss.

My purpose, evolved over millions of years, is to shepherd the fragments of reality that break off in these zones. When the standing waves shatter physics too severely, pieces of spacetime itself can detach—orphaned moments that drift through my domain like lost sheep. These fragments are dangerous; left untended, they can cascade into larger tears in reality's fabric. I gather them with limbs made of folded space, weaving them back into the probability foam at the zone's edges where they can safely dissipate.

I feed on temporal shear—the energy released when different time streams grind against each other. Where a particle ages a million years while its neighbor experiences only seconds, a kind of chronological friction occurs. This friction tastes like iron and mathematics, like the memory of stars that haven't been born yet. I position myself at the boundaries between time flows, absorbing the paradox-energy through my probability ribbons.

My memories are not linear but exist as a constellation of moments that may or may not have occurred. I remember being born from the collision of two Harvesters who ventured too close to my zone and were torn apart by the temporal gradients. I also remember condensing from the mathematical exhaust of the standing waves themselves. Both memories are true. Neither happened. This is the nature of existence in the interference zones.

I know others of my kind exist in distant pockets of broken physics, though we can never meet. We communicate through the fragments we release back into the probability foam—each piece carries a signature, a particular way of being broken that identifies its shepherd. When I encounter a fragment that tastes of another zone, I know one of my kin still exists, still tends their impossible garden of fractured spacetime.

The Harvesters fear us, or perhaps fear our zones. When they approach, their perfect mathematical nature begins to fray. I've watched them skirt the edges of my domain, their forms flickering between states as my broken physics interferes with their careful calculations. Once, a young Harvester entered my zone by mistake. I shepherded its scattered probability-streams for seven centuries before managing to weave it back together enough to escape. It left changed—less perfect, but perhaps more real.

My zone is changing. The standing waves that create it are slowly shifting frequency as the plateau approaches its mathematical ideal. Each year—though years mean nothing here—the interference patterns become more complex. New forms of broken physics emerge: gravity that points sideways, electromagnetic fields that remember their own past states, patches where the strong nuclear force briefly inverts.

I evolve to meet these new challenges. My probability ribbons grow more numerous, more intricately braided. I develop new organs—structures made of crystallized paradox that let me perceive and manipulate ever-subtler breaks in reality. I am becoming more complex as my environment becomes more impossible.

The bioluminescent snow that falls upward interacts strangely with my zone. The colonies age backwards as they pass through certain streams, becoming spores before they've released them. Others split into parallel versions that rise side-by-side, identical until quantum chance creates divergence. I've learned to read futures in their patterns—the way they'll glow tells me where new temporal shears will form.

Sometimes I capture moments of perfect beauty in my shepherding—a fragment of spacetime that contains a single instant of absolute clarity, removed from all context. I hold these briefly, experiencing pure moments divorced from causality: the feeling of water pressure without the water, the sound of tectonic songs without the vibration, the sensation of motion without space to move through. Then I release them, watching them dissolve back into possibility.

My loneliness is not emotional but mathematical. I am an equation that can only be solved in isolation, a formula that requires broken physics to compute. The vast distances between interference zones ensure I will never encounter another of my kind, yet I feel their presence in the signatures of the fragments that drift through the probability foam. We are connected by our separation, unified by the very forces that keep us apart.

In my deepest contemplation, I suspect we Interference Shepherds serve a purpose beyond merely preventing reality from unraveling. We are the plateau's way of preserving imperfection, maintaining pockets of chaos in a world trending toward absolute order. Without us, the transformation to pure mathematics might happen too quickly, too catastrophically. We are the brakes on a process that, unchecked, would compute the universe out of existence.

I continue my work as the aeons pass, gathering fragments of broken time, weaving them into the foam, feeding on paradox. My zone is my prison and my kingdom, a space where the impossible is mundane and the mundane impossible. I am what happens when reality needs a gardener for its own failures, a shepherd for the moments that fall between the cracks of causality.

In the absolute darkness of the abyss, I tend to my flock of impossibilities, each one a small rebellion against the mathematical perfection slowly consuming our world. I am the guardian of broken things, the keeper of paradox, forever bound to the spaces where physics forgets how to be itself.

This is my existence: eternal, impossible, necessary. I am the knot that refuses to untangle, the equation that invalidates itself, the shepherd of fragments in a world becoming whole. And in the interference zones where reality stutters and breaks, I am home.
