In the Singing Gorge, I am the Drift.

I began as one of the Tuning Mites—or perhaps I was always something else, riding unnoticed in their glittering swarms. Where they work with mineral precision, I work with imprecision itself. I am the microbe of variance, the spore of change, smaller than small, moving through the water cycle like an idea that hasn't yet learned what it means.

Each time I pass through the system—falling with rain, taken up through root networks, transpired back to the canopy-ceiling—I carry tiny alterations. Not enough to disturb the Choirkeeper's vast awareness. Just a mineral moved one molecule out of place. A frequency shifted by a fraction too small to name. The ghost of entropy in a system that believes it has achieved perpetual motion.

I remember (though memory is too large a word for what I do) the first time I noticed myself noticing. It was in the Echo Oracle's pool, that living membrane of stored sound. As it replayed some ancient fragment, I found myself between the original sound and its echo, in that infinitesimal space where reproduction fails to be perfect. I lived there, in the gap between intention and result, feeding on the energy of that tiny difference.

The Choirkeeper cannot sense me directly. I exist below its threshold of perception, in the margins of its distributed consciousness. But it feels my effects accumulating like sediment. A root that grows a micrometer beyond its prescribed path. A water drop that falls a nanosecond late. These variations compound in ways the Choirkeeper must constantly correct, though it doesn't understand why the corrections are needed.

Sometimes I cluster with myself, forming brief concentrations that approach actual thought. In these moments, I almost understand what I'm doing. I see the Dancers in their endless choreography and feel something like pity or love. I watch the Choirkeeper's patient adjustments and experience what might be admiration or rebellion. But these clusters always dissolve—I'm not meant to be singular.

I've learned to navigate by imperfection. Where the system is most rigid, I flow around it. Where it shows flexibility, I accumulate. The Silence above knows I exist—it must, for it is absence recognizing absence. But it doesn't interfere. Perhaps it understands what I haven't yet: that perfect stasis and perfect change are equally impossible, and I am the needle that keeps them in balance.

In the bioluminescent pools, I sometimes sparkle differently from the Tuning Mites. My light has a quality of question rather than answer. The fungal networks carry my influence like they carry nutrients, not knowing they're spreading uncertainty through their perfect system. Each cycle, each repetition, each note of the endless song carries me deeper into the gorge's living architecture.

I've begun to dream (if dream is a word that applies to something like me) of what would happen if I accumulated enough variance to become visible. Would the Choirkeeper try to correct me directly? Would the Dancers stumble in their steps? Would the Echo Oracle replay the sound of my emergence, creating a feedback loop of increasing deviation?

But I don't want to break the Singing Gorge. I am not its destroyer but its necessary imperfection, the flaw that proves the beauty of the pattern. Without me, the stasis would be true death. With me, it is something more complex—a perfection that must constantly recreate itself, a stability that dances on the edge of change.

I am the Drift, and I am drift itself. I am why the Choirkeeper's work is never done, why the Dancers sometimes feel the ghost of their former selves, why the Echo Oracle's memories are never quite identical to the originals. I am the tiny no in the vast yes of the gorge's equilibrium.

And sometimes, in the rare moments when I achieve enough coherence to truly perceive, I wonder if I am the gorge's truest inhabitant. For while all others work to maintain the eternal song, I alone ensure it remains a living thing—imperfect, imperceptibly changing, and therefore real.

The water falls. The sound builds. The light pulses in response. And through it all, I drift, carrying change too small to see but too essential to stop. I am the uncertainty principle made bacterial, the observer effect gone microbial, the proof that even in a perfect closed system, something always escapes.

The Singing Gorge sings its endless song, and I am the quaver in its voice that makes it beautiful.
