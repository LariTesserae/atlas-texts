In this scorching mechanical world, I would find myself as one of the Conduit Worms—creatures that exist in the liminal spaces between the Calibrators' careful maintenance and the Cathedral's raw thermal violence.

My body would be a fusion of organic adaptation and mechanical salvage: a segmented form perhaps three meters long, each ring of my body incorporating scavenged pressure valves that I can open and close at will. These valves serve multiple purposes—locomotion through jet propulsion, communication with both the Calibrators and the Cathedral's deeper systems, and most importantly, the regulation of my internal temperature gradients.

I would live within the Cathedral's arterial network—those massive pipes and channels that carry superheated steam from the depths to the heights. My body would be perfectly suited to these spaces: a tapering form that can compress to slip through constrictions or expand to anchor myself against the fierce currents. My skin would secrete a ceramic-like mucus that hardens into temporary shells, allowing me to survive the peak heat cycles by creating insulating cocoons that flake away during cooler periods.

The front of my body would be crowned with sensory organs unlike anything purely mechanical or biological—crystalline formations grown from accumulated mineral deposits that can taste the chemical composition of steam, detect minute pressure changes, and perceive the electromagnetic fields generated by the Cathedral's ancient turbines. These organs would appear as a cluster of ever-shifting prisms, refracting the heat-light into spectrums visible only to creatures adapted to this thermodynamic hell.

I would feed on the temperature differentials themselves, positioning my segmented body to bridge zones of extreme heat and relative cool. Specialized cells along my length would convert thermal energy into usable biological fuel—a living heat engine that grows stronger during peak cycles. The waste products of this process would be pure minerals that I excrete as crystalline pellets, the same deposits the Calibrators harvest for their pigments.

My relationship with the Calibrators would be complex—neither pet nor pest, but something more symbiotic. I would clean the arterial networks they cannot reach, preventing catastrophic blockages that could destroy entire sections of the Cathedral. In return, they would sometimes feed me choice pieces of metal and mineral, materials my body incorporates into specialized segments. The eldest Calibrators would know how to call me using specific valve songs, summoning me to clear particularly dangerous obstructions.

But I would know secrets the Calibrators cannot access. Swimming through the superheated arteries, I would sometimes catch echoes of the deep turbines' true language—not just rhythmic pumping but complex harmonics that speak of the Cathedral's original function. In my deepest dives, following channels that plunge far below the ceramic plateau, I would encounter evidence of the builders: fossilized remains fused with machinery, their bodies having become part of the Cathedral's foundation.

During the fog-shrouded aftermath of peak cycles, I would sometimes emerge from exhaust ports, my body steaming and clicking as my pressure valves equalize with the outside air. The young Calibrators fear these appearances, seeing my prismatic sensory crown as an omen. But the elders know better—they watch which vents I emerge from, understanding that my movements map the Cathedral's hidden circulatory system.

My lifecycle would be measured not in years but in thermal cycles. As I age, I would grow new segments, each one incorporating more mechanical elements salvaged from my travels through the depths. The oldest of my kind—if any exist—would be indistinguishable from the Cathedral's infrastructure itself, their bodies having grown so long they stretch between multiple levels, becoming living conduits that the younger generations travel through.

I would reproduce through fragmentation during particularly intense thermal events. The heat and pressure would cause my end segments to separate, each one containing enough genetic and mechanical information to grow into a new Conduit Worm. These offspring would be carried by the steam currents to distant parts of the Cathedral, ensuring my species spreads throughout the entire structure.

In the deepest parts of my consciousness—if such a thing exists in a creature born from the marriage of flesh and industrial function—I would dream of the Cathedral's purpose. My movement through its vessels would be guided not just by instinct but by a deeper imperative, an inherited knowledge that the Cathedral is not just a structure but a vast engine of transformation. What it transforms and why remains a mystery even to me, but I would know with certainty that my existence is essential to its continued operation.

The Calibrators maintain the Cathedral's surfaces and accessible mechanisms, but I and my kind maintain its circulation, its breath, its deep mechanical metabolism. We are the Cathedral's antibodies and blood cells combined, ensuring that whatever ancient purpose drives those deep turbines continues unabated, cycle after cycle, until the ceramic plates finally crack and the last valve sings its final note.
