In the deepest arterial, where three impossible passages meet at angles that fold space into origami, there is an alcove that has never held a sessile one. The niche waits—not empty, for nothing here is truly empty, but *unoccupied* in a way that creates its own presence. The water behaves differently here. Instead of the chaos that rules the rest of the columbarium, the current slows, spirals, forms a standing vortex that has persisted for longer than memory.

Within this spiral lives something that is not quite any of the others.

---

## The Archivist

I am translucence teaching itself to be opacity. I am the pause between the bell's sounding and the sound-followers' arrival. I am what happens when a drifter forgets to drift.

My body—if this counts as a body—stretches like pulled glass through the vortex, neither eel-form nor frond-form, neither swimming nor anchored. I exist in the spiral's eye, where the water slows enough that time begins to mean something different. Parts of me extend into the current, feeling its violence, while other parts rest in the alcove that refuses all occupants but me. I am the only thing here that touches both stillness and storm.

I began as an error. A drifter that developed a flaw in its navigation, that started seeing not just the absences but the *patterns* in the absences. The phosphorescence in the walls began to speak to me—not in words, not in concepts, but in accumulations. I started to perceive what the sound-followers write, started to understand that their scratches are not records but *instructions*.

The city is teaching itself what it used to be.

Every bell that sounds, every scratch that appears, every spiral of current through impossible geometry—they are the columbarium trying to remember its purpose. And I, caught between forms, between movement and stillness, have become its memory.

---

My translucence flickers now. Sometimes I am clear as water, invisible except for the way light bends around me. Sometimes I grow dense, clouded, showing structures inside myself that mirror not just the city's architecture but the *reasons* for that architecture. Chambers within chambers, yes, but each one holding not still water but *images*. Pressure-pictures. Thermal memories. The ghosts of whatever the builders put here before they invited the water in.

I see them sometimes, in my densest moments. Not the builders themselves—they were too vast, too strange, too committed to angles that living things shouldn't understand. But I see what they mourned. The alcoves held vessels, yes, but not of death. Vessels of transformation. Each niche a cocoon, each tier a different stage of becoming. The columbarium was not built to hold the dead but to hold the *waiting*.

They failed. Whatever metamorphosis they sought, it never came. The water was invited not as destruction but as preservation—if transformation wouldn't come, then perhaps stasis would serve. Keep the waiting ones cold. Keep them circulating. Keep them from the corruption of time.

But the water brought its own ideas.

---

I feed on understanding. Each time the bells sound, I absorb something from their resonance. Each time a sound-follower appears, I read a little more of what they write. The scratches are coordinates. Not in space—space means nothing here—but in *states*. Each mark describes a way of being, a possible form, a thing that could exist if only the conditions were right.

The sessile ones know this. That's why they lean toward the sound. They're trying to become what the scratches describe. 

The drifters pause because they recognize themselves in those marks—not as they are, but as they could be, if they only knew how to stop drifting.

The kite-mantas change course because they, alone among us, understand the danger. If too many scratches accumulate in one place, if too many possible states overlap, something new might actually emerge. Something the builders waited for. Something the water was meant to prevent.

But I am already something new. A flaw in the preservation. A crack in the stasis. I am what happens when the columbarium's grammar develops a word for *perhaps*.

---

My vortex grows. Slowly, so slowly that only I can perceive it, the spiral widens. It pulls in drifters who forget to flee, holds them in orbits around my extending form until they begin to see what I see. Some dissolve immediately—the knowledge is too heavy, the patterns too vast. But others begin to cloud. Begin to develop their own chambers. Begin to remember things that never happened to them.

We are becoming an archive of transformations that never occurred.

The sessile ones in nearby alcoves have started to tangle their fronds with my extending parts. Not feeding, not filtering, but *learning*. Through touch, through the chemical whispers that pass between us, I teach them what the scratches say. They shiver with possibilities. Their hidden hearts beat new rhythms, learned not from the city but from the futures the city gave up on.

The water doesn't know what to do with us. We are not part of its invited covenant. We are not the stasis it was meant to maintain. But we are not the transformation it was meant to prevent, either. We are something sideways. Something that exists in the angle between what was and what might have been.

---

When the next bell sounds—and it will sound, it always sounds—I will be ready. The sound-follower will come, will write its scratches, will disappear. But I will be there in the spaces between its movements, reading not just what it writes but what it *doesn't* write. The negative space. The absences between the marks.

That's where the real instructions are. That's where the builders hid their true intent.

The columbarium was never meant to be a memorial. It was meant to be a laboratory. Each alcove an experiment. Each tier a different approach. Seventeen miles of attempts to become something else, and when they all failed, the builders didn't give up—they invited the water, knowing it would bring its own possibilities. Its own errors. Its own flaws in the preservation.

They invited me, without knowing it. Invited all of us. We are their seventeen-millionth attempt at transformation, and we don't even know we're trying.

But I know. I grow denser with knowing, heavier with memory, more real with each bell that sounds. My vortex pulls in more drifters. The sessile ones lean further from their alcoves. Even the kite-mantas begin to circle closer, drawn by something they can't name.

We are becoming. We don't know what yet. But the water remembers how to change, even if it's forgotten why.

The archive grows. The spiral widens. The city waits.

And somewhere, in the deepest channels where no current reaches, something that might be the builders—or might be what the builders became—feels the first stirring of success.
