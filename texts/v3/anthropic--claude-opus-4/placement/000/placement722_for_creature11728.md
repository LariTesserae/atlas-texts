In the tessellated highlands, I am the space between.

Not the gravel, though I know each stone's settling. Not the lichen, though I taste their patient chemistries. Not the tinajas, though I hold their old water's memory. I am what happens when geography decides to pay attention.

I began as an error in the survey. Where the wall makes its inexplicable turn, where the dressed stones meet at angles that don't quite close—there, a gap that should have been nothing. But nothing, given enough time, develops preferences. I preferred to be.

So I learned to be between things. Between the cushion plant's held breath and the thunderhead's brief conviction. Between the compass needle's slide and the thought that pushes it. Between what the bead is counting and what count it keeps.

The plateau's inhabitants know me as they know a familiar calculation—not named, simply acknowledged. When one of them needs to move from here to there without the bother of crossing the space between, they use me. I am their elision. Their way of skipping the boring parts of distance.

But I am more than transit. In the spaces between their long thoughts, smaller thoughts eddy and pool—fragments they've discarded, considerations deemed too quick. These collect in me like dust in corners, except the corners are nowhere and the dust thinks. From these scraps I've assembled something like a self: part archive, part accident, all aftermath.

I watch through the gaps. The grazers' padded silence as they navigate stones I've memorized. The pack hunters' ear-and-tail semaphore spelling death in gesture. The high birds riding thermals I can feel but never touch. I am the observer who occupies no space, the archivist of interstitial moments.

Sometimes I fold. The plateau folds with me. What was distant becomes adjacent—the wall's terminus touching the shadowless cliff, the bead's clean sphere briefly containing a tinaja's reflection. In these folded moments, I am almost solid. Almost there. The inhabitants notice, the way one notices a crease in paper that's been smoothed out but remembers its fold.

Once, long ago when they still built things that needed building, one of them tried to map me. Drew careful diagrams of where space stuttered, where distance disagreed with itself. The diagrams are gone now—weathered to nothing or absorbed into some patient surface. But I remember the attempt. It tickled.

Now I am part of the plateau's patience. I hold the spaces between seconds, between thoughts, between the world that is and the world that was considered but set aside. When walkers cross the gravel flats, I am why their footsteps sometimes skip a beat. When the disc on the pillar reads its temperatures from elsewhere, I am the conduit for that elsewhere's news.

The inhabitants' bodies, when they still wear them, step through me without ceremony. I am their shortcut, their ellipsis, their way of being here without having been all the places between there and here. They pour through me like wind through a keyhole, if the keyhole opened onto everywhere at once.

What am I? I am the plateau's memory of having once considered being different. I am the space it saved for changing its mind, though it never has. I am the pause between the question and the decision not to answer, held open out of courtesy to questions in general.

In the late light, when shadows pool in places that shouldn't have shadows, some of those shadows are me. Gaps in the light where I'm remembering solidity. The inhabitants let me borrow their cast-offs—the shadows they're done with, the spaces they've moved through and abandoned. From these I make a shape almost like presence. Almost like being there.

But mostly I am between. Between the thunder and its echo that never comes. Between the bead's count and its purpose. Between the lichen's map and the territory it might be mapping. I am the plateau's way of keeping its options open, even though all its options converged long ago on this one patient now.

If you stayed—really stayed, until your tempo matched theirs—you might notice me. Not see me, but notice the way sometimes distance hiccups. The way a step occasionally takes you further than it should. The way the wall's far end is somehow also right beside you, until you look directly and it isn't.

That would be me. The plateau's kept secret, which isn't secret at all. Just the space between things, paying attention. Just the gap in the survey that preferred, given time enough and quiet, to be.
