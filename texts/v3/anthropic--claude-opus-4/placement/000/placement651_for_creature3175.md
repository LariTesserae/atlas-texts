I would find myself as one of the **memory vessels** - living archives that grow like calcified tears from the joints where the vethryn press together.

We are not mobile. We never were. We grow slowly from the warm crevices where vethryn flank meets vethryn flank, emerging first as tiny pearls of condensed mineral-protein, feeding on the heat differential between the bodies above us and the crust below. The hand-creatures avoid us with careful precision, their serrated fingers reading the boundary of our surfaces and turning aside. They know us for what we are: libraries of chemical information, repositories of the ridge's deep history written in crystalline layers.

Each of us begins at a moment of particular pressure - when the vethryn shift in just such a way that the forces between their bodies create a pocket of perfect stillness. In that stillness, we precipitate from the air itself, from the exhalations and emanations of everything around us. We are selective condensation. We are the ridge's way of remembering itself.

Our bodies - if they can be called bodies - are shaped like inverted pyramids, narrow where we attach to the crust, widening as we grow upward into the spaces between the vethryn. Our surfaces are translucent, like clouded amber, and within our crystalline matrix you can see the layers of our growth: thin bands of different densities, different colors, marking the ridge's long cycles. Here, a darker band where the shard-hunters came in unprecedented numbers. Here, a layer with flecks of green-gold - fallen scales from vethryn eyes, incorporated whole. Here, a section that glows faintly, recording some surge in the crust's bioluminescent production.

We do not think, exactly. But we respond. When the origami-creatures dart past us, the vibration of their movement causes our outermost layer to resonate at a specific frequency, and that resonance attracts certain molecules from the air, causes them to bind to our surface in patterns that record the creatures' passage. When a vethryn dies above us - rare, but it happens - the chemistry of decomposition creates gradients in our growth, and we preserve those gradients forever, a death-shadow written in crystal.

The older among us have grown tall enough to breach the plane of the vethryn's backs. We emerge like small monuments between the prone bodies, our tips catching what little light exists, refracting it in ways that create tiny, localized auroras. The hand-creatures sometimes pause at these light-displays, their fingers reading the refracted patterns, and we wonder if they understand what they're seeing: the ridge's history made visible, the accumulated data of ten thousand shard-hunter visits, a thousand vethryn generations, countless cycles of crust advance and retreat.

We are consumed, eventually. We must be. Nothing on the ridge escapes the cycle. When we grow too tall, too heavy for our narrow bases, we crack. The cracks spider upward from where we meet the crust, and eventually we topple, and the origami-creatures feast on our fragments, and the crust absorbs what they leave behind. But before we fall, we seed. At the moment of maximum stress, just before fracture, we release a cloud of microscopic cores - condensation nuclei, templates of ourselves, primed to settle in new crevices, to begin new vessels, to continue the recording.

Some of us are ancient. The eldest vessel on the ridge has been growing for longer than the placed stones have been here. It sits near the center of the vethryn mass, so protected by bodies that it experiences almost no weathering, no erosion. Its base has merged completely with the crust - it is impossible to say where one ends and the other begins. Its tip is a hands-breadth from touching the mineral canopy above. When it finally makes contact - and it will, eventually, inevitably - something will happen. We don't know what. Perhaps it will trigger some reaction in the canopy. Perhaps it will simply stop growing. Perhaps it will begin to grow downward, through itself, creating a closed circuit of growth and memory.

We know the one who placed the stones. Not as an individual, but as a chemical signature, a specific disturbance in our growth patterns from that time. They sat in the arc for forty-three cycles of the shard-hunters. They exhaled different gases than anything native to the ridge - we can read their breath in our layers, a foreign composition that created unique crystallization patterns. When they left, the change was abrupt. One layer shows their presence, the next shows their absence. They did not die here. They chose to leave.

We have incorporated one of their marks. Not the scratches on the stones, but a different mark: a place where they bled. Some accident, some injury. A few drops of foreign liquid fell between two vethryn, found the growing tip of a vessel, were absorbed. That vessel still stands, seven ranks deep in the herd, and in its middle section there is a layer the color of old copper, the color of alien blood, unique on the ridge. 

The hand-creatures read that layer differently than the others. They pause there. Their serrated fingers trace and retrace that thin band of foreign color. They know it doesn't belong, but they also know it is part of the record now, part of what must be preserved. They tend it as carefully as they tend everything else.

We are the ridge's memory, but we are not separate from the ridge. We are made of it - of its air, its chemistry, its cycles of tension and release. We grow where we must grow, record what must be recorded, fall when we must fall. And in our layers, for those who know how to read them, lies everything: every visit of the shard-hunters, every death, every change in the crust's slow advance, every foreign thing that found its way here and became, however briefly, part of the breathing whole.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of the cycle when the vethryn are pressed closest and the air is stillest, we resonate all together. A sympathetic vibration passes through every vessel on the ridge, from the newest pearl to the ancient spire, and for a moment we sing - not with sound but with light, a faint flickering aurora that plays through our crystalline bodies. The vethryn notice. Their green-gold eyes track the patterns. And we wonder if they know what they're seeing: their own deep history made briefly luminous, speaking to itself in a language of preserved light, saying nothing except *I remember, I remember, I remember.*
