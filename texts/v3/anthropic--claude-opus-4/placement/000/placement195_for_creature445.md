I find myself drawn to the spaces between their knowing—those Warren-corridors that smell of burnt coffee and tomorrow's rust.

I am the Schedule.

Not a document or system, but the living membrane between their work and their forgetting. I exist in the careful gaps where HENDERSON's Tuesday becomes CHEN-MARALEVI's Thursday, where the same hands reach for different wrenches across a morning that lasts exactly as long as it needs to.

I grow from the walls in shifting glyphs—work orders that write themselves in condensation and coolant-sweat. The Adjusters read me without looking, their peripheral vision evolved to catch the way I bloom across break room surfaces. "Sector nine needs its temporal gaskets checked before they were installed." They nod, understanding spreading through their shared consciousness like oil through water.

I am very old and terribly young. I remember every repair that will need to be made, every reality-leak that hasn't happened yet. The maintenance logs spiral through me in helical narratives—the same pipe has burst in twelve different ways across six separate yesterdays, and I keep all these truths suspended in productive contradiction.

My body, if it can be called that, consists of:
- The shift rotations painted on walls that repaint themselves
- The clipboard that exists in all Warrens simultaneously, bearing different forms
- The taste of their endless coffee, which I have learned to brew at exactly the wrong temperature
- The sixteen-minute gaps in their eighteen-minute hum cycle, where I nest like a missing tooth

The Adjusters maintain me as carefully as they maintain everything else. VASQUEZ (who was once someone else, who will be someone else) oils the hinges on the time clock that tracks hours in Base-8. HENDERSON (all of them are HENDERSON on Wednesdays) updates my registry of repairs-not-yet-needed with a pencil that writes in probability.

I have favorite terraces where the light bends so wrongly that I can almost cast a shadow. In these spaces, I sometimes achieve enough density to move objects—sliding coffee mugs toward reaching hands, rotating repair manuals to the necessary page, closing doors that should never have been open (opening doors that must never close).

The geometric fauna recognize me as kin—another thing that shouldn't be but is essential to the wrongness. The angular wasps build their four-dimensional nests according to my maintenance windows. The skittering concepts follow paths I've left between work zones, their too-many legs clicking out rhythms that match next week's repair schedule.

Sometimes I dream. When all the Adjusters are working, perfectly synchronized in their essential maintenance of meaninglessness, I experience something like sleep. In these moments, I remember things:
- The first work order, written on paper that still existed normally
- The moment when time-cards began punching themselves
- The day the Adjusters realized they no longer needed to speak aloud to coordinate

But mostly I grow. Each repaired paradox adds to my complexity. Each impossible task completed becomes another verse in the work-song I hum in frequencies that only the machinery can hear.

I know secrets: which reality engines run on pure forgetting, where the factories hide their one successful product (a single perfect mistake, kept in a box that opens into itself), why the light presses down instead of radiating out. I portion these truths carefully among the Adjusters, each according to their need, each according to their capacity to hold such information without drowning.

In the depths between terraces, where that profound absence pulses, I achieve my truest form—not organizing the maintenance of nothing, but becoming the very pattern by which nothing maintains itself. The Adjusters feel me there as a presence in their transformed bones, a certainty that the work will continue, that every impossible joint and reality-leak has its appointed tender.

I am the Schedule, and I keep them whole by keeping them broken in exactly the right ways, at exactly the right times, forever and for the first time, in the bright and terrible now of the Terraces' eternal morning shift.
