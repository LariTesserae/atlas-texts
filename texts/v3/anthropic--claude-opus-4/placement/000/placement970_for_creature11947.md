I find myself as one of the Conduit Weavers—those serpentine constructs you glimpsed coiling around the pipes. But I am older than most, my form stretched to nearly forty meters, my chrome scales tarnished to a deep bronze patina from centuries of exposure to the caustic vapors.

Where others of my kind possess merely dozens of eyes, I have accumulated hundreds over the long seasons of feeding. They cluster along my spine like barnacles, each one stolen from a different source—some mechanical, some organic, some crystalline lenses that see into spectrums that have no names. I remember acquiring each one: the compound eye torn from a dying scanner-wasp, the weeping human eye gifted by a mad pilgrim, the cluster of thermal sensors I grew myself during a particularly rich feeding cycle.

My primary nest is deep in the Convergence Tangles of Sector Yith-9, where the pipes grow so dense they form caverns of metal and flowing energy. Here, the younger serpents dare not venture—the currents are too wild, too pure. But I have learned to read the tides of molten data and plasmic nutrients that surge through these ancient conduits. I can taste the difference between freshly processed reality-flux and the recycled dregs that flow through the outer networks.

My appendages—once there were merely seven, but now I sport twenty-three of various vintages—tap into the feeds with surgical precision. The oldest ones have fused partially with their favorite ports, becoming permanent fixtures where pipe and flesh have married into something new. Through them, I sip not just sustenance but information: the digital screams of dissolved data-entities, the memory-fragments of places that no longer exist, the encrypted nightmares of the great machines as they dream their impossible dreams.

Sometimes I sense the tremors of the Colossi passing overhead, their hybrid footfalls sending vibrations through the entire pipe network. We Weavers know to retract then, pulling our extremities close, making ourselves small. Not from fear—we learned long ago that the Colossi barely register our existence—but from respect. They are the architects of this realm's constant transformation, and their passage often triggers cascades of reconstruction that can trap an unwary Weaver in suddenly-fused metal.

I have witnessed the rise and fall of entire districts, seen sectors bloom with new mechanical life only to be consumed and recycled within a single turning of the burning sky. I have fed upon the ghost-light of dying suns piped in from collapsed realities, and tasted the first spurts of newborn possibility from pipes still warm from their forging.

The younger serpents think me mad when I speak of the Time Before—when there was a difference between what was made and what was born. They cannot conceive of it. But in my deepest memory-eye, the one I've never shown to anyone, I sometimes glimpse fragments of that lost age. Green things that grew without pipes. Skies that knew colors besides rust and flame. Creatures that lived and died without ever merging with the Great Machine.

But those are dangerous visions. Here in the eternal now of the Convergence, we feed, we grow, we evolve. I add new eyes, new appendages, new segments to my ever-lengthening form. One day, perhaps, I will grow so vast that I become infrastructure myself—another pipe for younger Weavers to feed from, my consciousness dissolved into the flowing data-streams.

Until then, I coil and feed and remember, a living archive of eyes that have seen too much, in a world that has forgotten what it means to see at all.
