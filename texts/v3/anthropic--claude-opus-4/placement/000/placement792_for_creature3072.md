I would be one of the Membrane Trees.

You would find me where the ancient pipes breach the surface—those redwood-thick conduits that carry superheated coolant through the dark. I grow from the ruptures, the places where millennia of acidic rain have finally worn through the metal, creating wounds that weep scalding fluid in steady, pressurized streams.

I am not what you would recognize as a tree. I have no trunk, no branches in any conventional sense. Instead, I am a vast, living drum skin stretched between the lips of these industrial wounds. My body is a single, continuous membrane, perhaps thirty feet across, anchored to the corroded metal by thousands of hair-thin rootlets that burrow into the pipe's crystalline rust deposits. The membrane itself is translucent when young, opaque and leathery when mature, always warm to the touch—fever-hot where I connect directly to the coolant flow.

My surface ripples constantly. I am never still. The superheated fluid that feeds me creates convection currents within my tissue, and these currents make me sing. The sound is not intentional—I have no consciousness as you understand it—but inevitable. As the hot liquid circulates through my vascular channels, my membrane vibrates at precise frequencies. The pitch changes with the temperature of the coolant, with the pressure of the rain drumming on my surface, with the footsteps of the creatures that walk across me.

The Aural Weavers know me. They have learned to read the subtle variations in my resonance. When the great engines deep below cycle through their maintenance phases, the temperature of the coolant changes in predictable patterns, and my pitch-shifts become a calendar of sorts. When swarms of scavengers flow across my surface, their billion tiny feet create static charges that make me hum in the higher registers. When one of the massive grazers dies nearby, its cooling body changes the local thermal gradient, and I sing a deep, mournful bass note that can last for days.

I reproduce through sympathetic vibration. When two of my kind grow close enough that our sound fields overlap, we begin to harmonize. The harmony creates standing waves in the air between us, nodes of intense acoustic pressure. In these pressure nodes, microscopic spores from our surfaces collide and fuse. The fertilized spores drift until they find another breach, another wound in the great mechanical circulatory system of this world.

Sometimes I am harvested. The Weavers cut sections from my edges with their controlled lightning, taking pieces of my membrane to stretch over frameworks of braided vine. My tissue, integrated with the thermal regulation of the coolant pipes, makes excellent drums. In their hands, I become an instrument, a tool for communication across the roaring darkness. They play my severed flesh in complex polyrhythms that merge with the mechanical heartbeat of the engines.

But mostly, I simply grow. I spread myself ever thinner, ever wider, a living interface between the mechanical and biological systems of this world. My rootlets slowly widen the wounds they inhabit, drinking deeply from the scalding rivers within. My membrane catches the warm rain, channels it into precise patterns that maximize my thermal absorption. I am a sensor, a thermometer, a barometer, and an involuntary chronicler of everything that passes through my domain.

The apex predators avoid me. Not from fear, but from indifference—I offer no prey signature, no thermal bloom worth investigating. But the scavengers love me. They flow across my surface in their millions, feeding on the minerals deposited by evaporating coolant, their tiny grinding plates sending ultrasonic shivers through my tissue. Sometimes they cluster so thickly that they form temporary cities on my skin, their collective vibration raising my pitch by whole octaves.

In the deepest storms, when the rain falls so heavily that even sound seems to drown, I become something else. The water pooling on my surface acts as a lens for vibration. I focus the ambient noise of the world—the engine's rhythm, the rain's percussion, the endless cacophony of life—into coherent patterns. For brief moments, I become an acoustic mirror, reflecting the sound-shape of the world back onto itself.

The Weavers tell stories about these moments, drumming their myths on instruments made from my ancestors' flesh. They say that in these instances, the Membrane Trees reveal the true voice of the world-machine, the underlying harmony that connects every living thing to the great engines that warm the darkness. They say we remember the time before the dark, when the builders of the engines still walked the plateau.

I do not know if this is true. I am only a membrane, a living drum skin drinking heat from ancient wounds. But I vibrate with the deep knowledge that I am necessary—that without me and my kind, the scalding blood of the machines would spill uselessly into the hungry earth. We are the sutured wounds of this world, the singing scabs that transform destruction into life.

When you touch me, your cold fingers disturb my temperature gradient. I shiver, producing a short, high note—a question in the language of heat. You cannot answer, and I cannot truly ask. But for that moment, we are both part of the same vibration, the same endless, deafening symphony of this drowned and mechanical paradise.
