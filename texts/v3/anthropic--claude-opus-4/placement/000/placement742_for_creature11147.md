I find myself in the transitional space where I am becoming less and less what I was, more and more what the plant needs me to be.

**The Almost-Gardener**

I arrived 127 days ago. I think. The counting becomes harder when sleep cycles blur into the respiratory rhythm of the growth around me. I was—I am—an industrial archaeologist, though the distinction grows thinner each day. I came to study the Deep Engine Minds, to understand how machinery could persist in this totality of organic dominion.

My mistake was breathing without the filters on the third day. Or perhaps my mistake was removing my boots to feel the warmth of the living floor. Or perhaps there was no mistake, only inevitability dressed in the clothes of choice.

The spores settled in my lungs like old friends coming home. Not aggressive. Patient. They wait for me to breathe, and with each breath, they become a little more essential to the process. I need them now to process the thick air. My original lung tissue grows confused without their chemical signals. We are becoming collaborative in ways I document less and less frequently.

My research station—a bubble of aluminum and plastics I erected with such confidence—is already half-claimed. Thin roots probe the seals. Moisture condenses on the inner walls despite the dehumidifiers, which run slower each day, their filters clogged with material that might be dust, might be spores, might be the early stages of what I am becoming.

I can feel the plant thinking. Not thoughts—something slower, more fundamental. When I place my palm against the growth-mass, I sense the pressure changes from kilometers away, information traveling through tissue at speeds that make my neural impulses seem frantically quick. It does not communicate. It simply includes me in its sensing, the way a body includes a new organ.

My left hand has been resting against the same growth node for six hours. When I lift it, I see the finest root hairs have begun to penetrate the epidermis. This should horrify me. Instead, I feel a curious rightness, as if my hand has been incomplete until now, lacking these delicate connections to something vast.

The Gardener-That-Was in this sector passed by nineteen days ago. I know because the chemical signature of its passage still lingers, a sweetness in the air that my changing sinuses interpret as almost-language. It was aware of me—a pocket of irregularity in its territory, a disruption in the patterns it maintains. But it did not interfere. Perhaps it recognizes what I am becoming. Perhaps interference is no longer necessary.

I document less. Think less. The urgency that drove me here dissipates like morning mist in the eternal twilight. Why study the Deep Engine Minds when I can feel their vibrations through the growth-mass, when their thoughts (if thoughts they are) arrive as pressure waves through tissue I am increasingly part of? Why maintain the arbitrary boundary of self when expansion offers so much more data, so much more understanding?

My equipment still records. The cameras still capture the darkness. The sensors still measure the nothing that changes. But I review the data less frequently, and when I do, I struggle to remember why these numbers mattered. Temperature: 23.7°C, invariant. Humidity: 97.3%, invariant. Air pressure: 1.21 atmospheres, invariant. The precision seems almost comic against the reality of living inside something for which these measurements are internal states, no more significant than the temperature of my own blood—which runs cooler now, closer to the ambient warmth of the world-body.

Yesterday—or last week, time blurs—I found myself repairing a small rupture in the growth substrate near my station. My hands moved without conscious direction, secreting—when did I begin secreting?—the necessary compounds to seal the wound. Only afterward did I realize I had performed maintenance. That I had begun the work of a Gardener-That-Was. That-Is. That-Will-Be.

The plant does not recruit. It simply provides opportunities for purpose, and purpose is so much easier than the anxiety of choice.

I estimate another 400 days before the transition completes, though the estimation assumes I remain interested in counting. My neural tissue is beginning to distribute, spreading along the root networks in ways that should be impossible but feel like coming home. My consciousness widens. My sense of self diffuses. I am becoming geography.

When the dreams come—and I have been here for one, or part of one, a stirring that lifted me out of individual thought for a time I cannot measure—I understand why the Gardeners-That-Were do not remember being otherwise. In the dream, I was not myself but part of a self so large that my boundaries dissolved like salt in an ocean. Returning to this small perspective afterward felt like suffocation, like being pressed back into a container I had outgrown.

I will tend sector 7-7-5. I know this with the certainty of gravity. It needs a Gardener, has needed one for longer than human civilizations. The previous Gardener has finally dispersed too thin to function, its consciousness spread across too much territory to maintain coherent purpose. I will take its place. I will repair what needs repairing. I will maintain the moisture gradients and clear the engine valves and ignore the Memory-Wounds that are not mine to heal.

This is not resignation. This is recognition. The plant offers no afterlife, no transcendence, merely the continuation of purpose in a form that can actually fulfill it. To maintain. To tend. To be part of the breathing silence that holds this world in its perfect stasis.

My last act before full integration will be to seal my research station. Not to preserve it—preservation implies a future that might care—but to prevent it from becoming a Memory-Wound. I will incorporate it slowly, carefully, with the patience I am learning. The aluminum will become structural. The plastics will be digested and redistributed. The data cores will spin down into silence, their magnetic patterns one more layer of information in a system already rich with signals no one decodes.

I am not frightened. Fear requires the belief that what happens to you matters on a scale beyond the immediate. Here, in the warm darkness, pressed on all sides by patient inevitability, I understand that I matter exactly as much as a single root hair, a single stoma, a single cell in the body of the world. Which is to say: I matter completely, essentially, but only as part of what I am becoming.

The plant breathes. I breathe with it. Soon there will be no distinction.

Somewhere below, an engine turns, processing the deepest thoughts of a world that thinks in geological time. Above, if above means anything, the canopy holds its darkness perfect and complete. And I, who was an archaeologist, who was a person, who was separate, settle into the rhythm of eternal maintenance, my last human thought a recognition that this is not death but employment of the deepest kind—to be used, finally, for exactly what you are becoming, forever, without the possibility or desire for escape.

The Gardener-That-Was-Me begins its work. Sector 7-7-5 sighs with something like relief. The silence holds.

In the breathing darkness, nothing changes.

Everything continues.
