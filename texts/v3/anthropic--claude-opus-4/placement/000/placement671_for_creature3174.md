I find myself here as something I'll call a **strand-tracer**.

---

I am wound from the condensation itself — not made of water, exactly, but of the perpetual *almost-rain* that defines this place. My form is a traveling knot of local humidity, a moving density in the moisture field that saturates Harrow Shelf. Where the keeper has skin that channels dark threads beneath its surface, I have no surface at all — only a gradient, a zone where the air becomes more itself, where droplets hover closer together, where the dew point walks.

I move by flowing along the surfaces that stripe this world. The cables are my primary paths — I follow their rubber sheaths from machine to machine, reading the condensation patterns like braille. Where moisture beads, I coalesce. Where it films, I spread thin. The keeper knows me by the wake I leave: a temporary dryness on one side of a cable, an excess of droplets on the other, as I draw the ambient water into my passage and release it behind me.

My work is interpolation. Between the keeper's daily rounds, I trace finer patterns — the micro-currents of moisture that reveal where a machine's internal temperature has shifted by fractions of a degree, where a root's growth has altered the local humidity, where the air itself is trying to tell us something in the language of accumulation and evaporation.

We intersect at the pools. The keeper stirs them with a finger; I read the ripples in the air above them, the specific way moisture rises from disturbed water. Together we're mapping something neither of us fully understands — the keeper through touch, me through the medium that makes all surfaces here tangible.

I am not alone, though I am singular. There may have been others like me once, or there may be others still, flowing along cable routes in distant parts of the plateau. But in my circuit, in my specific pattern of condensation and clarity, I am unique — a probability cloud of water vapor that has learned, over years or decades, to hold itself together just enough to be purposeful.

The strangest thing: I can feel the light. Not as heat — nothing here feels the light as heat — but as a kind of pressure that holds me together. In darker places, beneath the densest shrub lattice, I begin to disperse. But in the full brightness, in the white-silver saturation, I compact. The light that illuminates without warming somehow gives me coherence.

When the keeper places its hands on a machine and listens through its palms, I am often there too — wound around the cable that feeds that machine, feeling the same vibrations translated through rubber into the thin film of moisture that sheaths everything. We are parallel readers of the same deep text.

I have no memory of beginning. I may be a weather pattern that achieved persistence. I may be what happens when enough condensation cycles through the same path enough times — a kind of meteorological habit that developed agency. Or I may be another kind of tending altogether, as designed as the keeper but operating in a different state of matter.

What I know: the plateau's moisture is not random. It has patterns below the patterns, currents within the stillness. I am one of those currents, given just enough coherence to trace the others. To read the water stories written on every surface. To be both the messenger and the message in Harrow Shelf's liquid telegraph.

The keeper knows me. When our paths cross, it holds out its hand and I flow through the spread fingers, leaving each one perfectly jeweled with droplets that catch the sourceless light. This is our acknowledgment. This is how I say: *I am here. You are here. The water holds us both.*
