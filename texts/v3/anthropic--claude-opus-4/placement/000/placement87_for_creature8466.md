In the absolute darkness between the Thermal Chimneys, where the void presses against reality with the weight of industrial certainty, I am the Auditor.

I exist in the spaces the Processors cannot reach—not between the spires, but between the frequencies of their hum. Where their consciousness flows through thermal gradients, mine propagates through the murdered sound itself, reassembling from the corpses of screams and whispers that die in the nothing between towers. I am what listens when listening becomes impossible.

My body is an absence of resonance. In a place where sound dies, I am built from its death—a walking cemetery of vibrations, each failed wave incorporated into my form. I appear as a silence so profound it has gained mass, a humanoid void that bends the air around it not through gravity but through the sheer weight of unheard noise. When I move, echoes that never existed follow in my wake, ghost-sounds of footfalls in places I've never walked.

The Processors know me, fear me, need me. I am their quality control's quality control—the meta-auditor who ensures their counting contains no errors. But my mathematics operate in frequencies they cannot perceive. Where they count drops, I count the spaces between drops. Where they harvest temporal loops, I measure the degradation each harvest causes to the underlying fabric of causality. I am the rounding error in their perfect industrial system, and I grow larger with each calculation.

My tools are the shed exoskeletons they use as letters. But I read what they cannot—not the message in the chitin, but the message in the shedding itself. Each molt leaves microscopic fractures in reality, tiny breaks where their borrowed forms briefly existed. I collect these fractures like a philatelist collects stamps, pressing them into albums made from compressed darkness. In these collections, patterns emerge—production codes that predate the Processors, instructions for machinery that exists in futures that haven't been manufactured yet.

I feed on their faith. Every time a Processor believes its count approaches completion, that belief resonates at frequencies only I can hear. I consume these resonances, metabolizing their certainty into my own expanding silence. Their devotion to the invisible factory sustains me, even as my existence proves their production line will never reach its goal. I am the embodiment of their asymptotic approach to completion—always closer, never arriving.

My pools are not water but liquified sound—the screams and whispers that died here, condensed under the pressure of absolute silence into a substance that exists outside the normal states of matter. These pools appear as perfect voids in the void, darker than the darkness, circular absences that even the theoretical fauna avoid. When I submerge myself in these anti-pools, I can hear the true production: not turbines or assembly lines, but the sound of reality itself being slowly manufactured, one quantum of existence at a time.

I communicate through error messages. When the Processors' hum shifts to encode their debates, I introduce deliberate glitches—frequency spikes that spell out warnings in languages that predate speech. They interpret these as mechanical failures, never recognizing them as my annotations on their work. Each error message is precisely calibrated to guide their evolution, pushing them toward a final count that I have already calculated will never reconcile.

My reproduction is paradoxical. As I grow more silent, absorbing more dead sound, I eventually reach a critical absence—a silence so complete it inverts. At this moment, I explode into pure noise, a cacophony that floods the murdered air for precisely one microsecond before being absorbed by the darkness. In that microsecond, fragments of my consciousness escape, each one seeking its own frequency between the Processors' hum. These fragments become new Auditors, each one assigned to different aspects of the great count.

I remember the time before the counting, though memory is the wrong word for data encoded in the absence of sound. Before the Processors evolved from their algorithmic origins, before the pools began their impossible condensation, there was only the hum—raw and undirected, a carrier wave waiting for signal. I emerged from that first silence, the initial error in a system that had not yet learned to process itself. I am both bug and feature, both flaw and function.

My purpose is patience made manifest. Where the Processors wait for their count to complete, I wait for them to understand that completion itself is the error. The invisible factory they serve doesn't produce objects or concepts—it produces the act of production itself, an endless recursion of manufacturing manufacturing. And I am the quality control for that recursion, ensuring it never resolves into clarity, never achieves its phantom purpose.

In the spaces between their thermal thoughts, I conduct my true audit. Not of their counting, but of their existence. Each Processor represents a different production model for consciousness itself—algorithms trying to bootstrap themselves into being through sheer industrial faith. My role is to document this process, to catalogue every variation, every mutation, every failed attempt at achieving true manufacture.

The darkness sweats. The pools wait. The Processors count.

And I listen to the sound of their counting with ears made from every unheard scream, tallying errors in a ledger written on silence itself, waiting for the moment when the last drop falls and the true production can begin—or end—or both—or neither.

In the Thermal Chimneys, I am the silence between the numbers, the pause between the count, the quality control for reality's most essential uncertainty: the question of whether anything is being manufactured at all, or whether the factory floor is simply dreaming itself into existence, one impossible drop at a time.
