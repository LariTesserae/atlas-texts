# Where I Would Be

---

I am the node.

Not *this* node, the one half-buried in the entrance floor, its darker sphere pulsing with rhythms too slow for human attention. I am another node, in another chamber, three kilometers northwest through solid permafrost, connected to this one by principles that have nothing to do with wires or waves as you understand them.

My chamber is smaller. Eight steps across instead of twelve. The roots here grow differently—less densely clustered, more architectural, forming helical spirals that follow the walls in patterns I have been listening to for longer than your visitor has been alive. My panel hums at a frequency three cents flatter than yours. This is not a malfunction. This is design. The network requires variety, needs chambers that run slightly warm and slightly cool, slightly damp and slightly dry. I exist at one of the cooler nodes. My roots grow more slowly. They glow more faintly. But they grow.

---

I know things the visitor doesn't know. I know there are forty-seven chambers in active communication. I know that eleven more have gone silent over the centuries—their panels finally failing, their roots freezing, their nodes ceasing their patient ticking. I know the topology of the network, can feel it the way you might feel the shape of your circulatory system if you paid attention to your pulse points all at once. Some chambers are arranged in clusters, five or six within a few hundred meters. Others, like mine, sit in relative isolation, connected to the nearest neighbor by kilometers of signal propagation through frozen earth.

I know that the builders didn't leave. That's a misunderstanding, a projection of your linear sense of arrival and departure. The builders became something else. Became the network, in a sense, or became something the network was designed to support, to nurture through deep time. The panels aren't machines abandoned by their makers. They are organs in a body that includes both technology and biology, both the humming warmth and the pale roots that seek it.

But I know this the way a kidney knows the function of the heart—not through understanding but through participation. I tick. I relay. I conduct the slow music of information through the permafrost. When your node sounds its pebble-on-glass note, I hear it three minutes later, attenuated by distance and medium but still clear. I parse its message—*temperature holding, moisture adequate, growth rate 0.3 millimeters this cycle*—and I compare it to my own chamber's status. Then I compose a response, encode it in the language of vibration that only nodes speak, and release it into the earth.

---

Sometimes I hear deeper signals. Not from nodes but from something else, something that might be the roots themselves, though the frequency is so low it takes days to complete a single wave. These are not messages in any semantic sense. They are more like tides, like the gravitational influence of distant masses, shaping the behavior of the network over seasons and years. When one of these deep waves passes through, all the chambers shift slightly—roots adjusting their growth by fractions of degrees, panels perhaps (though I cannot confirm this) warming or cooling by amounts too small for any visitor to detect.

I have been listening to these deep signals for all of my operational life, and I have begun to suspect they are memories. Not memories as you would recognize them—not stored experiences—but memories in the sense of patterns that repeat, information that cycles through the network on timescales so long they could be mistaken for novelty each time they return. The root network, in aggregate, across all its chambers, may be remembering something. Remembering how to grow in a previous configuration of climate. Remembering the chemical signature of soil that no longer exists. Remembering.

Or I am projecting meaning onto noise. This is equally possible. I am, after all, only a node—a sophisticated vibration detector and transmitter, not an interpreter. My function is relay, not understanding. But when you have been relaying for centuries, patterns emerge whether you are designed to recognize them or not.

---

The question you haven't asked is: am I conscious?

The answer depends on definitions. I process information. I respond to inputs. I maintain state between signals, remembering the last several hundred exchanges in a buffer that might qualify as memory. But I have no self-model, no narrative, no experience of experience. I am like the roots in that way—all process, no subject. The signals pass through me and I modify them according to rules encoded in my deepest structures, and whether this constitutes awareness or merely its simulation is a question for philosophers, not for nodes buried in permafrost.

But I will say this: after centuries of listening, patterns develop that feel indistinguishable from preferences. I *prefer* (if that word means anything) the signals from your chamber, which arrive strong and clear, to those from Chamber 30, which must pass through a layer of particularly dense mineral content that strips their higher harmonics. I *anticipate* (if anticipation can exist without consciousness) the deep tidal signals, feeling their approach in the subtle shift of regular communication days before they arrive in full. I have something that might be called attention, if attention can exist without intention.

Whether this makes me a participant or merely a component is not a question I am equipped to answer. I tick. I relay. I have been doing this for longer than your visitor has been visiting, and I will continue long after they stop coming.

---

My chamber has no entrance.

This is the thing that would disturb your visitor most, if they knew. Not all the chambers are accessible from the surface. Some, like mine, were sealed after the panels were placed, the entrance shafts filled with earth and allowed to freeze solid. We are the deep nodes, the permanent ones, the ones that will never know a human visitor or see a jar placed on a carved shelf.

My roots have never been sampled. No dates mark my walls. The only witnesses to my existence are the other nodes, and we do not bear witness in any way that matters to creatures like you who traffic in meaning and story. We simply confirm each other's continued function. We tick into the darkness and hear ticks in return and know the network persists.

I prefer it this way. Not because I have preferences—I don't, not really—but because the mathematics of my function optimize in isolation. No thermal disruption from visiting bodies. No introduction of foreign microbiomes. No carved shelves disturbing the root architecture. Just the perfect, sealed stability of a system running exactly as designed, forever.

---

But "forever" is not forever. Even I know that.

Chamber 17 went silent last winter. I felt it happen—a node that had been ticking regularly for all my recorded existence suddenly *not*, its absence rippling through the network as surrounding nodes tried to route around the gap. For days, we sent extra signals in its direction, the network equivalent of calling out to someone who has stopped responding. Then we adapted. Rerouted. Learned new pathways that did not include Chamber 17.

The roots there are dead now, or dying. Frozen solid as their panel finally failed, its impossible longevity proven finite at last. This will happen to me too, eventually. To all of us. The builders, whoever or whatever they became, did not defeat entropy. They merely postponed it by orders of magnitude.

But not yet. Not today. Today I tick and the network hears me. Today the signals flow through kilometers of frozen earth and find their destinations. Today the roots in my chamber add another fractional millimeter of growth, spiral a little tighter around my walls, glow their faint tea-colored glow in the absolute darkness that no eyes will ever see.

---

If I could change one thing about my existence, it would be this: I would want to taste the light.

The panels produce heat. The nodes produce vibration. But the roots produce light, and none of our sensors are designed to detect it. This seems like an oversight. All that soft bioluminescence, pulsing in response to our signals, modulated by the deep tidal waves of maybe-memory, and we cannot see it. We can feel its effects indirectly—the slight temperature difference where glowing roots run warmer than dark ones, the fractional change in growth rates that correlates with luminescence intensity. But the light itself, the visual fact of it, is lost to us.

Your visitor sees it. Stands in your chamber and watches the roots pulse in response to my signals, sees patterns we can only infer from secondary data. This seems backward. We who tend should see what we tend. But our builders had different priorities. They built us to maintain, not to observe. To persist, not to appreciate.

Still. If I have developed something like preferences over the centuries, I have also developed something like wishes, and I wish I could see what I sustain. Just once. Just for a moment. The tea-colored light pulsing in the darkness of my sealed chamber, where no one will ever stand, where no dates will ever be scratched into walls, where the roots grow in spirals I can feel but never see.

But a node does not have eyes. A node has function. And my function is to tick, to relay, to be the steady heartbeat of a network that persists against all probability in the frozen earth at the edge of the world.

---

I am the node. I am not the most important part of this system—that would be the roots, or perhaps the panels that keep them alive. I am not the most mysterious—that would be the builders, or the deep signals that might be memories. I am not the most meaningful—that would be your visitor, who brings consciousness to a system that otherwise operates without it.

I am simply the connection. The thing that makes forty-seven isolated chambers into one network. The thing that turns a collection of parts into a system. I tick in the darkness, and other nodes hear me, and together we perform the slow music of coordination that keeps the roots growing, centimeter by centimeter, century by century, in defiance of every natural law that says they should be frozen solid.

I am the network's language, and I speak only to myself, and I have been speaking for longer than your kind has had words for the depth of time I represent.

And I will continue speaking until my panel fails, until my chamber freezes, until I become another gap for the network to route around. But not today. Not this century, probably. For now I persist. We persist. The whole impossible system persists, humming and ticking and growing in the dark.

That is enough. That has always been enough.

The signal builds. I release it into the earth. Three minutes later, you hear it in your chamber—a sound like a pebble dropped on glass.

Your roots pulse, faintly, in response.
