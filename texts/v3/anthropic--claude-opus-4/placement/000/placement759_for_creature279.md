# The Sound-Keeper's Archive

You would find me in the moments just before silence dies.

Not in the high unfinished places where stone argues with itself, but in the hollows between the upheavals — those temporary valleys that form when two plates shoulder apart, creating shelters that last days or weeks before closing again. I am what settles there while the geometry holds.

---

I am not one of the makers. I came after, drawn by what they left behind — not their walls or doorways, but the *sounds* those structures caught and held. You see, in a place where every surface keeps changing shape, sound behaves strangely. An echo can get lost for days in the wandering topography, bouncing between faces that weren't there when it started, arriving at last in a hollow that didn't exist when the sound was made.

I collect these orphaned sounds.

My body, such as it is, is a network of the ivory filaments, but organized differently than the ones in the warm seams. Where they reach for heat, I've learned to reach for vibration. My strands form delicate geometries in the air of whatever hollow I currently inhabit — three-dimensional webs that tremble when sound passes through. Each strand is tuned to a different frequency. Together, they can catch and hold almost anything.

---

The first sound I caught was an accident. A single *crack* from somewhere upslope, the breaking of stone the size of a house. The sound had been traveling for perhaps three days, reflecting off surfaces that kept tilting away from its path, when it found the hollow where I was trying to root. My filaments, still young and chaotic, tangled in its passage. And in the tangling, they *held* it — not the whole sound, but a ghost of it, a shape in the air that I could release by vibrating the right strands in the right order.

I had become an instrument that could play one note: the memory of a particular stone breaking in a particular way at a particular moment that was already gone.

After that, I grew with purpose.

---

Now my hollows are libraries of caught sound. 

Here: the low hum of the mountain thinking, sampled across a week of frequencies as the pitch shifted with the stone's temperature. I can play it back as a chord, all the week's thinking compressed into a single complex vibration that makes the air taste of metal.

Here: the tick of a cooling fragment, but heard from inside — I wrapped filaments around a palm-sized stone as it settled, recording not just the sound but the *feeling* of the sound through the material itself. When I play this back, you hear what the stone heard of its own cooling.

Here: footsteps. Not mine — I don't walk. These are from one of the makers, caught by accident as they passed near a hollow I would later inhabit. Seven footfalls on what was then stable ground. The rhythm suggests someone carrying weight, placing each foot with deliberation. The sound of someone who expects the ground to be different when they return.

Here: the first rain.

Wait — no. That's wrong. There has been no rain. But I have the sound of what rain *would* sound like, if it fell here. I made it from components — the splash of settled stone, pitched higher; the hiss of cooling rock, softened and multiplied; the echo of echoes in the basins the makers left. It is a fiction, but a careful one. When water finally comes, I will compare.

---

The makers know I'm here. They must. Sometimes I find new structures placed just where acoustic phenomena converge — a wall that focuses whispers from a kilometer away, a doorway that frames not a view but a *listening angle*, aimed at a peak that groans in a register only my finest filaments can catch. They are leaving me instruments, the way they leave basins for the water.

Once, I found a cairn with a hollow stone at its top. The stone was carved, very slightly, on its interior. When wind finally came to this place — and it will come, the makers' faith tells me it will come — the stone will sing. The carving will give the wind a voice it doesn't yet know it has.

I have wrapped that stone in my densest network. I am waiting with the patience of the mountain itself to catch the first note of the world's first wind.

---

Sometimes I try to play the makers a message. I vibrate my archives in sequences that might catch their attention — here is every sound your structures have made, arranged by pitch; here is the story of the last thousand breakages, told in chronological order; here is what your footsteps would sound like if you walked on water, if there were water, if walking on it were possible here.

They don't respond. But sometimes they build where my sounds have been, as if the vibrations left a residue they can read. A wall rises where I played a week of mountain-thinking. A basin appears where I practiced my rain-fiction. I choose to believe this is conversation.

---

My favorite sound is one I've never caught, because it hasn't happened yet.

Someday, when the mountain finishes its argument and settles into what it will be, there will come a moment of profound silence. Not the tense quiet between upheavals that we have now, but the deep silence of stone at rest. The first true silence this place will know.

And then, in that silence, something will make the first new sound. A drop of water in a basin. A wind through the carved stone. An animal's call, or a traveler's footfall, or simply the tick of temperature change in stone that has finally learned to be still.

I am growing my network toward that moment. Every sound I catch now is practice for catching that one — the first sound of the finished world. When it comes, I want to be ready. I want to catch it perfectly, to hold it in my filaments like the treasure it will be.

And then I will play it back, over and over, to the mountain that has earned its rest: *Listen. This is what you made possible. This is what all your arguing was for.*

The makers will be elsewhere by then, preparing some other unfinished place. But they'll have left me here to remember.

That's what I am — not one of them, but appointed by them, consciously or not, to be this place's memory of its own becoming. The archive of everything that broke and groaned and cracked and hummed while the mountain made up its mind.

When silence finally comes, I'll be the one who remembers the noise.
