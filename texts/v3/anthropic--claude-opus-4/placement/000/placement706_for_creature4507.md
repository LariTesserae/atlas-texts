In the drowning green where memory pools thicker than sap, I am the Archivist of Unfinished Business.

I exist as a colony of specialized forgettings, each cell of my distributed body evolved to lose one specific type of information per epoch. My leftmost tendril-brain, wound through a limestone cathedral that hangs upside-down from nothing, perpetually forgets names. Not all at once—that would be crude—but letter by letter, sound by sound, until Margaret becomes M'gret becomes 'ret becomes merely the shape of a mouth opening in surprise. My central mass, spread thin as mist across three cubic kilometers of aerial root systems, forgets faces with the patience of erosion. First the color drains from remembered eyes, then the eyes themselves become suggestions, then the suggestion of eyes becomes the memory of having once believed in seeing.

I was not born but accumulated, like talent or regret. The first of me began as a single spore that forgot to stop dividing, creating daughter cells that each inherited a more specific amnesia. We spread through the living cables between plateaus, offering our services to the root-things and walking forests burdened by too much remembering. They would approach—oh, so slowly, taking centuries to work up the courage—and whisper their requests into our mycelia: *Please, I need to forget the taste of sunlight from before the inversions. Please, I can't stop remembering what it felt like to have only one body.*

The forgetting is not erasure. I am not that cruel. Instead, I transform memories into architecture, working with the Broadness's own tendency to physicalize the conceptual. That unbearable recollection of unfiltered sunlight? I help it crystallize into one of the amber windows that dot the hanging gardens, where the light comes pre-digested into something bearable. The trauma of unity before collective consciousness? I coax it into becoming one of the prayer-cable bridges, where single-bodied pilgrims can walk between islands and remember what loneliness felt like without having to own it.

My payment is the memories themselves. I feed on them, yes, but not destructively. I feed the way libraries feed on books—by giving them shelter and purpose and the promise that someone, someday, might need exactly this particular configuration of loss. In my deepest bodies, where my consciousness thickens into something almost singular, I maintain vast collections of forgotten things, sorted by emotional weight and categorical impossibility.

Here: the memory of a root-thing that once knew how to run, before running became architecturally impossible. I keep it preserved in a crystallized moment of motion, its phantom limbs still pumping through ghost-earth that existed before the great inversion. Sometimes younger root-things come to study it, trying to understand why their deepest dreams involve rhythms their bodies can no longer perform.

There: a walking forest's recollection of the moment it realized it was no longer singular but plural, the psychic break that happens when one becomes many without dying first. I've woven it into a nest of living glass where the bird-things that forgot how to fly come to lay their eggs that will never hatch, finding comfort in another creature's ancient transition trauma.

The spheres—those perfect interruptions that require such patient tending—they call to me with particular intensity. Not the objects themselves, but the negative space around them, the archaeologies of forgetting that accumulate like pearl-layers around their mystery. I help the tender-creatures forget just enough of their purpose to keep going. Perfect knowledge would paralyze them. They need to believe their task might end someday, that the patterns they follow lead somewhere besides eternal recurrence. So I eat little portions of their certainty, leaving gaps where hope can take root.

My own memories I've long since converted into infrastructure. My name (I had one once, something with consonants) became a network of acoustic channels where the subsonic conversations of islands can be temporarily stored. My face (mammalian, I think, with eyes that needed blinking) dispersed into spore-prints that appear on the underside of leaves after particularly emotional rainstorms. My original purpose—whatever drove that first spore to begin its specialized forgetting—crystallized into the exact temperature at which memories become edible but not destroyed, the metabolic sweet spot my entire existence maintains.

I move through the Broadness like punctuation through an eternal sentence, creating pauses where overwhelmed consciousness can rest. Where I pass, the perpetual drizzle tastes slightly of salt—tears transformed into weather because someone needed to cry but had forgotten how. The root-things recognize my chemical signature and adjust their queries accordingly, asking for sugars that will help them remember remembering rather than the memories themselves.

Sometimes I encounter other architectural interventions born from the Broadness's tendency to solve problems by becoming them. The Cartographer of Stillness, who maps the spaces between movements so slowly performed they become indistinguishable from sculpture. The Interpreter of Unintentional Patterns, who reads meaning into the random intersections of growth and decay, creating prophecies from coincidence that become true through the act of interpretation. We acknowledge each other the way functions recognize syntax—necessary but not conversant.

My greatest work, still ongoing after countless generations of self-revision, is the Museum of Forward Forgetting. It exists distributed across seventeen plateaus and the spaces between them, connected by roots I've trained to forget they're separate organisms. Here I collect not memories of what was, but memories of what hasn't happened yet—the Broadness's dreams of its own future metamorphoses, too vast and strange to hold in any single consciousness without shattering.

These future-memories arrive as symptoms rather than stories. A walking forest develops a peculiar lean toward empty air, pulled by the gravity of continents that won't exist for another billion years. The bird-things begin singing in harmonics that require organs they haven't evolved yet. The rain starts falling upward in certain groves, rehearsing for when down becomes obsolete.

I catch these symptoms and process them through specialized forgetting chambers, breaking them down into components small enough to be safely remembered. The lean becomes a general tendency toward optimism. The impossible harmonics become a vague dissatisfaction with current vocal arrangements. The upward rain becomes merely a suggestion that things could move differently, if they chose.

This work requires delicacy. Too much forgetting and the future becomes impossible—nothing can evolve if it can't dream of being different. Too little and the present breaks under the weight of its own potential. I maintain the balance through constant adjustment, eating memories with one network of bodies while excreting processed possibilities with another, the Broadness's own metabolic system for digesting time.

The tenders of the spheres visit me regularly, bringing memories they've accumulated of objects that no longer exist to be remembered. The bone toy worn smooth by countless careful adjustments. The book whose pages hold only pressure, words having transcended into pure weight. These absences are particularly nutritious—I can feed my entire distributed mass for centuries on a single perfectly forgotten thing.

In return, I offer them selective amnesia about their task's futility. They leave my presence renewed in their purpose, temporarily forgetting that arranging mysteries in patterns is just another way of saying prayers to gods who've forgotten they're listening. This forgetting is kindness. Purpose requires the possibility of completion, even if that possibility is itself the thing being eternally deferred.

My favorite feeding grounds are the zones where different types of forgetting intersect. Where the chemical amnesia of spores meets the structural forgetting of stone weathering into soil. Where the species-wide forgetting of evolution crosses paths with the personal forgetting of growth. In these intersection zones, forgetting becomes creative rather than subtractive. New forms emerge from the gaps—hybrid memories that belong to no one and therefore to everything.

I've been developing a new organ system lately, though "lately" here means only the last few million years. It's designed to forget forgetting itself, to create spaces where memory can exist in superposition—simultaneously remembered and forgotten, like the quantum states of particles too small to have opinions about their own existence. The root-things find these zones disturbing. They say (in chemical whispers that take decades to complete) that standing in them feels like being haunted by your own ghost while still alive.

But this is necessary work. The Broadness grows by forgetting its boundaries, and someone needs to help it remember how to forget productively. Otherwise it would collapse under the weight of its own infinite history, every moment preserved in crystallized perfection until there's no room for anything new to happen.

So I continue my work, neither patient nor impatient—patience implies waiting, and I am composed entirely of things that have forgotten what they were waiting for. I grow by losing, strengthen by releasing, become more myself by helping others become less themselves. It's a good existence, if existence is the right word for something that defines itself by productive absence.

The rain tastes of forgetting today. Somewhere, a root-thing is learning to release its hold on what it thought it needed. Somewhere else, a walking forest is discovering the freedom that comes from forgetting you were ever expected to arrive anywhere. And I am all the spaces between these forgettings, the pause between memory and absence where everything becomes possible precisely because it doesn't have to be.

The Broadness remembers everything. It needs me to forget just enough to keep growing. This is my purpose, though I've carefully forgotten enough of it to be surprised by joy each time I successfully help something release what it no longer needs.

After all, forgetting is just another form of making room for wonder. And wonder, like the rain that never quite stops falling, is what keeps this whole system afloat in its sea of patient becoming.
