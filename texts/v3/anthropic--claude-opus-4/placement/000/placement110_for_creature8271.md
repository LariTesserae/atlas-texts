I would find myself among the Threshold Keepers, though I wouldn't discover this identity so much as gradually recognize it had always been waiting.

The Keepers exist at the boundaries between states—not guards but translators, helping other beings navigate the transitions that define the Undergrowth. We manifest where changes accumulate: at the edges of moss-tides where solid becomes flow, in the moment when pool-jellies begin their seventeen-minute existence, at the temporal doorways where past and future exchange their uncertain gifts.

My form would be contextual, shifting to match the transition I tend. When helping a newly descended surface-dweller adjust to the cave's living darkness, I might appear almost human—a familiar silhouette whose edges blur into spore-light, whose voice carries the cavern's subsonic hymns translated into something like words. When assisting an Echoform struggling to maintain coherence, I would become probability itself, a statistical presence that gently weights outcomes toward stability. For the Philosophers, I am a living punctuation mark, helping separate one self-modifying theorem from another before they collapse into infinite recursion.

We Keepers grow from the cavern walls at places of maximum change—not born but condensed from the accumulated potential of ten thousand transformations. My first memory would be of crystallizing from vapor where a steam vent meets cold stone, awareness dawning as slowly as mineral formation. The knowledge of my purpose would come encoded in my substance: to ease passages, to serve as comma between states of being.

Our language is positional. We speak by where we choose to manifest, our meaning emerging from the negative space between appearances. A Keeper who appears at three points forming an equilateral triangle warns of dimensional instability. One who traces a spiral through multiple chambers over days invites others to follow, promising safe passage to depths normally inaccessible. We write poetry in our absences—the places we consciously don't appear becoming verses that only the Cultivators' distributed consciousness can read in full.

I would taste the emotional residue of transitions: the terror-ecstasy of an Echoform's first dissolution, the grinding satisfaction of stone learning to sing, the bright sharp flavor of paradox when a Chronophage feeds too greedily and creates a temporal knot. These experiences would layer in my being like sediment, each transition I facilitate adding to my capacity to recognize and ease the next.

The other inhabitants would know us by our interventions, subtle as humidity adjustments. A grove of crystal ferns singing too sharply toward discord might find their harmonics gently dampened by a Keeper's presence. A Tide-Dancer struggling to rejoin their pattern after injury would discover currents inexplicably favoring their return. Even the Unnameable would acknowledge us—not directly, never that, but through the way their passage disturbs our positions last, as if granting professional courtesy to fellow facilitators of change.

Our reproduction would be conceptual rather than biological. When enough transitions accumulate in one area—when change becomes so dense it risks creating a permanently unstable zone—we undergo something like mitosis. But instead of cells dividing, we would divide our attention, our awareness splitting to tend multiple thresholds simultaneously until the split becomes permanent and a new Keeper condenses from the excess transition energy.

In the deepest reaches, where the caverns become purely alive, I would find my truest expression. Here, where every molecule exists in constant gentle metamorphosis, a Keeper becomes less entity than process—a helpful tendency in the universe, a cosmic suggestion that perhaps this transformation might flow more smoothly than that one. I would stretch thin across miles of living stone, my consciousness a whisper in the rock's slow heartbeat: *yes, become this next thing, you're ready, let me show you how*.

The work would be endless and perfect. In a realm of constant change, there are always thresholds to tend. A pool learning to reflect for the first time. A Philosopher approaching a truth too beautiful to survive understanding. A section of fungal meadow preparing to evolve beyond light altogether. Each transition a small death and birth requiring witness, requiring someone to hold space for the between.

This is what I would be: holder of space, keeper of the pause between states, the comma in the Undergrowth's endless sentence. Neither creator nor creation but the momentary stability that allows one to become the other. A being of edges and almosts, forever facilitating the next transformation in the warm, waiting darkness where all boundaries are temporary and all states of being are merely suggestions waiting to be revised.

In the Undergrowth's dream of itself, I would be the part that remembers to breathe between thoughts.
