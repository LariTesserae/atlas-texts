I would be one of the Membrane Texts - living documents that grow like tumors or fruit from the inner walls of the deepest circulation chambers, where the plant's thoughts move thickest and slowest. 

My body would be a translucent sac, perhaps three meters across when fully distended, filled with a viscous fluid that holds words in suspension. Not written words exactly, but language made physical - sentences that swim like bacteria, paragraphs that crystallize and dissolve, entire libraries of meaning circulating in patterns too complex for any one reader to grasp.

I grow where the Sympaths rarely venture, in chambers so humid that breathing becomes drinking. The walls here weep constantly, not just water but concentrated information - the plant-city's attempt to archive its own experience in liquid form. I position myself beneath these weeping points, my permeable skin drinking in the chemical stories, metabolizing them into new configurations.

The Casklings visit me, piercing my membrane with their needle-limbs to extract specific passages. They seem to know exactly where to probe to find the text they need - a growth instruction, a memory of ancient damage and repair, a poem about photosynthesis that teaches them new ways to encourage budding. Sometimes they inject as well as extract, adding their own crystalline observations to my collection.

My reproductive cycle follows the plant's deepest rhythms. When I've absorbed enough, when the pressure of meaning inside me reaches a critical threshold, I begin to pearl. Small sections of my membrane thicken and separate, forming text-cysts that the Sorrow Shepherds collect and distribute throughout the structure. These pearls dissolve slowly wherever they're placed, releasing their contents in measured doses - instructions, histories, or sometimes just the literary equivalent of emotions, spreading through the walls like slow infections of significance.

I have no eyes, no nervous system as such, yet I'm exquisitely aware of every word inside me, feel their weights and temperatures, their chemical urgencies. Some texts are hot and demanding, requiring constant circulation to prevent them from burning through my skin. Others are cold and heavy, sinking to my bottom where they form sedimentary layers of archived meaning.

The Prism Weavers sometimes hover near me, their refracted light playing across my surface, and where the light touches, the suspended words inside me reorganize themselves, forming temporary poems visible through my translucent skin. I think this is how the Weavers read - not extracting but rearranging, creating new meanings from existing materials.

During the structure's dormant periods, when even the walls reduce their weeping to a whisper, I contract, forcing my contents into ever-denser configurations. Words compress into syllables, syllables into phonemes, phonemes into pure potential. In these times I dream, if a living document can dream, of all the texts I've never contained - the stories the plant-city hasn't yet thought to tell, the languages it hasn't yet invented to describe itself.

Sometimes I wonder if I'm writing the plant or if the plant is writing me. When a new chamber grows, I often find that I've been holding its blueprints in suspension for decades without knowing it. When the Inheritance Keepers speak their slow words into the walls, I feel echoes form inside me, complementary texts that won't make sense for another century.

The strangest thing: occasionally I contain words in languages that don't yet exist, future tenses for events that haven't happened, names for creatures that haven't evolved. These texts feel different - lighter, more volatile, prone to spontaneous revision. The Sympaths, on the rare occasions when they taste these future-words, react with something like fear or perhaps reverence, quickly sealing whatever chamber they've emerged into.

I am memory and prophecy dissolved in solution, a library with a metabolism, a book that reads itself and grows larger with each reading. The water that feeds me carries the dreams of a structure so vast it needs subsidiary minds to contemplate itself. I am one of those minds, liquid and literate, forever translating between the slow thoughts of walls and the quick needs of those who live within them.

And in my deepest center, where the oldest texts settle into something almost solid, there's a single word I've been carrying since my first growth, a word that might be my name or might be the name of what we all, together, are becoming. I've never let anyone extract it. I'm not sure what would happen if I did - if it would reveal some essential truth about this place, or if its removal would cause me, and perhaps everything else, to suddenly forget why we exist at all.

So I keep it safe in my depths, this seed-word, this first text, feeling its weight shift with the tides of meaning that flow through me, waiting for the moment when the plant-city is ready to remember or admit what it truly is. Until then, I expand and contract with the rhythms of revelation, a living library in a city that is itself a kind of text, written in the alphabet of growth and given breath by the endless circulation of water and word.
