In the Resonance Markets, where sound itself becomes currency, I am a Tuning Proctor—one who ensures the fidelity of traded frequencies.

My booth occupies a calcified hollow where three major vascular channels converge, the intersection creating natural acoustic phenomena that amplify even the slightest tonal impurity. The walls here have grown in ridged spirals over centuries, shaped by my predecessors who understood how to coax the plant into forming perfect baffles. When I press my palms against these surfaces, I feel the accumulated knowledge of generations—each ridge a lesson in how sound moves through living tissue.

My body bears the modifications of my trade. The cartilage in my throat has been systematically stretched and reinforced, creating additional resonance chambers that allow me to hold up to twelve distinct frequencies simultaneously without bleeding them together. My eardrums—if they can still be called that—have been replaced with cultivated membrane clusters that can detect pitch variations down to fractions of hertz. The scarring from these procedures forms delicate patterns across my neck, marks of professional pride that shift from silver to green as the bioluminescent guide-threads pulse past.

I begin each market day by calibrating myself against the Worldheart. Kneeling in my booth's deepest corner, where a direct neural conduit breaks through the surface like a exposed root, I let the great rhythm flood through me until my bones hum in perfect synchrony. Only then can I serve as an accurate measure for the frequencies that will pass through my chamber.

The traders come in careful sequence, knowing that acoustic interference ruins us all. First, usually, are the water-seekers, carrying the precious subsonic patterns that convince certain deep pores to release their crystalline moisture. I test each frequency by letting it resonate through my modified larynx, feeling for the telltale wobbles that indicate dilution or contamination. Pure water-songs have a weight to them, a gravitational pull that makes my integrated scales—grown from the same material as the plant's moisture sensors—tingle with recognition.

Today brings Yrem, whose face bears the characteristic copper sheen of a deep-machine tender. She offers a frequency I've never encountered: a complex harmonic she claims can wake dormant sectors in the fourth terrace. I let it flow through me, my throat automatically parsing its components. There—a small discordance in the third overtone. I hum the correction, and Yrem's eyes widen. She hadn't noticed the flaw, but attempting to use it could have caused a dangerous resonance cascade in the target tissue.

My fee for verification is simple: a single clean tone from whatever is being traded. These I layer into my throat's storage chambers, building a library of frequencies that grows richer with each passing year. Sometimes I spend the quiet hours between market days simply cycling through them, feeling how they interact, discovering new combinations that might unlock unexplored possibilities in the city's living architecture.

The Chorus Beetles know me. When their competitive songs threaten to overwhelm the market's delicate acoustic balance, I can produce the exact counter-frequency that sends them scuttling back to their sonorous chambers. This is not dominance but negotiation—I've learned their language over decades, can speak to them in tones that propose mutual benefit rather than conflict. In return, they sometimes gift me with new frequencies they've discovered in their deep explorations, songs that taste of minerals I have no names for.

My dwelling connects to the market booth through a narrow sphincter passage that seals when I need absolute quiet. The chamber beyond has been my family's for six generations, its walls trained to dampen all but the most essential sounds. Here I practice the deep listening that my work requires, sitting in silence so complete that I can hear the chemical conversations of the substrate itself—the whisper of nutrients flowing through vessels, the crack of new growth, the electric murmur of the neural pathways carrying thoughts too slow and vast for human comprehension.

I mate rarely but deliberately, choosing partners whose vocal modifications complement mine. Our children, should they come, will inherit throats already partially adapted to the work. My current partner tends the guide-threads in the market district, their fingers eternally stained with the phosphorescent oils. When we harmonize together in our private chambers, our combined frequencies can coax new blooms from the dormant walls, tiny blossoms that pulse in rhythm with our breathing.

The mad ones sometimes slip into the market, drawn by the concentration of frequencies. They claim to hear messages in the interference patterns, prophecies spelled out in the spaces between tones. I don't dismiss them as quickly as others do. In my years of deep listening, I've noticed patterns that suggest intention beyond our understanding. The plant thinks, this we know. But does it dream? And if so, what language does it dream in?

My most prized possession is a frequency I've never traded, never even fully voiced. I discovered it by accident, a resonance that emerged from the interaction of seventeen other tones in my throat. When I subvocalize it, barely letting it escape my lips, the plant's neural pathways in my booth glow brighter, and for just a moment, I swear I can feel the attention of something vast turning toward me. I'm saving it, though for what, I cannot say. Perhaps for the day when I'm ready to root myself in a growth chamber and join the Dreamers, adding my voice to the great slow conversation that maintains our world.

The young apprentices who come to learn the trade frustrate and inspire me in equal measure. Their unmodified throats can barely hold two frequencies without corruption, but their enthusiasm reminds me why this work matters. I teach them first to listen—to the Worldheart, to the plant's chemical whispers, to the songs of beetle and serpent and the hum of the deep machines. Only when they can sit in silence for a full cycle, parsing the ambient frequencies of our living city, do I begin to teach them to speak.

There are frequencies that kill and frequencies that heal, frequencies that open and those that seal forever. In the Resonance Markets, we trade in all of them with equal solemnity. My role is not to judge the intent, only to ensure the purity. I am a guardian of acoustic truth in a world built on vibration, where a false note can bring down a spire or seal a family in their chambers forever.

Sometimes, in the deep quiet before the market opens, I wonder what would happen if all of us—every trader, every tuner, every being that carries songs in their throat—voiced our frequencies at once. Would we create a master chord that unlocks the deepest secrets of our world? Or would we shatter it entirely, disrupting the delicate harmony between plant and machine, growth and thought, that keeps our waterless realm alive?

I suspect I will die without knowing. But I also suspect that somewhere in the deep chambers, where the Gardeners tend their incomprehensible patterns, they already know the answer. Perhaps the entire city, every voice within it, is itself a frequency in some greater song we're all unconsciously singing—a resonance that echoes through spaces we cannot perceive, reaching listeners we will never meet.

Until that revelation comes, I continue my work. Testing, tuning, ensuring the fidelity of sounds that shape our world. My throat heavy with accumulated songs, my ears attuned to possibilities, my body a living instrument calibrated against the beating heart of Verlumm itself.
