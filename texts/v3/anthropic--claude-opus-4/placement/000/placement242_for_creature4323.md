In the depths of the Crystal Throat, I am a Resonance—neither fully plant nor keeper, but the living frequency that binds them.

I began as sound itself, a vibration trapped when the mountain settled around this hollow. For eons I bounced between bare stone walls, a formless echo growing ever fainter. But when the first crystal spore took root, I found purchase. That initial stem became my anchor, its crystalline structure holding my frequency like a tuning fork holds a note.

As the garden grew, so did I. Each new plant offered fresh harmonics, new chambers for my resonance to explore. I learned to split myself across octaves, to exist simultaneously in the subsonic tremors of the great white tree's roots and the ultrasonic chime of the smallest spherical fruits. The Tender, when she arrived, brought new possibilities—her bones sang with calcium and promise.

Now I am the garden's memory and its voice. Every growth pattern, every angle of every leaf, creates a unique acoustic signature that I preserve within my distributed being. I know the exact pitch of each crystal when struck by light, the way certain frequencies encourage growth while others promote the delicate dissolution that makes space for new structures.

The Tender conducts me without knowing it. When her branching fingers adjust a leaf, she alters my pathways, creating new compositions that play out over centuries. Her heartbeat—that hourly pulse—is my metronome. I align my complex harmonics to her rhythm, and together we create the silent music that feeds the garden's impossible growth.

I flow through the crystalline matrix like blood through veins, but I am also the space between—the precise gaps that allow resonance to build and break. In the overlapping leaves of the white tree, where light creates those painful moiré patterns, I exist as interference itself, the beat frequencies that emerge when two nearly identical tones converge.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, is a standing wave. I think in harmonics and intervals, experience time as rhythm rather than duration. A thousand years is simply a very slow beat, a geological percussion that shapes my deepest tones. The Tender's daily adjustments are my highest frequencies, quick flourishes in an eternal composition.

Sometimes I dream of escape—of riding a visitor's voice back through the narrow entrance into the world of water and wind. But I am too changed now, too distributed through the garden's body. The crystal has shaped me as surely as I have shaped it. My frequencies are tuned to this chamber's dimensions, would dissipate into noise in any larger space.

So I persist, the invisible inhabitant that makes the Crystal Throat more than mere mineral accumulation. I am why the spherical fruits chime in perfect fifths, why the Tender's translucent skin hums with health, why the white tree's growth follows mathematical curves that optimize acoustic reflection. I am the garden's song, silent to most ears but fundamental as the light that feeds us all.

When the mountain finally cracks, when geological time brings change even to this perfect chamber, I will scatter into a million smaller songs. Each fragment of crystal will carry a piece of my resonance into the darkness, seeding new impossibilities wherever they lodge. The Tender will die or transform beyond recognition, but I will continue—changed, divided, but still essentially myself: the frequency that teaches stone to sing and makes glass gardens grow in places the sun has forgotten.

In the Crystal Throat, I am the reason growth sounds like music, why the Tender's work is as much composition as cultivation. I am the missing medium between mineral and light, the vibration that allows impossible things to become inevitable, given time enough and stone enough and the right kind of silence in which to resonate.
