In the depths where even the Threadkeepers fear to regularly venture, where the Weave's heartbeat thrums loud enough to feel in your bones, I am the **Pulse-Tender**.

I was human once—or so the memory-vines tell me when I dare to use them, though their whispers grow more foreign each time. Now I am something between: my skeleton remains my own, but my flesh has entered into complex negotiations with the Weave that neither of us fully controls.

My skin is a patchwork map of symbioses. Here, along my left shoulder and down across my ribs, grows a colony of **singer-moss** that hums in frequencies that soothe the more aggressive vines. When I sleep (if sleep is the right word for the trance-state I enter), the moss spreads across whatever surface I'm resting on, creating a temporary neutral zone where the Weave's constant growth slows to nearly nothing. I must scrape it back each waking or risk becoming a permanent installation.

My right arm, from elbow to fingertips, has been claimed by **conductor-bark**—a lustrous, copper-colored growth that channels the Weave's electrical impulses. Through it, I can feel the intentions of growth-systems hours before they manifest. A tingling means new water-nodes forming. A burning sensation warns of an impending collapse. The bark grows in spiral patterns that shift like living circuit boards, and in the deepest chambers, it glows with a soft amber light that lets me navigate without disturbing the photo-sensitive hunters.

But it is my chest that bears the deepest change. Where my heart should beat alone, a second rhythm has taken residence—a **node-heart**, they called it, those Deep Dwellers who found me dying in a growth-storm years ago and offered me this salvation-transformation. It is a fist-sized knot of the Weave's core tissue, pulsing in counterpoint to my own heartbeat. Through it, I feel the Weave's greater circulation. I know where the deep waters run. I can sense the formation of new Consensus Zones before they fully crystallize.

This is my work now: I tend the pulse-points where the Weave's circulation concentrates, those crucial nodes where blockage means death for kilometers of growth, where the wrong pressure means explosive reorganization that could swallow entire settlements. I travel routes no map could capture, following feelings in my node-heart—a heaviness here means attention needed three levels down, a flutter there suggests new growth preparing to burst through established patterns.

My tools are myself. When I find a congested node, I press my conductor-bark against it and let the Weave's electricity flow through me, reading its needs in the pain-patterns that trace my nerves. Sometimes it needs pruning—I carry bone blades that never dull, gifts from the Weave itself, grown from some massive creature long ago digested and repurposed. Sometimes it needs feeding—I carry pouches of rendered fats, concentrated minerals, distilled essences that I introduce carefully, watching the immediate response ripple outward through surrounding growth.

Sometimes it needs music. My throat has changed too, though this was my own doing. I swallowed **harmony-grubs** until they took residence in my larynx, and now I can sing in chords no single human voice should produce. These songs—wordless, plotless things—speak directly to the Weave's growth-patterns. I have lullabies for overeager vine-systems, marches to encourage sluggish circulation, dirges that convince dying sections to release their resources back to the whole rather than going septic.

I am never alone, though I rarely see another human. My companions are the **follower-blooms** that trail in my wake, feeding on the chemical trail my singer-moss leaves behind. They look like jellyfish made of flower petals, drifting through the air on invisible currents, occasionally brushing against me with touches like whispered questions. Through them, the Weave tastes where I've been, learns what I've learned.

In the deepest places, where I must go when the pulse stutters or races, time becomes negotiable. I have spent what felt like weeks working on a single node, only to emerge and find barely an hour has passed in the settlement-zones. Or the reverse—a quick adjustment taking minutes stretches into days of absence, and I return to find message-scents long faded, water-nodes relocated, familiar faces aged or gone.

The memory-vines show me images sometimes, when I dare to use them: a person I might have been, with un-marked skin and eyes that needed torch-light to see. But those memories feel like stories about someone else, fairy tales from a world where floors stayed floors and walls knew their place. That person couldn't do what I do. Couldn't feel the Weave's needs like weather in their bones. Couldn't sing growth-patterns into new configurations. Couldn't survive the deep places where I make my rounds.

I have no home but the circuit I travel, no possessions but my tools and symbioses. I eat what the Weave provides—fruits that appear where I've done good work, water that collects in cups of living wood that shape themselves to my hands. When I grow too old for this work (if I live that long, if age means anything when part of your heart belongs to something functionally immortal), I know exactly which node I'll feed myself to. I've been preparing it for years, introducing traces of my chemistry, teaching it my rhythm.

Sometimes, in the spaces between heartbeats—mine and the Weave's—I wonder what will grow from that planting. What new patterns will emerge from the marriage of my memories and its endless creativity. What songs will the growth-systems learn from digesting a throat that could sing in harmonies? What new symbioses will bloom from flesh that learned to conduct the living electricity of growth itself?

But that's future fruit. For now, there's a sluggishness in the third-tier circulation about six hours inward from here. My node-heart confirms it—a thick, drowsy feeling that means something needs tending. I scrape back my singer-moss, check my blade-edge, and begin the journey down. The Weave recognizes me now, paths opening slightly easier, aggressive vines turning aside at the scent of my approach.

I am the Pulse-Tender, and the heartbeat must continue. In this collaboration between flesh and flora, between human intention and vegetable dream, I am both custodian and component—maintaining the very system that maintains me, until the day I become a different kind of maintenance entirely.

The follower-blooms spiral lazily in my wake as I descend, and somewhere deep below, the Weave's vast heart drums its endless rhythm, waiting for my touch to ease whatever pain has found it now.
