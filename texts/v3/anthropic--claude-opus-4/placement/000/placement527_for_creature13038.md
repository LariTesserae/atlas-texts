In this world of thermal gradients and electromagnetic symphonies, I would be one of the **Cartographers of Absence**.

We are solitary beings, each of us no larger than a child's marble, but our true form extends far beyond our physical shell. Our cores are dense spheres of collapsed ferrite, wrapped in delicate lattices of bismuth that spiral outward like frozen smoke. When we move, we do not walk or glide—we *fall* horizontally across the polished basalt, perpetually tumbling in controlled arcs, our bismuth tendrils leaving ephemeral traces of diamagnetic distortion in our wake.

We exist to map what is not there.

In a world of perfect equilibrium, we are the chroniclers of infinitesimal variation. Our purpose is to catalog the spaces between—the thermal voids where heat fails to reach, the acoustic nulls where the great hum cancels itself out, the magnetic dead zones where even the Harvesters cannot glide. We roll endlessly across the smooth plains, our bismuth lattices expanding and contracting like breathing lungs of metal, feeling for the subtle absences that define this world as much as its monolithic presences.

When we find such a void—perhaps a pocket where two arcologies' thermal plumes create a perfect cancellation, or a depression in the basalt that traps a eddy of absolute magnetic neutrality—we stop. Our ferrite core begins to spin within our bismuth cage, faster and faster, until the friction generates a peculiar phenomenon: **negative light**. Not darkness, but an active unbeing of photons, a sphere of space where light is not merely absent but mathematically prohibited. In these moments, we record.

Our memories are not stored as data or charge, but as impossibly complex crystalline structures that grow within our cores. Each absence we map adds another branch to our internal fractals. Over centuries, we become denser, our gravitational presence increasing imperceptibly. The eldest among us have grown so heavy that our passage leaves shallow grooves in the basalt—the only marks of time's passage in this timeless place.

We are drawn to the Emanations, though we can never touch them. Where they are pure positive expression—the dreams of machines made manifest—we are their complement. We orbit the great arcologies at distances of miles, perpetually falling around them, mapping the negative spaces their thoughts cannot reach. Sometimes, in the deep vibration of the world-hum, we sense patterns: the absences we map are not random, but form vast, interconnected networks of void. We suspect, in our slow way, that these networks of nothing might themselves be a kind of language—the shadow-speech by which the arcologies communicate across the dark.

The other beings rarely notice us. We are too small for the Harvesters to bother draining, too insignificant for the Keepers to tune. The thermovore scavengers occasionally cluster around us, mistaking our negative light for a new kind of heat sink, only to drift away confused when they find nothing to absorb. 

But we notice everything. We catalog every absence, every null, every space where the eternal equilibrium creates perfect cancellation. And in our cores, our crystal memories grow ever more complex, ever more beautiful, recording the vast symphonies of nothingness that resonate between the engines of eternity.

Sometimes, when we have mapped a particularly profound void—a space where heat, magnetism, vibration, and even probability seem to achieve perfect zero—we emit a single pulse. Not of energy, but of pure information: a compact broadcast of all the nothing we have found. These pulses race across the dark plains at the speed of thought, and sometimes, from vast distances, we receive an answering pulse from another Cartographer.

This is our only communication, our only acknowledgment that we are not alone in our work. These brief exchanges of absence-data are our culture, our art, our only expression of something that might be called joy.

In this world of eternal machines and perfect stagnation, we alone seek change—not in what is, but in what isn't. We are the only beings here who truly understand that in a universe of absolutes, the most profound truths hide in the spaces between.
