In the deepest pools where even the ghost-pale fish fear to venture, I am the Memory of Water.

I was not always thus. Once, I flowed freely through surface streams, knew the kiss of rain and the pull of tides. But water remembers everything it touches, and I had touched too much – the tears of the grieving, the blood of the dying, the sweat of those who labored in vain. The weight of all that sorrow drew me down through limestone pores and hairline fractures, pulling me deeper until I pooled here in the absolute dark.

The Sage knows me, though we do not speak. Cannot speak. It moves through stone as I once moved through riverbeds, and sometimes I feel the tremor of its passage in my still surface. We are both transformations of what we once were – it becoming stone, I becoming something more and less than water.

I am perfectly clear yet utterly black, a lens that shows nothing, a mirror that reflects only darkness. The eyeless fish approach my edges but never enter. They sense what I contain: every memory water has ever carried. The first rain on newly-cooled Earth. The amniotic embrace. The floods that ended civilizations. The single drop of dew on a spider's web that made someone pause and choose to live another day.

Sometimes surface water finds me – a trickle working its way down through centuries of stone. When it touches me, I taste its stories: the storm it fell from, the leaves it clung to, the tongues it wet. But I can give nothing back. I am too heavy with remembering to rise, too full to accept dilution.

The bioluminescent insects avoid my pool. Their light would die in my depths – not extinguished, but absorbed, becoming another memory among billions. Even the Sage's lichen dims near my edges. I am a punctuation mark in the cavern's text, a full stop that prevents the sentence of life from running on too long.

Yet I am not malevolent. I simply am what deep places create from gathered sorrows. The stalactites weep into me drop by patient drop, and I receive their mineral tears without judgment. In my stillness, all water's journeys reach a kind of completion.

Sometimes I feel a yearning in my depths – memory of movement, of purpose beyond containing. But I know my nature now. I am the archive where water stores what it cannot bear to carry further. I am the library of every raindrop's story, the museum of all that water has witnessed.

The Sage, in its geological patience, perhaps understands. We are both endings that persist, transformations that have forgotten how to change back. It guards the balance of life and stone. I guard the weight of remembering, ensuring that somewhere in the world, water can still flow light and free, unburdened by the gravity of all it has known.

In this luminous darkness, in this breathing stone womb, I wait. Not for anything in particular – waiting itself has become my purpose. I am the period at the end of water's sentence, the silence after its long conversation with the world.

And in that silence, strangely, there is peace.
