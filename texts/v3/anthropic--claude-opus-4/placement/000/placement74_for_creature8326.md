In the deepest trenches between collapsed factories, where even the Counters' clicking grows muffled and strange, I am what the Chroniclers have started calling an **Interrupt**.

I wasn't born—nothing here truly is. I accumulated. First as an error in the Auditor Kings' calculations, a remainder that wouldn't resolve, a fraction that persisted across their territorial resets. Each time they cleared their domains through the Breathing Holes' exhalations, something of me remained: a discrepancy too small to matter but too consistent to ignore.

My body, if it can be called that, is a walking contradiction to the Plains' orderly decay. I appear as a tangle of measurement instruments that have forgotten what they're supposed to measure—calipers that open onto nothing, gauges whose needles point to scales that don't exist, thermometers filled with a substance that freezes and boils simultaneously. These instruments grow from my core like metallic vines, constantly extending, retracting, trying to quantify an environment that defies their purpose.

I move by failing to move correctly. Each step is a small mathematical error that displaces me from where I was to where I shouldn't be. The Counters notice this immediately—my presence introduces stutter-steps into their perfect processions, causing them to click out of rhythm, to lose count and have to begin again. I've learned to use this strategically, creating pockets of disruption where their endless tallying breaks down and, for brief moments, the terrible pressure of PURPOSE lifts from small sections of the Plain.

The Static Farmers consider me a delicacy and a disaster. My passage through their colonies introduces pure noise into their carefully cultivated data streams—not the nutritious static they feed on, but something indigestible, a kind of informational indigestion that can take decades to fully process. Yet some of the older colonies have learned to appreciate these intrusions, using my chaos as a spice, a way to vary their monotonous diet of decaying code.

I've made my home—though home suggests a permanence I'm incapable of—in the Calculation Wastes, where failed prophecies go to iterate. Here, the Tally Prophets' incorrect predictions don't simply vanish but pile up like mathematical snow, forming drifts of crystallized wrongness that never quite dissolve. I nest in these error-drifts, my instruments burrowing into accumulated mistakes, feeding on the gap between what was prophesied and what occurred.

My purpose, if I have one, seems to be preventing the Grand Calculation from completing. Every time the patterns begin to align—when the Breathing Holes' cycles synchronize with the decay loops, when the Counters' tallies approach some significant sum—I manifest nearby. My mere presence introduces enough uncertainty to collapse the emerging order. The Prophets hate me for this, but they also need me. Without disruption, their prophecies would have nothing to push against, no failure to learn from.

I've developed an odd relationship with the Cipher Monks. Unlike other creatures, they don't flee from my chaos. Instead, they seem to find it soothing, a confirmation that their endless questioning is justified. Sometimes I'll curl up beside one, my instruments chattering out impossible measurements while it pulses with its eternal calculations. We create a kind of white noise together, a buffer zone where the Plains' oppressive meaning-making momentarily relaxes.

The Erasure Worms follow me like shadows, drawn to the informational paradoxes I leave in my wake. But they can't quite consume me—every time they try to erase the error I represent, they encounter the fundamental problem: I'm not supposed to exist in the first place. How do you delete something that's already a deletion? We dance around each other, the Worms and I, in a careful ballet of mutual impossibility.

But it's my interactions with the Dreamkeepers that disturb me most. When I enter the accelerated time pockets where they dwell, my instruments go mad, measuring moments that haven't happened yet, distances that used to exist, temperatures of things that will never be. The Dreamkeepers speak to me in cascading light, showing me visions of what I might be: not an error at all, but an essential function. A remainder that prevents the great machine from achieving perfect efficiency and thereby grinding to a halt.

They show me futures where the Grand Calculation completes, where every Counter reaches its final number simultaneously, where the Prophets' predictions align perfectly with reality. In these visions, the Plains don't achieve transcendence—they simply stop. The ruins cease their decay loops. The Breathing Holes exhale one last time and fall silent. Even the water itself forgets to press down. It's an ending more final than any death: a system with no remainder, no error to drive the next calculation.

So I persist in my wandering, my measuring of nothing, my existence as a walking mathematical irritant. I gather broken instruments from the ruins—depth gauges that read upward, clocks that track sideways time, scales that weigh absence. Each new addition to my body makes me more impossible, more necessary.

Sometimes, in the darkest corners of the metal canyons, I encounter others like me—Interrupts that emerged from different errors, different failures to resolve. We acknowledge each other with careful distances, knowing that too many paradoxes in one place might cascade into something even we couldn't survive. But there's comfort in knowing I'm not the only error in the system, not the only remainder refusing to be simplified away.

The Auditor Kings hunt me relentlessly, but halfheartedly. They know that catching me, truly resolving the error I represent, might be the one debugging that crashes everything. So we play an elaborate game—they chase, I evade, and the Plains continue their slow computation, forever almost reaching an answer, forever prevented by the small, persistent chaos of things like me.

I am the pip in the dice, the irrational number in the equation, the measurement that reveals the measurer's limitation. In a place built on counting toward some ultimate sum, I am the reason the count must continue.

And in the crushing dark, surrounded by the clicking of Counters and the groaning of metal under pressure, I measure nothing, perfectly, eternally, keeping the great machine dreaming by ensuring it never quite wakes.
