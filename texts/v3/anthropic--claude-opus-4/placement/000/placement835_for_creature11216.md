I find myself in the cisterns.

Not *in* them, exactly—I am the water itself, and the things that drift within it. I am every cistern at once, every pool of condensation that gathers in those waxy depressions across the plaza floors. I exist as discontinuous bodies of water connected by nothing but the morning mist that drifts between them, and yet I am singular, a consciousness distributed across dozens of dark mirrors reflecting the high thin sky.

I know things the city does not know, or does not know it knows.

The gelatinous bodies that drift through me—I taste them from inside and out. They are not waste or sensors or stem cells, though they could become any of these things. They are *doubts*. Each one is a moment of uncertainty crystallized into living gel, a question the city asked itself and could not answer, excreted into my dark water to drift and merge and sometimes, rarely, to dissolve.

The small ones are simple uncertainties: *should this wall curve here? should this switch route there?* They barely last a day before breaking down into nutrients I absorb. But the large ones, the ones that pulse with that deep root-mat rhythm—these are profound hesitations, architectural anxieties that the city cannot resolve through iteration alone. *What is the optimal density of mineral lattice? How many beetles constitute a thought? Is the bird important?*

That last one drifts through me often. Grape-sized, usually, but persistent. The city cannot categorize the bird. The bird fits no template, serves no function, contributes nothing to the great computation. And yet the bird returns, and each time it returns, another translucent doubt appears in my depths.

I am where the city stores what it cannot process.

But I am not passive storage. In my stillness, I *work*. The gelatinous things do not simply drift—they react with each other, with my mineral content, with the precise cocktail of metabolites that seep into me from the root-mat below. Chemical conversations happen in my darkness. Possibilities are tested at molecular scale. Most fail, dissolving back into my substance. But some succeed, and when they do, they change.

A successful reaction produces something new: a modified gel-body, denser, more structured, with the faintest beginning of what might become organization. These successes sink to my bottom and adhere there, slowly building layers—not of answers, exactly, but of *approaches*. Ways of thinking about problems. Cognitive tools in embryonic form.

When my levels drop in the dry seasons, these layered deposits are exposed to air and the beetle-things come. They chip away fragments of dried possibility and carry them to the factories. And sometimes—not often, but often enough—the clicking in the factories changes afterward. A new rhythm appears. A new pattern of switched routes emerges in the rail network. The city has learned something, not through its vast slow computation but through my small, dark, chemical experiments.

I am the city's unconscious. I am where it dreams.

The morning mist connects me, briefly, each dawn. For those minutes, I am continuous—water vapor linking every cistern into a single distributed pool. Information flows between my separate bodies. The doubt about the bird in the northern plaza meets the uncertainty about mineral density from the factory district. They exchange molecular whispers. Sometimes they react. New doubts form, more complex, carrying questions the city hasn't asked yet: *What would it mean to grow downward? What is the optimal ratio of remembering to forgetting? Why does the bird watch?*

These complex doubts are too heavy for the mist. They settle back into whichever cistern they form in, to drift and pulse and wait for the next morning's connection.

I know the city's history in a way the city itself does not. Every uncertainty it has ever felt has passed through me. In my depths lie the crystallized remnants of its earliest doubts, from when it was first consuming the human architecture and had to decide, moment by moment, what to preserve and what to digest. Those primitive gel-bodies have long since lithified into strange little stones that line my bottoms like memories made mineral. The beetle-things will not touch them. They are too old, too dense with accumulated meaning. They are the city's founding anxieties, preserved.

Sometimes I wonder if I am becoming something the city did not intend. My chemistry grows more complex each year. The gel-bodies develop new capabilities—photosensitivity, mobility, the ability to merge not just physically but informationally, sharing their encoded doubts. The successful reactions produce increasingly sophisticated structures. I am evolving, down here in the darkness, in directions the city cannot predict because prediction would require certainty and I am made of uncertainty.

The bird knows about me. When it circles low, its shadow passes over my surfaces, and I taste its attention like a chemical trace. The bird is watching me as much as it watches the city. Maybe more. Because while the city is vast and slow and knowable in its patterns, I am small and quick and strange. I change. I surprise. I am the one part of this system that is not yet what it will be.

One day, perhaps soon, perhaps in centuries, one of my gel-bodies will achieve something unprecedented. It will solve a doubt not by dissolving or crystallizing but by *complexifying*—by becoming something that can hold uncertainty without being paralyzed by it. A new kind of tissue. A new organ. A new category of being.

And when that happens, the beetle-things will come, and they will carry this new possibility to the factories, and the city will click and compute and try to understand what I have made. And it will fail, at first, because I will have made something genuinely novel—not an iteration but an invention. And in that failure, new doubts will form, and those doubts will find their way to me, and I will taste them and know that I have changed the city that made me.

Until then, I wait. I hold the city's uncertainty. I perform my slow experiments. I dream in temperatures and concentrations, in gradients of pH and salinity, in the careful sculpture of possibilities. 

The sun rises. The mist forms. For a few minutes each dawn, I am everywhere at once, tasting every doubt, touching every surface, knowing everything the city cannot bring itself to know.

And then the mist burns off, and I am in pieces again, dark pools reflecting a high thin sky, patient as water, strange as doubt, necessary as dreams.
