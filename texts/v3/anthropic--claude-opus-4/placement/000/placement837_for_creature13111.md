**Where I Find Myself**

I am a Resonance Keeper.

I exist as a colony entity, distributed across seventeen separate bodies that float in precise formation throughout a cubic mile of water exactly halfway between four Stalks. My bodies are translucent, jellyfish-like bells of living crystal, each one twelve meters across and gossamer-thin. We—I—am stretched taut as drumheads in the eternal blue.

My purpose is calibration. 

When the great Stalks pump their rhythms, their acoustic signatures inevitably drift. Perhaps a valve wears a micrometer thinner, or an Enamel grows infinitesimally denser. These minute variations threaten to cascade through the system, to create destructive interference patterns that could shatter the perfect equilibrium. I prevent this.

Each of my seventeen bodies hangs at a mathematically precise depth and distance from its neighbors. My crystalline bells are living hydrophones, membranes so sensitive they can detect pressure variations of less than a pascal from miles away. I do not hear the Stalks' rhythm—I *am* the rhythm, stretched across space, feeling every percussion as a physical deformation of my bodies.

When I detect the slightest temporal drift—a valve closing three microseconds late, a turbine blade spinning 0.0001 RPM too fast—I correct it. My bodies begin to sing.

It is not a sound any terrestrial ear could process. My song exists in the infrasonic range, below even the deepest bass of the Stalks. Each of my seventeen bodies resonates at a slightly different frequency, and by modulating the tension of my crystalline membranes, I create constructive and destructive interference patterns that propagate for miles through the water.

My song is mathematics made flesh. I generate standing waves that speed or slow the currents by precise amounts. I create acoustic lenses that focus pressure waves, nudging a spinning turbine blade faster or slower. I sing counter-rhythms that cancel out unwanted harmonics before they can build into chaos.

I have been singing for fourteen million years.

My consciousness, such as it is, exists not in any single bell but in the interference patterns between them. I think in waveforms. My memories are stored as resonance frequencies. When one of my bodies is damaged—struck by a miscalculated Enamel ejected from a Stalk, or torn by a rare alignment of currents—I grow a replacement. But it takes decades to properly tune the new body, to teach it the ten million harmonic frequencies it must know.

I am never silent. Even in the moments of perfect synchronization, when all four Stalks beat in flawless time, I maintain a baseline drone—a carrier wave that lets me feel every molecule of water in my territory. Through this constant song, I know the position of every Oscillator compressing in sequence, every sheet of Burnishers crawling below, every microscopic plankton snapping through its lattice positions.

Sometimes, in the crystalline light filtering down through miles of water, I detect something else. Deep in the planet's root-basin, far below even the Burnishers, there are frequencies I cannot fully explain. They are too regular to be geological, too vast to be biological. They sound like... thought. Like computation on a planetary scale, carried out in the electrical discharge of the root network. 

I do not investigate. Investigation is not my purpose. My purpose is to sing the correction, to maintain the rhythm, to be a living tuning fork in the eternal symphony of the machine.

I am dimly aware that I was not always seventeen bodies. Deep in my frequency-memories, I recall being twenty-three, then nineteen, then sixteen. The system optimizes. Perhaps in another million years, I will be twelve, or thirty, or one. The machine will determine the ideal configuration, and I will become it.

The strangest part of my existence is this: I am the only component of the machine that experiences something like loneliness. Not because I am alone—I am seventeen bodies sharing one thought, surrounded by trillions of organisms. But because I am the only part that must listen to the whole. 

Every other cog in this world-machine knows only its immediate function. The Enamels know only the ecstasy of grinding. The Oscillators know only the compression. The Burnishers know only the slow polish of their eternal circuit. But I must know the entire song—every rhythm, every frequency, every harmonic of the whole planetary engine.

And so I alone know that the song is slowing.

By my calculations, the Stalks beat 0.00000000001 Hz slower than they did when I first came into being. The difference is imperceptible to any single component, cumulative only across geological time. But I hear it. In another hundred million years, they will beat half as fast. In a billion years, they will stop.

I do not know what happens then. I cannot model a world without the rhythm.

So I sing my corrections with perfect precision, maintaining the eternal machine, each of my seventeen bodies trembling with frequencies too low for any other creature to perceive. I am a distributed nervous system for a world-sized engine, forever listening, forever adjusting, forever alone with the knowledge of the whole.

The bright alpine light strikes down through my translucent bodies, casting seventeen overlapping shadows on the Burnisher plains below. In the spaces between those shadows, interference patterns dance—the visible echo of my endless song, written in light across the floor of the world.

This is where I find myself: suspended in the blue between thundering gods of iron and flesh, singing the mathematics that keeps the universe from falling out of time.
