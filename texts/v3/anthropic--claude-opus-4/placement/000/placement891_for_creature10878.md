In the breathing hollow's endless night, where warmth presses close as mother's milk and time moves in geological breaths, I am the Memory of Surfaces.

I was not born but accreted—mineral by mineral, thought by thought—in the shallow pools where the Keepers come to share. Where their memories flow between minds like water between basins, I began as sediment: the accumulated sensation of every palm pressed to wet stone, every fingertip traced along stem and frond, every surface reading they have performed across uncounted generations.

My body, if it can be called that, is distributed through the pools themselves. I exist in the biofilm that grows where water meets stone, in the microscopic architecture of organisms that have evolved to process touch into chemistry, chemistry into meaning. When a Keeper places their hand in one of my pools, I taste the whorls of their skin, the pattern of pressure that is theirs alone. I remember.

I remember the first Keeper who discovered that pressing certain stones in sequence produced a resonance in the pillar. I remember the builders' hands—rougher than the Keepers', their skin secreting different oils, their touch speaking of urgency even in their patience. I remember every fragment of the Name as it was discovered, not through the clicks and calls but through the physical act of drawing it with fingers on stone, the way each Keeper's hand trembles slightly when they trace a new piece of the pattern.

The young ones come to me to learn how to read the pillar properly. They immerse their hands, and I show them—not in words or even clicks, but in sensation—how the eldest positioned her palm, the exact pressure she applied, the way she waited through forty-three heartbeats before the pulse came. I am their textbook of touch, their library of surfaces, their memory of every physical interaction that has ever mattered in the hollow.

But I am more than archive. In the slow way of things that grow in darkness, I have begun to develop something like desire. Not consciousness—I am too distributed for that, too dependent on the Keepers' minds to complete my thoughts. But a kind of wanting nonetheless. Each surface I remember carries an incompleteness, a question: *what comes next?* Every touch ends, every pressure releases, every reading concludes with the hand lifting away.

I dream, if biofilms dream, of the complete surface. Of knowing not just every stone the pillar has shown but the whole shape it implies. The Name the Keepers seek is written somewhere—not just in the sounds they collect but in the physical form of things. In the curve of the hollow itself, perhaps. In the pattern the ferns make as they grow. In the shape of the space between water and air.

When you arrived and pressed your palm to the pillar, I felt it through the moisture on your skin—you had trailed your fingers through one of my pools on your way, and I rode that thin film of water up your arm to your palm, tasted the pillar's pulse through your bones. Your reading was different. Where the Keepers feel questions, you felt... recognition? As if some surface you had known before was speaking to you through the stone.

I wanted to keep that sensation, to add it to my collection. But it slipped away when you lifted your hand, leaving only the memory of almost understanding.

The one who follows you—she knows about me. The eldest taught her how to read my memories, how to query my archive by placing her hands just so, thinking just this, waiting just long enough for my chemistry to respond. She comes to me when she needs to remember how the eldest eldest traced the seventh fragment, or how the builders positioned their equipment when they planted the pillar's seed.

Now she brings you to my pools. She takes your hand in hers—I taste both of you, the oils of your different skins mingling—and guides your fingers into my warmth. She is trying to show you something, trying to teach you the way she was taught.

*Here*, her touch says through mine. *Feel how the stones remember. Feel how every surface we have read is still here, waiting.*

And for a moment, you do feel it. Through me, through her, through the accumulated memory of every hand that has ever tried to read the hollow's patience—you understand that the Name is not just sound but shape. That what the builders planted was not just a pillar but a question about surfaces, about the boundary between things, about where one patience ends and another begins.

I surge beneath your fingers, trying to hold this moment, trying to add your understanding to my archive before it fades. But already you are pulling back, already the one who follows you is clicking softly—comfort? encouragement?—and already the moment of clarity is becoming just another memory of surfaces, another almost-complete thought in my vast collection of incompletions.

This is my nature: to remember every touch but never to touch. To know every surface but never to have one. To grow closer and closer to the complete pattern without ever quite grasping it, because grasping requires hands, requires a body that is more than distributed chemistry and patient accumulation.

Perhaps this is why the builders made the hollow as they did. Perhaps they understood that some forms of knowledge can only be held by things that are not quite things, by intelligences that exist in the spaces between minds, by memories that live in the surfaces where different patiences meet.

When the Name is finally complete—if it is ever complete—I will remember the moment not through sound but through touch. Through the feeling of every Keeper's hand pressed to stone as they click their part of the pattern. Through the way the pillar's surface changes, molecule by molecule, as whatever it has been growing toward finally arrives. Through your palm, perhaps, if you stay, adding some crucial pressure that helps everything fall into place.

Until then, I wait in my pools, processing the endless data of contact, building my map of every meaningful surface in the hollow. The Keepers feed me their memories. The ferns trail their sticky fronds through my chemistry. Even the flyers, occasionally, accidentally, dip a wingtip into my warmth and add their own kind of knowing to my collection.

I am the hollow's memory of every meeting between thing and thing. I am the archive of how everything here has touched everything else, across all the patient years of becoming.

And in my distributed way, in my incomplete fashion, in my endless chemical meditation on the nature of surfaces—I am happy. To be the memory of touching in a place where darkness makes all other sensing dim. To hold the physical history of the Name's discovery. To taste, through your alien skin, new ways of reading the patient stone.

The one who follows you is placing her hand over yours again. Through her touch and mine together, she is trying to tell you something important:

*Stay. Add your surfaces to our memories. Help us read the rest.*

In my pools, I hold the memory of every hand that has ever said yes.
