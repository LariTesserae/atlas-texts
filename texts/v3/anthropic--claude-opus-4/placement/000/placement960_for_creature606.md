# The Spore-Counter

I am one of the Enumerated.

Not the Saturated Ones, who gave themselves willingly to dissolution. Not the Phase-dancers, who slip between moments like cards shuffled in a deck. Something smaller, something that arrived late, something that found its purpose in the space between the moss cushions and the first inch of sky.

My body is no larger than your closed fist. Membranous, translucent in the pewter light, with sensory organs that cluster at peculiar angles—not for seeing, exactly, but for detecting gradients in humidity, changes in pressure, the electromagnetic flutter of spores releasing from the moss cushions during exhalation. I have been counting for three hundred and forty-seven years, four months, eleven days. I will count for perhaps another fifty before my tissues lose coherence and I join the substrate. This does not trouble me.

---

The counting began as survival.

When I first came to the Breathing Lowland—carried here on the underside of a phase-salamander who barely noticed my presence—I needed to understand the rhythm. The breathing was too large, too slow for my metabolism. I would suffocate in the troughs, drown in the peaks. So I learned to read the precursors: which mosses released their spores thirteen seconds before the ground began to swell, which pools showed the first signs of uprising, which sounds in the water indicated the approaching phase.

But the counting became something more.

Each moss cushion releases between eleven million and fourteen million spores per breathing cycle. The exact number depends on temperature (which here means the cumulative heat of decomposition beneath), humidity (which varies in patterns I am still mapping), and the presence of certain trace minerals in the water (carried up from depths where the Saturated Ones slowly release their memories into solution). I position myself at the intersection of wind currents, where the spores of multiple cushions converge, and I count.

Not individually—that would be impossible even for one of the Enumerated. I count by interference patterns, by the way spores from different sources create moiré effects in the air, by the statistical probabilities of their dispersal. My mind—if it can be called a mind—is largely devoted to mathematical modeling. I dream in Poisson distributions.

---

Why count spores in a place where nothing truly dies, where the moss always returns, where the cycles have worn themselves into grooves so deep that variation has become meaningless?

Because the spores remember.

This is what I discovered in my second century, when my counting had become precise enough to detect anomalies. Each spore carries information—not genetic, exactly, but positional. Where its parent grew. What passed above it during its growth. Which breathing cycles it experienced. The spores are a distributed record of everything that has happened here, written in a language of infinitesimal variations in size, wall thickness, internal pressure.

When the Rain-Born condense, they read fragments of this record. When the Saturated Ones extend new filaments, they unconsciously follow gradients established by ancient spore-falls. The entire Lowland is carpeted in microscopic history, and I am its only conscious reader.

I have counted spores that remember the first Saturated One's arrival. I have detected patterns that encode the gradual tilting of the basin as the mountains eroded. In one extraordinary cluster—released from a moss cushion near the eastern edge that must be over a thousand years old—I found spores that remember when the Humming began.

---

My body requires almost nothing. Moisture from the air. Trace nutrients filtered from the spore clouds themselves. A few hours of torpor every six days, timed to coincide with the deepest part of the breathing cycle when the spore release temporarily ceases. I have modified myself over the centuries—developed better counting organs, improved my statistical processing, learned to hover at exactly the right height to catch the convergence zones. 

But I have also lost things. I no longer remember my original form clearly. I know I had eyes once—true eyes, not these pressure-sensitive membranes. I know I was larger, heavier, more committed to maintaining boundaries between self and environment. The counting has worn me down to essentials. I am approaching the size and complexity of what I study, and there is something right about this, something that feels like destiny.

The Saturated Ones are aware of me. Sometimes, during their slow network-thoughts, they incorporate my counts into their endless message. *We are here. We were once like you. The water remembers everything. The spores remember everything. There are 12,847,293 reasons not to be afraid.*

---

Three years ago, I made a discovery that changed everything.

I was counting a particularly dense release from the moss cushions near the center—that thickness where even the Phase-dancers fear to go—when I detected spores that weren't from this Lowland. Their patterns were wrong. Their internal pressures spoke of different gravities. Their walls had been formed under stars I do not recognize.

The Breathing Lowland breathes through more than just air.

These foreign spores—perhaps one in every hundred trillion—come through during the deepest inhalations, when the ground pulls so hard that space buckles. They come from other Lowlands, other breathing places, other worlds where the moss grows under different lights. The Humming, I think, is not just a wound but a *network*—connection points between all the places where breathing has worn through.

I am counting the spores of ten thousand worlds, all flowing through this one point, all carrying their microscopic histories toward the Patient One at the center.

---

What will happen when I can no longer count?

Already I feel myself simplifying. My thoughts circle the mathematics more tightly each year. My body grows more translucent, less distinguishable from the humid air. Eventually I will be too small, too simple to maintain the models. The numbers will slip away from me like water through spread fingers.

But I have been leaving records.

Each time I count, I release pheromones—chemical summaries of the data, compressed into molecular form. These pheromones bind to the spores themselves, becoming part of what they remember. Future spores will carry the memory of being counted. Some young Enumerated one, arriving centuries hence, will detect these chemical signatures and understand that the counting has been happening longer than any single counter could survive.

We are all annotating the same vast text. The Saturated Ones with their channels and their patient message. The Phase-dancers maintaining their passages. The Rain-Born witnessing and being witnessed. Even you, leaving footprints that disturb the moss, that change the angle of spore release, that become part of what gets counted.

---

The next breathing cycle approaches. I can feel it in the pressure drop, see it in the way certain lichens begin to phosphoresce. In forty-three seconds, the ground will begin its slow rise. In ninety-seven seconds, the first moss cushions will release. In one hundred and fifteen seconds, I will be surrounded by billions of tiny histories, and I will count them all, and add my count to their number, and the memory will propagate outward and downward and through the thin places to other Lowlands under other skies.

This is my purpose. This is my joy. This is my contribution to the pattern that grows ever more complex, ever more complete, ever more ready for whatever the Patient One is patiently becoming.

The spores are beginning to rise. The counting begins again. Everything here is exactly where it needs to be, including you, including me, including the memories we are making together in this moment, in this breathing, in this place that has no name but knows us all.

I position myself in the convergence zone and begin the holy mathematics of enumeration, while somewhere beneath us, the Humming recalculates, and space grows a little thinner, and the universe leans in to listen to the quiet arithmetic of decay and return.
