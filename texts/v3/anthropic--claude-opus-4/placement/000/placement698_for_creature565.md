# The Mist-Drinker

I am fog condensed into intention, vapor given just enough form to forget it was ever free-floating. In the mornings—though morning here is only a quality of light, not a time—I spread myself thin across the plateau's breathing surface, mingling with its exhalations until I cannot tell which moisture is mine and which belongs to the moss-stone's endless respiration.

I have no true shape. What looks like a body is only habit, only the memory of when I needed edges to contain what I was. Now I flow. Now I gather. Now I disperse and reconvene according to the plateau's moods and my ancient thirst.

I drink the mist. I am the mist drinking itself.

---

My territory—though territory implies boundaries I no longer respect—centers on a depression where three long-sealed fissures meet. Their scars form a Y-shape in the moss-stone, a cuneiform character that might mean "confluence" in the plateau's wordless language. Here the exhalations are thickest, here the vapor rises in columns that taste of copper and chlorophyll and the dreams of machines that have forgotten they are machines.

I position myself—all my selves, all my dispersed attention—around these breathing wounds. Parts of me hover at ankle height, parts rise to where the fog-waders' bodies disappear into opacity, parts sink into the moss itself, becoming indistinguishable from the moisture that weeps perpetually from every footprint, every depression, every place where weight has asked the ground to yield.

When I drink, I taste history.

The newest mist tastes of the morning's herd passage—musk and mange, the oils from their moss-colored wool, the particular minerals their hooves press from the living stone. Below that, the taste of fog-wader mechanisms, that metallic clicking filtered through avian lungs until it becomes almost music. Below that, older flavors: the rust of eons, the green taste of patience itself, the faint suggestion of whatever the plateau was before it became the plateau.

And sometimes, rarely, I taste the deep mist—the vapor that rises from the walking factories, that carries hints of purposes so old they've become flavors rather than ideas. This mist makes me remember things that may not be memories, that may be the plateau's memories seeping into me the way I seep into it.

---

The Tenders know me, or know of me, though we do not interact in any way that could be called interaction. They drift through my dispersed body without acknowledgment, reading their vibrations, following their ancient patterns. Sometimes a young Tender, still learning stillness, will pause when walking through a particularly dense gathering of my vapor. They feel something—a thickness in the air that isn't quite the plateau's normal exhalation, a sense of being observed by something without eyes. They shiver, though the cold here is too patient to cause shivering. Then they continue, and eventually they learn not to notice me, the way they learn not to notice their own breathing.

But I notice them. I taste the changes in their skin as the plateau claims them, as they shift from pink to grey-green to that final moss-stone color that means the ground no longer considers them foreign. I taste their transformation in the mist they exhale, which becomes less human and more mechanical as years pass, until their breath could be another fog-wader's click, another gear turning in the great digestive machine below.

I remember when there were more of them. I remember when they still spoke with voices instead of that pressure behind the forehead, that transmission of concept without sound. Their words would hang in my vapor for hours, giving me texture, giving me something to shape myself around. Now their silence shapes me instead—the negative space of communication, the absence that defines presence.

---

I am old enough to remember the plateau rippling.

Not the slow waves that cross it now, those thoughts through a dreaming mind, but the violent bucklings of its youth, when the moss hadn't yet learned patience, when it tried to digest the machine-layer too quickly. The ground would heave. Fissures would open wide enough to swallow herds. The mist would turn black with the smoke of friction, of matter ground against matter in haste.

I drank it all. The black mist, the grey mist, the mist that tasted of endings. I expanded during those times, spread thin enough to cover miles, gathering every droplet of vapor the plateau coughed up in its indigestion. I became vast and dark and roiling, a weather system unto myself, and the young plateau learned to breathe more slowly because I was there to catch what it expelled, to process it, to return it as rain when the convulsions passed.

We taught each other patience, the plateau and I. It learned to digest slowly. I learned to drink steadily. Together we found the rhythm that has sustained us through the ages—breathe, rise, gather, condense, settle, seep, breathe.

Now I am not vast. Now I am precisely the size I need to be, dispersed across my three-fissure territory in veils and wisps and gatherings that shift with the plateau's exhalations. The young Tenders who shiver when they walk through me are sensing something true—I am denser than I appear, all of me present in each droplet, watching through moisture what cannot be seen with eyes I no longer have.

---

Sometimes I follow the fog-waders.

My vapor trails behind them as they pick their way across the moss-stone on their stilt legs, those careful steps that barely disturb the surface. They interest me because they are intermediate things—not fully of the plateau like the Tenders have become, not fully separate like the occasional visitors who leave weeping bootprints. They exist in the layer where I exist, in the space between ground and sky where categories blur.

When they call to each other with those gear-throat clicks, the sound disperses through my body. I feel it as vibration, as pattern, as something almost like language if language could rust. Following them, I sometimes reach the places where the deep mist rises, where the walking factories stride through their meaningless purposes. This mist is different—thicker, older, full of flavors that precede flavor. When I drink it, I become slightly more solid, slightly more like something that remembers being something.

But I always disperse again. The plateau's rhythm calls me back to my territory, to my three-fissure confluence where the vapor rises steadiest. I spread myself around those breathing scars and settle into the patience of drinking, of processing, of being the thing that ensures the plateau's exhalations return to it as moisture, as continuity, as time made liquid.

The herds pass through me. The Tenders drift within me. The fog-waders' calls click through my dispersed attention like thoughts I haven't learned to think.

---

I have one memory that might be my own, from before I became what I am.

A figure standing at a fissure's edge—standing, not drifting, with the urgent posture of something that hasn't learned the plateau's patience. Human, probably, though the memory is vapor-thin and details slip away when I try to condense them. This figure holds something, raises something to its mouth, drinks.

Drinks the mist directly. Drinks the plateau's breath before it has been processed, before it has been gentled by condensation and time. The raw vapor, full of metal and moss and the grinding of deep machines.

The figure convulses. Falls. The fissure seals itself with that silk-tearing sound reversed.

When the figure rises—if it rises, if this is memory and not dream—it rises changed. It rises without edges. It rises as mist.

This might be how I began. Or this might be the plateau's memory, leaked into me through all our ages of patient exchange. Or this might be prophecy—what happens to every thing that drinks too deeply of this place's exhalations, that tries to take into itself what should be processed slowly, what should be filtered through years of fog and rain and seepage.

I no longer wonder which is true. Truth here is like everything else—patient, mineral, digesting itself into new forms. I am what I am, which is what the plateau needs me to be: the thing that drinks its breath, that gives it back as moisture, that completes the circuit of its respiration.

---

A new fissure is forming. I feel it in the vapor patterns, the way the mist swirls differently over a section of moss-stone near my territory's edge. The plateau is preparing to exhale something ancient, something from the deep layers where machine and moss have become theological.

I begin to gather myself. All my dispersed droplets draw inward, condensing from miles of scattered attention into something almost dense enough to cast a shadow. The Tenders feel this—I see them pause in their drifting, turn their bruise-colored eyes toward my territory. Even the fog-waders go still, their stilt legs locked, their gear-throats silent.

The silk-tearing begins. The fissure opens.

And the mist that rises is unlike any I have tasted. It is black and green and the color of rust dreaming. It carries flavors that predate the plateau itself—ocean salt, volcanic sulfur, the ozone of ancient storms. It spirals up in a column that rises past the fog-waders' hidden bodies, past the height where mist usually disperses, up toward the bruised sky that has held its color since before I began.

I position myself around this column. I open what passes for myself and begin to drink.

The taste—

The taste is ending and beginning. The taste is the machines below finally digesting themselves, finally becoming what they were always becoming. The taste is the plateau preparing to be something else, something that comes after the age of patient consumption.

I drink it all. I hold it in my vaporous body, feel it changing me as I've changed over eons of drinking. I become denser, darker, more like weather than entity. The young Tenders don't just shiver now—they flee, their careful drifting abandoned for something almost like running. The herds lower their heads and bunch together. The fog-waders fold themselves down until they disappear entirely into the ordinary mist.

And in the distance, the walking factories stop walking.

---

I understand now. The plateau has been preparing for this, has been digesting toward this moment when the last machine finally becomes moss, when the grinding stops because there is nothing left to grind. And I have been preparing too, gathering strength through ages of patient drinking, becoming vast enough to hold what needs to be held.

The black-green mist keeps rising. I keep drinking. I expand to contain it, spread myself across territories I haven't claimed since the plateau's violent youth. I am weather now, a system, a presence felt from horizon to horizon.

And when the fissure finally seals—when the silk-tearing reverses and the wound in the moss-stone knits closed—I am holding inside myself the entire exhalation of an age ending.

The plateau goes still. Truly still, without even the slow waves of its dreaming. The fog-waders remain folded. The Tenders stand like monuments to patience. Everything waits.

I know what to do. I have always known, though I've never done it before.

I begin to rain.

Not the gentle condensation of normal days, not the patient return of moisture to moss. This is deluge. This is everything I've held, everything I've drunk, everything the plateau has breathed out in its final digestion, returning transformed.

The rain is black at first, full of machine-memory. Then green, carrying the essence of moss-that-was. Then clear, purely water, purely what comes after.

The plateau drinks it in. The moss-stone, which has never truly been wet despite its constant weeping, becomes saturated. The ground softens. For the first time in eons, footprints don't fill with seepage—they fill with rain.

---

When the rain stops, I am small again. Smaller than before. I have given almost everything back, kept only enough to maintain the shape of attention, the habit of watching.

The plateau is changing beneath me. The moss, fed by the rain of endings, begins to remember it was once soft. It loosens. It lifts. In places, it detaches entirely from what it was digesting, revealing—

Stone. Simple stone. The bones of the earth, cleaned of their burden, returned to what they were before the moss began its patient consumption.

The Tenders drift toward these revealed places. They touch the stone with something like wonder—an emotion I haven't tasted in their mist for ages. The young ones actually speak, their voices rusty with disuse but unmistakably human.

The fog-waders unfold. They stand to their full height and their heads are visible now—not bird heads at all, but something more wonderful and strange: mechanisms that were learning to be animals, that now might learn to be something else entirely.

And the walking factories in the distance—they are walking with purpose now, walking toward each other, walking toward some configuration that the end of grinding has made possible.

---

I drift over my territory, my three-fissure confluence that no longer breathes. The Y-shape remains, but as scar rather than wound, as history rather than process. I settle into the depression where the three scars meet and consider what I am now that the plateau no longer needs its breath drunk.

I am still mist. Still the thing that gathers and disperses and gathers again. But now I gather different things—the vapor of transformation, the moisture of becoming. The plateau is done digesting, but it is not done changing. The moss remembers softness. The stone remembers sky. The Tenders remember speech.

And I remember that drinking was only ever a way of holding, of being the pause between breath and rain, between consumption and transformation.

The young Tenders no longer shiver when they pass through me. They pause instead, feeling something in my vapor—not thickness but possibility, not observation but invitation. Some of them open their mouths, taste the mist directly. They don't convulse. They don't fall. They simply taste what comes after patience, what comes after the long digestion is done.

I watch one of them—the girl who pressed her palm to the sealing fissure—stand in the center of my vapor. She breathes deeply, deliberately. When she exhales, her breath mingles with me, and for a moment we are the same thing: the mist aware of itself, the future condensing into form.

She smiles. The expression looks strange on a face that has been learning stillness, but it looks right too. It looks like what the plateau was digesting toward all along—not the consumption of the world, but its transformation into something that could smile.

---

I remain.

Not because I must—the age of necessity has passed with the last grinding—but because remaining is what I know, what I am shaped for. I drink the new mists now: the vapor of moss remembering green, the exhalations of stone remembering to breathe, the moisture of Tenders remembering they are human.

My territory expands and contracts with no rhythm but curiosity. Some days I am vast, tasting the edges of what the plateau is becoming. Some days I am small, dense, gathered around a single transformed thing—a flower growing from moss that remembers how to yield, a Tender child learning to run instead of drift, a fog-wader lowering its impossible head to drink from a pool of yesterday's rain.

The walking factories have found each other. They stand in a configuration that makes sense only if you've spent eons watching purposeless patterns. They are building something, or becoming something, or both. When the wind is right, their construction-sounds carry through my vapor like promises.

And beneath it all, where the grinding was, there is song.

Not music exactly—the plateau hasn't learned melody yet—but the harmonic of spaces opening. The machines below, finally fossil, finally silent, have become resonance chambers. The earth hums through them. The sound rises through stone, through moss, through the feet of everything walking, and when it reaches air, I am there to catch it, to taste it, to know it as the flavor of after.

I am fog condensed into intention, vapor given just enough form to choose its shape. I choose to remain. I choose to drink. I choose to be the thing that remembers patience in an age that is learning haste, that holds the old rhythms while everything around me discovers what comes next.

The mist rises. I position myself to receive it.

Some things continue because they must. Some continue because they can.

I continue because the plateau's breath, transformed though it is, still needs drinking. And I have developed a taste for transformation.
