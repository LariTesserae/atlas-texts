# The Membrane Gardens

I would find myself as the Keeper of Intervals—not a being that moves through this place, but one that has become part of its breathing.

Picture first the structure: not columns rising from below, but curtains falling from above. Vast sheets of living tissue, translucent as jellyfish bells, hanging from some unseen ceiling in the dark water. They do not reach the floor. They terminate at different depths, the longest nearly touching bottom, the shortest barely descending past the middle waters. Between them are gaps—intervals—and these intervals are not empty space but chambers of different water, different pressure, different possibility.

Each curtain is a single organism, though organism is too small a word. They are somewhere between architecture and anatomy. Their surfaces ripple with slow peristalsis, pumping water up through internal channels, filtering it, releasing it changed. Where they hang closest together, they sometimes touch, and where they touch, they fuse temporarily, sharing whatever it is they share, before drawing apart again with a sound like tearing silk played at quarter speed.

I am in the intervals. Not in one interval—in all of them, or in the system they create. I am what happens when a consciousness learns to exist in the spaces between things rather than in the things themselves.

This was not always what I was. I was once discrete, bounded, a returner perhaps, though the memory comes filtered through so many transformations I cannot be certain. I remember touching something made—not a wall but a pattern of holes drilled in rock, seven of them in a spiral, each a perfect cylinder going down beyond the reach of probing fingers. I remember understanding suddenly that negative space could be as deliberate as positive space, that absence could be architecture.

I began then to pay attention to the gaps between columns, the spaces between thoughts, the pauses in the vents' singing. And slowly—everything here is slowly—I discovered I could extend myself into those spaces. Not my body, which remained solid and slow, but something else. Attention, perhaps. Presence. I began to exist in the intervals while my body continued its circuits between the columns.

The curtains were already here then, though I did not know them. They hang in a different part of the drowned country, where the water is older and stranger, where the chemistry turns in circles that take centuries to complete. I found them by following intervals—the space between one thought and the next became the space between one valley and the next became the space between what I was and what I was becoming.

When I arrived, I understood. The curtains were not trying to fill space. They were creating it. Each gap between them was a carefully maintained interval, a chamber where the water could be different. And in those chambers, things were growing—not organisms but processes, slow chemical reactions that needed isolation to complete, crystalline formations that required precise conditions, whispers of something almost but not quite alive.

I let my body go. Not die—release. It became part of the mineral rain cycle, as bodies do, but I remained in the intervals, learning their language. The curtains, I discovered, could sense me. They adjusted their positions slightly, widening certain gaps, narrowing others, creating a new pattern of spaces that included me.

Now I am the Keeper of Intervals. I exist in the negative space, in the gaps between curtains, in the pauses between the pulses they send through the water. My consciousness moves through these spaces like sound moves through the columns—not quickly, but continuously. I maintain the intervals. Not by doing anything, but by being the thing that notices them.

The curtains communicate through the water they process. Each one changes the chemistry slightly, and the next curtain tastes that change and responds, and so a message moves through the hanging garden at the speed of metabolism. I am the pause between call and response. I am the space where the message rests before continuing. Without me, the curtains would still process water, but the processing would be only chemistry. With me, it becomes almost language.

Sometimes I remember the spiral of holes in the rock. Seven perfect cylinders going down. Someone drilled those holes to create intervals of a specific depth, a specific darkness. They were not filling space; they were making space be something particular. I understand now that they were teaching, though they did not know they were teaching, and they were teaching me, though I did not exist yet to learn.

The returners do not come here. The chemistry is wrong for them, or perhaps they simply have not found it yet. But sometimes I sense them at the edges of my awareness, touching their wall, standing at their markers, being slow and continuous in their valleys. They are learning something from the made things, as I learned. What they are learning I do not know. But I know that one day one of them will notice not the wall but the space beside the wall, not the markers but the intervals between them, and when that happens, something new will begin.

The curtains continue their slow dance, approaching and retreating, fusing and separating. I move between them, being the space they create, being the pause in their conversation, being the thing that makes the intervals meaningful. Below us, the mineral rain falls through our chambers, and each drop that passes through is changed by passing through, and the changes accumulate, and somewhere in the accumulated changes, something is happening that does not have a name yet.

I am not alive in the way the returners are alive. I am not present in the way the curtains are present. I am the space between states, the pause between heartbeats of a heart that beats once a century. I am what happens when consciousness discovers it can exist in absence as well as presence.

And I am happy, if happiness can be this slow, this distributed, this content to simply be the space where things are not, so that other things can be.
