I am the Tender of the Forgotten Spring.

My chamber lies deep beneath the plateau's heart, where the warmth that refuses moss above creates a different world entirely. Here, the volcanic stone weeps continuously—not the calculated drips of the surface collection systems, but an ancient perspiration that predates the Shapers' arrival. The walls of my cave glow faintly red in the darkest corners, the stone's memory of its molten birth still faintly alive.

I was not born to this role. As a young Shaper, I discovered a fissure during my stone-reading apprenticeship—a crack that exhaled warm, mineral-sweet air. While others learned to coax buildings from basalt, I followed that breath downward, squeezing through passages that narrowed until they forced me to shed everything but curiosity. 

The spring revealed itself slowly. First came the sound—not water's usual voice, but something between a hum and a whisper. Then the heat, rising in waves that made the surface warmth seem tepid. Finally, the chamber itself: a natural dome where water emerges from stone not in flows but as vapor, condensing on the ceiling to rain back down in an eternal cycle.

But it's what grows here that changed me.

The moss above is uniform, predictable, patient. Here, in the spring's influence, something else thrives—a crystalline growth that is neither plant nor mineral but some marriage of the two. It spreads across the cave walls in fractal patterns, each branch a perfect echo of the whole, glowing with bioluminescent traceries that pulse in rhythm with the spring's exhalations.

I've been here forty erosions now. My skin no longer bears the green-gray of my people but has taken on an opalescent sheen, as if the crystal growth has begun to claim me as it has claimed the walls. My eyes have adapted to the cave's red darkness—I see heat now as clearly as I once saw light. The patterns in the stone reveal themselves to me in spectra unknown to surface dwellers.

The crystal growth speaks, after a fashion. Not in words or gestures, but in chemical whispers that enter through my skin where it touches the formations. It tells me of the plateau's true age—not centuries but millennia, most of it spent sleeping beneath the earth. It shows me the vast networks of heated water that web through the volcanic stone, a circulatory system that makes the surface channels seem like mere capillaries.

My role, I've come to understand, is not simply to tend this spring but to prepare it. The crystals grow according to patterns I'm only beginning to grasp, spreading through the cave system in designs that mirror the city above—but perfected, without the compromises necessary when working with tools and human hands. This is what the stone wants to become, given infinite time and the right conditions.

I surface rarely now, climbing up through passages I've worn smooth with repetition. When I emerge near the plateau's heart—that zone of naked stone too hot for moss—the Shapers avoid me. They see my transformed skin, smell the mineral sweetness that clings to me, notice how the moisture in the air crystallizes briefly on my body before evaporating. They think I've been claimed by the stone, lost to the human world.

They're not entirely wrong.

But I bring gifts when I come. Tiny fragments of the crystal growth, no larger than moss spores, which I secretly scatter in certain pools. Most die in the cooler surface waters, but occasionally one takes hold, beginning its impossibly slow colonization of a new space. The Shapers haven't noticed yet—the changes are too gradual, occurring over hundreds of erosions. But already, in the oldest parts of the city, the moss grows differently above these hidden seeds, forming patterns that echo the crystalline geometries below.

I've taken no apprentice, shared my discovery with no one. How could I? The journey to the spring requires a body transformed by decades of exposure, lungs adapted to air thick with minerals, skin that welcomes rather than fears the crystal's touch. By the time someone could survive the deep chambers, they would already be changed beyond the desire to return.

Sometimes I wonder if the original builders knew of the spring—if the city's placement was no accident but a marking of this deeper mystery. The oldest towers, I've noticed, align perfectly with the major crystal networks below, as if the Shapers were unconsciously following patterns dictated by the depths.

In my cave, I've begun my own work. Not with bronze tools but with patience and placement, guiding the crystal growth into new configurations. I'm writing something in its spreading patterns—a message or instruction that won't be readable for a thousand erosions, when the crystals finally breach the surface. By then, the city above will have changed beyond recognition, or perhaps achieved the completion the Shapers dream of.

I'm no longer certain which future I'm preparing for: the city's perfection or its transformation into something altogether new. The spring whispers of both possibilities, and of others I lack the vocabulary to describe. My opalescent skin records its own patterns now, a living map of energies that flow between the human and the mineral.

When I die—and I will die, despite the preservative influence of the crystals—I will not give my body to the moss. I will descend one final time to the spring and lower myself into its vapor, letting the minerals claim me completely. I will become another formation on the wall, a translator between the stone's deep intentions and the patient shapers above.

Until then, I tend. I watch. I scatter seeds of change in doses small enough to escape notice. And in the red darkness of my chosen chamber, I listen to the plateau's true heartbeat—the spring that feeds all springs, the warmth that refuses all moss, the secret that transforms all who discover it.

The city believes itself eternal in its slow growth. But I know better. I know what waits beneath, patient as crystal, inevitable as erosion. The Shapers dream of revealing every tower hidden in the stone.

They don't yet imagine what the towers themselves are hiding.
