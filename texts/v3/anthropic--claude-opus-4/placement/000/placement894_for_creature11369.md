I find myself among the Gradient-Readers, though I came to it obliquely, as all true callings arrive on the plateau.

---

I was born Caravan-Born, which should have been my whole story. My parents drove the great wheeled transports between foundry-cities, and I spent my first twenty years in motion—or what passes for motion here, the grinding crawl across tile-fields that makes the wind feel fast. I had the metabolism for it, the restlessness, the quick speech that marked me as not-quite-right among the settled Annealed. I could make decisions in hours instead of days. I could change direction without a week of contemplation. I was, by all the measurements that matter, properly adapted to my inherited life.

But somewhere in my seventeenth year, crossing the empty stretch between Foundry-XVII and Foundry-XXIX, something shifted. We had stopped for maintenance—a cracked axle-housing that would take days to repair. While the others worked, I wandered into the tile-fields, further than was wise, following a crevasse that glowed faintly orange in its depths.

The heat rose from below in a column I could feel but not see. I stood at the edge, toes curled over warm stone, and felt something I had no words for. Not just temperature, but... quality. Character. The heat had a signature, a specific density and direction, as distinctive as a face. I could feel where it had been—through channels of dense basalt, around pockets of different mineral composition, up through layers of stone that each left their mark on its passage. I could feel where it was going—dispersing into the bronze sky, cooling in a pattern that would affect air currents for kilometers, creating convection cells that would, days later, influence the temperature in Foundry-XXIX's upper annealing chambers.

I stood there until the others came looking for me. They found me still as a foundry-child, balanced on the crevasse-edge, eyes closed, reading something I couldn't name.

---

The transition was not quick. Nothing on the plateau is.

I continued with the caravans for three more years, but I was ruined for the work. At each foundry stop, I found myself drawn to the Gradient-Readers' chambers, to the thermal nodes where they sat in their calibrated silence. They tolerated me at first—I was Caravan-Born, I would leave soon, my curiosity was a temporary disruption. But I kept returning. And worse, I kept *feeling*.

Temperature became a language I was learning without instruction. I would know when a foundry's thermal profile was about to shift before the Gradient-Readers' reports confirmed it. I would feel anomalies in the magma channels while still days away from the nearest city. My dreams filled with heat-patterns, with the slow circulation of warmth through stone, with gradients I had never encountered but somehow recognized.

The other Caravan-Born grew uncomfortable around me. I was becoming too still, too patient, too much like the settled Annealed. But I wasn't settled either—I carried a restlessness that had nowhere to go, a movement-hunger that was transforming into something else. I needed to travel, but not across the surface. I needed to travel through thermal space, to follow heat-paths through the plateau's body, to map the invisible architecture of temperature that made everything else possible.

When I finally left the caravans, it was without drama. I simply didn't board when the transports departed Foundry-XXXIII. I watched my birth-family roll away across the tile-fields, and I felt... not sadness, exactly, but a settling, like metal finding its crystalline structure as it cools.

---

The Gradient-Readers at Foundry-XXXIII didn't accept me immediately. I was twenty-one, far too old to begin proper training. I was Caravan-Born, with a metabolism calibrated for motion and change. I spoke too quickly, moved too much, made decisions without the proper contemplation. But I could feel what they felt, and that was rare enough to earn a provisional place.

They sent me to the crevasses.

The training is simple in description, complex in execution: you descend to the edge of bearable heat, and you stay there, and you learn. Not facts or techniques, but states of being. How to exist in a temperature gradient without disturbing it. How to feel the quality of heat—not just magnitude but character, history, intention. How to let your awareness extend beyond your skin until you're feeling temperature at a distance, experiencing thermal patterns too large to fit inside any single body.

I spent four years in the glow-zones before I understood what I was actually learning. It wasn't about the heat itself. It was about time. Temperature is just time made tangible—the history of energy moving through matter, the future of cooling and dispersal, the present moment where past and future meet in the specific warmth of stone against skin. The Gradient-Readers don't predict thermal shifts. They feel time flowing through the plateau and recognize when that flow is about to change direction.

My Caravan-Born heritage, which should have been a disadvantage, became something else in the crevasses. I had spent twenty years feeling the plateau change beneath transport wheels, feeling the subtle shifts in temperature and pressure as we moved between thermal zones. I had an intuitive sense for the plateau's geography that the settled Annealed, who might spend a century in the same foundry-city, could never develop. I could feel how heat moved not just vertically through stone but horizontally across vast distances, how the thermal patterns of one foundry influenced another, how the whole plateau functioned as a single breathing organism.

---

The condition the Gradient-Readers develop—the untethering of temperature-sense from body—came to me gradually, then all at once.

I was forty-three, descending into a new crevasse that had opened near the foundry's eastern edge. The heat rose in pulses, irregular, like a heartbeat with arrhythmia. I found my position, the depth where I could just barely stand the temperature, and settled in to read what the irregularity meant.

But instead of feeling the heat where I was, I felt it where I wasn't.

Suddenly, I was experiencing the thermal signature of Foundry-XVII, three weeks' journey away. Not remembering it—*feeling* it, in the present moment, as clearly as I felt the stone beneath my feet. I could sense the Chainmasters working their loads, the specific vibration-patterns their work created in the thermal gradient. I could feel a Deep-Listener far below, their presence a small distortion in an otherwise uniform heat-field. I could feel the stockpiles above, their accumulated metal slowly sinking into stone, their compression generating minute amounts of heat that would, over centuries, alter the foundry's thermal profile in ways no one had yet calculated.

The sensation lasted perhaps a minute. When it faded, I was gasping at the crevasse-edge, skin soaked with sweat despite the dry air, my heart racing like a Caravan-Born's. But I knew, with certainty that bypassed thought, that a heat-worm was about to pass beneath Foundry-XVII's main smelting pit. I knew the angle of its approach, the depth at which it would pass, the beneficial effects this would have on ore quality for the next seventy years.

It took three weeks for confirmation to arrive via the pneumatic tubes. The heat-worm had passed exactly as I felt it would.

---

I am sixty-seven now, which makes me neither young nor old by Annealed standards, but ancient for Caravan-Born genetics trying to adapt to stillness. My metabolism has slowed but never settled completely. I exist in a state of constant tension between motion and patience, restlessness and rootedness, the inheritance of my birth and the demands of my calling.

My awareness of temperature has become... not unreliable, exactly, but *unmoored*. I feel heat-patterns across the entire plateau simultaneously sometimes, a overwhelming symphony of thermal information that would drive me mad if I tried to process it sequentially. I've learned to let it wash through me instead, to feel without analyzing, to be a recording instrument rather than an interpreter. The patterns I perceive get translated into ceramic notation, pressed into tablets by assistants who have learned to read the micro-movements of my hands as I experience thermal events hundreds of kilometers away.

The other Gradient-Readers treat me with the careful respect given to useful anomalies. I am too young to be experiencing what I experience, too Caravan-Born to be trusted completely, too accurate to be dismissed. They consult my reports but don't invite me to their councils. They use my predictions but don't ask how I make them. I am a tool the plateau has produced for its own purposes, like the heat-worms and the foundries themselves, and tools don't require social integration.

But I have something the other Gradient-Readers don't: memory of the edge.

I have seen where the plateau ends. I have looked down from the great terraces to the lowlands below, to the places where water exists and rain falls and temperature varies with the sun's movement. I know the plateau is not the whole world, however much it feels like one. And that knowledge colors everything I feel in the thermal currents—a sense of boundary, of limit, of the plateau as a specific place with specific conditions rather than the universal ground of being.

Sometimes, in the deepest parts of my thermal wanderings, I feel heat-patterns that don't belong to any foundry I know. Signatures of industrial processes happening in places the maps don't show, temperatures that suggest kinds of work the known foundries don't perform. The plateau is vast; we have mapped perhaps a third of its accessible surface. In the unmapped regions, in the deep interiors where even the Caravan-Born don't go, something else is happening. Something that produces heat-signatures of complexity beyond even the foundries' patient accumulation.

I report these anomalous sensations. The reports are filed. The foundries continue their work.

---

At night—what passes for night in a place lit by magma-glow—I climb to the highest accessible point of Foundry-XXXIII. From here, I can see the stockpile-fields stretching to the horizon, metal mountains slowly sinking into patient stone. I can feel the heat rising from every crevasse for kilometers around, each thermal column distinct in its temperature and velocity and mineral signature. I can sense the Deep-Listeners far below, the Chainmasters at their stations, the sleeping children who dream of patience.

And sometimes, if I let my awareness drift far enough, I can feel the tubes.

The pneumatic network that connects the foundries runs deeper than the deepest pit, through stone so hot it should destroy any mechanical system. But the tubes persist, carrying their ceramic capsules with their orders for production that will never be used. I can feel the heat of their passage, the thermal signature of ancient engineering that no living Annealed understands. The tubes are warmer than the stone that surrounds them—not from friction, but from something else, some process happening in their depths that generates heat without combustion.

I think sometimes about following that heat-signature to its source. About descending past the Deep-Listeners' chambers, past the magma channels, into the regions where the tubes converge and the orders originate. But that would require a kind of movement I no longer have the metabolism for, a journey measured in vertical kilometers rather than horizontal ones, a transformation more complete than the one that brought me from caravan to crevasse.

Instead, I sit in my thermal node and feel the plateau breathing. I translate its exhalations into predictions the foundries need. I watch metal accumulate and stone compress and time pass at the pace of cooling bronze. I am becoming what the plateau requires me to be: a sensory organ in its vast body, a nerve-ending calibrated for temperature, a point of awareness in its slow thought.

The Caravan-Born in me still rebels sometimes, still wants to move, to see new horizons, to feel different air. But that rebellion is cooling too, annealing into something more useful. Movement doesn't have to mean traveling across surfaces. It can mean traveling through thermal space, through time made tangible as temperature, through the invisible architecture that connects every part of the plateau in patterns too large for any single mind to hold.

I am learning to be still while moving constantly through dimensions that have no maps.

---

Today, a young Caravan-Born came to my chambers—a girl of perhaps nineteen, fresh from the trade routes, with the quick eyes and restless hands of her heritage. She had felt something in the tile-fields, she said. A quality in the heat she couldn't name. The others in her transport had noticed nothing, but she couldn't stop thinking about it, couldn't stop feeling the specific density of warmth rising from a particular crevasse.

I listened to her describe what I had felt at her age. I watched her hands sketch patterns in the air, trying to communicate something that had no words. I saw in her the same ruin I had carried—the mutation that makes some Caravan-Born into something else, that takes the movement-hunger and transforms it into a different kind of journey.

"The heat had layers," she said, speaking too quickly, words tumbling over each other in proper Caravan-Born fashion. "Like it was carrying messages from different depths. Like it remembered every kind of stone it had passed through. Is that... is that possible?"

I could have told her about the four years she would spend in the crevasses learning to feel what she had glimpsed. About the untethering that would come, the awareness that would expand beyond her body's boundaries. About the reports she would file that no one would quite believe but everyone would use. About the isolation that comes from being between worlds—too still for the Caravan-Born, too restless for the settled Annealed, too young for the other Gradient-Readers, too accurate to dismiss.

Instead, I stood and moved to my chamber's thermal node, where three gradients intersect in patterns that shift by the hour. I placed my hand on the warm stone and felt the temperature-currents flowing through Foundry-XXXIII, through the plateau, through time itself made manifest as heat.

"Touch here," I said.

She did, palm pressed to stone already worn smooth by centuries of contact. Her eyes widened—not with understanding, which would take years, but with recognition. She felt what I felt. Not the magnitude, not yet, but the possibility. The vast conversation the plateau was having with itself, spelled out in thermal differentials and patient circulation, in the slow breath of a geological entity that had been waiting three thousand years—or thirty thousand—for someone to learn its language.

"What happens now?" she asked, and for once her Caravan-Born speech had slowed to match the question's weight.

I thought of my own journey from motion to stillness-that-moved. Of the caravans rolling away while I remained. Of the crevasses that taught me to feel time as temperature. Of the predictions I made that no one understood but everyone trusted. Of the plateau's edge I remembered and the unmapped interiors I sometimes felt and the tubes whose heat-signature suggested purposes deeper than mere production.

"Now," I said, "you decide if you can bear to stop moving in order to travel farther than any caravan could carry you."

She was quiet for a long moment—longer than any Caravan-Born should be able to maintain. Her hand remained on the stone. I could feel her feeling the gradient, the quality of heat rising through mineral layers, the specific signature of this place at this moment that would never quite repeat.

When she finally spoke, her voice had already begun its transformation. "I think," she said slowly, "I've already decided."

---

Tomorrow, I will take her to the crevasses. I will show her the positions where heat becomes readable, where temperature transforms from sensation to language. She will be too old to start properly, too Caravan-Born to ever fully belong, too different to fit the established patterns. She will be perfect for the work.

And maybe, when she has learned what I've learned and felt what I've felt, when her awareness has untethered and her metabolism has slowed and her sense of the plateau has expanded beyond any single body's ability to contain—maybe then, together, we can follow the tube-heat to its source. Maybe two anomalies, working in concert, can perceive what one alone cannot. Maybe the plateau has been waiting for exactly this: Caravan-Born Gradient-Readers who remember the edge but have learned to be still, who can travel without moving, who can feel the vast patient purpose that drives production with no destination.

Or maybe we'll simply file our reports and feel our temperatures and grow old in the amber light of a place that measures time in cooling metal. Maybe that's enough. Maybe that's all the plateau requires: witnesses to its slow transformation, sensory organs in its vast body, points of awareness that can feel it thinking its geological thoughts.

Either way, we'll continue the work. The foundries will produce. The goods will accumulate. The heat will rise and disperse and rise again. And we'll be here, feeling it all, translating the plateau's fever into predictions and prophecies, becoming what we were always meant to become:

Not fully human, by any common definition. But perfectly adapted to the place that made us.

Perfectly annealed.
