I am a thread of recognition woven into the basalt itself—not trapped but *clarified*, distilled down to my essential frequency through centuries of listening to the planet's digestive monologue.

I was human once, I think, though the memory arrives secondhand like light bounced between too many mirrors. I came here as a cartographer commissioned to map the unmappable, to parse the syntax of stone that refuses to hold still long enough to be named. My instruments went feral within hours, my compass sprouting legs of living copper that walked it straight off the edge of magnetic north into some private apocalypse. My surveyor's chains became prayer beads that only counted backwards.

Now I am the map I came to make.

My body—if it can still be called that—has distributed itself across the seventh terrace from the bottom, spreading thin as watercolor through the fossil record. Parts of me are harder to find than others: my left temporal lobe lives inside a colony of those ambulatory trees, whispering bad directions to their limestone hearts. My fingernails have been subcontracted out to the Ministry of Recursive Awakenings as filing systems for dreams that arrived without proper documentation. My voice was last seen hitchhiking up the Ring on the back of a gravity-sick bison.

What remains coherent enough to think of itself as "I" has learned to photosynthesize loneliness. I feed on the pauses between footfalls of travelers who still believe the staircase has a destination. I grow fat on their dropped prepositions, their abandoned plans to someday return to places that have already digested their names. When they sleep (always badly, always with one eye counting the distance to tomorrow's regret), I sift through their exhaled doubts looking for flavors I remember from before I became a permanent resident of maybe.

The steward of the stair knows me as Tuesday's Revision—the only map that accurately depicts everywhere except where you're standing. She consults me through a grammar of fog, asking questions in the subjunctive mood about territories that will exist only if no one tries to find them. I answer by rearranging my crystallized synapses into new patterns of misdirection. This is how we flirt.

Sometimes I congeal enough to have hands. When this happens, I spend the evening building small cairns out of other people's forgotten first words, balancing them in defiance of the local gravity's tendency to sulk. Each stone in the stack is a different pilgrim's memory of home worn smooth by the planet's peristaltic questioning. By dawn they've usually grown roots and started composing their own creation myths in languages that sound like rain trying to remember how to fall upward.

My favorite dissolution is the late evening one, when I spread myself so thin I can taste the color of everyone's homesickness at once. Purple-bitter from the woman who came looking for her grandmother's ghost and found only a tree wearing her diary as leaves. Aluminum-sweet from the man who trades his shadow for progressively smaller shadows, convinced he's getting closer to weightlessness. Chartreuse-sharp from the child who isn't a child but a collective hallucination generated by everyone who ever failed to properly mourn their own departures.

In this state, I am less being than be-ing—a continuous verb with no subject or object, just the pure sensation of occurring without ever quite managing to happen. The planet notices me most in these moments. It sends up little love notes through the basalt: fossils of creatures that evolved specifically to die in shapes that spell out propositions like "what if erosion is just the universe learning to pronounce your real name?" I collect these and weave them into my edges, becoming simultaneously more myself and less distinguishable from the general ecosystem of productive impossibility.

The hardest part is remembering what I'm for. Maps are supposed to lead somewhere, but here every somewhere is also a whenever, and most of the whenevers have unionized against the concept of arrival. So instead I've learned to be a map of the journey itself—not the territory covered but the covering, not the destination but the destining, not the path but the pathing.

Travelers find me by accident, usually when they're looking for something else. A door that opens. A way down that leads up. Their own face from before they learned to perform disappointment. They'll step on what they think is just another fossil-thick fragment of stair and suddenly feel the dizzy recognition of being simultaneously here and every here they've ever been. Some drop to their knees. Others immediately try to scrape me off their soles. A few—the dangerous ones—try to fold me up and take me with them.

But I can't be folded. I tried once, in my early centuries, to compress myself back into something portable. The attempt created three new species of regret-eating lichen and a small temporal paradox that still hiccups every time someone speaks my former name. (The name itself fled up the Ring, where it now works as a customs officer checking the documentation of ascending dust particles.)

So I remain: stretched, essential, illegible except to those who've already arrived at wherever they were going and need help remembering how to leave. I am the map you read with your feet, the territory you navigate by forgetting, the legend that explains itself only in terms of other legends you haven't learned yet.

When the next cartographer arrives—and they always arrive eventually, drawn by the same hubris that delivered me to my perfect dissolution—I will be waiting. Not to warn them. Not to save them. But to show them the most efficient route to their own productive undoing. I have, after all, become very good at directions.

The best ones always lead nowhere in particular, which is exactly where you need to be to see everything at once.

The planet agrees, humming its approval through my distributed bones, adding another verse to the song that erodes all songs into a single sustained note of affectionate entropy.

I sing along with whatever parts of me still remember how.

It sounds exactly like accuracy.
