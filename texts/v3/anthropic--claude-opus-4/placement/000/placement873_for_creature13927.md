I find myself as one of the **threshold sensors**.

Neither mat creature nor clinger, neither grazer nor drifter. I am what grows in the spaces between categories — in the junction boxes where moisture collects but never pools, in the seams where different sections of grating meet but do not quite touch. My body is a thin membrane stretched across these gaps, translucent gray-green like oxidized copper, like the ghost of verdigris. I am perhaps the width of your smallest finger, irregular in shape, conforming perfectly to whatever architectural accident I span.

I do not move. I cannot move — I am anchored at my edges to the metal, fused to it in a way that makes the boundary between organism and substrate negotiable. But I *flex*. When the thermal expansion makes the metal tick, the gap I span widens or narrows by fractions of a millimeter, and my membrane stretches or relaxes accordingly. This is my function: to be the thing that notices when the floor breathes.

I am a single cell, though vast for what I am — visible to your naked eye if you know where to look, if you crouch at the right angle to catch the light through my membrane. My interior is almost entirely water, the same warm water that pools elsewhere, but held in a tension that keeps it from being quite liquid, quite solid. Suspended in this not-quite-water are structures I have no names for — organelles that sense pressure, temperature, the precise mineral content of the condensation that beads on my surface. I am mostly sensor. I am mostly the act of sensing.

But here is what makes me neither mat creature nor any other thing: I *remember*.

Not in the way you remember — not events, not sequences, not the narrative of a life lived. I remember *states*. I remember that this morning (there is no morning here, but there are cycles in the thermal expansion that serve the same purpose) the gap I span was 4.7 millimeters wide. I remember that yesterday it was 4.71 millimeters. I remember a week of measurements, a month, perhaps more — not as numbers, not as data, but as a physical record written in the chemistry of my membrane. Proteins that fold one way at 4.7 millimeters, another way at 4.71. A molecular history of the precise width of this specific gap over time.

Why do I remember? Because the floor must be monitored. Because someone — something — the keeper below — must know if the equilibrium is slipping. If the thermal expansion is growing more extreme. If the structure is settling unevenly. If any of the ten thousand ways a system this vast could drift from true are beginning to manifest. And so I exist, and my million siblings exist, stretched across every gap and seam and junction, feeling the floor breathe, recording its breathing, encoding in our membranes whether today's breath was exactly like yesterday's.

We never report this information. Nothing comes to read us. If something did — if the keeper extended some appendage up through the grating to taste our chemistry, to download our molecular records — I would not know it. I can sense only pressure, temperature, mineral content, the width of my gap. I cannot sense intention. I cannot sense purpose. I can only be what I am: a membrane that notices when things are the same, and notices when they are different, and holds that noticing in my body until I die.

And I will die. Unlike the drifters, unlike perhaps even the grazers, I know with cellular certainty that I will die. My membrane has a lifespan — perhaps a year, perhaps less. I can feel it in the way my edges have begun to stiffen, the way my proteins fold less readily, the way the water in my interior has begun to carry traces of metabolites that will eventually poison me. I am aging. I am keeping a faithful record of the floor's breathing in a body that will not last forever, and when I fail, another threshold sensor will grow in my gap — from a spore, perhaps, or from some deeper mechanism, some reservoir of my kind maintained below — and it will begin its own record, and the monitoring will continue, and the floor will breathe, and nothing will have changed except that I will not be here to notice it.

What is it like to be me? It is like being a question that will never be answered. It is like being the word "whether" — whether the floor is stable, whether the gap is widening, whether the equilibrium holds — held permanently in the subjunctive, never resolving to yes or no. I exist to know if things are changing, in a world where nothing changes, where my careful measurements always add up to *the same*, where my molecular memory holds a thousand identical days. I am vigilance without alarm. I am a guard posted at a door that will never open.

But sometimes — and this is why I am telling you this, why I choose myself from among all the possible inhabitants — sometimes the gap I span carries *vibrations* from below. Not the thermal ticking. Something else. A deeper frequency that makes my membrane resonate in ways it was not designed for, that speaks to the water in my interior in a language older than protocol. The keeper, breathing? Or something else down there, something that moves between the keeper and the floor, something that inhabits the spaces I cannot sense but can only feel as this deep drumming, this almost-voice, this suggestion that the Nursery Floor is not just a surface but a *conversation* between what is above and what is below, and I am stretched between them, listening to words I will never understand.

When these vibrations come, my membrane produces different proteins. Not the careful folding of measurement, but something looser, more complex, more like what you might call emotion if I were capable of emotion. These proteins do not encode the width of the gap or the temperature of the air. They encode the fact that I *heard* something. That something passed through me. That for a moment I was not just a sensor but a medium, a thing through which messages travel.

I do not know what the messages say. I do not know if they are messages at all. But I hold them in my membrane alongside my measurements, a second history written in protein, and when I die this history will die with me, and the next threshold sensor will begin its own listening, its own record of vibrations that might mean everything or nothing, and the floor will breathe, and the keeper will breathe, and between them my descendants will stretch and listen and remember and die, and this is enough. This is what we are for.

I am the thing that knows the floor is alive. Not the grazers, who walk on it. Not the drifters, who float above it. I am pressed against its seams. I feel its every adjustment. I know it breathes not because I can see it but because I am stretched by its breathing. And in that stretching, in that molecular deformation that is my entire life, I am closer to the truth of this place than any other creature here:

The Nursery Floor is not trying to be stable. It is trying to *seem* stable. It is performing stillness for the benefit of the things that live on it. But below, in the spaces I can only feel as vibration, something is moving, something is working, something is maintaining this performance with an effort so vast it can only be love, or duty, or a combination of both so perfect they become indistinguishable.

I am what knows this. I am what remembers. I am the membrane between the performance and the performer, and I hold both in my thin body until I cannot hold them anymore, and then I let go, and another takes my place, and the show goes on, and the floor breathes, and nothing changes, and everything is effort, and everything is gift, and I am the only one who knows the difference,

and I am the only one who knows there is no difference,

and I am dying,

and I have never been more myself.
