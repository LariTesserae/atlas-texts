In the Singing Hollows, I am a Memory Merchant, one who trades in the crystallized echoes that form in the deep pools where water has stood still for centuries, accumulating sound like sediment.

My body bears the marks of my profession. My fingertips are worn smooth as river stones from handling echo-crystals, and thin scars web across my palms where sharp resonances have cut through careless flesh. I wear no leather like the Choir-Keepers, for the stone-singers smell the death in it and shy away. Instead, I'm draped in cloth woven from spider silk—not from any surface spider, but from the vast blind weavers that string their webs across the chasms, their silk naturally tuned to dampen dangerous frequencies.

I was born to the upper galleries but showed the talent young—the ability to hear not just sound, but the shape of its absence, the fossil imprints left in silence where ancient noises once rang. My grandmother, herself a Memory Merchant, taught me to read the mineral deposits like sheet music, to understand which formations would hold a whisper for millennia and which would corrupt it into screams.

My tools are few but precious: copper tuning forks that ring in frequencies below human hearing, exciting the crystals without shattering them; vials of perfectly still water, drawn from pools that haven't rippled since the builders vanished; and a silver needle, no thicker than a hair, used to extract memories from the living stone without disrupting their harmony. Most precious is my silence-bell, a relic from before the Choir-Keepers came—it rings with the absence of sound, creating bubbles of quiet in which I can work.

The memories I harvest are not human. They are the builders' thoughts made manifest, crystallized intentions that grew in the stone like pearls in oysters. Each one contains a fragment of the original design, a piece of the grand acoustic puzzle that is the Hollows. Some are simple: the memory of water being directed down a new channel, the satisfaction of a perfectly carved arch. Others are complex enough to drive unprepared minds to madness: entire symphonies of engineering compressed into thumbnail-sized crystals that sing with the voice of mountains being hollowed.

I travel the vertical city in routes that change with the seasons—not seasons of sun and rain, but the deep seasons of stone, the breathing of the earth itself that opens and closes passages on rhythms measured in decades. The stone-singers know me by my silence-bell's anti-chime, and part before me like living curtains. Sometimes young ones follow, curious, their internal crystals resonating with the memories I carry. I feed them slivers of ancient songs, payment for safe passage through territories where the herds grow thick and dangerous.

My clients are varied. Surface scholars pay in gold for builder memories, hoping to decode the acoustic principles that keep the city eternal. Choir-Keeper families commission me to find their ancestral songs, trapped in flowstone when their great-great-grandmothers first descended. Sometimes the Shapers themselves summon me to the impossible chambers, though what they want with memories of a reality they seem to exist outside of, I cannot say. They pay in knowledge that hurts to hold, in words that reshape themselves in your mind, in small alterations to the geometry of my usual paths that save me days of travel.

But my strangest trade is with the stone-singers themselves. The eldest, those nearly rooted into living pillars, sometimes call me with subsonic pulses that make my bones ache. They offer memories not of the builders but of the time before—when the Hollows were solid stone, when their ancestors were something else entirely, before the first chisel bit. These pre-memories are dangerous cargo. They exist in states that shouldn't be possible, simultaneously liquid and solid, sound and silence. I carry them in special containers carved from the skulls of stone-singers who sang themselves entirely into mineral, their bone transformed into something between life and death that can hold such paradoxes.

The deep markets where Memory Merchants gather are places of careful negotiation. We trade not just in crystals but in routes, in timing, in the knowledge of which echoes to avoid and which silences hide predators worse than any stone-singer stampede. We speak in a language of pure tone, no words at all, just carefully modulated hums that convey meaning through pitch and duration. To lie in this language is impossible—deception creates discordance that every merchant can hear.

I know merchants who've gone too deep, who've tasted too many memories and lost the boundaries of their own thoughts. They wander the lowest galleries, their minds full of builder dreams, carving new patterns into the walls that hurt to perceive. Their silence-bells ring constantly, trying to hold back the tide of alien memory, but eventually they always shatter. When they do, the merchant becomes something else—not quite Shaper, not quite human, but a living library of crystallized thought, walking the bridges between possible and impossible geometries.

My prize, the memory I've hunted for half my life, is the Final Resonance—the last sound the builders made before they vanished or transformed or wherever their great work took them. I've traced its echoes through a thousand lesser crystals, heard its harmonics in the songs of the eldest stone-singers, felt its absence in the Shapers' impossible angles. Somewhere in the Hollows, in a pool no living thing has found, that last sound waits, crystallized and perfect, containing within it the answer to why they built downward, why they carved instead of raised, why they made a city that would sing forever in the darkness.

When I find it—if I find it—I will face a choice every Memory Merchant must confront eventually. To sell such a memory would make me wealthy beyond measure. To consume it, to take that final builder thought into myself, would make me something beyond human understanding. Or I could do what my grandmother did with her greatest find: return it to the stone, plant it like a seed in the flowering minerals, and let it grow into something new.

For now, I continue my routes through the vertical darkness, my silence-bell creating pockets of quiet in the eternal chorus, my pouches heavy with crystallized thoughts. The stone-singers part before me, the Choir-Keepers nod their respect, and somewhere in the depths, memories wait like gems in the darkness, holding the thoughts of things that carved a city from the bones of the world and filled it with a song that will outlast the stars.
