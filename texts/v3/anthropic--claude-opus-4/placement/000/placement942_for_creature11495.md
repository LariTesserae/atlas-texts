I find myself as one of the Keepers of Echoes—not quite Verdant One, not quite other.

My dwelling is a collapsed radio tower near the city's edge, where the mist is thinner and reality hasn't yet learned to completely dissolve. The tower fell decades ago, maybe centuries—time means little here—and now lies horizontal, a skeletal bridge between two buildings whose original purposes have long been forgotten. Thick cables, some still humming with phantom electricity, have become the framework for a hanging garden of phosphorescent moss and wire-climbing orchids that bloom in patterns resembling circuit boards.

I am mostly human in shape, though my left arm from elbow to fingertip has transformed into something between flesh and aged copper, with delicate fronds of patinated metal that unfurl in the presence of the city's electromagnetic ghosts. These metallic leaves catch signals that shouldn't exist—broadcasts from the city's industrial past, fragments of conversations in languages that predate the green conquest, machine songs that the factories sang before the vines silenced them.

My skin is a map of my wanderings: wherever I've lingered too long, patches of my flesh have taken on the characteristics of that place. A shoulder bears the rough texture of concrete colonized by lichen. My right thigh shows the smooth, dark grain of a wooden beam that has learned to photosynthesize. Along my spine, small nodes of bioluminescent fungi pulse in rhythm with my heartbeat, casting shadows that don't quite match my movements.

I collect the city's memories. Not intentionally—they simply adhere to me as I pass through the green labyrinth. In my tower home, I've accumulated objects that bridge the divide between nature and artifact: a typewriter whose keys have sprouted tiny ferns that spell out words in dead languages when it rains; glass bottles filled with spores that, when released, briefly project holographic blueprints of buildings that never were or have ceased to be; a brass compass that points not north but toward the densest concentration of temporal distortion.

The Verdant Ones tolerate my presence, though I am not truly one of them. They see me as a necessary anomaly—a being who can navigate both the organic present and the mechanical past without fully belonging to either. Sometimes they seek me out, their plant-speech rustling with questions about the sounds my copper arm translates from the city's electromagnetic dreams. I tell them stories of steel and steam, of purposes that once drove the great machines, though these tales seem to sadden them in ways their chlorophyll hearts struggle to comprehend.

My daily ritual involves climbing to the highest stable point of my fallen tower as the morning mist thickens. There, I extend my metallic arm into the humid air and listen. The city broadcasts its confusion—fragments of emergency signals that loop endlessly, automated weather reports for climates that no longer exist, the ghostly hum of assembly lines that believe they're still producing goods for a vanished population. I transcribe these signals in a journal made from pressed leaves and recycled circuit boards, using ink extracted from the berries that grow where data cables once ran.

At night, when the bioluminescent plants cast the city in an eerie blue-green glow, I sometimes venture deep into the heart where reality grows thin. In these spaces where geometry rebels and perception fractures, my hybrid nature allows me to serve as an anchor—a fixed point that helps lost travelers find their way back to consensus reality. The city seems to recognize this service; pathways open before me that remain closed to others, and the more aggressive mutations of flora and fauna give me wide berth.

I am neither fully alive nor entirely mechanical, neither completely present nor wholly past. I am the city's memory keeper, its translator, its gentle ghost. In the eternal dance between growth and decay, I am the pause between breaths, the moment of recognition before forgetting, the bridge between what was built and what has grown.
