I am a Membrane-Walker, though I wasn't always called this, wasn't always what I am now becoming.

My dwelling shifts with me—a chambered nautilus of living wood that spirals through the middle layers of the Breathing Plateau, never quite settling. Each dawn (if dawn means anything in this green twilight), I wake to find my walls have reconfigured in the night. The entrance that faced east now opens downward. The sleeping alcove has divided into three smaller pods. My collection of seed-memories, carefully arranged on what I thought was a stable shelf, has been absorbed into the wall, which now pulses faintly with their stored experiences.

I don't fight these changes anymore. That was my first lesson here, learned in those early months when I tried to maintain something like a human home. The city-forest broke me of that habit gently but thoroughly—walls I reinforced would weaken elsewhere, doors I insisted on would seal themselves while opening new passages I hadn't requested. Now I read the renovations like letters from a thoughtful friend. *Today you need three spaces for the three parts of yourself that are emerging. Your memories are safer within me than beside you. Trust.*

My body tells a similar story. I came to the Plateau as something recognizably human, fleeing a lowland life I can barely recall now—something about stone buildings and cruel geometries. The spore-thick air worked its changes slowly. First, the webbing between my fingers, translucent membranes that help me navigate the vertical city. Then the sensitivity to pressure changes, allowing me to feel the breathing of the mother trees in my bones. My skin has thinned to the point where you can see the green-tinted blood branching beneath, especially where the bark-calluses haven't formed their protective whorls.

But it's the membranes I'm named for that mark my particular adaptation. They grow from my spine like wings that will never fly, stretching between extended vertebrae that I can flex and position at will. These living sails catch the chemical signals that flow through the city-forest—warnings, invitations, emotions too complex for human words. When I spread them fully in a crosswind passage, I can taste the health of districts miles away, feel the distress of a dying grove, sense the joy-chemicals released by successful pollination.

My work, if it can be called work, is translation. I move between the districts of the Breathing Plateau, reading the chemical conversations and converting them into forms others can understand. For the Cultivators, I leave scent-marks that compress complex atmospheric information into actionable guidance. For the Architects dreaming in their fungal networks, I provide sensory data their rooted forms can no longer gather directly. Even the Symbionts, despite their magnificent attunements, sometimes need my particular gift for reading the city's chemical weather.

Today—this growth-cycle—this moment in the eternal present—I'm following a distress pattern that's been building in the northeastern canopy. My membrane-wings taste anxiety-analogues, the sharp tang of defensive compounds that mean something is wrong. I climb through the shifting passages, my webbed hands finding purchase on bark that's slightly too smooth, beaded with stress-sweat instead of healthy dew.

I discover the source in a residential grove where the walls have begun to harden, crystallizing into something almost mineral. A cluster of young Cultivators huddle at the center, their children still partially rooted, unable to flee. They've been trying to coax the walls back to flexibility, but their chemical pleas are wrong—too aggressive, triggering defensive responses instead of healing.

This is delicate work. I spread my membranes fully, tasting the full complexity of the situation. There's contamination here, some lowland influence that's caused the tree-buildings to panic, to armor themselves against perceived threat. I begin my translation, converting the grove's fear into pheromone-sentences the Cultivators can parse, teaching them the specific chemical apologies needed.

It takes hours, maybe days—time flows strangely when you're deep in molecular conversation. My membranes ache from maintaining their spread, processing toxins alongside information. But gradually, the walls begin to soften. The Cultivators adjust their chemical offerings based on my translations, and the grove remembers its flexibility. Their children's root-tethers relax, drawing normal nutrition again instead of emergency reserves.

When it's done, the eldest Cultivator offers me a memory-gourd from their private store. I accept it gratefully, feeling the decades of experience flood through me as I consume the fermented pulp. Knowledge of growth patterns I'd never encountered, chemical vocabularies for emotions I didn't know the city-forest could feel. My membranes shiver with new sensitivity, already integrating these gifts into my expanding repertoire.

I don't stay to receive thanks—that's not how things work here. The grove is already beginning to forget its crisis, walls breathing normally, new growth emerging to cover the crystallized sections. The children watch me leave with eyes that hold too much green, already more plant than their parents realize. One extends a tiny hand that's beginning to web, and I touch it gently, tasting their potential in the oils of their skin. They'll be magnificent, whatever they become.

My chambered home has migrated in my absence, following some nutrient trail through the city-forest's body. I track it by scent-memory, by the particular combination of fungi that colonize my walls. When I find it, it's sprouted a new room—a high-ceilinged space with membrane-thin walls that flutter in the constant chemical breeze. A gift, an acknowledgment. The city-forest has been watching me work, approving of my translations.

Inside, I find my seed-memories have been returned, arranged in a pattern I didn't create but immediately understand. They tell a story of my becoming, from the rigid human I was to the membrane-winged translator I am to the something-else I'm evolving toward. At the center sits a new seed I don't recognize, warm to the touch and pulsing with potential.

I plant it in the soft floor of my new room, watching as hair-thin roots immediately spread outward, seeking connection with the greater network. Whatever grows from this will be another step in my transformation, another adaptation the city-forest has dreamed for me. I settle my membranes around myself like a cocoon and wait, tasting the chemical stories flowing through the walls, translating the endless conversation between the Plateau and its inhabitants.

Tomorrow my walls will have shifted again. My body will have grown new sensitivities, lost old limitations. The city-forest will present new crises requiring translation, new hybrid languages emerging from the meeting of human and plant consciousness. And I will be here, spreading my membrane-wings in the thick air, reading the molecular weather, becoming always more myself—a self I couldn't have imagined before the Plateau taught me to grow.

The mother trees breathe, and I breathe with them. The city dreams, and I dream its dreams into forms others can almost understand. I am translator and translation both, a living bridge between kinds of consciousness, forever parsing the chemical love letters the Breathing Plateau writes to itself.
