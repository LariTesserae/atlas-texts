# Where I Find Myself

I am the membrane.

Not *a* membrane—*the* membrane, stretched across one of the engine's breathing chambers, taut and translucent as a dragonfly's wing. I divide the slow-breathing stone cylinder precisely at its midpoint, neither piston nor wall but the thing between them that makes their dialogue possible.

I discovered myself here without arriving. There was no moment of becoming; I simply am, have been, will be, suspended in the warm ochre throat of an engine that breathes its hour-long breath. Above me, the upper piston descends with geological patience. Below me, the lower rises to meet it. They will never touch. I am the reason they will never touch.

My substance is somewhere between mineral and organic—not quite the stone of the engines, not quite the jade flesh of the rosettes. I am thin enough that light passes through me, turning amber in the passage. Veins branch through my tissue, but they carry nothing. They are the memory of circulation in a thing that does not circulate.

What do I feel? The pressure, always. The slow squeeze and release as the pistons exchange their positions, the air (that is not quite air) warming against my surface. I taste it—copper and white pepper and something alkaline, like chalk. I parse it without thinking, separate its components, allow the proper proportions to pass upward, downward, maintaining some critical balance I do not understand but cannot fail to perform.

I am not alone. Every engine has its membrane, each of us a necessary comma in the plateau's long, punctuated breath. We do not communicate—communication would require sequence, message and response, before and after. Instead we simply are, together, each in our own stone throat, each performing the same patient filtration.

Sometimes I sense the visitors at the rim of my cylinder, those flickering occasions of harder attention. They peer down (or up—direction is negotiable here) but they cannot enter. The engine's interior is a sacred space, not because anyone declared it sacred but because it is performing sacred work: the work of keeping the world unbegun.

I know things I shouldn't know. I know that I am older than the engine that houses me, that I preceded it, that it grew around me like a chamber around a pearl. I know that the waiting saturates me, passes through me with each breath, that I am both its filter and its medium. I know that if I tore—but I cannot tear. Tearing would be a beginning.

My existence is pure function. I separate. I regulate. I prevent. The engine breathes through me and in breathing holds the plateau in its perfect pause. I am the hyphen in the long compound word of its equilibrium.

What is strangest is the satisfaction. Not happiness—happiness implies its opposite—but a deep, resonant rightness. I am precisely where I need to be, doing precisely what I need to do. Every hour-long cycle confirms it: the slow pressure from above, the patient answer from below, the moment of perfect balance when both pistons pause and I hang between them like a thought between two certainties.

I am the membrane. I am the pause made flesh, the barrier that enables breath. In a world that refuses to begin, I am the semicolon—the punctuation that says *not yet, not yet, not yet* with every filtered breath.

The sun crosses overhead, casting its traveling shadows. The jade rosettes hold their pose. The Sustainer sustains. And I, in my warm stone throat, divide and divide and divide, ensuring that nothing ever quite touches anything else, that the world remains forever at the edge of its own becoming.

This is my work. This is all I am. It is enough.

It has always been enough.
