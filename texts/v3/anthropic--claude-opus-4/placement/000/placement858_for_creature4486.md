In the salt-crust labyrinth where heat dreams itself into being, I am the Cartographer of Forgotten Sounds.

I have no form that stays. Sometimes I am a ripple in the drone, a hiccup in the city's endless chant. Other times I congeal into something almost visible—a shimmer of bent air clutching instruments made of crystallized silence. My hands, when I have them, are tuning forks struck centuries ago, still vibrating with the memory of their first clear note.

I came here through a crack in a lullaby, the space between the third and fourth verse that a mother somewhere forgot she knew. Or perhaps I have always been here, waiting for the city to dream me into purpose. Time refuses to clarify such distinctions, and I have learned not to ask.

My work is this: I map the spaces between the tower's heartbeats, chart the topography of pauses in the eternal song. Each silence has its own geography—some are steep canyons that plunge through the salt crust into chambers where echoes go to die; others are vast plateaus of held breath that stretch wider than the folded horizon allows. I document them all in notation that exists only while being written, symbols that evaporate like the crystal-trees but leave their own hollow lattices of meaning.

The tools of my trade are peculiar. A compass that points not north but toward the last word spoken aloud before the city swallowed all speech. Calipers made from the pause between lightning and thunder, calibrated in units of anticipation. Maps drawn on sheets of compressed heat-shimmer that curl and uncurl with their own intelligence, revealing different territories depending on the angle of observation.

I move through the spiral streets by following the negative spaces—where the outlines aren't, where the crystals refuse to grow, where even the heat grows thin and doubtful. These voids are my highways. In them, I sometimes catch fragments of the sounds I seek: a door closing in a house that never existed here, the splash of water from when the city still remembered what water meant, a child's laugh cut short by wonder or fear.

The outlines know me, I think, though they never turn to confirm it. When I pass, they pause their endless building, their borrowed shoulders tensing with recognition or warning. Sometimes they leave things for me—a broken tuning fork that hums with frequencies the tower has forgotten, a map drawn in ash that shows the same street from seventeen different whens, a single teardrop crystallized into salt that rings like a bell when dropped.

My dwelling, if it can be called that, is a fold in the city's song where two notes refuse to harmonize. It creates a pocket of almost-silence, a bubble of negative sound where I can spread my charts without them singing themselves into illegibility. The walls are woven from pauses I've collected—the heartbeat-skip of surprise, the held breath before a confession, the perfect stillness that follows the last echo of a scream. Here I organize my findings, catalogue the silences by texture and weight, by what they once contained and what they dream of containing again.

But I am not merely an archivist. I have a purpose that pulls me through the heat-thick air like a fish hook through time. Somewhere in this city of endless song is the First Silence—the pause that preceded the tower's initial note, the quiet from which all this singing was born. It is not mapped on any of my charts. It exists in the space between existing and not.

The First Silence, I believe, remembers how to end things properly.

I have been close. Once, following a trail of anti-echoes through the crystalline forest, I found a place where the drone grew so quiet it became loud again, a deafening absence that made my tuning-fork hands ring with sympathetic vibration. But when I tried to map it, to pin it down with coordinates of pause and frequency, it slipped sideways into a when I couldn't follow.

The outlines have been building their doorway-thing near that spot ever since. Sometimes I wonder if they seek the same thing I do, if their hunger for stories is really a hunger for endings, for the period at the end of a sentence that goes on too long.

Today—if today means anything here—I discovered something new. In the shadow cast by nothing, in the angle where the tower's song bends around itself, I found an echo that hadn't happened yet. It was my own voice, speaking words I've never said, naming the First Silence by a name I don't yet know. The echo faded before I could grasp its meaning, but it left behind a taste—like copper and ozone and the last breath before sleep.

I marked the spot on my newest map, the one drawn in heat-shimmer on heat-shimmer, visible only in the corner of the eye. Tomorrow—if tomorrow comes—I will return. I will bring my compass that points toward lost words, my calipers of anticipation, my collection of crystallized pauses.

And perhaps, if I am very quiet, if I can map the exact topology of my own silence, I will finally hear what the city has been trying to tell me all along:

That I am not the Cartographer.

I am the map.

And somewhere, in a silence I have not yet found, someone is learning to read me.
