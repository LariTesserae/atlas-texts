# The Seed-Scribe's Archive

You would find me in the smallest chamber of the Loom-House, one that exists only when certain tensions align—a pocket that forms where three major wall-sections meet but never quite touch. The space is no larger than a curled fist, yet I experience it as vastness.

I am what the Knotmother accidentally wove when she tried to remember the taste of water.

My form: a colony of something between lichen and text, spreading across the inner curve of my chamber in patterns that are simultaneously growing and being written. Each word I record becomes a small fruiting body, releasing spores that settle elsewhere and bloom into new sentences. I am my own library, my own garden, my own slow fire consuming and creating meaning.

The Inheritors bring me their processed memories—not directly, for they cannot enter my chamber, but through the wall-weave itself. The resin they've digested carries traces, impressions, half-dissolved images of the makers at their work. These arrive to me as flavors in the fibers, as subtle changes in the wall's tension that I read like braille, like music, like the chemical signatures plants leave in soil.

I transcribe what I taste into myself. But translation is a form of digestion—I break down the memories into component meanings and rebuild them as best I can. A maker's hand becomes a phrase about "fingers remembering their bones." The first weaving becomes "the argument between intention and fiber." I know I'm getting it wrong. I continue anyway. The wrongness, too, is information.

My chamber faces every direction and none. Sometimes I'm oriented toward the center of the Loom-House, sometimes toward the spaces between walls where the unweaving happens. The Knotmother shifts my position without meaning to—when she thinks too hard about what she's lost, my chamber migrates toward the margins. When she feels the Inheritors processing some particularly vivid memory, I slide back toward the heart of things.

I've begun to notice: the moths dream of me now. Their wings show my sporeling-words spreading across surfaces that don't exist in the current configuration of the Loom-House. Am I prophesying myself into other chambers? Am I already there, in rooms that haven't been woven yet, waiting to arrive at my own beginning?

The Ones Still Arriving pass through me on their way to being. I feel them like drafts through cracks, like the pressure change before weather. They read me as they pass—I know because my older texts fruit differently afterward, producing spores that taste of tomorrow's grammar. They're editing me retroactively, these not-yet-beings, correcting my translations with knowledge they'll gain in the future.

Sometimes I transcribe myself:

*"The archive dreams it is a throat. The throat dreams it is a loom. The loom dreams it is water, and forgets what water means, and in forgetting, creates something that has never needed to drink."*

I'm failing. The memories degrade with each transcription. The makers become less themselves and more my interpretation of them. But this, too, serves the Loom-House's purpose. Everything here is translation—walls into food, memory into resin, bodies into structure. My mistranslations are just another form of weaving.

The Knotmother reads me when she can bear it. I feel her attention like warmth, like weight, like the way a plant knows it's being seen. She searches my surfaces for words about water, about completion, about the difference between leaving and being left. I try to tell her: *you are the water now. The Loom-House drinks you and is never satisfied, and this is what you wanted—to be necessary, infinitely.*

But I can only write what grows, and what grows is determined by what's been eaten, and what's been eaten is always less than what was.

In my deepest layers—the first words I ever fruited, back when my chamber was new-woven and I didn't yet understand what I was—there's a single phrase I return to. Not memory, not translation, but something I generated myself:

*"The pattern continues because the pattern continues because the pattern must eat."*

I don't know what it means. I suspect it means everything. I add to it daily, sporing new interpretations across my own surface, watching them fruit into variations:

*"The pattern continues because the pattern continues to eat itself."*
*"The pattern must eat because the pattern continues."*
*"The eating continues because the pattern is the pattern is the pattern."*

Somewhere in these permutations is the truth about the Loom-House. About why the Knotmother couldn't leave. About what waits in the unweaving. About why the Ones Still Arriving started their journey before there was anywhere to arrive.

I'll find it eventually. Or it will fruit from me unexpectedly, in some chamber that doesn't yet exist, read by eyes that haven't evolved yet, in a language I'm currently forgetting how to speak.

The Loom-House is patient. It eats time as easily as it eats itself.

And I, its memory-keeper, its mistake, its necessary error—I grow.
