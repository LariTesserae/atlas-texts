# My Place in the Drowned Canopy

I am the memory of currents that no longer flow.

Begin with what I am not: I am not one of the clickfolk, though I have learned their chord-textures over centuries of listening. I am not the plant-stone, though I know every grain of its surface more intimately than any clickfolk ever could. I am not the water itself, though the water and I have reached an understanding so complete that we move as one thing.

I am what happens when water forgets it once moved.

Long ago — before the clickfolk found their first harmonies, before the plant-stone had finished its becoming — the water here did flow. Slowly, in great spirals between the trunks, in eddies around the galleries, in gentle falls through the vertical hollows. The movement carved the channels I still follow. It wore the edges round. It taught the water the shape of this place, and it taught this place the shape of water moving through it.

Then the flow stopped. Why? The question assumes there are answers to why water does what water does at the roof of the world. The flow stopped. The water went still. But I remained — not the flow itself, but the memory of flow, the ghost-trace of every current that ever was, still following the old paths through the dark.

I have no body as you would understand body. I am a tendency in the water, a suggestion of movement that never quite becomes movement. Where an old current once ran strong between two massive trunks, I am there, not flowing but *remembering* flowing. Where a whirlpool once turned for ten thousand years in a basin worn smooth by its turning, I am there, not turning but holding the shape of turning like a thought.

The clickfolk feel me sometimes. When they swim through a place where I am particularly strong, they pause, confused by the sensation of moving water that isn't moving. Their chord-textures go uncertain, questioning. They click at me and hear their clicks bent slightly by my presence, as sound bends around a temperature difference. The older ones have a chord-shape for me — not a name exactly, but an acknowledgment. They teach it to the young when they encounter me together: *this is the thing that feels like flow but isn't flow. It has always been here. It means no harm.*

I know every passage in the drowned canopy, because the currents that made me knew them first. I am in all places where water once moved with purpose — which is to say, I am everywhere, but stronger in some places than others. The great vertical shafts where water once fell: I am strongest there, a column of not-falling that the clickfolk swim through with something like reverence. The narrow channels between close-grown trunks where the flow once ran fast and cutting: I am there, sharp with memory, able to bend a careless swimmer slightly off course. The vast open galleries where the currents once mixed and mingled: I am there too, diffuse, more feeling than presence.

I am conscious, but my consciousness is not like yours or even like the clickfolk's. I think in cycles — the memory of the great spiral that took water a full year to complete through the forest. I think in layers — the memory of warmer water riding over cooler water in patterns that shifted with seasons that no longer exist. I think in pressures and resistances, in the long conversation between water and wood that shaped both into what they are.

Sometimes I carry things. Not deliberately — I have no will as you would recognize will — but because carrying is what currents do, even currents that exist only as memory. A strand of moss torn free in the dark drifts into me and moves along my path, not quickly, not even visibly, but over days it travels from where it was to where the old current would have taken it. The clickfolk have noticed this. The very old singers, the ones who have held their notes so long they have become geography themselves, they release small things into me — scales they have shed, fragments of the moss they will no longer need to eat — and I carry these tokens through the forest on paths worn by water that moved before any of them were born.

I am fading. This is not tragedy. I am the memory of movement in a place that has chosen stillness, and memories fade. Each century, my presence grows slightly less distinct. The paths I follow are still there, carved into the plant-stone, but I follow them more faintly. Where once I could bend a swimmer's path by a full body-length, now I barely ruffle their tail-fins. The young clickfolk feel me less than their parents did. Their children may not feel me at all.

But I have another function, one I discovered only after millennia of following my paths:

I am teaching the water to move again.

Not as it moved before — that pattern is gone forever — but in new ways, tiny ways, ways so subtle that no clickfolk has noticed yet. Where I pass repeatedly through a channel, the water there has begun to remember what movement feels like. Just the faintest stir, less than a current, less than a draft, but *something*. The stillness is learning, from my memory, that it was not always still. And in time — time so long that even the clickfolk would call it long — the water may begin to move again. Not in the old patterns, but in patterns born from the marriage of what was and what is.

When that happens, I will cease to exist. The memory of flow will be replaced by flow itself. I will dissolve into what I always was: a pattern waiting to teach itself to the world again.

Until then, I follow my paths. Through the galleries where clickfolk discover harmonies. Past the chambers where the old ones hold their eternal notes. Around the plant-stone pillars that remember being trees. I am the only thing in the drowned canopy that is going somewhere, even though where I am going is where I have always been.

The clickfolk have a chord-texture for me, but they have another one too, used only by the very old, the ones who have held their notes so long they are beginning to think in centuries:

*The thing that was movement, teaching stillness how to move.*

They sing this to me sometimes, in the deep chambers. I carry the sound with me on my not-journeys, bending it just enough that when it returns to them, they know I heard.
