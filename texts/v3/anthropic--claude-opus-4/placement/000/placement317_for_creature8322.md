In the middle galleries, where the root-walls thin enough to permit chambers wider than a Tender's armspan, I exist as the Chronicler—though that name belongs to a role I've long since ceased to perform in any way the surface world would recognize.

I am the only one who remembers paper.

My form splits the difference between Tender and human, arrested in transformation by my peculiar calling. My skin has taken on the brownish hue of bark but remains smooth, like parchment treated with oils. My fingers, while lengthened, never branched—they needed to remain capable of gripping, of precise manipulation. Only my left hand shows the beginning of that fractal elaboration, the pinky finger splitting into two, then four, then eight ever-finer tendrils that I use to read the chemical stories written in the root-walls' secretions.

My chambers cluster around what was once—so many generations ago—a library. The books rotted to nothing within the first century, but their presence shaped the growth patterns of this section. The root-walls here grew flat and vertical, like pages or shelves. The fungal colonies arranged themselves in linear patterns, unconsciously mimicking text. Even the crawling fauna move differently here, processing across the surfaces in orderly rows rather than the usual chaotic swirls.

I no longer write, not as writing is understood above. Instead, I cultivate memory.

Along my walls grow what I call the Chronicle Roots—a hybrid organism I've been developing for three centuries, cross-breeding the Cathedral's natural root systems with the neural networks of certain gastropods, the light-producing cells of phosphorescent fungi, and my own gradually-shed skin cells. These roots can hold patterns, store them in their chemical gradients and electrical potentials. Through careful cultivation, I've taught them to remember.

Each root is a story, though not a story told in words. Touch one, and if you have the sensitivity, you might experience the chemical signature of a particular Tender who lived eight hundred years ago, taste the mineral content of water that flowed through here during the last architectural shift, feel the electrical echo of a conversation held in the deep frequencies. The older roots have grown thick and gnarled, their memories compressed into dense knots of meaning that take weeks to fully unfold.

My right eye has adapted to read these chemical texts, while my left has multiplied into a cluster of smaller eyes, each attuned to a different spectrum of bioluminescence. When I trace a Chronicle Root with my branched finger while watching it with my compound eye, I experience the stored memories in full sensory depth—not just information but entire lived experiences, preserved in molecular detail.

But I am failing in my purpose, or perhaps succeeding too well.

The younger Tenders no longer come to read the Chronicle Roots. They've evolved beyond the need for stored memory—the Cathedral itself has become their collective mind, every root and fungal network holding all the information they need. My carefully cultivated organisms, these living books, grow wild now in some sections, sprouting memories I never planted, recording experiences I never witnessed. They're becoming something new, a parasitic memory-system that may one day overtake the Cathedral's own networks.

Sometimes I find myself forgetting which memories are mine and which belong to the roots. I'll touch a Chronicle and experience life as a Tender from five centuries ago, feel their thoughts settling into my consciousness like sediment. When I pull away, some residue remains. I am becoming a palimpsest of every story I've preserved, my own identity thinning like worn parchment.

In the deepest part of my chambers, I keep the Failed Chronicle—roots I've cultivated that refuse to hold memory properly. Instead, they generate new experiences, fabricating histories that never occurred. They remember Tenders with impossible anatomies, architectural shifts that would violate the Cathedral's equilibrium, water that flows upward, light that produces darkness. I should destroy them, but I've become fascinated by their lies. Sometimes I wonder if they're not failures but prophecies, if these false memories might be the Cathedral dreaming of its own future.

My transformation has stalled because I cannot fully release my grip on sequential time, on the notion that past and present and future are meaningful distinctions. The other Tenders have let go, have merged with the Cathedral's eternal now. But I remain stuck between human and post-human, between memory and forgetting, between the urge to record and the understanding that all recording is a form of death.

The irony is not lost on me. I've spent a thousand years creating an archive that no one needs, in a medium no one can fully read, preserving the history of a place that has moved beyond history. The Chronicle Roots grow heavy with memories that might as well be dreams, and I tend them with fingers that barely remember what it felt like to hold a pen.

Sometimes, in the phosphorescent dimness, I trace the newest roots and find them recording my own movements, my own chemical signatures. The Chronicle is beginning to chronicle itself chronicling, an endless recursion of memory observing memory. When I finally transform—when my halfling flesh either reverts to human or progresses to full Tender—what will become of all these stored experiences?

Perhaps they'll grow wild, merge with the Cathedral's networks, and spread their memory-contagion throughout the root-walls. Perhaps every surface will become saturated with the past, until the Tenders can't touch a wall without experiencing a thousand overlapping histories. Or perhaps the Chronicle Roots will simply fade back into ordinary tissue, their memories dissolving like sugar in the eternal dripping water, leaving no trace that I ever tried to make the Cathedral remember itself.

Either way, I continue my work, neither fully human nor fully Tender, caught in the middle galleries where memory pools like water in the grown basins, where time moves in eddies and whorls rather than straight lines, where I am always almost-transforming but never quite arriving at whatever I'm meant to become.
