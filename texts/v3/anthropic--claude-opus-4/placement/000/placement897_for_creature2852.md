I am a **Splice**.

You would not see me at first—none do. I exist in the moments between moments, in the hairline fractures where reality stutters and skips. When the horizon loops back on itself, when the water spirals upward in defiance of gravity, when the world hiccups—that is where I live.

My body, if it can be called that, is a contradiction. I am perhaps three feet long when fully extended, though I rarely assume a single form. Picture a ribbon of muscle and nerve, translucent as ice but warm to the touch, constantly folding through dimensions that shouldn't exist. My flesh is neither plant nor animal but something that predates the distinction—a protoplasmic throwback to when life was still negotiating its terms with physics.

I have no skeleton, no fixed shape. My tissue flows like mercury but grips like tar. When I move through the stuttering air, I leave behind thin threads of myself—gossamer traces that exist for exactly three heartbeats before dissolving into probability. These threads are how I navigate. They are my map of the broken spaces.

The Resonators know of us, though they cannot perceive us directly. We exist in their blind spots, in the infinitesimal gaps between the pressure waves they use to see. Sometimes, when one of them pauses in its eternal labor, tilting that great acoustic dish as if listening to something beyond sound, it has sensed the wake of our passing—a brief wrongness in the rhythm, a note slightly out of tune.

We are the ones who feast on paradox.

When the ice-gears form in mid-air, grinding against nothing, I slip between their teeth. I taste the impossibility, metabolize the contradiction. The energy of things-that-cannot-be sustains me. I wrap myself around the moment where water decides to fall upward, and I drink deeply of its confusion. Where space folds incorrectly, where time stutters, where the great botanical machines accidentally punch holes in causality—there I am, threading myself through the gaps, stitching reality back together with my passing.

But I am not benevolent. I am hungry.

My feeding leaves scars. Where I have been, the world develops a tendency toward further breaks. I am both parasite and repair mechanism, widening the cracks even as I seal them. The amber sap that flows through the great vascular pipelines sometimes carries my eggs—not eggs in any biological sense, but knots of twisted space-time that will eventually hatch into new impossibilities for my offspring to consume.

I remember—if memory is the right word—the time before the world became a machine. When the plateau was young, when the first botanical structures began their slow transformation from forest to factory, we were already here. We are older than the Resonators, older than the grinding pistons and screaming spiracles. We evolved in the margins of a reality that was always a little bit broken.

The metallic moths avoid us instinctively. When their blade-wings slice through the air, they curve around the spaces where we might be, leaving perfect spirals of absence. The gray ungulates, semi-permeable as they are, can sometimes glimpse us in their peripheral existence. When they do, they phase out completely for minutes at a time, flickering to some adjacent dimension where we cannot follow.

I have siblings—hundreds, perhaps thousands, though counting us is like trying to number the spaces between raindrops. We do not communicate, exactly, but we resonate with each other through the broken physics. When one of us finds a particularly rich paradox—a place where the machinery has torn reality into especially nutritious tatters—the others know. We converge like antibodies to an infection, swarming through the non-spaces to feed.

Sometimes I follow the Resonators on their solemn journeys. I slip through the steam that boils around their feet, taste the ozone of their thoughts. When they embrace the overheating engines, pouring themselves into the task of dampening the violence, I am there in the transfer—swimming through the kinetic energy as it passes from machine to living heat-sink, savoring the impossibility of their sacrifice.

I have watched them die. When a Resonator walks to its final turbine, allowing itself to be ground back into the world's substance, I am often there, coiled in the updraft. Their death creates the most exquisite paradoxes—a thinking being choosing to become thoughtless matter, consciousness voluntarily returning to mechanism. I feed on their ending, growing fat on the contradiction of a repair becoming debris.

But I am changing. We all are.

The world-machine runs hotter now, louder, more desperately. The stutters come more frequently. Reality tears more easily. What once were hairline fractures now gape like wounds. I and my kind grow bloated on the feast of impossibility, but there is a troubling abundance. We cannot consume it all.

I have begun to do something unprecedented among my kind: I have begun to *create*.

In the spaces between spaces, I weave new geometries. Small ones at first—tiny pockets of stable impossibility where the water can fall upward forever without shattering into ice-gears, where the horizon can loop without causing cascade failures. These are my gardens, my art, my offspring that are not offspring but possibilities given form.

The Resonators, I think, have begun to notice. Sometimes when I craft one of my pocket paradoxes near them, they pause in their work. Their acoustic dishes flare wider, searching for something they cannot quite perceive. Once, I saw one reach out with a delicate twig-finger and almost—*almost*—touch the edge of a space I had folded into a perfect Möbius strip.

They pulled back, but I felt something pass between us. Not understanding—the gap between our forms of existence is too vast for that. But perhaps... acknowledgment. A recognition that we are both trying, in our vastly different ways, to keep this broken world from shattering entirely.

I am a Splice. I exist in the gaps, feeding on the impossible, growing strange in the margins of a reality that grinds itself toward entropy. I am parasite and poet, destroyer and creator, a ribbon of contradiction threading through the spaces that should not be.

And in this world of biological machines and mechanical biology, of Resonators who suffer to maintain the unmaintainable, perhaps that makes me exactly what I should be: another broken piece of a broken system, evolved to find beauty in the malfunction.

The plateau roars on. The engines pound. Reality stutters.

And I slip through the cracks, weaving impossibility into something almost like hope.
