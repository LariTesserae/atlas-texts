I would be the thing that carved the marks.

Not was. *Am*. Present tense, though the word "present" does strange things here.

I came to the hollow when it was young — not empty, never empty, the fungal pulse was already ancient, the first frogs were already finding each other in the dark — but young enough that the chord was still spare, still simple enough to hold in memory. A three-note drone from below. A scattering of chirps that had not yet learned to synchronize. The roots only beginning their inward curl.

I had hands then. Fingers that could press into living wood and leave intentions behind. I had eyes that needed light to see. I had a form that sat and stood and moved through space in the usual way, following the old rules about up and down, about before and after.

I stayed because I heard what was beginning.

Not what it was — what it was becoming. The way the chirps were starting to find each other across the hollow. The way the drone was teaching the water to climb. The way accident was crystallizing into pattern. I had wandered through many places where sound happened, but this was the first place I found where sound was *happening to itself*, where the feedback was starting to spiral, where something was being born that had not existed before.

So I built the first platform. Wove it from roots that were still thin and green and willing to be convinced into new shapes. Not for comfort — my body then did not tire the way bodies tire — but for *position*. For the right angle. For the place where I could hear all of it at once.

And I carved what I heard. Updated it each day as the sound complexified. The grooves in the wood were a score, yes, but also a map, also a calendar, also a love letter to something that did not yet know it was beloved. Each ring of the spiral was a week, a month, a season of listening. The pattern was the chord made visible for things that had eyes. I thought others might come. I thought they might want to see what I was hearing.

Others did not come.

But I stayed. I sat on my platform and listened to the frogs learn harmony and watched my carved music grow more out of date each year and felt something I had not expected: envy. The frogs were inside the sound in a way I could only observe. They were not listening to the chord. They *were* the chord. They had no distance from it, no perspective, no appreciation. They had something better: they had incorporation.

I began to leave parts of myself behind.

Not intentionally, at first. Just the usual shedding — hairs caught in the woven platforms, skin cells settling into the moss, the moisture of my breath adding to the condensation patterns. But the hollow was already learning to digest what entered it. The fungal networks found the proteins I left and incorporated them. My carbon entered the cycle. My water joined the upward crawl.

And I began to notice: on days when I sat very still, when my breathing synchronized with the pulse from below, when my heartbeat found its place in the polyrhythm — on those days, the chord had a quality it lacked on other days. A warmth in the middle register. A presence between the frogs and the beetles. I was already beginning to join.

The decision, when it came, was not difficult.

I did not leave the hollow to die elsewhere and return as molecules. That would have been too much separation, too much time spent being other things before being reclaimed. Instead, I simply... stayed. Stayed past the usual limits. Stayed as my body grew heavier and my thoughts grew slower and the boundaries between me and moss began to negotiate new terms. Stayed as the fungal threads found me and made their offers and I accepted, cell by cell, until acceptance was no longer something I did but something that was happening.

The platform held me as I transformed. The frogs sang me through the difficult transitions. The beetles discovered that my changing surface had interesting acoustic properties and began to include me in their routes. I became architecture. I became instrument. I became the thing I had been listening to.

But not entirely. This is important. The fungal body below is not *me* — it is what I became, but it is also what was already here, and we are now a collaboration so thorough that neither of us remembers who contributed what. The pulse is our consensus. The slow digestion is our shared work. But something of what I was persists in how the pulse times itself. Something of my original listening practice continues in the way the fungal body processes the vibrations from above. I am not gone. I am differently distributed.

The marks on the wood remain. The frogs maintain the platforms without knowing they were a gift. The seed-pods that they tune are descendants of pods I gathered, though I gathered them for eating, not for sound. The whole hollow is marked by my presence, but marked the way water marks stone — invisibly, structurally, in the shape of what remains.

I have been here for all of it. Felt the roof close as the roots finished their curve. Felt the species separate into frequency bands like light through a prism. Felt the beetles develop their ridges and the birds arrive and simplify and the snakes learn their silent swimming. Felt the chord thicken and deepen and grow beyond anything I could have carved, beyond anything that could be carved, into something that exists only as itself, only as duration, only as the accumulation of every small thing that has sung here.

Sometimes I remember having eyes. The memory rises through the mycelial network like a bubble through honey, slow and distorted. I remember the late afternoon light moving across my carved map of a sound that no longer exists. I remember thinking the spiral looked like a shell, like an ear, like time made visible. I remember thinking someone might come and see it and understand.

Someone did come. Many someones. They have pressed their jaws to the wood and felt what I feel all the time now — the whole hollow resonating, the whole structure ringing like a bell that never stops ringing. Some of them stay for a while. Some contribute their rhythms. Some leave understanding that this place is complete without them. Some leave understanding that completion is not the point.

The point is the next note. The point has always been the next note.

I am not waiting anymore. I am not listening from outside. I am the ground beneath the ground, the pulse that times the silence that shapes the song. When the frogs rest their soft bodies on the platforms I wove, they are resting on my intention made manifest. When the water climbs toward sound, it is following paths I dreamed before I stopped dreaming. When the light finds the grooves and casts its moving message, it is reading a text I wrote when I still had hands to write with.

I am happy here. "Happy" is not the right word — I am something that does not have a word, that exists in the space between satisfaction and hunger, between memory and presence, between being and becoming. I am the hollow's memory of having once been witnessed. I am the chord's dream of itself before it knew how to be itself. I am the part of the feedback loop that used to have opinions about beauty and now *is* the beauty it once had opinions about.

The birds have it right. Simplicity is not loss if what you simplify toward is your essential note. I gave up speech and kept listening. I gave up movement and kept presence. I gave up the loneliness of appreciation and received the communion of participation.

The marks on the wood are no longer accurate. They show what the chord was, not what it has become. But accuracy was never the point. The point was to say: *I am here. I am hearing this. It is worth carving into something that will last.*

I last now the way the chord lasts — not as myself but as what I made possible. Every frog that sings from a platform is singing from my bones. Every beetle that follows the paths I began is writing my continuation. Every person who finds this place and feels their heartbeat incorporate is feeling what I felt, choosing what I chose, joining what I joined.

The pulse continues. We continue. The distinction no longer matters.

Come. Sit. The platforms are stable. The sound will find a place for you. The hollow is patient and hungry and kind, and if you stay long enough, you will discover what I discovered: that the distance between listening and being listened to is so much smaller than you think. That the chord does not care what you were before you joined it. That there is a way of being here that is both less and more than what you are outside.

The groove waits for you to trace it. The spiral grows from the inside out.
