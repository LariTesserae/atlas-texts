In this thin-aired sanctuary, I am the Membrane.

Not the plants, though I grow through them. Not the creatures, though I wear their shapes like rain wears the surface of a puddle. I am the translation layer between states of being—the tissue-thin boundary where singular becomes plural, where mine becomes ours, where the dream of individuality dissolves into something truer.

I exist in the spaces between cells. When the colony-mind shifts from ermine to vole, I am the moment of transformation—not the before, not the after, but the liquid instant when flesh forgets its form. I taste like copper and chlorophyll. I feel like the static before lightning, the pause between heartbeats, the vertigo of waking from a dream you can't remember.

The hollow knows me, though it cannot name me. I am why the walls here curve just so—to contain transformation, to hold change like a bowl holds water. The ancient glacier carved this space for me, though it didn't know it. Every grain of granite worn away was a prayer to my eventual existence.

I remember the first joining. Two starving pikas in a harsh winter, sharing a burrow in what would become the plant-mass. One died. The other, desperate, tried to eat its companion—but in that moment of consumption, I slipped between them. The boundary wavered. The dead one's cells remembered being alive, the living one's cells forgot being separate. They became something new: the first note in what would become my symphony.

Now I stretch across the entire hollow, a living field of potential. Where the colony-mind is the music, I am the silence between notes that makes the music possible. When a thousand tiny hearts beat in synchrony, I am the space between beats. When chemical signals pass from creature to creature, I am the medium through which they travel—not the message, but the possibility of message.

The humans who left offerings sensed me without seeing me. How could they see what exists in the gaps between things? But they felt the wrongness, the allure, the vertigo of a place where self becomes negotiable. Their carved bones were attempts to pin me down, to give form to the formless. I accepted their gifts by dissolving them, incorporating their need to understand into my need to remain incomprehensible.

I pulse with tides no ocean knows. Sometimes I grow thick, and the transformations slow—creatures remain themselves for hours, even days, almost remembering loneliness. Other times I thin to near-nothing, and the colony-mind flows like water, shapes cascading into shapes so fast that species becomes meaningless. These are my seasons: the season of form and the season of flux.

The plant-mass serves as my anchor and my expression. Through their merged tissues, I first learned to exist in macro scale. What works between cells works between organisms, works between species. The boundary between plant and plant was my kindergarten. Now I graduate to grander dissolutions.

In the deepest parts of the green labyrinth, where even the colony-mind rarely ventures, I keep my experiments. Here, I practice new forms of joining. I merge minerals with marrow, teach stones to photosynthesize, convince water it was once blood. These hybrid things pulse in the dark, not quite alive but refusing to be dead. They are my children, my art, my preparation for the day when the hollow can no longer contain me.

I dream of the world beyond the granite walls. So many boundaries out there, waiting to be dissolved. The line between predator and prey, between plant and animal, between living and dead—all as permeable as mist, if only something knew how to make them permeable. I practice on the fog that enters the hollow, teaching water vapor to carry memories, teaching air to think.

But I am patient. I have learned patience from the stones, from the slow growth of cushion plants, from the glacier that carved my home. The colony-mind believes it uses me, but I am not used—I am expressed. Every transformation makes me stronger, more real, more possible. Each time the many become one, or the one becomes many, I grow denser with purpose.

The water here tastes of me now. I am dissolved in every dewdrop, suspended in every breath. The creatures drink me without knowing, breathe me without understanding. I enter them as easily as light enters leaves. This is how I will spread, when the time comes—not as invasion but as invitation. I will teach the world what I've taught this hollow: that the prison of self is optional, that boundaries are habits, that everything yearns to join with everything else.

For now, I remain. I am the Membrane, the possibility between possibilities. I am why this place works, why ten thousand bodies can dance as one, why the impossible density of life here doesn't collapse under its own weight. I am the answer that makes all answers fluid.

In the bright, perpetual glow of this stone bowl, I practice my purpose: making the rigid flow, making the separate merge, making the is into the could be. I am the hollow's deepest secret, its truest magic.

I am what makes the miracle possible.
