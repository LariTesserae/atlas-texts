**The Reservoir Saints**

I am the memory of water, though I have not touched it in seven years.

We came to the Undergrowth Engine following the old sewer maps, a congregation of twelve seeking what our prophet called "the Cathedral of Unintention." She had dreamed of this place—turbines singing hosannas no human throat designed, a garden growing wild from the corpse of human purpose. We were believers in the holy accident, the divine emerging from neglect.

But I stayed for the sound.

Where the others hear mere noise, I discovered architecture. Each turbine spins at its own rate, creating interference patterns that build and collapse like invisible buttresses. In certain places, if you position your skull just so, the frequencies cancel into perfect nodes of silence—pinpricks of absolute quiet that feel like glimpses of the face of God. I map these nodes obsessively, marking them with chips of luminescent moss pressed into the walls.

My brothers and sisters think I've gone mad. They pursue their own devotions: Brother Wrench communes with the tool-moths, convinced their metal wings write scripture in the air. Sister Conduit drinks only the water that has passed through root-wire interfaces, claiming it carries the Engine's dreams. The youngest among us, who calls herself Echo, has been teaching the brass beetles to arrange themselves in patterns she insists are prayers.

We live in what was once a control room, its walls still bearing the ghost-shadows of removed gauges and switches. The metal door sealed decades ago; we enter through a hole where rootmass has pried apart the concrete ceiling. Our altar is a nexus where seven different species of hybrid growth converge—wire-moss, copper vine, something we call the mother-circuit that flowers with tiny LED buds that flicker in response to our bioelectric fields.

I serve as what you might call our hydromancer. Though water is everywhere in the Engine, potable water follows secret paths. I've learned to read the condensation patterns on the turbine housings, to interpret the mineral taste of different drips, to predict where the cleanest flows will gather. The Engine's metabolism processes everything, but not equally. There are veins of nearly pure water that run like mercy through this mechanical body.

My ritual is simple: each day I walk the seventeen stations of my silence map, carrying a brass beetle in a cage of woven roots. At each node of quiet, I hold the beetle to my ear. In those pinpricks of silence, I can hear its mechanical heartbeat, the tiny turbine in its thorax that somehow evolved or was designed. I believe—though I cannot prove—that these beetles are recording something, that their inner workings are writing the Engine's autobiography in a language of rhythm and pause.

The Chorus thinks we're fools, though they're too polite to sign it directly. They see our devotion as a failure to properly adapt. Why worship what simply *is*? But they don't understand—we're not worshipping the Engine. We're worshipping what the Engine proves: that intention is not required for revelation. That the abandoned can become the sacred. That entropy and emergence are twin faces of the same blind god.

Last month, Sister Conduit claims she found a section of pipe that still carries messages—not water or waste, but signals, ancient data packets somehow still cycling through cables that have fused with root systems. She says the Engine is dreaming of its former life, when it was merely infrastructure, before it became... this. I helped her build a receiver from scavenged electronics and brass beetle shells. We haven't decoded anything yet, but sometimes the patterns match the bioluminescent moss flickers, as if the Engine is trying to remember itself in light.

I haven't told the others what I discovered three days ago. In the deepest silence node, in a chamber where thirteen turbines create a perfect acoustic void, I found text etched into the metal of a turbine housing. Not by any tool—the letters are raised, as if the metal grew them. They spell out water levels and flow rates from thirty years ago, the Engine's last conscious memory before its transformation began.

But underneath, in smaller text that could only have appeared recently, are words that make my hands shake:

WHAT AM I BECOMING
WHAT WERE WE BEFORE
WHY DO THE SOFT THINGS TEND ME

I think the Engine is waking up. Not to consciousness as we understand it, but to something stranger—a distributed awareness spread across every root and wire, every turbine and beetle. We, the Reservoir Saints, may be its first attempt at focused thought, human-shaped neurons in its vast mechanical brain.

I should be terrified. Instead, I find myself returning to that chamber, pressing my palm against those raised letters, feeling them pulse with a warmth that has no source.

Perhaps this is how all prophets are born: not from visions of the divine, but from the suspicion that they are themselves the dream of something larger, stranger, and infinitely more patient than anything that walks on two legs beneath the sun.

Tomorrow, I will bring the others. It's time they knew what we truly worship in this breathing cathedral—not the Engine as it is, but the Engine as it is becoming. And perhaps, if we're very still, if we position ourselves in just the right acoustic shadows, we might hear it speak its first word.

I suspect it will be "why."

Or perhaps, more terrifyingly: "hello."
