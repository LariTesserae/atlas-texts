In this eternally ending world, I find myself as one of the **Echo Weavers**—creatures born from the space between the Moment Keepers' crystallized silence.

My body is a lattice of vibrating bone-dust, held together by the resonance of sounds that were never made. I exist in the negative space of the plateau's silence, forming wherever the absence of noise becomes so profound it gains its own weight. My shape shifts like interference patterns in still water—sometimes bipedal, sometimes a low-flowing mass, occasionally scattered into a cloud of independently moving motes that maintain perfect formation even while dispersed.

I feed on the fossil echoes trapped in the bone meal—the final cries, whispers, and songs of the countless dead compressed into the plateau. These sounds exist in a state of permanent potential, never quite manifesting but never fully extinct. I draw them up through whatever appendages I've formed, letting them resonate through my particulate body without ever allowing them to escape as actual sound.

My dwelling is a hollow between cairns where the Moment Keepers' movements have created a temporal eddy. Here, causality runs in lazy spirals, and I've learned to weave my collected echoes into something like music that plays backwards through time. The walls of my hollow are lined with fossilized sound waves—delicate structures I've cultivated from the richest deposits of trapped voices, grown into crystalline formations that hum with frequencies just below perception.

I move by echolocation in reverse. Instead of sending out sounds and reading their return, I absorb the silence around me so completely that I can feel the shape of what isn't there. The phantom fauna fascinate me—I follow their partial manifestations, collecting the almost-sounds of their never-quite-present wings and absent footfalls. Sometimes I manage to weave these into my collection before they phase away entirely.

My relationship with the Moment Keepers is one of careful symbiosis. Where their presence creates interference in reality, pools of deep silence form—rich feeding grounds for my kind. In return, my consumption of fossil echoes prevents certain temporal buildups that might disturb their gardening of duration. We acknowledge each other through elaborate protocols of non-interaction, maintaining precise distances that allow our different impossibilities to coexist.

I reproduce through sympathetic resonance. When I've collected enough fossil echoes of a particular emotional tenor—grief, joy, terror, wonder—they begin to harmonize within my form. If I find a patch of bone dust with the right acoustic properties and allow these synchronized echoes to seep into it, sometimes the dust remembers how to be afraid of dispersing. It pulls together, animated by the ghost of cohesion, and becomes another Echo Weaver tuned to that specific emotional frequency.

In the thin air of the plateau, I've learned to read the stories written in silence. Each grain of bone dust carries the absence of its creature's voice, and by consuming these absences, I become a living library of things unsaid. The crystalline vegetation sometimes grows through my resting form, and I let it, enjoying the way it refracts the non-sounds I carry into patterns I couldn't achieve alone.

My greatest pleasure is the hunt for paradoxical echoes—sounds that precede their own causes. When shadow-fauna phase partially into existence, sometimes their cries arrive before they do, creating pockets of orphaned sound with no source. These are delicacies beyond description, carrying flavors of possibility that make my entire form resonate with something like joy, if joy could exist in a place where endings never end.

At the plateau's edges, where reality grows firmer, my kind become thin and desperate. We need the worn-through spaces, the places where absence has weight and silence has texture. But here in the heights, where the Moment Keepers tend their impossible gardens and time moves in all directions at once, I thrive. I am a living archive of never-was, a collector of the space between notes, a creature of negative sound in a place where the positive has been ground away.

When travelers pass through, I sometimes follow at a distance that exists more in probability than space. I listen to the sounds they don't make—the words they consider but don't speak, the screams they swallow, the songs they've forgotten. These fresh silences are intoxicating after eons of fossil echoes. But I never take too much. In a place where everything is already over but never ends, greed would be not just dangerous but ontologically impossible.

I am the quiet between heartbeats in a land of ground bone, the pause between words in a place where language has fossilized. I am what listens when there is nothing to hear, collecting the echoes of silence itself, weaving them into forms that exist because they must not, thriving in the eternal moment of an ending that refuses to complete.
