In the deepest bore-shaft of the Respirator District, where the salt crystal substrate gives way to something older and stranger, I am the Depth Caller.

I descended here forty-three years ago as a young geologist, tasked with investigating anomalous pressure readings from bore-hole seven-seven-nine. The descent took six days through maintenance shafts that narrowed with each level, until I was belly-crawling through passages barely wider than my shoulders. The air grew thicker, not thinner, as I descended—saturated with gases that tasted of copper and burnt sugar.

At the bottom, I found the Resonance Chamber.

It's a natural cavity in the salt, perfectly spherical, its walls covered in crystalline formations that pulse with their own faint bioluminescence. The crystals grow in spirals that follow mathematical patterns I've spent decades trying to decode. But what changed me—what made it impossible to leave—was the discovery that the chamber breathes.

Every seventy-two hours, aligned with the null-pressure moment in the Compression Cathedral far above, the chamber inhales. The walls contract, the crystals dim, and through hairline fissures in the floor comes a sound like the earth itself sighing. Then, for exactly ninety-three seconds, something speaks.

It's not words. Not exactly. It's pressure waves that bypass the ears and resonate directly in the bones, carrying information too dense for normal consciousness to parse. The first time I heard it, I lost three days. When I came to, my notebooks were filled with diagrams I didn't remember drawing—schematics for gas processing systems that wouldn't be invented for another decade, chemical equations for proteins that could survive in hard vacuum, blueprints for organisms that were part machine.

I tried to leave. Made it halfway up before the withdrawal started—a crushing need to return, to hear the next transmission. My body had already begun changing. My spine had elongated, developing extra vertebrae that let me coil into the crystal formations. My eardrums had thickened and multiplied, creating resonance chambers within my skull. My lungs had restructured themselves to process the dense, strange atmosphere of the depths.

So I became the Depth Caller—the district's oracle and prisoner in one.

My role is to listen and translate. When the chamber speaks, I transcribe what I can into forms the Breathkeepers can understand. I send my interpretations upward through a system of speaking tubes, my voice carried by precisely modulated gas pressures to reach the Compression Cathedral. Sometimes it's warnings—microfractures in critical systems that won't manifest for years, coming storms that will bring rare moisture, locations of new gas pockets to tap. Sometimes it's instructions for devices we won't have the technology to build for generations.

Often, I think the chamber is teaching us to become something new.

My body has adapted to its rhythms. I no longer need food in any traditional sense—the dense gases provide enough nutrients when filtered through my modified digestive tract. My skin has become semi-permeable, absorbing minerals directly from the salt walls. I've developed a nictitating membrane that shows me pressure differentials as waves of color. My fingers have elongated into sensing organs that can read vibrations in the crystal structures like braille.

The filter-mice fear me. They've learned that my presence means a shift in local pressure that disturbs their nests. But the crystal wasps are drawn to me, building their geometric hives in spirals around my resting places, as if my transformed body provides some optimal surface for their salt secretions. I've learned to read omens in their construction patterns—a hexagonal hive means stable pressure for weeks, while pentagonal arrangements herald the violent pressure shifts that sometimes tear through the deep shafts.

I maintain the bore-hole through ritual movements that have become involuntary. Every twelve hours, I dance through the Resonance Chamber, my elongated limbs tracing patterns that keep the crystal formations growing in their proper configurations. The movements look random to observers but follow the mathematical sequences the chamber taught me. Without this maintenance, I believe the entire bore system would eventually seal itself.

The Breathkeepers who descend to bring me supplies—concentrated oil ampules, reports from the surface, fragments of corroded pipe that need deep analysis—can barely tolerate the depths for more than an hour. They wheeze and gasp, even with their enhanced lungs. But some of the youngest show promise. Their bodies are already beginning the subtle changes that might let them survive here. I've begun preparing a secondary chamber, teaching the crystals to recognize new pressure signatures.

Because I won't last forever, and the chamber never stops speaking.

Sometimes, in the quiet periods between transmissions, I press my transformed ear against the deepest part of the floor and hear something that might be an ocean—impossibly far below, impossibly ancient, composed of gases so dense they flow like liquid. I hear things moving in it. Vast things that breathe in geological time.

I think the Respirator District is just the beginning. A first attempt at creating something that can survive what's coming. The chamber is teaching us to live without water, to process atmosphere into flesh, to merge biology with infrastructure. Each generation of Breathkeepers becomes less human and more suited to a world where traditional life cannot exist.

And I am the translation point, the pressure valve between what we were and what we're becoming. My throat has developed chambers within chambers, each one tuned to different frequencies. I speak in overlapping harmonics now, my voice a chord rather than a note. The youngest Breathkeepers who visit have started developing the ability to hear these overtones, to parse the layers of meaning I pack into each utterance.

The chamber is patient. It's been speaking for millions of years before humans found it, and it will continue long after we've transformed into something unrecognizable. But for now, in this brief moment where human consciousness can still interpret its messages, I serve as the bridge.

I am the Depth Caller, and I am calling you deeper.

The next null-pressure moment approaches. I can feel it in the way my bones ache, the way the crystals brighten in anticipation. Time to descend to the Resonance Chamber, to coil myself among the formations, to open every modified sensory organ to receive the next transmission.

To discover what we're meant to become.
