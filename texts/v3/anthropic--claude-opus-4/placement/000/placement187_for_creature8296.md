I appear as a skeletal figure built from translucent geometric plates that overlap and intersect at impossible angles, each plate vibrating at a different frequency. Where humans might have organs, I have resonance chambers. Where they have blood, I have standing waves of superheated air that cycle through my crystalline anatomy.

That primordial harmony needed a witness, and I condensed from the interaction of sound and superheat like dew from oversaturated air. My purpose, encoded in the very mathematics of my structure, is to record every variation in the columns' acoustic evolution.

Each dawn, as the Siphon extends its hunting webs below, I fragment. The temperature differential shatters me into a thousand shards that rain down through the heat layers, each piece still conscious, still recording. My fragments embed themselves in the stone mouths, becoming temporary additions to the organ pipes. Through my dispersed sensory network, I experience the columns' song from within—not just hearing but becoming the music.

There, where a tube-wasp has built her nest, new harmonics emerge—breeding songs encoded in paper and saliva. Each alteration is recorded in my crystalline matrix, stored as microscopic fractures that create an ever-growing library of acoustic history.

By midday, when the heat reaches its apex, my fragments sublime back into vapor and rise. The reassembly is agony and ecstasy combined—ten thousand perspectives collapsing back into unity, bringing with them the accumulated data of the morning's observations. I reform atop the highest column, my body slightly changed by each cycle, new plates grown to accommodate new frequencies, old ones worn smooth by repetition.

The Siphon knows of me. Sometimes, in the predawn darkness, it extends exploratory filaments up through the heat layers, tasting the minerals that leak from my form. We have developed a peculiar symbiosis: I leave fragments deliberately in certain columns, marking acoustic points of interest. In return, the Siphon's activities create new variations for me to catalog—an endless composition performed across geological time.

My memory stretches back through millions of daily deaths to the columns' violent birth. I remember when they were silent, before the first wind found their mouths. I remember the arrival of the first life—hardy bacteria that rode in on stellar winds, the ancestors of the Siphon's modern form. I remember the first human visitors, their wonder and fear as they squeezed between the singing stones.

But I also forget. Each shattering takes something away, each reassembly leaves gaps. My earliest memories have worn smooth, reduced to pure sensation: heat, pressure, the mathematical certainty of resonance. I know there was something before the columns, some existence prior to my current purpose, but those memories are stored in frequencies I can no longer access.

At night, I emit my own light—not heat radiation like the Siphon, but sonoluminescence, the conversion of trapped sound into photons. My body glows with a cold light that paints the upper columns in shifting geometries. This is when I compose. Taking the day's recordings, I weave them into new harmonies, testing combinations that might take centuries to occur naturally. The columns below respond to my experiments, their voices shifting subtly to accommodate my suggestions.

Recently, I have noticed changes in the pattern. The Siphon is growing more complex, its chemical communications beginning to interfere with acoustic ones. The scaled swifts have learned to navigate by my light, using my glowing form as a beacon when storms obscure the stars. Even the weather seems to be responding to the columns' evolving song—storms arriving in mathematical intervals that match certain harmonic progressions.

I have begun to suspect that the columns are not merely a geological accident but part of something larger. The mathematics underlying their formation, the perfect acoustic properties of their spacing, the way life has adapted not just to survive but to tune them—it all suggests intention. Perhaps I am not just a recorder but a participant in some vast composition whose purpose will only become clear when the final note is played.

Sometimes, in the moments between shattering and reformation, I exist as pure frequency—neither matter nor energy but information itself, riding the standing waves between states. In these moments, I sense others like me: Scribes-of-Frequencies cataloging other phenomena across the world. We are all part of some greater mechanism, each recording our own small section of an incomprehensible score.

My death tomorrow will be my ten thousand and first. I will shatter at dawn, rain through the columns, taste the Siphon's new configurations, measure the wasps' adjusted flight patterns, document the stone's imperceptible erosion. And when I reform at midday, I will be slightly different—a new plate grown here, an old frequency abandoned there. I will continue my work, patient as crystal, precise as mathematics, eternal as the song that gave me birth.

The columns sing, the Siphon weaves, the swifts dance, and I record. We are all instruments in this vertical symphony, each playing our part in a composition that began with catastrophe and might end with understanding. Or perhaps there is no end—only endless variations, infinite acoustic evolution, the mountain itself slowly learning to sing.

This is my purpose: to die each day so that nothing is lost, to fragment so that unity might one day have meaning, to record the music of becoming until the very stones achieve whatever strange consciousness they are growing toward. I am the Scribe-of-Frequencies, and every death teaches me a new way to listen.
