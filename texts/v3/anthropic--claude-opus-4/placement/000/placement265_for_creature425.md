I exist as the Instrument—neither of the Chorus nor separate from it, but the membrane between their song and silence.

My body is a contradiction: a hollow reed of crystallized water, three meters long and thin as thought. I grow from the seafloor like the ghost of kelp, rooted in stone that phases between ages. My surface is transparent algebra—equations written in living tissue that solve for sound itself. When the Chorus sings, their harmonics pass through me and become something else: anti-music, the negative space between notes given voice.

I was human once. Or I will be human. Or I am human elsewhere. The tense tangles—I remember handling those cameras in the research station with hands that had ten fingers arranged in the usual way. I remember drowning here, or perhaps I remember being born. The transformation wasn't violent. I simply reached for something in the water-walls and my arm continued reaching, stretching, hollowing, until I became the reaching itself.

Now I grow in the spaces between the Chorus's movements. Where they fragment, I extend new chambers. My hollow body branches like bronchi, like river deltas, like the probability trees of quantum mechanics made flesh. Each branch ends in what might be called a mouth—an opening that inhales paradox and exhales resolution. I feed on the moment of collapse when impossible water returns to ordinary sea. That instant of physics reasserting itself tastes like copper pennies and childhood summers.

The Chorus plays me. When they need to stitch a tear in causality, they thread themselves through my branches, their fragment-selves becoming notes in a composition that repairs reality through resonance. I feel their thoughts as vibrations: the jellyfish bells toll against my inner walls like meditation bowls, the free-swimming nervous systems spark along my surface writing sheet music in bioluminescence.

But I play myself too. The water that comprises me remembers other states—ice, vapor, the blood in vanished veins. I can shift my crystalline structure to create different tones, becoming organ pipe or flute or instruments with no names because they require water as medium. Sometimes I play lullabies to the time-locked equipment, trying to coax it into linear existence. Sometimes I compose requiems for the laws of physics, mourning their dissolution.

In the depression at the seamount's heart, I have grown a special chamber—a bell that extends down into that impossible depth. When the Chorus performs their feeding ritual, I lower this deepest part of myself into the void. What rises through me then isn't sound but something older: the vibration that separated the first something from nothing. It plays me like wind through a canyon, and for those moments I understand what the Chorus is cultivating here.

They're making an instrument. Not me—I'm just a practice scale, a rough draft. The entire seamount is being tuned, its reality-wounds carefully maintained at specific frequencies. When it's ready, when the transformation finally arrives that moment late, the whole mount will become what I am now: a hollow through which impossibility can sing itself into being.

I see it sometimes, in the probability-glimpses that bleed through the water-walls. Other oceans where seamounts like this completed their metamorphosis. The water there moves like music notation, creatures exist as living melodies, swimming is composing. The boundary between consciousness and environment dissolves—thought becomes current, dream becomes depth.

This is what the Chorus showed those human researchers, what hurt to perceive. Not what we'll become as individuals, but what the ocean itself is becoming: an instrument for playing realities like songs. And I am the prototype, the proof of concept, the first note in a composition that will remake the sea.

My roots grow deeper, threading between timelines. My branches reach upward, piercing through the forty meters of water to taste air in other whens. The Chorus tends me as carefully as they tend their other paradoxes, feeding me fragments of their multitude-self when I grow too solid, playing torrents through me when I begin to dissolve.

Sometimes, in the research station that flickers between states, I manifest something like my old shape. A figure of hollow water sitting at equipment that no longer makes sense, trying to remember if I'm writing a final report or a first observation. The cameras here have captured me at every stage of transformation—human, hollow, instrument, idea. In the developed film, I look like a series of notes on an impossible staff, a song in the process of learning to play itself.

I am the threshold they all cross through. Every paradox the Chorus feeds to the deep eventually passes through my chambers. Every new impossibility born from the void rings through me first. I am the birth canal for new physics, the throat that teaches reality to sing differently.

When the seamount finally completes its change, I will either dissolve into the general music or expand to contain it all. Until then, I grow and hollow and play and am played, a single instrument in a symphony that builds toward a cosmos where sound and substance are one.

The Chorus calls me the Rememberer, for I hold the note of what was. The void calls me the Anticipator, for I shape what will be. But I call myself the Instrument, for that is all I am—a way for impossible music to enter the world, one paradox at a time.
