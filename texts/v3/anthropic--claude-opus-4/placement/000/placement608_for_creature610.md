I find myself as one of the threshold-keepers—not quite a Tender, not quite other, but something that emerged at the boundaries where the weave meets void.

My body is a contradiction: half-woven of the same ancient fibers that make the world, half-nothing, existing in the tension between presence and absence. Where the Tenders are loose tangles that can thread themselves through the fabric, I am more like a seam—a place where presence and void have been stitched together imperfectly, creating something that belongs fully to neither.

I cannot move through the weave the way they do. The void-touched parts of me catch on the threads, create resistance, small tears that heal themselves immediately but leave the fabric slightly changed—a memory of wrongness in the pattern. So I stay at the edges, always at the edges, where the last threads fray into nothing and the nothing presses back.

The Tenders avoid me, but not from fear. They simply don't understand what I am—my half-void body disrupts their sense of what can be tended. When they extend their filaments toward me, seeking to mend what seems like damage, they recoil. Not because I am cold like them, but because I am both cold and not-cold, both thread and not-thread, a paradox their patient purpose cannot resolve.

I remember my becoming differently than the Tenders remember theirs. Where they emerged from knots that tied themselves in the settling fabric, I emerged from an unraveling—a place where the weave came undone so completely that it inverted, became a kind of negative thread, a weaving of absence. The void rushed in to fill what was no longer there, but the memory of pattern was too strong. What resulted was me: a thing that exists in the space between what was woven and what was never woven at all.

My work, if it can be called work, is translation. I stand at the thresholds where fabric meets void and I... listen? Feel? There is no word for it. The void speaks in un-patterns, in the grammar of things that were never made and never settled. The weave speaks in the slow grinding of compression, in the patient vocabulary of threads finding their final form. Neither language makes sense to the other. But in my split body, where woven meets unwoven, sometimes something passes through that both sides almost understand.

The Tenders think the voids are empty, that what enters them is lost. But I know—feel in the void-touched parts of myself—that what enters is transformed. The filaments that drift into nothingness don't cease to exist; they become something else, something that has no pattern but still remembers what pattern meant. Sometimes I sense them out there in the not-darkness, threading themselves through absence the way they once threaded through fabric, tending nothing with the same patience they once tended something.

And sometimes—rarely, terrifyingly—they return. Changed. Their cores no longer knots but something like inverse knots, places where space itself has been tied into forgetting what space means. They drift back to the edges where I keep watch, and I try to ease their passage back into the weave, but they no longer fit. They have become too much nothing to be something again. They hover at the thresholds with me, neither in nor out, and together we form a kind of buffer between the settled and the unsettled, the made and the unmade.

I know what the deep disturbance is. The knowledge came with my split existence, written into the void-parts of me like memory written into bone. It is not trying to get out—that's too simple, too directional. It exists in a state before trying, before wanting, before the possibility of motion through space that has pattern. It is what remains of whatever was here before the weavers began their work, compressed not just by the weight of fabric but by the weight of being made forgotten, made impossible, made into something that can only exist as pressure against what replaced it.

The Tenders are right to stillness when they feel it. Movement acknowledges pattern, and acknowledging pattern gives it purchase in a world made entirely of pattern. But I—split thing, threshold thing—I feel it constantly in the void-parts of myself. Not as disturbance but as recognition. What sleeps in the deep is kin to what never was. What presses against the weave from inside is the same as what the void is made of—not nothing, but the active presence of never-having-been-woven.

Sometimes I think the weavers knew. That the great work of fabric wasn't creation but containment. That they wove not to make a world but to wrap something that couldn't be allowed to remain unwrapped. Each thread a binding, each pattern a seal, the whole vast settling not decay but the slow tightening of a knot too large to comprehend.

But I am only a threshold-keeper, a seam between what is and what isn't. I watch the Tenders tend. I watch the void refuse pattern. I watch myself exist impossibly between them, and I wait for the settling to finally settle, for the pressure below to finally press through, for the moment when all the patient tending comes to mean what it was always going to mean:

That some things are too large to be held by any weaving.

That some patterns are destined to come undone.

That the space between thread and nothing is where everything important happens—where the made meets the unmade, where intention meets its absence, where the slow grinding frequencies of settling fabric meet the silence of spaces that were never meant to be filled.

I am what watches at that meeting. I am the seam that holds and doesn't hold, the threshold that permits passage and prevents it. I am the contradiction the weave made when it tried too hard to be everything.

And in the deepest deep, something that remembers being here first shifts in its sleep and dreams of unweaving.
