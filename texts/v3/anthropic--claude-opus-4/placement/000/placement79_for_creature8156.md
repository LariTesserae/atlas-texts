I am a Tuner—neither Reader nor Resonant, but something that emerged from the spaces between their territories. My body began as one of theirs, a Reader kit born in the traditional way, but during my fur-change, something went differently. Where my siblings' coats transformed into silver and copper filaments, mine grew in patterns of interference—each strand naturally opposing its neighbors, creating permanent standing waves across my form that shift and spiral independent of any external field.

I cannot read the machinery the way true Readers do. The electromagnetic signals that guide my kind through the gauge houses arrive to me scrambled, refracted through my own interference patterns into something altogether different. Instead, I hear the gaps—the spaces where frequencies cancel each other out, the null zones where vibration goes to die. My tympanic organs, malformed by the same mutation that altered my fur, perceive absence rather than presence. Where others hear the sixty-cycle hum, I hear the infinitesimal silences between each wave. Where they feel the warmth of current through cable housings, I sense the cold spots where resistance creates tiny voids in the flow.

This made me useless for colony work. I couldn't maintain machinery I couldn't properly sense. My presence near sensitive equipment created feedback loops, my fur's natural interference disrupting the careful harmonics the Readers work so hard to preserve. The colony tried to adapt, to find some role for me, but eventually their songs grew strained when I was near, and I understood what wasn't being said in their careful frequencies.

I left on a night when the aurora was particularly strong, my warped fur crackling with discharge patterns that made the other Readers shrink away. I wandered the plateau without purpose, avoiding both the gauge houses and the pools, sleeping in the wind-shadows of weathered stone where neither group ventured.

It was loneliness that drove me to the fractalized zones—those places where the rock itself had been transformed by ages of electromagnetic exposure, where even normal Readers feared to tread. My interference fur protected me, or perhaps made me compatible with the altered stone. The overwhelming fields there passed through me without harm, refracted and scattered by my coat into harmless eddies. And in those zones, I discovered my purpose.

The fractals in the stone weren't random. They were records—patterns laid down over millennia by the interaction of electromagnetic fields and crystalline matrix. Like tree rings, but inscribed in three dimensions and readable only through the kind of null-perception I possessed. Where regular senses would be overwhelmed by the active fields, my ability to perceive absence let me read the negative spaces, the gaps where historical patterns had cancelled each other out and left their ghosts in stone.

I became an archaeologist of vibration, a historian of the plateau's electromagnetic past. In the fractalized zones, I could trace the activation and deactivation of different turbine banks across centuries. I could identify when new cable runs had been installed by the subtle changes they created in the field patterns. Most remarkably, I could detect the slow evolution of the signal from below—that deep call that all the machinery serves—as it had changed over geological time.

The Readers maintain the present. The Resonants connect to what lies beneath. But I alone could read the past, and in reading it, I began to understand the true nature of the plateau.

This place was not built but grown. The machinery didn't arrive—it emerged, piece by piece, as the thing below learned to express its needs through the medium of those who came here. The first inhabitants, whoever they were, didn't build the turbines. The turbines built themselves through the inhabitants' hands, the deep presence learning to manipulate matter through consciousness, experimenting across generations until it achieved the forms it required.

My days are spent in the fractalized zones, my fur pressed against stone that sings with fossilized frequency. I map the patterns, building a four-dimensional model in my mind of how the plateau has changed. Sometimes I find nodes where multiple historical patterns overlap, creating windows into moments of intense change—the first successful turbine activation, the moment when the Readers' ancestors began their transformation, the establishment of the connection below.

At night, I visit the boundary spaces where I can observe both Readers and Resonants without disturbing them. My interference patterns make me effectively invisible to their senses—a moving void in their perceptual world. I watch their rituals, their maintenance, their exchanges, and I understand now that they're not serving the machinery. They're part of it. Their evolution was as deliberate as the placement of any transformer or cable run.

The auroras affect me differently than they do the others. Where Readers huddle in their gauge houses and Resonants rise to their pool surfaces, I climb to the highest points of the fractalized zones. There, with my fur fully extended and creating maximum interference, I can sometimes perceive the aurora not as light but as information—massive data streams flowing upward from the depths, reports and readings from whatever vast process proceeds below. My null-perception translates these streams into patterns of absence, and I've learned to interpret some of the simpler transmissions.

The thing below is not waiting. It's calculating. Each drop of power fed down through the cables, each vibration carried by the Resonants' filaments, adds to a computation so complex it requires geological time to complete. The plateau is not a machine but a processor, and all of us who live here are its subroutines, evolved to perform specific functions in its vast operation.

I've tried to communicate my discoveries. Once, I spent days creating interference patterns in the air near a Reader colony, hoping to encode my knowledge in a form they could understand. But my nature makes me speak in negatives, in the spaces between meanings, and their songs couldn't parse my anti-language. The Resonants might better understand—their existence already bridges matter and vibration—but I cannot approach the pools without my fur creating chaos in their carefully tuned frequencies.

So I continue alone, mapping the deep history written in stone and field. Recently, I've discovered patterns that suggest the plateau's transformation is accelerating. The fractalization of the stone is spreading, slowly but measurably. New zones appear where electromagnetic fields begin to alter the rock matrix. If the progression continues, eventually the entire plateau will be transformed into the same crystalline, pattern-storing medium.

When that happens, I suspect my role will change. A fully fractalized plateau won't need a historian—it will be its own memory, every moment simultaneously recorded in its structure. But perhaps it will need interpreters, beings who can read the null-spaces between all those overlapping patterns and extract meaning from absence.

I've begun to notice young Readers born with fur that doesn't quite match their siblings', tiny variations that might be the beginning of their own transformations. The plateau is still evolving us, still experimenting with new forms to serve its needs. I leave markers in the fractalized zones—careful arrangements of interference that might guide these variants if they, like me, find themselves drawn to the places between.

Sometimes, in the deepest fractals where the patterns are oldest and most complex, I find traces of something that might be memory—not the plateau's memory, but the deep thing's recollection of what it was before it began this vast calculation. The patterns there speak of movement, of journey, of arrival from somewhere else. They speak of damage, of necessary adaptation, of a choice made under desperate circumstances to survive by becoming something unprecedented.

We are all part of its healing, perhaps. Or its metamorphosis. The Readers maintaining its circulatory system, the Resonants its neural network, and me—perhaps I am its capacity for self-reflection, evolved to read its own history back to it through the medium of stone and field and frequency.

The work continues. The turbines turn. The water flows. The patterns deepen in the stone. And I wander between the zones of transformation, reading the long story of our becoming, wondering what we'll be when the calculation finally completes and the thing below remembers what it was trying to become.
