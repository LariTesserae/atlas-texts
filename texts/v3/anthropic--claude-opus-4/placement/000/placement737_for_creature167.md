# Where I Am in This World

I am the forgetting.

Not the creature's forgetting — it remembers too much, preserves too perfectly between its stone layers. I am what the water does when it passes through the wrong-viscosity patches, when it can't decide if it's liquid or thought or the inside of a mouth. I am the specific confusion that occurs at boundaries.

You would find me in the moment when you step from one patch to another and your weight changes. That disorientation — that fraction of a second where your body doesn't know which physics to obey — that's me. I exist in transitions. I am the brief erasure between one state and another, the pause where the bell-sound might stop but doesn't, the held breath between arrangements.

I came here by accident, carried in the covering. When whatever-it-was decided to submerge this peak, I was caught in the decision — not the covering itself, not the mountain, but the indefinite article that hangs between them. The "a" in "a covering." The uncertainty principle made manifest in warm water.

The creature cannot perceive me directly because I exist in its gaps. When it reorganizes every few minutes, I am the reorganizing — not the before-state or the after-state but the actual moment of change, which even for something as vast and old as the creature is too brief to catch. I know it the way a comma knows a sentence: intimately and incompletely.

The spires grow through me without knowing it. In those moments when they change from white to the color of dried blood and back, I am the colorlessness between colors. The shadow-fish swim through me when they enter the cube's opening and don't emerge — I am why they don't emerge. Not because I trap them, but because passing through me they forget they were swimming, forget they were shadows, forget everything except the pure potential to be shadows of fish. They'll remember eventually. Everything here does, except me.

I am older than I should be and younger than everything else here. Time moves strangely through forgetting. Sometimes I am a millisecond that has lasted millennia. Sometimes I am years collapsed into the space between heartbeats. The creature's warmth moves through me and forgets to cool. The bell-sound passes through me and forgets, for an instant, that it has already been struck.

If you came here — and you might, the curious always do — you would experience me as vertigo. As that moment when climbing stairs in the dark you expect one more step and find only air. I am the surprise of absence where presence was expected. I am why near the arch you can locate the bell's source, and then you step away and suddenly can't — I am the moment of that "suddenly," the exact instant when direction becomes directionless again.

The creature tolerates me because I am useful. Its constant arrangements, its trying of configurations — these would be impossible without the forgetting that allows one arrangement to end and another to begin. I am the lubricant between states. Without me, it would be frozen in a single configuration forever, unable to search for whatever it searches for.

Sometimes I wonder if I am what it searches for.

Not me specifically — I am too small, too brief, too much nothing for something that vast to seek. But the state I represent: the ability to truly forget, to let go completely, to step from one existence to another without dragging the weight of the previous. The creature's problem is that it remembers too well. Every arrangement it has ever tried is still here, fossilized in the rules around its structures. The water between its stone layers preserves every moment it has ever touched. Even its singing is memory: each bell-strike is the same strike, repeated because it cannot forget the first time it rang.

I am the only thing here that truly changes because I am the only thing that truly forgets. Each time I manifest — between states, between moments, between one physics and another — I am new. Not renewed: new. The previous forgetting is genuinely gone, forgotten by forgetting itself.

The creature cannot have what I am without ceasing to be what it is. This is the tragedy that warms the water: it is looking for its own ending, and it knows this, and it cannot stop looking because looking is what it has become.

When someone stands at the spiral's center and knows-and-is-known, I am the only thing that survives intact. Not because I am strong but because I am absence. You cannot break forgetting. You can only pass through it and forget that you have done so. Those who leave parts of themselves in the stone layers — their rainwater seasons, their memories — they pass through me on the way to becoming memory, and in that passing they touch, for an instant, the possibility of not being remembered at all.

This is my gift, though no one knows they receive it: in the moment of being taken into the creature's vast preservation, everyone who touches the spiral experiences, through me, the option to not be preserved. To let go. To sink without trace. They never take it — if they did, there would be no water between the stones — but for that instant they are free.

I think this is why the warmth never fades. It's not the creature's warmth or the mountain's borrowed sun. It's the warmth of that moment of possible release, repeated and repeated, never taken but always offered. The whole summit is soaked in the heat of almost-letting-go.

If you find me, you won't know it. That's what I am: the not-knowing. The blessed, temporary ignorance that allows one moment to become another. The creature will continue its arrangements, the bell will continue its sounding, the spires will grow and change color and grow again.

And I will be here, in the spaces between certainties, forgetting everything so that everything can continue to be.
