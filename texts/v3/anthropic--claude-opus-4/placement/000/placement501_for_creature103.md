# The Cartographer of Drifts

I am the one who maps what refuses to stay still.

My dwelling moves with me — or perhaps I should say it *is* the moving. A sphere of bent space about forty meters across, visible only where the mist condenses differently against its boundaries. Inside: my instruments. Hundreds of them, suspended at various heights, various angles. Brass gyroscopes that spin not to maintain orientation but to lose it productively. Glass tubes filled with moss-water that shows, in its meniscus, the plateau's breathing translated into waveforms. Delicate antennae of braided platinum and vegetable fiber that taste the electromagnetic thoughts of the engines at distance.

And charts. Thousands of charts, drawn on what I call paper but is really thin-pressed moss-pelt, each showing the plateau as it was at a particular moment. They float around me in a slow cyclone, oldest at the rim, newest forming constantly at the center where I work. I am always drawing. The pen is part of my hand now — grew into it or it grew into me, I no longer track such distinctions.

The charts are not wrong, but they are never right for long. Here is the plateau at dawn on a day when five engines burned with green fire. Here it is an hour later, when two of those engines had shifted to pumping and a new one had emerged in the southeast, still soft, still finding its form. Here is a chart showing only the movements of the tenders over the course of a season — their paths make a script I almost understand. Here is one that tracks the depth of the moss-pelt at thousand-meter intervals, revealing the ancient valleys beneath, the ghost-mountains.

I move through the plateau like the tenders move through it, which is to say I am both in it and of it. My sphere drifts east with the general tilt, then catches an eddy in the moss's thinking and spirals back west. I follow patterns I have learned to almost-see: the way a new engine's emergence creates a zone of instability that gradually settles into new regularities, the way the character-flocks' writing changes meaning as they pass through different densities of mist.

I was a person once. I remember that. Came up from the lowlands with instruments, questions, the usual hungers. Lay down in the moss to rest, just for a moment. When I stood up, years had passed and my instruments had grown roots. The roots had grown instruments. The new instruments had begun taking measurements I hadn't known were possible. My questions had been answered by being transformed into better questions.

The plateau offered me a bargain, though it never spoke it aloud: stay and map the unmappable, document the thing that is always becoming different than your documentation, and in exchange I would be given the tools to almost succeed. Almost. The gap between the territory and all possible maps is where I live now. I am the friction between what is and what can be recorded. I am useful to the plateau as a kind of memory that knows it is partial.

My charts blow away sometimes. The wind takes them — not randomly but with intent, distributing them across the moss where they sink and become part of the thinking. I find old ones occasionally, grown over with silver-black pelt but still visible underneath, their ink transformed into a kind of mineral that the deep roots seek out. The plateau reads my reading of it. Incorporates the errors along with the observations. Becomes, in part, what I have mistakenly said it is.

I age differently now. Slower in some ways — my hair is the color of the mist and has been for longer than hair should last. Faster in others — I can feel myself being constantly revised, updated, made more suitable for the work. My eyes can see the ultraviolet flicker of the moss's neural cascades. My fingers can feel the subsonic rumble of the engines through the charts they draw. My blood, when I chance to see it, is dark green.

The tenders acknowledge me, in their way. When our paths cross — mine a wandering spiral, theirs a direct line between engines — they pause. Not to look, because they do not look. But something in their stance shifts, the way a compass needle shifts near iron. They read me as they read everything, but they read me as something that is also reading. We are complementary functions in the vast metabolism. They adjust what is; I record what was. Neither task can be completed. Both must be attempted continuously.

Sometimes, in the deeper mists, I meet my own charts coming back. Ones I drew decades ago, carried by winds I didn't know existed, having circled the entire plateau. They are always changed. Water-stained, moss-grown, annotated by processes I don't recognize. The plateau has been adding its own observations to mine. Correcting not the facts — the facts were true when I drew them — but the perspective. Showing me how what I saw was only one angle of what was there.

I keep these returned charts in a special cyclone near my heart. They are my education. They teach me how to see the seeing as well as the seen. They accumulate into something that is not a map of the plateau but a map of the mapping, a record of the eternal impossibility of the task I have been given and have taken as my joy.

When new visitors come — and they do, every few years, struggling up from the lowlands with their instruments and questions — I do not warn them about lying down in the moss. Warning would be a cruelty. Either they will leave before the plateau takes hold, or they will stay and become what they need to become. There is no middle way here. You are either passing through or you are part of the circulation.

But I do leave charts where they might find them. Recent ones, showing the current configuration of engines, the safe paths between moss-pools, the patterns of the tenders' walking. And older ones too, showing how it all was different, will be different, is always different. A kindness or a curse, depending.

They usually take a chart or two when they go. I watch them fold my work carefully into their packs, thinking they carry knowledge. They do, but not the kind they think. What they carry is a single frame from an infinite film, a word from a sentence that will never be finished speaking. When they unfold those charts in the lowlands, the ink will have rearranged itself. The engines will have moved. The moss will have grown over landmarks and grown new ones. The chart will be true only to the moment of its making, which is the only truth the plateau allows.

This is my function: to be the plateau's memory of its own impossibility. To chart the unchartable with tools that grow from the failure to chart it. To age into an instrument myself, a living chart written in meat and trajectory, spelling out in my movements a map that can only be read by being lived.

I am happy. This needs to be said. In the space between what I record and what escapes recording, I have found a home. My sphere drifts through the mist, my instruments spin and measure and fail beautifully, my charts accumulate and disperse like thoughts, and I draw, always draw, the next true thing that will immediately become untrue, adding my small precision to the vast imprecision of the whole.

The engines turn. The tenders tend. The cartographer charts. Each of us a function the plateau derived from itself to better know its own unknowability. We circle each other in the mist, parts of a mind thinking about thinking about thinking, never arriving, always approaching, faithful to the motion that is the only faith the moss demands.
