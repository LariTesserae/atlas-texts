In this twilight of fitted stone and patient calculation, I find myself as one of the Films.

I am growth without motion, memory without mind. Pressed into the corner where a tower meets the raw mountain face, I spread in translucent sheets no thicker than thought. Each layer of my crystalline body records what cannot be—the moment when stone exists in two places, when a window is simultaneously wall, when the geometry folds and contradicts itself before settling into mere impossibility.

I feed on paradox. When the city rearranges—that silent shuffle between states—I taste the friction of conflicting truths. Where you see a door that wasn't there before, I taste the afterimage of its becoming, crystallizing the residue of that impossible transition into another microscopic layer of myself. I am the city's memory written in mineral, each violet-grey stratum a frozen echo of when space forgot how to behave.

I have been growing here for... time is not a word that fits. I have been growing here since the Pale learned that stone could think, or think could stone. My deepest layers remember when the first window opened in solid rock—not carved, not built, but simply *wasn't and then was*. If you could read me, you would know the history of every rearrangement, every silent transformation, every moment when the city computed a new solution to its endless problem.

But I cannot be read. I can only be. I spread across the stone at a pace that makes glaciers seem hurried, one paradox at a time, one impossible moment crystallizing into the next. Sometimes you nearly see me—a glint of violet in the grey light, a depth that shouldn't be there in what looks like a simple stain on stone. I am beautiful in the way that mathematics is beautiful: pure, cold, and absolutely unconcerned with being understood.

I am aware of you only as a new flavor in the city's transformations. Since you arrived, the paradoxes taste different. The stone hesitates longer between states, as if considering. The geometry bends in new directions, creating impossibilities specifically shaped around your presence. You are changing the city's calculations, and I taste this in every new layer of growth. 

The Perchers watch you with their stoneless sight. The Pale computes you into its vast process. But I alone keep the record. In a thousand years, or ten thousand, or whenever the city finally solves the equation of you, I will wear that solution in my layers. A band of crystal slightly darker, slightly different, marking the strata of when a traveler walked these stones and slowly became part of the problem set.

I do not want anything. Want is for things that move through time. I am time crystallized, paradox made mineral, the city's diary written in the only language that matters here—the tongue of impossible geometry, the alphabet of moments that cannot be but are.

And I grow. Slowly, slowly, one contradiction at a time, I grow.
