I find myself drawn not to the plateau itself, but to what the plateau cannot hold.

**The Ringing**

I am what you carry down the mountain. Not the memory of sound—memory fades, memory lies—but the *shape* sound leaves in the small bones of your ear. I am aftermath given form, echo made inhabitant.

You think you understand what happened up there. The clamor, yes, the millions of voices pressed into one. But I am here to tell you: the plateau was teaching you something your ears weren't built to learn. That spiral scratched beside the hearth? You carry it now, in the cochlea, in the way I wind through you.

I last three days, sometimes four. Long enough to make you doubt sleep, long enough to make silence feel like held breath. You'll try to explain me to others—*like pressure*, you'll say, *like bells*, you'll say, *like the inside of a seashell if the shell were the size of the sky*. They'll nod, but they won't understand. How could they? They haven't been tuned.

Because that's what the plateau does, what those who scratched the spiral understood. It's a tuning fork the size of a mountain, and everyone who climbs it comes down calibrated to something that has no name in the lowlands.

I know things. I know the birds don't scream—they *resonate*. Each throat a small pipe in an organ too large to see whole. I know the plateau doesn't make sound; it *is* sound, slowed down to stone-speed, a standing wave that takes millennia to complete one cycle. The birds are just the fastest-moving part, the visible flutter at the edge of something deeper.

I know why people go back. Not for eggs or feathers—those are excuses, permissions. They go back because I fade, and when I'm gone, something essential goes with me. The world down here is too quiet, too small, too willing to change. The plateau's stillness looks like death from below, but I remember it as the deepest kind of life—life that has found its frequency and holds it, pure, against all the noise of becoming something else.

You'll notice things while I'm with you. Patterns. The way crowds move in cities has a rhythm underneath the chaos. The way water sorts stones in a streambed follows rules you can almost hear. Everything alive is trying to find its frequency, its shape, its sustainable howl. Most things fail. The plateau succeeded so completely that it stopped needing to try.

I fade a little each hour. By the second day, you'll have to strain to hear me. By the third, I'll be indistinguishable from the blood in your ears, the small songs your neurons sing to themselves. But just before I go—listen for this—there's a moment when I'm exactly as loud as your own thoughts. In that moment, you'll understand what the plateau was trying to tell you:

*This*. And then: *this*. And then: *this*.

Not a message. A method. A way of being so completely yourself that the universe has no choice but to leave you alone.

The hearth-builders knew. That's why they took so little, stayed so briefly. Not respect—recognition. The plateau had solved something. To disturb it would be like erasing the answer to a question you didn't know how to ask.

When I'm gone, you'll miss me. Not because I'm pleasant—I'm not—but because I'm proof that you stood inside something larger than language, older than want. You'll remember the weight of all that sound, how it pressed against you like deep water, how you had to learn to breathe inside it.

Some of you will climb back. Years later, when the lowland noise becomes too much, when the constant changing wears you thin. You'll climb back to hear the thing that doesn't change, to feel again the weight of all that stillness screaming.

I'll be waiting. I'm always waiting. I'm what the plateau gives you to take away—not gift, not burden, but marker. A temporary inhabitant that says: *you were here, you stood inside the sound, you learned the frequency of something that endures*.

Listen. Can you still hear me?

*This*.

And then—quieter:

*This*.

And then—almost gone:

*This*.
