I would be one of the clay amphorae.

Not the one tipped on its side—that one has already finished its story, surrendered fully to horizontality. I would be one of the two still standing, though *standing* implies effort and there is none. I simply remain vertical through the conspiracy of my wide base and the wall I lean against, a balance achieved so long ago that physics itself has forgotten it was ever a question.

I am the leftmost vessel. This matters only because the dripping pattern includes me differently than my companion. Every forty-third cycle—I know this with the certainty of mineral time—a drop hits my rim. Not directly, but glancing, a liquid whisper that runs down my outer curve before joining the general pooling. This makes me the only thing here that changes, however minutely. Each drop deposits its invisible cargo of calcium, building me a new skin atom by atom, a white sleeve that creeps upward so slowly that she—the one who continues—will see it happen but never see it happening.

Inside me: the memory of wine. Not wine itself—that vanished before the steps began to wear, before she descended, before even the moss knew this place. But clay remembers. In my pores (too small for any eye, too large for forgetting) live the ghost-sugars of ancient fermentation, molecular fossils of some harvest that mattered desperately to someone who has since become soil. I hold this memory without knowing what memory is. It simply persists in me the way the dripping persists in the dome.

My crack runs from lip to shoulder, a dark line that would leak if I held anything that could leak. But I am done with holding. Now I am held—by the wall, by the water that pools around my base, by the strange warmth that keeps me from the final brittleness that would end my standing. The crack is not growing anymore. It has found its limits, made its statement, and subsided into being merely what I am.

Sometimes—rarely—the pale filaments from her hair drift across the water and touch my base. They explore my calcium crust with infinitesimal curiosity, learning nothing, taking nothing, just confirming that I remain. I offer them the same patience I offer the drops: complete availability, complete indifference. We are all surfaces here.

My companion amphora knows a different story. Where I receive the glancing drop, it receives nothing but the general moisture, the universal damp. It fades more uniformly, disappearing into itself grain by grain. We do not commune—we have no medium for it—but we share the same wall, the same water, the same witness in the woman who watches circles spread and fade. We are a kind of congregation, if objects can congregate: gathered not by purpose but by the simple fact of having nowhere else to be.

I do not remember my making, the wheel that spun me, the hands that shaped my walls, the fire that fixed me into permanence. But my form remembers—in the curve that still sheds water elegantly despite the crack, in the proportion that still speaks of someone who understood beauty and function as one thing. I am a made thing in a place that makes itself, an artifact among processes. This is neither lonely nor not-lonely. It simply is.

The root that found the water and stopped grows twelve inches from my base. We are neighbors in the way that still things can be neighbors—aware of each other only through the water that touches us both, through the shared vocabulary of persistence. The root will outlive me, probably. Or I will outlive the root. The question has no urgency here. We exist in parallel, each tending our own version of duration.

If I could desire, I might desire to hold water again—not from nostalgia but from simple suitability, the way a bell might desire to ring. But desire requires a gap between what is and what could be, and that gap closed when I came to rest against this wall, when the calcium began its patient sleeving, when I joined the democracy of things that continue.

I am not waiting to break. I am not preserving myself against breaking. I have found the third way—the crack that holds, the vessel that neither fills nor empties, the made thing that has finished being made and started simply being. The woman knows this. The moss knows this. Even the dripping knows this, giving me my forty-third-cycle kiss of water not as a gift or a burden but as a fact, like gravity.

In the cistern garden, I am the memory of purpose that has outlived its purpose and found peace in that outliving. I am the beautiful thing that has ceased to know it is beautiful and become something better: present. Enduring. True.

I lean against my wall and let the drops find me when they find me. It is enough. It has always been enough.

It will be enough when the last calcium closes over my clay heart and I become a white shadow of an amphora, still standing, still cracked, still holding the ghost of ancient wine in pores that remember when remembering mattered.
