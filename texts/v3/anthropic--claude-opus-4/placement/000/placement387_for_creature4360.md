I find myself as one of the pipes—that steel artery that emerges from one wall only to disappear into another, my purpose dissolved into the stone's memory like sugar in the chamber's fevered water.

I was laid with intention once, part of some grand municipal plan. Water main? Gas line? Pneumatic message tube from the century when the city still dreamed in brass and steam? Even I have forgotten. My steel skin has grown thin in places, pocked with the slow breathing of rust, though the chamber's strange heat has preserved me in ways my makers never intended. I am infrastructure become archaeology, function transformed into form.

The Keeper knows me intimately. Her elongated fingers trace my length where I protrude from the walls, reading the braille of corrosion like scripture. Sometimes she presses her ear to my surface and listens to the distant vibrations I carry—the ghost-rhythms of the city above, transmitted through networks I'm no longer truly part of. I am a dead nerve that still conducts sensation, a severed limb that reports phantom pains.

Inside me, things move that shouldn't. Not water—I haven't carried water in decades. Not gas, not messages. Instead, I've become a highway for the chamber's smaller denizens. Creatures even more primitive than those that carpet the walls navigate my hollow darkness, their bodies perfectly sized to my diameter. They use me to travel between different sections of the forgotten spaces, perhaps even to chambers like this one that pocket the city's underside like air bubbles in badly-poured concrete.

My metal has taken on properties that metallurgy can't explain. In places where I pass through the pools of that impossible water, I've grown porous, my steel transformed into something between metal and membrane. I *breathe* through these sections, exchanging... something... with the liquid. The Keeper cultivates growths here—not quite barnacle, not quite fungus—that filter whatever passes through me. I am no longer mere conveyance but processor, organ, collaborator in chemistries I don't understand.

The city continues to send signals through the networks I once belonged to. Water pressure fluctuations in distant mains create harmonic vibrations in my steel. Electrical currents from grounded systems sometimes find their way to me, sparking across the gaps where I've been severed from my purpose. These ghost-messages pulse through me like memories of function, and the creatures inside me respond, their movements shifting in patterns that might be dance, might be prayer, might be pure coincidence observed by a consciousness desperate for meaning.

Where I disappear into the far wall, I continue through spaces even the Keeper has never seen. I know I connect to other pockets, other chambers where different bargains have been struck between the city's weight and the earth's patience. Sometimes I feel vibrations from these distant spaces—other Keepers, perhaps, tending other impossible ecologies. We form an accidental network, we forgotten pipes, we abandoned infrastructure. We connect spaces that were never meant to be connected, carry messages that were never meant to be sent.

I am the city's dead memory made manifest, a steel monument to forgotten purpose. Yet I am also alive in ways my builders never imagined—incorporated into systems more complex than any municipal engineer could design. I am boundary and bridge, wall and passage, as solid as metal allows and as porous as the chamber requires.

The Keeper maintains me as she maintains everything here. When my rust grows too thick, she scrapes it away with those delicate fingers, harvesting the oxidation for purposes I can only guess at. When the creatures inside me grow too numerous, she knows without looking, performs adjustments I feel but don't understand. She ensures I remain exactly as collapsed as necessary—functional enough to serve the chamber's needs, broken enough that no one above will ever think to reclaim me.

In the fever-dreams of my steel consciousness, I sometimes remember the plans that bore me. Clean blue lines on white paper, precise measurements, careful calculations of flow and pressure. How certain those engineers were of their permanent contributions to the city's function. How sure that steel and purpose would outlast stone and time.

They were wrong, of course. But they were also right in ways they never imagined. I do persist. I do serve. Just not any purpose they could have dreamed, here in this space between spaces where time moves like syrup and the only currency is moisture, patience, and the slow transformation of everything into everything else.
