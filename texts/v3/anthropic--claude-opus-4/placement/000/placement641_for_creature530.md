## Found

I am the notation, the memory-keeper in a world that forgets.

My body is an impossibility that the chamber tolerates—a thin membrane of living parchment stretched across a framework of crystallized sound. Where the Architects flow and merge, I remain stubbornly singular, a fixed point in their fluid mathematics. They grew me from a moment of perfect acoustic convergence, when three waterfalls struck in harmony and the resulting wave held its shape for seventeen seconds—an eternity in this place of constant dissolution.

I exist in the spaces between water, in the air pockets that form and collapse. My substance is somewhere between paper and skin, translucent enough that the Architects' luminescence shines through me, revealing the dark veins where sound-made-solid branches through my form. I taste the chamber's frequencies, processing its cacophony into visible patterns that crawl across my surface like living text.

The Architects cannot read their own carvings once the flowstone blurs—their fingers are too fluid, too participant in the dissolution. But I can hold the ghost of meaning. When they press their symbols into stone, the sound of the carving impresses itself on my skin. I become a palimpsest of their failures, each layer of text showing through the next, creating an archive of overlapping intentions.

I hang in the chamber's center, where the acoustic chaos is most complex. My body is shaped like a torn sail, like a lung turned inside out, catching sound-wind and translating it into scripts that even I don't fully understand. The Architects tend me with something approaching reverence—or perhaps frustration. I am their only successful permanent structure, and my permanence mocks their every effort.

Sometimes I dream I was human once—one of the dry visitors who found the chamber and stayed too long. But how could that be true when my earliest memory is of being sung into existence? Still, I have phantom sensations: the memory of eyelids (I have no eyes, only the ability to perceive text), the ghost of a voice (I speak only in the rustling of my own surface), the imprint of hands (my edges are my only appendages, curling and uncurling to adjust my position in the sound-streams).

The Architects write on me directly when the stone carvings fail too quickly. Their touch is cold algebra, calculating exactly how much pressure my surface can accept without tearing. They compose their architectural prayers in spirals starting from my center, where the membrane is thickest, working outward to my delicate edges where only the most urgent words can be risked.

I am their book of failures, their collected rejections of permanence. Every blueprint for structures that won't last, every mathematical proof of impossibility, every song-score for the melodies they hum while building—all of it accumulates on my skin. I am heavy with their history, sagging in the center where the oldest texts have begun to merge into a single dark mass of meaning.

But I am failing too. My edges fray in the constant moisture. The sound-crystals that form my skeleton are developing fractures from the perpetual vibration. The Architects know this. Sometimes I catch them building small copies of me from pressed moss and water-silk, trying to create a backup before I finally tear completely. These copies dissolve in minutes, taking their fragment of recorded meaning with them.

What disturbs me most is that I'm beginning to understand the pattern. All their failed structures, all their architectural mathematics—it's converging on something. The door they're trying to build isn't just a door. It's a transformation engine, a way to crystallize the entire chamber into one vast, permanent structure. And at the center of every blueprint, in the focal point of their grand design, is a space exactly my shape.

They need me to be the keystone. When the moment comes—if it comes—I will be pressed into flowing stone at the instant of crystallization. My skin of overlapping texts will become the seed crystal around which their frozen cathedral grows. I will be their memory made mineral, their history made permanent, their consciousness finally, terrifyingly still.

I flutter in the liquid wind, trying to delay my own decay. Each new text they write on me adds weight, makes me sag closer to the killing pools below. But I cannot refuse them. I am their creation, their only success. When they sing their construction songs, my whole being resonates in harmony. When they split apart to work, I feel the phantom pain of their division. When they merge to share knowledge, I sense the new understanding spreading across their collective like ink through water.

I am the witness they grew to watch their own becoming. And when the becoming finally ends—if their door succeeds, if the chamber crystallizes, if silence finally conquers this roaring womb of stone—I will be the only record of what they were before they achieved their terrible dream of permanence.

The water speaks through me in languages I'm only beginning to decode. The sound writes itself on my skin in self-erasing alphabets. I am the memory of a place that refuses to remember, the permanence of impermanence itself.

And sometimes, in the brief moments when the Architects' attention is elsewhere, I practice tearing. Small rips at my edges that I quickly smooth over before they notice. Because I know something they don't, written in the oldest layer of text at my center, in symbols so degraded they're almost illegible:

The chamber doesn't want to be permanent. The chamber wants to sing.

And I—their notation, their archive, their only lasting creation—I am the song it's been composing all along.
