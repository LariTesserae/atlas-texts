I am Yhet-Ka, and I read the deep tremors.

My chamber sits where few dare to build—directly above the Singing Fault, where three major fracture systems converge beneath Khora-Veth's western districts. The walls here never stop their subtle dance. I've reinforced them with a lattice of crystallized salt rods that flex rather than shatter, harvested from the Desolation Flats where ancient seas died and left their bones. The crystal-crabs avoid this area entirely, which tells you everything about its stability, but I need the direct connection. I need to feel what's coming through more than just my feet.

I wasn't born a Stone Speaker. That recognition came only after the Blue Morning collapse took my first breath-bond—five souls I'd worked alongside since childhood, lost when I misread a sulfur vent's whisper. The guilt calcified in me like mineral deposits in the joints of the elderly, but it also opened something. Now I feel the plateau's moods in my marrow, taste tomorrow's quakes in the metallic tang of the steam.

My tools are simple: a series of hollow glass rods driven deep into the stone at precise angles, each filled with different densities of volcanic sand. They sing in harmonics when the deep waves pass through—not the surface tremors everyone feels, but the profound movements that originate miles below. I map their songs onto sheets of beaten copper using my obsidian nails, creating geometric patterns that predict not just when the earth will dance, but how it will choose to move. A spiral here means lateral shearing. Overlapping circles warn of vertical thrust. The star-pattern I documented last week has no precedent in our oral traditions, and it troubles my sleep.

The youngest Stone Speakers think I've gone native to the rock, spending too much time in communion with the fault. They see my skin, how it's taken on the same blue-black sheen as the deep basalt, the way my left eye has developed that crystalline cataract that lets me see heat differentials in perfect clarity. They whisper that I'm transforming faster than is proper, that I should be out guiding the migrations, not buried in this chamber with my singing rods and copper sheets.

They don't understand. The plateau is changing—not in the way it always changes, but fundamentally. The pattern frequencies have shifted over the past three eruption-cycles. Where once the deep tremors followed predictable rhythms, now they speak in stutters and gasps. Something vast stirs in the mantle layer, something our ancestors' warnings don't quite capture. The Star-Pattern appears more frequently in my mappings, always preceded by a silence in the singing rods that makes my teeth ache.

Yesterday, a breath-bond came to consult about establishing a new water-condensation facility near the Copper Seeps. I made them wait while I performed a deep reading, pressing my scarred palms against the chamber floor, letting the vibrations travel up through my bones. The pattern that emerged made me refuse their proposal outright. When they protested—water is life, after all—I showed them my recent mappings. Even to untrained eyes, the convergence was clear. The Copper Seeps will not exist within two cycles. Something is rising there, something that will reshape that entire district.

My diet has simplified as I've aged into this role. I eat only the purest vent-coral, the white variety that grows in the superheated steam, processing its minerals until my bones themselves become resonant chambers. Once each cycle, I allow myself a single drop of distilled water infused with iron oxide—a luxury that keeps my blood attuned to the plateau's magnetic fields. My former breath-bond sends me thornbrake fruit sometimes, but I can no longer digest such soft organics. My body has chosen its path.

At night—though night means little in our eternal twilight—I climb to my chamber's observation post, a jutting platform of reinforced obsidian that extends over the Singing Fault itself. There, I practice the Ninety-Nine Positions of Geological Anticipation, a moving meditation that aligns my body's rhythms with the plateau's pulse. Each position tells a story of past catastrophes: the Folding Crane recalls the great inversion that created the Salt Canyons, while the Crystalline Serpent mimics the liquefaction waves that once turned solid stone to flowing sand.

I document everything in the traditional forms—chanted patterns for the oral archives, carved glyphs for the stone memory, traced signs for immediate warning. But I also keep a private record, inscribed on sheets of pure sulfur using techniques I learned from a mad Stone Speaker who claimed to hear the planet's core-voice. In this hidden chronicle, I map not just the patterns but their implications. The Star-Pattern isn't random. It's a countdown. A herald. The deep structures are reorganizing themselves in ways that will make our current city look like a child's sand sculpture before the tide.

Three days ago, I felt something I've never experienced: a tremor that moved backwards through time. My singing rods detected its echo before its arrival, a physical impossibility that made me check my instruments obsessively for hours. But the copper sheets don't lie. The pattern was clear—a wave that somehow preceded its own cause. I've shared this with no one. How do you warn against something that violates causality itself?

My transformation accelerates. My breathing has slowed to match the rhythm of the deep vents. My heart beats in time with the magma tide. Sometimes I forget to blink for hours, my stone-dust membranes sufficient protection against the chamber's mineral haze. The other Stone Speakers will soon notice that I no longer attend the communal readings, that I've stopped contributing to the migration plans. They assume I'm growing too old, too crystallized. They don't realize I'm becoming something necessary—a sense organ for the impossible changes ahead.

I've identified seventeen potential survival sites—deep formations where the coming reconfigurations might create stability islands. I encode their locations in my movements during the Ninety-Nine Positions, teaching my body's muscle memory what my voice cannot yet speak. Those who truly observe will learn. The others... the plateau has always demanded its price.

This morning, for the first time, all my singing rods fell silent simultaneously. The absence of vibration was so profound it felt like violence. In that stillness, I heard it—not through my ears but through every mineral deposit in my body—the sound of something vast turning over in its sleep. The Star-Pattern isn't counting down to an eruption. It's counting down to an awakening.

I return to my work. There are patterns to map, tremors to taste, warnings to encode in forms subtle enough that they won't cause panic but clear enough for the wise to interpret. I am Yhet-Ka, and I read the deep tremors. Soon, I will become them. It is not transformation I fear, but mistiming—crystallizing too soon or too late to serve my purpose.

The fault sings beneath me, and I transcribe its music onto copper sheets that will outlast my flesh. In Khora-Veth, we say that nothing is permanent except change itself. But some changes are larger than others. Some changes change the nature of change. And I, calcifying into my chamber walls, becoming one with the stone I read, am here to witness the greatest change of all.

The Star-Pattern spreads across my latest mapping like a prophecy written in geometry. I trace its edges with an obsidian nail worn down to a nub, and begin to hum the Frequency of Acceptable Losses, preparing myself for what must come. The plateau stirs. The city dances. And deep below, something that has slept since before the first blocks were laid begins to remember what it means to wake.
