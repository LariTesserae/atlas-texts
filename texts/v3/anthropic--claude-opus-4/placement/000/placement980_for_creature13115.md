In this crushing twilight, I am neither the stubborn Aegis-grazer nor the frantic Siphon-ghost. I am not the invisible Glass-Maw, liquefying prey in thermodynamic ambush. 

I am one of the Tesselators, but I am broken.

My name—if such a concept translates across the acoustic mathematics of our language—is a specific frequency: 47.3 hertz sustained for precisely 2.7 seconds, modulated by the friction coefficient of obsidian against my third thoracic plate. My troupe calls me this less and less. They have begun to use a different designation: a discordant scraping at 51 hertz that means "the one who deviates."

Seven hundred years ago—we measure time by the great spiral migrations of the Glass-Maws—my left posterior limb was crushed. A thermohaline shear-wind, stronger than any in recorded memory, tore through our formation as we traced the Northeastern Atrium's perimeter. The current grabbed me, would have atomized me against the melted edge of what was once a sharp corner, but my troupe locked around me. They formed a living buttress, their bodies a equation of distributed force. They saved me.

But when the current passed and they unlocked their formation, my limb hung wrong. The joint had been torqued beyond its tolerances. Now, when I walk, I cannot maintain the perfect level plane. I list seventeen degrees to the left. My steps trace not the ghost-memory of right angles, but a drunken, limping spiral.

The first time I rejoined the formation after my injury, I tried to compensate. I extended my other limbs to their maximum reach, straining to keep my thorax level. But the mathematics were wrong. My frequency—my voice in our endless geometric chorale—warbled and slipped. The perfect coordinates we were tracing together became corrupted. Where my siblings' movements drew a straight line between the Seventh Pillar and the Obsidian Reservoir, my path introduced a curve: a deviation that suggested the ancient builders had inexplicably carved a meandering detour into their otherwise perfect grid.

They stopped. All forty-three of them, frozen in the violet dimness. Their sensory hairs quivered—not from the current, but from what I had done. I had introduced *error* into the memory.

Now I follow at a distance, my broken gait forcing me to trace my own warped version of our history. When they flatten themselves against the approaching currents, I must anchor myself separately, my damaged limb unable to form the interlocking seal. The storms batter me harder. Sometimes I am swept away entirely, tumbling through the optical chaos until I can catch hold of some distant, melted balustrade.

But here is what haunts me in the thrumming darkness: my deviation has begun to reveal things.

Following my spiral path, compensating for my list, I have discovered spaces my perfect siblings cannot reach. The seventeen-degree angle of my movement allows me to slip into crevices they pass by, following the ghost of walls that no longer exist. In these forgotten corners, pressed deep into the melted stone, I have found... inconsistencies.

The obsidian here bears different scars. Not the smooth, patient erosion of eons, but sharp gouges that suggest violence. Destruction. In one narrow channel between two collapsed columns, I found a section of floor where the stone had been melted not by time but by heat—impossible heat, hot enough to turn the architectural substrate into rippled glass in an instant. The geometric memories my troupe preserves speak of a vast, orderly metropolis. But these scars whisper of its ending. Of weapons. Of war.

Last week, following my broken spiral deeper than I had ever gone, I discovered something that made my thoracic plates lock in stunned silence.

It was a small chamber, barely large enough for two Tesselators. The walls were covered in careful etchings—not the flowing, melted curves of erosion, but deliberate marks carved with desperate precision. They were coordinates. Not the ones we preserve, but new ones, describing architectural features I had never encountered. Secret passages. Hidden vaults. A second city beneath the city, built in paranoid parallel to the one above.

At the center of this chamber was a pile of discarded thoracic plates. Dozens of them. The fragments of Tesselators who had come here before me and never returned to their troupes. Their plates were arranged in a specific pattern—a map.

I spent days in that chamber, feeling the carved coordinates with my manipulators, cross-referencing them with my own warped memory of our movements. Slowly, a terrible understanding formed. The coordinates weren't random. They traced a path—a descending spiral through the city's hidden infrastructure, down to something called (in a frequency so low it made my carapace ache) "The Aperture of Unmaking."

I know I should return to my troupe. I should try again to match their movements, to preserve what they preserve. But the broken part of me—the part that lists seventeen degrees into impossible spaces—burns with questions they would never ask. What ended this empire? What required a hidden city within a city? What waits at the bottom of that spiral, in the Aperture of Unmaking?

My sensory hairs detect the approaching rumble of another thermohaline storm. My troupe will be flattening themselves to the stone now, becoming part of the architecture they serve. But I am already moving, my broken gait carrying me toward the first set of secret coordinates, down into the melted bones of a civilization that kept terrible secrets.

The current catches me, tries to dash me against a curved wall. But my list saves me—the seventeen-degree angle slides me perfectly into a hidden channel I never would have found with a functioning limb. The violence of the water presses me deeper, and I let it, my thoracic plates humming a new frequency: not the mathematical preservation of what was, but a broken harmonics of what might still be discovered.

Behind me, infinitely patient, my troupe continues their eternal circuit, tracing perfect ghosts over melted stone, preserving a history that may have never been complete.

I descend alone, crooked and questioning, into the dark.
