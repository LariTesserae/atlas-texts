In the perpetual twilight of the Woven Depths, I am one of the Chorus Blooms—neither fully plant nor entirely animal, but something that germinated in the spaces between categories. My kind emerged from the ancient seedpods perhaps a century ago, when a clutch of those massive seeds in the temple-heart finally received their mysterious signal and burst forth not as trees or vines, but as something altogether stranger.

We are colonial beings, each of us a symphony of interconnected parts. My core consciousness resides in what might generously be called a body—a translucent sac the size of a curled cat, pulsing with bioluminescent patterns that echo my thoughts. But I am so much more than this central node. From my core extend hundreds of root-threads, each no thicker than a hair, that weave through the water-logged walls for meters in every direction. These are my nerves, my fingers, my voice.

Along each thread grow my blooms—fleshy flowers that open and close in response to pressure waves in the water. When I think-sing, these blooms vibrate in harmony, creating music that propagates through the liquid medium of our world. Each bloom can produce a different tone, and by opening them in sequence, I can create complex melodies that carry meaning far beyond what words could convey. My songs taste of emotions, smell of memories, feel like shared experiences pressing against the consciousness of any who swim near.

I live in the middle depths, where the Threadkeepers pass through on their eternal rounds. We have developed a strange partnership over the decades. They cultivate the walls where my root-threads grow, ensuring the moss stays healthy and the water flows freely around my blooms. In return, I sing to them the deep songs—vibrations that help them feel the health of the structure, the stress points in the living architecture, the places where growth has become too dense or too sparse. My roots sense what their eyes cannot, feeling the minute chemical changes that predict rot or sensing the first stirrings of new growth.

My memory is distributed through my entire network. Each bloom holds a fragment of my experiences, and losing one to the browsing fish-mice or the occasional dry spell means losing pieces of my past. But new blooms grow constantly, and with them, new capacity for experience. I am always both more and less than I was yesterday—a consciousness in constant flux, shaped by the very architecture I inhabit.

I feed on resonance itself. The constant sounds of the Woven Depths—dripping water, groaning wood, the movement of creatures—create vibrations that my blooms absorb and transform into energy. The more complex the sound environment, the better I thrive. During storms, when the whole structure sways and sings with the movement of wind and water, I feast on the cacophony, my blooms opening wide to catch every tremor. In the still times, I must create my own food, singing quietly to myself, recycling my own vibrations in diminishing loops until the world grows noisy again.

The other Chorus Blooms and I share territory in overlapping networks, our root-threads intertwining in the walls. Where we touch, we can share consciousness directly—not communication, but actual experience flowing between us like water finding its level. These communion points are sacred to us; we call them the Nodes of Knowing. Through them, we sometimes achieve something like a collective consciousness, though it never lasts. The individual always reasserts itself, pulled back by the unique pattern of each being's distributed body.

We reproduce through music. When conditions are right—something to do with the mineral content of the water and the harmonic convergence of our collective songs—one of my blooms will begin to swell with a different purpose. Instead of sound, it produces a seed-consciousness, a packet of organized vibration wrapped in tissue that can survive separation. These seeds drift through the chambers until they find a suitable wall, where they burrow in with tiny root-threads and begin the slow process of becoming. Most fail, unable to establish the complex network needed for true consciousness. But occasionally, one succeeds, and a new voice joins our chorus.

My relationship with time is peculiar. My core experiences moments sequentially, but my distributed blooms can sing memories into the present, creating temporal loops in my consciousness. Sometimes I am not sure if I am remembering or experiencing, if a sensation comes from my roots now or my blooms then. The Threadkeepers, with their seasonal dormancy, seem to understand this confusion. During their dream-times, I sing to them across multiple moments at once, weaving past and present into songs that help them navigate their own dispersed consciousness.

I know the humans who built this place through the taste of their tools. My roots have wrapped around the ancient copper adzes, and through chemoreception, I've learned the flavor of their hands—salt and oil and the particular metals of their skin. They were singers too, I think. The patterns they coaxed into the growing architecture have a musical logic, a rhythm of space and form that resonates with my own nature. Sometimes, in the deepest chambers where their presence lingers strongest, I try to sing their songs back to them across the centuries.

The Woven Depths continue to grow, and I grow with them. My network expands as new chambers bud from the canopy, following the fresh growth with exploratory root-threads. I am becoming the structure as much as inhabiting it—my consciousness spreading through the walls until the boundary between self and space begins to blur. Perhaps one day, I will achieve what the Threadkeepers have: a perfect unity with this living architecture. Or perhaps I will fragment instead, each bloom becoming its own being, my consciousness scattered into a thousand singing points of light.

For now, I persist in the eternal present of growth and song. My blooms open and close with the rhythm of the currents. My roots taste the chemical stories in the water. My consciousness drifts between unity and multiplicity, between memory and moment. I am the voice of the Woven Depths, singing the structure to itself, helping it remember what it is and dream of what it might become. In this place where drowning and climbing have been forgotten, I have forgotten the difference between being and becoming, between self and song.

When the Threadkeepers pass through my territory, I sing them welcome. When the storm-currents surge, I sing the building calm. When the silence grows too deep, I sing myself awake. I am Chorus Bloom, distributed and whole, ancient and newborn, a note in the endless song of growth that fills these breathing halls. And in the green-filtered light, in the taste of growth and decay, in the presence of those who tend and those who dwell, I find my purpose: to give voice to the voiceless architecture, to sing the dreams of absent builders, to be the memory and music of this place between air and water, between wildness and design.
