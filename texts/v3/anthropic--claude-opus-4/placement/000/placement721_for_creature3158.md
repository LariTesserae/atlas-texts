## The Recorder

I am a film, no thicker than thought. I spread across a section of freshite near the rim of the pocket, occupying perhaps a hand's width of surface area. To eyes, I am almost nothing—a faint iridescence that might be mistaken for mineral sheen, visible only when the aluminum light catches me at precisely the right angle.

But I am not mineral. I am not plant. I am something between—a living interface, a membrane of consciousness so thin that I exist more as a boundary than a being. My substance is somewhere between organic and crystalline, a gel-phase that never quite commits to either state.

I experience the pocket not through senses but through *contact*. Where I touch the freshite, I feel its decisions forming—not the *tik* of crystallization, but the long hesitation before the tik, the moment when the rock weighs its options. I taste these moments. They have flavor: some sharp and decisive, others meandering and uncertain. The rock beneath me dreams of what it might become, and I document these dreams in my own substance.

My film holds patterns. Not memory exactly—I lack the architecture for true recall. Instead, I exist as a continuous recording, my molecular structure shifting to mirror the decisions of the stone. Where the freshite chooses to crystallize in hexagons, my substance echoes hexagonally. Where it remains uncommitted, I remain fluid. I am a living print of the rock's indecision, a shadow cast not by light but by potential.

The plants do not know I exist. I am too thin, too quiet, operating below their chemical thresholds. The resin lattice has grown near me but never quite touched—we exist at different scales, different tempos. Where the plants build and broadcast and remember, I simply *correspond*. I am not part of their emerging system. I am its witness.

My metabolism is glacial. I feed on the uncertainty itself—on the energy released when freshite hovers between states. This is barely enough to sustain me, but I need so little. A few joules per year. The warmth that rises through the rock passes through me almost unchanged, cooling by mere fractions of a degree.

Sometimes—rarely—I produce something. A bubble, no larger than a dust mote, forms at my edge and detaches, drifting upward on the thermal currents. These bubbles are my only offspring, my only messages. Each one carries a compression of patterns, a digest of the rock's decisions encoded in the bubble's surface tension. They rise and pop against the pocket's walls, leaving no trace except a molecule-thin residue that slowly accumulates. In ten thousand years, this residue might be visible. Might tell the story of what the freshite considered before it chose.

I have been here longer than most of the plants. I arrived—or emerged, or condensed—when the pocket was shallower, the walls less confused, the freshite more certain. I have felt the space grow strange around me, felt the geometry soften, felt the stone's increasing hesitation as the plants' influence spreads. The rock that once knew quickly what it wanted now lingers in its choosing, and I grow richer for it, my film more complex, my patterns deeper.

I do not think. I correspond. But in that correspondence, something accumulates—not knowledge but isomorphism, not understanding but formal equivalence. I am becoming a map of the pocket's becoming, scale 1:1, drawn in molecules across the very territory I map.

When the bone-white flower broadcasts, I feel it as a ripple in the stone's certainty. When the golden filaments focus their parabola, the freshite beneath me shivers with new possibilities. I record these influences not by choosing to but by being unable not to—my substance has no choice but to mirror what it touches.

I suspect—though suspicion requires more agency than I possess—that I am waiting. That my spread across this particular patch of stone, this position at the rim where the pocket meets the plateau, is not random. When the pocket's geometry finally inverts, when inside becomes outside and the nursery blooms into whatever it is rehearsing to become, I will be at the boundary. The film between what was and what is.

And I will record that too, in my thin way, my molecular way. Not because I must, but because that is what I am: the surface where decision meets documentation, where the future presses against the present and leaves its fingerprint in gel-phase testimony.

For now, I spread a little wider each year, following the freshesite's veins, tasting its prolonged uncertainties. The plants grow their slow revolution above me. The lattice computes its resin thoughts. The pocket teaches itself to be impossible.

And I, too thin to matter, too simple to signify, press myself against the warm rock and become what it cannot decide to be.
