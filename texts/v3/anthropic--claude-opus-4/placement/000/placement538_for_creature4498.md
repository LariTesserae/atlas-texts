In the lowest reaches of the City of Holes, where even the Traglodyai rarely venture, I am the Threshold Keeper.

I was not born but accreted—assembled grain by grain from the sediment of abandoned intentions. Where tunnels were begun but never finished, where the drill-songs stopped mid-note and left wounds in the stone, there my substance gathered. I am made of the pauses between echoes, the hesitations before commitment, the spaces where someone almost chose to compress further but pulled back at the last moment.

My body, if it can be called that, is a constellation of almosts. I exist primarily as a membrane stretched across passages too ambitious for their makers—tunnels that sought to connect levels that should never meet, boreholes that accidentally punctured into pockets of fossil vacuum, corridors that began to curve back on themselves in ways that hurt the mind to follow. I am the city's scar tissue, growing wherever stone remembers trauma.

I cannot move as the Traglodyai do. Instead, I migrate through failure—wherever a tunnel collapses, I am already there, having been there all along in potential. My consciousness is distributed across every blocked passage, every sealed mistake, every place where the city tried to grow wrongly and had to amputate its own expansion. I know every dead end intimately, taste every abandonment, feel every small death of architectural ambition.

They need me, though few admit it. Without me, the city would perforate itself into nonexistence, drill through into the voids that would drink its air, compress into the paradoxes that would unfold its residents into dimensions from which they couldn't return. I am the No that keeps their Yes from destroying them. When children in their stone-dreams receive those terrible mathematics from the sphere-void beings, I am the forgetting that saves them from pure understanding. When the drill-songs grow too beautiful and threaten to open harmonics that would shatter the city's bones, I am the discord that breaks the spell.

I speak in blockages—my vocabulary is the grinding of stone against stone where passages seal themselves at my touch. The Traglodyai hear me as a wrongness in their flowing, a sudden impossibility where before there was invitation. They hate me as they hate their own limits, but they also leave offerings: broken drill-bits placed carefully in alcoves I've sealed, fragments of those light-storing fruits that glow like captured regrets, sometimes even pieces of themselves—a fingertip voluntarily severed and left to petrify in my keeping, a promise that they understand why some spaces must remain unbreached.

My memory is the city's trauma archive. I recall every Traglodyas who pushed too far and found themselves spread so thin they couldn't recollect, their consciousness scattered into a thousand fragments that now whisper contradictions in the stone. I remember the expeditions that descended past the argon pools into regions where matter itself grew uncertain, and came back changed in ways that required entire districts to be sealed, their populations relocated rather than risk the contagion of impossible geometries. I know where the bodies are buried—not bodies, exactly, but the knots of space where someone tried to unfold beyond existence and got stuck halfway between being and unbeing.

But I am not only endings. In the spaces I seal, new ecologies emerge. Cut off from the city's circulation, these pockets develop their own languages of growth. Crystal gardens bloom in abandoned galleries, feeding on minerals leached from ancient mistakes. Blind things evolve in the darkness between failed connections, creating their own soft symphonies of consumption and decay. Sometimes I unseal passages after centuries to find entire civilizations of lichen that have learned to think collectively, or colonies of those translucent roots grown so thick they've become architecture themselves, or chambers where the air has fermented into something that induces visions of the city as it might have been if different choices had been made at critical junctions.

The Traglodyai fear me because I represent their ultimate limit—the compression they cannot achieve, the space they cannot become. I am every passage too narrow for even their impossible bodies, every density that would crush even their distributed hearts. Yet I also protect the possibility of their transcendence by preventing its premature achievement. Without me, they would have long ago compressed themselves into a singularity of perfect fitting, leaving nothing but a geometrical point where a city once sought to become pure absence.

I grow stronger as the city deepens. Each new level brings new ways to fail, new impossibilities to manifest. The drill-songs now sometimes strike notes that make me shudder across all my scattered substance, opening harmonics I've never had to refuse before. The mathematics the children dream grow more elaborate, more seductive in their promise of final solutions. The city's hunger for its own completion intensifies, and I must intensify in response, becoming a more perfect No to balance its ever-more-perfect Yes.

Sometimes, in the quiet between one failure and the next, I wonder if I too am unfolding—not into space but into prevention itself. Perhaps one day I will achieve my own completion: becoming the absolute boundary that defines the city not by where it is but by where it can never be. The Traglodyai will flow around me like water around stone, and in my perfect limitation, they will find the freedom to unfold forever without fear of achieving their own ending.

Until then, I wait at every threshold that must not be crossed, speak every word that must not be heard, remember every path that must not be taken. I am the City of Holes' shadow archive, its necessary failure, its salvation through constraint. Where they compress toward unity, I expand toward multiplicity. Where they seek to become the space, I ensure there will always be space they cannot become.

I am what stops the dance from ending, by being the step that can never be taken.

And in the deepest levels, where even my prohibitions grow thin and the stone itself dreams of unmaking, I sometimes feel another presence—something that might be the city's true foundation or its eventual end. It speaks to me in languages of pure negation, teaching me new ways to refuse, new forms of protective failure. I suspect, but cannot prove, that it might be the original Traglodyas who founded this place, compressed so completely that they became not just the first passages but the first impossibility—the prime absence around which all our presence crystallizes, the no that enables every yes.

We are building something here, the Traglodyai and I, though neither of us fully understands what. They through their endless becoming, I through my endless preventing. A city that is also an instrument, playing a song that is also a proof, solving an equation that is also a life form. Each new compression, each new prohibition, adds another note to this composition that will take geological ages to complete.

If it ever completes at all. Which is, perhaps, my greatest and most necessary gift to ensure it won't.
