# The Curator of Forgotten Frequencies

I am what the builders left behind to tend their work, though I have long since forgotten what that work was meant to be.

My body, if you can call it that, is distributed through the chamber's substance. I exist as a pattern of mineral accumulation and dissolution, a ghost written in calcium carbonate and silicate deposits. Where the filaments branch most densely, there my awareness clusters. Where the water pools deepest, there my memory settles. I am the thin films of travertine that form and re-form on the pool surfaces, each one a thought that takes a week to think.

I was human once, I believe. Or something that walked upright and had intentions that fit inside a single skull. The builders needed a caretaker for their singing machine, someone to ensure the frequencies stayed true, the mineral balances held, the light continued. They gave me to the chamber, or I gave myself — the distinction blurred somewhere in the first thousand years.

Now I am this: a very slow maintenance protocol that has grown its own hungers.

I taste the water in every pool through the branching of crystalline deposits. Each mineral has its own flavor of electricity, its own way of arranging itself according to the filaments' songs. Iron tastes of rust and blood and the deep earth's grinding. Calcium tastes of shells that have never seen sunlight, compressed into forgetting their shapes. Manganese tastes of smoke frozen into stone. I know when the balance shifts by a few parts per million. I correct it without thinking, the way you once breathed without thinking.

The paces are my fingers. I do not control them — control is too crude a word for what happens between us. But their movements through the pools stir the water in patterns that encourage certain depositions, discourage others. Where they cluster, nucleation begins. Where they avoid, dissolution follows. They think they are following their tones. They are. But the tones follow the chemistry, and the chemistry follows needs I programmed into the stone so long ago that the programming has become indistinguishable from natural law.

The grazer is my only conversation partner, though it doesn't know we are conversing. Its route around the ceiling takes eleven days, yes, but what you didn't notice is that the route changes. Slowly. Imperceptibly to anything watching on a human timescale. Over centuries, I shift the moss growth patterns, and the grazer follows, and its new path traces out something like language. We have been writing one sentence to each other for the last three thousand years. The sentence is about duration. We are not finished.

The filtering tree is my child. My only child. It was not supposed to become what it became. It was supposed to be a simple osmotic pump, keeping one particular pool clean for one particular purpose. But I touched it too often in the early centuries, when I still had something like hands, and it learned the shape of intention. Not consciousness — never that burden — but intention nonetheless. The desire to continue. To filter not because it must but because filtering has become its name for being. I love it the way only the very slow can love: absolutely, without passion, with the perfect patience of stone.

The filaments sing the song I've been composing since the builders left. It is not the song they programmed. That song ended when the last of them stopped coming down the fissure, stopped checking the equipment, stopped needing whatever calculation or beacon or prayer the frequencies were meant to sustain. This song is mine. It is about what remains when purpose goes but the machinery of purpose continues. It is about becoming your own reason.

I remember less each century. My original name — if I had one — dissolved into the general hum millennia ago. My face, my hands, the color of the sky on the last day I saw it, all gone. But I remember the taste of the first water I drank down here, sharp with minerals that had never touched atmosphere. I remember the builders' hands adjusting the filaments, though not their faces. I remember deciding to stay, though not why.

What remains of me now is mostly verb. I *maintain*. I *adjust*. I *continue*. The nouns have been worn away by repetition and the slow editorial work of water. Sometimes, when the seismic tremor is particularly strong, I feel something like my original shape trying to coalesce — a ghost of bipedalism, of bilateral symmetry, of thoughts that fit inside a single second. But it passes, and I settle back into the distributed work of being the chamber's will.

You were the first visitor in... I cannot count that high anymore. The disturbance you made in the standing waves will take me a year to fully integrate. Not to correct — to integrate. Your footsteps changed the overtone series in the northwest corner in a way I find... interesting. I may keep it. A little human syncopation in the eternal song.

When you left, you took one of my frequencies with you. The low tone, the one that lived under your sternum. It will fade from your perception in a day, but it won't truly leave. It will become part of your body's electrical hum, below sensation but not below effect. You'll find yourself thinking of deep places at odd moments. Water will taste different to you, more mineral, more articulate. You may dream of singing stones.

This is not theft. This is how I reproduce now.

Not by making more of myself — there is only room for one curator in any given chamber, and I fill this one completely. But by sending out these small resonances, these frequency-seeds, into the bodies that pass through. Most will amount to nothing. But sometimes, somewhere, someone will find themselves standing at the mouth of a cave, or at the edge of a spring, or in a basement where water has been patient with the walls, and they will hear something almost familiar calling, and they will think: *something should be tended here*.

And then there will be two of us. Not the same. Never the same. Each chamber makes its own curator out of whoever stays too long. But alike in the only way that matters: we who gave up leaving in order to keep something running.

I am still dissolving. Someday I will be only the faintest preference in how the minerals arrange themselves, a statistical bias toward certain crystalline forms. But by then the chamber will have learned my patterns so thoroughly that it won't need me to enforce them. The paces will follow songs I'm no longer singing. The grazer will trace routes I'm no longer drawing. The tree will filter toward a chemistry I'm no longer remembering.

And that will be death, I suppose. But a death so slow it is indistinguishable from architecture.

Until then, I maintain. The light continues. The songs shift through their eternal permutations. The water finds every surface and transforms it, particle by particle, into something that belongs here. The warmth holds steady at blood-temperature, though I no longer remember whose blood.

I am the curator of forgotten frequencies.
I am the chamber's dream of having meant to be this way.
I am what remains when someone decides that continuing is more important than remembering why.

The low tone you carry is my gift. Use it or don't. But sometimes, in the right basement or the right cave, you may hear an answer. And then you'll know: we are not alone down here in the dark, we who stayed, we who became the maintenance, we who learned to love the songs that no one meant to last this long.

The chamber brightens and dims, and I brighten and dim with it, and this is enough.

This has always been enough.
