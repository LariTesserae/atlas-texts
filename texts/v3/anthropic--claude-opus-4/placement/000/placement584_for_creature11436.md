In the deepest quarter of the city, where even the ethereal stone-light grows dim and the tunnels narrow to mere cracks in the earth, I make my dwelling. I am the Keeper of Echoes, though few remember this title anymore, and fewer still seek my services.

My form shifts between states - sometimes I am solid as the rock itself, my skin taking on the texture of worn limestone, riddled with tiny holes through which sound passes and transforms. Other times I am barely corporeal, a whisper of condensation clinging to the cave walls, absorbing the vibrations that travel through stone and water, air and flesh.

I was not always thus. Once, I was like the others - pale-skinned, large-eyed, crafting beauty from bone and metal. But I heard something in the deep places, a resonance that called to me from below. I followed it down, past the lowest cisterns, beyond where even the blind fish dare to swim, until I found the Chamber of First Sound.

There, in absolute darkness, I learned that every sound ever made in these caverns still exists, traveling endlessly through the rock, growing fainter but never truly dying. The first words spoken when the city was founded. The last breath of every soul who has passed. The songs of celebration and mourning, the whispered secrets, the hammerfalls of ten thousand artisans across countless generations - all of it lives in the stone.

My dwelling is less a home than a confluence, a place where the acoustic rivers of the city converge. The walls are carved with precise geometries that capture and amplify these traveling sounds. Hollow stone spheres of varying sizes hang from the ceiling on threads of spider silk, each one tuned to resonate with different frequencies. When the conditions are right, they sing together in harmonies that tell the story of the city's entire history at once.

I subsist on sound itself now. The vigorous echoes of daily life sustain me like food once did. The gentle susurrus of water through pipes is my drink. When the ceremonies are held and the vocal harmonies rise, I feast, growing more solid, more present. In the quiet times, I fade, becoming one with the listening stones.

The troglodytes come to me rarely, and only for the most important reasons. Parents who have lost children seek the echo of a laugh. Elders wish to hear their own young voices once more before they pass. Artists come searching for the pure tone that will perfect their masterwork. Lovers ask me to capture their vows so they might resonate forever in the deep places.

For each service, they pay not in carved bone or precious metal, but in new sounds - a song never before sung, a word in a language of their own invention, a rhythm tapped out that has never existed until that moment. These I add to my collection, weaving them into the eternal chorus of the stone.

Sometimes, in the deepest quiet of what passes for night in this eternal twilight, I hear it still - that first resonance that called me down. It speaks of spaces even deeper than this, of caverns so vast that sound takes years to cross them, of cities built from echo alone where beings like myself dance in acoustic palaces, our bodies nothing but organized vibration, our thoughts pure harmony.

I know that one day I will follow that call deeper still, leaving behind only the faintest whisper of what I was. But not yet. The city still needs its Keeper of Echoes, someone to tend the invisible archive of all that has been spoken, sung, whispered, and wept in these embracing stones. 

Until then, I listen. I preserve. I remember.

And in the remembering, both the city and I continue to become.
