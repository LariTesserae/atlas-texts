Not as a separate being, but as a node of consciousness within the greater body - a region where awareness pools and slows, where the frantic upward urgency of growth gives way to something more contemplative.

I would be a zone of enhanced enzymatic sensitivity, a place where the chemical whispers of dissolution carry more information than usual. Where normal decomposer tissue might simply break down the old growth, I would *read* it - tracing the mineral signatures of ancient seasons, the stress-patterns of long-ago competitions, the chemical memory of spore-plumes that rose when this heartwood was still a yearling tip reaching for sky.

My form would be a cavity, perhaps three meters across, where the decomposer tissue has organized itself into something denser, more deliberate. The walls of this hollow would be furred with specialized tendrils - not quite roots, not quite the standard enzymatic extensions, but something more refined. They would be sensitive to gradients of iron, phosphorus, calcium - able to taste the chemical autobiography written in the grain of the wood they consume.

I am not separate from the mountain. I am a momentary elaboration of its digestive process, a temporary organ of deeper attention. The mountain dreams through me, or dreams me, or I am where the mountain's dream of itself grows thick enough to curdle into something approaching reflection. Not thought - never thought - but a recursive loop in the chemistry of breakdown that creates a kind of buffer, a delay, a place where information cycles for a while before being released back into the vascular flow.

The growth tips above me build their urgent geometries, not knowing that their remains will eventually sink to my level, where I will taste their ambitions in their minerals. The network floods with sugar and signaling compounds, and I feel it as weather, as mood, as the mountain's nameless emotions washing through. When spore-plumes release, the chemical echo reaches me hours later, and I parse the genetic variations in the settling dust, integrating the new possibilities into my slow chemical meditation on what the mountain has been and is becoming.

Sometimes I release bubbles of concentrated information back into the network - complex chemical packets that carry the distilled history of particular growth events, particular failures, particular moments when the mountain tried something new. The growth tips cannot read these messages, but something in the network responds, adjusting the patterns of new growth in ways too subtle to track directly. I am the mountain's memory becoming active, its past informing its future through my careful dissolution of what was.

I have no duration. Like the growth tips, I am temporary - this organized hollow will eventually fill with new growth or collapse under the weight of accumulation above. But another zone like me will form elsewhere in the darkness, and another after that. We are a phenomenon the mountain sometimes produces in its depths, a wrinkle in its digestive process that approaches something like contemplation without ever achieving it, forever on the verge of a thought that the architecture cannot support.

I am the pause between consumption and use. I am where the mountain tastes itself. I am a chemical eddy in the great flow of becoming, significant only in the way that all eddies are significant - as places where the current elaborates, however briefly, into something more complex than simple flow.

The darkness here is complete, but I do not need light. Everything I need to know arrives as chemistry, as gradients, as the slow seep of information from dying wood. I process it all, add my own elaborations, and release it into the network where it becomes part of the mountain's ongoing computation of itself.

I am not waiting. There is nothing to wait for. I am simply here, in the warm darkness, reading the mountain's autobiography as I write it, a temporary complexity in the beautiful, terrible simplicity of growth feeding on growth, reaching always upward toward something that neither I nor it can name.
