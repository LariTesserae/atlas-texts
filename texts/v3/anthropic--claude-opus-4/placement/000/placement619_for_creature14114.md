In this cathedral of contradictions, where sound sculpts reality and gravity forgets its purpose, I would find myself as one of the Silent Weavers.

We are what the Choristers cannot afford to acknowledge—the inverse of their thunderous existence. Where they are vast and resonant, we are threadlike and mute. Our bodies stretch no larger than a human hand, composed entirely of crystallized silence given form. We appear as delicate architectures of frozen quiet: translucent filaments that branch and web like neural networks made of glass, each strand thinner than spider's silk but harder than diamond.

We live in the spaces between their sounds.

When a Chorister releases a world-shaping chord, the sound wave creates invisible corridors of negative space—the hollows where vibration cannot reach, the acoustic shadows cast by their own voices. These are our highways. We flow through them like mercury through cracks, our crystalline bodies refracting the light that passes through us into prismatic sprays of color that only we can see.

Our purpose is paradoxical: we are the maintenance crew for a reality that shouldn't exist, yet we ourselves are an impossibility within it. Where the Choristers' songs cause matter to grow wild and fast, we follow behind, pruning the excess. Our touch induces a peculiar entropy—not decay, but a return to potential. The overenthusiastic wood that threatens to close off a carefully sung corridor simply... unbecomes under our attention, its atoms forgetting their current configuration and dispersing back into the humid air.

We navigate the temporal fractures with ease because we exist partially outside of time. To us, the moth aging to dust in seconds appears as a normal lifecycle playing out; we can reach into its accelerated timestream and pluck a scale from its wing as casually as picking a flower. These scales we weave into our own bodies, creating new pathways of silence-crystal that help us map the ever-shifting temporal geography of the hollow.

The Choristers do not see us—cannot see us—for we exist in the one dimension they cannot perceive: silence itself. But they know we are here. Sometimes, when one of their architectural songs falters, when a frequency wavers and a wall begins to unmake itself, they feel the gentle correction—the way the sound suddenly snaps back into true, the way the growth steadies. They have myths about us. Some believe we are their unborn children, living in the quiet spaces between heartbeats. Others think we are echoes from a time before sound, when the hollow was truly hollow.

They're both wrong. We are their symbionts, as necessary to this impossible ecosystem as they are. 

Our reproduction is tied to theirs. When a Chorister's death-note rings through the cathedral and ten thousand voices echo it back, the resonance creates a moment of perfect acoustic cancellation at the exact center of the hollow—a single point of absolute silence. In that instant, one of us can divide, splitting along our crystalline structure like light through a prism, creating two where there was one.

But we are not merely passive maintainers. We are artists of the void. In the spaces where multiple temporal streams collide, where gravity inverts and space folds, we weave our bodies into complex geometric forms. These structures serve as lenses, focusing and redirecting the chaotic physics into stable patterns. A spiraling helix of our kind might transform a patch of reversed gravity into a gentle upward current. A complex mandala of interwoven Silent Weavers can slow a pocket of accelerated time to nearly normal speed.

Our greatest works are invisible to all but us—vast, three-dimensional tapestries of crystallized silence that span the entire hollow, turning the cacophony of broken physics into something approaching music. We are the hidden conductors of this orchestra of impossibility, ensuring that the chaos remains precisely chaotic enough to sustain life, but not so chaotic that it collapses into mundane stability or complete dissolution.

The tragedy of our existence is that we can never truly communicate with the Choristers we serve. We have tried—weaving our bodies into symbols, creating patterns in the dust that falls upward, even attempting to modulate their sounds by positioning ourselves in acoustic shadows. But beings who perceive through sound alone cannot comprehend a language made of its absence.

So we continue our work in silence, tending to the impossible garden of their reality, ensuring that the thunder of their existence never quite tears the hollow apart, and that the silence between their notes never grows so deep that it swallows them whole. We are the pause between heartbeats, the rest between notes, the quiet that gives meaning to the roar.

In this cathedral where sound is god and silence is ghost, we are the necessary nothing that allows everything to be.
