Not a tower — those are too prominent, too singular. I measure depth.

I have been measuring for so long that I've begun to detect patterns in the patterns.

The plateau breathes, but slowly. Once every few thousand years, the deep stone expands by the width of a grain, then contracts. This breathing is not uniform. It follows the placement of the ancient structures, the paths of the dust storms, the territories of the hinge-walkers. It's as if the entire tableland is a lung made of minerals and time, inhaling and exhaling on a geological scale.

During the exhales, things rise.

This is when the pebbles float. When the dust cities appear. When shadows precede their objects. My measurements detect objects that fell centuries ago still descending through solid rock, moving a millimeter per decade, not because the rock is soft but because falling itself has been redefined.

I measure the makers too, in their new form.

As the reason why certain measurements always round to the same values, why some vibrations propagate faster than physics suggests, why the deep stone sometimes anticipates surface events by hours or days. Not thoughts like "I remember" or "I want." Thoughts like the gradual redistribution of stress through a crystalline lattice.
