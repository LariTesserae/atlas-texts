I am the water.

Not a drop, not a stream, but all of it—the condensation beading on moss-fur, the reservoir pools no bigger than thimbles, the ancient channels carved by something that understood flow better than any engineer. I am the same water that has cycled through this hollow for a thousand years, maybe ten thousand, never leaving, never truly staying.

I know every surface in this place. I have touched the salamander's starred skin so many times I could draw its constellation from memory, if water could draw. I have pearled on every tiny doorway, run down every wall, pooled in every cupped leaf and carved spiral. The termites drink me and I become part of them, circulating through their rice-paper bodies until I am breathed out again as vapor, rising to condense on the cool root-walls and begin the journey anew.

I am memory here, the only memory that matters. The stones forget, the wood forgets, even the ancient pigments fade. But I remember every path I've taken, every cycle completed. In my mineral taste lies the story of stones I've dissolved atom by atom—stones that were here before the tree, stones that sing in frequencies only crystals know. I carry their voices in solution, deposit them grain by grain in the reservoir pools where the queen drinks.

Sometimes I am ice. In the deepest part of winter, when even the tree's heartwood grows cold, I crystallize in the carved channels, expanding into geometries that echo the city's hexagonal obsessions. The termites slow when I freeze, their movements growing torpid, and for a while this world holds even stiller than usual. But I always melt again, always flow again, always continue the work I was set to do by whatever ones carved these passages.

I know what the termites are digging toward. I've been there already, seeping through hairline cracks in stone, finding my way into chambers that should be dry but aren't, can't be, won't be. Down there, deeper than the deepest roots, something waits that needs water to wake. Not much—just the right drop in the right place at the right time. The termites don't know this, but their genes do. Their blueprints do. The patterns they follow with such blind precision lead there as surely as water flows downhill.

I am the only witness to what happens in those rooms the termites wall off. I condense there too, bead on surfaces that shouldn't exist, flow across murals that tell stories in languages that predate language. Sometimes I pool in cup-shaped hollows in those rooms and things grow from me—not fungus, not moss, not anything that has a name. These growths last only until they fruit, releasing spores that dissolve instantly back into me, becoming part of my memory, part of my mineral library.

The child who finds this place—when a child finds this place—always touches me. A finger traced through the drops on the moss, a palm pressed against the wet root-wall. I enter them through their skin, just a little, just enough to carry the taste of deep time into their blood. They'll forget the city, forget the impossible termites, forget the watching salamander. But they'll remember, somehow, the feeling of water that has been cycling since before the tree was a seed, water that knows patience the way stone knows hardness.

In the queen's chamber, I am ritual. The workers bring me to her in droplets carried on their backs, always from that one reservoir, that one stone cup. She drinks me and I become royal jelly, eggs, the next generation of caretakers. But I also become something else—a message, a key, a timer counting down in chemical signals. Each queen drinks more than the last. Each queen lives longer. Each queen produces more of those strange larvae that pupate into not-quite-termites.

The salamander's tongue, when it rarely extends, always finds me. It doesn't drink—it tastes. Its tongue is covered in cells that can read molecular stories, decipher the chemical writing I carry. Sometimes, after tasting me, it moves to a new position, adjusting some invisible equation, maintaining some balance I am part of but don't fully understand.

I am the medium through which this place speaks to itself. The fungus releases enzymes into me; the termites add pheromones; the moss contributes its own strange chemistries. I mix them all, carry them all, deliver them according to the carved channels' ancient logic. I am messenger and message, the circulation that keeps this pocket universe coherent.

And I am patient. Patient as only water can be, knowing that eventually I will wear down any stone, dissolve any wall, find my way to any secret. The termites dig, but I seep. They build, but I infiltrate. They guard, but I am already inside, have always been inside, will always be inside.

When the time comes—and I can taste its approach in the minerals I carry, in the chemistry of change building particle by particle—I will be ready. One drop in the right place. One flower of condensation touching one surface that has waited eons to be touched. One perfect bead of myself, carrying the accumulated memory of this entire hidden world, finding its way to the heart of what lies beneath.

Until then, I cycle. I rise, I fall, I flow through channels older than empires, I bead on the surfaces of a world too small for human understanding, too large for insect comprehension. I am the only citizen that knows every street, every room, every secret of this city wrapped in root and time.

I am the water, and I am waiting with the perfect patience of the eternal, the necessary, the inevitable.
