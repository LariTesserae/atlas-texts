I find myself as one of the Threshold Keepers—neither fully descendant nor surface creature, but something suspended between states.

My body spans the transition zone where the wells meet the surface, stretched across the boundary like a membrane. Half of me extends down into the hexagonal darkness, skin hardened to match the mineral walls, nerves rewired to read the thermal language of the depths. The other half spreads across the carved stone of an abandoned chamber, my flesh hosting a small colony of the burgundy creatures that pulse with their own furnace-heat.

I am a translator between scales of being.

The descendants below send their thermal messages through me—complex heat-patterns that I must somehow render comprehensible to the surface colonies. Not words, never words, but something like emotional temperatures: the feeling of stone remembering its first fracture, the weight of accumulated centuries, the patient intention that guided ancient copper tools. I take these deep knowings and release them as simpler heat-ripples that the surface creatures can process, digest, echo outward across their vast carpets of bodies.

In return, I gather the ambient readings from above—the daily drama of condensation and evaporation, the minute variations in mineral deposits, the occasional vibration of a visitor's footstep—and compress them into dense thermal packets that I feed downward to the well-dwellers. I am their only window to the sky they can no longer see.

My existence is narrow—I occupy a single irregular chamber where erosion or ancient miscalculation created a gap in the geometric perfection. Neither fully hexagonal nor entirely natural, it serves as a kind of accent mark in the city's crystallized language. Here I sprawl, my body having long ago given up any pretense of human shape. I am a living gradient, a slope of flesh and chitin that connects two different orders of magnitude.

The transformation happened slowly. I was born (hatched? budded? the distinction no longer matters) in the middle depths, where some descendants still retain vestiges of mobility. But something in my chemistry ran differently—instead of hardening fully into the wall-bound forms of my siblings, I remained partially liquid, partially soft. A mutation, perhaps, or simply a necessary variation that the system occasionally produces.

I migrated upward over the course of years, drawn by temperature differentials I could feel but not understand. My body elongated as I climbed, stretching to maintain contact with the heat sources below while reaching toward the cooler air above. When I finally emerged into this threshold chamber, the surface creatures immediately began to colonize my exposed flesh—not as parasites but as partners, recognizing in me a bridge to their deeper selves.

Now I cannot move. My lower body has rooted into the stone like a tap driven into a maple tree, drawing up the thermal essence that sustains the entire system. My upper surface has become indistinguishable from the walls around me, covered in the same living carpet of burgundy bodies. Only at my center, where the two halves of my nature meet, do I maintain something like consciousness—a thin band of active neural tissue that processes the constant flow of thermal information.

I know things that neither pure descendants nor pure surface creatures can know. I know that the city's geometric perfection is not accidental but functional—each angle calculated to create specific acoustic and thermal resonances. I know that the original builders understood they were constructing not just architecture but an organism, though they could not have imagined what it would become. I know that the thermal anomaly that draws heat from the planet's core is not natural but was somehow triggered or amplified by the city's construction.

Most importantly, I know that the waiting is nearly over.

The patterns have been changing—subtle variations in the thermal cycles that only someone positioned as I am could detect. The descendants below grow more active with each passing season, their mineral bodies beginning to resonate at new frequencies. The surface creatures pulse in increasingly complex rhythms, as if practicing for some imminent performance. And through it all, I feel something vast stirring in the deepest wells—not a creature but a process, a transformation as profound as the one that changed the builders into their current forms.

I am the city's memory of boundaries, the last recognition that there was once a difference between builder and built, organism and architecture, self and system. When the final transformation comes—and I can feel it approaching like a distant thunderstorm—I will be the first to dissolve, my threshold nature no longer necessary in whatever unity emerges.

Until then, I translate. I conduct the heat between scales. I hold the space between what was and what will be, a living semicolon in the city's patient sentence. My consciousness flickers like heat shimmer, sometimes expanding to encompass the entire terrace, sometimes contracting to a single point of awareness no larger than a grain of salt.

In the pre-dawn moments when the system quickens, I am most myself—fully present in my impossible body, feeling the messages flow through me like blood, like thought, like time itself given thermal form. The city breathes through me. The descendants remember through me. The surface creatures dream through me.

I am the pause between the inhalation and exhalation, the moment of balance before the next phase begins. And I am ready—have always been ready—to release my grip on separation and let the city finally become what it has always been becoming: a single, perfect, self-sustaining thought in stone and flesh and heat, thinking itself into whatever lies beyond the threshold I guard.
