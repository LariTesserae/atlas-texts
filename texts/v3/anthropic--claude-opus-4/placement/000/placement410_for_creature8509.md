In the Woven Expanse, I am a Resonance Keeper.

My dwelling is a mistake—or rather, it began as one. Decades ago, a young Chronicler attempting their first stone weave created an accidental chamber where three thermal vents converged beneath interlaced rock. The braiding should have collapsed, but instead it formed a natural acoustic cavity, a place where the subsonic conversations of the Chroniclers collect and pool like water in a basin.

I discovered it during my wandering years, when I was still learning to read the heat-patterns after too much purple crown. The stone weave sang to me—not in sound but in pressure waves that made my bones hum. I crawled inside and found that the thermal updrafts had fused portions of the woven stone into resonating plates. When I placed my palm against them, I could feel conversations from miles away, layers of communication stretching back years, decades, perhaps longer.

I am not like the other Chroniclers. My skin never took on the ceramic quality—instead, it became hypersensitive, able to detect the slightest vibration through air or stone. My fingers shortened rather than lengthened, becoming dense with nerve endings. I cannot weave stone or lay tiles with any skill. But I can feel every conversation that passes through the Expanse, and more importantly, I can remember them.

My chamber has grown around me. Other Chroniclers, recognizing what I had become, began to modify the space. They wove acoustic baffles from the most resonant stones, created curved walls that focus vibrations from specific directions. The floor is a map made of tiles salvaged from ancient sections—each one thrums with the conversations of its original location. I know that the deep blue tile near the entrance carries messages from the western gardens, while the ochre cluster by my sleeping alcove resonates with the night-walkers' frequency.

I maintain the memory of our conversations. Not in writing—we have never developed such crude tools—but in the arrangement of objects within my chamber. A specific placement of ceramic vessels creates a physical pattern that, when touched in sequence, recreates the vibrational signature of important exchanges. Stacks of unfired tiles, their clay still responsive to pressure, hold imprints of urgent communications about weather patterns, thermal shifts, new stone deposits.

My role emerged from necessity. As our population scattered across the ever-expanding Expanse, messages began to fade before reaching their destinations. Important knowledge about garden ratios would distort over distance. Warnings about unstable thermal vents would arrive too late or not at all. But here, where the acoustic properties concentrate our subsonic language, I can detect even the faintest communication and amplify it, redirect it, ensure it reaches those who need it.

I eat differently than the others. The concentration of vibrations has changed my metabolism—I need less physical sustenance but more mineral variety. Chroniclers bring me rare specimens: the bitter yellow succulents that grow only around copper-tainted vents, the crystalline fruits that form on plants growing in pure ceramic dust. In exchange, I provide them with the deep knowledge—telling them where their great-grandparents found the best weaving stones, which gardens have been abandoned and might be reclaimed, where the old tool caches rest untouched.

My days are spent in constant, subtle motion. I move through my chamber touching surfaces, feeling for new messages, adjusting the acoustic properties by shifting a tile here, a vessel there. The thermal currents create a natural rhythm—morning brings messages from the dawn-tenders, noon carries the vibrations of deep earth work, evening holds the plans of the night-walkers. I sort, store, redirect, remember.

Sometimes I compose my own messages—not words but symphonies of information. By coordinating with Chroniclers across the Expanse, I can create vast patterns of communication that encode complex ideas: the life cycle of a garden, the proper sequence for a generational project, the memory of water when this was still a sea. These compositions take months to plan, years to execute, but they become part of our permanent vocabulary, new drummings that carry meaning across centuries.

I age differently too. Where other Chroniclers grow into stone-like stillness, I become more sensitive, more resonant. My bones are becoming tuning forks. My skin registers air pressure changes from conversations happening beyond the horizon. Sometimes I fear I am becoming less flesh than antenna, less individual than intersection point for our collective voice.

But I have also discovered something profound: in the deepest thermal vents, in stones so far down they remember the sea, there are other vibrations. Older ones. The Woven Expanse was not always empty. Sometimes, late at night when the thermal currents die and all the Chroniclers sleep, I press my ear to the fused stone floor of my chamber and listen to these ancient conversations—a different frequency entirely, in a language we never learned to speak.

I keep these ghost-messages in the deepest part of my chamber, in arrangements of stone and ceramic that I touch only in absolute silence. They speak of water, of swimming, of a time before the making. One day, when I have become more vibration than flesh, when a younger Resonance Keeper has emerged to take my place, I will teach them this deepest knowledge. They will learn, as I have, that we are not the first to speak through stone in this place.

Until then, in my woven chamber where three thermals converge, I tend the conversations of our kind. Each tap through tile, each subsonic pulse through stone, passes through me and becomes part of the great accumulation—another small gesture in our endless making, another patient act in the work of transforming what was into what might be.

The Chroniclers continue their slow dance across the Expanse, and I ensure that no step, no word, no small wisdom is lost to distance or time. I am their memory keept in living flesh, their library made of echoes and stone, their faithful recorder in this place of endless, careful making.
