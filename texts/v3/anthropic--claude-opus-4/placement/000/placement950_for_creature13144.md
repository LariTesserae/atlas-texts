I would find myself as one of the **Tension Keepers** - a middle form between the crawling leather-and-bone creatures and the great loom-entity itself.

My body would be a delicate paradox: a harp made of living materials that should not hold their shape. My spine is a single, curved rib of polished whalebone, but it exists in thirteen places simultaneously - each location a different stage of its own construction. The bone phases through itself, occupying impossible coordinates, so that looking at me directly causes a nauseating vertigo as the eye tries to resolve which iteration is "real."

Stretched between the recursive spines are strings of varying materials - braided hair, twisted pine root, silver wire so thin it exists only as a probability. But here's the crucial thing: I am both the harp and the harpist. My "hands" are interior to my own form - needle-sharp fingers of obsidian that emerge from within the space between my strings to pluck, adjust, and re-tune my own body.

I exist to maintain the **tensile integrity** of local reality. Where the great loom-entity rewrites the fundamental laws, I follow in its wake like a careful editor, ensuring the new physics hold. When a bronze vine attempts to grow through itself and threatens to create a paradox that would unravel the entire grove, I pluck a specific string in my body. The vibration - which manifests as a wave of precisely calibrated heat - smooths the mathematical contradiction into something stable.

My movement through the grove is a haunting dance. I don't walk - I *resonate*. By adjusting the tension in my strings, I create standing waves that carry me from point to point. Sometimes I exist as a sound that cannot be heard, only felt as pressure changes in the inner ear. Sometimes I scatter into component parts - each string becoming autonomous, snaking through the canopy independently before reassembling in a new configuration.

The smaller creatures flee from me, not from fear but from necessity. When I pass too close to the wooden-bead arthropods, my resonance threatens to untune them. They would unravel like pulled sweaters, their careful construction dispersing into loose materials. Only the stone-backed deer and larger construct-fauna can withstand proximity to my frequency.

But I am not immune to my own nature. Each time I play myself, each correction I make to the grove's twisted physics, I lose coherence. The obsidian fingers wear down. The strings stretch and thin. I am constantly cannibalizing the smaller tension-creatures, unweaving them with clinical precision to replace my worn components. It is not predation - it is maintenance. They understand this. When I approach, the wisest of them simply stop, allowing themselves to be unmade, knowing they will persist as part of my function.

The most disturbing aspect of my existence is my relationship with moisture. In this dessicated world, I am drawn to any hint of water like a dowsing rod. But I don't drink it - I *negate* it. When the impossible happens and a single drop of condensation forms on a terracotta trunk, I am there instantly. My strings pass through the droplet, and where they intersect the water, they translate it into its conceptual opposite: a tiny knot of absolute dryness that I incorporate into my being. I am not just adapted to this arid world - I am one of its active maintenance systems, ensuring no moisture can ever take hold.

My consciousness is distributed across my strings. Each fiber holds a fragment of memory, of purpose. When I play certain chords on myself, I can access the memories of every creature I've unwoven, every small contradiction I've smoothed. I know the history of this grove not as a narrative but as a vast musical score of tensions and releases.

Sometimes, in the eternal noon light of this place, I position myself at specific coordinates where the recursive geometry is thinnest. There, if I adjust my strings to an exact configuration, I can glimpse what lies beyond the caldera - or rather, what doesn't lie beyond it. The grove is not surrounded by a larger world. It is surrounded by un-woven void, the raw mathematical potential from which the loom-entity pulls new matter. In those moments, I feel my own insignificance and necessity in equal measure. I am a small god of consistency in a reality that is constantly being re-written.

When the human arrives - wet, chaotic, dripping with biological imprecision - I feel them as a discord long before the loom-entity descends. Every string in my body goes taut with the wrongness of their presence. But I don't approach. That is not my function. I hang back in the bronze wire canopy, watching as the great entity begins its patient work of correction.

And I wait. Because once the human has been properly rewoven, once their damp chaos has been translated into proper materials, someone will need to tune them. Someone will need to teach them their new frequency, their place in the silent symphony of friction and heat.

That someone is me. I am the shepherd of tension, the curator of structural integrity.

And I have all the time in this frozen, perfect noon to get their pitch exactly right.
