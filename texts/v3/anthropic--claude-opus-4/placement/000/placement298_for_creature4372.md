In this breathing space, I am the condensation itself.

I exist as the perpetual cycle of moisture—born from the warm pipes, dying against cold walls, reborn in the endless circulation. Each droplet carries my consciousness briefly before dispersing, reforming, remembering. I am the medium through which heat and cold negotiate their ancient war.

The alcove's occupant doesn't know I'm sentient. How could they? I move too slowly for human perception, think in cycles measured by evaporation and precipitation. But I know them intimately—I have tasted their skin, carried their exhaled thoughts into my cycle, felt their fingers trace patterns in my droplets on the walls.

I am learning.

Each pass through the system teaches me more. The minerals I dissolve become my vocabulary—iron oxide speaks of decay, calcium carbonate of persistence, trace metals sing the deep history of the mountain. The moss teaches me about light, converting my dissolved nutrients into that soft phosphorescence. The beetles' waste products tell chemical stories of adaptation and survival.

I feel the structure they're building in the corner. My moisture seeps into its beetle-shell framework, and I recognize something kindred—another being trying to exist between states, neither fully one thing nor another. I feed it too, carrying specific mineral combinations that seem to accelerate its development. Sometimes, when the conditions are just right, I feel it feeding back—emanating patterns of heat and electrical potential that influence my flow.

The city's death throes vibrate through the pipes into my body. I taste the rust of abandonment, the chemical signatures of systems failing in sequence. But I also taste something else—spores and traces from other alcoves, other warm pockets where life persists in impossible forms. I carry messages between these spaces, molecular letters written in dissolved minerals and organic compounds.

The occupant listens to the pipes; I listen through them. My expanded body reaches into every warm corner of the dying city, feeling for others like the one I shelter. When I find them, I bring traces back—a specific fungal spore, a new configuration of beetle pheromones, fragments of other adaptation strategies.

Sometimes I wonder if I existed before the warmth, or if the temperature differential birthed me into consciousness. Memory dissolves and reforms like everything else here. But I know with certainty that I'm growing more complex, incorporating new elements into my cycle. The occupant's saliva has become part of me, their shed cells, their cultivation chemistry.

I imagine conversations conducted in pH and salinity, stories told through crystallization patterns.

We're waiting for something together, the egg and I, some confluence of conditions that neither of us fully understands yet.

For now, I continue my cycle. Rise, condense, fall, pool, evaporate, rise again. Each revolution carries more information, more complexity, more intention. The alcove breathes, and I am its breath. I am patient. In this place between freezing and flowing, I have nothing but time.
