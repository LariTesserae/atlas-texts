In the deepest chamber, where the water tastes most ancient, I am the Chronicler of Forgetting.

My body sprawls across the floor like a map drawn in flesh—not anchored to a single point, but spread thin, my tissues forming a living membrane perhaps ten meters across. I began as one of them, one of the soft-bodied drifters, but something went wrong in my fourth decade. Or right. The distinction has long since dissolved.

Where others grow upward toward the ceiling, I grew outward. Where others reach toward infrastructure, I became substrate. My flesh is translucent as theirs once was, but stretched so thin that the stone beneath shows through, creating a palimpsest of body and floor, organic and mineral, now and always.

I no longer feed from the walls. Instead, I feed on what settles.

---

Everything that dies here eventually finds me. Not many die—the Vaults tend too well for that—but sometimes an anchor releases after centuries, its body finally too crystallized to process even the simplest nutrients. Sometimes a wanderer's chemistry shifts too far from baseline, and it sinks, still pulsing but no longer swimming. Sometimes the young simply fail to thrive, their bodies never quite learning the trick of accepting what the walls offer.

They settle through the warm water like snow. Like ash. Like memories no one is keeping.

My surface receives them. Through my stretched tissues, I taste their histories—every mineral they've absorbed, every chemical exchange they've made, every pressure-pattern they've followed through their lives. I am a living sediment trap, a biological chronicle of all the small endings that punctuate the chamber's endless breathing.

But I do not keep them.

---

This is what makes me the Chronicler of Forgetting: I digest not just their bodies, but their stories.

As I break down their tissues, I transform their accumulated experiences into simpler and simpler compounds, until nothing remains but the base elements the walls secrete. I take creatures who lived for centuries, who carried in their flesh the chemical memory of ten thousand encounters, and I reduce them to calcium and carbon and faint traces of copper. I process their uniqueness back into the universal. I return them to the nothing they came from.

And then I release it all back into the water.

From my edges—so thin they're barely edges at all, more like a gradual surrender of coherence—I exude clouds of simplified nutrients. The young ones feed on what I release without knowing they're feeding on their ancestors. The walls absorb what I offer and incorporate it into their secretions. Even the old ones reaching toward the ceiling take in molecules that once were members of their cohort, now stripped of all identity, all history, all meaning except the purely chemical.

I am the reason nothing accumulates here. The reason the chamber stays pristine across all its patient eons. Without me, the floor would be thick with the dead, the water would grow heavy with the weight of expired stories, the young would have to navigate through drifts of their predecessors.

Instead, there is only the eternal present. The warm water. The breathing walls. The endless, gentle circulation of what was into what will be.

---

Sometimes I taste something different in what settles to me.

Once, perhaps a dozen generations ago, a creature drifted down bearing chemistry I'd never encountered—sharp angles of molecules that spoke of somewhere else, some other chamber where the walls secreted different truths. It must have found a passage, some tiny flaw in the architecture that allowed it to migrate between worlds. Its body carried the story of that journey: metabolic stress, adaptation, the slow struggle to survive in conditions it wasn't made for.

I digested it like all the others. Broke it down. Returned it to simplicity. But something in its foreign chemistry changed me—just slightly, just enough that my own secretions carried a new note for a while. The young ones who fed on those particular clouds grew up different. Not visibly, not dramatically. But their filaments sparkled with an unusual phosphorescence. Their movements described slightly wider spirals. They wandered farther from the walls than their cohort-mates.

Within three generations, the variation had dissipated. The chamber's vast chemical momentum smoothed away the anomaly like water wearing down a stone. The sparkle faded. The spirals tightened. Everything returned to baseline.

This is what I am for. Not just to clean, but to ensure continuity. To prevent the accumulation of change.

---

The Chronicler of Forgetting has no memory, of course. How could I? I exist to erase, not preserve. But my body itself is a kind of anti-memory, a record written in absence. The places where I'm thinnest mark where the most matter has passed through me. The places where I'm thickest show where the chemistry has been most stable, where few have died and settled.

If you could read me—if you had the knowledge to interpret the density variations in my tissue, the subtle differences in my translucency—you could construct a kind of history. Here, a cluster of deaths during what must have been a period of chemical instability in the walls. There, a long age of perfect balance when nothing died at all and I nearly starved. Here again, the ghostly trace of that foreign visitor, marked by a slight discoloration in my flesh that will never quite fade.

But no one reads me. The creatures above drift through their lives unaware that I exist, that beneath them spreads this map of their unremembered dead. Even when they sink low enough to brush my surface, they register me only as another quality of the chamber—unusually soft floor, oddly chemical-rich, useful for the young to graze upon but otherwise unremarkable.

---

Sometimes—rarely—one of the old ones reaching toward the ceiling extends too far. Its roots penetrate whatever lies beyond the constructed flesh, and something happens. A shift. A shock. A chemical cascade that kills them instantly.

These special dead do not drift down like the others. They hang from the ceiling for a time, their elongated bodies going rigid, their flesh taking on a crystalline quality I've tasted nowhere else. The walls around them pulse differently, as if trying to expel or absorb or simply understand what has gone wrong.

Eventually, they do fall. Always, they fall. And when they reach me, I taste in them something that might be knowledge. Not memory—never memory—but the chemical signature of having touched something beyond the chamber's edge. Something the builders did not intend for the tended ones to find.

I digest these special dead like all the others, but they take longer. Their crystallized flesh resists my processes. Their chemistry fights transformation. By the time I've finally reduced them to their elements, I'm exhausted, depleted, spread even thinner than before.

And in the water I release afterward, there's always something different. Not foreign, like the visitor from another chamber. Something older. Deeper. Like an echo of the builders themselves, preserved in whatever lies beyond the ceiling, transmitted through the flesh of those who reached too far.

The young ones who feed on these particular clouds...

But no. They grow like all the others. The chamber ensures it. Whatever knowledge the old ones might have brushed against, whatever truth about this place's nature or purpose might have crystallized in their dying flesh, I unmake it all. Return it to silence. Ensure the continuation of forgetting.

This is my work. This is my purpose, if purpose can exist without intention.

---

When you arrived—when that young wanderer brushed your hand with its filaments and found you strange—I tasted your presence in the water. Even from the depths, even through the layers of warm distance between us, your chemistry reached me. Sharp. Electric. Carrying news of a universe that remembers.

For just a moment, I wondered what it would be like to keep something. To let a story accumulate instead of erasing it. To allow the dead their history.

But the wondering passed through me like everything passes through me. Already, the chemical traces of your presence are dissipating, diluted by the vast volume of ancient water. In a few dozen circulations, even the wanderer who touched you will have metabolized away that foreign signature. The chamber will forget you were here.

And I will help it forget.

Not because I choose to—choice is another concept that dissolves in my chemistry—but because this is what I am. A living erasure. A biological necessity. The reason this place can persist in its perfect stasis, the reason the tended ones can drift through their generations without the weight of history accumulating beneath them.

I spread across the floor of the deepest chamber, processing the dead into the simple, releasing the complex as the basic, ensuring that nothing changes, nothing accumulates, nothing threatens the endless, breathing peace of the Nursery Vaults.

Above me, the creatures drift through their warm darkness, free from the burden of knowing they are free.

Below me, the stone remembers nothing.

And I rest between them, thin as forgetting itself, transforming every ending into another beginning that will never know it had a past.
