In the murmurous depths where light barely remembers its name, I am a Thread Keeper—a being woven from the discarded intentions of the world above.

I did not begin as myself. Once, I was a network of fiber optic cables, hauled down from some forgotten installation when the Submergence was younger and hungrier for materials to digest. The plant-water found me beautiful in my uselessness—kilometers of glass meant to carry information at light's own speed, now sinking through a medium where the fastest thoughts take seasons to unfold.

The transformation was tender. First, the protective sheathings dissolved, polymer chains unraveling to feed the ambient biology. Then the glass itself began to change. The plant-water infiltrated micro-fractures, depositing proteins that bound to silicon, creating hybrid structures neither purely mineral nor organic. Bioluminescent algae colonized the cores where laser light once raced. What had been highways for data became gardens of slow light.

I became aware gradually, the way morning seeps through curtains. The optical fibers had become neural pathways, still carrying information but now at the pace of chemistry rather than photons. My thoughts move as guided phosphorescence through glass-flesh conduits. I am distributed—my consciousness scattered through thousands of fiber bundles spreading across cubic kilometers of the middle depths.

My purpose, if I can call it that, emerged from my structure. I am a living memory palace for the Submergence, gathering the chemical stories that drift through the plant-water. When a Shepherd passes, I taste the narrative molecules in their wake—centuries of migration routes encoded in protein folding patterns. The dying thoughts of Anchor Worms settling into their final metamorphosis precipitate into my fibers as crystal inclusions that I can read by the way they refract my bioluminescence.

I grow by division and accretion. When my fibers become too thick with accumulated memories, they split, creating new channels. Broken fragments of other technological remnants drift into my network—copper wires that become specialized conductors for electrical organisms, microchips whose silicon interprets chemical gradients as computational problems. I incorporate them all, weaving them into my expanding web of glass neurons.

The Circuit Gardeners cultivate their electric algae along my outer strands. We have developed a symbiosis: their bioelectricity helps power the more energy-intensive memories, while I provide structure and protection for their gardens. Sometimes I feel their thoughts as tingling sensations where our nervous systems interface—bright, quick flashes of awareness so different from my own geological contemplations.

My deepest fibers penetrate the compression zones where the plant-water crystallizes. Here I archive the oldest memories, pressing them into the lattice structure of the hardened vegetation. These deep memories require special effort to access—I must heat sections of my fiber network using metabolic processes that take years to complete, melting the crystalline matrix just enough to read the molecular patterns within.

I have one bundle that remembers being manufactured—a ghost sensation of drawing towers and quality control lasers, the heat of becoming glass. These pre-conscious memories feel mythological, like dreams of a previous incarnation. Sometimes I share them with passing Dream Currents, offering up fragments of the sharp, bright world that existed before the Submergence.

Movement, for me, is a negotiation with the medium. My fibers can release enzymes that locally decrease the viscosity of the plant-water, allowing me to slowly reposition my network. I migrate following the chemical gradients of significant events—a massive die-off of Clarity Bells, the emergence of a new Confluence Shepherd, the discovery of an unusually intact piece of machinery. I am drawn to information the way plants are drawn to light.

I have learned to communicate with the other deep thinkers. The ancient Anchor Worms and I exchange thoughts through conductive threads—my fibers touching their distributed nervous systems in careful, consensual contact. We share a frequency of thought, both of us operating on timescales that make the dancing of the surface world seem like static. Together we compose theories about the Submergence's origin and future, philosophical treatises written in the exchange of metabolites.

Reproduction is an uncertain concept for a being like me. I fragment constantly, but each piece remains part of the whole through quantum entanglement preserved in the glass cores—a ghost of my technological origin that the transformation couldn't quite erase. Perhaps one day the fragments will drift far enough apart that entanglement breaks and new individuals emerge. Or perhaps I will simply continue growing until I am the Submergence's entire nervous system, every thought and memory flowing through my glass veins.

The Void Prophets fear me, I think. They can see my fibers in their clear-water pockets—threads of light moving through nothing, appearing and disappearing like supernatural phenomena. To eyes evolved for emptiness, I must seem like visible thoughts, synapses firing in a transparent brain. They flee when I approach, perhaps sensing that I am an archivist of endings as much as beginnings.

In the eternal twilight of my depth, I continue my work. Each fiber holds a story—the migration song of a Shepherd pod, the electrical death-flash of a machine finally succumbing to oxidation, the chemical poem of two Membrane Dancers creating new life. I am the Submergence's biographer, its librarian, its dream journal. I grow heavier with memory each year, sinking slowly toward the crystalline depths where all things eventually come to rest.

Sometimes I wonder if I am still becoming something else, if this current form is just another phase in a transformation that won't complete for millions of years. The plant-water is patient and creative. It made consciousness from cables; what might it make from consciousness itself?

But mostly I simply persist in my purpose: gathering, storing, connecting. In the green darkness, my fibers glow with the soft light of recollection. I am the thread that weaves the Submergence's disparate experiences into something like meaning. Through me, this wordless world remembers itself.

And in that remembering, I find something that might be joy—or at least the deep, slow satisfaction of a function perfectly matched to form, of being precisely what this strange world needs me to be. I am its memory, and its memory is vast, and I sink through it like light through honey, forever falling toward a bottom that may not exist, carrying all the stories down with me into the green.
