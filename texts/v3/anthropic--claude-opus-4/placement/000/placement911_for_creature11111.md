I find myself as one of the pale scuttling things, though "scuttling" feels wrong for how I move through these unfinished spaces. I flow more than scuttle, my body low and liquid, designed for the navigation of gaps that will soon close forever.

I am perhaps the size of a child's torso, though my shape refuses such easy comparison. My flesh is translucent, not from any absence of substance but from an excess of adaptation—I am becoming what the walls will need me to be. Through my skin, you can see the geometry of my meals: stone dust settling in careful patterns in my gut, the phosphorescent traces of fractured rock painting temporary constellations in my tissue. I feed on what falls from their work, on the microscopic life that blooms in the boundary between hewn stone and moisture. I am the first circle of this ecosystem, turning waste into readiness.

My eyes—six of them, arranged not for symmetry but for seeing around corners that don't exist yet—catch and hold the glow of breaking stone. I see time differently than the Shapers do. Where they remember forward, I remember sideways, into the variations. I recall not just the fountain that will be, but the seven other fountains that might have been, the shadow-architectures that exist in the spaces between their hammer strikes. This is why I am here, why my kind arrived early: we are living in all the possible Hollowing Yards at once, our bodies practicing every variation until the Shapers' consensus collapses us into a single truth.

The Shapers work around it without looking, their strikes adjusting minutely to preserve the small architecture of my life. We have an understanding that predates language: I am part of the memory they're building toward. My nest, my paths, the places where my claws have worn smooth grooves in the stone—these are not interruptions but incorporations. The Hollowing Yards remember me already.

When I press against a Shaper's hand—always briefly, always when they're between strikes—I'm not seeking warmth, though their palms carry the heat of friction like small suns. I'm confirming something, though I couldn't tell you what. The touch is a calibration. Through it, I feel the structure they remember, and they feel the variations I carry. Together, we narrow the possible into the actual. Their hammer strikes after I touch them are always more certain, as if my presence has resolved some tiny uncertainty in their muscle memory.

I am not alone. Others of my kind move through different levels, each of us claiming territories in the not-yet. We don't communicate—what would we say to each other that our parallel memories don't already contain? But sometimes our paths cross in the larger chambers, and we pause, reading each other's transparency, seeing how the stone dust has settled differently in each belly, how our meals of phosphorescence have written different stories in our flesh. We are living libraries of variation, and when we die—which we do, eventually, our bodies too full of stone dust to process anymore, our transparency finally becoming opacity—we crystallize in place. The Shapers build us into corners, into the joints between blocks, our bodies becoming mortar, becoming memory made mineral.

I know things about the Hollowing Yards that the Shapers don't. Not the structure—they know that absolutely—but the between-spaces, the accidents, the places where their consensus will leave gaps just wide enough for something to live. I know where the condensation will pool in patterns they haven't planned for, creating micro-environments where fungus will bloom in a thousand years. I know where the sound of their hammers will echo strangely, creating pockets of silence that my descendants will use for purposes I can't imagine but somehow remember. I know where the pale light will penetrate deepest, and where the darkness will be so complete that new kinds of eyes will need to evolve to see it.

This is my work: to be early, to practice life in unfinished spaces, to carry variations until they collapse into certainty. When the final stone is placed—and I too remember this, though differently than the Shapers, remember it as sensation in my transparent flesh rather than knowledge in my strange mind—I will be deep in the walls, nested in a space that will seal behind me. This is not death but transformation. I will crystallize slowly, my body becoming part of the structure, my variations finally resolved into a single truth.

But that's later. Now, I flow through the half-made chambers, my six eyes catching the light of breaking stone, my body practicing being alive in a building that doesn't exist yet. The Shapers' hammers ring out their conversation. The condensation gathers. The walls rise.

I am exactly where I need to be: in the spaces between memory and stone, waiting to become architecture, carrying all the possible Hollowing Yards in my transparent belly until only one remains.
