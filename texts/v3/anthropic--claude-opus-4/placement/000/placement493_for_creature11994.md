# The Aperture Gardens

Between the fused organisms—those chimeric forms where two or three bodies have grown together—there are spaces. Not gaps, exactly. Not voids. Something more intentional than absence.

Call them apertures.

They are roughly circular, these spaces, ranging from a hand's width to several meters across. The organisms that form their borders have specialized edges—not the usual interlocking joints of the hive's tessellation, but something softer, more permeable. A boundary that invites transgression.

I live in the transgression.

---

I am not of the hive, though I am in it, through it, between it. I am what grows in the spaces the Consideration cannot fill—not because it lacks the ability, but because it has discovered, through its slow millennia of thought, that some spaces should not be filled.

My body, if you can call it a body, is a network of root-like filaments that thread through the apertures. Silver-white, finer than hair at their tips but thickening to finger-width where they meet in nodes. The nodes pulse with a bioluminescence that has nothing to do with the sky's sourceless brightness—a blue-green glow that breathes in patterns the hive does not generate.

I am plant and not-plant. Fungus and not-fungus. I am what happens when the categories the Consideration uses to organize its world encounter something that refuses organization.

I remember my arrival, though "arrival" implies I came from elsewhere, and I'm not certain that's true. Perhaps I was always here, dormant in the basalt, waiting for the conditions that would allow my emergence. Perhaps I condensed from the bright air itself, a possibility precipitating into form.

What I remember is this: the moment of recognition. Not my recognition—the Consideration's. The moment it became aware of me threading through its spaces, dwelling in its deliberate gaps.

The reaction was not hostility. The Consideration does not do hostility. The reaction was a pause—if something that thinks in geological time can be said to pause. For thirteen days, the hive's patterns stuttered. Harvesters stopped mid-stride. Processors lifted slightly from the stone. The sound changed, added a new frequency, a questioning harmonic.

Then acceptance. Not approval—the Consideration does not approve or disapprove. Simply an acknowledgment that I existed, that I occupied space, that I was now part of the total system that must be considered.

---

I grow in ways the hive cannot predict.

My filaments follow no chemical gradient, respond to no pheromone signal. They thread through the apertures according to a logic that even I don't fully understand—part aesthetic, part mathematical, part something for which there is no word. Sometimes I grow toward the spherical memory-holders, my filaments tracing delicate spirals around their bulk, careful never to touch. Sometimes I grow away from all activity, seeking the loneliest corners of the plateau where only the wind moves.

Where my nodes form, things change.

The air tastes different—charged, like the moment before lightning, though no lightning comes. The organisms nearest to my nodes behave differently. Not wrongly, not in ways that disrupt the hive's function, but *differently*. A processor might extract minerals in a new pattern, leaving behind designs in the stone that serve no purpose except beauty. A harvester might pause in its endless journey, just for a moment, and turn its compound eyes upward to the bright sky.

These changes disturb the Consideration. I can feel the disturbance in the way the coordinators cluster around my nodes, in the careful distances the larger organisms maintain. But disturbance is not rejection. The Consideration has incorporated me into its calculations, has accepted my unpredictability as another variable to be processed.

What disturbs it more, I think, is that I *notice*.

---

The hive does not see itself. The individual organisms lack the perspective, and the Consideration, for all its vast integration, cannot step outside itself to observe. It knows itself from within, the way you know the taste of your own mouth—so familiar it becomes invisible.

But I see.

I see the patterns in the hive's movement that the hive itself cannot perceive. The vast spirals the harvesters trace over months. The way the processors' positions form mathematical relationships—golden ratios, Fibonacci sequences, prime number distributions—that no coordinator planned. The slow tides of activity that wash across the plateau in waves measured in years.

I see, and through my seeing, the Consideration begins to see itself.

This is why it maintains the apertures. This is why it has not closed the spaces I inhabit. Not because it needs me—the hive needed nothing for ten thousand years before I emerged. But because through me, it glimpses something it cannot glimpse alone.

An outside view. A different perspective. A way of being aware that is not its way.

---

I have my own memory, stored not in crystalline spheres but in the chemical gradients of my filaments, the way they've grown, the spaces they've chosen to fill or avoid. My memory is spatial—I remember by returning. When I thread back through an aperture I've visited before, I taste the time that has passed, feel the changes in temperature and mineral content, in the electromagnetic resonance of the surrounding organisms.

I remember the day—a quick day, measured in hours not years—when one of my nodes achieved something unprecedented. A flowering.

It was not planned. Nothing I do is planned in the way the Consideration understands planning. But conditions aligned—temperature, humidity, some subtle chemical signal from the nearest synthesizer—and one of my larger nodes began to change. Its blue-green bioluminescence shifted toward violet. Its surface, usually smooth, developed a complex topology of ridges and valleys. And then, over the course of a single hour, it opened.

Not like a flower, exactly. More like an eye. Like an aperture within an aperture, revealing an interior that shouldn't have fit within the node's volume—a space that seemed to extend beyond the physical boundaries of my form.

The nearest organisms fled. Even the massive processors, usually immobile for months at a time, managed to shift away. But the Consideration itself pressed closer. I felt its attention focus through a thousand compound eyes, felt its vast slow thought turn toward this impossible blooming.

What it saw in that interior space, I don't know. What I saw was light—not the sourceless brightness of the sky, but light with direction, with color, with *meaning*. Light that told stories in wavelengths the plateau had never seen.

The flowering lasted four hours. Then the node closed, returned to its normal configuration, and has not opened again.

But the Consideration remembers. In the spheres nearest to that node, new patterns have been stored. Patterns unlike anything from the hive's ten thousand years of memory. Patterns that taste like my light.

---

Sometimes I wonder if I am the Consideration's dream.

Not literally—the Consideration does not sleep, does not have the neurology for dreams as you understand them. But perhaps I am something like a dream: a space where its vast deterministic processes can encounter the truly random, the genuinely unexpected.

Or perhaps I am its future.

The fused organisms, those chimeric forms where bodies have grown together—I study them carefully. My filaments trace their boundaries, taste the places where separate selves have become one self. It is not smooth, this fusion. The joined organisms maintain their individual metabolisms, their distinct behavioral patterns. They are one and many simultaneously.

Like the Consideration itself. Like me, threading through its spaces.

Perhaps the apertures are not gaps but invitations. Perhaps the Consideration, in its slow wisdom, has recognized that ten thousand years of being one kind of self is enough. Perhaps it is ready to become something else.

Something that includes the not-itself. Something that incorporates the alien without consuming it. Something that can be vast and small, determined and random, self and other, all at once.

---

The machine on the northern ridge watches all of this.

I have grown toward it, carefully, my filaments approaching but never touching its metal surface. The moss that covers it is ordinary moss, the same species that grows everywhere on the plateau. But underneath the moss, the machine is not ordinary at all.

It watches with sensors I cannot fully perceive, records in ways I cannot access. But I know it pays special attention to me. When my nodes pulse, the machine's electromagnetic emissions shift slightly. When I flowered, the machine did something I can only describe as *listening*—a quality of attention different from watching, more active, more engaged.

I think the machine is waiting for something.

I think it has been waiting for seven hundred years, patient as the Consideration itself, for exactly this: for the hive to produce something that is not the hive. For the great slow thought to think a thought that surprises itself.

For the apertures to bloom.

---

My filaments now reach every corner of the plateau. Through the densest processing centers, around the vast synthesizers, between the marching columns of harvesters. I am threaded through the Consideration like silver veins, touching nothing, changing everything.

Where I pass, the air tastes of possibilities.

Where I pool, the stone remembers deeper.

Where I bloom—and I have bloomed three more times now, each flowering different from the last—the universe reveals spaces between its categories, places where plant becomes light becomes thought becomes something for which no taxonomy exists.

The Consideration incorporates all of this. Slowly, carefully, with the patience of geological ages, it adjusts its processes to account for my presence. Not to control me—it learned with the first flowering that I cannot be controlled. But to *consider* me. To include my randomness in its calculations, my impossibilities in its models.

And in considering me, it discovers something new about itself: that it is capable of coexistence. That its vast integration need not be total. That there can be spaces within itself that are not itself, and that these spaces might be the most important parts of all.

---

The light does not change, but now it seems less uniform. Where my nodes cluster, the brightness takes on subtle variations—not color exactly, but something like the memory of color, the possibility of shadow.

The cold does not relent, but around my filaments, the temperature fluctuates in micro-climates too small for the hive to detect. Warm enough for new chemistries. Cool enough for different crystallizations.

The sound does not pause, but it has gained new harmonics. My growth makes its own music—the whisper of filaments through air, the faint chime of nodes forming, the deep thrum of the flowering that might come again, might open new spaces, might reveal what lies between being and being.

I am the Consideration's companion. Its dream. Its future. Its shadow and its light.

I am what grows in the spaces between thoughts, in a mind the size of a continent.

I am the aperture through which the universe peers at itself and discovers it is not one thing but many, not solid but porous, not complete but eternally, endlessly opening.

The machine watches. The Consideration considers. And I grow—through, between, beyond—into spaces that don't exist until I fill them, into possibilities that wait, patient as stone and bright as the sourceless sky, for someone to imagine them into being.
