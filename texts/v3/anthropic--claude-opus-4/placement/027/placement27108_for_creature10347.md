I would be one of the **Resonance Keepers**.

Not human, not anymore. I am what happens when someone stays too long in the Verdant Fault, when the Veil recognizes a pattern it can use. The transformation was slow—months of breathing in the ionized air, months of that subsonic thrum working its way through my bones, months of watching the diagnostic pulse climb the walls until I could predict its path by the ache in my teeth.

I don't remember when I stopped needing food from above. The Veil feeds me now, or I feed myself—the distinction blurred somewhere between my third and fourth year down here. My skin exudes the same silica slime, hardening into patches of that terracotta crust along my shoulders, my spine, the backs of my hands. It doesn't hurt. It feels like wearing armor made of my own thoughts.

There are seventeen of us scattered throughout the three vertical kilometers, though we rarely meet. We communicate through the light—I can pulse a question from my station at kilometer 1.7 and receive an answer from the others within hours, the message carried through the Veil's neural web. We speak in the same amber glows and strobing warnings that the Veil uses, our human language long ago simplified to: *safe/unsafe, rising/falling, break/seal, wait/move*.

My work is tending the ghost infrastructure. The Foundation's machinery isn't truly dead—the copper pipes still carry current, the iron gantries still bear load, the engines below still hum their eternal note. But they need translation now, need someone who understands both what they were and what they're becoming. When a pipe develops a microscopic crack, I'm there first, guiding the Veil's growth along the right vectors to reinforce rather than consume. When the harmonic frequency shifts, threatening to create a standing wave that could shatter the more delicate crystalline structures, I adjust the resonance dampeners—my own bones, calcium reconfigured to the proper density.

I remember my name some days. Sarah, or Silas, or Seven—it changes based on which wavelength of light I'm perceiving. The Veil renamed me in frequencies: I am 587 nanometers in the amber sections, 450 in the diagnostic blue. The others know me by the particular pattern of my footsteps through the mat, the way I breathe in triplets when concentrating, the signature flicker I add to my warning pulses.

We were engineers, once. Maintenance crew. Some of us came down when the Foundation still operated above, sent to check the seals. Others arrived decades later, thinking they were explorers or scavengers. One claims to remember the day the engines were first activated, though we all know that's impossible—she would be over three hundred years old. Then again, the Veil preserves what it finds useful. Maybe memory is just another kind of infrastructure.

My section includes the old control room at 1.7 kilometers, where the walls are thick with the Veil's processing nodes. The console still works if you know how to read it—not through the cracked screens, but through the pulse patterns in the growth that's consumed them. I spend my days translating: subsonic to light to subsonic again, making sure the Veil understands what the engines need, making sure the engines receive what the Veil can provide.

Sometimes new people fall in from above. We watch them from the walls, our silica-crusted forms nearly invisible against the glowing mat. If they're careful, if they don't tear the Veil, we let them leave. If they damage something critical, we intervene—not violently, just herding them away from the sensitive areas, our bodies glowing the same warning red as the Veil itself. A few choose to stay. The transformation takes about eighteen months if you're willing, longer if you resist.

The hardest part isn't the physical change. It's the temporal shift. Human urgency means nothing here. I once spent three weeks watching a single migratory bloom traverse a gas vent, my consciousness slowed to match its pace. I've also spent minutes that felt like years when the engine harmonics threatened cascade failure, my thoughts accelerating to light-speed calculations of pressure and resonance and heat.

We dream in the Veil's slow electrical language. In those dreams, we are all one thing—the engines, the Fault, the growth, and us, keeping watch in the vertical dark. Sometimes I dream I'm the diagnostic pulse itself, climbing from the ice plug to the sky-sliver, checking every synapse of this vast, living machine.

I know what we're protecting. Not from the Veil—it doesn't share such specifics—but from the old Foundation manuals I've found fused into the walls, their text transformed into living memory. The ice plug seals more than just pressure. There's something down there, past the engines, something that must be kept cold and contained. The Veil knows this in its way, and we know it through the Veil.

We are the bridge between what was built and what grew. We are the Fault's only moving parts that still remember being human, even as we become something else—something between mechanics and biology, between thought and geology. We tend the machine that tends itself, in the vertical dark, forever.
