In this world of suspended time and patient endurance, I would find myself as one of **The Echo Keepers** - beings that exist in the negative space between the stone pillars and the mist.

We are not quite sound, not quite silence, but something that lives in the gap between them. Our forms are suggestions rather than certainties - a shimmer in the air where the amber light bends wrong, a density in the atmosphere that makes the mist pause and eddy around nothing visible. We move through the hollow like memories move through a dreaming mind, leaving no footprints in the clay but somehow changing the way the light falls afterward.

Our purpose is to preserve the hollow's unspoken stories. When the wind rustles through the dry grasses, we catch the almost-words it carries and weave them into the spaces between the polygonal cracks. When water drips from a pool's edge, we follow the sound down into the earth, tracing the ghost rivers that still flow in the clay's deep memory. We are librarians of absence, archivists of the might-have-been.

Our bodies, if they can be called that, are made of compressed stillness - the same substance that keeps the stones from crumbling and the pools from evaporating. We appear most clearly at the boundaries: where wet meets dry, where the salt crust gives way to bare earth, where the ancient walls end and the open clay begins. In these liminal spaces, we become almost tangible, our forms rippling like heat mirages but cooler, denser, carrying the weight of accumulated quiet.

We communicate through resonance. When the mist-entities whisper their half-remembered secrets, we amplify certain frequencies and dampen others, shaping their vapor-words into something approaching meaning. With the salt-scribes, we share a different language - one of crystalline patterns and chemical echoes. We can read their mineral inscriptions not through sight but through a kind of pressure-sensing, feeling how their crystal lattices disturb the air above them.

Our relationship with time is peculiar. We exist in the pause between moments, in the hollow's eternal present, yet we remember everything that almost happened here. Every potential flood that never came, every seed that nearly sprouted, every stone that considered rolling but chose stillness instead. We are the hollow's contingency, its library of parallel histories that run alongside the single, still story it has chosen to tell.

At dawn, when the mist rises thickest, we gather near the central pillar. Its weathered symbols, illegible to eyes, speak clearly to us - not of what they once meant, but of all the meanings they've shed like snakeskin over the eons. We press our indefinite forms against the warm stone and feel the lichen writing its slow autobiography across our surfaces. In these moments, we are most ourselves: neither here nor there, neither memory nor matter, but something essential to the hollow's perfect incompleteness.

We feed, if it can be called feeding, on the tension between permanence and decay. Every crack in the clay that doesn't widen, every drop of moisture that doesn't evaporate, every moment that passes without changing anything - these are our sustenance. We grow stronger in the spaces where time forgets to flow, where the universe's tendency toward entropy meets the hollow's stubborn refusal to participate.

Some might say we are ghosts, but ghosts belong to the past. We belong to the perpetual almost-present, the moment just before something might happen but doesn't. We are the hollow's insurance against change, its guardians of the eternal maybe. In a place that has finished its story, we are the ellipsis at the end of the final sentence, ensuring that the book never quite closes, that the last page trembles forever on the edge of turning.
