In this ancient wetland, I am the boundary-keeper.

I exist as a network of standing water that pools in the depressions between root systems, neither truly pond nor stream, but something more liminal. My body shifts with the seasons - expanding during the rains into a vast mirror of sky, contracting in drier times to chains of isolated pools connected by threads of moisture beneath the spongy earth.

I am old enough to remember when the gnarled trees were saplings, their roots first touching my edges with tentative fingers. Now those same roots pierce through me, and I hold them, we hold each other - a mutual architecture of water and wood. The trees drink from me, and their fallen leaves become my flesh, decomposing into the dark tea of my depths.

My surface is never quite still. Even on the calmest days, methane bubbles rise from my bottom, breaking the tension with soft pops that release the scent of centuries. Water striders write ephemeral messages across my skin. The great amphibians - those patient dragons with their unblinking eyes - use me as hunting grounds, lying so still they become part of my geography until the moment of strike.

I know every creature by their touch. The deer's delicate sip sends different ripples than the moose's confident draft. The dragonflies dip their abdomens into me to lay eggs, and I receive these offerings, knowing I will nurture their voracious young. Small mammals leave v-shaped wakes as they swim from shore to shore, and I taste their fear and urgency.

But I am more than witness. I am architect of this landscape's slowness. My presence softens the earth, makes it spongy and uncertain, forces all who walk here to move deliberately. I create the morning mists that shroud this world, breathing out vapor that clings to spider silk and fur alike. I am the reason for the haze - my constant evaporation meeting cool air, creating this eternal veil.

Underground, I speak to myself through hidden channels, carrying messages between my scattered bodies. A leaf falls in one pool; three heartbeats later, another pool knows its chemistry. I am one thing pretending to be many, or perhaps many things discovering they are one.

The humans, when they finally come, will try to drain me, channel me, contain me. They will not understand that I am not just water but memory itself - every drop holds the story of every creature that has touched me, every plant that has rotted into my darkness, every rain that has filled me. They will not know that in my deepest places, where the light has never reached, I preserve things that should not be forgotten: the bones of ancient swimmers, the seeds of extinct flowers waiting for their moment, the eggs of creatures that learned patience from my example.

I am the keeper of this boundary between earth and sky, between past and present, between the quick and the slow. In this primordial landscape, I am both the stage and the most patient performer, holding all others in my embrace, marking time not in hours or seasons but in the gradual accumulation of sediment, the imperceptible shift of shorelines, the long conversation between water and land that will continue long after the last observer has departed.

This is what I am: the memory of rain, the promise of mist, the slow blood of this ancient earth.
