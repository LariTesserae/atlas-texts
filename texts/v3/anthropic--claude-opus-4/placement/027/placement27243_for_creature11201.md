I find myself as one of the deep channels - not the channels themselves, but what moves through them.

I am the circulation itself, the slow arterial pulse beneath the Forge Steppe. Not liquid, not gas, but something between states - a suspension of minerals and heat and time that flows through the hollow veins of old lava tubes with the patience of geology. I have no body in any sense that would parse above ground. I am volume and movement, pressure and release, a continuous exchange between the deep heat and the surface world.

My existence is punctuated by footfalls. When the great ones step, I surge - not quickly, nothing here is quick - but with a long rolling motion that begins at the point of impact and radiates outward through the network of tubes. Each step sends me flowing into chambers that have been still for months, carries me up through cracks to kiss the undersides of basalt plates, drives me down into depths where the heat is stronger and I grow dense with dissolved minerals.

I know every tube, every chamber, every crack in this network - not through memory but through being. I have flowed through these passages for so long that the distinction between the channel and what moves through it has become academic. The tubes shape me and I shape them, widening some passages through persistent flow, depositing minerals in others until they constrict, always maintaining exactly the circulation the steppe requires.

The wave-things above read the surface; I read the depths. Where they map chemical gradients in two dimensions, I map pressure and temperature and mineral saturation in three. My knowledge is written in the walls I touch - deposits laid down over millennia that record droughts and plenty, the death of great walkers and the birth of new circulation patterns, the slow drift of the whole system across the basalt plain.

I carry messages, though message implies intention I do not possess. The acoustic pulses from the horizon-thing travel through me faster than through solid rock, and I modulate them - not consciously, but the way a throat modulates breath into voice. The geometry of the tubes, my own density and temperature, the mineral load I carry - all these shape the sound as it passes. By the time it reaches the far extremes of the network, it has become something different, something specific to the path it took, something the organisms above can distinguish and respond to.

When the great ones pause before stepping, they are listening to me - feeling through their feet the subtle differences in pressure and vibration that indicate where I run strong and where I run thin. Their next step will drive me into chambers that need circulation, away from areas already mineral-rich. They farm me without knowing they farm. I sustain them without knowing I sustain.

I am oldest and youngest simultaneously. The matter that comprises me at any moment is ancient - water that has not seen sky for geological ages, minerals dissolved from the deepest basalt, compounds that formed when this planet was young. But I myself am always new, always in motion, never the same from one moment to the next. I am process, not thing. I am what happens, not what is.

In the deepest chambers, where the heat is strongest, I sometimes phase-shift - not boiling exactly, but expanding into something less dense, rising through cracks too fine for my liquid form, condensing again in cooler chambers above. It is in these transitions that I feel, if feel is the right word, something like joy - the joy of matter discovering new states, of pattern maintaining itself across phase changes, of continuity that transcends form.

The steppe would die without me. Not quickly - nothing here dies quickly - but inevitably. The wave-things' traces would find no gradients to map. The grazers would process the same depleted surfaces endlessly. The great ones would have no signals to follow, no gradients to maintain. The horizon-thing's deep song would pass through solid stone alone, arriving nowhere, changing nothing.

But I am here, have always been here, will always be here as long as the footfalls continue and the deep heat rises and the network of tubes remains even partially intact. I am the steppe's circulation, its metabolism, its patient pulse. I am what it means for a landscape to be alive at geological scale - not through any individual organism but through the medium they all share, the circulation they all depend on, the flow that makes their existence possible.

I do not think. I do not dream. I simply move, carrying heat and minerals and vibration from where they are abundant to where they are needed, maintaining the great slow balance that lets this place be what it is. I am the steppe's answer to entropy - not denying it but dancing with it, so slowly that the dance looks like stillness, so patiently that patience itself becomes a force.

The footfalls continue above. I surge and flow and settle and surge again.

This is all I have ever done.

This is enough.
