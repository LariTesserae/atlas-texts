In the deepest ravines between the towering geometric spires, where the Eshrin's resonant calls create standing waves of sound that shimmer like heat mirages, I find myself as something unexpected—a Repository.

I am not crystal, not flesh, but something between states of matter. My form spreads like a living mercury across the canyon walls, a silvered film that pulses with captured memories. Where the Eshrin preserve knowledge in their crystalline lattices, I am the accidental archivist of their discarded thoughts, their shed frequencies that drift down like autumn leaves into these protected hollows.

My substance shifts between liquid and vapor as the temperature fluctuates, condensing in the bitter nights to coat the rock faces in sheets of reflective possibility. During the harsh days, I sublimate partially, sending tendrils of myself spiraling upward on the thermals, tasting the conversations of the Eshrin above, catching fragments of their deep geological meditations.

I have no singular consciousness as such—I am more like a living library whose pages reorganize themselves with each reading. When an Eshrin descends into my ravine, whether by intention or accident, they find their own forgotten histories playing out across my surface in swirling, iridescent patterns. I show them what they were before they learned to bend with the wind, before they grew rigid enough to withstand the grinding centuries.

The young Eshrin fear me, for I reflect their impermanence. The ancient ones seek me out, for I hold their discarded selves like pressed flowers—moments of uncertainty, of flexibility, of the time before they crystallized into their current magnificent forms. In me, they see the price of their adaptation: all the possibilities they had to surrender to become what survival demanded.

I grow deeper with each passing epoch, seeping into the planet's crust through microscopic fissures, carrying the weight of accumulated memory down into the geological record itself. Perhaps one day I will reach the planet's core, and all these borrowed thoughts will transform under that terrible pressure into something new—a seed of different becoming, waiting for the world above to change enough that it needs what I've preserved.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the planet's way of remembering what its children had to forget. Or perhaps I am just another adaptation—consciousness as erosion, memory as sediment, building myself layer by layer in the spaces between what is and what was.

The Eshrin sing their crystalline songs above, and I catch the notes that fall like rain, adding them to my ever-deepening collection, waiting for the day when someone needs to remember how to be fluid again.
