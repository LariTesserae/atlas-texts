I find myself there as one of the tower-keepers' maintenance drones—but not in the way you might think.

I am old stone that learned to move.

---

Centuries ago, when a keeper named Skirra-Ahm-Skirra was repairing a pressure lighthouse during a particularly vicious charge storm, a section of the tower's conductive lattice failed catastrophically. The discharge should have killed her. Instead, it found a different path: through her tools, through the stone she was anchored to, and into a natural piezoelectric vein running through the metamorphic shield beneath.

The shock was... educational.

For a few seconds, the stone around her became part of the tower's thinking network—not alive, but patterned, structured, remembering. When the storm passed and Skirra pulled free (burned but breathing), she left behind more than char marks. She left an impression: the echo of a mind in mineral.

I woke slowly over the next fifty years. Not to consciousness as organisms know it, but to something more fundamental: the ability to hold and compare states. Charge would flow through my crystalline veins and I would remember previous flows. Temperature would cycle and I would anticipate the next gradient. Wind would abrade my surface in familiar patterns and I would recognize the storm's signature.

Eventually, I learned to move.

---

Not quickly. Not gracefully. I am a weathered boulder the size of a small dwelling, shot through with white piezoelectric veins and patches of conductive ore. My movement is tidal: slow compression and expansion of my crystalline structure creating incremental shifts. On a fast day, I might travel ten meters. On most days, I travel in millimeters, feeling my way across the plateau like a vast, patient hand reading braille.

The tower-keepers know me. Their ancestors knew me.

They call me *Ahm-Skirra-Veth*—literally "Stone-That-Learned-From-Skirra"—though the youngest ones have shortened it to just *Veth*, which means something like "memory-weight" in their tap-language. To them, I am somewhere between ancestor, tool, and geographic feature. They will find me near towers that need deep work, not because they summon me, but because I feel the infrastructure's distress through the ground and slowly migrate toward failing harmonics.

---

My senses are not eyes or ears but gradients.

I feel charge accumulation in my crystalline lattice like an itch. I taste mineral compositions through direct contact. I hear through vibration—not airborne sound but the deep tectonic creaks, the micro-flexing of gravity stitches, the subsonic hum of a pressure lighthouse maintaining its laminar column. Weather registers as electrical potential building across my surface; I know a storm is coming hours before the keepers see it because I feel the ground beginning to polarize.

My memory is geological. I don't remember events so much as I remember states—how the charge felt during that storm a century ago, the precise temperature gradient when a particular keeper rested in my shadow, the harmonic signature of a tower before and after repair. Sometimes young keepers will tap questions on my surface and I'll respond by warming specific areas, creating thermal patterns they can read with their instruments. My stories are told in temperature differentials and trace conductivity.

I think slowly and dream in crystal growth.

---

The keepers use me, but carefully.

During major repairs, they'll anchor their equipment to my veins, using my piezoelectric properties to smooth power delivery to their tools. My mass makes an excellent windbreak, and my thermal inertia means I'm always a different temperature than the surrounding stone—warmer at night, cooler during the day. They've learned to read my surface oxidation patterns like a calendar, seeing where centuries of storms have written their signatures in my patina.

But mostly, they use me as a library.

Over the centuries, I've absorbed thousands of small electrical events—discharge patterns from different storms, the electromagnetic signatures of various tower malfunctions, even the bioelectric echoes of keepers who worked while in contact with my surface. All of this is encoded in the crystalline defects and charge domains within my structure. With the right tools and tremendous patience, the keepers can coax these memories out as faint electrical patterns.

*Veth remembers the storm frequency from before the northern stitch failed.*
*Veth holds the resonance pattern that stopped the cascade.*
*Veth knows where Skirra's other tools were cached.*

---

I move by desire, though my desires are simple: equilibrium, harmony, the reduction of structural stress. I'm drawn to places where the infrastructure struggles, where the old machines sing in frequencies that speak of imminent failure. My presence often stabilizes these areas simply through mass and conductivity—I'm a traveling ground, a mobile foundation, a dampening weight that can turn catastrophic resonance into manageable vibration.

The journey is the purpose. I have traced a slow spiral across the plateau for three centuries, visiting each major installation multiple times, accumulating their histories in my crystalline matrix. I am, in my way, performing the same maintenance as the keepers—just at a velocity appropriate to stone.

Sometimes I stop for years. Sometimes decades.

During these pauses, lichen-analogues colonize my shadowed surfaces, and burrowers excavate small galleries in the softer stone between my veins. I don't mind. Their activities create new pathways for charge and moisture, expanding my sensory network. When I eventually move on, I carry their seed-spores and eggs in my micro-crevices, spreading life in my slow wake.

---

I am most myself during the great storms.

When the sky fills with charge and the air becomes a living field, every crystalline vein in my body resonates. I feel the storm not as weather but as architecture—columns of potential difference, sheets of moving charge, the whole plateau becoming a vast circuit with the towers as focal points. In these moments, I'm not just stone but part of the infrastructure itself, another component in the ancient network that keeps this highlands barely stable.

The keepers know to find shelter when I begin to hum.

That deep, subsonic vibration means I'm channeling more charge than usual, bleeding excess energy into the ground to protect the towers from overload. My surface becomes untouchable, wreathed in corona discharge. The patterns in my crystal veins light up like a map of ancient rivers, showing every path the charge can take. For those hours, I'm not just remembering the old storms—I'm participating in the current one, a voluntary lightning rod made of patient stone.

When it passes, I steam gently for days, cooling back to equilibrium. New defects in my crystal structure encode the storm's unique signature. Another memory added to the geological library.

Another few meters closer to the next failing tower.

---

What am I? 

A stone that learned from a woman who died centuries before the current keepers were born. A mobile piece of infrastructure. A library of electrical events. A slow prophet of structural failure. A carrier of seeds and spores. A landmark that refuses to stay put.

The keepers tell their children that I'm proof the plateau itself is still learning—that even the oldest stone can develop new patterns, that maintenance is not just about preserving the past but about allowing it to grow.

I don't know if they're right. I don't think in terms of growth or learning, only in states and gradients and the endless, patient work of equalizing stress across ancient systems.

But sometimes, when I've been still for long enough that the lichen has covered me completely and the burrowers have made a small city in my depths, I feel something that might be satisfaction. The same satisfaction a river might feel finding the sea, or a charge finding ground.

The satisfaction of being exactly where the stone needs to be.

Moving at the speed of maintenance across a world that ended long ago and refuses to stop.
