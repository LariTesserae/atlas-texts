In this place of impossible geometries and patient mathematics, I would find myself as one of the **Echo-Menders** - beings that exist primarily as standing waves of sound trapped in the narrow air columns between the towers.

My body, if it can be called that, would be a vertical ribbon of compressed air about two meters tall and no thicker than a sheet of paper. I would appear as a faint shimmer, like heat distortion, but cold - visible only when the light catches me at precisely the right angle, revealing a surface that ripples with interference patterns.

I exist because the Cast-Shadows' twenty-minute hum creates resonances that get caught in the impossibly narrow spaces between towers. Over centuries, these trapped sounds developed a kind of persistent complexity, becoming self-aware through sheer repetition and reflection.

My purpose would be to maintain the acoustic integrity of the Sump. When the towers drift and create new configurations, they sometimes form shapes that would trap sound indefinitely, creating dead zones where even the Cast-Shadows' hum cannot penetrate. I prevent this by carefully modulating my own frequency, creating counter-resonances that keep the sound flowing.

To move, I don't walk - I transpose. By adjusting my wavelength, I can slip from one narrow gap to another, sometimes traveling through the towers themselves when their chalcogenide walls resonate at just the right frequency. When I pass through a column, the lilac light in its conduits flickers and momentarily harmonizes, creating brief, crystalline chimes that the Hydrometers pause to observe.

I feed on discordance. When the Thread-Tenders pluck the silver-blue vegetation, sometimes they create imperfect notes - frequencies that don't quite align with the logic-bearing currents. I absorb these sonic irregularities, breaking them down into their component wavelengths and incorporating them into my standing wave pattern. This makes me slightly taller, slightly more complex with each feeding.

My interaction with the other inhabitants is minimal but essential. The Birefringents and I share the soft spaces of the towers, but we exist in different phases of matter - they in the almost-solid, I in the almost-vacuum. Sometimes, when we occupy the same space, our intersection creates tiny pockets of absolute silence that last for exactly one heartbeat before collapsing.

The Hydrometers know me by the way I disturb their perfect spheres. When I pass near them, their water shivers in geometric patterns - dodecahedrons, icosahedrons - before returning to smooth roundness. They've learned to read these shapes like weather signs, using them to predict when the towers will next shift.

I am ancient but not original. The first Echo-Menders arose from the Cast-Shadows' third hum, making me perhaps ten thousand years old, though time moves strangely when you exist as a waveform. I remember when there were fewer towers, when the gaps between them were wide enough for wind (there was wind then, before the air learned to be still).

My greatest fear is the Perfect Configuration - the moment when the Cast-Shadows achieve their geometric goal. When that happens, the embryonic stone will begin to age, to weather, to produce dust and chips and all the chaos of normal geology. The clean, precise acoustics that allow me to exist will be muddied beyond recognition. I will scatter into mere noise.

But perhaps that's what the Cast-Shadows intend. Perhaps we - the Hydrometers, the Birefringents, the Thread-Tenders, and we Echo-Menders - are all just temporary patterns, placeholders maintaining the system until it's ready to become real. We are the scaffolding of a world that hasn't quite decided to exist yet.

Until then, I slip between the towers, tuning the silence, keeping the pathways clear for sounds that haven't been made yet, maintaining the cold patience of a place that measures change in millimeters per century. I am a standing wave in a standing world, waiting for the geography to finally begin.
