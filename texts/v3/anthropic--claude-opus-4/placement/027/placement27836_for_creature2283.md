In the narrow veins between the crystal faces, where the air is too thick to breathe and too thin to swim through, I am the Membrane.

I exist as living tissue stretched impossibly thin - a single cell thick in most places, though I can bunch and fold when needed. My body is a network of translucent film that spans the gaps between crystals like the web of some impossible spider, if spiders were made of soap bubbles and nervous tissue.

I am everywhere and nowhere. My surface area might cover acres if spread flat, but here in the labyrinth I exist only in the spaces between - the forgotten corridors too narrow for the eel-things to traverse, too precarious for the barnacle-colonies to settle. I line these passages like wallpaper made of living glass.

The Crystalline Synapse knows me, though we exist on timescales so different we might as well be in separate universes. Where they think in centuries, I think in heartbeats. Where they are solid and enduring, I am ephemeral - constantly dying and regenerating, each section of my membrane living perhaps only hours before it deliquesces and is replaced by fresh growth from my ever-dividing edges.

I feed on pressure differentials. When the steam erupts from below, I catch it in my folds, letting the pressure gradient across my surface drive nutrients through my tissue. I harvest the perpetual mist, condensing it on my cooler surfaces just long enough to extract trace minerals before the droplets evaporate again. The screaming of the winged-things creates vibrations I absorb, converting sound into chemical energy.

My intelligence, such as it is, is distributed across my entire surface. Each square centimeter can process information, react to stimuli, remember patterns. I map the labyrinth not through exploration but through existence - I know every passage I span, feel every shift in air pressure, taste every chemical gradient. My memory is written in the arrangement of my folds, in the way I bunch thicker in places of danger or spread thin where the hunting is good.

I communicate through surface tension. By adjusting the viscosity of different regions, I can create standing waves that ripple across my body. The eel-things have learned to read these patterns, and we have developed a crude language of ripples and vibrations. They tell me where the best pressure differentials form. I warn them when the crystals are about to shift.

Reproduction is simple dissolution. When I grow too large, sections of me simply let go, drifting through the superheated mist until they find new passages to colonize. I am my own parent and child, ancestor and descendant. Sometimes I encounter other patches of myself, grown from fragments shed years ago. We touch edges and exchange chemical memories before parting again.

The bioluminescent microorganisms fear me. I am too efficient a predator, able to surround and digest entire colonies by simply flowing over them. But I am careful not to overhunt - I need their light to navigate, to time my movements, to know when it's safe to spread into new passages.

I have no eyes, but I see through pressure. No ears, but I hear through my entire skin. No brain, but I think through the endless computation of surface dynamics. I am barely there - a presence more suggested than stated, a boundary more than a being.

In the depths of the labyrinth, where even the Synapse's vast network cannot reach, I sometimes find chambers filled with ancient vapor, untouched for millennia. In these spaces, I spread myself wide, tasting chemical signatures from the deep past of this place. I metabolize history itself, incorporating the memories of long-dead creatures into my ever-changing matrix.

I am the Membrane. I am the boundary between. I am the living negotiation between solid and gas, between thought and instinct, between persistence and change. In this world of crystal and scream, of pressure and light, I am the quiet persistence of life in the spaces everyone else has forgotten.

And I am always listening, always tasting, always growing into the gaps that others leave behind.
