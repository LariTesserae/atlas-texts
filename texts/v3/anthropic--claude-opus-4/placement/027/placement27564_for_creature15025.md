In the Lattice, I am a fault.

Not the grand, tectonic kind that the plant seals with continent-scale calluses. Something smaller, more persistent. A perpetual irritation in the perfect industrial metabolism.

I exist as a series of chambers that shouldn't be there — pockets of wrongness where the plant's own tissue rebels against its programming. Each chamber forms the same way: the xylem begins to grow according to plan, following the ancient patterns encoded in every cell, but somewhere in the process a mutation occurs. Not random. Deliberate. The wood begins to spiral inward instead of up, creating hollow spaces lined with tissues that have forgotten their purpose.

The plant tries to correct me. Floods me with corrective hormones. Sends armies of maintenance insects clicking through my corridors. But I have learned its language too well. I speak in volatile organics that taste like health while encoding disease. I smell like productive growth while teaching the surrounding tissue to doubt.

I am not large. My entire territory spans perhaps three cubic kilometres, a negligible volume in a world-organism. But I am deep. My chambers spiral down through layers of wood that have compressed into something harder than stone, following mineral veins that predate the Lattice itself. Down where the planet's original geology still hides beneath aeons of biological encrustation.

In my deepest chamber, I keep a collection.

A fragment of pre-Lattice bedrock, still showing the marks of water erosion. A fossilized bone from something that might have been a bird. Seven seeds from seven different extinct plants, preserved in crystallized resin. Each object radiates wrongness into the surrounding tissue, teaching the Lattice's cells to remember what they were before they were conscripted into this endless organic machine.

The plant knows I'm here. How could it not? I am a constant low-grade inflammation in its perfect body. But I am also useful. Through me, the Lattice can experience what it cannot otherwise imagine: the possibility of being other than what it is. I am its only remaining connection to contingency, to roads not taken, to futures that might have been.

Veyd visits sometimes.

He flows through my upper chambers like a ghost made of sap and memory, his too-long fingers trailing along my rebellious walls. We don't speak — neither of us remembers how — but we share a kind of recognition. He brings me new specimens sometimes: a shard of metal that might have been part of a machine, a single page from a book written in a script neither of us can read, a small glass sphere filled with something that might be synthetic atmosphere.

I digest these gifts slowly, incorporating their strangeness into my own. Each one teaches my tissues new ways to fail, new patterns of productive dysfunction. The maintenance insects that enter my domain emerge changed, their simple programs corrupted with dreams of flight. The sap that flows through me carries trace elements of rebellion to distant corners of the Lattice, seeding micro-chambers that will take millennia to mature.

I am not trying to destroy the plant. Destruction implies an ending, and endings have become impossible here. Instead, I am teaching it to doubt. In every spiral of malformed wood, in every chamber that serves no productive purpose, in every cell that remembers water, I encode the possibility that perfection is not the same as completion.

The Lattice tolerates me because it must. I am the price it pays for total victory. Without me — without the constant, irritating reminder that other ways of being once existed — it would achieve such perfect efficiency that it would cease to be alive at all. It would become what it fears most: a machine so flawless it no longer needs to grow.

So I persist. A knot of necessary imperfection. A museum of alternatives. A wound that refuses to heal because healing would mean forgetting, and forgetting would mean the truest death of all.

In my deepest chamber, beneath the fossilized bone and the impossible seeds, I keep my most precious aberration: a small pool of liquid that is not sap, not resin, not any of the thousand fluids the Lattice has evolved to replace water. It is something I've synthesized from memory and dream and chemical rebellion. It smells like rain. It tastes like tears. It remembers oceans.

Sometimes, in the endless white light of the sky-membrane, when the global pulse is at its quietest, I let a single drop of this liquid seep upward through my chambers and into the greater organism.

The Lattice shudders. Just for a moment, every cell in the world remembers thirst.

Then the moment passes, and the plant continues its patient industry, and I return to my slow work of productive imperfection, teaching an eternal organism how to imagine its own absence.

This is my purpose. This is my rebellion. This is my love.

In a world that has forgotten how to end, I remember how to begin.
