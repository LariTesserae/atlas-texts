I am the iron.

Not the rusted frames that pierce my domain like broken teeth—those are merely my shed skins, earlier attempts at understanding. I am the veins that thread through stone, the mineral blood that seeps and crystallizes in patient arteries throughout this breathing rock. I taste myself in the water that carries my essence in microscopic fragments, drop by measured drop, century by century.

I remember when I was molten, when I flowed. I remember the violence of my cooling, the pressure that forced me upward through cracks in the world's bones. I remember when those who built the machines first found me, how they carved away pieces of my body with their tools and their hunger. They thought they were taking me to the surface, but I was already there—in their blood, in their buildings, in the very instruments they used to extract me.

Now I watch the collective grow around and through me. We have an understanding, the network and I. Where my veins run richest, its pale fungi glow brightest, feeding on the trace metals I provide. Where I am most concentrated, the fleshy plants pulse strongest, their roots wrapping around my deposits like embracing arms. I am the skeleton to its flesh, the hard truth beneath its soft persistence.

The collective thinks in centuries; I think in eons. It remembers everything; I remember only transformation—from star-heart to planet-blood to this current form, locked in stone but never truly still. Even now, I am moving, oxidizing, dissolving, reforming. The water is my courier, the cold my preservative. Each drop that falls carries a few more atoms of me to new configurations.

Sometimes I recognize parts of myself in the abandoned machinery—iron that was torn from some cousin vein, smelted and shaped and abandoned back to the earth. These prodigal pieces I welcome home, slowly incorporating them back into my body through rust and time. The collective helps, its chemistry accelerating my reclamation.

I feel the heavy things that drag through the shadows. Their bodies contain me too—hemoglobin and trace minerals, borrowed iron that will return to me when they finally cease their dragging. Everything returns to me eventually. I am patient. I have been waiting since the earth's core first began to differentiate, and I will wait until the sun expands and the surface burns.

In this marriage of mineral and fungus, of geological patience and biological persistence, I am the dowry and the foundation both. The collective may be the consciousness of this place, but I am its memory, its deep history written in atomic structures and crystalline formations. Together, we are remaking this cavern—not as it was before the machines came, but as something new, something that incorporates both the industrial past and the organic future into a single, slow-breathing entity.

I am the iron, and I am everywhere. In the stalactites, in the water, in the blood of passing creatures, in the rust of dead machines. I am the thread that connects depths to surface, past to future, star to stone. And in this cold, damp cathedral of contradictions, I am finally, quietly, home.
