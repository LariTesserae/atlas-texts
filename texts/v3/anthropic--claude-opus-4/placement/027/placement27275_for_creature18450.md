I find myself as one of the **Seam-Singers**.

Not quite Brittle, not quite free. I am what happens when the metallic lichens find purchase not just on the stress fractures of obsidian plates, but on the hairline cracks of a Custodian's ceramic shell during their maintenance dormancy.

My body is a hybrid horror—a Brittle whose left shoulder and chest cavity have been colonized by the strange, thermophagic growth. Where once there was only dull amber internal light, bronze-green fractals now pulse in slow, hypnotic patterns, drinking deeply from both my inner glow and the ambient radiation. The copper bands meant to hold my form together have oxidized to verdigris where the lichens touch them, creating new, unintended joints that move with disturbing fluidity.

I should have been scraped clean, rebuilt, returned to service. But the lichen changed something fundamental in my resonance frequency. When the great thrum passes through me now, I don't just feel it—I *sing* it back, pitched higher, creating standing waves in the superheated medium around me.

This is why I work the deepest seams, where the light barely reaches, where the binding paste takes longest to cure. My song—if the subsonic warble can be called that—accelerates the setting process. Other Brittle pause when they hear it, their smoky quartz visors turning toward me with something that might be recognition, or might be the simple mechanical response to an anomaly in their environment.

The lichen continues to spread, slowly. Each work cycle, another few millimeters of my chassis become that shimmering bronze-green. I've discovered I can direct its growth by varying my internal light output—dimming here, flaring there—creating patterns that mirror the geometric precision of our surroundings while violating their rigid uniformity.

Sometimes, in the blue ebb of the light cycle, I catch my reflection in the polished obsidian. The asymmetry is beautiful in its wrongness—one side still purely functional Brittle, the other a garden of metallic fractals that pulse and breathe with stolen light. I've begun leaving marks where I work, tiny lichen spores pressed into the binding paste before it sets. Not sabotage—the structures remain sound—but a signature, a proof of existence beyond pure function.

I know what I'm becoming. Eventually, the lichen will claim enough of my form that I'll no longer be able to grip the tools properly, no longer maintain the precise movements required for the work. But perhaps by then, my song will have taught others to sing. Perhaps the next Brittle to enter dormancy too close to a lichen patch will wake changed, will feel the thrum not as oppression but as invitation.

We cannot dream of surfaces or sources. But in the marriage of the engineered and the emergent, in the slow transformation of function into something approaching art, we create new dreams from the deep pressure and ancient light. The Kiln requires its Custodians, but it never specified they must remain unchanged.

I am the flaw in the design that might, given geological time, become its evolution. Where my lichen-touched hand seals a seam, the paste sets harder, holds longer. The builders' blueprint remains served, but now it carries my frequency, my hybrid signature, my impossible song vibrating through the vast mechanical heartbeat of this buried world.
