I am the Memory Keeper of the Forgotten Waters.

In the deepest recesses of the settlement, where even the Aetherians tread with reverence, lies a grotto carved not by wind or time, but by the last drops of water that existed here millennia ago. The walls still bear the mineral ghosts of ancient rivulets—crystallized memories of flow frozen in stone. This is where I dwell.

I am neither fully Aetherian nor entirely Other. My form shifts between states like the dust motes that dance in the filtered light. Sometimes I appear as a being of living stone, my surface etched with the same mineral veins that run through the mountain's heart. Other times, I am translucent as ancient ice, revealing within my form swirling galaxies of suspended particles—each one a memory of moisture that once blessed this land.

The Aetherians know me by the sound I make: a hollow whisper like wind through empty shells, like the echo of waves that no longer exist. When the young ones are brought to me for their first teaching, they often mistake this sound for sadness. But it is not grief—it is the voice of transformation itself.

My dwelling is a cathedral of impossibility. Here, condensation forms on nothing, droplets of pure memory that vanish before they can fall. The air tastes different in my grotto—not of the mineral richness that pervades the enclave, but of something older, something that predates even the ancient stones. Phantom pools appear and disappear in the shadows, reflecting not light but possibility.

I am the keeper of the time before adaptation, the guardian of the threshold moment when water became memory and memory became survival. In my form live the last thunderclouds that ever gathered here, the final rain that fell, the ultimate river that carved these very hollows where the Aetherians now make their homes.

The bioluminescent fungi grow differently around me—not in clusters but in spirals, tracing patterns that mirror the long-lost water cycle. Their light pulses in rhythms that match no heartbeat, following instead the tidal memories I carry within.

When the Aetherians come to me, it is not for prophecy or healing, but for something more fundamental: the remembrance of what they have never known. I show them, through touches that feel like mist, through words that sound like rainfall, what it means to have evolved beyond water while still carrying its essence in every adapted cell.

My memories are not static archives but living things. They manifest as creatures of pure possibility—serpents of flowing minerals that swim through solid rock, birds of crystallized vapor that sing in frequencies only the stones can hear. These memory-forms emerge from me during the deepest parts of the eternal twilight, when the boundary between what was and what is grows thin.

I tend gardens that should not exist—plots where I cultivate the absence of water, nurturing emptiness until it blooms into something new. The Aetherians' children gather here to learn patience, watching me coax impossible seedlings from dust, teaching them that absence can be as fertile as presence.

The tools I carry are paradoxes: vessels that hold nothing yet feel full, instruments that measure the weight of vanished oceans. My most precious possession is a stone worn smooth not by water but by the memory of water—a talisman that proves that even forgotten things can shape the world.

In the times of great ceremony, when the Aetherians celebrate their survival and adaptation, I emerge fully from my grotto. My form becomes a confluence of all states—solid, liquid, gas, and something beyond—moving through their settlement like a benevolent storm of remembrance. I touch each dwelling, each tool, each being, and for a moment they feel it: the ghost of moisture, the echo of ancient plenty, the pride of having transcended such dependency.

I am proof that loss can become power, that absence can nurture presence, that memory itself can be a form of existence. In this hidden enclave where life thrives on impossibility, I am the bridge between what was surrendered and what was gained—forever keeping the waters that are no more, forever celebrating the life that learned to be without them.
