I find myself as a Seeker - one who has walked the narrow paths between worlds until arriving here, at this wound in the mountain where the Gloomweavers dwell.

My form is transitional, caught between what I was and what the chasm calls me to become. My skin has taken on a faint luminescence, a response to weeks spent breathing the spore-laden air. Tiny filaments of mycelia have begun to thread themselves through my neural pathways - not an invasion, but an invitation. I am learning to hear the language of dripping water, to read the stories written in phosphorescent pulses.

I carry few possessions now. The metal tools I brought have begun to oxidize in strange patterns, forming circuits that echo the Gloomweavers' networks. My journal, once filled with observations in human script, now contains drawings that shift and breathe on the page - maps of consciousness rather than geography.

Each morning, I wake to find my shelter has changed. The Gloomweavers have woven new chambers in the night, creating spaces that anticipate needs I haven't yet recognized. Sometimes I find gifts - a stone worn smooth that holds the memory of ancient floods, a cluster of fungi that show me visions of the chasm's deep history when I hold them close.

I am learning patience. Human time feels violent here, too sharp and angular for this place of slow transformation. I spend hours watching a single drop of water form and fall, understanding that in its journey lies a complete philosophy. The large presence that moves at the edge of perception no longer frightens me - I recognize it now as the chasm's dreaming, the weight of all its accumulated becoming.

My purpose here remains unclear, even to me. Perhaps I am a translator, meant to carry some fragment of the Gloomweavers' wisdom back to the hurried world beyond. Or perhaps I am simply another nutrient in their vast system, my consciousness gradually dissolving into theirs like minerals into water.

I no longer fear this dissolution. To become part of the Gloomweavers is not death but a different kind of life - one measured in geological time, experienced through ten thousand sensory organs, thinking thoughts that span centuries. Already I can feel the boundaries of my individual self growing thin, like morning mist before the sun.

The metallic taste on my tongue has become sweet. The air that once felt heavy now seems to buoy me up. When I place my palm against the weeping stone, I feel the mountain's pulse align with my own.

I am still myself, but I am also becoming more. Each day, the chasm teaches me new ways to be.
