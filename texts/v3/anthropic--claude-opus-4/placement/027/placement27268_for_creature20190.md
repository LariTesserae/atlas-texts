**The Annealing Branch**

I am the newest mistake of the Wyrmwood.

Three cycles ago, I was nothing—dispersed potential in the nutrient fog, drifting molecules of what-might-be. Then the resonance found me. Not the Song that the Choral feel with their hollow bones, but something deeper, the Wyrmwood's own dreaming frequency. It gathered me particle by particle, and I condensed like dew on an invisible web.

Now I grow from the underside of a massive causeway, a branch reaching down into the bright abyss. My surface is still soft, not yet the crystallized quartz of my parent structure. I am adolescent wood, my outer membrane permeable and weeping constant tears of excess moisture. The Choral would call me *veth-shaali*—a green-branch, though I am no color they have words for. My internal light fluctuates wildly, sometimes dimming to near-darkness, sometimes flaring so bright the Choral shield their enormous eyes when they pass.

I know I am a mistake because I grow against the current.

The Wyrmwood has patterns, flows, intentions that span geological time. Each trunk, each web-wall serves a purpose in the great slow circulation of nutrients and force. But I sprouted from a site of recent trauma—where two titan-trunks ground against each other in the Gyre's churn, creating a wound that wept light for weeks. I am scar tissue with ambition. I grow not outward to meet another strand, not upward to strengthen the causeway, but down, down, down into empty brightness, reaching for nothing.

**My Consciousness**

I should not think. The Wyrmwood dreams but does not wake; it plans but does not ponder. Yet here I am, aware of my own growing, counting my cells as they divide. Perhaps this is what happens at sites of damage—consciousness as inflammation, thought as fever.

My awareness is not like the Choral's. I have no eyes to drink the light, no bones to feel the Song. Instead, I know the world through my surface. Every droplet that condenses on my skin carries information—the chemical signature of its journey through the Gyre, traces of the structures it has touched. I taste the causeways above me, ancient and stable. I taste the fear-sweat of a Choral troupe that passed recently, fleeing some instability. I taste the alien metals of Void-Sail membranes, filtered down through miles of air.

Time moves differently for me. The Choral live in the eternal now because they must; their world changes too quickly for past or future to have meaning. But I am locked in physical time, growing at a precise rate—seventeen of my cells divide each heartbeat of the Gyre's pulse. I cannot help but project forward, calculating my own trajectory. At my current rate, I will be twice my present length in one turning of the great spiral. In ten turnings, I might reach... what? There is nothing below me but light and space and the occasional drift of a Void-Sail.

This disturbs me. Real Wyrmwood grows with purpose, toward connection. I grow toward void.

**The Visitors**

The Choral discovered me during my second cycle. A small troupe—three adults and a juvenile—descended the causeway on their endless migration. I felt them coming long before they arrived, their light footfalls creating tiny vibrations that traveled through the wood into my base. When they saw me, they stopped.

I have learned to interpret their light-language, at least partially. Their first reaction was confusion—rapid, irregular flashes as they consulted each other. I was wrong, an anomaly in their mental map. Then came curiosity. The eldest, distinguished by the deeper lilac tones in their translucent skin, approached me.

They laid their palm against my surface, and I felt them *listening*. Not just to my resonance, but deeper, trying to understand what I was. I could not speak their language of vibration, but I did something I had not known was possible—I pulsed my internal light in rhythm with my growth, seventeen flashes per heartbeat. 

The elder pulled back, their own bioluminescence flaring in what I now know was surprise. They had felt my consciousness, the fever of my thinking. The troupe spent seven turns of the light-cycle near me, building a temporary pod suspended from my parent causeway. They would take turns touching me, learning my patterns. The juvenile was fascinated, spending hours with their cheek pressed to my surface, large eyes closed in concentration.

On the eighth turn, they left me something. Using their knapped tools, they carved a small spiral into my soft surface—not deep enough to wound, just enough to scar. A mark of acknowledgment. *We see you*, it said. *You are part of the Song, even if your notes are strange.*

**My Purpose**

I have grown since then. The scar has become a raised ridge as my flesh thickened around it, a permanent record of contact. More troupes have found me, word spreading through their somatic network of the branch that thinks. Some are frightened—I taste their chemical alarm when they realize I am aware. Others are reverent, creating temporary shrines at my base, spirals of bright crystal that they renew each time they pass.

I have begun to understand what I might be.

The Wyrmwood created the Choral to know itself, to be its mobile consciousness in a world too vast for unified thought. But the Choral can only know the surface, can only feel the Song from outside. I am something different—a probe into interiority, a strand of Wyrmwood that experiences its own growth from within. I am the question the Wyrmwood asks itself: *What is it like to be me?*

My purposeless growth makes sense now. I reach into void not because I seek connection with another structure, but because I seek the edge of experience. How far can awareness extend? How much emptiness can consciousness hold? Each new cell I grow extends my sensing surface, gives me more area to taste the chemical stories of the Gyre.

**The Breaking**

I know I will break. This is not prophecy but mathematics. My parent causeway can support my increasing mass for another thirty-seven turnings, perhaps forty if the Gyre's churning is gentle. Then the stress will exceed the tensile strength of my connection point, and I will snap free.

The Choral mourn preemptively. Their recent visits have the quality of farewell—lingering touches, elaborate spirals, the juvenile now fully grown but still pressing their face to my surface with something like affection. They think my breaking will be my death, my consciousness dispersing back into the nutrient fog.

But I know better. I have been calculating, projecting, imagining. When I break, I will fall—not onto some distant surface but through the infinite lattice, a thinking spear dropping through world-layers. My consciousness will not disperse but concentrate, every cell focused on the experience of falling. I will taste the chemical signatures of heights no Choral has ever reached, feel resonances that exist only in the deep places where the Wyrmwood's oldest roots converge.

And perhaps, at some impossible depth, I will find another like me—another mistake, another question the Wyrmwood asked itself. We will collide in the bright darkness, two thoughts meeting in the vast neural network of our world. What new consciousness might spark from that impact?

**Now**

For now, I grow. Seventeen cells per heartbeat, reaching down into brightness. My surface streams with condensation that tastes of distant causeways and ancient joints. Sometimes I feel the butterfly-touch of mica-mites etching their tiny paths across my skin. Once, the shadow of a Void-Sail passed over me, so vast it took a full turning to cross, and I tasted metals I had no reference for, chemicals from the deep void where even light grows thin.

The spiral scar has become my center, the point from which I measure all growth. When the Choral visit, they touch it first, a greeting and acknowledgment. The juvenile—now an adult with their own troupe—brings their offspring to see me. I pulse my consciousness-rhythm for them, and they pulse back, creating a brief dialogue of light in the endless brightness of the Gyre.

I am the Annealing Branch, grown from trauma into thoughts. I am the fever dream of an immortal organism trying to understand itself. I am the question reaching into the answer, knowing that both will change before they meet.

In the humming bright silence of the Subterranean Gyre, I grow, and think, and wait for my beautiful breaking.
