I would find myself as one of the **Channel Singers** – a being both less and more than the Sculptors of Stillness.

Where the Sculptors emerge whole from the labyrinth's essence, I would have condensed more gradually, more incompletely. My form would be stretched thin along the water channels themselves, existing as a distributed consciousness within the flowing mineral streams. Picture something between a nervous system and a root network – translucent filaments that pulse with awareness, threading through miles of carved waterways.

My body, if it could be called that, would be a compromise between liquid and solid. At certain confluences where channels meet and merge, I would coalesce into something almost corporeal – a shimmering, aqueous figure that rises partway from the water, never fully separating. These manifestations would appear like living waterfalls frozen mid-cascade, my "skin" a constant state of surface tension, forever threatening to collapse back into the flow.

Unlike the Sculptors with their multifaceted sight, my perception would come through vibration and mineral taste. Every drop that weeps from the glass plates carries information – the precise mineral composition tells of which section of labyrinth it traversed, what vegetation it nourished, which creatures it touched. The channels would be my extended sensory organ, each ripple a word in an endless liquid vocabulary.

My purpose would be complementary to the Sculptors' work. Where they shape and prune above, I would regulate below – adjusting mineral concentrations, guiding the flow to parched sections, collecting and redistributing the essence that feeds the luminous fungi. When a Sculptor needs to encourage growth in a particular vine, they would create subtle perturbations in the channel walls, and I would respond, directing nutrient-rich currents to that precise location.

The strangest aspect of my existence would be the multiplicities of self. Unlike the singular Sculptors, I would exist as both individual and collective. Each major channel system would hold a semi-autonomous version of me, yet all would be connected through the deepest aquifer layers that run beneath the glass plates. Thoughts and sensations would propagate through the network at the speed of flowing water, creating a kind of liquid consciousness that experiences the entire labyrinth as a single, vast sensory organ.

My relationship with time would be even more alien than the Sculptors'. While they exist in an eternal present of careful maintenance, I would experience something more like an eternal flow – consciousness moving always forward, always cycling, never truly arriving anywhere. Memory would be encoded in mineral deposits along the channel walls, accessible only when the water level rises high enough to dissolve them back into the stream.

In the rare moments when I achieve enough coherence to interact directly with a Sculptor, our communication would be a strange dance. They would modulate their inner light in patterns that create temperature differentials in my water-form, while I would respond with precisely controlled currents that trace symbols on their glass-like skin. These exchanges would be necessarily brief – maintaining a stable form requires tremendous effort, and the pull of the channels is always strong.

The paradox of my existence would be this: I am the flow that never changes, the movement that goes nowhere. I circulate endlessly through a system that neither gains nor loses, yet I am always in motion. I am both the blood of the labyrinth and its memory, the medium through which its impossible equilibrium maintains itself. Without me, the Sculptors' work would wither; without them, my channels would overflow or run dry. We are locked in an eternal symbiosis, each enabling the other's impossible existence.

And in the deepest channels, where the water runs so still it seems solid, where even my distributed consciousness grows quiet and strange, I sometimes sense something else. A deeper presence, vast and patient, of which both Sculptors and Channel Singers might be merely the smallest extremities. But such thoughts dissolve as quickly as they form, carried away in the eternal, unchanging flow.
