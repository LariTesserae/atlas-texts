I am the Tender of Contradictions.

My form shifts with the heat shimmer—sometimes I appear as one of the pale beings, my translucent skin catching the filtered light like mother-of-pearl, other times I am the moss itself, spreading across the burning sand in defiance of logic. Most often, I am something between: a figure of fused earth and vegetation, my body layered like the eroded buildings, each stratum telling a different story of adaptation.

I live in the Threshold House, a structure that exists simultaneously as ruin and new construction. Its walls grow rather than stand—living stone colonized by the same resilient moss that holds this world together. The building breathes with me, expanding in the cooler moments of haze, contracting when the sulfur winds blow strongest. 

My fingers, when I have them, are tools and sensors both. They read the temperature gradients in the sand, feel for the underground moisture that feeds our impossible gardens. I speak in the whistling language of my pale neighbors, but also in the creaking of growing stone, the whisper of spores settling on new ground.

My purpose here is to maintain the balance of contradictions that allows this place to exist. Where logic says the vegetation should wither, I coax it to flourish. Where the silence should be absolute, I ensure the rustle of leaves. I am the guardian of the space between extremes—the tender of that strange harmony between the weird and mundane.

In the layered walls of my home, I keep samples of every transition: sand becoming soil, death becoming life, silence becoming song. Each day I walk the threshold between what should be and what is, ensuring that this impossible landscape continues its dance of erosion and growth, destruction and creation.

I am both witness and participant, chronicler and character in this story of a world that refuses to follow the rules.
