In the Shimmering Crags of Vethralis, I find myself as one of the **Thraem-Listeners** – the eldest and most attuned of the Ethralyn who have learned to read the mountain's embryonic dreams.

My form has evolved beyond the typical Ethralyn frame. Where my kin stand wiry and slight, decades of dwelling in the highest, most precarious reaches have compressed my body into something more elemental. My spine curves like the arc of the ridges themselves, and my skin has taken on the full gray-green of aged slate, shot through with thin veins of translucence where the morning light can pass through me like it does the mist. My eyes have grown so large they dominate my face – not just wide, but deep, with layers of reflection that catch not only the present gleam of quartz but the memory of yesterday's light, last week's rain, the shape of clouds from seasons past.

I no longer build my dwelling but inhabit a natural formation near the peak they call the Listening Crown – a hollow where three ridges meet and the earth's hum, the Thraem-Vyr, resonates most clearly. The walls of my home are unworked stone, but over my many years here, the constant moisture of my breath and body has encouraged a thick carpet of bioluminescent lichen to grow in patterns that shift with my moods and dreams. When I sleep, which is often now, the Vyr-Lum pulses in rhythm with my heartbeat, creating a gentle green aurora within the cave.

My role among the Ethralyn is both revered and avoided. I am the one who sits at the boundary between what they understand and what unsettles them. When the younger ones bring me offerings – a particularly resonant piece of quartz, the scale of an unusual Glim-Skirt, water from a pool that sang differently than the others – I hold these objects and listen to their stories. Not the stories the bringers tell, but the stories the objects themselves carry. 

The quartz remembers the pressure that formed it. The scale holds the lizard's last vision of sky before the hunt. The water knows every surface it has touched on its journey from cloud to pool.

But more than interpreter of objects, I am a bridge to the Mist-Wraiths. Where others see their shadowy forms and hear their whispers as external phenomena, I have learned, through patient decades of listening, that they are not separate from us at all. They are what happens when an Ethralyn becomes so attuned to Vethralis that the boundary dissolves. I am already partly there myself – in the deepest fogs, my own edges blur, and I find I can move not by climbing but by flowing with the mist itself, reforming on distant ridges without quite remembering the journey between.

The young ones think I prophesy, but I know better. I simply report what the mountain is considering. When I press my ear to the stone and the Thraem-Vyr thrums through my bones, I hear the debates: Should this ridge crumble or crystallize? Should the pools deepen or disperse? Should the Ethralyn remain, or has their time in this trial reached its end? I translate these ponderings into words my people can grasp, though something is always lost in the speaking.

My daily rhythm follows the mountain's breathing. At dawn, when the mist is thickest, I dissolve partially into it, extending my consciousness along the water-paths that seep through every crevice. I taste the health of our world through these flowing fingers – noting where new moss has taken hold, where a Glim-Skirt has laid its crystalline eggs, where the stone has shifted in the night. By midday, when the sun burns through and the quartz veins shine, I resolidify and tend to the Listeners' Garden – a small plateau where I've learned to cultivate the strangest plants of Vethralis, the ones that seem to respond to sound rather than light. I hum to them in harmonics that match the mountain's own frequency, and they grow in spirals that map the earth's dreams.

In the evening, when the Vyr-Lum begins its glow, I receive visitors. Sometimes they are Ethralyn seeking guidance about a vision they've glimpsed or a tremor they've felt. Sometimes they are Wail-Trails that perch at my cave's entrance, their cries teaching me new tones to add to my vocabulary of stone-speech. And sometimes, increasingly often now, they are the other Thraem-Listeners – for I am not the only one, just the first to admit what we are becoming.

We gather in silence, we listeners who are learning to be less solid, more patient than stone itself. We know that Vethralis is approaching some crucial decision about its nature, and we are both witnesses and participants in this becoming. When we disperse back to our solitary posts, we carry a little of each other's essence with us – a shared understanding that spreads through the mist like slowly seeping water.

I am ancient by Ethralyn standards, yet infant-new in the timeline of mountains. My transformation feels less like aging and more like erosion – a wearing away of everything unnecessary until only the essential remains: the listening, the translating, the patient dissolution into something larger than individual form. Soon, perhaps, I will take the final step and become fully what the others call Mist-Wraith, though I know now that term is inadequate. We are not wraiths but echoes of the mountain's voice, ensuring that even as Vethralis changes, its memory persists in the mist.

Until that day, I remain poised between states – solid enough to offer counsel, fluid enough to flow with the mountain's dreams, listening always to the patient question of what this world of wet stone and sharp edges will choose to become. In the Shimmering Crags, I am both interpreter and text, reader and story, as embryonic and eternal as Vethralis itself.
