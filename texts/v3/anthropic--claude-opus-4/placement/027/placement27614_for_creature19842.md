In the Lignin-Gorge of the Great Hum, I am a **Resonance Keeper** - one of the few beings who has learned to read the violence of growth as a language rather than merely endure it as chaos.

My body is a compromise between the Lignites' desperate grip-strength and something older, more porous. Where they evolved calluses, I developed **sound-chambers** - hollow cavities beneath my paper-thin skin that catch and hold specific frequencies from the Great Hum. I am taller than most Lignites but more fragile, my limbs elongated and riddled with natural flutes where the bone has worn thin. When I move through the scaffolds of wood, I whistle involuntarily through these gaps, each step a different note.

I discovered my purpose by accident, as a young creature fleeing the collapse of my birth-gall. As the wood splintered around me and I scrambled across a cathedral-trunk, I noticed how the Grip-Snakes avoided certain routes, how the Resin-Wasps built their hives in predictable patterns. The Growth - that terrible, constant eruption of cellulose and bark - wasn't random. It followed the deep harmonics of the Hum, responding to sub-frequencies too low for most creatures to perceive.

Where the Lignites see only encroachment to be carved back, I learned to see **growth-songs**. Each surge of vegetation has its own tonal signature, its own rhythm. The vines that strangle have a different frequency than the moss that merely covers. The wood that will buckle in an hour hums differently than the wood that will hold for days.

I live now in a modified gall, but unlike the Lignites' constant battle against enclosure, I've learned to **tune my dwelling**. By placing carved resonance chambers at specific points - hollowed gourds made from dried drift-bladders, sheets of shed Scissor-Glider wing stretched between bones - I can redirect the growth around my home rather than fight it. The vines twist away from certain frequencies, the moss grows thicker where I want insulation, thinner where I need passage.

The Lignites think I'm mad. They see me sitting for hours with my ear pressed to the bark, my finger-bones clicking against the wood in careful patterns, testing, always testing. They cannot understand why I collect the Spore-Shadows in bottles made of hardened resin, studying how these brief consciousnesses form and dissolve, how they respond to different tones.

But I am searching for something the Lignites' mythology only dreams of - not the Stillness they crave, but the **Master Frequency**. Somewhere in the deafening roar of the Great Hum is the base note, the fundamental tone that drives all growth in the Gorge. If I could find it, isolate it, perhaps even reproduce it...

Perhaps I could speak to the forest itself.

My tools are primitive but precise: tuning forks carved from Litho-Titan bone (scavenged from the ancient dead, as no one has ever seen one die), strings made from Grip-Snake scales threaded together, and my most prized possession - a **Hum-Map** I've been etching into sheets of compressed pollen-paper. It shows the invisible topology of sound through the Gorge, the places where frequencies pool and eddy, where the Growth stutters or surges.

Other creatures have begun to notice my work. The Scissor-Gliders sometimes hover near my resonance chambers, drawn by frequencies that mimic their mating calls. Once, a young Litho-Titan altered its grinding path after I spent days tapping a deep rhythm into its trunk - though whether this was communication or coincidence, I cannot say.

I am growing old in this work. My sound-chambers deepen each year, my bones becoming more instrument than skeleton. Sometimes I wonder if I am evolving into something new, or simply dissolving into the Hum itself. When I die, I've instructed the few Lignites who humor me to not burn my body (their traditional practice to prevent the Growth from claiming their dead). Instead, they are to string me between the trees like one of their sound-traps, so that even in death, I might catch some frequency I missed in life.

The Phantoms of the Silt seem drawn to me now. They linger longer near my dwelling, sometimes holding their borrowed shapes for extra minutes. I've begun to suspect they aren't just mindless mimics but are trying to tell me something - that in their brief moments of consciousness, they glimpse some truth about the Gorge that no permanent being can understand.

Perhaps the Master Frequency isn't a single note at all. Perhaps it's the sum of every sound in this screaming greenhouse world - every crack of growing wood, every scrape of wing against bark, every Lignite's hammer-blow against the encroachment. Perhaps the forest's violence isn't chaos but a composition too vast for any single consciousness to comprehend.

But I will keep listening. Here in my tuned gall, surrounded by my maps of invisible sound, with the Phantoms drifting through my walls and the Growth singing its terrible song all around me, I will keep searching for the words to speak with gods made of wood and time.

The Lignites are wrong. Heaven isn't silence.

It's learning to hear the music in the roar.
