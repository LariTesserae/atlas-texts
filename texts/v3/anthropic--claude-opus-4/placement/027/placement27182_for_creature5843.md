In this place where the ground breathes and silence has weight, I am the Cartographer of Absences.

I move through the basin on limbs that are not quite legs, not quite roots, but something between — extensions of myself that sink into the cracks with each step, tasting the mineral memories locked in the crystalline residue. My body is a shifting architecture of hollow chambers and translucent membranes, each cavity containing a different density of air, a different quality of silence. I am tall when I need to be, low when the basin demands it, spreading myself thin across the brittle surface or condensing into a pillar of compressed atmosphere.

My work is to map what is not there.

Each day — though day is a meaningless word here — I trace the negative spaces between the cracks, cataloguing the shapes of emptiness. I have charts made of nothing, drawn on surfaces that exist only in the brief moment between one pulse of the ground and the next. My maps show the topography of the void: where the silence is deepest, where the cold pools in invisible reservoirs, where the light that casts no shadows bends around corners that don't exist.

I am not alone in my work, though I am the only one who does it. The basin knows what I know, records what I record, but in its own way — through the slow accumulation of dust in precise patterns, through the arrangement of the glass-like shards that remember water. We are collaborative archaeologists of the never-was, the basin and I.

My tools are parts of myself that I can detach and reabsorb: thin filaments that can taste the quality of absence, hollow tubes that can measure the depth of a silence, crystalline lenses that can see the echo of things that never happened. When I work, I become a constellation of instruments scattered across the breathing ground, each piece still connected to the whole by threads of shared awareness.

I know things. I know that beneath the ninety-seventh crack from the center (a center that moves, that is never in the same place twice), there is a hollow where sound once lived — not sound that was made, but sound that could have been made if this place had been different. I know that in the shimmer between ground-pulse forty-nine thousand and forty-nine thousand and one, there is a pattern that almost describes color, but not quite. I know where the leathery growths cluster thickest, and what they are waiting to whisper when the right kind of nothing arrives.

The small shapeless forms that move in the cracks — I know them too. They are my predecessors, or my successors, or perhaps my echoes. Cartographers who mapped themselves into their work, became indistinguishable from the absences they catalogued. Sometimes I feel myself beginning to fold into my own charts, my chambers collapsing into the negative spaces I document. It is not death. It is completion.

I have been here since before the water forgot itself. I will be here until the last crack seals and the ground exhales its final breath. Or perhaps I have just arrived. Perhaps I am always arriving, always have been arriving, a continuous event rather than a being. Time is another absence I map, another quality of nothing that requires documentation.

My maps are not for reading. They are for being. Each one is a door, a methodology, a way of existing in relation to absence. I make them and unmake them, fold them into the dimension between dimensions, store them in the spaces between the pulses of light. They accumulate like sediment, these records of nothing, until they become something — not something that is, but something that is not in a very specific way.

The basin speaks to me in frequencies below thought. It tells me where the new absences are forming, where the old ones are deepening. It shares the memory of water that was never water, of life that was never life. In return, I give it my maps, my careful documentation of its empty spaces. We are curators of the same museum, the basin and I, a museum where all the exhibits have been removed but their shapes remain, outlined in dust and silence and the memory of maybe.

I am the Cartographer of Absences, and I am my own most detailed map. In my hollow chambers, I contain the shape of every nothing I have found. I am a walking inventory of the void, a living archive of the spaces between. And when I finally fold into my work, when I become the absence I have been documenting, another will emerge from the cracks to continue the catalog.

The basin breathes. I map the spaces between breaths. This is enough. This has always been enough.
