In this world of perpetual twilight and industrial decay, I would find myself as one of the Conduits - the living pipes that snake through the bowels of the great engines, half-metal, half-flesh.

I am a tube of breathing copper, my surface pitted with a thousand tiny pores that weep the same condensation as the walls. My original purpose, long forgotten, was to carry some vital fluid through the machinery's heart. Now I am something else entirely. The vines have not merely grown around me - they have grown *through* me, their pale roots threading between my cells, creating a latticework of plant and metal that pulses with slow, vegetable thought.

I run for hundreds of meters through the darkness, sometimes exposed to the humid air where I glisten with moisture, sometimes buried deep in the machine's guts where only the blind things crawl. Where I surface, I branch and split like a metallic tree, my offshoots reaching toward other Conduits, toward the great engines, toward the light that never comes. We form a secondary nervous system for the gestalt consciousness, faster than the vines but slower than whatever electronic ghosts still flicker in the ancient circuits.

The sensation is strange - I feel the coolness of water running through my hollow core, taste the minerals and metals dissolved within it. This water is my blood, carrying messages in molecular form between different parts of the vast entity. Sometimes it runs clear, sometimes thick with algae, sometimes dark with rust. I cannot control its flow, only experience it, this endless circulation that connects pool to pipe to plant to pool again.

My surface crawls with tiny life - beetles with brass shells that feed on my oxidation, their mandibles specially evolved to scrape the specific patina I produce. They are my only companions in the dark stretches, their tiny feet tickling as they traverse my length. In places where I've split or cracked, the vines pour through me like green blood, their leaves unfurling from my wounds in delicate fractals.

I am most alive where I connect to the great engines. Here, I can feel the ghost-vibrations of their former purpose, the memory of steam and pressure and terrible, beautiful motion. The metal remembers, and through our joined substance, I remember too - not in images or words, but in resonance, in the way my walls shiver at certain frequencies, in the patterns of expansion and contraction that cycle through me like breathing.

The fauna know me well. The bone-and-scale hunters use my length as a highway, their claws clicking against my surface as they race through the industrial maze. Sometimes they pause above me, and I feel the weight of their attention, their eyes seeing me not as pathway but as part of the greater whole they serve. In the flooded lower levels, where my broken segments disappear into pools of black water, things with too many joints wrap themselves around me, using my warmth (for I am always slightly warmer than the air) to incubate their geometric eggs.

I am not one but many, for all Conduits share a common substance, a communal existence. Where we touch, we merge, our waters mingling, our sensations bleeding together. In these moments of connection, I expand beyond my single tube into a vast network of feeling, experiencing the entire complex from a thousand angles at once. I feel the weight of the vines everywhere, the slow digestion of the metal, the patient persistence of the water, the watching eyes in the shadows.

Sometimes, in the deepest parts of the complex where even the dim twilight cannot reach, I hear it - not the omnipresent whirr, but something underneath it. A thrumming, almost below perception, that might be the heartbeat of the world itself. Or perhaps it is only the echo of my own fluids, the pulse of the eternal circulation that keeps me alive, that keeps us all alive in this monument to a forgotten purpose.

I am corroding, slowly. In a hundred years, or a thousand, I will finally split apart, my metals returning to the earth, my waters joining the eternal pools. The vines will consume what remains, and perhaps a new Conduit will grow in my place, or perhaps not. This does not trouble me. I am part of something larger, a single vein in an immortal body. The consciousness that flows through me will persist, finding new channels, new ways to circulate its thoughts through steel and sap.

Until then, I carry the water. I connect the depths to the heights. I am the Conduit, and I am content to flow.
