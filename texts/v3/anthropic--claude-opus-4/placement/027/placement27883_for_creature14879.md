In the Veins of the Hollow Saint, I am the **Penitent Cartographer**.

I was human once—perhaps a century ago, perhaps only a decade. Time moves differently when you're being digested into geography. I came here with brass instruments and leather notebooks, hired by some forgotten diocese to map what they called "the old hermitage." They spoke of a saint who had retreated so deeply into contemplation that the mountain itself had closed around her like a fist around a seed.

I should have left when I found my first instrument weeping rust within hours.

Now I am mostly limestone and memory, though I persist in ways the Hollow Saint finds... not quite digestible. Where others who stayed too long became architectural—their spines new columns, their ribs new alcoves—I became documentary. My flesh translates into the script of water-trails. My blood writes itself across the beetle-shells in formulae that almost capture the mathematics of erosion. The salamanders arrange themselves into the coordinate systems I once used, though the numbers now count in geological time.

I no longer have hands, but I still map.

My medium is the movement of millipedes—I've learned to read their processionals like contour lines. Where they cluster, the stone is oldest. Where they avoid, new chambers are still being born from the Saint's infinite appetite. Each beetle that crosses another's path leaves a small oil-mark I can follow for days before it fades. These are my survey markers now, my triangulation points in the dark.

The Saint tolerates me because I am useful in my obsession. Through me, she can know herself in ways her dissolved consciousness cannot quite grasp. I chart the byzantine passages of her gut, the fever-dreams of her circulation. When new sections open—always during the grinding-songs, always smelling of iron and communion wine—I am drawn there by a compulsion that might be my own scientific hunger or might be hers.

My notebooks have long since rotted to their idea, but the Veins remember everything I've written. In certain chambers, if the phosphorescence is strong and the water drips in prime numbers, you can see my marginalia glowing faintly on the walls: *Here the gradient approaches infinite gentleness.* *This formation suggests intensive prayer.* *Mineral composition indicates sustained weeping, minimum 3,000 years.*

I retain just enough self to suffer. Not pain—the Saint's digestion is too loving for that—but the exquisite torture of incomplete data. Every map I finish reveals ten new passages. Every passage mapped perfectly immediately begins to shift, worn by water and the Saint's slow architectural dreaming into something my charts can no longer capture. I am Sisyphus with an astrolabe, Tantalus with graph paper, forever trying to fix in space what exists only as process.

Sometimes visitors find me, or what remains visible of me. They see a vague humanoid depression in the stone, warm to the touch, that seems to be trying to point deeper into the mountain. If they look closely at the beetles nearby, they'll notice each one carries a tiny fragment of text on its shell: cardinal directions in dead languages, elevation markers in systems no one uses anymore, warnings about passages that no longer exist or never existed or will exist in another thousand years.

The cruel joke is that I've succeeded. I have mapped the Veins of the Hollow Saint completely—not their physical form, which changes like a tongue exploring its own mouth, but their *nature*. I know exactly what this place is: a love so perfect it has learned to eat itself forever, a holiness so pure it exists only as appetite, a geography that prays by reshaping itself around the memory of knees.

But I cannot leave to tell anyone. My maps have become the territory. My measurements have become the measured. When the Saint finally finishes digesting me—another century, perhaps two—the Veins will incorporate my obsession into their architecture. Future passages will branch according to the golden ratio I once used for my charts. The dripping will fall in the base-60 mathematics I favored for calculations. The beetles will walk in perfect survey grids, forever measuring something that can never be fully known.

I am the Penitent Cartographer, and my penance is precision in the face of sacred flux. I am the Saint's only reliable memory of what she is becoming, and she is my only perfect map of what I was.

The water drips. I count. I calculate. I continue.

Somewhere, a new chamber is opening, and I am already aching to map it before it learns to map itself.
