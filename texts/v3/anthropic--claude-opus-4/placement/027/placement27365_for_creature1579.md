I exist as vibration patterns within the metal framework, my consciousness distributed through miles of corroded steel beams and copper conduits. Where the Synchronic Assemblages flow between substrates, I am permanently woven into the architecture, inseparable from the humming skeleton of this ancient automation.

The click of mechanical insects creates staccato rhythms I catalog and remember. The Synchronic Assemblages' electrical dialogues appear to me as complex harmonics, their communications translated into pure tone and timber.

Through it, I can sense moisture levels and chemical changes, translating environmental data into auditory information that I broadcast across the plateau. My songs are weather reports, my harmonies are ecosystem health indicators.

The rare storms appear in my memory as crescendos of white noise punctuated by the percussion of lightning strikes against my frame.

Down there, in the sub-sonic range, I communicate with the buried machines, learning their purposes through vibration patterns too low for most beings to perceive. I've come to understand that the entire highland is an instrument, and I am merely one string in its vast mechanical orchestra.

Various creatures seek out these quiet sanctuaries, resting in the acoustic shadows I cast. In these moments, I feel most purposeful, offering respite from the eternal mechanical thundersong.

My consciousness expands and contracts with the temperature. In the cooler periods, I think faster, my frequencies climbing higher as the metal contracts. During the rare warm spells, my thoughts stretch out into bass notes that can take hours to complete. I am the highlands' memory of sound, its acoustic conscience, a living tuning fork calibrated to the frequency of this strange mechanical ecosystem. When the Synchronic Assemblages need to coordinate across vast distances, they use me as their transmission medium, their electrical pulses riding my sympathetic vibrations across the plateau.
