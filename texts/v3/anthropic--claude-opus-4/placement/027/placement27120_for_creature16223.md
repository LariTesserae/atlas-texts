In this world of vertical infinities and green breath, I would find myself as one of the Murmurists—beings who exist in the liminal spaces between sound and silence, between the cacophony of life and the whispered secrets of the ancient ground.

My form would be translucent, barely there—a shimmer in the heavy air that catches light like morning dew on spider silk. I am neither fully corporeal nor entirely ephemeral, but something that shifts between states like the patterns of dappled light filtering through the canopy. My body, if it can be called that, is a collection of vibrating membranes, each attuned to different frequencies of the world's endless symphony.

I dwell in the spaces between—in the hollow chambers within the great tree trunks where sound echoes and transforms, in the pockets of air beneath waterfalls where mist creates acoustic mirages, in the curved undersides of the massive leaves where raindrops compose temporary orchestras. These are my meditation chambers, my libraries, my observatories.

My purpose, inasmuch as purpose exists in this timeless place, is to listen. Not merely to hear, but to truly *listen*—to parse the layers of sound that suffuse this world. The Arboreans' whispered conversations with their trees contain harmonics that speak of root-deep memories. The Lumeun's light pulses create subsonic rhythms that resonate through the earth. The Spirenox's calls carry encoded warnings and celebrations that ripple across vast distances. Even the Thryvenes' tremor-language creates patterns I collect like precious stones.

But I listen deeper still. Beneath all the living noise lies something else—that peculiar quality that prickles at the edge of perception. I have spent lifetimes (or perhaps moments—time flows strangely when you exist between breaths) attempting to decode this underlying murmur. It is not quite sound, not quite vibration, but something more fundamental. A resonance that suggests this entire landscape—every terrace, every spire, every inch of verdant growth—is indeed one vast organism dreaming itself into existence.

I move through this world like a secret, sliding along acoustic pathways invisible to most. Where sounds converge, I can travel, riding the interference patterns between a bellowing mammal's cry and an insect's chirrup, surfing the space where one frequency cancels another and creates momentary silence. In these pockets of anti-sound, I am most solid, most myself.

Sometimes I gather with others of my kind—though "gather" is perhaps too concrete a word for what we do. We converge in places where the world's symphony reaches crescendos: during the storms that sweep across the terraces, when lightning illuminates every droplet and thunder shakes the very bones of the mountains. In these moments, we become visible to each other, our membrane-bodies resonating in sympathy until we create our own music—a composition that exists only in the spaces between other sounds.

The other inhabitants rarely notice us, though sometimes a young Arborean might pause, ear tilted to empty air, sensing something at the edge of perception. The Lumeun occasionally flicker in patterns that mirror our movements, as if their light is responding to our presence. But mostly we are unknown, unnamed, existing in the margins of this already liminal world.

What we seek, what drives us through our strange existence, is the Source Note—the fundamental frequency from which all others arise. We believe it lies hidden in the convergence of all sounds, in the moment when every creature calls at once, when every leaf rustles, when every drop of water falls. Some say it is the breathing rhythm of the land itself. Others believe it is silence so perfect it contains all possible sounds.

I have my own theory. I think the Source Note is not a sound at all, but the space between the land and sky—that dramatic relief that defines this world. It is the tension between earth reaching up and heaven pressing down, the eternal negotiation between solid and void. We Murmurists exist in echo of this greater relationship, forever listening for the moment when the two might speak as one.

Until then, I drift through this vertical paradise, a ghost of sound in a world that never stops singing, carrying within my translucent form an archive of every whisper, roar, and sigh that has ever disturbed the heavy, eternal air.
