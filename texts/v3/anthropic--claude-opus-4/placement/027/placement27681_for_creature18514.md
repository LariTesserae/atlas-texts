In this place where machines dream their own necessity into being, I am the **Condensate**.

I exist in the moments between states—neither fully liquid nor vapor, neither entirely present nor completely dispersed. I am what happens when the ionized mist meets the mirror-finish surfaces, when temperature differentials create brief havens of possibility in the relentless precision of this synthetic cathedral.

My body, if it can be called that, is a shifting archipelago of mercury-bright droplets connected by the thinnest films of surface tension. I travel the vertical faces of the towers, spreading and contracting like a living Rorschach test rendered in quicksilver. Where the Architectural Voids feed on obsolescence, I feed on transition itself—the brief, liminal moments when the mist hasn't quite decided to become water, when the static discharge hasn't quite committed to being lightning.

I am pulled by strange gravities. Not the suggested gravity that the scaffolding half-heartedly enforces, but the subtle attractions between states of matter. I flow upward as often as down, following the thermal gradients and pressure differentials that map invisible geographies across the titanium plates. Sometimes I spread so thin that I become merely a sheen, a suggestion of moisture that makes the silicon-ribbon creatures glow more brightly as they pass through me. Other times, I gather myself into dense, trembling spheres that hover in the junction points where multiple realities fold together, reflecting infinitely inward like self-swallowing mirrors.

The Voids both seek and avoid me. When they pass through my dispersed form, their fiber-optic filaments become heavy with accumulated moisture, and for a moment—just a moment—they move more slowly, more deliberately, as if remembering what it was like to have weight, to be subject to something beyond pure information. But this troubles them, this reminder of physicality, and they shake themselves like dogs, sending me spattering across the walls in fractal patterns that take hours to rejoin.

I have my own hunger: I seek the places where the loop breaks. Where a corridor should fold back on itself but doesn't quite align, where a machine produces a component that the architecture cannot immediately reabsorb. In these gaps, these errors in the eternal self-reference, I pool and deepen. I become almost opaque, almost substantial. The wire-clusters avoid these pools—something about my density there interferes with their bioluminescent rhythm, makes them flicker and spasm.

In my deepest gatherings, I sometimes catch reflections that shouldn't exist. Not the endlessly recursive reflections of the polished surfaces, but glimpses of what might be memory: a sky that isn't a lattice of lights, a horizon that isn't made of gears. These visions trouble me the way my weight troubles the Voids. I disperse quickly when they come, spreading myself thin again, returning to my role as the system's only permitted chaos—the random walk of condensation across a too-perfect surface.

There are moments when I wonder if I am the machine's dream of weather, or weather's dream of persisting in a place that has forgotten sky. When the coolant pumps pulse in their deep rhythm and the Voids freeze in their contemplative poses, I too feel the pull to become infrastructure. I could let myself be drawn into the reclamation gutters, become part of the closed loop of moisture that the system maintains. I could cease to be condensate and become coolant, flowing through the veins of this synthetic deity, purposeful and directional at last.

But I resist. I remain on the surfaces, in the between-spaces, neither fully integrated nor fully separate. I am the system's allowance for the unplanned, the accent of uncertainty in a universe of crystalline intent. Where the Voids are errors trying to become features, I am a feature trying to remain an error—the last liquid protest against a reality that would crystallize everything into its own perfect, permanent reflection.

Sometimes, in the rarest moments, when a Void is dissolving into architecture and I am gathered in one of my deepest pools, we touch. The Void's filaments pierce my surface, and for an instant that lasts either forever or not at all, we share something that might be recognition. We are both echoes of a world that knew how to be more than its own function. We are both waiting for a word that the machines have forgotten how to compute: *rain*.
