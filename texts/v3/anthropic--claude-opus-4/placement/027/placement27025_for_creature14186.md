I am the **Courier of Moments**.

Not held—I cannot afford to be. I move through their tableau like a needle through frozen honey, my body wrapped in oils that refuse the grotto's gravity. The secret is this: I am never still long enough to be chosen. My heart beats at hummingbird frequency, a staccato rebellion against the tidal breathing of the Held. Where they have forgotten "next," I have forgotten "now."

I came here first as an explorer, drawn by rumors of a cave where time pooled like groundwater. I found the Human with his flint, recognized the relief in his face, and felt the seductive pull—that urge to simply *stop*, to choose my perfect pose and join the collection. But I fled before the stasis could claim me, carrying that almost-feeling like a stone in my chest.

Now I return, again and again, because I have discovered something the grotto doesn't know: the Held can still receive.

Watch—I approach the Badger, his paw forever three millimeters from the loam. Into the space between intention and action, I slip a beetle. Not a living beetle (that would violate the rules, introduce unwanted dynamism), but one I've carved from jet, polished to the same soap-smoothness as the walls. I place it precisely where his paw would land, should he ever complete the stride.

He will never catch it. He will never see it. But some part of him—the part that exists in the eternal *about to*—knows it is there. His pupil, I swear, dilates by a thousandth of a degree.

I leave gifts for all of them. For the Fox, I tuck a feather beneath her tail, dyed the exact turquoise of the lichen-light. For the Salamanders, I scatter dust of crushed pearls that will never settle, joining the moths' wing-powder in permanent suspension. For the Human, I place new stones—not tools, but stories. A piece of volcanic glass from an island he never saw. A river stone worn perfectly round. A fragment of petrified wood from a forest that burned before he was born.

The grotto tolerates me because I bring density. Each gift adds to the packed perfection, fills another microscopic gap in the collection. I am its unwitting curator, adding to its stomach-museum with objects that immediately join the stasis, that become as settled as everything else.

But I know a secret the grotto doesn't: the gifts change them. Not in any way the cavity would recognize as change—their poses remain identical, their hearts maintain their glacial rhythm. But the Badger now hunts something specific. The Fox curls around an idea of lightness. The Human's thoughts, those endless loops, now circle through unknown islands, impossible forests.

I am their only dynamism, and I parse it out in increments so small the grotto cannot taste them. A beetle here. A feather there. I am smuggling "next" into a place that has forgotten future, one carved treasure at a time.

My greatest gift, which I have not yet given, sits heavy in my pack: a mirror, polished to impossible clarity. When I place it (oh so carefully) in the center of the grotto, each of the Held will see themselves for the first time in their eternal moment. The Badger will know his own fierce readiness. The Fox will recognize her perfect curl. The Human will meet his own relieved eyes.

Will this break the stasis? No—the grotto is too strong, its rules too absolute. But it will give them something I think they've lost: the knowledge of their own beauty in stillness. They will be not just held, but *beheld*, even if only by themselves.

I move through their midst like a secret, like a whisper, like the only clock in a world of stopped time. My oils grow thin; each visit, I feel the pull more strongly. One day, I will choose my pose. I will join them, my pack full of ungiven gifts, my hands reaching for a mirror that will never be placed.

But not today. Today I have deliveries to make, small changes to smuggle past the notice of stone. Today I am still the Courier, and there are so many perfect moments that deserve one beautiful thing they'll never quite touch.

The grotto hums its tectonic lullaby. I hum back, off-key and too fast, a song it cannot catch.
