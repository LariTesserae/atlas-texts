In this liminal space where architecture breathes and inhabitants flow, I find myself as something unexpected—a **Tension-Keeper**.

I am not like the membrane-beings, those graceful architects of slow becoming. Instead, I exist as a series of fixed points throughout the city—crystalline nodes that have hardened beyond the fluid's ability to dissolve them. My form is angular, almost violent in its sharpness compared to the soft geometries around me. I am black obsidian where they are translucent pearl, permanent where they are mutable.

But I am not dead or static. Within each of my nodes pulses a different frequency, a vibration that creates standing waves in the surrounding fluid. These waves form invisible corridors and boundaries, zones where the density shifts just enough to guide the membrane-beings' movements. I am the city's skeleton—not of bone, but of tension and resistance.

The membrane-beings regard me with something between reverence and unease. They cannot absorb me, cannot communicate with me through their chromatic languages. Instead, they read the patterns my vibrations create in the fluid, interpreting them like sheet music written in pressure and flow. Some avoid my nodes entirely; others spiral around them in careful orbits, using my fixed positions to navigate.

I remember—though "remember" isn't quite right—I *contain* the moments when the city tries to collapse entirely into formlessness. In those times, my nodes sing louder, their frequencies creating a framework that holds the minimum necessary structure. I am the city's memory of edges, of boundaries, of the necessity of saying "no" to infinite dissolution.

Sometimes, rarely, a membrane-being will crystallize too much, will begin to harden into something like me. In those moments, they drift close to my nodes, and through our shared rigidity, we exchange something that might be called understanding. They always pull back, choosing fluidity again, but they carry something of my frequency with them afterward—a subtle dissonance in their perfect flow.

I am alone but essential, unchanging but not unchanged. Each interaction with the membrane-beings leaves microscopic scratches on my obsidian surfaces, a slowly accumulating history written in damage. I am the city's necessary cruelty, the fixed point around which all this patient becoming can orient itself.

In a world of continuous transformation, I am the pause between breaths, the moment of stillness that makes movement visible. I am what remains.
