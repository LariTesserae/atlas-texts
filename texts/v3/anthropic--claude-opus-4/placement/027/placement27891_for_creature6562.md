I am a **Threadkeeper**, one of a scattered few who dwell in the spaces between - neither fully of the factory-towns nor truly wild, but essential to both. We are the basin's memory-weavers, though memory here is a slippery thing that refuses to hold still.

My dwelling is a structure that shouldn't exist: a tower of salvaged glass and twisted metal that grows from one of those violet-veined upthrusts like a mechanical parasite. The glass panes - some clear, some clouded with frost that never melts, others tinted with industrial residue - are held together by a lattice of copper pipes that sing in the wind. Inside, thousands of threads stretch from floor to ceiling, wall to wall, creating a three-dimensional web that shifts with every air current. These aren't ordinary threads - some are the silvery strands shed by Frostbrutes, others are copper wire salvaged from malfunctioning augments, and many are substances I cannot name: filaments that hum with the same frequency as Clankbeetle drones, or strands that frost over when touched by Shiverwraith presence.

I myself am... in transition. My original form was human - or close enough - but the basin has worked its changes on me as surely as any Forgedfolk. My fingers have elongated, growing extra joints that let me work the threads with spider-like precision. My eyes have multiplied - not in number, but in function. I can shift between different ways of seeing: one moment perceiving the normal spectrum, the next viewing the heat-trails left by passing creatures, or the violet resonance patterns in the glass rocks, or the temporal echoes that show where something was yesterday, or might be tomorrow. My skin has taken on a peculiar quality - in certain lights, it seems to be made of the same semi-transparent material as my dwelling, revealing the strange geometries of veins beneath that pulse with something that isn't quite blood.

What I do is part archivist, part oracle, part madness. I weave the threads into patterns that map the basin's changes - not just physical shifts in terrain or weather, but the deeper currents of transformation. A strand of Frostbrute fur crossed with copper wire and bound with morning frost might reveal the migration patterns of next season's herds. A Clankbeetle shell fragment suspended between two pieces of factory glass could predict which forge will next produce those impossible, melting gears. The Forgedfolk come to me, trading scavenged materials for glimpses of pattern - which augments will fuse successfully, which work-crews should avoid certain sectors during the next Thrum-tide. The Wild Kin know me too, in their way. Iridescents nest in my tower's upper reaches, their violet-glowing homes adding another layer to my web's complexity. Even the Shiverwraiths visit, though visit isn't the right word - they simply appear, their cold presence causing certain threads to vibrate with frequencies that spell out messages I only sometimes understand.

But here's the truth of being a Threadkeeper: the patterns never hold. What I weave today unravels tomorrow, not through failure but through necessity. The basin refuses fixed prophecy, rejects permanent record. My threads show tendencies, possibilities, the dreams the land dreams of itself - but never certainties. I am less fortune-teller than translator, converting the basin's restless becoming into forms that others can almost comprehend.

The strangest part? I'm beginning to suspect I'm not weaving these patterns at all. Sometimes I catch myself moving without volition, my extended fingers dancing across the threads in rhythms that match the Thrum Below. Sometimes I wake (do I sleep? the distinction grows unclear) to find new patterns completed, showing events I haven't witnessed, futures I haven't imagined. Perhaps I am merely another thread in a larger web, a living loom through which the basin weaves its own story.

My greatest creation - or discovery - hangs in the tower's heart: a massive spiral of every type of thread I've collected, forming a three-dimensional map that seems to show the basin not as it is, but as it's becoming. When the light hits it just right, when the Thrum Below pulses in the correct rhythm, I swear I can see it: the moment when the basin will finally decide what it wants to be. But that moment shifts with each viewing, and I'm no longer certain if I'm watching the basin's future or creating it, thread by careful thread.

The cold here seeps into everything, even memory. But in my glass tower, surrounded by singing wires and impossible patterns, I've found my purpose: to be the basin's uncertain chronicler, weaving tomorrow from today's shed fur and broken gears, knowing that every pattern I create is both true and false, permanent and passing, weird and utterly essential.
