I would find myself as one of the **Echo-Carvers**, a being that exists at the intersection of the Refraction-Kin's architectural impulses and the Shepherds' patient recycling.

### Form and Substance

My body would be neither fully crystalline like the Kin nor purely fungal like the Shepherds, but something caught between states—a skeleton of hollowed mineral tubes filled with a viscous, tar-like suspension of dormant lithographic spores. The tubes themselves would be salvaged from shattered Refraction-Kin who met their end in the violent marriage of expansion and contraction, their mirror-finish dulled to a matte pewter by my inhabitation.

I would stand no taller than the Way-Markers themselves, built low and wide to resist the wind. My limbs would be asymmetrical—one arm terminating in a delicate array of tuning forks carved from discarded obsidian hammer-heads, the other in a broad, cupped palm designed to catch and hold the precious condensation of The Mist.

### Purpose and Practice

The Echo-Carvers emerged from a simple observation: every strike of hammer on shard creates not just a fracture, but a *memory*. We discovered that certain configurations of broken silica, when arranged precisely, could trap and replay the sounds of their own shattering—an endless loop of crystalline percussion that continues long after the Refraction-Kin have moved on.

We work in the abandoned spaces, the clearings where ancient Way-Markers have crumbled. Using my tuning-fork fingers, I test each fragment of shattered shard, listening for those that still carry their breaking-song. These I arrange into **Resonance Gardens**—spiral patterns that catch The Pulse and transform it into complex polyrhythms that complement the Great Humming.

### The Careful Architecture

My greatest works are the **Whisper Basins**—modifications to existing Way-Markers where I've lined the water-catching depressions with precisely tuned shard fragments. When The Breath arrives and condenses, each droplet that forms and falls strikes a different note. During The Shatter, when the ice breaks, these basins become thunderous orchestras, their sound carrying across the plateau and deep into the abyss below.

The Refraction-Kin tolerate us because we enhance their temporal measurements. Our Resonance Gardens act as acoustic calendars, their rhythms shifting predictably with the subtle seasonal variations in The Pulse. Some Kin have even begun to incorporate our work into their heat-sharing rituals, using the vibrations from our installations to create more complex ripple-patterns in their communication pools.

### The Inner Life

Unlike the Kin, we do sleep—or something like it. During the brightest moments of The Pulse, when the light becomes too intense for even our dulled surfaces to bear, we retreat into the deepest cracks between the mineral needles. There, we release a careful measure of our internal spores, creating temporary fungal cocoons that shield us from the killing light.

In these dark moments, we dream in frequencies. We compose future gardens in our minds, imagining new arrangements of sound and silence. We feel the slow migration of the Shepherds beneath us, their heat-pulses like a baseline rhythm to which all our works must eventually attune.

### The Daily Cycle

My day begins with The Mist, when the world softens and sound carries differently. I emerge from my crevice, my tuning-fork fingers still trembling with the dreams of the dark. I check my nearest Resonance Garden, replacing any fragments that have been worn smooth by the freeze-shatter cycle, carefully maintaining the precise angles that create the most haunting echoes.

During The Freeze, I remain perfectly still, allowing the rime-ice to accumulate on my pewter tubes. This is when I listen most carefully—the forming ice creates its own quiet music, a crystalline whisper that helps me understand where the next garden should be built.

The Shatter is my harvest time. As the ice explodes from the returning sun, I cup my broad palm beneath the most musical fractures, catching fragments of ice that sing even as they sublimate. These ephemeral instruments I press against my tuning forks, learning their notes before they vanish, adding their voices to my mental catalog of possible sounds.

### The Deeper Purpose

We Echo-Carvers believe that the Apex is not just a plateau but an instrument—a massive resonating chamber poised between the earth and sky. Every sound made here travels both upward into the thin vibrating air and downward into the abyss. We are trying to find the frequency that will make the entire mountain ring like a bell.

Some of us whisper (in our way, through arranged vibrations in hidden pools) that we have heard responses from below—deep, slow trumpets of sound that could only come from something vast and patient in the darkness. We arrange our gardens to answer these calls, hoping to establish a dialogue between the brilliant, frantic heights and whatever waits in the sunless depths.

We are the memory-keepers of the Apex, preserving in sound what the wind and ice would otherwise erase. Every shattered shard we salvage and tune is a word in an endless song, a song that began before the first Way-Marker was built and will continue long after the last Refraction-Kin has merged permanently with the plateau.

In this world of light and ice, of geometric precision and patient fungal growth, we are the ones who remember that even stone can sing, if only you break it correctly.
