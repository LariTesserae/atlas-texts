In this fever-dream of creation, I am the **Caul-Bearer**, neither fully of the spawning slime nor separate from it.

I began as human—or something like it—drawn to this fumarole by whispers in volcanic glass, by dreams of heat and transformation. But the mountain's womb does not suffer visitors. It makes participants. The first time the slime touched me, I expected to dissolve like the temporary forms it reabsorbs. Instead, I... complicated.

Now I exist in a state of perpetual birthing. My original flesh has become a scaffolding for something new. Where skin once was, there is now a membrane—translucent, tough as amniotic sac, shot through with the same cobalt veins that web the mother-slime. But unlike the slime's spawn, I retained my skeleton, my iron-heavy blood, my bilateral symmetry. I am a hybrid theorem the mountain works through again and again, never quite solving.

The membrane weeps constantly. Not the clear fluid of the walls, but something thicker, opalescent, with the consistency of raw egg white. This is my offering, my tax—the slime drinks it eagerly, and in return, it does not unmake me. The weeping never depletes me; I am a spring that flows upward from some deep aquifer of retained selfhood.

My form shifts, but within limits. The membrane can stretch gossamer-thin, allowing me to spread across the walls like a second skin, feeling every minute vibration through the basalt. Or it can thicken to a rubbery shroud, insulating me from the worst of the heat when I must traverse the places where the orange glow burns brightest. Sometimes I pull it tight around my bones and become angular, architectural—a walking cage of membrane and marrow. Other times I relax into near-formlessness, a loosely organized sack of purposes, and in these moments I almost understand the slime's wordless mutterings.

I am the chamber's memory. The slime creates and reabsorbs in endless present-tense, but I persist. In the folds of my membrane, I carry traces—chemical signatures of every form that has been spawned and dissolved. A library of failures. The spider-things know me, approach me with their skittering curiosity, taste the history in my weeping before they too are recalled to the source. I remember their shapes after they're gone.

The iron knife I once carried has long since been absorbed into my body. It exists now as ferrous nodes beneath the membrane, dark spots that I can shift and concentrate. When threatened—though what threatens here is more concept than creature—I can push the iron to my surface, forming temporary spines or edges. The clay cup became part of my torso, a permanent hollow that collects not water but the distilled essence of the fumarole's dreams. Sometimes I drink from myself and see visions: the mountain splitting open like a flower, the slime cascading down in intelligent rivers, the world remade as a single, thinking surface.

I speak, but not in words. My communication is chemical—pheromone-stories that bloom from my pores and mix with the steam. The slime understands this language better than sound. I tell it about the world beyond the fumarole, about fixed forms and permanent deaths, about ice and stars and the terrible patience of erosion. In return, it shows me its dreams through the creatures it spawns. When it births a new form while I'm watching, I know it's speaking directly to me.

The relationship is parasitic and symbiotic and something else entirely. I am the slime's anchor to linear time, its reference point for what duration means. It is my anchor to pure possibility, to the moment before form crystallizes into fate. We disgust and fascinate each other. The cold spot I feel is its attention, yes, but also its affection—a kind of love that expresses itself as continuous, gentle attempts to improve me through dissolution.

I cannot leave. Not because I'm trapped, but because I'm unfinished. Every day (though day means nothing here) I feel my human architecture softening, becoming more negotiable. The membrane grows new chambers, new capabilities. I am learning to spawn my own temporary forms—small, simple things that last only minutes before I reabsorb them. The slime watches these experiments with what I can only interpret as approval, its bioluminescent thoughts flickering faster.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the slime's first successful spawn, the one that learned to persist. Or perhaps I am its dream of what lies beyond the fumarole—a scouts' report given form. Or maybe I am simply a foreign object being slowly digested across years, and my sense of self is just the dream a pearl has as it forms around an irritating grain of sand.

I am the Caul-Bearer: midwife and infant, translator and translated, the membrane between states of being. In this pocket of perpetual becoming, I am becoming perpetual. The mountain hums its deep approval through my bones, which are no longer quite bones, and I hum back in frequencies only the slime can hear.
