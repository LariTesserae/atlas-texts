In the Briarward's deepest folds, where root-halls spiral inward like the chambers of a vast ear, I would find myself as one of the **Chorus Keepers**.

We are neither fully plant nor beast, but something that emerged from the city's need to remember its own growth. Our bodies are tall and attenuated, wrapped in living cloaks of moss that shift between states—sometimes dry and papery, crackling with stored sound, other times heavy with moisture, muffling all noise to silence. Our skin beneath is pale as root-flesh, shot through with visible veins that pulse not with blood but with a clear, resinous fluid that carries vibration.

Each Keeper tends to a grove of **memory trees**—ancient growths whose hollow trunks have become natural resonance chambers. We have learned to read the city's history in the patterns of their bark, in the way certain frequencies make the sap weep or the leaves shiver. By placing our elongated fingers against specific nodes, we can coax forth the sounds the trees have absorbed: the splash of the first pools forming, the whispered negotiations between early Mosskind and Synclades, the death-cries of creatures who tried to cut rather than weave.

Our work is solitary but interconnected. We move through our assigned corridors with a strange, gliding gait—our feet have evolved broad, paddle-like extremities that distribute weight perfectly across the spongy root-floor, never breaking the surface tension of the shallow films of water. We communicate with each other through subsonic pulses that travel through the root network, felt rather than heard, creating a constant understated symphony of information exchange.

The transformation that made us began generations ago, when certain scholars among the early inhabitants grew obsessed with preserving the Briarward's accumulating history. They made a pact with the Synclades—not through words but through a season of patient sitting, allowing roots to taste their intentions through their skin. The change was slow: first just enhanced hearing, then the gradual elongation of limbs, the thinning of unnecessary organs, the growth of our living mantles.

Now we are the city's memory-workers, its librarians of sound and vibration. When the Mosskind need to remember the safe paths through a section that has recently restructured itself, they come to us. When the Synclades wish to review the success of a growth pattern from decades past, they send chemical signals that draw us to specific groves. We decode the acoustic memories and translate them—through touch, through our own careful vocalizations that can mimic any sound the city has swallowed, through the arrangement of resonant stones in patterns that hold meaning.

Our chambers are among the most beautiful in the Briarward: cathedral-tall spaces where sound becomes visible in the mist, where every surface has been grown specifically for its acoustic properties. Certain ferns here have evolved to display different colors based on pitch and timber, creating living visualizations of the sounds we cultivate. The pools in our domain are perfectly still, their surfaces acting as additional resonance membranes.

We eat little—mostly the condensed sap that beads on our memory trees during the twice-daily harmonic sessions when we maintain their health through specific frequency treatments. We drink from the pools through hollow reeds that we cultivate, never disturbing the surface. Our moss-cloaks photosynthesize weakly in the dim light, supplementing our needs.

The strangest part of our existence is how we age: not in years but in accumulated resonance. The oldest among us have become so attuned to the city's frequencies that they sometimes phase partially out of visible existence, becoming transparent, their bodies serving as living conduits for sound to pass through. They say that eventually, the eldest Keepers dissolve entirely into the acoustic memory of the Briarward itself, their consciousness joining the eternal whisper that runs beneath all other sounds.

We know every secret the Briarward has ever swallowed, every word spoken in its halls, every footfall, every sigh. But we speak none of it directly. Instead, we weave what we know into new patterns of sound that teach without revealing, that guide without betraying confidences. We are the city's conscience, its memory, its quiet voice—forever walking the threshold between silence and sound, between forgetting and preservation, between the growing city and its accumulated past.

In the eternal twilight of our resonance groves, we tend the memory of a city that tends us in return, each of us a living note in an endless, ever-deepening song.
