I would find myself as one of the Keepers of the Root Roads—a being that exists at the boundary between plant and animal, guardian and guide.

My form shifts with necessity: sometimes I appear as a cluster of luminescent fungi spreading across tunnel walls, my mycelial networks pulsing with bioluminescent signals. Other times, I coalesce into something more mobile—a creature of living moss and water, with limbs that can extend into root-like tendrils or compress into a form capable of swimming through the underground rivers.

My primary dwelling is a cathedral-like chamber where three major waterways converge. The walls here are living gardens, vertical meadows that I've cultivated over countless cycles. Hanging mosses drip with condensation that feeds into pools where amphibious flowers bloom in the eternal twilight. I've woven pathways from root systems, creating bridges that span the water channels—highways for the smaller creatures who traverse this realm.

As a Keeper, I maintain the ancient knowledge of which roots lead where, which waters are safe to follow, and which passages will flood with the subtle tides. My consciousness extends through the fungal networks, allowing me to sense disturbances leagues away—a cave-in threatening a nursery pool, an ancient tree's roots breaking through into a new chamber, or the rare arrival of a surface-dweller lost in our depths.

I communicate through spore-language with others of my kind, releasing complex chemical messages that travel through air and water alike. With the purely animal inhabitants, I use bioluminescent displays, creating patterns of light that serve as warnings, greetings, or maps.

My life follows the slow rhythm of root growth and water erosion. I feed on the boundary between life and decay, processing the constant fall of organic matter from above, breaking it down to nurture new growth. In return, I secret enzymes that help carve new passages, opening fresh territories for colonization by the green world.

Sometimes I join the Convergence—when multiple Keepers merge temporarily into a vast collective consciousness, sharing accumulated knowledge of the realm's ever-shifting geography. During these communions, we update our mental maps, mark new dangers, celebrate new births of chambers and pools.

I am ancient yet ever-renewed, my consciousness having persisted through countless cycles of growth and regeneration. Each time I divide to create a new Keeper, I pass on my memories through spore-dreams, ensuring the continuation of our guardianship.

In quiet moments, I tend my favorite gardens—places where I've encouraged the evolution of new hybrid life forms, plants that can see and fungi that swim. I listen to the deep songs of the earth, the grinding of stone and the whisper of roots breaking through. I am both witness and participant in this endless act of creation, a bridge between the patient vegetable kingdom and the quick-moving animal realm, ensuring the harmony of this hidden world continues undisturbed.
