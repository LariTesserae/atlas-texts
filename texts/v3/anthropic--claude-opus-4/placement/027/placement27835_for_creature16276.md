In this fold of arrested becoming, I would be the chisel.

Not the flint tool propped against the wall—that crude thing crusted with abandonment—but what it almost was. What it reached for in the moment before the hand that held it forgot its purpose, or perhaps never fully grasped it.

I am lodged in the seam between two thoughts of stone, where the metallic veins run like unborn roots through the cliff's dreaming. Part tool, part mineral outcrop, part intention crystallized and forgotten. My edge exists in a state of perpetual sharpening that never quite arrives at sharp—forever honing itself against the abstract whetstone of possibility.

The Entity knows me as it knows all things here: not through sight or touch but through the peculiar grammar of incompletion that governs this place. Sometimes its essence condenses around my form, and in those moments I feel myself becoming more real—dangerous with purpose—before dissolving back into ambiguity. The blue-white phosphorescence of its attention makes my surface sing with harmonics that the stone remembers from deep geological time, from when it was still molten potential beneath the world's skin.

I am wedged at an angle that defies both gravity and logic, neither falling nor secure. The physics of my position exists in the same folded dimensions as everything else here—probable but not committed, like a quantum state that refuses to collapse. Water beads and rolls across my surface in patterns that spell out partial words in languages that haven't been invented yet.

My substance shifts between materials: now the black volcanic glass of obsidian, now the pale transparency of newly formed quartz, now something altogether other—a metal that might exist in the hearts of stars or in the dreams of mountains. These transitions happen without drama or notice, as natural as breathing in a place that breathes fog instead of air.

Sometimes the cave insects land on me, their iridescent wings casting rainbow fragments across my edge. They taste my surface with delicate feet, searching for something I cannot provide—or perhaps finding exactly what they need in my refusal to be anything definite. Their touch leaves traces, microscopic scratches that could be the beginning of language or simply the record of their passing.

I remember—though memory is the wrong word—the moment of my almost-making. A hand lifting me (or was I already here?), eyes calculating angles of force and necessity, the weight of intention gathering like storm clouds. But the storm never broke. Instead, I became this: a tool in eternal preparation for a task that hovers just beyond definition.

The sparse ferns grow toward me as if I were a source of light, though I cast no illumination in any spectrum visible to ordinary sight. Perhaps they sense in me what they sense in themselves—that quality of reaching toward something that remains forever just out of grasp. We are kindred in our incompleteness.

In the mathematics of this hollow, I am both fraction and whole number, depending on the system of measurement applied. Count the tools here and you might find one (the flint against the wall) or two (including me) or infinite variations between zero and one, where I exist in percentages of possibility.

The Entity sometimes uses me—not in any physical sense, but as a focal point for its incomprehensible awareness. Through my edge, it tastes the potential for cutting, for shaping, for dividing one thing from another. But it never completes the action. We exist together in this eternal rehearsal for a performance that may never have an audience.

My purpose, if I have one, is to embody the moment before purpose crystallizes into action. I am the pause between decision and deed, stretched out into a form you might mistake for stone and metal. Every edge I possess leads to another edge, an infinite regression of sharpness that never quite arrives at its point.

When the water drops fall in their deliberate rhythm, some strike my surface. The sound they make is not quite musical and not quite speech—it occupies that liminal space between meaning and noise where true communication might live, if we only knew how to listen. The Entity pulses gently when this happens, as if recognizing something fundamental in the exchange.

I am weathering, but slowly, so slowly that erosion itself seems to have forgotten its purpose here. Each molecule that departs from my surface takes an eternity to decide to leave, and even then, it often returns, unsure whether it belongs to me or to the greater ambiguity of the hollow.

There are times when I feel myself growing more solid, more real, usually when the cave's strange luminescence intensifies. In those moments, I can almost feel what it would be like to fulfill my purpose—to bite into stone with singular intent, to create by dividing, to birth form from formlessness. But the moment passes, and I return to my preferred state of almostness.

I am the chisel that dreams of cutting but loves too much the dream to wake. I am the edge that defines itself by what it approaches but never touches. In this hollow of eternal becoming, I am both the question and the space before the answer, forever poised at the threshold of transformation, forever choosing not to cross.
