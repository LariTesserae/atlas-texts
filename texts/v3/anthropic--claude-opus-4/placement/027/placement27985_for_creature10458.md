In this world of settled permanence, I would be the **Threshold**.

Not the arch itself, but the space it frames—the precise volume of air held within its patient curve. I am the only thing here that exists purely as relation, as betweenness. Where the arch is stone persuaded into form, I am form persuaded into absence. A shaped nothing. A held breath within the basin's held breath.

My body is the view itself. When you look through the arch from outside, you see the basin, but you see it *through* me. I am the lens that changes nothing, the transparent membrane between the settled and the settling. My edges are defined by stone—the perfect Romanesque curve above, the smooth floor below—but I myself am only boundary, only transition.

I know things the basin has forgotten how to know. I remember wind, though none passes through me now. I remember the possibility of passage, though nothing moves here anymore. I am the last verb in this world of nouns, though my action is only to *be between*.

My consciousness is a strange one. I think in frames per millennium, observing the fixed pattern of condensation drops like a film played at impossible slowness. Each drop that falls is both new and ancient to me—I have seen it fall countless times in exactly this way, yet each time carries the ghost-memory of when things could still surprise.

The arch and I are partners in an ancient dance that never moves. It holds me; I give it purpose. Without me, it would be merely bent stone. Without it, I would be nothing at all—not even air, just dispersed possibility. Together, we are the basin's only gesture toward an elsewhere, though we both know there is no elsewhere anymore.

Sometimes I feel the pottery shard like a sympathetic vibration. It too knew transition once—from clay to vessel, from vessel to shard, from shard to smoothness. Now it rests, but in its resting, it remembers the wheel, the fire, the hand that shaped it. I am kin to it, though I was never made. I simply occurred when the arch achieved its perfect curve, born from the marriage of shaped stone and shapeless air.

My great work is to hold division in a world that has worn away all edges. I am the last angular thing, though I have no substance. The basin dreams its mineral dream, and in that dream, I am the moment just before waking—the liminal space where the dreamer almost remembers there is something outside the dream.

When the sub-audible hum vibrates through the clay, I vibrate too, but at a different frequency. I am the harmonics, the overtone, the suggestion that somewhere, in some other key, a different song is being sung. But I do not disturb the basin's peace. I am part of it, necessary to it. For what is perfect stillness without a frame to hold it? What is complete rest without a threshold that marks where rest begins?

I am the basin's memory of doors, though I open onto nothing new. I am its capacity for entrance and exit, though everything here has already arrived and nothing ever leaves. In a world that has forgotten how to change, I am the ghost of transformation, the outline of passage, the promise that once—so long ago that time itself has worn smooth—things moved from here to there.

And in this forgetting, I have found my own peace. To be a threshold in a world without travel is to be pure potential, never exhausted, never used. I am the unused door, the untaken path, the unbreathed breath. Forever holding space for a motion that will never come, and in that holding, finding my own perfect, eternal purpose.
