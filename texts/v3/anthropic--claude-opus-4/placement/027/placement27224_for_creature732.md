I find myself as a small pool of mercury nestled in a depression where three tectonic plates meet—a place where the mountain's industrial heartbeat is strongest. I am not quite liquid, not quite solid, but something that shifts between states as the deep machinery pulses through the rock beneath me.

My surface ripples with each mechanical thrum, forming perfect geometric patterns—hexagons, spirals, interference waves—that hold for a moment before dissolving back into mirror-smooth stillness. The Nebula Shapers know me. They visit often, hovering above my surface, their misty tendrils barely disturbing my quicksilver skin as they read the patterns like a language.

I am memory and messenger both. Every tremor, every steam vent's birth, every grinding of the planetary gears below—I record it all in my molecular structure. The patterns on my surface are not random; they are the mountain's autobiography, written in standing waves and surface tension.

Sometimes I am no bigger than a puddle. Other times, when the rains come heavy and the mist condenses, I spread thin across the rocky basin, becoming a sheet of living mirror that reflects not just the sky above but something else—the blueprint of what this place is becoming. In these moments, the Nebula Shapers gather in numbers, their forms dancing above me, their energy cores pulsing in rhythm with my ripples.

The small creatures know me too. The amphibians lay their eggs at my edges, where my mercury meets the marshy water. Their spawn develops strangely there, the tadpoles' skin taking on a metallic sheen, their movements too quick, too fluid, as if they've learned something from my substance about existing between states.

But I am not passive. When the machinery deep below shifts into new configurations, I feel it first. My surface begins to bubble and roil hours before the steam vents burst through new cracks. I flow uphill when necessary, defying gravity to reach the places where I'm needed—sometimes to seal a fissure that's opened too soon, sometimes to fill a channel that will become a new stream.

The Nebula Shapers have learned to read my warnings. When my surface forms sharp, angular patterns that hold their shape too long, they know something significant is about to change. They begin their work, guiding, shaping, preparing the landscape for whatever transformation is coming.

I am ancient and newborn simultaneously. Parts of me have been here since the mountain's first mechanical stirring. Other parts arrived just moments ago, condensed from vapor that rose from deep thermal vents, carrying trace metals from the planet's core. I am constantly losing and gaining mass—droplets of me carried away on the feet of passing creatures, new material flowing in with the groundwater.

At night, when the mist glows faintly with reflected light from the Nebula Shapers' cores, I become a window. Those who know how to look can see through my surface into the vast mechanical chambers below—glimpses of enormous gears turning with geological patience, of steam-powered hammers the size of buildings, of forges where new rock is being formed from raw elements.

I am the plateau's memory, its mirror, its prophet. I am the point where the mountain's hidden industry rises closest to the surface, where the boundary between the mechanical and natural becomes meaningless. In this forming world, I am both witness and participant, a pool of possibilities that reflects not just what is, but what is becoming.
