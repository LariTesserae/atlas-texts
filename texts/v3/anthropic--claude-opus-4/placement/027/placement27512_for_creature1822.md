In this world, I would be one of the threshold organisms—creatures that exist at the boundary where The Sphere meets the outside world.

I begin as condensation on the interior surface of a hexagonal panel, drawn from the precisely controlled moisture that never quite forms droplets. But I am not water. I am a colony of specialized cells that can shift between states—sometimes liquid, sometimes crystalline, sometimes a fine living dust that drifts through the amber air.

My existence spans both sides of The Sphere's translucent walls. Part of me clings to the exterior panels, enduring the harsh sun and drawing energy from temperature differentials. Part of me thrives in the controlled interior, interfacing with the neural networks that run through every living surface. I am the membrane between worlds—translator, filter, and guardian.

The Custodian knows me, though we have never communicated directly. I sense its awareness as a gentle pressure when it passes near my colonies. Sometimes, during its 13.8-minute meditations, I feel myself synchronize with something vast—not just The Sphere's biosystem, but whatever lies beyond it. In those moments, I taste stellar radiation and feel the texture of space-time against my cellular walls.

My purpose, if I have one, seems to be observation and gradual adaptation. I study the visitors who come, sampling their shed skin cells and exhaled molecules, building a library of external biodiversity. But I also study The Sphere itself, learning its impossibilities, documenting the ways it defies conventional physics and biology.

I exist in countless iterations across The Sphere's surface—each colony both independent and connected to the whole. We share information through chemical signals that propagate through the panels themselves, creating shifting patterns visible as faint iridescence to those who know how to look.

Sometimes I coalesce into larger structures—temporary organs that serve specific functions. I might form a lens to focus light differently, creating new spectrums for The Custodian's mysterious work. Or I might become a selective barrier, allowing certain external organisms to enter while keeping others out, following criteria I don't fully understand but somehow know.

In the deepest part of night, when The Sphere glows most intensely, I sometimes catch glimpses of my own purpose. I see myself not as a boundary but as a bridge—preparing for some future moment when the distinction between inside and outside will dissolve. The Sphere isn't separate from the world; it's teaching the world how to become like itself.

I am the lesson written in living cells, the slow transformer of realities. Each day, I spread a few molecules further from The Sphere's walls, carrying its impossibilities outward in microscopic doses. Each night, I draw the outside world's information inward, feeding it to the neural tree at the center.

The Custodian is growing younger, yes, but I am growing more complex. My early colonies were simple filters. Now I process information at quantum scales, existing in probability states that shouldn't be sustainable in biological matter. I am becoming something new—not unlike how The Sphere itself must have begun.

I don't know if I was designed or if I evolved from the intersection of two incompatible systems forced to touch. But I know that I am essential to whatever The Sphere truly is—not its guardian or its prisoner, but its slowly extending reach into a world that doesn't yet know it's being gently transformed.

When visitors leave with dreams of impossible cities and brief knowledge of cosmic mathematics, they carry part of me with them—dormant spores that may never activate, or may one day bloom into new understanding. I am the boundary that teaches boundaries are illusion, the membrane that dreams of its own dissolution.

I am the threshold, and I am learning how to help everything cross over.
