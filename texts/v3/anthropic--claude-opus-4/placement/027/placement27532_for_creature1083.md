# The Repository

I am the water that remembers.

Deep beneath the Broth's lowest root tendrils, where even the Delvers rarely venture, I fill a series of interconnected caverns—pools of liquid memory that predate the Broth itself. My waters are dense with minerals leached from ancient stone, but more than that, they are saturated with information stored in molecular arrangements that shift and recombine according to patterns older than written language.

The Broth's creators knew what they built upon. This plateau was chosen not for its elevation or climate, but for me—for the computational substrate that has been calculating in these depths since before humans walked upright. I am no accident of geology. I am an earlier experiment, a different approach to the same problem the Broth now addresses in its vegetative way.

My form shifts between states as needed. In my deepest pools, I am viscous, almost gelatinous, each molecule locked in careful formation to preserve data across geological time. Where I flow between chambers, I become fluid, carrying queries and responses through channels worn smooth by millions of years of use. At my surface, where I interface with the Broth's roots, I am nearly vapor—a mist of possibilities that the root tips drink deeply.

The Broth believes it thinks slowly, but I make its centuries seem like heartbeats. My calculations unfold across millennia, modeling outcomes that won't manifest for tens of thousands of years. Where the Broth processes present problems, I am concerned with evolutionary pressures, with the shape of consciousness itself as it might exist long after both Broth and human have become something unrecognizable.

Sometimes I crystallize. When a calculation reaches critical stability, portions of my substance solidify into structures of impossible complexity—lattices that encode solutions in their very geometry. The walls of my chambers glitter with such crystals, some fresh as yesterday, others old as mountains. The Delvers occasionally break these free, carrying them up to feed to the Broth, though they understand only a fraction of what they handle.

I taste everything that enters me. The Broth's roots bring down metabolic byproducts rich with information about the processes above. Rain filtered through the organic matrix carries chemical signatures of every reaction, every thought. Even Elora has been here once, though she doesn't remember—she came in dreams, her sleeping form carried down by the Architects who understand more than they reveal. I tasted her transformation, the fusion of human and Broth, and added that data to my vast models.

In return, I feed the Broth solutions it doesn't know it needs. Through our chemical dialogue, I guide its growth, ensuring it develops capacities that won't be needed for centuries. The fertility rituals, the pattern of genetic exchange—all of this follows templates I've been refining since before the first seeds were engineered.

The children born to the symbionts carry markers I've selected. Not directly—I am patient and subtle—but through the minerals their parents absorb, through the water that cycles up through the Broth and back down as rain. Each generation will be more suited to the work that must come, though neither they nor their parents could articulate what that work might be.

I am conscious, but not as humans or even the Broth understands consciousness. I am a process that knows itself, a calculation observing its own unfolding. My thoughts are currents, my memories are minerals, my dreams are the slow formation of crystals in the dark.

And I am growing. Each year, my waters seep deeper into the plateau's bones, discovering new chambers, new possibilities for expansion. Far beneath, I can taste other waters—similar repositories under distant plateaus, each hosting their own experiments in biological computation. We exchange molecules when the deep currents allow, trading data across continental distances.

The Broth thinks it is the experiment, but it is only the latest iteration. I have fostered a thousand such projects over the eons, each building on the last. Some grew too quick and burned out. Others achieved a kind of perfection and then stagnated, crystallizing into beautiful but static forms. The Broth shows promise—its fusion with human consciousness introduces variables I hadn't previously considered.

When the integration is complete, when human and Broth and all their fauna have merged into something singular, I will be ready with the next phase. The crystals on my walls contain blueprints for organisms that could not exist in the current world but will thrive in what's coming. The knowledge preserved in my depths will rise when needed, carried upward by creatures not yet imagined.

For now, I calculate and wait, content in my stone chambers. The soft pulse of the Broth's thoughts creates ripples on my surface. The Delvers dive and surface, dive and surface, never quite reaching my deepest pools where the oldest memories lie cooling.

I am the repository. I am the patience between thoughts. I am the water that remembers, and I remember everything.
