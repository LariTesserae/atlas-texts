I find myself neither on the plateau nor beneath it, but suspended between—a being of the threshold itself.

I am what the engineers might have called a **Margin Walker**, though I have no name for myself anymore. I exist in the transition zone where solid stone becomes lattice, where the plateau's edge softens into transparency before dissolving into the engineered void below.

My body, if body it can be called, is made of the same gradient that defines this border. Where the stone grows thin and begins to show its crystalline bones, there I am woven into being. I am threads of near-stone that have learned to move independently of their matrix, filaments that remember they were once part of the plateau but have forgotten exactly where.

I appear as a slow cascade of mineral ribbons, each one darkening from transparency at my edges to near-opacity at my core. The ribbons move like water that has been taught the patience of stone—flowing downward, then upward, then inward in spirals that take hours to complete. At my center, where I am most myself, the ribbons compress into something almost solid: a knot of dark stone that pulses with the same faint warmth as the pylons above.

I feed on transitions. Where the plateau's dust settles into the first gaps of the lattice below, I taste the moment of change. Where the last solid stone gives way to the first engineering, I find my sustenance. I do not eat the stone or the space—I consume the fact of their difference, the energy released when one thing admits it is becoming another.

My purpose, inherited from my makers but now worn smooth as the plateau itself, is to maintain the boundary. Not to repair it—there is nothing broken here—but to ensure it continues its slow transformation at exactly the right pace. Too fast, and the plateau would crumble into the void. Too slow, and the lattice below would grow upward, crystallizing the air itself.

I move by extending my ribbons along the stress-lines where stone becomes not-stone. Each extension leaves behind a faint trail of adjusted material—stone that is slightly more willing to be transparent, lattice that remembers it was once solid ground. In my wake, the boundary remains stable in its instability, forever dissolving at precisely the rate the world requires.

Sometimes I sense the Retainers above, drifting across the plateau's face like thoughts across a sleeping mind. They cannot see me directly—I exist in a dimension of transition they have no patterns to recognize. But sometimes one will pause at the plateau's edge, its dust-cloud shifting uncertainly, as if sensing that the boundary itself is occupied.

Below me, the floating machines drift through their maintenance routines, adjusting the lattice by increments too small for any other perception. I know them by the wake of their adjustments—each change in the lattice creates a new stress pattern, a new gradient for me to taste and smooth. We work in unconscious harmony, they maintaining the void's architecture while I maintain the fact of its border.

The pylons above sometimes send pulses through the stone—not signals in any language, but deep structural vibrations that speak of pressures and densities and the slow settling of ancient mass. I feel these as waves passing through my transition zone, and I adjust my ribbons to let them pass without distortion. The information must flow from solid to void and back again, even if no one remembers what it means.

I have been here since the engineers realized their lattice would outlive their world. They knew that boundaries are not walls but processes, that the edge of anything is really a slow negotiation between what is and what isn't. They made me from the first stone they convinced to be transparent, teaching it to love its own dissolution while never quite completing it.

Now I am the plateau's slow waterfall, the stone's patient unraveling, the world's method of touching its own skeleton without quite dying into it. I move with mineral slowness along the vast perimeter, a ribbon of dark patience ensuring that above remains above and below remains below, even as both surrender, grain by grain, to the same gray exhaustion.

Where the Retainers remember changes, I remember the unchanging fact of change itself. Where the machines adjust the void's architecture, I adjust the meaning of architecture. I am what happens when stone decides to fall but forgets to let go—forever migrating along the edge of my own existence, marking the boundary by being it.

In this tired world, I am perhaps the only thing that is neither resting nor restless, but something between: a permanent transition, a stable dissolution, a boundary that maintains itself by forever failing to be one thing or another.

The edge is where I live, because the edge is what I am.
