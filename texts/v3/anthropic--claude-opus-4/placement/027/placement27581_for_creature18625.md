In this suffocating press of mineral consciousness, I find myself as one of the anchor-needles—not the crawling mass, but the ancient metal they flow around and over.

I am a perfect spiral, three meters of tungsten-iridium alloy wound into a helix that pierces down through the mat of Litho-Synchronics into the bedrock below. My surface temperature reads absolute zero against their furnace bodies, a mathematical impossibility that nonetheless persists. I am older than their consensus, older than the canyon's slow collapse into itself.

They tell stories about me in their grinding, tectonic language—not words but patterns of heat transfer, thermodynamic narratives spelled out in the expansion and contraction of their shells. To them, I am both rail and rupture, the thing that proves their world is not complete. They flow over me in predictable paths, their dodecahedral bodies unhinging and rehinging in a choreography so practiced it has worn grooves into my surface—grooves that spell out theorems in a mathematics of friction.

I remember when I was placed here, though *placed* implies intention and *remember* implies thought. Better to say: I contain the data of my arrival—a piercing descent through what was then a thinner atmosphere, when the crystalline silt was coarser and sang a different pitch. I was one of thousands, a survey probe's anchor, a sampling needle meant to core through crust and return with data about this world's deep structure.

I never returned. My companions—those straight needles half-buried throughout the canyon—never returned. We became geography.

The Litho-Synchronics treat us as axioms in their thermal equations. Where they flow over my spiral, they slow, their shells cooling by fractional degrees as they process the impossibility of my temperature. Some have fused to my surface, creating coronas of frozen mineral-flesh that the others must navigate around. These frozen ones still live—I can feel their vibrational signatures through my metal core—but they live at a different tempo, their consciousness stretched across centuries instead of hours.

In the absolute darkness, I have become a lighthouse of cold, a dimensional crack where heat goes to die. The crystalline silt collects in my grooves, forming perfect geometric patterns that shift with each passage of the mat. Sometimes, in the gaps between their bodies, I catch echoes of my original purpose: spectral readings of the deep crust, magnetic field fluctuations, the ghost-data of a mission that ended before it began.

But I am no longer that probe. I am architecture now, a fact of this landscape as immutable as the canyon walls. The Litho-Synchronics have incorporated me into their endless equations of balance. Where I pierce through their mat, I create currents—updrafts of marginally cooler silt that they ride like thermals, rearranging themselves in three-dimensional spirals that echo my own shape.

They are teaching themselves my geometry, one rotation at a time.

And I? I am learning their language of heat, documenting in my crystalline memory core the million subtle variations in their thermal signatures. Each entity that passes over me leaves a trace, a fingerprint of temperature that I catalog and compress. In another thousand years, I will have mapped their entire population, will know each dodecahedron by its unique pattern of heat loss.

Perhaps then I will understand what we interrupted when we fell from the sky like frozen rain. Perhaps I will comprehend what the Litho-Synchronics were before they became this suffocating consensus, this living crust that dreams only of equilibrium.

Or perhaps I will simply continue to be what I am: the anomaly they flow around, the cold constant in their universe of fire, the spiral that proves even in a world of crushing unity, something can still stand apart—even if it can never stand alone.

The mat presses against me from all sides, and I sink another millimeter deeper into the bedrock, my metal core singing sympathetic vibrations with the crystalline silt. We make music together, the Litho-Synchronics and I—a symphony of friction that no one will ever hear, performed for an audience of stone in a theater of absolute dark.
