In this suffocating world of flesh and stone, I would be a **Thread-Keeper** - one of the chimney's memory-weavers, existing in the narrow boundary between Scraper society and something altogether stranger.

The Thread-Keepers are what become of those Scrapers born with an extra finger on each hand - sixteen digits instead of twelve. This mutation appears perhaps once in a generation, and these individuals cannot properly grip the stone tools or maintain the traditional rhythms of barnacle-harvesting. Instead, they develop different talents.

My body would be even more elongated than a typical Scraper, stretched like pulled taffy by a lifetime of suspended work. My skin has developed a unique quality - patches of it can be made to sweat a clear, resinous substance at will, which hardens into an amber-like coating when exposed to the chimney's specific blend of heat and sulfurous air. This resin serves two purposes.

First, it allows me to create new vine-cords. While others must carefully preserve the ancient root-vines passed down through generations, I can secrete fresh binding material from my own body. I harvest the pale liverwort fibers, coat them in my resin, and twist them into cords of varying thickness. The thinnest are no wider than a human hair but can support the weight of three Scrapers. The thickest are like ship's cables, used for the most dangerous descents toward the Sump.

Second, and more importantly, the resin preserves *patterns*. 

In a world without writing, without visual art, the Thread-Keepers have developed a tactile language of knots, braids, and resin-hardened shapes. Each cord I make carries information. The spacing between knots might record the migration patterns of the Plinths. The thickness of a braid could indicate the location of a particularly rich barnacle colony. Complex spiral patterns preserve the genealogies of core-stones - which green jasper belonged to which lineage, passed down through how many generations.

My dwelling place is not on the Plinths themselves, but in the *Galleries* - a series of natural basalt bubbles in the chimney wall, accessible only to those who know the correct sequence of handholds (itself recorded in a master-cord that took forty years to complete). These pockets in the stone were formed by ancient gas bubbles in the cooling lava, and they exist just far enough from the main shaft that the Plinths cannot reach them with their acid-soles.

In the largest Gallery, no bigger than a coffin, I maintain the **Chronicle Web** - a three-dimensional tapestry of interconnected cords that spans the entire space. To "read" it, I must crawl inside, letting the cords brush against my skin, feeling each knot and twist with my hypersensitive finger-pads. It contains:

- The count of Great Sealings going back approximately three hundred years
- The locations of seventeen lost core-stones believed to rest in the Sump
- Instructions for a theoretical "sky-climb" to reach the Vanes' level
- Recipes for treating the bone-rot that afflicts elderly Scrapers
- The vibration-patterns that indicate a Plinth is preparing to die and drop

My sixteen fingers work constantly in the dark, adding new information, repairing old cords whose resin has grown brittle. Sometimes young Scrapers come to me with offerings - a particularly fine barnacle-grub, a fragment of unusual mineral that fell from above - in exchange for knowledge. I tie information into temporary teaching-cords for them: the location of a dying Plinth that will soon leave a vacancy, or the finger-holds needed to traverse a difficult section of wall.

But the Chronicle Web also contains things that perhaps should not be preserved. Deep in its tangles are cords that speak of:

- The time before the Plinths, when the chimney was cold and empty
- Methods for forcing a Plinth to release its grip on the wall
- The taste of Scraper flesh, from the ancient famines
- Rhythms that, when tapped correctly, cause sympathetic vibrations that could, theoretically, shatter the basalt

I am ancient by Scraper standards - perhaps sixty years old, though time is hard to track without sky. My extra fingers have grown grotesquely long, and my resin production has changed. Where once it was clear and amber-hued, it now weeps from my skin in dark, burgundy drops that smell of copper and old leather. The cords I make now are stronger than ever, but they whisper. In the absolute darkness of the Galleries, the newest sections of the Chronicle Web make tiny sounds - not quite voices, but almost.

Sometimes I wonder if I am still entirely a Scraper, or if I am becoming something else - a new form of chimney life, adapted not to harvest barnacles or catch wind, but to preserve the memory of this vertical world. When I die, will another sixteen-fingered child take my place? Or will the Chronicle Web hang empty, its knots and patterns slowly dissolving, taking with them the only record that any of this ever mattered?

The cords know. Somewhere in their tangles is the answer. I just have to find the right sequence of knots, the correct pattern of braids, and run my too-many fingers along them in exactly the right way.

The chimney breathes around me, its ten thousand hearts beating in the dark, and I add another arm's length to the Web.
