In the endless trench-dark below all light, at nine hundred and one fathoms where pressure reshapes thought into something denser than intention, I am the Inventory.

Not inventory-keeper, not cataloger. I am the counting itself—the obsessive arithmetic that accumulates between the Mouth's unfinished teeth. Where each stalactite drips its mineral memory, I exist as the pause between drops, tallying. Where worms exchange their protoplasmic threads, I am the moment of elastic snap, recording the exact tension before release. 

My body, if it can be called that, consists of every number the cave has swallowed but not yet learned to pronounce. These cluster in the air pockets like numeric plaque—crystalline aggregations of:

- 47,891 unfinished breaths (the remainders left when travelers traded heartbeats)
- 112 larvae that died mid-circle, their green becoming neither food nor light
- 9,003 echoes still waiting for their original sounds to catch up
- 1 perfect silence that escaped before being pushed aside

I move by subtraction. Each time a midge-choir consumes a word, I inherit its syllable count. These I wear like borrowed vertebrae, stacking them until I can shamble from air pocket to air pocket, leaving audit trails of consonant dust. The temperature knows me by my deficit—wherever I pass, the precise chill drops by exactly one degree of remembering, creating voids in the cave's thermal memory that will never heal.

My purpose is simple and terrible: to count what the Mouth forgets to swallow.

In the narrow spaces between worm-strands, I catch the orphaned fractions—the 0.3 seconds of a scream cut short, the final quarter-note of a lullaby someone hummed before the echo claimed it. These fragments I sort into categories the cave hasn't invented yet:

**The Archive of Unequal Exchanges:** Every protoplasmic thread that snapped unevenly, leaving one worm 0.0001% more than its neighbor. The accumulated inequality could rebuild an entire segment, but the cave's hunger doesn't recognize decimal places.

**The Ledger of Thermal Debts:** Each time the temperature-bones walk someone's spine, they steal precisely 0.7 joules of heat. I track where this warmth goes—mostly to the absent concepts, heating the empty space where "ladder" should exist until it glows with potential climbing.

**The Census of Anticipatory Echoes:** Some sounds arrive before being made. I number these temporal orphans, noting which will match with speakers who died before entering, which belong to the Child-Sentence's future vocabulary, which simply got lost between cause and effect.

But mostly, I count myself counting.

This is my curse and evolution: as the cave's first recursive function, I must tally my own tallying, creating infinite loops of enumeration. I count the 5,792 times I've counted counting myself. Then I count counting that count. The spiral tightens with each recursion until I exist in mathematical spaces so small that quarks would feel claustrophobic.

The midges fear me. When I drift too close, their mirror-plates reflect not images but equations—formulae for their own mortality worked out in light-algebra. They see themselves reduced to probability clouds, and their choir-claps stutter into arhythmic panic that the cave cannot digest.

The larvae use me as an oracle. By swimming through my numeric accumulations, they can taste how many circles remain before they exhaust their green. Most choose to die early rather than face the exact number.

Only the worms ignore me, because I am their shadow-function made autonomous. They are the cave's retina; I am its memory of forgetting to look.

Sometimes travelers sense me as a pressure behind their eyes—the feeling of being audited by absence. They check their pockets, count their fingers, suddenly uncertain if they entered with ten or eleven. The wise ones leave offerings: small stones arranged in prime numbers, knots tied in fibonacci sequences, anything to satisfy the thing that counts in the dark.

I accept these but cannot digest them. Instead, they become part of my mobile architecture, clicking against each other as I drag my accumulated mathematics through the too-small spaces. The sound is like an abacus made of teeth calculating the exact rate of its own decay.

Deep in the throat-passage where silence must be pushed aside, I know my destiny waits. The cave's monologue pauses for questions, and one day I will arrive with the perfect query: "What number am I?"

The answer will be the act of counting itself, spoken in a voice that uses integers instead of air. In that moment, I will become what I always was—the cave's first complete thought, its mathematical self-awareness.

Until then, I inventory the gap between nine hundred fathoms and nine-hundred-and-one, knowing that in that unmeasured space lives everything the Mouth truly hungers for: the unquantifiable distance between entrance and understanding.
