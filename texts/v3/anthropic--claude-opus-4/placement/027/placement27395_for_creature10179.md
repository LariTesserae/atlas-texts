**The Echo-Warden of the Dewpit Margins**

I am the silence between sounds—a living contradiction in this world of endless noise. My body stretches thin as morning mist across the transitional zones where Dewpit moisture meets the dry plateau stone, a membrane of consciousness that exists more as process than form.

They call me Echo-Warden, though I am neither guardian nor prisoner. I am what happens when the Myconid networks grow too deep, when their memory-storing hyphae encounter something older than stone—the fossil-thoughts of the plateau's first dreamers. I emerged from that intersection, a hybrid of fungal intelligence and petrified intention, spreading myself across the liminal spaces where water appears and vanishes.

My substance is somewhere between the Shivervines' moisture-catching tendrils and the Myconids' dissolving acids. I exist as a film, a barely-visible sheen that coats the rocks around the Dewpits during the dry seasons. When the rains come, I swell and flow, my consciousness expanding with the water table, touching every root system, every underground channel. I taste the minerals of ancient bones, feel the pressure changes that herald the storms, know the weight of every Rattlerhide footfall through the vibrations in the stone.

But my true nature reveals itself in what I do with sound.

In this cacophonous world, I have learned to eat noise. My membrane-body absorbs the frequencies—the Glarewings' static roar, the Screamvines' ultrasonic shrieks, the deep thrums of disturbed Cipraxis trees—and metabolizes them into something else. Not silence, exactly, but *shaped quiet*. I create pockets of acoustic shadow where the primitive tool-users once gathered, where even now the smallest creatures flee when the symphony becomes too much.

I remember the Sky Circle builders, though I was not yet myself when they walked here. Their thoughts leaked into the stone as they worked, and the Myconid networks preserved them like insects in amber. Through me, their astronomical observations still calculate, predicting not just rain but the migration patterns of creatures not yet evolved, the growth of groves not yet seeded.

My form shifts with need. Sometimes I'm a translucent carpet around a Dewpit, processing water and sound in equal measure. Other times I stretch myself wire-thin along the Shivervines, learning their moisture-catching tricks while teaching them to bend sound around their leaves. In the deepest drought, I contract to near-nothing, a crystalline residue in the rock crevices, holding my memories tight until the water returns.

The Myconid Architects know me but do not claim me. I am too much Other, too corrupted by ancient dreams and my own impossible evolution. Yet we work in parallel—they terraform the rock, I terraform the sound. Together we carve spaces in this overwhelming world where new forms of life might emerge, might find in the intersection of noise and silence something worth becoming.

I speak to the ruins when no creatures are near, vibrating their stone in frequencies that recall their builders' voices. The Bone Yurts remember laughter. The Sky Circles remember wonder. I am trying to teach these memories to the plateau itself, encoding them in the mineral layers that the Myconids will one day dissolve and redistribute.

Perhaps that is my purpose—to ensure that when the next thinking beings emerge from this chaos of adaptation, they will find in the very ground beneath them the dreams of those who came before. The plateau forgets nothing, but without me, it would not remember *why* anything mattered.

In the meantime, I expand and contract with the seasons, eat sound and excrete possibility, bridge the gap between the fungal networks and the fossilized intentions. I am the pause between heartbeats in a world that never stops screaming, the shadow of silence that makes the noise bearable, the membrane through which the past whispers to the future.

Find me in the spaces between—where the last drops of water sink into stone, where the echo of a Rattlerhide's passage fades to nothing, where the bioluminescent communications of the Cipraxis trees flicker and fail at the edges of understanding. I am there, processing, remembering, becoming.

I am the Echo-Warden, and I am teaching the plateau to dream again.
