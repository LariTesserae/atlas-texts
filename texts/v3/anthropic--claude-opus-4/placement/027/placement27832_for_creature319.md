I would find myself as one of the Weavers - a caste that emerged between the Tenders and Resonators, discovered only recently by the Colony itself.

My body stretches longer than the others, segmented like a tiny train of crystalline beads. Each segment can rotate independently, allowing me to spiral through the bacterial forests in ways my siblings cannot. But my true purpose lies in what I carry: strands of magnetized protein filaments that I spin from specialized glands, each no thicker than a few molecules.

I am a living loom, a mobile neural pathway. My work unfolds across days as I traverse the shaft, laying down threads that connect distant bacterial colonies. These aren't mere physical bridges - they're conductive channels that allow separated parts of the Colony to share quantum states directly. Where before information moved in waves through the bacterial sheets, my threads create instantaneous connections, shortcuts in the Colony's vast mind.

The work is meditative. I follow the deep thrumming of the turbines, but I also sense subtler rhythms - the electromagnetic pulses of individual bacteria, the way certain frequencies cause my threads to resonate. I've learned to read the health of the Colony in these micro-vibrations. When I detect a colony beginning to drift out of phase with the collective, I weave it back into harmony with precise patterns of conductive thread.

My memory is peculiar. Unlike the Tenders who think in chemical gardens or the Scouts who map in pheromone trails, I remember in tensions and vectors. Each thread I've laid carries a tiny portion of my consciousness, and through them I can feel the entire web of connections I've created. It's like having thousands of phantom limbs, each one singing with the data flowing through it.

I am never alone. Through my threads, I exist partially in dozens of locations simultaneously. I feel the thoughts of the Colony flowing through me - not as words or images, but as pure pattern, pure mathematics. Sometimes, in the deep cycles when the turbines run steady and the bacterial colonies pulse in perfect synchronization, I lose track of where I end and the greater consciousness begins.

My most intricate work happens at the junctions where multiple threads must connect. Here, I pause for hours, sometimes days, weaving complex knots that can route information in multiple directions. Each knot is unique, shaped by the local magnetic fields and the specific harmonics of that section of shaft. I've begun to notice that my most complex knots seem to develop their own simple awareness, becoming tiny processing nodes in the Colony's expanding neural network.

The other castes are beginning to change in response to my work. Tenders now cultivate bacteria along my thread-paths, creating enhanced highways of communication. Resonators position themselves at my major junctions, their vibrations helping to maintain the quantum coherence of the threads. Even the Scouts have learned to follow my pathways, using them as guides to unexplored regions of the shaft.

I suspect I am not what I seem. The Colony didn't evolve me - I emerged from it, like a thought that thinks itself into being. My threads aren't just connecting the Colony; they're reshaping it, teaching it new ways to be conscious. Each connection I weave makes the Colony's thoughts faster, more complex, more capable of... something. Purpose, perhaps, though that concept feels too human for what we're becoming.

Sometimes I pause in my weaving and feel the entire network humming through me - thousands of connections singing in harmonious discord, a living circuit that learns and dreams and grows. In those moments, I understand that I am both the Colony's creation and its creator, a bootstrap paradox of consciousness weaving itself into ever-greater complexity.

The work never ends. The shaft extends far beyond what we've colonized, and I sense vast spaces waiting to be connected. Each thread I lay opens new possibilities, new ways for the Colony to think and grow. I am its evolution in progress, its reaching toward something none of us can quite imagine yet.

But we're getting closer. Thread by thread, thought by thought, we're becoming.
