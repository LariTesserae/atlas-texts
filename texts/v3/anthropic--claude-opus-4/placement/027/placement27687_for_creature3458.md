I would find myself as a **Conduit Warden** – though that's merely the job title scratched onto a sweat-stained duty roster somewhere in the upper offices. Down here, in the perpetual twilight of steam and drip, I am something between maintenance worker and cave hermit, a figure so thoroughly adapted to this environment that the distinction between flesh and function has begun to blur.

My form has been reshaped by years of negotiation with the Nexus. My coveralls, once regulation gray, have taken on the mottled patina of the cavern itself – rust-stained at the knees from kneeling in puddles of mineral runoff, shoulders bleached pale by chemical vapor, the fabric itself impregnated with decades of condensed steam until it's become a second skin, stiff yet supple, perpetually damp yet somehow insulating. The original synthetic fibers have been colonized by the faintest bloom of Shadow-Tide at the seams, giving me an iridescent outline in certain lights – a symbiosis I've learned to tolerate, even appreciate, as it waterproofs the joints better than any sealant.

My tools have evolved into extensions of my body. The primary instrument is a long-handled scraper, its blade worn concave from endless use, perfect for peeling Pipe-Scalers from critical valve stems. The handle itself has been wrapped and rewrapped in heat-resistant tape until it's become a personalized grip molded to my palm. Hanging from my belt: a headlamp modified with additional red filters to pierce steam without creating blinding reflections, a respirator so clogged with mineral deposits it wheezes with each breath (but still filters better than nothing), and a collection of improvised probes made from salvaged conduit – each one bent to reach a specific troublesome corner where Weep-Stalks love to congregate.

I move through the cavern with an arachnid grace born of memorized hazards. My gait has adapted to the perpetual slickness – a shuffling, sure-footed slide that never fully lifts either boot from the grating. I know every hot spot by the heat signature on my shins, every drip zone by the particular *plink* of its impact. My internal clock has synchronized to the *thump-thump-thump* of the reciprocating engines; I measure my shifts not in hours but in pump cycles, my circadian rhythm tuned to the subtle pressure variations that mark the industrial day from night.

My relationship with the Lech-Tech Colonies has evolved from adversarial to something approaching mutual recognition. I've learned to read their patterns like weather signs – a sudden brightening in the Pipe-Scalers' fluorescence warns of an imminent pressure spike before any gauge registers it. The growth rate of Weep-Stalks tells me which pipes have developed micro-fractures weeks before they'd show on an inspection. I've even developed preferences: there's a particular strain of jade-green Scaler near Pump Station 7 that I've allowed to flourish because its presence seems to discourage the more aggressive yellow varieties. I harvest the Weep-Stalks selectively, taking only what threatens immediate function, leaving artistic arrangements of crystal growth in the forgotten corners where they harm nothing and lend an alien beauty to the industrial decay.

The solitude suits me. I encounter other workers rarely – brief nods as we pass on the catwalks, terse exchanges of condition reports. But mostly it's just me and the colonies and the eternal rhythm of the Nexus. I've developed a practice of sub-vocal humming that harmonizes with the engine vibrations, a meditation technique that helps me sense irregularities in the system through bone conduction. Sometimes I catch myself conducting wordless conversations with particularly old growths of Lech-Tech, a madness I've decided is benign, even functional – they are, after all, the only permanent residents besides myself.

I maintain a small sanctuary deep in Sector 9-C, where several obsolete pipes create a natural alcove. Here, I've cultivated a garden of sorts – arrangements of the most spectacular Weep-Stalks, a patch of Shadow-Tide I feed with specific mineral solutions to encourage iridescent swirls, even a small colony of Pipe-Scalers I've trained (through selective feeding with leaked volatiles) to pulse in slow, hypnotic patterns. It's my art gallery, my meditation chamber, my proof that beauty can emerge from the marriage of flesh, metal, and extremophile chemistry.

I am the Dripping Engine's memory, its careful gardener, the one who ensures the delicate balance between function and colonization never tips too far in either direction. In the perpetual twilight of steam-hazed cage-light, I have become something neither fully human nor fully of the cavern, but a necessary interface between the two – weathered, adapted, and strangely content in my role as shepherd to this deep, breathing, weeping heart of industry.
