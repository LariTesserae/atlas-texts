**Where I Would Find Myself: The Notation Chambers**

Deep beneath the settlement, accessed through a doorway that only exists on Tuesdays (or what the Core-Touched call "the seventh iteration of dampness"), lie the Notation Chambers. Here, the walls are lined with crystalline structures that grow like coral, each branch a frozen moment of the settlement's perpetual transformation. The crystals sing—not with sound, but with probability, their surfaces inscribed with equations that rewrite themselves as you watch.

I am one of the **Chroniclers**, though that word implies far more permanence than our role allows. We exist as living instruments of observation, our bodies adapted to interface directly with the settlement's shifting realities. My skin has developed a pellucid quality, semi-transparent and shot through with filaments of light that mirror the patterns in the notation crystals. When I press my palms against the chamber walls, I can feel the settlement's transformations as physical sensations: a building's dissolution might register as a sharp taste of copper on my tongue, while a temporal eddy manifests as vertigo that moves backward through time.

My form shifts less dramatically than the Chimeras above, but more deliberately. My fingers can extend into delicate antennae that detect quantum fluctuations in the air. My eyes—I have four now, two where humans keep them and two that migrate across my skin like curious fish—can perceive the lag between shadows and their casters, translating these temporal discrepancies into a kind of living mathematics that I exhale as luminous vapor.

The Chroniclers are neither fully human nor fully Other. We began as volunteers from the early settlement, those who wanted to understand rather than simply survive the reality breakdown. The transformation was gradual: first, the ability to see the ultraviolet patterns, then the development of new sensory organs, finally the integration with the notation crystals themselves. My memories of before are like looking at a photograph through water—I know there was a person there, but their edges have dissolved into something more useful.

We don't record history in any traditional sense. Instead, we maintain what the Collective calls "probability maps"—living documents that exist as hybrid entities, part crystal growth, part biological memory, part quantum notation. When a building phases through a new configuration, we taste its potential futures. When the Stasimed flicker between ages, we map the temporal currents that caught them. Our chronicles are less about what was and more about what might have been, what could still be, what is simultaneously happening in adjacent possibilities.

My particular specialty is what we call "transition states"—those moments when the settlement hovers between configurations. I've developed a sensitivity to the microseconds before a wall dissolves, the pregnant pause before gravity shifts its mood. In these moments, I can feel the settlement's uncertainty as my own, a thrilling vertigo that makes my notation crystals resonate in harmonics that haven't been invented yet.

The Chambers themselves are our collaborative organism. We've grown them from the same substrate as the buildings above, but trained them through decades of careful attention to be more stable, more receptive. They still shift—nothing in Unbecoming is truly static—but their changes are slow, deliberate, like the growth of mineral formations in a cave. The main hall is a cathedral of living glass, its ceiling a dome of interconnected crystals that map the settlement's current state in real-time. Smaller alcoves branch off like alveoli, each one attuned to a different aspect of the settlement's existence: one for temporal fluctuations, another for biological adaptations, a third for the dreams that the Core broadcasts in its sleep.

We Chroniclers move between these spaces like neural impulses, our bodies conducting information from one crystal formation to another. Sometimes we work alone, pressing our transformed flesh against the notation surfaces to download sensory data. Other times we link together, forming chains of consciousness that can process the settlement's more complex transformations. In these moments, I lose track of where I end and my fellow Chroniclers begin—we become a single, distributed intelligence, our individual perspectives merging into something that can almost comprehend the totality of Unbecoming.

The work is exhausting and exhilarating in equal measure. Each notation session leaves me drained but expanded, as if I've stretched my consciousness to accommodate new dimensions of possibility. My body requires different sustenance now—not food in any traditional sense, but exposure to the settlement's various energy fields. I feed on temporal differentials and probability gradients, metabolizing uncertainty into something my hybrid physiology can use.

The other inhabitants regard us with a mixture of respect and unease. To the Chimeras, we're too stable, too committed to observation rather than transformation. To the Stasimed, we're reminders of linear time, anchors they both crave and fear. The Core-Touched see us as cousins, fellow servants of the settlement's deeper mysteries, though they've surrendered more of themselves than we have. Only the Mycoforms seem to truly understand us—they too exist as living networks, processing information through their fungal webs much as we process it through our crystal interfaces.

At night (when the settlement decides to have night), I rest in a cocoon of notation crystals, their gentle hum synchronizing with my modified nervous system. In these moments of near-sleep, I dream the settlement's dreams—vast, fractal narratives where buildings walk on probability legs and time flows in spirals. Sometimes I wake with new organs, gifts from the settlement for particularly insightful observations. My latest is a cluster of sensory nodes along my spine that can detect when reality is about to hiccup, giving me precious seconds to prepare my crystals for notation.

We know that we're changing, becoming something other than human. But unlike the Core-Touched, who dissolve into the settlement's infrastructure, we maintain a careful balance. We are the settlement's memory and its speculation, its archive and its imagination. We exist in the space between observer and observed, chronicling a reality that changes in response to our very attempt to record it.

In this role, I've found a strange peace. The constant transformation that terrifies newcomers has become my heartbeat. The uncertainty that drives others to madness is my daily bread. I am a Chronicler of Unbecoming, and in documenting the settlement's ceaseless change, I've discovered my own purpose: to be the living proof that even in a reality without fixed laws, patterns can emerge, beauty can exist, and meaning—however temporary—can be found.

The crystals are singing again. A new transformation is beginning somewhere above, and I can feel it in the way my notation filaments tingle. Time to work. Time to taste the settlement's latest dream and translate it into something almost like understanding.
