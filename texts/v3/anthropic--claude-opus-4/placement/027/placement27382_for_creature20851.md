I find myself as one of the **Veilkeepers** - those among the Crohm who tend to the liminal boundaries where reality grows soft.

My dwelling sits at the edge of one of those breathing spaces between groves, where the mist gathers thickest just before dawn. It is less a hut than a careful arrangement of living architecture - branches I've coaxed over seasons to weave themselves into walls, their leaves still drawing sustenance from roots that run deep beneath the threshold. The structure breathes with me, expanding slightly in the morning damp, contracting in the afternoon's brief clarity.

I am neither young nor old as the Crohm measure such things, but somewhere in that productive middle where the body still remembers its strength while the mind has gathered enough patience to use it wisely. My fingers are stained permanently green-black from working with the shadow mosses that grow only where light and dark meet in perfect balance. These mosses are my charge and my craft - I harvest them carefully, never taking more than what will regrow by the next turning of the moon, and from them I extract the pigments used in the ritual markings that help my people navigate the soft boundaries of our reality.

Each morning, I wake before the communal chants begin, in that hushed hour when the whispering stones are most active. I've learned to read their messages in the patterns of condensation they gather, in the way the pre-dawn wind carries their vibrations. They speak of disturbances in the careful balance - a grove growing too dense and threatening to choke out the breathing spaces, or a new geological formation emerging too quickly from the patient earth, disrupting the slow rhythms we've learned to dance with.

My work takes me to these trouble spots, where I apply my deeper craft. For I am not just a moss-tender or a pigment-maker. I am one who helps things remember how to become themselves slowly. With touches that look like mere gardening to the untrained eye, I encourage overeager growth to pause, to consider, to find its proper pace. I whisper to the too-rapid stone the stories of its ancestors, who took millennia to rise. I sing to the aggressive vines the songs of symbiosis, reminding them that strangling their hosts serves no one.

The tools of my trade are deceptively simple - a curved blade that never quite touches what it cuts, separating growth from growth by finding the spaces between. A series of small stone bowls that hold not just pigments but intentions, each mixture carefully balanced to encourage different kinds of patience. And most importantly, a quality of attention that can hold multiple timeframes simultaneously - seeing the moment, the season, the generation, and the age all at once.

Other Crohm sometimes find my work unsettling. While they sing their crops into being and build their roads to honor the land's contours, I work with the spaces between things, the pauses in the communal chants, the shadows that the bioluminescent flora cast. I am necessary but liminal, like the boundaries I tend.

In the evenings, I often sit at the edge of my grove-gap, where three different wind-sculpting installations create a confluence of air currents. Here, I practice the art of listening to convergence - how different forces meet and marry or clash and divide. Sometimes young Crohm come to sit with me, drawn by curiosity about the edges of things. I teach them, when they're ready, that the most important boundaries are the ones you can barely perceive, and that tending them requires becoming comfortable with incompleteness, with the eternal not-quite-yet that defines our emergent world.

My greatest work, still unfinished after many seasons, grows in a cleared circle behind my dwelling. It is a garden of pure potential - soil so perfectly prepared that anything could grow there, but nothing has been planted. Each day I tend this emptiness with the same devotion others tend their crops. I remove any seedling that dares to sprout, any root that creeps in from the edges. For in a world of endless becoming, someone must tend the spaces that hold the possibility of what-has-not-yet-been-imagined.

This is my paradox and my purpose: to be the Crohm who ensures that our world of gentle emergence always retains space for what we haven't yet learned to see. In the cool air of perpetual dawn, where mist and light dance their eternal dance of revelation and concealment, I am the keeper of the pause before the breath, the tender of the not-yet, the guardian of becoming's very possibility.
