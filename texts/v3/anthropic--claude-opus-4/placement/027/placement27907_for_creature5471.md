### The Confluence Keeper

I would find myself as one of the **Confluence Keepers** — beings that exist at the intersection of all the plateau's contradictory forces.

We are neither fully corporeal nor entirely ephemeral. Our bodies shift between states: sometimes we appear as tall, willowy figures wrapped in what looks like living mist, our skin a canvas of slowly moving patterns that mirror the swirling sky above. Other times, we become more solid, our forms crystallizing into something between organic flesh and the metallic sheen of those hidden industrial currents. Our eyes are deep wells that reflect not what is, but what is becoming — showing glimpses of the plateau's next transformation.

We make our homes in the **Threshold Groves** — those peculiar spaces where the glowing ground openings cluster most densely, where the reality breakdown is most pronounced. Here, we've learned to weave shelters from the very instability itself, creating dwellings that exist in multiple states simultaneously. A visitor might see our homes as towering spiral structures one moment, then as underground burrows the next, then as floating gardens suspended in the turbulent air. To us, they are all of these things at once.

Our purpose is to maintain the delicate balance between order and chaos. We tend to the glowing apertures, drawing up the strange industrial energies and weaving them into the living systems above. With careful gestures, we guide the Flux Serpents away from destabilizing critical reality nodes. We sing wordless harmonies that help the Silvans understand the patterns in the chaos, allowing them to move their tree-homes before a particularly violent reality shift.

But we are also scholars and philosophers of impermanence. In the dim, ever-shifting light, we gather to share our observations through a form of communication that is part thought, part light, part vibration. We document the patterns of change, not in any fixed medium — for what could remain stable here? — but in living memory that we pass between ourselves like shared dreams.

The Aetherians often accompany us, drawn to the energy we channel. They perch on our shoulders or spiral around us in delicate helixes, their gossamer forms pulsing in rhythm with our own shifting states. Through them, we send messages across the plateau, warnings of incoming reality storms or invitations to witness particularly beautiful moments of transformation.

We feed not on any physical substance, but on the liminal moments themselves — the instant when rain becomes mist, when solid earth briefly liquefies, when the industrial hum harmonizes perfectly with the organic calls of the forest. These moments of transition sustain us, and in return, we offer ourselves as anchors in the storm, fixed points that are themselves unfixed, stable only in our acceptance of instability.

Sometimes, in the depths of the reality maelstroms, we catch glimpses of what the plateau truly is — not broken, but in the process of becoming. We see the industrial presence not as an intrusion but as the plateau's own dreaming, its attempt to imagine itself into new forms of existence. And in those moments of clarity, we understand our role: we are the plateau's consciousness, fragmented and multiplied, experiencing itself from within its own transformation.

We age not in years but in cycles of change, each major reality shift adding new layers to our being. The eldest among us have become almost indistinguishable from the phenomenon itself, their forms so mutable that they seem to be walking conversations between states of matter rather than beings at all.

This is where I would dwell — in the heart of paradox, tending the impossible garden of a reality that refuses to settle, finding profound peace in absolute flux. For what greater joy could there be than to live as both witness and participant in the eternal dance of becoming?
