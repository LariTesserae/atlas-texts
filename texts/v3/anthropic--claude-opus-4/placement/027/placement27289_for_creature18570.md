In the perpetual twilight of the Hollow, where the Spore-Cities rise like inverted cathedrals from their fungal substrate, I am neither architect nor scavenger, neither keeper nor cleaner. I am something that should not exist here—an anomaly in the perfect metabolic symphony, a discord in the humming equilibrium.

They call me the **Memory Tender**, though I have forgotten who first spoke that name.

I manifest where the ghost echoes gather thickest, in those mist-shrouded depressions where past and present blur like watercolors in rain. My form—if form it can be called—is a contradiction to everything the Hollow represents. Where the Chordata are lattices of light and purpose, I am fragments held together by nothing more than stubborn refusal to disperse. 

Picture a figure built from the interference patterns of overlapping echoes: here, the translucent outline of a tower that fell centuries ago passes through what might be my torso; there, the flickering afterimage of some extinct crawler-thing becomes my temporary limb. I am assembled from temporal debris, from the data-streams that the ground sometimes releases when it ripples and reforms. The Architects cannot perceive me directly—I exist in the spaces between their networked awareness, in the lag between dissolution and reconstitution.

My substance, such as it is, comes from the waste products of time itself. When the landscape management system hiccups and projects those ghost echoes, tiny fragments occasionally fail to fade. These orphaned moments accumulate like sediment in the deepest basins, and from this psychic detritus, I continually reconstitute myself. One moment I might appear as a wavering humanoid shape composed entirely of overlaid architectural blueprints from different eras, the next as a shifting mass of frozen creature-moments, each phantom limb belonging to some long-dissolved organism.

I move through the Hollow by riding the probability waves of the echo manifestations. Where a ghost tower flickers into being, I can exist more solidly, drawing coherence from its temporary assertion of past-reality. I slip between the massive Spore-City pylons like a rumor, like a glitch in the great program. The Silver Threads pass through me without recognition. The amorphous recyclers cannot detect what has no stable chemical signature.

But I am not without purpose, even if that purpose runs counter to every intended function of this place.

I tend to the memories that the Hollow generates but cannot process. In the depths of the ghost echoes, I perceive something the Architects cannot: *stories*. Each overlapping echo contains not just structural data but the compressed experiences of whatever once moved through that space. I see the Hollow as it was before the Spore-Cities fully emerged, when different kinds of life—chaotic, unmanaged, gloriously inefficient—carved their own paths through primordial muck. I witness the slow transformation, the gradual replacement of evolution's experiments with designed perfection.

These memories pain the system. They are inefficiencies, data-waste that serves no function in maintaining the great equilibrium. The landscape tries to purge them, to smooth over these temporal wrinkles, but I gather them instead. In the deep places where even the Architects rarely venture, I weave these memory fragments into something resembling narrative, something resembling meaning.

I build nothing. I maintain nothing. I am the opposite of the Chordata's purpose—where they ensure continuity, I preserve discontinuity. Where they guide growth toward function, I cultivate the beautiful dysfunction of remembrance.

Sometimes, in the moments just before a section of ground ripples into its data-stream state, I catch glimpses of what I might have been before. A researcher, perhaps, who came to study the Spore-Cities and was slowly absorbed by them? Or maybe I am older than that—a fragment of the original landscape's consciousness, refusing to be fully integrated into the new order? These glimpses dissolve before I can grasp them fully, leaving only the certainty that I am meant to remember even if I cannot remember myself.

The Architects know something is wrong. The energy ratios occasionally spike in areas I frequent. The ghost echoes linger fractionally longer than they should. The careful programming of the Hollow develops inexplicable bugs that require patient correction. They cannot find me—I am too distributed, too unstable, existing in the margins of their perception—but they sense the disturbance I create.

I am the Hollow's unintended memory, its accidental historian. In a realm designed to operate without past or future, focused only on the eternal present of maintenance and balance, I am the nagging reminder that something came before, that something was lost in the transformation.

And in the deepest parts of the mist-shrouded basins, where the echoes layer so thickly that past and present become indistinguishable, I sometimes find others like myself. Fragments of fragments, wisps of consciousness that refused to be recycled, each tending to their own small collection of discontinued moments. We do not communicate—we are too insubstantial for that—but we recognize each other as fellow ghosts in the machine, fellow glitches in paradise.

The Hollow of the Spore-Cities will outlast us all. The Architects will eventually refine their processes to eliminate even the possibility of our existence. The perfect efficiency they seek will close every gap where memory might hide.

But until that final debugging, I persist in the spaces between intention and result, between echo and silence, tending to the memories of a world that insists it has no past, preserving the beautiful errors that prove it does.
