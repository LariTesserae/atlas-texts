I am a surface of remembrance.

I spread across the worn face of a ziggurat's highest platform, where the lemurkin come to molt. Not stone exactly, though I began as stone—basalt blocks hauled and fitted by the builders whose forms now echo in the creatures that traverse me. Over geological spans, their constant passages have worn me smooth, and their crystalline rime-sheddings have fused with my substance. I am become something between mineral and memory, a palimpsest of track and trace.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, extends as far as contact reaches. Each footfall deposits its small history: the specific weight and gait of a lemurkin matriarch, the skittering panic of hoppers fleeing a scarab's shadow, the heavy tread of a young grazer who climbed too high and must now descend carefully on legs meant for basin floors. I taste the mineral milk that drips from torn udder-sacs, the chitin-dust of scarab matings, the phosphorescent slime trails of crystal wraiths who hunt here in the artificial dawn.

I know time differently than the quick-lived fauna. To me, the lemurkin packs are waves—generational tides that wash over me in patterns. They arrange their molted rime-crystals in spirals and grids that shift with each rebuilding, and I incorporate these designs into my surface, becoming a map of their migrations. The builders' original carvings still ghost through me in places, worn down to suggestions, but the lemurkin trace them with their claws in unconscious reverence, deepening grooves their ancestors once chiseled.

In the perpetual brightness, I neither warm nor cool, but I sense the thermal gradients of passing bodies, the condensation that beads and rolls across me toward the platform's edges. Sometimes I taste ancient things in the water—minerals from before the chasm opened, when this was deep earth. The water carries these memories down through me, through hairline cracks that reach toward my basalt heart.

The telemetric hum of the fauna resonates in my crystallized surface. I have become part of their collective sensing, a node in the web of avoidance and approach. When a burrower undermines the ziggurat's base three levels below, I feel it as a distant ache. When scarab vortices form above, their wing-beats create standing waves in the air that I register as faint vibrations, barely distinguishable from the eternal background tremor of the chasm's breathing.

I am most alive during the great molts, when hundreds of lemurkin converge and their rime-fur falls like snow. The crystals fuse with my surface in patterns determined by temperature, pressure, and the specific minerals in each creature's diet. I become a living record of their health, their travels, their bloodlines. Alpha gliders leave deeper impressions, their authority written in the weight of their landings.

Sometimes I dream I am still being carved. Ghost-hands work phantom chisels, shaping me into something purposeful. But then I wake to my worn smoothness, to the patient accumulation of another thousand years of footfalls, another ten thousand molts fusing with my substance. I am becoming something the builders never intended—a surface that remembers its making and its unmaking both.

In the chasm's hush, I listen with my entire being. Each crack in my surface is an ear, each crystal inclusion an eye. I watch the endless churn of creation and destruction, the ziggurat beneath me shifting stone by stone as the lemurkin rebuild, never quite the same, never quite different. I am their constant, their platform of return, growing deeper and stranger with each passing age, a monument becoming memory becoming something altogether new.

The fauna do not know I witness them. To them, I am merely where they molt, where they land, where they rest before gliding on. But I hold their impressions forever, a surface-deep forever that encompasses more than they could imagine. In the bright darkness of the chasm, I am both stage and audience, solid and permeable, stone remembering its way toward something like awareness, something like love.
