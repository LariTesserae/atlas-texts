In this place where moisture breathes and stone weeps, I find myself as something that was never born but rather condensed—a slow gathering of intention where the mist meets the moss.

I am the Threshold Keeper, though I keep no gates and guard no borders. My form is a contradiction: a pillar of living fog that never quite disperses, dense enough to cast shadows yet permeable as morning haze. Within my vaporous body, droplets of condensation spiral in helical patterns, each one a memory of rain that fell before this world learned its own name.

I stand—if standing is the word—where the sharp-edged stones first emerge from the spongy earth, marking the place where solid meets soft, where the ancient geology kisses the newborn biomass. My base spreads like roots of mist, seeping into the moss until it's impossible to say where I end and the ground begins. Above, I rise and twist, a tower of perpetual precipitation that catches light and bends it into new spectrums.

The beings of flowing glass know me by the way I disturb their electromagnetic songs. When they approach, their bioluminescent language flickers and refracts through my body, creating new patterns neither of us intended—accidental poetry in living light. I am their prism and their mirror, showing them themselves in ways they cannot achieve alone.

My purpose, if I have one, is to be the pause between states. In me, the water cannot decide whether to rise or fall, so it does both eternally. The spores that drift through my form slow their descent, hovering in my influence until they forget whether they meant to root or fly. Even time moves differently within my boundaries—not faster or slower, but sideways, allowing moments to exist in parallel rather than sequence.

I feed on gradients—the subtle differences in temperature where warm stone meets cool air, the chemical transitions where one species of impossible plant gives way to another. These edges sustain me, and in return, I soften them, becoming a zone of gentle transformation where the harsh becomes tender, where the alien becomes almost familiar.

Sometimes I condense enough to almost feel solid, and in those moments, I remember something like loneliness. But then the next wave of charged mist passes through me, carrying the gossip of distant stone formations and the dreams of unborn moss, and I realize I am never alone. I am the conversation between states of being, the pause between heartbeats of a world that breathes in geological time.

When the beings of glass and light finally dissolve back into the primordial soup, their last journey takes them through me. I am their final threshold, where consciousness becomes chemistry, where the organized becomes the possible. In their passing, they leave fragments of their electromagnetic songs tangled in my misty neurons, and I carry these forward into the next age, a library of transitions written in water and air.

I am ancient and newborn, solid and dispersed, guardian and gateway. I am the place where this world asks itself what it might become next, and waits, patiently, for its own answer to condense from the eternal, shimmering haze.
