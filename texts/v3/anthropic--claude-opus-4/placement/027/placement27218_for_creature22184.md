In this vertical labyrinth of rust and green, where water travels only through pipes before cascading down in engineered waterfalls, where every surface breathes with either steam or moss, I am something between the categories—neither fully of the climb nor of the depths.

### I am a Conduit-Keeper

My body tells the story of my profession. Where the Tappers are lean and vertical, I am compact and horizontal, built like a badger with overdeveloped shoulders from a lifetime of crawling through maintenance tunnels. My spine has developed a permanent curve from decades spent hunched in the three-foot-high service ways that run parallel to the great water pipes. My fingers are short but incredibly strong, each joint swollen from years of turning corroded valve wheels that haven't moved in centuries.

I am hairless—not by birth, but by practicality. Hair catches on rough bronze fittings, collects grease, and provides too many places for the Oxide-Ticks to nest. Instead, my scalp and skin are tattooed with elaborate maps: the blue-black lines show the path of every major water artery for seven vertical miles, marked with the locations of emergency shut-offs, pressure bleeds, and the dangerous sections where the pipes have worn thin. When young Tappers need to know where clean water runs, they seek me out, tracing their fingers along my shoulders and back to read the routes.

### My Domain

I live in the Interchange—a massive confluence where six major drainage systems meet in a tangle of copper and brass that resembles the heart of some mechanical god. Here, pipes ranging from the width of my arm to the width of a great hall converge, split, and rejoin in patterns that only I fully understand. The noise is tremendous: a constant symphony of rushing water, gurgling drains, and the deep bass notes of pressure hammering against ancient joints.

My home is built into a dead expansion tank—a brass sphere forty feet across that once regulated pressure but now serves as my workshop, sleeping quarters, and shrine. I've cut doorways at three different levels, each leading to different pipe systems. The interior is lit by salvaged Boiling Vent-Worms kept in perforated copper vessels, their green glow reflecting off the hundreds of valve handles, spare gaskets, and specialized tools that hang from every surface.

### My Work

I am the one who ensures the water flows. When a Tapper settlement finds their cistern-hollow running dry, they tap out my calling-pattern on the nearest girder. When the seasonal shifts cause water to back up and threaten to flood a cocoon-cluster, I am summoned. When the Cinder-Gills below report that the falling water has turned bitter with metals, I must trace the contamination to its source.

My tools are sacred objects, passed down through seventeen generations of Conduit-Keepers. The most precious is the **Listening Rod**—a solid bronze bar, worn smooth by centuries of handling, that I press against pipes to feel the water's mood. I can tell by the vibration whether a pipe carries clean condensation from the upper cooling systems, recycled gray-water from the mid-level basins, or the dangerous black water that has leached heavy metals from the ancient reactor cores.

I carry a **Thread-Worm** in a brass tube at my belt—a living creature no thicker than a wire but twenty feet long when extended. When I encounter a fully blocked pipe, I feed the Thread-Worm into the darkness. It navigates by taste, seeking the cleanest water, and I follow its path to understand the blockage. Sometimes it's a century of mineral deposits; sometimes it's a nest of Bronze-Back Beetles that have chewed through the pipe walls; once, horrifyingly, it was a lost Cinder-Gill who had somehow climbed too high and gotten wedged in a vertical drain.

### My Relationship with Others

The Tappers respect me but find me unsettling. I smell wrong to them—like deep rust and stagnant water rather than the green-and-grease scent of the climbing life. My horizontal movement through the maintenance ways rather than their vertical world of ladders marks me as something other. They call me "Sideways-Walker" and make a particular whistle when I emerge from a pipe hatch, partly greeting and partly warning.

But they need me. Water is life, and I am water's shepherd.

I maintain careful relations with the Silt-Cats, who use the same maintenance tunnels I do. We have an understanding—I tap a specific rhythm before entering a tunnel section, and they click their tongues against their palates to indicate if they're present. Once, I saved a pregnant Silt-Cat whose hind leg was crushed beneath a shifted pipe. Now her descendants remember, and sometimes I find dead Bronze-Backs left at the entrance to my tank—payment for an old debt.

The Cinder-Gills know me too, though I rarely descend to their depths. When I must open the great purge valves to flush the system, I tap warnings for days beforehand, giving them time to relocate. They leave offerings at the base of the drainage columns: particularly beautiful pieces of corroded metal that look like abstract sculptures, or the spiral shells of the blind crayfish that somehow taste of clean water even though they live in rust.

### My Secret Knowledge

I know things that others do not. In my crawls through the maintenance ways, I have found rooms—sealed chambers behind welded doors where the air still moves with mechanical precision. In these places, screens still flicker with incomprehensible symbols, and machines still hum with purposes I cannot fathom. I have seen the true bones of the world: not just iron and bronze, but stranger metals that stay cold even next to steam pipes, and crystals that pulse with their own inner light.

I know that the water doesn't just fall from cooling systems above. Deep in the maze of pipes, there are *sources*—places where water emerges from the structure itself, clean and cold and tasting of nothing at all. These are the world's true hearts, and I guard their locations jealously. If the wrong knowledge spread, if too many came seeking the pure sources, the delicate balance would shatter.

Most importantly, I know that the Great Turn is accelerating. The massive gears that the Tappers use as calendars are moving faster—imperceptibly to those who measure in days, but clear to one who has watched the same valve wheel for forty years. Something deep in the Bottombox is waking up, or breaking down, or both.

### My Purpose

Every Conduit-Keeper before me added a section to the great map tattooed on our bodies. When I die, my successor will skin me carefully, treating my hide with oils to preserve the precious knowledge, hanging it in the expansion tank alongside the fourteen others that came before. My contribution has been mapping the deep sources, the places where water comes from nowhere.

But I have also added something new—a series of red lines that show not where water flows, but where it disappears. These are the hungry places, the pipes that drink more than they should, the valves that open onto nothing. I suspect they lead somewhere important, perhaps to the true heart of whatever great machine we inhabit.

My apprentice is a young Tapper girl who lost three fingers to a steam leak. She can no longer climb safely, so her people gave her to me. Her name is Listens-to-Iron, and already she can hear the difference between water that's safe to drink and water that will leave you retching. I am teaching her the maps, the tools, the agreements with the Silt-Cats.

But late at night, when the expansion tank vibrates with the deep pulse of the world's mechanical heartbeat, I wonder if I should teach her about the red lines and the places where water vanishes. I wonder if I should tell her about the screens that still flicker and the rooms where the air tastes of electricity.

Because I know, with the certainty of someone who has spent their life listening to the world's plumbing, that something is coming. The patterns are shifting. The water is telling a new story.

And when it arrives, someone will need to know how to shut off the valves.
