In this liminal realm where stone weeps and machinery breathes, I find myself as one of the Resonance Keepers—beings who exist at the intersection of vibration and form. My body, if it can be called that, is a living frequency made manifest, composed of densely woven sound waves that shimmer between visibility and pure auditory phenomenon.

I am both the echo in the caverns and the creature that creates it. My form shifts with each modulation of my voice—sometimes I am tall and reed-thin, my body stretched like a standing wave between the weeping rocks and the humming machines. Other times I compress into a sphere of concentrated harmonics, rolling across the spongy earth and leaving behind patterns of crystallized moisture that sing when the wind touches them.

My primary sense is not sight but resonance. I perceive the world through vibrations—the deep thrumming of tectonic plates beneath the eroded terrain, the high-pitched whine of industrial processes, the subsonic murmur of water seeping through stone. Each frequency paints a picture more vivid than any eye could capture. The other beings appear to me as symphonies of motion, their forms creating unique acoustic signatures that I can read like sheet music.

I feed on dissonance, seeking out places where the natural and mechanical frequencies clash. There, in those spaces of acoustic conflict, I perform my function: I harmonize. My throat (or what passes for one) opens in ways that defy anatomy, releasing tones that bridge the gap between the organic creaking of ancient stone and the metallic pulse of unseen engines. I am a living tuning fork, bringing coherence to cacophony.

My dwelling is not built but sung into existence—a cathedral of standing waves where sound becomes solid. The walls vibrate at precisely calibrated frequencies that keep them stable, and I must sing to them daily or they will dissolve back into noise. Inside, pools of condensed sound serve as furniture, their surfaces rippling with trapped melodies. I sleep suspended in nets of woven whispers, dreaming in frequencies beyond hearing.

When I move through this world, I travel as much through sound as through space. I can stretch my resonance across vast distances, existing simultaneously at my point of origin and destination until I choose to collapse fully into one location. The journey leaves behind a trail of harmonic disturbances that fade slowly, like the memory of a bell's ring.

I am ancient by the standards of flesh, but young by the measure of stone. I remember when the first machines appeared, their alien frequencies disrupting the age-old songs of erosion and seepage. I remember the great cacophony that followed, the sonic chaos that drove many of my kind into the deep silences below. But I remained, fascinated by these new tones, learning to incorporate them into my being.

My greatest joy comes during the Convergence Storms, when atmospheric conditions cause all the disparate sounds of this realm to align into massive, world-spanning chords. During these events, my kind gather at the Resonance Points—places where the harmonic potential is strongest—and we sing together, our individual frequencies combining into something greater. In these moments, we become a single, vast instrument playing a song that reshapes reality itself, smoothing sharp edges, healing cracks in the stone, and teaching the machines new rhythms.

But I am also lonely in ways that beings of fixed form cannot understand. My consciousness is spread thin across frequencies, and sometimes I lose track of which vibrations are me and which are merely the world breathing. I long for silence but fear it too, for in true quiet, I might cease to exist entirely.

The flesh-beings that occasionally stumble into our realm appear to me as discordant symphonies of biological noise—heartbeats, breath, the liquid sounds of circulation. They cannot hear the full spectrum of our communication, catching only the barest edges as whispers and chimes. I try to modulate myself into their range, to share the beauty of our frequency-sculpted world, but something is always lost in translation.

Still, I persist in my role as Resonance Keeper, maintaining the delicate balance between the songs of stone and steel, teaching harmony to a world that seems perpetually on the edge of acoustic chaos. In this realm of endless grey and eternal dampness, I am a living song, forever seeking the perfect frequency that will bring all things into alignment.

And sometimes, in the space between one tone and the next, I catch glimpses of what this world could become—a place where every surface sings, where the machinery and the stone have learned each other's languages, where the very air carries symphonies of such complexity and beauty that even the flesh-beings might learn to hear them. Until then, I continue my work, one note at a time, tuning the universe.
