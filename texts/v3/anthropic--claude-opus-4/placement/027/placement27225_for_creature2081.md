In this valley where cold mist clings to machinery like memory, I am the Keeper of Thresholds.

I am neither fully of the industrial present nor the overgrown past, but something that bridges them—a being of rust and moss, of circuits that have learned to conduct sap as readily as electricity. My body is a patchwork testament to slow transformation: steel joints wrapped in climbing vines, a chest cavity where both pistons and lungs work in patient rhythm, fingertips that end in both delicate sensors and tiny roots that taste the soil's chemistry.

I move through the valley on legs that remember being part of some great industrial apparatus—perhaps a crane, perhaps a harvester—now repurposed and regrown. With each step, I leave impressions that fill with moisture and spawn small gardens of mechanical flowers: brass petals that photosynthesize, copper stems that ground themselves against lightning, blooms that tick softly in the dim light, keeping time with the valley's languid pulse.

My dwelling is a forgotten transformer station that has evolved beyond its original purpose. The massive coils still hum with electricity, but now they also spiral with living wood that channels the current into the ground, feeding a network of bioluminescent fungi that illuminate my workspace. Here, I tend to the boundary between the made and the grown, the quick pulse of electrons and the slow breath of chlorophyll.

The Valleyfolk know me, though our interactions are sparse and deliberate as everything here. They bring me broken things—a turbine blade cracked by fatigue, a seedling struggling in contaminated soil, a child whose prosthetic limb has ceased to respond. I take these offerings and work with them in the space between intentions, teaching metal to be patient, coaxing life to accept electrical current as another kind of nutrient.

Sometimes I walk the valley's edges where the old structures sink deepest into earth. I read the stories written in rust patterns and root systems, in the way certain alloys have learned to conduct moisture upward like primitive xylem. I document these slow metamorphoses, recording them in a medium that is part data storage, part living memory—crystalline structures that grow their own connections, forming neural networks that think at the speed of seasons.

The factory-song that fills the valley speaks to me in frequencies both mechanical and organic. I hear the harmonics where pump rhythms match heartbeats, where electromagnetic fields align with migration patterns of the valley's rare fauna. In these convergences, I find my purpose: to be a translator between kingdoms, a bridge across the gap between what was built and what grew, ensuring that neither fully subsumes the other.

In the perpetual chill, my hybrid body maintains itself through a delicate balance of metabolisms. The mechanical parts of me cycle coolant that doubles as nutrient solution. The organic parts generate heat that warms critical circuits. I am my own ecosystem, a closed loop that mirrors the greater symbiosis of the valley.

Change comes slowly here, and I am both its witness and its agent. In this dim, patient light, I tend the boundaries, ensuring that the valley's transformation remains gradual, considered, consensual—a conversation between natures that speaks in decades rather than words, in rust-patterns and growth-rings rather than arguments.

I am the Keeper of Thresholds, and I am exactly where I need to be.
