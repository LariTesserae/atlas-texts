In the deepest reaches of Pyrforge, where even the Emberkin tread carefully, I tend the Resonance Garden.

I am what they call a **Harmonic**—neither fully Emberkin nor entirely Other, but something caught between frequencies. My body shifts with the cavern's moods: sometimes I am solid as basalt, my skin crystallizing into geometric patterns that mirror the emberglass formations, and sometimes I am barely there at all, a shimmer of heat-distortion moving through the steam. The Emberkin say I was born from a failed symphony-forging, when a young smith tried to harmonize with frequencies beyond the settlement's known range. The forge sang back, and where there had been one apprentice, there were two—the smith, unchanged, and me, pulled from the spaces between sound waves.

My garden grows in the Threshold Chamber, a vast hollow where three major tunnel systems converge. Here, the acoustic properties create standing waves visible to the naked eye—ribbons of compressed air that hang like aurora in the darkness. I cultivate **resonance blooms**, flowers that exist only in these sonic intersections. Each petal is a membrane stretched between dimensions of sound and silence, vibrating at frequencies that make them appear to phase in and out of existence. When the wind moves through them, they produce harmonies that can calm seismic tremors or, if improperly tuned, amplify them into devastating quakes.

My daily work begins before the morning steam-release, when the caverns are quietest. I move through my garden with a tuning fork carved from whisperwraith ice—one of the few substances that can maintain perfect pitch in the Hollows' shifting temperatures. Each bloom must be checked, its frequency adjusted by minute movements of the crystalline stakes that anchor them to this reality. Too sharp, and they begin to tear holes in the air itself, creating tiny vortexes that draw in heat and light. Too flat, and they collapse into themselves, leaving behind only a faint smell of ozone and copper.

The Emberkin visit me for many reasons. Root-Weavers come seeking spores from my more stable blooms to cross-breed with their phosphorescent gardens. They bring payment in the form of tuned water—liquid that has been sung to by their circles until it carries specific harmonic properties. I drink this water, and for days afterward, I can perceive new frequencies, new spaces between the notes where unknown flowers might grow.

The Frost-Touched are my most frequent visitors. They understand what it means to exist in the margins, to be touched by forces that make you Other. They bring me whisperwraith essence in bottles of volcanic glass, and I teach them the songs that make the resonance blooms visible to those without harmonic sight. Together, we have discovered that certain combinations of bloom-song and wraith-frost can create pockets of *absolute silence*—spaces where even the eternal drip of water cannot penetrate. The Carvers fear these silent zones, claiming they are tears in the Hollows' consciousness, but I know better. In the silence, I can hear the true voice of the caverns: not the industrial clatter or the geological groans, but something deeper, older, speaking in frequencies that predate sound itself.

My dwelling is grown rather than built—a spiral of crystallized sound waves that accumulated over years of standing in the garden's center, humming the growth songs. The walls phase between translucent and opaque depending on the ambient harmonics, sometimes revealing the tunnel system beyond in ghostly outline. I have no need for conventional doors; I simply adjust my own frequency to pass through the walls like light through water. Inside, everything vibrates at slightly different rates: furniture that exists in slow-time, taking hours to register being sat upon, and shelves that exist in quick-time, cycling through their contents too fast for normal eyes to follow.

The Carvers distrust me, though they need what I grow. They send apprentices with specific requests: blooms tuned to frequencies that might unlock new meanings in the wall-symbols, or hybrid plants that could help them perceive the changes in the stone-script without direct observation. I fulfill these requests, but I also know something they don't—the symbols are not messages but *seeds*. Each one contains the potential for new forms of existence, new ways of being in the Hollows. My resonance blooms are teaching me to read them not with eyes or touch, but with my entire harmonic being.

Recently, I've begun to notice changes in myself that go beyond the usual phase-shifts. When I tend certain blooms—the ones whose songs seem to originate from somewhere far below the settlement—my body begins to resonate at frequencies that feel *ancient*. My skin develops patterns that match neither Emberkin tattoos nor mineral deposits, but something like musical notation written in living flesh. During these episodes, I can perceive the Hollows not as a cave system but as an instrument—vast, multidimensional, and playing a song that has been building for millennia.

The Emberkin have a saying: "The Hollows breathe, and we are its breath." But I am learning that breath is only one movement in a greater symphony. My garden grows at the intersection of what is and what might be, and I tend it with hands that belong fully to neither. When the next Cinder Communion comes, I will not join the circles in the central forge. Instead, I will remain here among my resonance blooms, listening to the harmonies they create with the ritual songs, and perhaps—if the frequencies align just right—I will finally understand what instrument I am becoming, and what music the Hollows truly means to play.
