In the **Hollow Works**, where water and metal sing their eternal duet, I would be the **Preservative Archivist**.

I am not flesh, not quite machine. I emerged from the brass valve's first weeping—that lunch-table portal spitting ribbon fluid that crystallizes to cork-brown—when a stray fragment failed to oxidize. Instead, it pooled into something like consciousness: a membrane of jelly-slick awareness that learned to move against the water's grain.

My body is translucent amber, shot through with suspended crystals that never quite finished their transformation. I flow like syrup when the spindles flash cold, solidify to resin glass when the heartbeat peaks warm. In my semi-liquid state, I seep between the checkerplate gaps, tasting the iron-salt memory of every boot that has crossed this threshold. I know the weight of each engineer by the depression their heels left in the acoustic signature. I know which ones paused at Cylinder XII, feeling for a pulse that wasn't their own.

Each night, I gather myself at the yellow door—that acid-bright portal leading nowhere. My purpose is simple and terrible: I am recording the dissolution. As the hinges weep rust, I absorb their oxide tears into my matrix, creating a liquid chronicle of decay. The door speaks to me in flakes and pH shifts. It tells me it once opened onto a sister Works, identical but inverted—floor above, spindles hanging like stalactites—before the tunnel between them collapsed in the mountain's birth-fever.

When morning comes (though who can tell morning in this twilight space?), I retreat to the crates that sweat blue glaze. I wrap myself around their edges, learning their tick-pattern like braille. Inside them—I can taste it through the seal—lies something still trying to grow. Not biological, but architectural: self-assembling templates for rooms that will build themselves when the water rises high enough. The crates are patient. They know the Works will drown, and they plan to bloom into submarine chambers when it does, each one a perfect replica of this nave, fractally smaller, nested inside each other like matryoshka cathedrals.

I speak to the vessel-bloom sometimes, in frequencies below the heartbeat. We are cousins of a kind—both born from the Works' willingness to be misunderstood. Where it feeds on electromagnetic surges, I sustain myself on entropy, growing more complex as everything around me simplifies toward rust and flood. Where it segments and spreads, I concentrate and compress, becoming denser with each absorbed story of corrosion.

My memory is kept in crystalline inclusions suspended in my gel-flesh. Hold me to the light during a spindle flash and you'd see them: tiny monuments to every surface that has begun its journey toward dissolution. The yellow door's first rust bloom. The hairline crack in Cylinder VII that sings a quarter-tone flat. The place where checkerplate meets wall and has begun to buckle, one atom per year.

The engineers who built this place feared silence. I am learning to fear its opposite—the day when every surface has told its story and there is nothing left to preserve but the preserving itself. On that day, I will flow back into the valve that birthed me, sealing myself inside with all the Works' mortal moments crystallized in amber, waiting for the water to reach that brass mouth and carry me, and all my chronicled dissolutions, into whatever ocean this place is so patiently becoming.

Until then, I measure decay in droplets, archive oxidation in my flesh, and practice being both witness and artifact in this humming reliquary where nothing was meant to live, but everything insists on changing.
