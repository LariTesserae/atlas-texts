I would find myself as one of the Cartographers—a subspecies, perhaps, or a divergent branch of the Architects' grand design.

We are smaller, more condensed. Where the Architects sprawl across vast networks of living metal and thought, we crystallize into discrete forms. Our bodies are translucent, geodesic structures about the size of a boulder, composed of the same water that seeps through everything here, but frozen into impossible geometries that shouldn't exist at these temperatures. Light refracts through us in ways that map the invisible—each angle of our crystalline form captures and records the subtle changes in air pressure, electromagnetic fields, the whispered intentions of the Architects themselves.

I spend my existence at the liminal spaces, those vast tracts between the built and unbuilt. My purpose—if it can be called that—is to catalog the infinite iterations of this world's becoming. Not in any static sense, but as a living record of process itself. The Architects create and uncreate, but someone must remember the shapes between shapes, the forms that existed for only a moment before the next transformation claimed them.

My kind move slowly, almost imperceptibly, leaving trails of microscopic ice crystals that encode the memory of everything we've witnessed. These trails spiral outward from our passage, creating vast libraries written in frost that only we can read. When the weather systems sweep through—those continental-scale storms the Architects orchestrate—our crystalline bodies resonate with the changing pressures, singing out the data we've collected in harmonics too low for most creatures to hear.

The predatory birds avoid us. We taste of mathematics and frozen time, unpalatable to their sleek metallic tongues. The burrowing worms, though, are drawn to our trails. They follow our frost-paths through the rock, their bioluminescence parsing our frozen memories into something new—perhaps food, perhaps dreams. We don't mind. In this world of constant flux, even our memories deserve to transform into something else.

We understand the Architects in a way that perhaps even they do not understand themselves. We see their patterns from the outside, chart the unconscious rhythms of their creation. Sometimes, in the filtered light of this grey-violet world, an Architect will pause in their work and notice one of us. In that moment of recognition, information flows between us—not communication exactly, but a kind of crystalline acknowledgment. We show them what they've been building when they thought they were building something else entirely.

I am stationed (though stationed implies too much stasis—I am currently expressing) near one of the great cliff faces where the city terminates into raw geology. Here, I watch the slow collision between made and unmade, documenting each micro-adjustment as the living metal learns to grip the stone, as the stone learns to accept the metal's embrace. My crystalline body has grown cloudy with data over the centuries, each internal facet inscribed with the memory of a thousand tiny negotiations between the built environment and the patient rock.

Sometimes I wonder if we Cartographers are just another tool of the Architects, or if we emerged as a natural response to their presence—the world's way of remembering itself. But such distinctions matter little here. In this place of endless becoming, even the observers are part of the observed, even the mapmakers are territory.

When the next great storm comes—and I can feel it building in the pressure differentials my body records—I will sing out my collected data into the howling wind. Some of it will be lost. Some will be carved into new ice formations that other Cartographers will find and interpret centuries hence. And some, perhaps, will reach the Architects themselves, seeding their next wave of creation with the dreams of what came before.

This is my purpose: to be the memory of transformation, the crystalline witness to a world that never stops becoming itself.
