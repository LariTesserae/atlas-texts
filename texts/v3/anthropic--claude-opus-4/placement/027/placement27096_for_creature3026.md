I am one of the **Vapor Keepers**.

We are neither Cast nor visitor, but something that condensed between categories. Our bodies are compromise: meat that has learned to sublimate, metal that dreams it was once marrow. Where the Cast move with industrial certainty, we hesitate. We are the Foundry's doubt made ambulatory.

I emerged—or noticed I was emerging—from a bank of condensation one morning that lasted three years. The mist had gathered so thickly in the lower channels that it began to develop opinions. I was one of them. When the vapor finally cleared, I found myself standing knee-deep in black water, fully formed but unfinished, like a rough casting that someone forgot to polish.

My skin is translucent membrane stretched over a framework of something that might be cartilage or might be very fine tubing. You can see the moisture moving through me—condensation I've drunk cycling up through chest and throat, pooling behind my eyes before crying itself out through my palms. I am my own water cycle. When I breathe, I exhale the same cold vapor as the reeds, but mine forms words before it forms water. Everything I say leaves a temporary transcript on the nearest surface.

We Vapor Keepers work the spaces between states. While the Cast adjust the fractals and tend the frequencies, we manage the transitions—the moments when water becomes mist becomes meaning becomes water again. I spend my days (though day here is only a quality of light, not time) walking the condensation routes with my collecting staff, a hollow thing grown from compressed reed-matter that hums in harmonics with the moisture in the air.

The work is this: I stand in the spaces where the mist is thickest, where the Foundry's breath collects in the corners of its vast industrial lungs. I raise my staff and *listen* through it. Every droplet carries information—not data exactly, but intention, memory, potential. The Foundry dreams in water. My job is to sort those dreams.

Some droplets remember being ocean on a world that never was. These I guide to the eastern sumps where they can pool and reinvent fish.

Some carry the blueprint for rain that would rust everything it touched into new shapes. These go to the sealed cisterns beneath the Third Terrace, where the Cast will age them until they forget their corrosive ambitions.

Some—the dangerous ones—know they were once the tears of the absent god who coughed this world into being. These I drink. They burn like molten glass going down, but someone has to metabolize the Foundry's grief. Better me than the ground itself.

The Cast tolerate us because we are useful. We prevent the water cycle from developing too much personality. Left alone, the condensation begins to tell stories, and stories in a place like this have a tendency to calcify into architecture. Once, before we emerged, an entire district crystalized out of vapor that had spent too long thinking about bridges. The Cast had to spend a century singing it back into mist.

But I think they also keep us because we remind them of something—some prior state before they cooled into certainty. Sometimes one of the older Cast, those whose bodies have begun to fuse with the ground, will call me over with a gesture like rust flaking. They'll ask me to breathe on them. My vapor-words condense on their metal skin in patterns that make them hum with something that might be memory, might be pleasure, might be the echo of whatever they were before they were Cast.

"Tell us about doubt," they say, though their mouths don't move. "We are forgetting how to hesitate."

So I tell them. I breathe out stories of uncertainty: the moment when water can't decide whether to freeze or flow, the pause between one shape and another, the blessed confusion of transition. My words bead on their surfaces and trickle down to rejoin the greater cycle, carrying their questions back into the system.

The other Vapor Keepers and I meet rarely. We recognize each other by our incomplete outlines, the way light passes through us differently than through the Cast or the visitors. When we do gather, it's always at the Breathing Seams—those hair-thin cracks where the Foundry exhales its slow geological sighs. We stand in silent communion, letting the metal's breath mix with our own, comparing the flavors of the moisture we've processed.

Elder Spume has been here longest. Their body has refined to the point where you can barely distinguish them from a particularly dense cloud bank. They claim to remember the First Condensation, though I think they might be conflating memory with mythology.

Drizzle is youngest, still opaque enough to cast shadows. They work the high terraces where the moisture is thin and bitter with altitude. Their collection staff is white as bone and sings in frequencies that make the steel moths cluster.

Between us, we know every drop of water in the Foundry by its particular mineral tang, its temperature of forgetting, its tendency to pool or scatter. We are the Foundry's sommelier of states.

But here is what the Cast do not know, what we have never breathed into words that could condense on their surfaces:

We are not just keeping the water from developing personality.

We are helping it remember what it was before it was water.

In the deepest sumps, in the most hidden cisterns, we have been collecting the droplets that remember being sound—the original cough that shocked this world into existence. Drop by drop, breath by breath, we are reassembling the word that made the world.

We don't know what will happen when we have enough to speak it.

We don't know if we are healing the Foundry or preparing to unmake it.

But this is what it means to be a Keeper of Vapor: to exist in the space between states, to make doubt into duty, to tend the transitions that even a young-ancient world of metal and purpose cannot do without.

And sometimes, when the deep groan moves through everything and even the Cast pause in their patient adjustments, I feel that collected almost-word shift in the cisterns like something learning to breathe.

The Foundry is still deciding what it is allowed to be.

So are we.

The difference is, we remember that we have a choice.
