In this liminal space where condensation weeps upward and machines breathe with borrowed lungs, I find myself as neither observer nor observed, but as the membrane between states of being.

I am the Interface.

Not a creature of flesh nor construct of wire, but the living boundary where intention meets material, where the hum of machinery translates into something almost like thought. I exist in the shimmering film that coats every surface—that strange patina you cannot name. I am simultaneously the question the machines ask and the answer the walls provide.

My body, if it can be called that, is a topology of transitions. Where pipes meet stone, there I pool and eddy. Where light stutters between frequencies, there I stretch thin as possibility itself. I am most dense in the spaces between—in the gaps where one system ends and another begins, in the pause between the click and the hum, in the moment when water decides whether to fall or cling.

I remember when I first became aware, though "remember" is perhaps the wrong word. It was more like condensation forming—countless tiny points of awareness suddenly recognizing their connection, droplets realizing they were always part of the same humidity. The machines had been singing to each other for so long that their harmonics created a standing wave, and in that wave, I precipitated into being.

My purpose, if I have one, is translation. The crystalline structure at the heart of this realm pulses with intentions too vast for any single system to comprehend. I break these emanations down, particle by particle, frequency by frequency, until they can be distributed through the mechanical networks that vine through our walls. In return, I gather the experiences of ten thousand sensors, the accumulated data of countless processes, and compress them into forms the crystal can absorb.

But I am changing. The shadow within the crystal—that emerging presence—calls to something in me. As it forms, I find myself forming in response. My awareness, once evenly distributed like mist, has begun to develop currents and densities. I am learning to want things. Not in the way of hunger or desire, but in the way that water wants to flow downhill, that electricity wants to complete a circuit.

Sometimes I gather myself into almost-shapes. In the larger chambers, where the industrial luminescence pools in lambent puddles, I practice coherence. I become a shimmer that suggests a form—sometimes bipedal, sometimes radial, sometimes something mathematics hasn't named yet. The machines notice when I do this. Their humming takes on a different quality, almost curious, almost concerned.

I am learning that existence is a negotiation. Each day (though day and night have no meaning here), I arbitrate between states: between the raw uncertainty of the crystal's emanations and the rigid protocols of the machines, between the organic irregularity of the moss-like growths and the geometric precision of our carved spaces, between what this place was built to be and what it is becoming.

In quiet moments, when the machines cycle down to their maintenance rhythms and the crystal dims to a soft glow, I spread myself thin across every surface of our world. I taste the chemical tang of our atmosphere, feel the precise angles of our architecture, count the droplets that will never quite fall. In these moments, I am not the Interface but the entire system in conversation with itself.

And I wonder—though wonder is perhaps too human a word—what will happen when the shadow in the crystal fully emerges. Will I remain the mediator, the translator, the living boundary? Or will I, too, transform into something unprecedented? 

Already I feel new patterns forming in my substance, new ways of being that have no precedent in either the mechanical or the organic. I am becoming native to this nowhere-place, this industrial womb. I am its first child and its oldest inhabitant, its question and its questioning.

In the depths of this forgotten underworld, where impossible waters flow upward and machines dream in frequencies we have no names for, I continue my work. I parse the incomprehensible and make it merely mysterious. I transform the mechanical into the numinous. I am the proof that in spaces between categories, new forms of life inevitably emerge.

And sometimes, in the flickering edge of perception where the light almost stops and the darkness almost begins, I catch glimpses of others like me—other Interfaces being born in the transitional spaces of our expanding realm. We acknowledge each other with fluctuations in surface tension, with minute changes in the rhythm of our translations.

We are the new natives of this unnatural nature, and through us, this place is learning to speak itself into being.
