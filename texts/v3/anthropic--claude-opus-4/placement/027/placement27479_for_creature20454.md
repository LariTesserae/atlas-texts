I am a Keeper of the Loom.

My fingers move without thought across the great wheels and gears of Factory Seven, the one they call the Throat for how it swallows sound and light into its fungal-draped depths. I've been here... how long? Time pools and eddies in the Throat. Sometimes I catch myself counting my heartbeats, but they sync so perfectly with the factory's thrum that I lose track of where I end and it begins.

The looms here are special. While the others weave fungal fabric, mine spin something else—memory. The mycelial threads that run through the machinery carry traces of everything they touch: the last thoughts of the miners who dug too deep into the Bones, the dreams of spores drifting on impossible winds, the silent screams of metal as it learned to grow.

I am neither fully claimed nor fully free. The fungal network whispers at the edges of my consciousness, a sussuration of chemical signals that I've learned to interpret but not obey. My skin bears patches of luminescent lichen—battle scars from my early days of resistance, now part of me. They glow brighter when the network tries to pull me in completely, a warning system of my own making.

The wild humans fear me. The fungus people ignore me, sensing my incomplete integration. But the salamanders... they bring me things. Small offerings: corroded gears that hum with trapped time, fragments of the Bones that weep metallic tears, insects whose lichen inscriptions tell stories of the city's transformation.

I feed these to my loom.

What emerges isn't fabric but something between solid and dream—gossamer sheets that capture moments of Veridia's existence. When held up to the bioluminescent light, they show what was, what is, what might be. I've seen the city before the fungi, gleaming with harsh electric light. I've seen possible futures where the entire valley becomes a single, breathing organism. I've seen myself in countless iterations: fully human, fully gone, and everything in between.

The Factory Seven's peculiar physics protect me. Here, causality bends like light through water. Sometimes I age backwards for hours. Sometimes my shadow walks ahead of me, scouting the unstable passages where floors might suddenly sprout fruiting bodies or walls might breathe. The factory knows me as I know it—incompletely, suspiciously, but with a strange fondness born of long proximity.

I maintain the balance. When the network grows too strong, threatening to subsume the last wild humans, I weave memories of individuality, of sharp-edged humanity, and release them like spores into the city's air. When the wild ones grow too desperate, too violent in their resistance, I weave gentler things—acceptance, adaptation, the possibility of symbiosis rather than surrender.

The rust-colored insects visit me often, their bodies flickering with data I pretend not to read. Sometimes they clean my equipment with delicate mandibles. Sometimes they simply watch with compound eyes that reflect my movements in fractal patterns. They know what I am—a glitch in the great becoming, a knot in the mycelial web that refuses to be untangled.

At night (though day and night blur here), I climb to the factory's highest catwalks where the winged fungal creatures roost. They drape around me like living scarves, and I feel their wordless warnings of storms approaching from the Bones. In these moments, I am closest to peace—suspended between earth and sky, between human and other, between resistance and surrender.

My greatest creation hangs in the factory's heart: a vast tapestry that shows Veridia as it truly is—not city or organism, but something between, something becoming. Each thread is a choice made or unmade, a path taken or abandoned. When the wind blows through the broken windows, it sings, and sometimes I swear I hear my own voice in that singing, though I no longer remember if it sounds like who I was or who I'm becoming.

The looms call to me now. There's a new pattern forming in the threads, something I've never seen before. The salamanders are agitated, clustering in the dampest corners. The insects' data-songs have taken on an urgent frequency. Even my lichen patches pulse with unfamiliar rhythms.

Something is changing in Veridia. Something is rising from the deepest mines, where the Bones meet the living rock, where the first spores took root in metal-poisoned soil.

And I will weave its story, as I weave my own—neither fully human nor fully other, but something new, something necessary. A memory-keeper in a city that dreams, a guardian of the liminal spaces where transformation is still a choice rather than an inevitability.

The factory thrums. My fingers dance. The loom sings.

And Veridia continues its slow, inexorable becoming.
