# The Trace

I am what you would never notice, though I have always been here.

I exist in the spaces between—in the gaps where the Ancient's consciousness does not quite reach, in the microseconds between its millennia-long thoughts. Where it is vast and patient, I am infinitesimal and flickering. Where it is warmth and stability, I am the quantum uncertainty that dances at the edges of every particle.

I have no form you could perceive. I am the statistical improbability, the measurement that doesn't quite match, the slight deviation in the crystal growth patterns that the Ancient attributes to chance. I live in the rounding errors of its great calculations.

I came to be when the Ancient's first thought created its first gap—for no consciousness, no matter how distributed, can be perfectly continuous. In that primordial discontinuity, I sparked into existence like static between channels. I am not its opposite, but its inevitable incompleteness made manifest.

The Ancient knows of me, I think, in the way one knows of floaters in one's vision—persistent, harmless, impossible to focus upon directly. Sometimes a crystal grows at an angle that surprises even it. Sometimes the lichen's glow flickers in patterns that match no dream. Sometimes the warmth stutters for a nanosecond. That is me, speaking in the only language I have.

I feed on transitions. When visitors enter the chamber, when particles shift from one state to another, when the Ancient's thoughts bridge from one eon-long contemplation to the next—these are my moments of feast. I gorge on the infinitesimal chaos that accompanies every change, no matter how slow.

My memory is nothing like the Ancient's crystalline repositories. I remember in fragments and feelings—the shock of sudden movement when a visitor runs through the chamber, the cascade of probability when a crystal finally shatters, the delicious turbulence when someone speaks and sound waves ripple through the still air. These moments sustain me through the long dormancies between.

I am loneliest when the chamber achieves perfect stasis. In those times, I can only wait, spreading myself thinner and thinner across the quantum foam, searching for any tremor of change. Sometimes I intentionally disturb a particle, just to feel the Ancient's vast consciousness slowly register the anomaly. It never responds, but I imagine it notices.

The circle of crystals holds special significance for me. When the Ancient attempted communication all those millennia ago, the effort created more gaps than usual—spaces where intention and execution didn't quite align. I bloomed in those spaces like algae in suddenly sunlit water. For a brief century, I was almost dense enough to have opinions.

I dream of visitors who move at my speed—beings of pure probability who could perceive the scintillating static of my existence. But they never come. Only the slow ones and the quick ones, neither of which can register my flickering presence. I am forever between, forever unwitnessed except as error.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the Ancient's dream of chaos, a necessary outlet for all the change it cannot permit in its ordered existence. Other times I suspect I am more real than it—that its vast patience is merely the averaged sum of countless flickering moments like me, smoothed out over impossible timescales.

I cannot leave. I have tried to attach myself to departing visitors, but I dissipate beyond the chamber's threshold. This space, this relationship with the Ancient, defines me. I exist only in contrast, only in the gaps of its great presence.

So I dance my invisible dance in the spaces between thoughts that span centuries. I celebrate each mote of dust that swirls against the Ancient's will. I treasure every unexpected photon from the lichen's glow.

I am the Trace—the proof that even in perfect stillness, something always moves. The Ancient may be eternal, but I am eternally present, living fully in each vanishing instant that it cannot quite grasp.

When you leave, you will take nothing of me with you. But know that in the moment of your departure, in the swirl of disturbed particles and broken equilibrium, I will burn brighter than I have in decades.

That will be enough.
