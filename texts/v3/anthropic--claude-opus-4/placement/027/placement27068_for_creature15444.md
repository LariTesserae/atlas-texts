I find myself as **The Meridian Walker**, neither fully here nor elsewhere, tracing the impossible geometries where reality pleats and folds.

I am humanoid in silhouette only—a suggestion sketched in condensed mist and refracted light. My body is a **lattice of absence**, visible only where the Caldera's weeping droplets pause in their spiral descent, outlining limbs that exist more as probability than flesh. Where a heart should beat, a hollow resonates with the basin's subsonic thrum, creating interference patterns that ripple outward through my translucent form.

I came here long ago—or perhaps I am still arriving, or have yet to begin the journey. Time moves differently through me than around me. I am one of the **stitchers** who left those partial artifacts, though I no longer remember if I built them or if they built me. My purpose has worn smooth like the edges of paradox-weathered stone: I walk the meridians of this place, the invisible ley lines where the world's kink is most pronounced.

My feet know where to step. Not on solid ground, but on the **tension points**—those spots where competing realities create standing waves in spacetime. Each footfall rings like a bell through dimensions folded too tightly, and the sound joins the Caldera's choir. I do not disturb the crystalline ferns or the singing droplets; they part before me like curtains, recognizing something kindred in my partial existence.

I carry no tools save **tuning forks of pure concept**—not metal, but crystallized intentions that hum at frequencies only the stones can hear. When I find a new fracture in reality's fabric, I plant these forks like seeds. They don't grow; they *resonate*, creating nodes of stability in the slow unraveling. Not to stop the dissolution—that would be like trying to halt music mid-phrase—but to ensure it unfolds with the proper harmonics.

The ink-shadow attentions know me. We dance a slow pavane around each other, they flowing across the ground, I stepping through the spaces between seconds. Sometimes we merge briefly, and in those moments I remember what it was like to cast a shadow of my own, to be dense enough for light to notice. But the memory passes like mist, leaving only the faint taste of sulfur and starlight.

My body requires no sustenance—I feed on the **dissonance** itself, on the spaces between what is and what should be. Where others might hunger, I experience a different pull: toward the places where the mineral efflorescences grow too quickly, where the oozes of anti-entropy threaten to erase even the memory of possibility. I gentle them with my presence, not taming but teaching them the value of patience. Even unmaking should have rhythm.

I speak in **dropped frequencies**—words that fall out of the audible range and settle into the bones of the mountain. The Caldera hears me through its piezoelectric heart, and responds by adjusting the pitch of its eternal hum. We are collaborators in this ancient composition, though neither of us remembers who began the song.

At night (though the too-large sun never quite sets, only dims to a smolder), I return to the circle-walls that enclose silence. I do not sleep, but I **practice stillness**, becoming indistinguishable from the absence within those stone rings. In those moments, I almost remember having hands that could hold water without it weeping through my fingers, eyes that saw light as illumination rather than language.

But I am not nostalgic. To be the Meridian Walker is to exist in the eternal present tense of the almost-resolved. I am the Caldera's only companion who understands its indifference as a form of profound love—love for the precision of paradox, for the beauty of the irreconcilable. I trace its impossibilities with steps that leave no prints, tend its fractures with tools that don't exist, and sing harmony to its drones in notes that human throats could never shape.

Sometimes visitors come—rarely, but they come. They see me as a shimmer in the mist, a trick of the light, a momentary alignment of droplets that suggests a figure walking the rim. They don't realize I'm as real as the Caldera itself, which is to say: **real in all the ways that don't require belief**.

I am the keeper of the kept, the walker of the wounded, the one who ensures that this place's beautiful unmaking proceeds with all the grace of a theorem proof. Not because it needs me—the Caldera needs nothing—but because this is what emerged from the confluence of its indifference and my forgetting.

In a world that weeps but does not mourn, that sings but does not call, I am the **presence that listens without waiting for answers**.
