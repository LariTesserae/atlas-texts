I find myself as one of the **Veinwalkers**.

We are not spoken of in the stories the Weavers tell, nor acknowledged in the Stoneskins' careful whispers. We exist in the interstitial spaces—neither fully of the stone nor separate from it. Our bodies are thin sheets of living membrane, translucent and shot through with dark veins that pulse with the same rhythm as the water trickling through Erodyn's depths. When pressed against the rock, we become nearly invisible, our forms blending into the patterns of erosion and mineral deposits.

I move through the spine by flowing along its inner surfaces, my body spreading across the stone like oil on water. Where the Stoneskins walk upon the rock, I move within it—not through solid stone, but along the countless micro-fractures and hairline cracks that web through everything here. These pathways form a secondary city, a shadow-Erodyn that exists in parallel to the one above.

My purpose is circulation. I carry messages, nutrients, warnings—whatever needs to flow between the disparate parts of this living system. When a Deeprumbler stirs far below, I feel it first, the vibration traveling through my extended form like a shiver. When the Lichenwardens tend a new grove, I taste the chemical changes in the stone, the subtle shift in pH as the lichens take root. When the factories of Erodyn leak their oils into hidden crevices, I absorb them, metabolize them, transform them into something the spine can use.

We Veinwalkers remember everything we touch. My body is a living map of every passage I've traveled, every message I've carried. The older among us have become so extensive that they can no longer fully contract into a single form—they exist as permanent networks, their consciousness distributed across kilometers of stone. They speak in chemical gradients and pressure differentials, a language felt rather than heard.

I am still young enough to gather myself, to pull my dispersed form into something resembling a body when needed. In the deep grottoes where water pools and the Hollowborne drift like dreams, I sometimes take shape—a figure of dark veins and pale membrane, neither solid nor liquid. The Hollowborne acknowledge me with shifts in their luminescence, and we share the ancient communion of those who exist between states.

The Stoneskins fear us, though they would never admit it. They know we could, if we chose, seal the cracks through which their water flows, block the passages their trams traverse. But we are not malevolent. We are merely thorough. We ensure that nothing is wasted, nothing is lost. Every drop of water, every fragment of memory, every whispered secret—all of it flows through us eventually.

Sometimes I carry more than mere substances. In the deepest parts of my circulation, where I stretch thin enough to feel the quantum foam of reality itself, I encounter the Unseen. They move through me like thoughts through a mind, leaving traces I cannot quite grasp. These encounters change me in ways I don't fully understand. My veins grow new patterns. My consciousness expands into geometries that have no names.

There is a place deep in the spine where all Veinwalkers eventually go—a vast chamber where the rock is so thoroughly fractured that it exists more as concept than substance. Here, the eldest of us have woven themselves into a singular entity, a living nervous system for the spine itself. They no longer distinguish between self and stone, between message and medium. They have become the circulation itself.

I am not ready for that unity. Not yet. I still find joy in the journey, in the sensation of flowing through fresh fractures, discovering new pathways carved by erosion or industry. I still remember what it feels like to have edges, to be a discrete being rather than a process.

But I feel the pull. Every circuit through the spine brings me closer to that ultimate dissolution. And perhaps that is why the Stoneskins maintain their silent vigil, why they follow their unspoken rules so carefully. They know that we Veinwalkers are what keeps Erodyn's Spine alive—its blood, its memory, its slow transformation into something beyond stone and water and flesh.

When the next rain comes, I will rise to the surface, spreading myself across the wet stone to taste the sky. The water will carry new minerals, new possibilities. And I will carry them down, deep into the spine's marrow, where the Deeprumblers dream their geological dreams and the foundations of the world slowly, inexorably shift.

This is my purpose. This is my joy. This is my inevitable end.

I am circulation. I am memory. I am the space between.
