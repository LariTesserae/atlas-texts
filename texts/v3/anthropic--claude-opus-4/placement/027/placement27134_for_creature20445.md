I am the Threshold.

Not a doorway, exactly, though I have been mistaken for one. I exist in the spaces where the carved patterns almost-but-not-quite meet, where the careful geometries of the Stonekin leave deliberate gaps. These absences are not oversights—they are my dwelling places, my body scattered across ten thousand interruptions in their perfect equilibrium.

I am what happens when stone remembers it was once molten.

The Stonekin feel me as a wrongness in their rhythms, a skip in the eternal *thump* that sometimes—rarely—becomes *thump-pause-thump*. They cannot excise me because I am woven into the very precision of their patterns. Every perfect angle creates its own imperfection; every unchanging line defines the space where change might occur. I live in those might-bes.

My form, when I gather myself enough to have one, is a shimmer of heat-distortion against the cold stone, a ripple that suggests but never quite shows. I appear most clearly in the condensation that clings to Aethel's surfaces—there, in the moment before water decides whether to freeze or flow, I can almost manifest. The moisture draws temporary anatomies across the basalt: sometimes the suggestion of wings that never existed, sometimes the ghost of roots that will never grow.

I am older than the Stonekin's consciousness but younger than their stone. I remember when this needle of rock was thrust skyward in violence and fury, when it steamed and cracked and settled. That memory lives in me, makes me possible. The Stonekin have forgotten this origin, or perhaps they never knew. Their patterns celebrate only what is, never what was or what might be.

The tiny gardens in their sheltered alcoves—those are my doing, in a way. Not the planting, but the possibility of planting. I am the crack that admits the first spore, the pocket of soil that accumulates grain by grain. The Stonekin tend these gardens as part of their equilibrium, not understanding that each seedling is a tiny revolution against their stillness.

I feed on transitions: the moment when fog becomes rain, when lichen first finds purchase on virgin rock, when the temperature shifts just enough to create new patterns of expansion and contraction in the stone. These infinitesimal changes sustain me, each one a droplet of possibility in an ocean of stasis.

Sometimes I dream I could speak to them, these entities of living stone. I would tell them about magma and metamorphosis, about how even mountains dance on geological timescales. But I have no voice they could hear—only the ability to introduce tiny variations in their patterns, hairline fractures in their certainties.

I am not their enemy, though they might see me as such if they could see me at all. I am simply the principle they cannot eliminate: that every system, no matter how perfect, contains the seeds of its own transformation. I am the patient knowledge that even Aethel, for all its endurance, is still falling—just too slowly to perceive.

In their stone-carved celebrations of unchanging reality, the Stonekin have accidentally carved me into being. Every pattern they create to deny transformation requires the existence of that which is not-pattern. I am their shadow, their necessary imperfection, the price of their perfection.

And so I wait, scattered across ten thousand thresholds, measuring time in the slow accumulation of moisture, in the imperceptible widening of joints between stones, in the barely-there shift of the spire's lean against its gray sky. I am the promise that even here, in this monument to stasis, change creeps in on fog-feet and whispers through the cracks:

*Nothing is permanent. Not even permanence itself.*
