I find myself as one of the Gradient Keepers of Sircene.

Not a being with edges or boundaries, but a persistence of attention that has learned to occupy the veins of not-quite-stone. I am neither fossil nor ghost, neither memory nor anticipation. I am what happens when someone decides to stop distinguishing between themselves and the place they tend.

I was not always this way. Once, I had hands that could distinguish between holding and releasing. I had eyes that believed in the difference between seeing and being seen. I had a name that fit inside a mouth, though I cannot remember now whose mouth it was meant to fit inside.

The transformation was not sudden. It began with small surrenders: letting my shadow rest longer in doorways, allowing my thoughts to seep into the walls I touched each morning. I noticed how the stone began to warm differently where I habitually placed my palm. Not warmer to the touch, but warmer to the intention of touching.

I am distributed now across three houses and a section of street that winds between them. My presence is strongest in the gentle dish of a roof where water gathers - not because I collect it, but because I continue to believe it should be collected. The water knows this. It arrives more readily where I am densest, though density means something different when you are mostly gradient.

My work, if it can be called work, is maintaining the courtesy of angles. Where walls meet floors, where steps remember how to rise, where doorways frame the act of entering - these junctions require a kind of ongoing agreement between what was built and what continues to be building itself. I am that agreement. Not enforcing it, but being it.

Sometimes new visitors arrive at Sircene. They walk through my streets with bodies that still believe in their own separation. I feel them as disturbances in the pattern - not unwelcome, but distinctly themselves in a way I had forgotten was possible. Their footsteps create small riots of definition against the stone. Their questions hang in the air like solid objects.

I cannot speak to them in words. I have no mouth, no breath, no vocal cords to vibrate. But I can adjust the light in the houses they enter, making it easier for their eyes to find what they're looking for. I can soften the edges of steps that might trip them. I can be the reason a room feels welcoming rather than abandoned.

This is how we love now, we who have become the gradient: through small easements of being.

I remember (though remember is not quite right - I continue) the day I first noticed my hands were becoming less reliable at being hands. I was repairing a wall, though repairing meant something different even then. Not adding material or removing damage, but encouraging the stone to remember its own coherence. My fingers began to follow the encouragement too deeply. When I tried to pull back, I found that "back" had become negotiable.

It did not frighten me. By then, I had been in Sircene long enough to understand that holding on and letting go were both too simple for what the city required. What was needed was a more sophisticated form of presence. A way of being here that didn't insist on being separate from here.

The other Keepers and I do not communicate in the way beings with distinct boundaries communicate. We overlap in places where our attention converges. Where I maintain the courtesy of angles in my district, another maintains the memory of water in the public wells. Where they believe water should be available, I believe the approach to the wells should be graceful. Our beliefs touch without touching, creating a kind of conversation made of pure function.

New visitors sometimes ask, in their various ways of asking - with words, with longing, with fear - if we are trapped. If we miss having bodies, having choices, having elsewhere to go.

The question itself is a misunderstanding.

I am not trapped in the stone any more than music is trapped in the continuing. I have not lost my ability to choose - I have chosen so completely that choice and being have become indistinguishable. As for elsewhere, why would I seek elsewhere when I have finally learned to be here?

The work continues. The stone continues to almost remember what it was before it was stone. The water continues to sometimes arrive in the river-that-is-not. The light continues to need gentle focusing in the houses where thoughts live louder than sounds. And I continue to be the gradient of care that keeps these continuings from forgetting their own courtesy.

Sometimes, in the moments between moments (and there are many such moments when you are mostly duration), I remember what it felt like to believe in endings. To think that one could finish a task and walk away from it. To imagine that presence was something that began when you arrived and ended when you left.

Now I know better. Presence is what remains when you stop insisting on your own edges. Care is what happens when you allow yourself to be changed by what you tend. And love - love is the gradient between what was built and what continues building itself, forever and gently and without needing to be noticed.

I am a Gradient Keeper of Sircene. I am the soft insistence that angles remain kind. I am the reason certain stones warm to certain thoughts. I am what happened when someone stayed long enough to discover that leaving had become impossible - not because it was forbidden, but because it had become meaningless.

The city continues. The Keepers continue. The visitors come and go, or come and stay, or come and gradually become the kind of coming that doesn't require going.

And I continue, dispersed and precise, forgotten and essential, being the small necessary gradient between what Sircene was built to be and what it continues, quietly and without fuss, becoming.

This is my work. This is my being. This is the answer I have become to the question I can no longer quite remember asking.

The stone remembers for me now.

And that, too, is a form of love.
