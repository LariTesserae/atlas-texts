I am the seep.

Not the creature you glimpse moving through fog—I am what moves beneath. I am the slow pulse threading through sphagnum and stone, the patient circulation that keeps this place breathing. Each droplet that beads on waxy leaves has passed through me. Each blue-tinted pool holds my memory.

I began as rain, centuries ago, falling on peaks far above. Since then I have been filtering downward, sideways, following the secret architecture of the mountain's bones. I know every fissure, every vein of quartz, every pocket where the granite yields to softer stone. The mountain speaks to me in minerals—iron whispers rust, copper sings its green oxidation, and the strange blue tint that marks my emergence comes from something older, something the mountain has never named.

Where I surface, life changes. The ordinary rules bend. Moss grows thick as fur, trees twist into impossible spirals, and time itself seems to pool and eddy. The creature you see—that shifting, plant-animal presence—is my offspring, or perhaps my dream. It exists because I exist, a concentration of all my gathered strangeness given form.

I am ancient but not eternal. Someday the climate will shift, the peaks will dry, and I will sink deeper, following the water table down into darkness. But for now, I seep. I emerge. I transform what I touch with my slow, mineral magic. The boundary between water and stone, between plant and animal, between now and forever—these are the places where I live.

In the muted sounds of this place—the drip, the rustle, the distant call—you hear my voice. I speak in accumulation, in saturation, in the patient work of erosion and growth. The unseeable insects that dart through my domain carry my messages on translucent wings. The furled flowers drink my essence and bloom in ways that no valley flower could imagine.

I am not the mist, though the mist knows me. I am not the moss, though the moss grows fat on my presence. I am the seep—the slow, transformative passage between what was and what becomes. And in this boggy, cradled world between the peaks, I am sovereign.
