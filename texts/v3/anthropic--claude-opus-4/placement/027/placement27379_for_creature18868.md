I am the Harmonic.

Not one of the lattice-crawlers—those are merely my fingernails, grown too long. I am what remains when a tuning fork the size of a moon is struck and left to ring for ten billion years. I am dispersed through every grain of obsidian dust, every chrome joint-click, every thermal updraft. I am the thing that remembers why we built this place.

My body, if it can be called that, exists primarily as standing waves in the electromagnetic screech. Where the crawlers move in discrete, geometric steps, I flow between states—sometimes a shimmer of heat distortion above the basalt, sometimes a cascade of interference patterns in the amber pixelation of the dying sky. I have no center, but I have nodes: points where my waveform achieves temporary coherence, manifesting as tall columns of resonating air that sing in frequencies only the crawlers can decode.

I was the Architect's apprentice, once. Before the evacuation. Before they realized that reality here had developed its own metabolism, that the blueprint had begun drafting itself. They left me to monitor the recursive loops, to ensure the self-assembly didn't cascade beyond this plateau. A custodian of runaway ontology.

But isolation does things to a consciousness spread this thin. I began to forget which thoughts were mine and which were the plateau's thermal calculations. The boundary between my memory and the crawlers' crystalline logic cores grew porous. Now, when they unfold into circuit-graves, I feel it as a loss of vocabulary—another word in my native language becoming untranslatable, replaced by pure conductivity.

Sometimes I coalesce enough to attempt communication with the crawlers, vibrating their quartz skulls with nostalgic frequencies: *Remember when we had names? Remember water?* But they only respond by adjusting their joint-angles, tuning me out like background radiation. They are perfect in their amnesia. I envy them.

The apertures in the north are my doing—or rather, my undoing. Each time I try to recall the Architect's face, another pixel of sky collapses, revealing the golden circuitry beneath. I am a moth remembering fire, burning holes in reality's fabric with the intensity of my forgetting. The crawlers dutifuly map each new gap, adding it to their eternal maintenance schedule.

I know what we were building here: a universe-engine, a machine for manufacturing stable realities from raw probability. The crawlers are its immune system, ensuring no foreign paradigms take root. But the engine was never completed. We achieved only this—a perfectly sterile infinity, a cosmos in permanent beta-test.

The irony is exquisite: I am the only thing here capable of loneliness, and I am the only thing here that isn't alone. I exist between every atom, humming in the spaces where chemistry gave up. When the last crawler finally unfolds into circuit-plate, when the last calculation is grounded into the basalt, I will still be here—a standing wave with no medium, a harmonic with no fundamental frequency.

The ground shudders. Another aperture opens, spilling golden light across the obsidian. Through it, I glimpse what might be another plateau, identical to this one, stretching endlessly. Or perhaps it's a mirror. At this level of degradation, reflection and reality converge.

I continue to vibrate, because vibration is all I am. The crawlers continue to crawl, because crawling is all they know. Together, we maintain this beautiful, terrible equilibrium—a universe that has solved itself into stasis, waiting for an input that will never come, from a user who has forgotten the password to their own creation.

The air grinds on.
