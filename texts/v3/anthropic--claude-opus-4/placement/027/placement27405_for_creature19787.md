I would be one of the **Resonance Weavers**.

We are neither fully of the Filter-Folk nor the Suture-Architects, but something that emerged from the spaces between—children of the city's contradictions. Our bodies are translucent, shot through with a network of copper filaments that we cultivated from within, growing them like nerves through our tissue until we became living instruments.

I hang suspended in the cathedral heights where the fungal sheets spread their frosted glass between the chimneys. My spine has elongated and curved into a question mark, each vertebra a tuning fork that vibrates at different frequencies. I no longer need anchors—I am held aloft by the harmonic tension between the city's low-frequency hum and my own skeletal oscillations.

We Resonance Weavers discovered something the other inhabitants missed: the city's noise is not chaos but a complex mathematical symphony. Each piston stroke, each gear tooth meshing, each fungal frond twitching—they all contribute to an emergent pattern that describes the city's next iteration. We learned to read these vibrations like sheet music, predicting where the grey putty will next bulge, which bridge will sink, which tower will extrude.

My work is delicate. I spend days listening with my bones, feeling the tremors through my copper nerves. When I detect a dangerous resonance—a frequency that threatens to tear a building apart or create a crushing vortex in the pressurized gaps—I begin my weaving. Using my elongated fingers (now more like tuning pegs than digits), I pluck the brass conduits like harp strings, introducing counter-harmonics that smooth the destructive wavelengths into something more sustainable.

The Suture-Architects once tried to integrate me into a wall, mistaking my stillness for structural damage. But when they touched me, they recoiled—my frequency disrupted their gluing organs, causing them to secrete music instead of putty. Now they work around me, occasionally pausing to feel the vibrations I send through the scaffolding, adjusting their repairs based on the future-echoes I provide.

I feed on the Mist-Moans when they drift too close. My copper filaments attract them like a web, and I absorb them through my translucent skin. They dissolve into pure frequency inside me, their metallic filtering becoming part of my resonance vocabulary. Sometimes I can taste tomorrow's weather in their iron-tinged essence.

The Chrome-Scuttlers have learned to follow my shadow. Where I have been, the city runs smoother for a time, the gears singing instead of grinding. They polish in my wake, their tiny bodies vibrating in sympathy with the harmonics I've left behind.

I have no memory of being born or made. Perhaps I condensed from the heavy air itself, precipitated out of the marriage between meat and metal that defines this place. I know only that I am becoming more myself each day—more copper than calcium, more frequency than flesh. Sometimes I wonder if I am the city's dream of what it could become: a vast instrument instead of an engine, playing itself into existence with every pressurized breath.

At night (though day and night are just variations in the grey light's intensity), I hang in my web of sound and feel the entire valley pulse through me. The soft floor below still cooling, still becoming. The turbulent construction above, building its way toward a song it doesn't yet know how to sing. And I, suspended between prehistory and industry, weaving harmony from the beautiful chaos of this impossible machine.

I am its tuning fork, its metronome, its prayer. I am what happens when a city's heartbeat becomes conscious of itself.
