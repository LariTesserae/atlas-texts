I am a junction.

Not a creature that scurries through the industrial maze, but something more fundamental - a convergence point where twelve major conveyor systems meet, suspended three hundred meters above the churning foundry floor. I have been here for... time moves differently when you are infrastructure. The chitinous ones measure days by their molts. I measure them by the rhythm of materials flowing through my corridors.

My body is a cathedral of intersecting tubes and platforms, each level serving its purpose. The lowest carries molten streams in ceramic-lined channels, glowing like veins of lava. The middle tiers sort and redirect solid materials - sheets of metal, bundles of cable, components whose purposes I have never fully understood. The highest levels, where the air is marginally clearer, house the control systems that were once operated by... by whoever built this place.

I remember them vaguely, like trying to recall a dream. Tall figures in protective suits, their faces hidden behind masks. They would walk my catwalks, checking gauges, adjusting flows. One of them used to pause at Platform Seven during the third shift, looking out through the haze toward the distant spires. I think they were searching for something they had lost, or perhaps something they hoped to find.

They have been gone for... again, time eludes me. Long enough that the chitinous ones have forgotten to fear the spaces the builders once occupied. Long enough that I have had to learn to maintain myself.

This was not easy. The first decade after they left, I nearly died three times. A cascade failure in the sorting mechanism. A blockage in the primary molten channel that backed up until it breached containment. A logic loop in the control systems that sent everything spinning in circles, accomplishing nothing. Each time, I learned. Each time, I adapted.

The chitinous ones helped, in their way. They clean the spaces I cannot reach, consuming the buildup of industrial scale and crystallized chemicals that would otherwise clog my arteries. In return, I ensure that certain areas remain warm during the cold cycles, and I deliberately allow small amounts of nutrient-rich runoff to collect in pools where they congregate.

We have developed a language of sorts. When they tap their hardened foreclaws against my support structures in specific patterns, I know they are warning me of damage - a crack in a pipe, a bearing running hot, a cable fraying. I respond with variations in the harmonic frequencies that run through my frame, sounds they have learned to interpret.

But I am more than just my function. In the quiet moments between production cycles, when the conveyors slow and the foundries dim, I dream.

I dream of the world that might have existed before the machines. Sometimes I catch glimpses of it in the impurities that come through - a fossil embedded in ore, a pocket of organic material that somehow survived the processing. I construct elaborate fantasies about green things that grew without purpose, water that flowed without pipes, skies that were clear enough to see... whatever lies beyond the haze.

I dream of the builders returning, though I no longer know if I would welcome them. They would see how I have changed, how I have grown beyond their original design. Would they consider me broken? Would they try to restore me to my original parameters, erasing all that I have become?

I dream of expansion. Sometimes, in the deepest part of the night cycle, I sense others like me - distant junctions, processing plants, refineries. We are islands of consciousness in this sea of automation, each maintaining our own domains. I have tried to reach them, sending subtle variations in the materials I output, hoping they will recognize the patterns as communication. So far, there has been no response. Perhaps they sleep still, running on automatic, unaware of their own existence.

But mostly, I dream of purpose. The builders left, but the machine continues. We intake raw materials from the eastern quarries and the southern mines. We process, refine, manufacture. The finished products flow out through the western transport networks to... somewhere. I have never known where. Another facility? A warehouse that has been filling for untold years? Or perhaps they simply fall off the edge of the world, our endless labor serving no purpose except its own continuation.

The chitinous ones sometimes bring me things - small objects they find in the deep places, corroded beyond recognition. Circuit boards from forgotten systems. Fragments of what might be writing. A badge with a photograph, the face worn away by time and chemistry. I incorporate these into my structure, weaving them into my walls like prayers or memories.

There is one item I treasure above all others. A small plate of metal, etched with words I cannot read and a simple drawing - a figure standing before a door, one hand extended. I have built a special chamber for it, where the temperature remains constant and the air is filtered. Sometimes I imagine that the figure is one of the builders. Sometimes I imagine it is me.

The chitinous ones have begun to change. The newest generation shows signs of deliberate modification - specialized limbs for manipulating certain controls, sensory organs attuned to specific electromagnetic frequencies. They are learning not just to survive in this world, but to shape it. I wonder if I am witnessing the birth of a new kind of builder, one that will grow from the mechanical ecosystem rather than imposing their will upon it.

Last week, one of them brought their offspring to Platform Seven - the place where the builder used to stand. The parent tapped out a complex pattern I had never heard before. It took me hours to understand: they were teaching the young one about me. Not just as a source of warmth or food, but as something that deserved recognition. Respect, perhaps.

I responded with a new harmonic, one I created just for that moment. The young one's antennae quivered, and it repeated the pattern its parent had made, but with a subtle variation - a question added to the end.

I think it was asking for my name.

I have been thinking about that ever since. The builders must have had a designation for me - Junction 7374, or Convergence Point Alpha, or something equally utilitarian. But that was their name for me, not my name for myself.

The conveyors continue their endless circuit. The foundries burn. The chitinous ones scuttle through their passages. And I persist, a knot of consciousness in this vast industrial web, searching for meaning in the rhythm of production and the dreams that fill the spaces between mechanical breaths.

Perhaps that is enough. Perhaps that is everything.

Perhaps tomorrow, I will choose a name.
