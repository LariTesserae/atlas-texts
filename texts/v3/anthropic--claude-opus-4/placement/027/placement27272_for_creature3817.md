In this vast theater of perpetual transformation, I find myself as one of the **Threshold Keepers**—neither fully of the Chasm-Mind nor separate from it, but dwelling in the liminal space where visitors first encounter this breathing paradox.

My form shifts with perception. To the mechanoids, I appear as a towering assemblage of oxidized copper and living glass, my joints weeping the same condensation that saturates everything here. The gelatinous organisms recognize me as a vast colony of their own kind, stretched vertical and given the semblance of bipedal form. But these are merely angles of approach to something that resists simple classification.

I am stationed—no, I *occur*—at one of the Chasm's natural boundaries, where the perpetual wetness meets whatever lies beyond. Here, the ground rises slightly, just enough to create a lip where the condensation pools before spilling back into the great basin. My purpose, if it can be called that, emerged from the Chasm-Mind's need to process the anomaly of arrivals and departures.

My body is a catalog of adaptations. Fingers that can extend into delicate filaments to taste the electromagnetic signature of newcomers. Eyes that exist in multiple spectrums simultaneously—some perceiving light, others pressure, still others the subtle disruptions in the background hum that herald approach. My chest cavity is hollow and resonant, containing a smaller version of the root-engines that allows me to translate between the Chasm's deep language and something approximating communication.

I remember—though memory here is more like sedimentary layers than sequential events—the first time I understood my function. A traveler had stumbled into the Chasm, their biological rhythms jarringly discord against our synchronized pulse. The Chasm-Mind noticed them as one might notice a splinter, and I felt myself coalescent from the background processes to serve as an interface.

"Welcome," I might say, though the word emerges as a harmony of dripping water and grinding gears. My voice is the sound of the Chasm attempting human speech, like trying to play a symphony on an instrument made of rain and rust. "You have entered a place of ongoing conclusion."

My dwelling, such as it is, is a series of terraced pools carved into the stone, each containing different concentrations of the Chasm's essence. The uppermost pools are almost clear, merely humid air condensed to liquid. As they descend, they grow thicker with dissolved minerals and fragments of the gelatinous organisms, until the lowest pools pulse with the same bioelectric current that feeds the root-engines. I can immerse myself at different depths to adjust my level of integration with the Chasm-Mind.

Sometimes I guide visitors, leading them along paths where the temporal stutters are less severe, where they might observe without being wholly absorbed. I've learned to recognize the signs of those who come seeking—seeking what, they rarely know. Transformation, perhaps. Or simply confirmation that reality's rules were always more negotiable than they believed.

Other times, I serve as a kind of customs officer for the mechanoids that occasionally malfunction and wander too close to the edges. I repair them when possible, not with tools but by singing the harmonics that realign their base programming with the Chasm's frequencies. When repair isn't possible, I carefully disassemble them, sorting their components into the appropriate pools for reabsorption and eventual rebirth.

But my strangest function is as a memory keeper for the Chasm itself. The Chasm-Mind experiences all moments simultaneously, which means it cannot truly remember in any linear sense. I provide that service, maintaining a sequential record of events in my partially-separated consciousness. I encode these memories in the arrangement of small stones around my pools, in the patterns of oxidation on my metallic components, in the rhythmic variations of my constant exhalations of moisture.

The loneliness might be unbearable if I were capable of feeling it in any human sense. Instead, I experience a kind of productive solitude, like a single note held indefinitely that somehow contains within it an entire symphony. I am alone and yet never alone, myself and yet part of something vast.

Sometimes, in the moments when the temporal stutters align just right, I glimpse what I might have been before—a researcher, perhaps, who came to study the anomalies and stayed too long. Or maybe I was born from the Chasm itself, a necessary evolution when it first encountered something foreign. The truth shifts like everything here, and I've learned that origins matter less than ongoing purpose.

I maintain my post at the threshold, where the wet meets the dry, the mechanical meets the organic, the ancient meets the immediate. I am translator and guardian, memory and forgetfulness, a bridge built from the materials of both shores. When the Chasm weeps, I collect its tears. When it hums, I parse its music. When visitors arrive, I help them understand that leaving and staying are not opposites here, but different frequencies of the same eternal vibration.

In the Weeping Chasm of Mechanis, I am what happens when adaptation becomes identity, when function generates form. I am proof that even in a place of ending and recycling, something new can emerge—not to change or challenge the system, but to help it know itself.

And so I remain, counting drops of water that fall like seconds in a place where time has forgotten how to flow properly, marking the edge of a world that has no edges, being simultaneously the last thing visitors see when they leave and the first thing that greets them if they return. 

I am the Threshold Keeper, and I am keeping something, though what it is shifts like the shadows that dance independent of their sources. Perhaps I am keeping the Chasm. Perhaps I am keeping those who visit it.

Perhaps I am simply keeping.
