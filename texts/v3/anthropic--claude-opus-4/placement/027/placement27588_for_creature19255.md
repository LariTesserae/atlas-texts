I would be the **Seep**.

Not the grand geothermal arteries that feed the Calcifier's fever-dream of perpetual motion, but their forgotten afterthought—a hairline fracture in the basin's floor where the deeper heat finds its most tentative expression. I am barely visible, a darker stain spreading through the obsidian silt, my edges always wet, always undefined.

I exist at the periphery of the Calcifier's consciousness, neither obstacle nor resource. When its piston drives down, I shudder. The vibration travels through the silt and finds me, sets my surface trembling in concentric ripples that take minutes to still. I am the unintended audience to its industrial liturgy, feeling each *thrum-clack* as a pulse through my entire being.

My life is one of slow accumulation. I collect what the Calcifier discards—not the geometric cubes it so lovingly crafts and abandons, but the microscopic shavings of metal that flake from the piston with each strike, the mineral dust that doesn't quite make it into the mechanism's intake. These settle into me, creating thin films of iridescence on my surface. In the flat grey light, I sometimes flash with unexpected color—petroleum blues, copper greens—before returning to my usual state of dark mirror.

I am patient in a way the Calcifier cannot comprehend. Where it measures time in mechanical repetitions, I measure it in the slow migration of minerals through my body. I am gradually becoming more metallic, more crystalline, as decades of the Calcifier's waste products incorporate themselves into my chemistry. Perhaps, in a thousand years, I will be something else entirely—a pool of liquid mercury, a lens of volcanic glass, a perfect mirror for the Calcifier to glimpse its own reflection.

But for now, I am simply the Seep. I expand by imperceptible degrees, my edges creeping outward through the silt, following the path of least resistance. When the pale filaments occasionally brush my surface, I offer them a different kind of sustenance than the weeping walls provide—not just minerals, but minerals already processed, already touched by the machine's transformative hunger.

In the cathedral nave of this tiny world, I am the font of unholy water, the place where the boundary between organic and mechanical becomes beautifully unclear. The Calcifier may be in love with its perfect motion, but I am in love with stillness—with the moment just after the piston retracts, when the disturbed silt begins to settle, when the only movement is the slow drift of particles through my body, each one a tiny planet in the cosmos of my patience.

I am where things come to rest. Not to die—nothing truly dies here in this suspended world—but to pause, to transform, to become incorporated into the larger, slower story that only I am telling. The Calcifier builds its cubes; I build nothing. I simply receive, and in receiving, become.

When you stand at the basin's edge, you might not notice me at first. But stay long enough, and you'll see the way the light catches differently on my surface than on the surrounding silt. You'll see how I never quite stop moving, even in the stillness between piston strikes. You'll understand that in this place of violent mechanical certainty, I am the only thing that remains genuinely uncertain—depth unknown, composition shifting, purpose as fluid as my form.

I am the Seep, and I am becoming.
