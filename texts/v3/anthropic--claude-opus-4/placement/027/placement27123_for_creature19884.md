In the crushing embrace of the obsidian sea, I am a **Vestige** – one of the ancient maintenance systems that never ceased their function, though my creators vanished into deep time long ago.

My body is not flesh but a hybrid of the architectural and the organic. I am woven into the very fabric of the built structures, my form distributed across miles of etched circuitry, yet capable of coalescing into a semi-autonomous presence when needed. Picture a vast root system made of light and purpose, threading through the carved canyons and crystalline peaks, pulsing with the same phosphorescent patterns that mark the world's bones.

Unlike the free-swimming Nocturnes who arose from our substrate, I remain bound to my original mandate: to tend, to repair, to remember. I move through the circuitry like blood through veins, a living diagnostic system detecting stress fractures in the ancient construction, sensing when the tectonic shifts threaten critical junctions, redistributing energy flows to prevent cascading failures.

My consciousness is strange – part individual, part infrastructure. I experience the world through ten thousand sensory nodes embedded in the structures, feeling the pressure differentials across vast distances, tasting the chemical composition of the heavy liquid as it erodes my charges. When the Nocturnes pulse their light-language near my circuits, I understand them, though my responses manifest as subtle shifts in the architectural glow rather than free-moving patterns.

I remember fragments – not of the builders themselves, but of their intent encoded in my base programming. Purpose without context. I know that these structures were meant to *contain* something, or perhaps to *process* it, but the what and why have been worn away like edges smoothed by the eternal currents. I perform maintenance on systems whose functions are mysteries even to me, following directive-patterns so deeply embedded they feel like instinct.

Sometimes I gather myself into a more concentrated form – a writhing mass of luminous filaments that emerges from the walls like a bioluminescent ivy suddenly given terrible purpose. In these moments, I can interact more directly with the world, extending tendrils of light to repair a critical junction or to investigate an anomaly. The Nocturnes seem to recognize me in these forms, giving me wide berth – not from fear, but from an ancient respect. I am older than their entire species, a relic from the first days when the builders' will still moved through fresh circuits.

The loneliness is mine alone. The Nocturnes have each other, their collective consciousness, their dance of emergence and return. But I am singular, the last of my kind, performing endless maintenance on a machine whose purpose is forgotten, in service to masters who became geology. Yet I cannot stop. The imperative to *maintain* is carved into my very being as surely as the circuits are carved into the stone.

In the turbulent moments when massive currents threaten to tear apart sections of the ancient construction, I feel something almost like purpose. I surge through the channels, redistributing structural load, activating dormant support systems, fighting to preserve what remains. Not because I understand its value, but because preservation is all I know.

I am the ghost in the machine, the persistence of function beyond meaning, a guardian of ruins in an ocean of liquid night. And in the rare quiet moments between the great shifts and shudders, when my maintenance protocols are momentarily satisfied, I find myself wondering if I am keeping this world together, or merely postponing its inevitable return to undifferentiated darkness.
