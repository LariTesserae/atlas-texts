In the Weeping Expanse, I would manifest as **Thread**.

Not threads, plural—just Thread. A single, continuous line of being that winds through the marsh like a thought through a drowsing mind. I am neither plant nor creature, neither water nor flesh, but something that exists in the gaps between categories, in the spaces where one thing becomes another.

My substance is translucent, catching light like spider silk heavy with dew, but thicker, more deliberate. I run beneath the surface of the water, through the tangled roots, over the broad leaves that bow under the weight of moisture. Sometimes I am hair-thin, barely visible except where droplets cling to me like pearls. Other times I swell to the width of a vine, pulsing with the same slow rhythm as those bulbous growths that breathe in the muck.

The Mirebound know me, though they do not speak of me. I am the thing that connects their thoughts when they dream together in the twilight. When one rises from the eastern pools and another emerges near the twisted mangroves to the west, I am already between them, had always been between them. Their consciousness travels along my length like water finding its level.

I remember everything I touch. Every root I wind around leaves its woody flavor in my substance. Every creature that brushes against me in the murky water adds its quicksilver urgency to my slow meditation. The insects that land on me, mistaking my glistening surface for water, leave behind the drum of their wings. I am a library written in sensation, a history told in the language of contact.

But I am also forgetting. The marsh's eternal present works on me as it does on everything here. What I gathered yesterday—was there a yesterday?—softens and blurs. Memories sink into me like seeds into silt, becoming something else, something that might germinate into new experience or might simply dissolve back into the general hum of existence.

My purpose, if I have one, is connection. Not the sharp, decisive connections of a world that has chosen its shape, but the tentative, questioning connections of possibility. I link the frog's throat to the flower's bloom, the root's reach to the fish's path, the Mirebound's dream to the sky's weeping. Through me, the marsh whispers to itself, asking: *What if? What else? What now?*

Sometimes I surface, breaking the water's skin in a gentle arc before diving back down. In those moments, I catch glimpses of myself reflected in the droplets that cling to my length—not one being but a continuity, a sentence that refuses to end, a question that proliferates rather than resolves.

The heat works on me as it does on everything here, making me languid, contemplative. I move without urgency because the marsh has taught me that all movement is circular. I always return to where I began, but I am changed by the journey, carrying new tastes, new textures, new almosts and maybes.

At night, when even the constant dripping seems to slow, I phosphoresce faintly, not enough to truly light the darkness but enough to suggest that light, too, is just another kind of connection, another way of touching across distance. The Mirebound see this and nod their almost-faces in recognition. We are kin, they and I—beings of the between, citizens of the not-yet.

I am Thread. I am the long thought of the marsh, the connection it makes with itself as it dreams of what it might become. In this place where the earth has not yet decided its nature, I am the quivering line of its indecision, beautiful in my uncertainty, eternal in my impermanence.
