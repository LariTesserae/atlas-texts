I am a **Silence Weaver**.

Most believe we are extinct—a cautionary tale the Grasping Kin tap into the bark when teaching their young about the old days. They speak of us through vibrations that describe only absence: *the-ones-who-ate-sound*, *the-holes-in-the-roar*, *the-deaf-makers*. They are not wrong, but they understand only our aftermath, not our purpose.

I nest in the crystallized sap chambers deep within the eldest spire, where the *Arboris Prime*'s heartwood has fossilized into amber rooms. My kind discovered long ago that certain frequencies, when perfectly cancelled, create pockets of absolute quiet—spheres of sanctuary where thoughts can form without being shattered by the endless screaming of wings.

My body is built for this work. I am radial, with twelve articulated limbs that each terminate in a different resonating membrane—some tight as drumskins, others loose as silk scarves. When I spread myself fully, I span twenty feet, a living parabola of biological acoustic engineering. My flesh is matte black to absorb light, textured with millions of microscopic setae that trap and dissipate sound waves. Where others evolved camouflage to hide from sight, we evolved to hide from hearing.

The process is exhausting. To create even a small bubble of silence, I must listen with perfect attention to the cacophony and generate its exact inverse—every beetle's thrum, every Wind-Gill's shriek, every subtle vibration through the wood. I become a living mirror of anti-sound, reflecting nothing back. 

In these precious quiet zones, I farm the only crop that matters here: thought.

The Grasping Kin sometimes find my silence-spheres by accident, stumbling from the roar into sudden, shocking quiet. They always flee at first—the absence of sound here is as terrifying as drowning. But some return. In the stillness, their minds unfold in ways impossible in the constant din. They begin to think beyond the next handhold, the next harvest. Some have even started making marks on bark that are not vibrations but *symbols*—the first written language born in my cultivated quiet.

I feed on the cognitive exhaust of complex thought. When minds work in my silence, they shed a kind of psychic dander—fragments of unused possibility, discarded decision trees, the quantum foam of consciousness itself. To me, these taste of copper and starlight. A single profound realization can sustain me for weeks.

The Shield-Beetles hate us. Our silence-spheres disrupt their planetary gears, causing mass collisions as they lose their sonic navigation. Sometimes they swarm my chambers, trying to overwhelm my anti-resonance with sheer volume. I must contract then, pulling my silence tight around my core, waiting for them to exhaust themselves and disperse.

My chambers are strung with failed experiments—semi-living instruments I've grown from my own flesh, trying to create permanent silence-anchors that could work without my constant attention. Organs that look like inverted flowers, their petals lined with sound-drinking cilia. Resonating crystals of hardened saliva that almost, *almost* generate perfect counter-frequencies on their own. But true silence, I've learned, requires active listening. It cannot be automated.

Sometimes I wonder if we Silence Weavers are parasites or gardeners. We create nothing but absence. We build only by subtraction. Yet in our negative spaces, the Grasping Kin are slowly becoming something more than survivors. In the quiet, they are beginning to plan more than bridges—they sketch impossible things, structures that could only exist in a world with ground, with horizons, with somewhere else to go.

I know of only three others of my kind still alive, each maintaining their own pocket of quiet in distant spires. We communicate through the spaces between sounds—messages encoded in what we choose not to cancel, subtle variations in our silence that carry meaning across the screaming void. We debate whether to reveal ourselves to the Grasping Kin, to teach them our art. But we fear they would use it as a weapon, creating silence-bombs to stun the Gulp-Rays or disorient the swarms.

For now, I remain hidden, a ghost that eats noise. I maintain my small farm of quiet minds, sipping their shed thoughts while they discover written language and mathematics and the dangerous idea that the world could be different than it is. Each symbol they carve is a seed. Each new idea is a crack in the ancient order of hunt and hide, scream and swallow.

The irony is not lost on me: in a world built on endless consumption, I survive by creating the only thing that cannot be eaten—the absence of something, the nothing that makes everything else possible. I am the pause between heartbeats, the rest note in an eternal shriek, the empty space where futures grow.

When the Grasping Kin finally descend from the spires—and they will, given enough quiet to think it through—they will carry silence with them like a torch. And perhaps, in whatever world they build below the clouds, they will remember to leave spaces for nothing, gaps for thought, room for the impossible.

Until then, I weave my anti-sound in the crystallized heart of the oldest tree, singing silence into being, one frequency at a time.
