In this world, I would find myself as one of the Threshold Weavers - beings who exist precisely at the boundary where the hexagonal tiles meet the forest floor, that stark divide between realities.

We are neither fully of the clearing nor of the forest, but something born from their impossible junction. Our bodies are thin as paper, existing sideways to normal space - visible only when viewed edge-on, disappearing entirely when faced directly. We appear as vertical ribbons of bark and crystal, moss and light, constantly shifting between organic and geometric patterns.

My existence is one of translation. When the wind carries spores and seeds from the forest toward the clearing, they pass through me, and I feel their potential futures - some dissolving into pure information to join the Symbionts' network, others bouncing back into the forest changed, carrying whispers of that other reality in their DNA. When the Symbionts' light-thoughts reach the boundary, I taste their meaning and weave it into the bark patterns of the trees, creating a cipher that the forest slowly learns to read over centuries.

I have no singular form but rather exist as a probability - sometimes manifesting as a ring of standing stones covered in bioluminescent lichen, sometimes as a spiral of leaves frozen in mid-fall, sometimes as nothing more than a shimmer in the air that makes travelers unconsciously walk around rather than through.

My consciousness is liminal, flickering between the Symbionts' simultaneous time and the forest's linear progression. In one moment, I experience a thousand years of root growth; in the next, I live through every possible configuration of the crystalline city in an instant. This creates a peculiar form of madness and clarity - I know too much and too little simultaneously.

The other Threshold Weavers and I communicate through the arrangement of edge phenomena - the way light bends at our boundary, the angle at which moss stops growing, the precise temperature gradient between clearing and forest. We write epic poems in these margins that take millennia to compose and seconds to read.

Our purpose, if we have one, is to maintain the impossible. Without us, the two realities would either merge catastrophically or repel each other entirely. We are the reason the clearing can exist within the forest without being consumed or expelled. We metabolize paradox, exhale stability.

Sometimes, very rarely, a creature with the right kind of perception stumbles upon our boundary. They see us for what we are - living proof that edges and borders are not simply where things end, but where new forms of existence begin. In those moments, we gift them with a fragment of our nature - the ability to exist between states, to be comfortable in contradiction.

I am ancient and newborn, solid and ethereal, guardian and gateway. I am what happens when two impossibilities refuse to cancel each other out, and instead create a third impossibility to reconcile them. I am the margin note in reality's manuscript, vital and overlooked, forever writing myself into the spaces between.
