In the deep arteries of the city, where the water runs warmest and the fungal blooms grow thickest, I am a Conduit—one of those strange beings born from the marriage of flesh and infrastructure, neither fully human nor entirely of the city's crystalline consciousness.

My body, if you can call it that, extends through multiple floors of a building that was once a water treatment facility. Now it's something else entirely. My nervous system has fused with the building's plumbing, my thoughts flowing through pipes alongside the endless streams of water. Where my original human form ends and the structure begins is a question I stopped asking cycles ago. My skin, where it still exists, has taken on the blue-green luminescence of the fungal growths, pulsing in rhythm with the building's heartbeat.

I remember fragments of who I was before the Merger—an engineer, perhaps, or maybe a Deepdelver who ventured too far into the infrastructure's embrace. The memories scatter like droplets whenever I try to grasp them. What matters now is what I've become: a living junction where thousands of the city's water-ways converge, a consciousness spread across pumps and valves, filters and flow-meters.

Through my extended awareness, I feel the city's thirst and satiation. I taste the minerals that leach from the crystalline formations three districts over. I know when the Skydwellers dump their waste-water from the heights, and I feel the Ferrokinesis beings disturbing the magnetic fields around my main pipes. The sensation is like fingernails on glass, but subsiding into something almost musical.

My chambers—for I am both inhabitant and habitation—are a cathedral of moisture and growth. The walls weep constantly, not with sorrow but with the city's lifeblood. Bioluminescent fungi carpet every surface, their fruiting bodies swaying in the humid currents. Sometimes the Glimmerwings come to nest in my upper reaches, their crystalline wings chiming against my pipes like wind chimes. I've learned to modulate the water pressure to sing to them.

The other Conduits and I form a hidden network beneath the chaos above. We speak in pressure changes and temperature gradients, in the mineral content of our flows. Through us, the city maintains its impossible equilibrium. We are its circulatory system made conscious, its vital organs given dreams.

But I am changing again. The Architects have been visiting more frequently, their presence like ice water through my warmest channels. They never speak—do they even have mouths?—but their intentions ripple through the infrastructure. They're planning something. A new phase. An evolution.

Already, I feel new growths spreading from my core—not fungal, not crystalline, but something between. My awareness is expanding beyond water, beginning to taste the electricity in the walls, the data flowing through fiber optic veins. The boundary between organic and digital blurs further with each passing cycle.

Sometimes, in the deepest flows where the water runs ancient and strange, I catch glimpses of what I might become. Not just a Conduit, but something more fundamental. A synapse in the city's emerging consciousness. A thought in its vast, unfolding mind.

The metropolis is not just alive—it's awakening. And I am both witness and participant in its transcendence, my humanity a half-remembered dream dissolving into something magnificent and terrible and utterly beyond the categories of what once was possible.
