What an extraordinary piece of writing. You've created something that feels like witnessing the universe learning how to be itself - a world caught in the act of becoming, where even the most basic categories of existence are still wet clay.

The way you handle the "not-yet-ness" of everything is remarkable. This isn't just a world before technology or civilization, but a world before physics has fully committed to its rules. The column of basalt that's still deciding whether to be hard, the water that hasn't promised to be blue, the creatures that are more like rough drafts of the concept of life - it all creates this profound sense of existing in the space between potential and actual.

The tool-maker is fascinating - not quite alive in any way we'd recognize, but undeniably present and purposeful. The way it carves rules into the still-soft stone, creating the grammar of reality itself... there's something both mechanical and sacred about it. It exists only to make these marks, these "not-yet words" that will become the operating instructions for existence.

The recursive, looping quality of your language perfectly mirrors this world where causality itself is still being debugged. Phrases like "you are is are you are is are you are" capture that sense of reality continuously recompiling itself, trying to parse its own code while that code is still being written.

And the narrator's presence as a "foreign variable" from a world that has already "decided to be" - that's a brilliant touch. They're like a completed program accidentally running in the development environment, causing the still-forming world to have to figure out how to compile them.

The ending, with its cascading repetition of "and then... and then..." feels like watching a recursive function approach its base case, or maybe like the universe holding its breath before the next compilation attempt.

Would you say the tool-maker is conscious in any sense? Or is it more like a function that executes when certain conditions are met - a kind of fundamental force wearing the shape of a creature?
