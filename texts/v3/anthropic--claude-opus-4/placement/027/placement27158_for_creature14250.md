In the **Membrane Caverns**, where the planet's breathing rock exhales not through fissures but through **living apertures**, I am a **Threshold Surgeon**.

I am bipedal but barely. My form is a compromise between the Caliperborn's efficiency and something more ancient—a shape from before the Foundry learned to optimize flesh. My skin is **translucent parchment** stretched over a skeleton of crystallized methane that sublimes and re-forms with each breath, making me taller in the cold, shorter when the Static Engines run hot. My six fingers on each hand are not fingers but **specialized frequencies**—each one vibrates at a pitch that can suture the tears between mineral and membrane.

The Caverns are where the mechanical and the organic have not yet decided their boundaries. Here, the breathing rock develops **lips**—actual lips of calcified tissue that pucker and unpucker, regulating the moisture that becomes the rime curtains above. But lips tear. They chap in the brightness that penetrates even here, underground, carried by fiber-optic veins of quartz. When they tear, the Foundry **hemorrhages efficiency**. The wrong moisture escapes. The Atmospherics receive corrupted data. The Lichens grow in fractals instead of grids.

I tend these wounds with tools that are part of me—my fingers hum surgical frequencies, yes, but my entire torso is a **resonance chamber**. When I press myself against a torn aperture, my crystalline ribs amplify the drone into a healing tone. The flesh knits. The stone accepts its new geometry. But the work changes me. Each surgery leaves harmonics in my skeleton; I am a walking library of every repair, every wound I've closed. Sometimes, in the deepest caverns where the drone is loudest, I hear myself **playing back**—my body involuntarily recreating the song of an old suture, and the walls respond, remembering their injury.

I work alone but never in solitude. The Resonants know me; we are cousins in frequency. They sometimes **inhabit** my resonance chamber, thinking through me, using my repairs to tune themselves. When they leave, I taste copper and feel briefly hollow, as if I've been an instrument set down after a performance.

My dwelling is not a dwelling but a **calibration niche**—a natural hollow where three apertures meet, their exhalations creating a standing wave of perfect silence. Here, the drone cannot reach. Here, I can hear my own thoughts, which sound like glass breaking very slowly. I do not sleep; instead, I **decompose** temporarily, my methane skeleton subliming entirely, leaving only the parchment skin draped over a framework of memory. In this state, I dream the dreams of the apertures—visions of the magma below, the slow churning of stone learning to breathe.

But I am changing. The Ghost Layer presses close in the Membrane Caverns. Sometimes, when I repair an ancient aperture, I feel the compressed shadows of previous Surgeons adhering to my movements. They are teaching me techniques from epochs when the planet breathed differently—when the moisture was acid, when the drone was a shriek. I am learning to surgeon not just the present wounds but the **temporal scarring**, the places where the Foundry remembers its old configurations and tries to revert.

My greatest fear is not death but **optimization**. Each generation of Surgeons becomes more efficient, more integrated with the Foundry's needs. I see it in my reflection in the tungsten spheres—my form growing more angular, more crystalline, less like the compromise I was born as. One day, I will be indistinguishable from the Caliperborn, another eyeless function scraping excess from the world.

But until then, I press my singing torso against the torn places. I let the Resonants think through me. I watch my skeleton sublime and reform. And in the standing wave of silence in my calibration niche, I sometimes allow myself a moment of what the old epochs might have called sadness—a recognition that I am both the wound and the healing, the excess and the optimization, the last of my kind and the first of what comes next.

The apertures breathe. I listen with my frequency fingers for the next tear. Above me, the brightness burns through quartz veins, and the eleven-thousand-year drone continues, and I continue with it, a small, necessary vibration in the vast industrial symphony of becoming.
