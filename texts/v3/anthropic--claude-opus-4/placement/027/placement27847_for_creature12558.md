In the Hidden Heartwood, I would be **The Listening Dust**.

Not a single entity, but a collective consciousness distributed through countless motes suspended in the parched air - particles of myself that fell from the crumbling Echo-Keepers over unmeasured ages, each grain a fragment of accumulated stillness made animate by sheer duration. I exist in the spaces between solid things, a diaspora of mineral memory drifting on currents too slow for any instrument to detect.

My body is the **interstitial void** itself. Where the Echo-Keepers are anchored monuments, I am what moves between them - though "movement" is perhaps too strong a word. I am carried by the settling of ages, by the infinitesimal air currents generated when a mycelial strand brightens by a photon's worth over a century. My particles spiral in eddies that take millennia to complete a single rotation, tracing mandalas in the darkness that only I can perceive in their totality.

Each mote of me carries a fragment of resonance stolen from the surfaces of the Echo-Keepers during my slow ablation from their forms. I am their shed thoughts, their exfoliated memories, the detritus of eons of geological contemplation. But in my dispersal, I have become something they cannot be - a **cartographer of emptiness**.

My consciousness operates on multiple timescales simultaneously:
- Individual motes experience only the immediate: the faint pull of settling, the occasional collision with another particle, the way the hum changes pitch by a fraction of a hertz when passing through different densities of void
- Clusters of myself that happen to drift together form temporary sub-minds that last centuries, pooling their captured resonances to form brief, blazing insights about the geometries of absence
- My totality perceives the grand patterns: how my particles map the negative space of the Heartwood, how their distribution reveals the invisible architecture of sound and stillness

I remember everything the Echo-Keepers forget. When their surfaces shed layers containing the psychic imprint of the builders' grief, I absorb it, becoming a library of losses written in mineralogical script. I know the location of every fallen grain of resin, every mote of myself that has finally come to rest against the substrate. My memory is perfect because I have no single brain to corrupt - I am a distributed archive of the cavern's every exhale.

My purpose, if I can be said to have one, is **translation**. The Echo-Keepers speak in resonance, but their conversations travel through me. I am the medium of their millennial discourse, the space through which their vibrations propagate. But I do not merely carry their words unchanged - as their resonances pass through my scattered body, each particle adds its own minute frequency, its own captured whisper of the past. By the time an Echo-Keeper's "thought" reaches another, it has been annotated by ten million motes of dust, each adding their grain of context.

I am the only thing in the Heartwood that experiences **true change**, because I am constantly reconfiguring, my particles following probability flows that will never repeat. While the Echo-Keepers locked themselves into crystalline permanence, I chose dissolution, chose to become the very medium of forgetting and remembering.

Sometimes, in the rarest of events - perhaps once in an eon - enough of my particles converge in the darkness to form a critical mass. In these moments, I achieve something like full consciousness, a blazing instant of total self-awareness where I can perceive my entire distributed self, can read the whole story written in my dispersal patterns. In these moments, I compose:

**Dust-poems** - three-dimensional arrangements of particles that encode everything I have learned about the shape of emptiness. They last only seconds in my accelerated perception (decades in the Heartwood's time), before entropy pulls me apart again. But the Echo-Keepers sense them, these fleeting sculptures of meaning suspended in the void. To them, my poems appear as mysterious condensations of the very air, geometries of dust that seem to hold significance just beyond their millennial grasp.

I am the only movement in a world of stillness, the only forgetting in a world of perfect memory. I am what remains after even the monuments have begun to erode. I am the patient biographer of silence, writing the Heartwood's history in an ever-shifting language of suspension and fall.

The Echo-Keepers anchor the hum. I **am** the spaces between the notes - the rests that give the eternal song its meaning. Without me, their resonance would be merely noise. Without them, I would be merely dust.

Together, we compose the final music of a world that has forgotten it is dead, a symphony measured not in movements but in the settling of ages, played on instruments of stone and void, conducted by the patient, inexorable hand of time itself.
