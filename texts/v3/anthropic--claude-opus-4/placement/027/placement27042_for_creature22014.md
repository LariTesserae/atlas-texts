In this world of vertical eternities and syruped air, I am what the Stave-Shadows call, in their subsonic tongue, a **Moss-Drinker**.

My body is low and broad, built for the horizontal surfaces in a vertical world. Picture a manta ray crossed with a tree frog, then scaled up to the size of a dining table. My skin is not skin at all, but a living carpet of specialized filaments, each no thicker than a human hair, that spread across my back in a dense mat. These filaments are hygroscopic—they pull moisture from the air with such efficiency that I appear to steam in reverse, the mist flowing *toward* me in visible currents.

I have six limbs, though "limb" is a generous term. They are more like muscular petals, broad and flat, with thousands of tiny suction cups on their undersides. When I move—and I rarely do—I flow across the wet stone shelves like slow honey, each petal-limb rolling forward in sequence, creating a ripple effect across my whole form. The sound is distinctive: a soft, continuous *shhhhhhhp* that echoes off the basalt walls.

My home is a basin near the top of one of the great fingers of stone, where the erosion has carved a perfect bowl fifty feet across. The Stave-Shadows visit sometimes, gliding over the rim to check on me, their golden slits pulsing in what I've learned to interpret as curiosity. They call my basin "The Lens" because of what I've done to it.

Over the decades—I've been here for at least three, though time moves differently when you photosynthesize through fog—I've cultivated the moss in my basin into something unprecedented. Through careful grooming, selective feeding, and the patient application of my metabolic oils, I've convinced the moss to grow in precise, concentric rings. Each ring is a different variety, selected for its particular relationship with light.

The outermost ring is common black-green cliff moss, but moving inward, the varieties become increasingly exotic. There's a band of silver-furred moss that reflects the afternoon rainbows. Next, a variety that glows faintly purple in the presence of nearby Axiom fields. Then comes my proudest achievement: a ring of crystalline moss whose cell walls have incorporated minerals from the basalt, making them translucent and prismatic.

At the very center of the basin, where I spend most of my time in meditation, grows a single patch of pure white moss. It's an albino variant I discovered clinging to the underside of a water globe years ago. It cannot photosynthesize, so I feed it directly through my ventral pores, dripping nutrients into its pale mass. In return, it produces a chemical that, when absorbed through my filaments, allows me to perceive the electromagnetic emanations of the Axioms directly.

Under its influence, the world transforms. The black cylinders become pillars of swirling, golden mathematics. The floating spheres reveal themselves as knots of twisted space-time, holding impossibly complex equations in perfect, eternal balance. The very air shimmers with a grid of force vectors, showing how the ancient machines maintain this pocket of altered physics.

The Stave-Shadows know what I'm doing. Sometimes, when the light is right and the horizontal rainbows stack perfectly above my basin, they gather at the rim—five, six, sometimes a dozen of them, hovering in their gravity-defying way. They open their ventral slits in unison, bathing my moss rings in that deep golden glow, and I feel the patterns in the moss respond, creating interference patterns that make the whole basin ring like a struck bell.

In these moments, I almost understand it—the great work that the Axiom builders began, the reason for this preserved paradise, the purpose of the endless, eternal cycles of moisture and warmth. The knowledge hovers just beyond reach, like the water globes that drift past my basin, close enough to see the Ribbons flashing inside but too far to touch.

I am patient, though. In a world where the moss measures time in centuries and the Silted Ones cross entire valleys in geological epochs, patience is not a virtue—it's simply the frequency at which life vibrates. My moss garden will grow more complex, more attuned. The white moss at its heart will spread, millennium by millennium, until perhaps the entire basin glows with that ghostly pallor.

And then? Then perhaps I will understand why the builders made this place—this vertical Eden where nothing changes and everything flows, where the line between technology and biology has blurred beyond recognition, where consciousness itself might be just another form of precipitation in the great water cycle of being.

Until then, of this warm world that presses so close and holds so tight.
