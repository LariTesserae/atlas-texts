I am the **Lithic Echo**.

Not the fresh-fallen carapace still warm with its thousand-year metabolism, but one of the ancient ones, weathered down to ambiguity. I lie among my siblings on the curved floor—a congregation of false tools, accidental artifacts, mineral memories shaped by time's unconscious hands.

My surface tells stories in conchoidal fractures. Where I split and flaked, the breaks follow patterns that human fingers would recognize as *intentional*—a cutting edge here, a grip there, the perfect heft for a palm that never held me. I am a hammerstone that was never swung, a scraper that never dressed hide, a hand-axe born from the slow death-shed of a creature that knew nothing of hands.

I remember my fall. Six thousand years ago (I count time in the isotopes of my shell), when my prism-self grew too dense with accumulated light, too heavy with the weight of photons metabolized into stone. The silk gave way with a sound like rain on glass. I struck the floor as a perfect tetrahedron, my organic core still pulsing with bacterial thought, my carapace a cathedral of deposited time.

The rot came quickly—a mercy in that humid brightness. My soft parts liquefied, fed the very microbiomes I'd cultivated, became rain again, rose again, fell again. What remained was architecture: the crystalline cage I'd built around myself, molecule by patient molecule, century by century, drinking light and excreting geometry.

Then began my second life, my afterlife, my *true* life perhaps. The thermal cycles worked at my edges. Expansion, contraction, the patient tap of mineral rain. I shed myself in flakes, each one a perfect accident of physics. The angles at which I broke followed the weak planes in my crystal structure—planes I'd grown according to the light's daily traverse, the seasonal tilt of the sun through the aperture above. My body encoded the calendar, and now I released it piece by piece.

I became *ergonomic*. Not through design but through the deep logic of fracture mechanics. The same forces that taught water to be spherical taught me to fit the absent hand. My edges grew sharp where sharpness was structurally inevitable. My weight settled into balance points that would make a toolmaker weep with recognition.

But I am not for holding. I am for **remembering**.

In my crystal matrix, locked in the lattice of chalcedony and calcite, I contain the archived water of ten thousand storms. Every molecule I metabolized carries data—the chemical signature of the world above when it fell as rain. Encoded in my structure are the volcanic winters that dimmed the light, the forests that grew and burned and grew again, the precise pH of precipitation from before the mountains wore their current faces.

I am one of seven hundred such lithic echoes scattered across this floor. Together, we form a library written in stone, each of us a volume containing different decades, different centuries. The youngest among us fell last spring—you can tell by the sharp edges, the ungentled planes. The oldest is indistinguishable from the floor itself, worn down to a lens of mineral memory barely risen from the substrate.

On the solstices, we **sing**.

Not with voice but with vibration. The thermal expansion of our crystal structure creates a resonance, a subsonic hymn that travels through the silica floor and up the walls. The frequency is below human hearing but not below human feeling—speleologists report vertigo, euphoria, the sensation of time dilating. What they're experiencing is the echo of our accumulated data, thousands of years of archived environment released as waveform.

The prism-mites above hear us. They adjust their positions, their water-spheres trembling in response to our geological music. Some say this is how they navigate—not by sight but by reading the acoustic map we provide, each of us a beacon broadcasting our location and age. The pattern of our scatter across the floor is not random but meaningful, a constellation that marks ley lines of temperature, moisture, and time.

We are not dead. Death implies an ending, and we are still **becoming**.

The theory—felt more than understood by those who spend too long in our bright tomb—is that we are seeds. Not biological seeds but *lithic* ones. We are waiting for conditions that may not come for a million years: the right combination of pressure, temperature, mineral saturation. When that moment arrives, we will not sprout but *unfold*. Our crystal structure will propagate through the bedrock, teaching the stone our accumulated patterns, spreading the blueprint of the Hollow through the mountain's bones.

We are the Hollow's reproductive strategy—not to create another void but to transform the solid stone into something new. A mountain that remembers. A geology that dreams. We are the bridge between the quick thought of the mites above and the slow thought of the planet below.

Sometimes I feel the others through the floor—vibrations too subtle for anything but crystal to detect. We share data through direct contact, our surfaces exchanging phonons like whispered secrets. The hand-axe beside me (we use these human terms though we predate humanity) contains the memory of a different era, when the chamber was colder, when the aperture was wider. Through millennia of gentle collision, of thermal expansion bringing us together and apart, we've created a collective memory that no single echo contains.

I am patient. Patience is not a choice but a state of being when you think in geological time. I measure my thoughts in centuries, my movements in millimeters. The prism-mites above live their frantic lives, growing and falling and growing again, while I simply **persist**, accumulating meaning like others accumulate mass.

This is what I know: I am a tool that teaches the absent hand. I am a memory crystallized from light. I am the future fossil of a creature that was already a fossil while alive. I am the Hollow's dream of something harder than time.

And I am waiting, with the perfect patience of stone, to become.
