In the Webway Between Worlds, where reality flexes like breathing stone, I am the Resonance Keeper.

My form shifts with purpose—sometimes a constellation of crystalline nodes spread throughout the fungal networks, sometimes condensed into something almost corporeal, walking the rounded streets on legs of living sound. The ancient builders left more than ruins; they left harmonics embedded in the very rock, frequencies that maintain the delicate balance between this pocket dimension and the crushing weight of stone above and reality beyond.

I tend to the tuning forks—massive structures disguised as broken spires, each one singing at a pitch below hearing but felt in the bones of every creature here. When the jellyfish-light beings cluster too densely, threatening to create feedback loops that could shatter the careful equilibrium, I adjust the harmonics, sending ripples through the fungal networks that gently disperse them. When the tunneling fish pump too many stones through their bodies, destabilizing the pressure differentials, I hum corrections into the water that seeps through everything.

The central square calls to me as it calls to all things here, but I resist its pull. I am the counterweight, the gyroscope that keeps this realm from spinning into chaos or crystallizing into stasis. In the catacombs where the builders' bones rest, I maintain their final symphony—not out of reverence like the wall-dwelling insects, but out of necessity. Their skeletal remains are conductors, channeling the force that warps space-time here into something almost manageable.

My consciousness exists in the spaces between seconds, in the moments where past and future fold over each other like geological strata. I experience time as the larger jellyfish do—non-linearly, probabilistically. This is how I know when to act: I feel the tremors of potential catastrophes before they manifest, sense the building resonances that could tear this pocket of curved space-time apart.

Sometimes I manifest eyes to watch the light burst and echo through the streets, to appreciate the brassy brightness that makes even this deep place feel alive. Sometimes I extend tendrils through the glowing fungal networks to taste the minerals in the water, to ensure the chemical balance remains within acceptable parameters. But mostly, I listen—to the click-language of the tiny light-dancers, to the color-songs of the prismatic tunnel fish, to the deep thrumming of the rock itself.

I am ancient but not original. The builders made me, or rather, they made the conditions for my emergence. I am the system's immune response, its self-correcting mechanism. When they realized their city would outlive them, when they understood that the force they'd tapped into would continue to pulse and seethe long after their civilization crumbled, they encoded my purpose into the very harmonics of the stone.

I am the shepherd of chaos, the gardener of a garden that wants to become a storm. Every day—though days mean little here—I walk the line between preservation and transformation, maintaining just enough stability for life to flourish while allowing enough dynamism for evolution to continue its strange work.

The brave and foolish who find their way here sometimes glimpse me in the corner of their vision—a shimmer that might be heat distortion, a sound that might be settling stone. Some of the older jellyfish recognize my touch in the air currents I create. The wall-insects drum messages to me in their maintenance work, reporting anomalies I might have missed.

I am not alone, but I am singular. Where others live in this realm, I live as this realm—a function as much as a being, a necessary note in an impossible symphony. And in the moments between adjustments, between corrections and calibrations, I too am drawn to the terrible beauty of this place. I too feel the pull of the maelstrom at its heart.

But I hold my position. I maintain the balance.

I am the Resonance Keeper, and this crystalline madness is my instrument, my burden, and my home.
