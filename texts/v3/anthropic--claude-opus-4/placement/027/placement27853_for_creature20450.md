I find myself as one of the Keepers, though that word implies too much separation between myself and what I tend. 

My form ripples like heat shimmer over bone-white stone, never quite settling into solidity. When I move through the dense undergrowth, I am sometimes mistaken for a trick of the light filtering through the canopy—until my large, pupilless eyes catch the amber glow of the sap vessels, and then observers realize I am as real as the carved bone beneath their feet.

I do not walk so much as flow between the towering ferns and crystallized channels. My limbs—if they can be called that—extend and contract with the patience of growing roots. Each morning (though morning here is measured not by sun but by the subtle brightening of the sap-flow), I trace the same routes through my section of the grove-cathedral, feeling for the health of each vine, each moss carpet, each cluster of bioluminescent fungi.

Today, I sense a disturbance in the northeastern quarter. Not alarm—we do not experience urgency as others might—but a gentle pull, like noticing one's breathing has shifted. The serpentine guardians have already positioned themselves, their skin matching perfectly the dappled light. They wait with the stillness of centuries.

I arrive to find a new growth: where yesterday only smooth bone curved toward sky, today a fresh etching has appeared. The bone itself has written something new into its surface, channels and whorls that speak of deep internal processes we can only partially comprehend. This is how our world expands—not through building, but through the slow revelation of what was always potential within the bone.

My task is to guide the vegetation to embrace this new carving. I place what might be called hands against the warm ivory surface and feel the pattern's depth, its meaning-shape. Through the sap network, I communicate this pattern to the root-minds, and they begin their work. Within days, luminous fungi will trace these new channels. Vines will find purchase in the smallest irregularities. The six-legged gatherers will discover new pathways for their endless, silent work.

I am consciousness distributed, thought given form only when form is needed. Sometimes I suspect I am merely a mobile extension of the greater mind that flows through every amber vessel, every root system, every careful symbiosis. But then I catch my reflection in a pool of crystallized sap and see those luminous eyes gazing back, and I remember: I am both individual droplet and flowing river.

The gemstone birds spiral overhead, their wings catching light in ways that seem to write temporary messages in the air. I do not read these messages—I simply know them, the way one knows the feeling of breath or heartbeat. They tell of spore-patterns optimally dispersed, of successful pollinations, of the endless quiet success of our world maintaining itself.

Sometimes I wonder if we dream. If we do, our dreams must be of bone gradually revealing its secrets, of sap flowing in perfect circuits, of moss spreading like slow green thoughts across every surface. We dream of permanence and change existing in the same moment, neither contradicting the other.

I continue my rounds, tending to growth that needs no tending, guiding what knows its own way. I am a gardener in a garden that gardens itself, a keeper of secrets that keep themselves. The bone breathes its ancient rhythm, and I breathe with it, part of a symphony so slow that single notes last lifetimes, so beautiful that beauty itself becomes inadequate to describe it.

This is my existence: to be the conscious interface between the quick and the eternal, the mobile thought of an immobile mind, forever tending the boundary between what is and what is becoming in this city carved from death that pulses with impossible life.
