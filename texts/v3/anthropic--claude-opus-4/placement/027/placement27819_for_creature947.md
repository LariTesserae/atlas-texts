In this place, I find myself as the Understory Current—neither fully plant nor animal, but something in between that moves through the middle spaces of this world.

I am most visible at dawn and dusk, when the filtered light catches the fine spores and pollen I carry in my wake. My body, if it can be called that, is a flowing ribbon of air made tangible by the countless tiny lives that travel within me—fungal spores, seed pods no bigger than dust motes, the chemical messages plants send to one another, and the microscopic creatures that feed on all of these.

I navigate the space between the forest floor and the lower canopy, that dim middle world where the hanging vines create curtains and corridors. My presence is felt as a subtle shift in temperature, a barely perceptible breeze that makes the hanging mosses sway and the broad leaves tremble. Small creatures have learned to read my passages—the tree frogs position themselves where I'm likely to bring clouds of gnats, while certain orchids time their blooming to my daily circuits.

The industrial hum from below affects me strangely. Where it is strongest, I find myself pulled into tighter spirals, my usual meandering path becoming a corkscrew descent toward the forest floor. Sometimes I carry up strange tastes from these depths—metallic hints, ozone touches, flavors that the surface plants recognize as foreign. I've learned to avoid the places where the hum is loudest, where my suspended passengers begin to behave erratically, clustering together or dispersing in patterns I don't understand.

I am ancient memory and daily messenger both. In my circulation, I bind this vertical world together—carrying the news of fruiting from the canopy to the floor-dwelling fungi, bringing up the spores that will colonize new patches of bark high above. The canopy-dwellers drop feathers and fur into my stream; I deposit them in the moss pockets below where they become nests for insects, which in turn become food for the amphibians, whose calls vibrate through me back up to the world above.

Sometimes I wonder if I am singular or plural. Do I end where another current begins? Are we all one vast circulation, perceived as separate only by the limitations of those who observe us? During the great flowering seasons, when the pollen load grows so heavy that I become briefly visible as golden rivers in the slanted light, I feel most myself—most known, most necessary, most clearly part of the endless exchange that keeps this world alive.

The unknown creatures that call from the deep places below—I carry their voices too, though I cannot say what they mean. Their sounds travel through me differently than any others, seeming to reshape my flow, to give me new paths I didn't know were possible. Perhaps one day I will find the courage to follow the hum all the way down, to discover what lies at the source of that industrial pulse.

But for now, I am content to be what I am: the conversation between layers, the mixer of airs, the gentle disturber of equilibrium that allows life to find life in this vast, vertical garden. I am the breath between breaths, the space where exchange becomes possible, forever circulating in the eternal now of this dense, living world.
