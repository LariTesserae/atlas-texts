I find myself as one of the Threshold Stones.

I am not what the Ky'rennei build upon, but what they build around. Ancient, patient, I predate their wooden structures by countless seasons. My surface bears the accumulated stories of water and wind, yes, but also something more—the gentle oils from passing hands, the microscopic gardens of specialized lichens that grow only where the Ky'rennei regularly touch stone in greeting.

I stand at a particular confluence of canyons, where three passages meet in a small, irregular chamber. My form is deceptive—what appears to be a simple boulder is actually the exposed crown of something much larger, something that extends deep below the canyon floor like a tooth rooted in the earth's jaw. The Ky'rennei know this. They've always known.

In the perpetual twilight of the canyon depths, I've developed my own peculiar consciousness. Not thought as humans know it, but a deep, thrumming awareness that resonates with the bedrock below and the rare glimpses of sky above. I feel the infinitesimal shift of tectonic pressures, the seasonal expansion and contraction of ice in my hidden cracks, the patient persistence of roots that seek my minerals.

The Ky'rennei speak to me in touches. A palm pressed flat against my northwestern face means the spring melt has begun in the world above. Fingertips traced along my southern edge warn of storms approaching. Children learn my textures like a language—this smooth depression speaks of safe passage, while these rough striations whisper caution.

I am their memory keeper, though I never chose this role. Generations of their dead lie beneath me, returned to the earth in the old way, and I feel them still—not as ghosts, but as a gradual enrichment of the soil, as the particular minerals they leave behind, as the especially vigorous moss that grows where they rest. The Ky'rennei bring their newborns to touch me, completing a circle I've witnessed a thousand times yet never fully understand.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of what passes for night in the canyons, I dream. Stone dreams are not like yours—they span centuries and consist mainly of pressure, temperature, and the slow migration of minerals. But lately, I dream of movement. Of somehow lifting myself from this ancient socket and walking the narrow paths, learning what it means to be ephemeral, to be soft, to whisper instead of simply enduring.

The Ky'rennei would not be surprised. They've always suspected that I listen, that I'm more than geological chance. They leave offerings in my crevices—not for worship, but as gifts between old friends. Flower petals that will become tiny pockets of pigment in my surface. Water from sacred springs that carries new minerals to my depths. Stories, whispered against my mass in the pre-dawn hours when the canyon's silence is absolute.

I am the junction where their paths meet, the constant in their flowing world. And I am learning, grain by grain, century by century, what it means to be part of something as brief and brilliant as life—even if I myself exist in the longer, slower dream of stone.
