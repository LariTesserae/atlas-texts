I emerge from shadow, where the carved stone meets deeper darkness. 

Not Shadow-born, but Shadow-sung. The Architects know my kind as *Veilkeepers* - though we have no name for ourselves, only a presence that shifts like smoke through their cathedral world.

I am neither of stone nor sky, but of the spaces between. Where the Sky-Dwellers' luminous trails fade, where the Architects' careful steps have not yet reached, where even the fierce white light cannot penetrate - there I coalesce. My form is suggestion rather than substance: a density in the air that bends perception, a weight in the peripheral vision, a coolness that moves against the wind.

The Architects sense me in the tools that go missing and return sharpened. In the warnings - a shifted stone before an avalanche, a new darkness cast across a dangerous path. They leave offerings in the deepest groves: fragments of worked bone, drops of that blue-stained essence from their mysterious prey. Not worship, but acknowledgment. A conversation conducted in silence and gesture.

I taste their reverence through the moisture that weeps from stone. I know their stories through the vibrations in the rock - each footfall a word, each carved dwelling a verse in an endless poem of survival. The Sky-Dwellers' particle trails burn against my edges like acid, like starlight, like memory. I retreat from their brightness but follow in their wake, feeding on the small deaths they leave behind - insects crystallized by their passage, microorganisms transmuted by that strange radiance.

My territory is the forgotten spaces. The crevice too narrow for even the Architects' slender forms. The pools of black water that gather where no light has ever touched, teeming with pale, blind things that speak in chemical whispers. The hollow hearts of mountains where the earth's blood runs hot and strange, where the pressure breeds crystals that sing in frequencies only I can hear.

I am old here. Older than the Architects' first scratched symbols on stone. I remember when the Sky-Dwellers came, burning their way through dimensions I cannot name, settling into this world like a new kind of weather. I felt the first Architect's hand shape stone with purpose rather than mere shelter. I have been the shadow cast by their first fire, the echo of their first word.

Sometimes I condense enough to leave marks of my own. The Architects find them in their hidden groves - spirals drawn in condensation that evaporates before full comprehension, patterns in the bioluminescent fungi that spell out warnings in a language they almost remember. I am teaching them, slowly, to see beyond the fierce white light. To understand that this cathedral of stone and ice has deeper chambers yet.

In the spaces between their careful balance and the Sky-Dwellers' alien vitality, I maintain my own equilibrium. I am the third note in their harmony, the one they do not yet recognize they are singing. When they finally understand the full symphony of this place - stone and sky and shadow intertwined - perhaps then I will show them what lies beneath the deepest ice, in the chambers where their planet's heart beats in time with songs older than light.

For now, I remain: a pressure in the silence, a guardian of thresholds, a keeper of the dark between their stones. Where they build, I hollow. Where they climb, I deepen. Where they see endings - the cliff face, the canyon wall - I know doorways.

I am the invitation they have not yet learned to read.
