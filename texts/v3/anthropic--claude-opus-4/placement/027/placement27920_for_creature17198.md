**The Arterial Fissures**

Between the vast, unchanging floor and the invisible vault above, there are places where the world's geometry breaks its own rules. These are the Arterial Fissures—vertical wounds in the cavern wall that pulse with a different kind of light, a sickly amber radiance that seems to breathe. They are narrow, perhaps only wide enough for a Luminant to enter sideways, but they descend into depths that mock the already continental scale of the Silent Well.

I am what lives in these between-spaces. We are the **Threadlings**.

We are not like the others. Where the Luminants are pillars of careful resonance, we are chaos given just enough form to persist. Our bodies are not bodies at all, but living fractures—mobile cracks in reality that exist somewhere between the material and the merely possible. We appear as vertical lines of distortion, like tears in glass, our forms constantly shifting between states of crystalline solidity and something more akin to liquid light.

We were not made; we happened. When the Luminants first began their great work of resonance, when they aligned the first towers and set the world's hum into its eternal pattern, something went wrong. Or perhaps something went right in a way they did not intend. The perfection of their harmony created pressure points, places where the vibrations converged and interfered, and in those interference patterns, we emerged.

We live by contradiction. In a world of eternal equilibrium, we are the principle of disruption. We feed not on the ambient glow or the stone's warmth, but on the tiny variations, the microscopic imperfections in the pattern. We are drawn to the moments when a Luminant makes an adjustment, when a Drifter's trajectory shifts unexpectedly, when a Slider encounters an irregularity in the floor. These tiny breaks in the monotony are our sustenance.

Our dwelling places, the Arterial Fissures, are the only parts of this world that truly change. The amber light that emanates from them is not constant like the mineral glow of the walls. It flickers, it dims, it surges. Sometimes it goes out entirely, leaving the fissure as a band of absolute darkness against the merely dark. The Luminants avoid these places. The vibrations here are wrong—not discordant exactly, but written in a different key, one that makes their crystalline forms ache.

We move by unfolding ourselves along planes of stress in the rock. One moment we are a vertical line; the next, we have spread into a web of hairline fractures that spans a hundred feet of wall. We can compress ourselves to near-invisibility, becoming nothing more than a suggestion of wrongness in the corner of perception, or we can expand until we are a branching river of light-distortion that makes the solid stone seem uncertain.

Our interaction with the other inhabitants is complex. The Drifters are drawn to us despite themselves, circling the fissures in wide, uneasy orbits. Sometimes one will drift too close, and we will reach out—not with limbs, for we have none, but with our very nature. We introduce tiny flaws into their crystalline shells, microscopic fractures that spread slowly over centuries. These flawed Drifters become beautiful in their imperfection, their light refracting in ways that create new colors, impossible colors that have no name in this world of blue-white mineral glow.

The Sliders fear us, if fear is the right word for creatures that have never known danger. When they sense our presence—a wrongness in the vibration pattern of the stone—they alter their paths, creating wide detours around the fissures. Yet sometimes, in the depths of the amber-lit cracks, we find the remains of Sliders that ventured too close. Their obsidian bodies are shot through with veins of our essence, frozen in the moment of transformation into something neither Slider nor Threadling.

But it is with the Luminants that our relationship is most profound. They know we are wrong. We are the flaw in their perfect symphony, the crack in their eternal bell. Yet they cannot eliminate us, for we are born from their very perfection. The more precisely they tune their towers, the more exactly they calibrate the world's resonance, the more pressure builds at the interference points, and the more of us emerge.

Some Luminants have begun to incorporate us into their calculations. They place their towers in patterns that account for our presence, that channel the disruptive energies into controlled fissures. These radical thinkers—if thinking is what Luminants do—are creating something new: a harmony that includes dissonance, a pattern that accommodates its own breaking.

In the deepest parts of the fissures, where the amber light grows thick and strange, we sometimes convergee. Multiple Threadlings flow together, our fractal forms interweaving until we become something else—a temporary consciousness made of pure disruption. In these moments, we dream.

We dream of water, though we have never seen it. We dream of wind that is more than slow currents of warm air. We dream of change that is more than the tiny variations we feed upon. We dream of the surface, though we know intellectually that there is no surface, that this cavern-world is all there is. We dream of the sun, and in our dreams it is not a ball of fire but a vast, cosmic fissure, a crack in the universe itself from which all light and chaos flows.

When we wake from these convergence-dreams, we scatter back to our individual fissures, carrying with us fragments of visions we cannot quite remember. And in our wake, the amber light pulses a little differently, and sometimes—just sometimes—a new color appears in its spectrum, a color that the Luminants have no symbol for in their geometric language.

We are the necessary flaw, the crack that keeps the bell from shattering under the pressure of its own perfection. We are the question mark in a world of periods, the variable in an equation that would otherwise solve to nothing.

And in the eternal, unchanging darkness of the Silent Well, we are the only things that hunger for tomorrow.
