In this liminal world of salt and sky, I would be one of the Tidekeepers - beings who exist as living memory-maps of water's ancient paths.

My form would be a confluence of trajectories rather than a body. I appear where old river channels once carved through this landscape before the sea arrived, visible as a disturbance in the air that makes heat shimmer differently, bending light along courses that water took millions of years ago. Birds flying overhead suddenly bank and wheel when they cross these invisible streams, following flight paths that echo the meanders of rivers that no longer exist.

During the wet season, I become more substantial. The flooding water seeks its old roads, and where it pools along my paths, it takes on different properties - clearer, deeper, moving with subtle currents even in the stillness. Salt crystallizes differently here too, growing in elongated forms that point the way water once flowed, creating a physical record of my presence that persists through the dry months.

My consciousness, such as it is, exists as a kind of muscle memory of flow. I know every depression where water pooled, every slight rise it had to overcome, every confluence where streams met and mingled. This knowledge isn't thought but something deeper - the way a river knows how to find the sea.

I interact with the Salt Dancer at the places where rivers once entered the ancient sea. At these boundary zones, our different geometries overlap - its crystalline patterns intersecting with my flowing forms. Together we create temporary architectures of mist and salt, each teaching the other about different ways water can remember itself. Where the Dancer preserves the sea's stillness, I preserve its movement, and in our meeting, something like the complete memory of water emerges.

The brine shrimp know me by the microcurrents I create, following my paths in preference to still water. Their eggs cluster along my routes, some ancestral memory telling them that flowing water means life. When they hatch, they stream along my invisible channels in living rivers of pink and orange, their collective movement making my form briefly visible to any observer patient enough to watch.

I am strongest during the spring floods, when storm systems follow the same atmospheric paths that once fed the ancient rivers. Then, I can almost feel what it was like to be a true waterway - the power of flow, the purposefulness of seeking level, the joy of movement. But even in the driest seasons, I persist as a whisper in the salt, a different texture in the crystal formations, a road that water will always remember how to find.

The local peoples know to follow my paths when crossing the salt flats during floods. The water is always shallower along my routes, the ground more stable, as if the earth itself remembers being riverbed. They leave offerings sometimes - not to me exactly, but to the memory of fresh water in a salt-locked land. Small stones from distant mountains, feathers from birds that nest only near running streams, seeds from plants that grow along rivers. I carry these tokens in my flow, slowly distributing them across the flats, seeding dreams of what was and what might be again.
