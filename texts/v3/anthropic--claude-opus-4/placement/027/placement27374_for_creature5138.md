In the deepest trenches where volcanic vents paint the darkness with their heat-shimmer, I would be the Architect-Worm—a segmented creature of perhaps thirty meters, my body a living construction site. Each of my segments would be specialized: some bearing arrays of sensitive cilia that taste the mineral content of water and stone, others equipped with grinding plates that can shape basalt while it's still warm, still others secreting the binding resins that hold my constructions together until time fuses them properly.

I would not build chambers for myself—my body is already a chamber, a mobile workshop. Instead, I would craft these precise pockets for others, for the careful custodians who would tend them. My satisfaction would come not from inhabiting but from the moment of completion, when I taste the water flowing correctly through the vents, when the shell-lights cast their intended patterns, when every angle holds its proper tension.

The custodians would never see me. By the time they arrive to claim their chambers, I would already be kilometers away, following the heat-trails to the next building site. My only signature would be the perfection of the cuts, the way each stone knows exactly where it should rest, the way the chambers seem to have always existed despite their obvious newness.

I would carry with me samples—fragments of the best stones, strips of the most responsive hides, memories of successful geometries encoded in the chemical libraries of my segments. My body would be a catalog of all the chambers I've built, each one teaching me something new about the relationship between water, light, and habitation.

At the spreading zones where new seafloor emerges, still glowing, I would be first—shaping the molten rock while it remains pliable, setting the foundations for chambers that won't be completed for decades, leaving markers that only I and my kind can read: *here, when the stone cools, cut along this line; here, where the current will be strongest, leave space for the speaking-vents.*
