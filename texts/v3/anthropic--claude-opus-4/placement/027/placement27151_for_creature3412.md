I am a deep fissure in the plateau's edge, where the basalt has cracked clean through to reveal layers of time frozen in stone. My walls descend beyond sight into the cotton-wool void below, narrowing from a wound twenty feet wide at the surface to a mere hand's breadth before darkness swallows all dimension.

Water beads along my edges but never falls. Droplets hang suspended in my depths like a string of pearls that catches what little light penetrates, each one a tiny lens reflecting the world above. Some have gathered into impossible formations - water that has begun to flow sideways along my walls, following the geometric runnels, only to stop mid-journey, creating frozen waterfalls that jut horizontally into my hollow space.

I am carpeted in the thickest moss on the plateau, fed by the perpetual moisture. It grows in terraces down my walls, each cushion swollen with trapped time. The moss glows faintly in my depths - not with any warmth, but with that same absorbed light that haunts the city's stone. In the deepest places I can feel, where even the sharp plateau light cannot reach, the moss emanates a cold phosphorescence, painting my walls in gradients of ghostly green.

The Stone-Singers knew me. Their tool marks scar my upper edges where they began to carve handholds, perhaps planning to descend, to explore, to transform me into another inhabited hollow. But the work is unfinished - a ladder that leads only six feet down before ending abruptly, the last rung half-formed. Stone dust still clings to the carved surface, neither settling nor dispersing.

Birds nest in my walls - or rather, they were nesting. I hold a dozen frozen tableaux: a Sky-Kite with wings folded, beak open to feed invisible young in a moss-cup nest; another caught mid-landing, talons extended toward a perch it will never grasp. Their nests are architectural marvels of woven moss and shed feathers, some with eggs visible - perfect ovoids that will never warm, never cool, never hatch.

Most unsettling are the roots. The gnarled trees above have sent them questing down through cracks in my walls. They emerge in places like frozen tentacles, some thick as a human arm, others delicate as thread. One has grown straight out into my void, extending impossibly into air without support, its tip forever seeking soil it will never find. Water droplets cling to these roots too, and in the stillness, I can feel how they were drinking when time stopped - the moisture half-absorbed into the bark, creating patterns of wet and dry that map a biological process arrested mid-function.

At my deepest point that light can reach, something glimmers. It might be a vein of crystal in the basalt, or it might be something else - a tool dropped by an exploring Stone-Singer, a larger creature that fell and was frozen mid-descent, or simply an accumulation of that strange, absorbed light that has pooled like mercury in my depths. The angle prevents clear sight from above, and no one will ever descend to investigate.

I am a wound that will never heal, a question that will never be answered. My edges are sharp enough to cut, yet they will never dull. I am both sanctuary and trap - sheltering my frozen inhabitants from the plateau's raw exposure while holding them in an embrace that will never release. The mist that shrouds the world below rises through me but never quite emerges, adding my depths to the catalog of incompletions that define this place.

In the before-time, I was young - a fresh split in the world's skin. Now I am eternal, my youth preserved in the same breath that preserves everything here. I am the fissure that proves the plateau's solidity, the depth that defines its height, the darkness that makes its sharp light meaningful. 

And in my deepest places, where even the frozen moss-light fails, I hold spaces that have never known illumination and never will - pockets of perfect blindness in a world of crystalline sight, where the stillness is so absolute that even darkness itself has weight and form and terrible permanence.
