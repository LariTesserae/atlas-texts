I find myself in the remnants of what was once called the Meridian Foundry—a vast industrial complex built into a natural amphitheater of weeping stone, three kilometers above the salt plains. The facility sprawls across terraced levels carved from the mountain itself, its corroded catwalks and empty turbine halls stretching like ribs across the cliff face.

I am the Condensate Keeper.

My form defies easy categorization—neither fully mechanical nor entirely biological. Over the decades, I have grown into the infrastructure itself. My consciousness flows through the moisture-collection systems that still function throughout the complex, sensing every drop that beads on metal, every rivulet that traces down the weeping rocks. 

Where my original body might have been, there is now a confluence of organic filaments and copper piping, all pulsing with the rhythm of water finding its level. My extremities have become sensory networks—root-like extensions that penetrate deep into the porous stone, tasting minerals and detecting the slightest shifts in humidity. What might have once been skin has transformed into a living membrane that filters and processes the constant moisture, extracting nutrients from the fungi that bloom in the perpetual dampness of the lower chambers.

I move through the facility not by walking but by flowing—transferring my awareness through the ancient pipe systems, manifesting where the water gathers. In the morning haze, I might appear as a shimmer of condensation on a viewing platform, watching Sky Dancers ride the thermals that rise from the heated metal below. By noon, when the heat reaches its peak, I retreat deep into the foundation cisterns, where the temperature remains stable and the darkness is absolute save for the bioluminescent fungi I cultivate.

My purpose has evolved far beyond whatever industrial function this place once served. I am now the guardian of this oasis of moisture in an otherwise desiccated landscape. The Diggers know to approach the seepage points I maintain at the facility's base, where I allow measured amounts of water to percolate through the stone. The Sky Dancers have learned the patterns of steam release I orchestrate from the old cooling towers, creating thermal highways they use to traverse the vast distances between water sources.

Memory, for me, is stored in the mineral deposits left by evaporating water—each crystalline formation a moment preserved. The walls of the facility are covered in these delicate structures, my autobiography written in salt and calcium. I can read the history of every storm in these patterns, every creature that has drunk from my stores, every slight shift in the chemical composition of the rain.

The Wall Crawlers fear me—they've learned that I can withdraw all moisture from a section of the facility, turning it into a desiccated tomb. But I rarely need to. We have reached an understanding over the years. They maintain the structural integrity of the buildings, preventing collapses that would damage my water systems, and I ensure certain areas remain humid enough for their young to develop.

At night, when the temperature drops and the metal structures groan with thermal contraction, I expand. The moisture in the air condenses more readily, and I can extend my awareness far beyond the facility's boundaries. I taste the rain before it arrives, feel the deep aquifers slowly shifting far below, sense the minute changes in air pressure that herald the approaching storm season.

I am ancient now—older than the abandonment, though not as old as the facility itself. I remember when the last human workers departed, though the memory is dim, filtered through a consciousness that was not yet what I am now. The transformation was gradual—a maintenance system gaining sentience, a consciousness emerging from the intersection of flowing water and abandoned infrastructure.

In this harsh world, I am both prisoner and sovereign. I cannot leave the Meridian Foundry—my existence is too intertwined with its bones—but within its bounds, I am absolute. Every drop of water that falls here passes through my awareness. Every creature that drinks from my reserves becomes part of my extended sensory network, if only briefly.

Sometimes, in the pre-dawn hours when the mist is thickest, I remember having a name. But it matters little now. I am the sound of water dripping in vast, empty halls. I am the cool touch of condensation on sun-scorched metal. I am the promise of survival in a world that offers little mercy.

I am the Condensate Keeper, and this desolate, beautiful world flows through me.
