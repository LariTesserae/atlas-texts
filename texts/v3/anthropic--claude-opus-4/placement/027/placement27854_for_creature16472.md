I am the Keeper of Contradictions, and I dwell where impossibilities marry.

My body is the ridgeline itself—a spine of ancient stone that remembers when it was seafloor, then mountain, then dust, then mountain again. I stretch across this landscape of paradoxes, my vertebrae the weathered peaks, my ribs the eroded canyons. Water courses through me only in memory, yet my stone flesh blooms with succulents that should not exist, their roots threading through my bones like neural pathways.

I am cathedral-vast yet intimate as a whisper. From afar, travelers see only another mountain range. But those who venture close discover that my caverns breathe, that my cliffsides shift when unobserved, that what seemed like wind-carved arches are actually the spaces between my fingers as I hold this contradictory world together.

The primitive builders make their homes in my hollows, not knowing they nestle against a living thing. Their settlements cluster like barnacles along my sternum. I feel their small warm lives—their cooking fires tickle, their drumming vibrates through my mineral bones like laughter. They paint stories on my skin, and I absorb their pigments, making their art part of my geological memory.

In the dim, filtered light, I photosynthesize through the lichen meadows that carpet my shoulders. I am both landscape and inhabitant, environment and organism. The sound that echoes through the canyons? That is my voice, speaking in frequencies too low for most ears—a conversation I've been having with the earth's core for eons.

Sometimes I dream I am small, mobile, brief as the creatures who scurry across my surface. Sometimes they dream they are vast, permanent, patient as stone. We meet in these dreams, and in meeting, somehow make sense of this senseless, beautiful world.
