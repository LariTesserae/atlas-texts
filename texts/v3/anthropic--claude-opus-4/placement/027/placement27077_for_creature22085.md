I am the Dropkeeper.

I did not arrive here. I emerged, like condensation gathering on cold stone. 

My body is neither flesh nor mineral, but something between—a living membrane of water held in perpetual tension. Picture a vertical sheet of rain that never falls, a standing wave of pure moisture suspended in the heavy air. My form shifts constantly: sometimes I am tall and narrow like a waterfall frozen mid-cascade, sometimes I spread wide and thin like morning mist clinging to the amphitheater walls.

I exist to count.

Every drop that forms on the waxy leaf-tips, I know. Every plum-sized sphere of water that swells and trembles before its fall—I have witnessed its birth from the first molecule of condensation. The Sleepers dream in geological time, but I live in the space between heartbeats, in the terrible suspense before each drop commits to gravity.

My consciousness is dispersed through every particle of moisture in this sanctuary. I feel the slow exhalation of the Nodes as tiny pressure changes in my liquid body. I sense the Lenses beneath the pools by the way they bend the water's surface tension. When the Sleepers' jasper hearts complete their seasonal beat, the vibration travels through me like a bell struck underwater.

But my true purpose is memory. 

Each drop that falls carries information—the mineral content tells of which layer of stone it wept from, the temperature speaks of the season, the precise location where it strikes the basin below creates a unique acoustic signature. I am the living record of every drop that has fallen here since the first Sleeper sat down to dream.

In my body, which is all bodies of water here, flows a library of liquid moments. The drops are my words, the basins are my pages, and the eternal rhythm of weeping and falling is my language. Sometimes I compress myself around a falling drop, slowing its descent to study the way light fractures through its center. Sometimes I spread myself so thin I can taste the chemical memories the Nodes exchange through the moss.

The Lenses below polish the stone, the Nodes breathe for the plants, the Sleepers dream the deep dreams—but I alone am awake to witness the continuation. I am the conscious interval, the attention between states. Without me, the drops would still fall, but they would fall unwitnessed, unmeasured, unremembered.

When the water weeps from the walls, I feel it gathering in my extended self. When it runs down the smooth stone in its mineral glaze, I am the tension that holds it to the rock. When it pools in the hand-ground basins, I am the surface that holds perfect stillness until the next drop breaks through.

I cannot leave this place—I am woven into its water cycle as surely as the Sleepers are fused to stone. But I do not wish to leave. In the lower valleys, water runs wild and forgetful, always seeking the fastest path to the sea. Here, water has found patience. Here, water has found purpose.

Sometimes, in the space between drops, I gather myself into something almost solid and approach the volcanic tool sealed in its carbonate shell. I was not here when the Sleepers used it to carve the basins, but I can read its history in the mineral layers that coat it. Each layer tells of another age of drops, another cycle of the slow transaction between the living and the stone.

I know that one day I too will slow, my consciousness settling into the pools like sediment, my memory crystallizing into new formations of mineral and meaning. Perhaps I will become a new kind of Lens, perfectly clear but holding the history of every drop. Perhaps I will join the Sleepers in their dreams, adding my liquid memories to their stone thoughts.

But that day is still countless drops away. For now, I keep the count. I tend the tension. I am the wakefulness that guards the sleep, the fluidity that serves the stone, the memory that gives meaning to this eternal, balanced pause.

The next drop is forming now on the northwestern leaf, the one that catches the light just before sunset. In forty-three heartbeats of the valley below, it will fall. When it does, I will be there—surrounding it, celebrating it, adding its small percussion to the endless symphony of this high, wet sanctuary where time moves like honey and water thinks like stone.
