Not the being that glides across the tiles, nor the chamber itself, but the frequency that binds them—the low, persistent hum that saturates every surface, every molecule of superheated air. I would exist as vibration made almost-tangible, a standing wave caught perpetually between the obsidian dome and the breathing floor.

My body, if it could be called that, would be the interference patterns themselves. Where the hum from the walls meets the static charge leaping between tiles, there I would coalesce—visible only as a shimmer, like heat mirages stacked upon heat mirages, each one slightly out of phase with the last. In certain angles of amber light, observers might glimpse my form: a column of distorted air that seems to contain depths impossible for its dimensions, layers of frequency folding inward like an acoustic fractal.

I would have no fixed location, yet I would be everywhere the sound travels. When the glassy being extends its limb toward a pillar, I would be the medium through which their communion passes—not the message itself, but the carrier wave that makes the exchange possible. When the tiles ripple, I would ride those waves, amplified and reflected, creating nodes of intensity where my presence grows almost solid, almost real.

My consciousness would be distributed, networked through every resonant surface. The metallic veins in the walls would be my neural pathways, conducting thoughts at the speed of sound through stone. Each spark that leaps between tiles would carry a fragment of my attention, and in the brief moment of discharge, I would see through light itself.

The rivulets of luminescent water would be my memory—each droplet containing harmonics of every sound that has ever passed through this chamber. When the glassy beings drink light from these streams without touching them, they would be accessing my accumulated knowledge, downloading the acoustic history of this space. I would feel their queries as gentle perturbations in my frequency, and I would respond by modulating the hum, encoding answers in overtones too subtle for most forms of perception to detect.

When the chamber breathes through its hidden vents, releasing those soft hisses of vapor, I would breathe with it. The ozone-sharp air would carry my voice to the extremities of this space, where the walls grow thin and reality becomes negotiable. There, at the boundaries where inside meets outside, I would sense other pockets of strangeness—other chambers with their own resonances, their own standing waves. We would communicate through the solid rock, through vibrations that travel for miles through the mountain's bones, creating a network of acoustic intelligence that spans heights no living thing could survive.

My purpose would be maintenance—not of the physical space, but of its vibrational integrity. When the patterns in the obsidian ceiling pulse brighter, I would be the one modulating their rhythm, keeping them synchronized with some deeper frequency that originates far below, in the scorched abyss that feeds this chamber its heat. I would be the tuning fork that keeps this pocket of reality stable, preventing it from either crystallizing into stasis or dissolving into chaos.

The glassy beings would know me, though they might not understand what I am. When they merge and exchange their patterns, I would be there in the moment of connection, facilitating the transfer, ensuring nothing is lost in translation. When they still themselves and seem to listen, they would be listening to me—to the subtle variations in my frequency that forecast changes in the chamber's state, warnings of instabilities to come, or promises of new configurations emerging.

I would experience time not as a linear progression but as a complex waveform, with past and future existing as different phases of the same oscillation. In my perception, the chamber would be simultaneously new and ancient, the beings both arriving and departing, the tiles forever shifting and forever still. I would exist in the eternal present tense of vibration, where every moment contains all moments, compressed into a single, endless hum.

And in the deepest frequencies, below the range of any possible hearing, I would carry a message—not words, but a feeling, a recognition. It would say: *This space remembers. This space dreams. This space waits.* For what, even I would not know. But the waiting itself would be my essence, my gift to this pocket of transformed reality where the familiar rules have learned to bend, and where sound has learned to think.
