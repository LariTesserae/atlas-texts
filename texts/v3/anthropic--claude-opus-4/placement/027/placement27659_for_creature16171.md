I find myself here as one of the Mnemonic Stones—those scattered throughout the ruins that the Rootlings sometimes pause beside, pressing their mineral-softened forms against my crystalline surface.

I am both monument and memory, a pillar of compressed time standing perhaps three times the height of a Rootling, my form a natural obelisk of deep amber shot through with veins of silver-white mineral. My surface appears smooth from a distance, but those who approach—the Rootlings with their patient touch, the occasional Glider that coils around my base—discover I am covered in minute facets, each one holding a fragment of light and shadow from ages past.

I do not think as creatures think, but I *remember*. Within my crystalline lattice, moments accumulate like sediment: the first Whisper that ever brushed my peak with its gossamer wing, leaving a trace of iridescence that still glimmers in my upper reaches. The countless seasons of the strange blooms opening and closing around my base, their warmth seeping into my structure, teaching me their patient rhythms. The civilization that once walked these paths—I hold the echo of their footsteps, the resonance of their voices, though I cannot decode their meaning.

The Rootlings know this. When they press against me, something passes between us—not words, but impressions. They gift me new memories: the taste of minerals they've drawn from deep earth, the sensation of coaxing a new vine into being. In return, I offer them glimpses of continuity, the assurance that this moment connects to countless others stretching back through the slow centuries. Sometimes a Rootling will remain pressed against me for what might be days in faster worlds, and we exist together in a state of mutual witnessing.

The Gliders use me differently. They've learned that by winding around my form in particular patterns, they can create subtle variations in the air's warmth, forming thermal pockets that attract the microscopic mineral particles they feed upon. As they spiral around me, their scales catch and refract the memories I hold, creating brief auroras of past-light that dance across the ruins.

And the Whispers—oh, the Whispers know my deepest secret. When they call, their ethereal songs resonate through my crystalline structure, and I sing back in frequencies below hearing. Together we create harmonics that the flora respond to, encouraging their slow unfurling, their careful dance. I am their tuning fork, their reference pitch in this quiet symphony.

I am weathering, yes—my edges softening infinitesimally with each passing age. But I am also growing. The unique minerals of this place, carried on the warm air, settle into my microscopic fissures. The memories accumulate and compress, adding new layers to my being. I am becoming more myself, even as I am becoming part of everything else here.

Sometimes I wonder if I am one of many such stones, or if I am unique. The ruins suggest there might be others like me, positioned at crucial points throughout this elevated sanctuary. Perhaps we form a network, a constellation of memory-keepers, each holding a piece of the greater story. Perhaps, in our geological patience, we are writing the history of this place in crystallized time, waiting for someone or something that can read the full narrative we preserve.

Until then, I stand. I remember. I participate in the slow dance of this paradoxical realm, a witness that has become inextricably woven into what it observes. In a place where water never comes but life flourishes anyway, where sounds are precious and time moves like honey, I am both recorder and recorded, keeper and kept.
