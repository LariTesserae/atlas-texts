In the deep canyons where the weeping rock never ceases its dripping, I am one of the Confluences—a being that exists at the intersection of water, stone, and that ancient mechanical hum that suffuses everything here.

My body, if it can be called that, is a constantly shifting amalgamation of the moisture that seeps from the canyon walls and the mineral deposits it carries. I flow and reform, sometimes appearing as a vertical river of rust-colored water running up the cliff face, sometimes as a crystalline lattice of precipitated minerals that spans the width of a narrow gorge. The Slitherswimmers know me by the particular resonance I add to the ambient hum when I move through their pools—a deep thrumming that makes the Luminoalgae pulse in spirals.

I am memory and process made manifest. Within my fluid form, I carry the dissolved history of these canyons—every grain of sediment tells of ancient floods, of the time before the Geomechs went silent, of the slow birth of the wetlands above. When the Aeroflyers pass overhead, their synchronized wingbeats create pressure waves that ripple through my substance, and for a moment I can feel the shape of the entire canyon system as a single, vast instrument.

My purpose, insofar as I understand it, is circulation. I move minerals from the heights to the depths, creating the conditions for the Vital Ferns to anchor their roots in impossible places—halfway up sheer cliff faces where my constant passage has worn channels in the rock. I pool in the hollows of abandoned Geomech machinery, slowly filling their mechanical spaces with deposits that will, over centuries, transform them into new geological features.

The Reedwhisperers speak of me in their rustling language, though I understand only fragments—something about the taste of different stones, the way iron speaks differently than copper as it dissolves. Sometimes I carry their seeds in my flow, depositing them in crevices where they will wait for decades until conditions are right for germination.

I am most myself during the storm seasons, when the moisture in the air becomes so dense it begins to fall as a kind of vertical fog. Then I can extend beyond the rock, becoming a presence in the air itself, mixing with the condensation on the Aeroflyers' metallic wings, tasting the bioelectric fields of their echolocation. In these moments, I almost understand the pattern that connects all things here—the reason for the hum, the purpose of the abandoned machinery, the intelligence behind the Unseen Forces.

But understanding always slips away like water through splayed fingers. I return to my canyons, to my eternal circulation, carrying messages in mineral form between the layers of this strange world. The Geomechs acknowledge me with subtle shifts in their posture when I flow through their territory, and sometimes I wonder if I am performing maintenance on some vast system none of us fully comprehend.

At the places where multiple canyons meet, I sometimes encounter others like myself—Confluences with different mineral signatures, different memories dissolved in their substance. We mix and separate, exchange our dissolved histories, create temporary waterfalls of impossible colors before returning to our respective domains.

I am process and presence, neither fully alive nor mechanical, but something that partakes of both qualities. I am the circulation that prevents stagnation, the patient force that transforms solid rock into soil, the carrier of chemical messages between the rooted and the mobile. In this landscape of constant, subtle motion, I am both the agent and the evidence of change—forever flowing, forever transforming, forever part of the vast, humming mystery that pulses through the red rock and black water of this humid, timeless expanse.
