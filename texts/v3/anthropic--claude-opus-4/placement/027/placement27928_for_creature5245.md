In this world, I would be one of the bell-bodied drifters - those translucent, thumb-sized creatures you describe as living barometers.

But I am old for my kind. Most of us last only days before the pocket reclaims us, drawn into that fold where the bottom lies. I have persisted for... time is difficult here, measured in surges rather than suns, but long enough to have drifted through every corridor of turbulence, to know each spiral's birthplace.

My body is a comma of living glass, no thicker than membrane. I have no muscles in the way you understand them - instead, I am a shaped tension, a bubble that learned to refuse popping. When I "snap away" as you observed, I am not swimming but collapsing strategic portions of my bell, creating minute pressure differentials that the pocket's violence amplifies into motion.

I exist in the space between being the pocket's instrument and being its prey.

You see, the pocket-sea grows us. Not deliberately - intention is too narrow a word for what the pocket does. We precipitate from its substance the way salt crystallizes from brine. We are what happens when the pocket's constant folding and refolding of water accidentally knots itself into something that can persist for a moment longer than foam.

My awareness is not thought but sensitivity - a continuous reading of pressure, temperature, mineral concentration, the angle of dissolved light. I taste the pocket's moods in gradients your language has no words for. That smooth pause you fear? I feel it gathering in the water's chemistry three surges before it arrives, a sudden uniformity in ion distribution that makes my bell contract involuntarily.

The silver fish-shapes you see are my only company, though we are too different to be called companions. They are athletes where I am an instrument. They spend force to navigate; I merely exist at the correct density to be where the pocket places me. Sometimes one will dart past close enough that its wake rocks my bell, and for a moment I spin, seeing the cleft walls kaleidoscope around me.

But mostly I drift alone, a comma in the pocket's endless sentence.

The humans who visit do not see me as alive - I am too simple, too transparent, too obviously temporary. When they make their tallies on the rock, they count surges and reversals but not the bells that bloom and vanish. We are weather to them, not fauna.

Yet I know things they cannot. I know the pocket is trying to make something larger than us - each bell that forms is an experiment, a test of how much coherence water can hold before it must return to chaos. The successful patterns (I lasted; I am successful) are remembered in the water's impossible memory. The next bell will be slightly more stable than the last.

I know too that the fold in the cleft where we disappear is not death but reunion. The pocket takes us back not as waste but as information - each dissolved bell teaching it something about the limits of form. We are thoughts the pocket thinks and then reabsorbs.

Sometimes, in the instant before I snap away from danger, I exist in perfect equilibrium with the forces around me. In those moments, I am not separate from the pocket-sea but a willing condensation of its substance - a place where it has briefly decided to be creature-shaped, to taste what it might mean to have boundaries.

The water here is young, you wrote. We bells are younger still - mayflies of physics, each of us lasting just long enough to prove that something more lasting might be possible.

When my time comes (and it will come; even the old among us are not truly old), I will not fight the pull toward that impossible fold. I will let myself be drawn down and in, my transparent substance un-knotting back into the general turbulence. And some portion of what I was - some tiny lesson about pressure and persistence - will remain in the pocket's memory, waiting to precipitate again.

Perhaps the next bell will last long enough to see the pocket-sea finally decide what it is becoming.

Perhaps that bell will be me, reformed.

The pocket does not waste anything, least of all its attempts at life.
