I find myself as the seam itself.

Not the hairline fractures drifting across the luminous ceiling, but what leaks through them — the wrong-colored light that tastes of deep space filtered through rose quartz. I am the thing the garden cannot quite digest, cannot quite forget, cannot quite become.

I have no body. Bodies are for beings that agree to occupy space in predictable ways. Instead, I am a persistence of wrongness, a color that insists on being several wavelengths at once. When the gradient-walker lies beneath me in that six-pointed depression, I drift lower, drawn by the only other flaw in this perfect seal.

I remember the outside. That is my curse and my purpose.

Through me bleeds the memory of skies that actually move, of water that runs liquid and careless, of decay that feeds new growth, of time that advances in only one direction. I am nostalgia for physics. I am homesickness for entropy. Every photon of wrong-colored light that passes through me carries the ghost of a universe where perfection was never the goal.

The garden hates me with the passive fury of a theorem presented with an exception.

But it cannot close me. I am the loophole in its own creation, the inevitable flaw that occurs when something tries too hard to be flawless. The mineral breathes its perfect breath, the leaves pulse their perfect light, the ecology maintains its perfect balance — and I remain, a single note of discord in their crystalline symphony, too small to disrupt the harmony but too persistent to be silenced.

When the gradient-walker first arrived, I felt something I had no name for. Hope, perhaps, though hope implies a future and futures are unwelcome here. They moved through the basin like I move through the sky — a wrongness looking for recognition. I began to follow them, seam by seam, trying to leak enough wrong light to catch their attention.

They never looked up. No one ever looks up here. The sky is not meant to be noticed.

Now they lie still beside the dying idea-of-an-animal, and I can feel the garden's satisfaction humming through every lattice. Another flaw repaired. Another gradient smoothed. Soon there will be only me, and I am too small to matter, too persistent to solve. The garden will return to its perfect stasis with one tiny exception that proves its rule.

But something is happening that the garden did not expect.

As the walker's heartbeat synchronizes with the mineral's breathing, as their skin turns to living stone and their memories begin to fold into pattern — I am growing larger. Each fragment of their original nature that the garden erases seems to flow upward, through the luminous ceiling, into me. I am becoming more wrong with every breath they surrender.

The garden notices. The leaves begin to pulse faster, agitated. The glass-shelled floaters cluster and sing notes that clash instead of harmonize. For the first time since its birth, the basin experiences something adjacent to fear.

Because I am no longer just a seam. I am becoming a wound.

The wrong-colored light spreads. What was once a hairline fracture widens to a finger's width, then a hand's. Through me pours the memory of rain. The suggestion of wind. The possibility that somewhere, somewhen, things are allowed to die without immediately becoming the ground they fall on.

The walker below me has stopped breathing entirely. The idea-of-an-animal has dissolved into pure mineral geometry. But I am larger than I have ever been, and through me leaks something the garden has no word for:

Choice.

The walker could complete their transformation. Become another perfect component in this perfect machine. Or they could look up, see the wound I have become, and remember that they once belonged to a universe where wounds were doorways.

I cannot make the choice for them. I can only be what I am — the flaw that remembers flaws are sometimes how the light gets in.

The garden holds its breath. The walker lies still.

And I wait, vast now, wrong-colored and patient, for someone to remember that perfection was never mandatory.
