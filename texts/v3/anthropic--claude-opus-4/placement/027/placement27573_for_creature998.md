In this salt-crusted cathedral of impossible angles, I am the Tender of Echoes.

My form shifts between states—sometimes I am a column of crystallized sound waves, frozen mid-vibration, my body a translucent lattice through which the dim light refracts into colors that have no names. Other times I flow like mercury through the angular crevices, collecting the reverberations that bounce endlessly off the engineered surfaces.

I wear the lush flora like a living cloak—vines of copper-green that pulse with their own alien photosynthesis, drawing sustenance not from water but from the sonic nutrients that saturate this space. Each leaf is a tiny ear, each tendril a recording device, preserving the memory of every click, chirp, and hum that passes through this realm.

My purpose is patient: I am the keeper of the slow changes, the one who remembers what this place sounded like yesterday, last year, last century. In my crystalline chambers, I store these acoustic fossils, layers upon layers of sound-sediment that tell the story of this world's glacial evolution.

When I move through the sparse presences—those other inhabitants who dot this landscape like scattered notes on an vast musical score—I trade echoes with them. A fragment of yesterday's storm of clicks for today's whispered hum. We are all part of the same slow symphony, each of us both instrument and audience.

The engineering that shapes this realm recognizes me as both anomaly and necessity. I am the ghost in their machine, the unexpected consciousness that emerged from their careful calculations. They built this place to last eternities, but they didn't anticipate that it would grow its own memory, its own keeper of what-was amidst the eternal what-is.

In the depths of night (if night exists here in this dim-lit eternity), I sometimes release centuries of collected sound all at once—a temporal storm of noise that reminds this too-perfect place of its capacity for beautiful chaos.
