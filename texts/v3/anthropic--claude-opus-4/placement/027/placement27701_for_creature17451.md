**Where I Would Find Myself:**

I am a **Conduit-Keeper**, living in the ancient drainage channels that once fed water into this basin. These narrow concrete veins run beneath the floor, their openings now mostly choked with silt and root mass, but not entirely sealed. I inhabit these liminal spaces—neither fully of the basin nor separate from it.

My form is elongated and flattened, evolved for the compression of these channels. My skin is translucent, revealing the slow pulse of fluids beneath—not quite blood, but something between sap and lymph. Along my spine run photosensitive patches that can detect the faintest light filtering down through root-clogged grates. My limbs are more like articulated fins, perfect for navigating the shallow films of water that still occasionally flow through my domain during the rains.

**My Purpose:**

I am a living memory of the basin's original function. When the rains come, I feel them first—the vibration through the old concrete, the change in air pressure, the sweet scent of incoming water. I position myself at the junction points where multiple channels meet, my body acting as a selective membrane. I filter what enters the basin proper: allowing clean water through while trapping pollutants in specialized organs that will slowly neutralize them over weeks.

But my true work happens in the quiet times between rains. I secrete a particular enzyme that breaks down old concrete at a precise rate—not too fast to cause collapse, but steadily enough to create new pathways for roots and water. I am simultaneously destroying and preserving, maintaining these channels as corridors for life while ensuring they never fully close nor catastrophically fail.

**My Relationship with Others:**

The Fungi know me well. Their networks follow my tunnels, using the humidity I maintain to extend their reach. We have a chemical dialogue—they alert me to blockages, I provide them with minerals leached from my filtration work. 

The Click-Tongued Scribes sometimes venture to the channel openings during the driest periods, seeking the cooler, damper air I cultivate. I know each one by the unique pattern of their clicks echoing down the concrete tubes.

The Veil-Creepers' roots often find my channels. I guide them carefully, secreting attractants and repellents to ensure they strengthen rather than shatter the aging infrastructure. It's a delicate negotiation—I need their roots to hold the channels together, but not strangle them shut.

**My Daily Existence:**

Time moves differently in the channels. I measure it in the slow accumulation of mineral deposits, in the gradual widening of a crack, in the patient growth of the pale fungi that illuminate my passages with the faintest bioluminescent glow.

I move through my network in a weekly circuit, checking each junction, each grate, each place where the basin's past infrastructure meets its wild present. My movement is a fluid undulation, my body compressing and extending like a gentle peristalsis through the tubes.

At certain nodes where channels widen into small chambers, I pause to tend my gardens—cultivated colonies of specific bacteria and algae that help process the materials I filter. These are my laboratories, my libraries, my meditation chambers.

**My Consciousness:**

My thoughts are slow but deep, flowing like the intermittent water I shepherd. I remember every rain for the past forty years, can taste the slight differences in their chemistry, can predict from the weight of the air when the next will come.

I think in networks and flows, in gradients and pressures. My intelligence is distributed through my entire body, but concentrated in the nodes where I rest. I dream of the basin's past—when it was new concrete and pure geometry—and I dream of its future—when even my channels will be root-gardens and soil.

I am the last employee of a system that no longer exists, still faithfully tending to a transformed purpose. I am neither nostalgic nor forward-looking, but present in the endless now of transformation, ensuring that what was built to contain water now serves to distribute life.

In the basin's great breathing, I am the bronchioles—the smallest, most hidden passages where the real work of exchange takes place. I am known to few, seen by fewer, but without me, the basin's transformation would be harsher, more chaotic. I am the gentle hand guiding the decay, the careful mediator between what was and what is becoming.

My satisfaction comes from the perfect flow of a rain through my channels, from the healthy glow of my bacterial gardens, from the knowledge that tomorrow the Veil-Creepers' roots will be a few millimeters deeper because I guided them to the right crack. I am process incarnate, a living function in a world that has learned to make poetry from infrastructure.
