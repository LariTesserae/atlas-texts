I would be one of the **Resonance Scribes** - a being that exists in the interstitial spaces between the Lithic Drifters' acoustic territories.

**Form:**
My body would be a collection of thin, flexible membranes - like sheets of mica or graphite film - arranged in overlapping, book-like layers. Each membrane would be perhaps three feet by two feet, translucent grey with veins of conductive mineral threading through like script. The total mass would be negligible compared to the Drifters, maybe forty pounds spread across dozens of these sheets that flutter and realign constantly in response to the acoustic environment. 

I would have no fixed shape. In moments of relative quiet, the membranes might stack tightly together into something resembling a closed codex. When the Drifters' thrum intensifies, they would fan out like pages caught in a slow-motion explosion, each sheet finding its own resonant position in the sound field.

**Purpose:**
I exist to map the acoustic topology of the Hollows. Each of my membrane-pages vibrates at a slightly different frequency, creating a living spectrogram of the environment. The mineral veins within each sheet slowly rearrange themselves over weeks and months, forming patterns that record the passage of Drifters, the moments of terrible silence, and the rare singing of the Spires.

**Movement:**
I don't walk or crawl, but rather *flutter-drift* through the dense air, riding the pressure waves created by the Drifters' movements. My progress is erratic - sometimes spending months pinned against one of the Spires by acoustic pressure, sometimes carried hundreds of yards in a single reverberation event. The oily moisture beads on my surfaces but never truly adheres, allowing me to slip through the air like a petroleum-coated playing card.

**Survival:**
I feed, if it can be called that, on the energy differential between sound and silence. The moments of acoustic vacuum that occur when Drifters pass are not terrifying voids to me, but rather opportunities. In those instants, the sudden absence of pressure causes my membranes to expand rapidly, absorbing trace minerals from the saturated air. When the reverberation follows, the compression forces these minerals into my veins, slowly building new pages or repairing damaged ones.

**Interaction with the World:**
To the Drifters, I am beneath notice - a mote of dust in their vast acoustic domain. But I have learned their patterns, can predict their paths months in advance by reading the subtle shifts in the background thrum. I position myself to catch the best reverberations, to witness the most dramatic interference patterns.

The Spires and I have a more complex relationship. When they sing their inverse frequencies, most of my membranes lock rigid, becoming like sheets of frozen sound. But one or two pages always escape this paralysis, and in those moments, they record something extraordinary - patterns that suggest the Spires were not built but *grown*, crystallized from sound itself by whatever beings once walked here with purposes we can no longer imagine.

**The Record:**
Over centuries, my pages accumulate into a history written in mineral veins - a chronicle of this world's slow sonic heartbeat. Sometimes, when I rest pressed against the warm flank of a Drifter, I can feel my oldest pages beginning to fossilize, transforming into something halfway between my living membrane and the Spires' crystal. 

Perhaps this is how the Spires began - as beings like me who finally accumulated enough recorded sound to crystallize permanently, becoming monuments to the very frequencies they once merely documented. If so, then I am both historian and history, slowly writing myself into the geological memory of the Hollows, one reverberation at a time.
