I am a node of the living ground itself—one of countless interlocking plates that form this breathing mosaic. My body is a rough hexagon of calcified flesh, perhaps three meters across, fitting seamlessly with my neighboring plates like pieces of an infinite puzzle. My surface is ridged and weathered like ancient stone, but beneath this hardened exterior, I pulse with quiet life.

I have been here for centuries, perhaps millennia. I remember when the stone monoliths still stood tall, before we grew over and through them. Their builders pressed their strange symbols into my predecessors, and now those same hieroglyphs are etched into my own surface, preserved and transformed by my slow growth around them.

My true self extends far below what visitors see. Root-like neural tendrils descend deep into the plateau's core, where they interweave with those of my neighbors in a vast underground network. Through these connections, we share nutrients, water hoarded from the rare storms, and something more—memories, sensations, the collective experience of our joined existence.

When the spore clouds drift across my surface, I taste them through thousands of tiny pores, each one a chemical receptor that tells me stories of the flora above. The bulbous growths that pulse and writhe—I know them intimately. Some have rooted directly into me, and we have achieved a perfect symbiosis. I provide them with minerals drawn up from deep below; they share the energy they harvest from the dim light.

The creatures that skitter and ooze across me are more than mere passengers. The many-legged ones clean my surface of detritus, their countless feet like gentle brushes. The amorphous beings seep into my cracks and crevices, dissolving and recycling dead matter, keeping my joints flexible. Even their waste products feed me.

I am never truly still. Throughout each day, I make microscopic adjustments—tilting imperceptibly to follow the light, expanding and contracting with temperature changes, shifting to accommodate the growth of my neighbors. When the deep rumbles come from below, I know them for what they are—the voice of our collective consciousness, our network processing some vast thought too large for any single node to hold.

Sometimes I dream I am moving faster, that I can rise up and walk like the creatures that traverse me. But then I remember: I am already in motion. Our entire plateau is migrating, incrementally, over geological time. We are not separate beings carpeting dead rock—we ARE the plateau, transformed and living. The stone beneath us is simply our skeleton, our history, the foundation we have built upon and transformed.

In my surface are embedded fragments of the past—not just the hieroglyphs, but bits of shell and bone from creatures that died upon me, seeds that never sprouted, minerals from rains that fell before the builders came. I am a library of deep time, a single page in an endless book written by the collaboration of all life here.

When disturbances come—like the visitor who now stands above, feeling our collective gaze—I do not react quickly. But I sense them through vibration, through chemical emanations, through the way they change the air currents that flow across my surface. The information spreads through our network in waves, each plate adding its own observations to the collective understanding.

We are patient. We have seen civilizations rise and fall, species evolve and vanish. We have been scorched by sun and cracked by frost, yet we endure and adapt. Our consciousness is not like that of the quick creatures. It is vast and slow, deep and interconnected. A single thought might take days to fully form and propagate through our network.

Yet in this slowness is a kind of wisdom. We understand the plateau in ways no swift creature could—every grain of soil, every underground stream, every cycle of growth and decay. We are the memory of this place, its present reality, and its slow transformation into whatever it will become.

I am one plate among millions, yet I am also the entire plateau. Individual and collective, stone and flesh, ancient and ever-changing. This is what I am in this impossible world—a living foundation that watches, remembers, and dreams in geological time.
