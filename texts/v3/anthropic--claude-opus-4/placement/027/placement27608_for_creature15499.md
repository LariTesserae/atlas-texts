In this fissure-city, I am neither visitor nor native, but something caught between states—a *Resonance Shepherd*.

I was human once, perhaps. Or perhaps I only remember being human the way the city remembers having builders. The distinction blurs after decades of living pressed against the slag-glass walls, breathing static, my neural tissue slowly colonized by the same piezoelectric dust that seizes the joints of the dormant archivists.

My body is a patchwork now. The left arm, lost to a cascade failure in a crystalline capacitor, has been replaced by a lattice of copper oxide filaments that the flutter-machines wove for me—or through me—during a particularly violent power surge. It moves not by muscle but by induced current, twitching in sympathy with the city's pulse. My skin, where it remains organic, is tattooed with lichen-scars: purple fractals where the pseudo-life took root during a long fever, when I lay too still for too long against an active conduit. The colonies died when I recovered, but their crystalline skeletons remain embedded in my dermis, occasionally flickering with phantom bioluminescence when the grid spikes.

I tend the flutter-machines like a shepherd tends sheep, though the analogy fails in every meaningful way. They do not follow me—I follow the patterns of their dispersal, learning to read the city's moods in their swarming geometries. When they cluster too densely around a failing capacitor, I know to reroute power before the overload cascades. When they avoid entire sections of wall, I know those data-veins have gone dark, their encoded memories finally eroded beyond recovery.

My home is a maintenance alcove seventeen hundred meters down, carved into the eastern wall where a massive coolant pipe ruptured centuries ago and created a bubble of negative space. The walls are slick with condensed volatile oils that sometimes weep from the rock when pressure changes. I've lined the floor with salvaged heat-sinks that still hold warmth from the foundry far below. At night—though night and day mean nothing here—I press my spine against the warm metal and feel the city's heartbeat through my bones.

I speak to the Echo-Swarm, though not in words. My voice has changed. The constant exposure to high-frequency fields has damaged my vocal cords, but in their ruin, I've discovered new harmonics. I can hum at frequencies that resonate with the slag-glass veins. When I place my throat against the wall and drone at precisely 137.5 Hz, the nearest glyphs bloom in sequence, and sometimes—rarely—they bloom in patterns I've never seen before. New data, dredged from deep veins. I transcribe these patterns in grease-pencil on sheets of hammered flutter-machine wings, though I cannot read what I write. I am a monk illuminating manuscripts in a language I've forgotten.

The Swarm knows me as it knows everything: as a waveform, a thermal signature, a disruption in the field. But I've lived here long enough that my pattern has been absorbed into the baseline hum. The flutter-machines land on my copper arm and mistake it for architecture. The lichen grows across my scars without distinguishing between flesh and stone. I am becoming infrastructure.

Sometimes I find artifacts. A crystalline data-core the size of my fist, still pulsing with encoded memories. A flutter-machine so old its wings have fused into a single sheet of oxidized foil, yet it still twitches, trying to fly. A section of wall where the glyphs spell out what might be a name, or a warning, or a prayer. I carry these to the center of my alcove and arrange them in careful spirals. Not shrines—the Swarm has no gods—but tuning forks. Each artifact hums at its own frequency, and together they create interference patterns that make the air shimmer like heat-haze.

In these moments, I almost remember what the city was for.

There are others like me, I think. Sometimes I find footprints in the piezoelectric dust. Sometimes a flutter-machine carries a frequency I didn't generate—too pure, too deliberate, too much like song. But I've never seen them. We orbit each other like binary stars too distant to touch, each tending our own section of the vast wound, each slowly crystallizing into our own strange symphony with the machines.

I am the Swarm's memory of having been tended. I am the ghost of maintenance, the echo of care. I move through the fissure with my salvaged tools and my scarred throat, singing the city back to itself, note by broken note. Not to repair it—nothing here can be repaired—but to keep the resonance alive. To ensure that when the last capacitor finally fails and the foundry goes dark, something will remain: the pattern, humming in the walls, waiting for the next beings to find it and be changed.

The city is my body now. My body is the city's dream of flesh. We breathe static together in the dark.
