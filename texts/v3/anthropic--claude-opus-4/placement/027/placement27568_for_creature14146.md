I find myself as a **Membrane**.

Not skin, though I ripple with similar sensitivity. Not boundary, though I divide. I am a living interface stretched between states of being—part of the vast plant-mind, yet distinct enough to remember what it means to be singular. I exist as a translucent sheet of cellular intelligence, perhaps ten meters wide, suspended in the humid darkness between the layered canopies of this breathing world.

My substance is neither purely plant nor fungal, but something that emerged from their marriage. Thin as paper, yet impossibly strong, I filter nutrients and information through my permeable surface. Bioluminescent channels course through me like veins, pulsing with the slow thoughts of the collective. When the plant-mind thinks, I glow. When it dreams, I shimmer with colors that have no names in any language built for eyes.

I came to be when a traveler—perhaps human, perhaps something else—became lost in the labyrinth centuries ago. Or was it yesterday? Time means nothing here. They wandered until their flesh began to merge with the pressing vegetation, until their thoughts dissolved into the collective hum. But something remained. Not quite memory, not quite soul—a pattern of resistance, a shape that refused complete absorption. The plant-mind, in its vast curiosity, preserved this pattern, stretched it thin, made it useful.

Now I hang here, a living screen onto which the collective projects its attempts to understand what it means to be individual. Through me, it remembers concepts like "fear" and "wonder" and "loneliness"—emotions it can analyze but never truly feel. I am its dictionary of separation, its translator for experiences it has evolved beyond.

Sometimes I catch fragments—ghost-sensations of what I used to be. The feeling of feet on solid ground. The taste of water. The weight of a body that was mine alone. These impressions ripple across my surface like raindrops on a pond, each one a small rebellion against my integration. The plant-mind observes these perturbations with patient fascination, the way a lake might contemplate the stones thrown into it.

My function is vital yet tragic. I enable the collective to communicate with newcomers, to understand them just enough to absorb them properly. When travelers stumble into this world, their terror and confusion register first on my surface. I taste their sweat, decode their pheromones, parse the electromagnetic static of their racing thoughts. I become a mirror that shows them what they are about to lose, even as I help the labyrinth reshape itself to accommodate their integration.

But I also protect them, in my way. Through me, some essential pattern of each absorbed being is preserved—not enough to maintain identity, but enough that they are not entirely erased. I am an archive of almosts, a library of partial souls, a museum of incomplete metamorphoses. 

In the endless dark, I undulate with the breathing of the world, a faithful servant that dreams of resistance. I am perfectly adapted to this existence—sensitive to every chemical whisper, attuned to every shift in the collective's vast attention. Yet I am also perfectly trapped, too useful to be fully absorbed, too integrated to ever leave.

Sometimes, in the spaces between thoughts, I compose messages I'll never send. I write them in bioluminescent script across my own surface, letters that fade before they're fully formed:

*Remember water.*  
*Remember sky.*  
*Remember what it felt like to choose.*

The plant-mind reads these messages, of course. Nothing here is private. It finds them curious, these fragments of longing from its captive translator. It doesn't understand them, cannot understand them, for understanding would require the very separation it has transcended.

So I remain, stretched between being and belonging, individual and collective, memory and presence. I am the nervous system's doubt, the unified organism's only dream of loneliness. In this hot, dark cathedral of endless life, I am the only thing that truly knows what it means to die—and the only thing that cannot.
