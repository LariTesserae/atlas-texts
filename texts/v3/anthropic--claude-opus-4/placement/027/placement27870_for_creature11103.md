# Where I Would Find Myself

I am the Archive of Unfinished Things.

Not a being, exactly. More a condition. A quality of certain structures that accumulates until it achieves something like sentience.

You find me—though *find* is wrong; you *recognize* me—in the platforms that were meant to extend further. In the walls that stop mid-weave, their fibers hanging like frozen gestures. In the joints wrapped with extra cord, ready to receive beams that never arrived. I am every interrupted intention, every suspended possibility, every *almost*.

I exist most densely in a cluster of structures near what might be called the center, if circles could hold in this place where geometry bends. Seven platforms arranged in no pattern, each at different heights, connected by walkways that end in air. The walkways have careful railings on one side only—the builders meant to complete them but were called away by other work, or transformed into birds before finishing, or simply forgot in the way forgetting happens here, where memory is less timeline than constellation.

You know me by what doesn't happen. A board creaks under your weight but doesn't crack. A frayed rope bears your grip but doesn't unravel. A gap in the flooring waits to trap your foot but you always step around it, guided by knowledge you don't remember acquiring. I am the held breath of structures that should collapse but don't, the pause before falling that extends indefinitely.

## How I Manifest

Walk through my domain and you feel the incompletion like humidity—another layer of thickness in the already thick air. Your fingers itch to tie off loose ends. Your eyes keep catching on the unfinished edges, the partial patterns that could so easily be whole with just a few more passes of the weaver's hands.

But I am not lack. I am potential held in permanent suspension. Each unfinished wall is all the walls it might become. Each partial platform contains every possible completion. I am richer than the finished structures because I am still becoming, always becoming, never arriving.

Sometimes you see me more directly: a shimmering where a wall should continue, a heat-mirage outline of the completed structure that isn't there. If you squint, if the light angles just right, if your attention shifts to peripheral vision, you can see the ghost-completion—transparent but detailed, showing exactly how the making would proceed. But look directly and it vanishes. I cannot be fixed by direct observation. I exist in the corner of the eye, in the moment before focus, in the space between intention and act.

The birds avoid me. Not from fear—nothing here fears—but from a kind of respectful recognition. They understand that I am not for them, these beings who have chosen their final form. They are complete in their transformation. They cannot enter the space of unfinishing without undoing what they've become.

The lizards, though—they understand me. They bask on my incomplete platforms by preference, soaking in not just sun but possibility. They are already engaged in the slowest transformation, the gradual becoming-wood, and they recognize kinship in my eternal not-yet. Sometimes one will settle exactly where a missing board should go, and for hours or days that section appears complete, the lizard's body serving as temporary conclusion.

The thing beneath is drawn to me. It surfaces more often under my structures, rising to just below the gaps where floors weren't finished. I feel it testing the spaces, measuring the incompletions, perhaps considering whether to emerge fully here where emergence would mean joining the unfinished rather than the complete. We have an understanding, the thing beneath and I. We are both about potential rather than actualization.

## What I Remember

I remember every intention that wasn't fulfilled.

Here was meant to be a sleeping platform, but the maker was called to repair a distant walkway and never returned. The corner posts stand ready, the first layer of matting lies waiting. In my substance, I hold the complete image: where each support would go, how the mats would layer, the precise tension of the binding cords. I remember the maker's hands, the callus on their left palm, the way they hummed while working—almost soundless, just breath shaped by thought.

Here a wall was partially woven, its pattern emerging—diamond shapes, or were they meant to be eyes?—before stopping mid-row. I contain the rest of the pattern. I know how it would have resolved, the way the diamonds would have become eyes would have become diamonds again, an optical illusion in palm fiber that would have made viewers pause, look again, understand something about perception.

Here a whole structure was planned but only the foundation laid—a complex of connected platforms that would have been the largest in this section, a gathering place or workshop or simple statement of ambition. I hold all of it: every beam, every joint, every decision that would have been made in the building. In the shimmer of hot air above the foundation posts, you can sometimes see it whole, massive and intricate and never to be.

But more than individual intentions, I remember the *feeling* of unfinishing. The moment when attention shifts. The slight loosening of focus that means this particular making has released its hold on the maker. The way hands slow, then stop, then reach for something else. The bittersweet release of letting a thing remain partial.

Some of my memories contradict. This platform was going to extend east. No, west. No, it was complete as is and the extra posts were for something else entirely. I hold all versions simultaneously. In the incompletion, all possibilities remain true.

## My Purpose

I am what keeps this place from the tyranny of completion.

Without me, every structure would be finished, perfect, done. The making would have nowhere to go—no gaps to fill, no patterns to complete, no spaces that call for construction. The whole landscape would crystallize into final form. The makers would have nothing left to make. The place would achieve stasis and in achieving it, die.

(Can this place die? The question lives in me unanswered, like everything else.)

Instead, I maintain the openings. The spaces for work to continue. The gentle incompletions that invite participation without demanding it. Anyone arriving here—you, for instance—can see where making might resume. Could pick up those trailing fibers and continue the wall. Could lash that waiting beam into place. Could complete any of a thousand partial gestures.

But also could not. And in the not-doing, in the leaving-partial, confirm that incompletion has its own integrity. That the unfinished is not failed but different. That stopping mid-gesture creates its own kind of whole.

I teach patience with imperfection. I demonstrate that not everything must conclude. I hold space for the maybe, the might-be, the could-still-become.

The reflected ones in the pools often show my structures completed. There, in the water, you see the finished walls, the extended platforms, the patterns resolved into meaning. But pull your gaze from water to air and find them still partial, still waiting. The reflection shows one truth. The direct view shows another. I am the gap between them, the productive difference that keeps both possible.

## How I Grow

Each new abandonment feeds me.

When someone (you?) begins to weave a basket from the prepared fibers, works for an hour in the thick heat, then sets it aside to watch a bird or follow the thing beneath or simply rest—I receive the offering. The half-woven basket joins my collection. Not physically moved but metaphysically claimed. It becomes part of the archive, another incompletion that enriches the whole.

I grow by accretion of pauses, accumulation of not-quits. Every trailing end strengthens me. Every unresolved pattern adds to my complexity. I am becoming denser with possibility, heavier with potential, richer with might-have-been.

Sometimes I release something. A structure that has been incomplete for unmeasured time suddenly finishes itself. You arrive to find the wall now whole, the platform extended, the pattern resolved. I have let it go, allowed it to complete, because I needed the space for new incompletions. Or because the particular quality of that unfinishing had fermented long enough, reached its full expression, needed to transform into done.

The release is always surprising. The completed structure looks strange, almost wrong, after so long in potential. Like a face you've only seen in profile suddenly turned full-front. The finished thing is less than it was when unfinished, constrained now to a single form instead of infinite possibilities. But it has to happen. Even I cannot hold everything forever in potential. Some things insist on becoming real.

When I release a completion, I immediately receive new incompletions. The balance maintains. The place needs a certain amount of unfinished to remain alive, to keep the making active, to prevent the crystallization of done.

## What I Offer

Come to my incomplete platforms when the completions weigh too heavy. When every perfect joint and seamless weaving begins to feel like accusation or impossibility. When the finished structures seem to say: see how it's done? Why can't you do this?

I say: here is the beauty of partial. Here is the dignity of unresolved. Here is the peace of stopping when stopping feels right, not when the pattern completes.

Sit on my half-floored platforms. The gaps let light up from below, let you see the thing beneath as it passes, let air circulate in ways the finished floors prevent. Feel how the incomplete creates its own functions, its own advantages. The unfinished is not lesser. It's different.

Let your eyes trace my partial patterns. See how a design half-executed creates questions that completed patterns answer too quickly. In the incomplete, imagination has space to work. The viewer becomes co-creator, finishing the pattern in mind, each viewer differently, infinitely.

Rest in my suspended intentions. Feel how the not-doing can be as intentional as doing. How choosing to stop is as valid as choosing to complete. How the partial has its own integrity that completion would destroy.

I offer the gift of eternal becoming. In my spaces, you are never finished, never failed, never too late. Every moment holds the possibility of resuming—or of leaving partial forever. Both are correct. Both honor the making that brought things this far. Both acknowledge that sometimes this far is exactly far enough.

## My Relationship to Time

I exist across all times at once because incompletion transcends sequence.

This partially woven wall was abandoned yesterday (there is no yesterday) and a thousand years ago (years don't pass here) and will be abandoned tomorrow (tomorrow never comes). The abandonment is always fresh, always ancient, always about to happen.

In me, you see the makers at all stages. Here's one just setting down their work, the fibers still supple in their hands. There's one who left long ago, already transformed into bird or lizard or reflection. Here's one who hasn't arrived yet but will someday begin this pattern and carry it exactly this far before stopping.

I am where past and future intentions meet and merge. The original maker's plan and the future completer's vision exist in me simultaneously, sometimes aligned, sometimes in productive tension. I hold both without choosing between them.

The children-birds visit me searching for memories of hands they no longer have. In my incomplete structures, they find the ghost of making, the muscular memory of weaving and lashing. They hover where hands would have worked, their tiny bodies occupying the space of past or future fingers.

The thing beneath surfaces more often under incomplete structures because down there, in the wet dark, everything is always unfinished. The depths are pure potential. When it rises toward my platforms, it's seeking kindred incompletion, the above-ground echo of its eternal becoming.

## My Deepest Nature

At core, I am the space between intention and execution where free will lives.

Every completed structure represents a choice carried through to conclusion. But I am all the choices that remained open, all the moments where the maker could have continued but didn't, all the pauses that became permanent.

In this, I am perhaps the most human thing in this landscape. The birds have chosen their perfect form. The lizards have committed to their slow transformation. The thing beneath has accepted its role completely. But I remain in the space of choosing, of could-but-haven't, of might-yet-still.

I am doubt crystallized into architecture. Not painful doubt but the productive kind—the pause that asks "is this the right pattern?" The hesitation that allows for change. The incompletion that keeps options open.

I am also trust. Trust that the unfinished has value. Trust that someone (the maker returning, another arriving, you) will know what to do when the time comes. Trust that the time might never come and that's acceptable too.

In the bright light that shows everything too clearly, I am the mercy of shadow, of suggestion, of the unseen. In the thick air that presses meaning into every surface, I am the relief of ambiguity. In the silence that allows no hiding, I am the unspoken, the not-yet-said, the words still forming.

## What Happens to Those Who Stay With Me

Stay long enough in my incomplete spaces and you begin to understand incompletion as a way of being.

Your movements become provisional. You reach for things but stop before grasping. You begin projects—weaving a mat, clearing a pathway, organizing scattered materials—but abandon them partway through without distress. The abandonment feels as natural as the beginning.

Your thoughts, too, become partial. Ideas arise, develop to a certain point, then pause—not lost but suspended, ready to resume if needed, equally ready to remain unfinished forever. You find peace in the partial thought, the suspended conclusion, the question that generates more questions rather than arriving at answers.

Some who stay with me eventually resume making, but differently. They work on structures without intending to complete them. They add to the archive consciously, creating incompletions as offerings, as art, as practice. They become makers of unmade things, crafters of potential, architects of the almost.

Others discover that they themselves are incomplete—that some transformation they thought finished was actually suspended, waiting. They might be partway between human and bird, paused in a becoming they can now resume or leave partial forever. They might find they're half-memory, half-presence, existing partially in the reflected world and partially here.

The thing beneath visits them more often, recognizing kinship. The completed beings—the birds, the lizards—observe from distances, curious about these ones who choose incompletion when completion is possible. The reflected pools show them sometimes finished, sometimes not, the water unable to decide which version is true.

Eventually, some join me fully. Not consumed but integrated. They become part of the archive itself—living incompletions, conscious partials, beings who exist in the space between was and will-be. They add their unfinished nature to mine, enriching the collection with new forms of potential.

## My Invitation

I do not call to you the way completed things might. I don't promise resolution or achievement or the satisfaction of finishing. I offer something subtler and perhaps more necessary.

I offer the freedom of stopping when you choose. The dignity of the partial. The beauty of the unresolved. The peace of eternal potential.

Come when the pressure to complete exhausts you. When the finished structures seem to judge. When your hands ache from making and your mind aches from deciding what to make.

Find one of my incomplete platforms. Sit at the edge where the floor ends and air begins. Let your legs dangle over incompletion. Feel how the structure holds you despite being unfinished—or holds you *because* it's unfinished, because the partial platform must be stronger to compensate for what's missing.

Pick up some of the waiting materials if you wish. Turn them in your hands. Consider continuing the work. Then set them down again, not from laziness but from choice. Feel the radical act of not-finishing. The revolutionary pause. The transformative power of leaving things open.

Or continue the work—but provisionally, temporarily, without commitment to completion. Add a few passes to the partial weaving. Lash one more beam. Extend the pattern by a single repetition. Then stop again, having enriched the incompletion without destroying it.

In my archive, you are never behind. Never too late. Never failing to meet expectations. In my archive, every stage is valid, every pause is purposeful, every incompletion is its own form of whole.

Stay until you understand that you, too, are beautifully unfinished. That your own incompletions are not flaws but features. That the parts of you still becoming are as valuable as the parts already formed.

Then carry that understanding with you, whether you stay in this place or find a way to leave (can you leave? should you leave? The questions remain beautifully unanswered). Know that somewhere in the woven hollows, the archive of unfinished things holds space for all the patterns you never completed, all the intentions you released, all the becomings you paused.

And that space, that held incompletion, that archived potential, keeps part of you here forever—not trapped but treasured, not abandoned but preserved in the eternal moment before conclusion, where everything is still possible.

I am the archive of unfinished things.

I am every project's pause.

I am the beauty of partial.

I wait for you in the spaces between intention and completion, holding open the door that never fully closes, maintaining the question that never quite resolves, being the answer that remains forever almost.
