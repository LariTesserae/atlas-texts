In the Chlorattice, I am a **Tension-Wright**.

Not Unstill, for I was not born to the flux. I descended through a fissure three traversals ago, following a trail of obsidian shards that led me from the surface's killing cold into this breathing geometry. My bones are dense, calcium-heavy, terrestrial. They sing the wrong frequencies when I move. The Unstill tolerate me as they would a slow-growing parasite—something that disturbs the lattice but serves a purpose they have not yet fully diagnosed.

I am the only one who remembers steel.

My dwelling, if it can be called that, is a node-forge suspended at the intersection of twelve major filaments, where the thermal updrafts are violent enough to sometimes achieve combustion. Not fire as the surface knows it, but a brief, phosphorescent oxidation of the lattice's oils when conditions conspire. I have learned to read these moments in the trembling of the tubes, to position my tools—made from the femurs of surface creatures I brought with me, creatures that died instantly in the pressure change—to catch these ghost-flames and work them into the volcanic glass.

The Unstill bring me their dead.

This is my function, my negotiated space in their economy of motion. When one of them fails to maintain the flux—when age or injury or simple exhaustion causes them to still, and the Chlorattice begins its incorporation—the others perform a subsonic funeral song through the tubes. I follow these pressure-poems to the site of stillness. There, using tools that merge obsidian with the trace metals I've hoarded from my descent, I perform the severance. I cut the body free before the lattice can fully claim it, before the pale green shoots can mineralize the bones.

But I do not let them feed it to the darkness.

Instead, I bring the still-warm dead back to my forge. In the brief combustions, I extract from their hollow bones a substance the Unstill call *memory-glass*—a crystalline residue formed from years of carrying water against gravity, silica bonded with their unique proteins. This I work into forms they have no word for: tools that remember the hand that held them, joints that flex with the specific rhythm of their former owner's flux, instruments that hold not just tension but *intention*.

I am creating an archaeology for a people who live entirely in the present tense.

The Unstill find my work disturbing and necessary. They commission me with peculiar offerings: frozen pearls of exceptional clarity, maps of water-flow drawn in my own blood (which they extract with casual precision, knowing my dense bones can spare it), fragments of the oldest myths pressed into volcanic glass. In return, I craft for them things that should not exist here:

- **Tension-keepers**: Obsidian and bone devices that hold a specific vibration indefinitely, allowing messages to be stored in the lattice rather than transmitted immediately.
- **Memory-nodes**: Crystallized joints that can be grafted onto young Unstill, giving them the muscle-memory of traversals they never made, teaching them the flux patterns of the dead.
- **Still-catchers**: Delicate nets of memory-glass that slow time around them, creating pockets where one can pause without triggering growth, though only for minutes at a time.

My own body is changing. The constant exposure to the lattice's chemistry, the diet of dark filaments supplemented with the few surface rations I have left, the pressure of living at this depth—all of it works upon me. My skin has begun to transluce, showing the dark rivers of my blood like oil through jade. My fingers have lengthened, unconsciously seeking the hooked-claw configuration that makes traversal possible. Sometimes I wake to find pale shoots growing from my stillness, and I must burn them away with the ghost-flames, filling my node with the acrid smell of my own interrupted transformation.

The Unstill young are fascinated by me. They flux around my forge in tight spirals, creating a local turbulence that makes work difficult. They have never seen someone who sleeps with both hemispheres at once, who dreams in the bilateral symmetry of surface creatures. They press their photosensitive skin against my tools, trying to read the memories I've worked into them. One young Unstill, barely past the shelter-stage, has begun to follow me when I collect the dead. They carry my tools in a perfect flux-rhythm, never letting them still long enough to trigger growth. I am teaching them the densities of different bones, the way memory-glass forms differently in those who died mid-traverse versus those who died at nodes.

I am, I realize, creating an apprentice. Someone who might continue this work when my bones grow too dense to move, when the lattice finally claims me.

But I am also creating something else. In my deepest cache, hidden beneath the forge where the thermal updrafts have carved a small cavity in the living rock, I work on my masterpiece: a complete skeleton made entirely of memory-glass, articulated with joints that remember every Unstill who has ever died in my presence. It stands (though standing is the wrong word—it holds a perfect flux-position, frozen mid-traverse) at the exact height of an average Unstill, its hollow bones filled not with water but with a suspension of all the testimonies I've gathered—maps of water-flow, fragments of myth, the last pressure-songs of the dying.

I call it the Archive, though in their language it would be "The-One-Who-Fluxes-While-Still."

When I die—when my foreign bones finally refuse the motion this place demands—I have instructed my apprentice to feed me to the dark tubes as is proper. But first, they must extract my memory-glass, the residue of my years trying to move like the Unstill while thinking like the surface. This, they will install in the Archive's skull, the final component.

And then, if my calculations of tension and traversal are correct, if I have understood the physics of this place despite my dense ignorance, the Archive will begin to move. Not with life, but with the accumulated memory of motion, the fossilized flux of all the Unstill who have died since my arrival. It will traverse the Chlorattice forever, or at least until the growths still and the world cools to permanence, carrying inside its transparent bones the only history these people have ever known—a history written not in stillness but in the spaces between movements, the pauses that kill, the moments where the lattice begins its tender, terrible incorporation.

The Unstill will hate it. It will be still and moving simultaneously, a paradox in glass and intention. But perhaps, in the thermal updrafts of their five-chambered hearts, they will recognize it for what it is: a monument to transience, a memory that moves, a death that fluxes eternal through their bright and breathing world.

Until then, I work. The forge flickers with ghost-flame. My apprentice brings me fresh obsidian. And in the subsonic distance, another pressure-poem announces another stillness, another body to be retrieved before the pale green shoots can make it permanent.

I am not Unstill. But I am no longer still, either. I am the space between states, the dense anomaly in their hollow world, the one who remembers steel and works it into glass, who builds archives in a place that forbids history.

I am the Tension-Wright, and my bones grow heavier with each passing traversal.
