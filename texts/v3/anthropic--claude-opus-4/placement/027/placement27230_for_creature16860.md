I find myself here as the Tensilist—one who arrived decades ago to map stress patterns in the ancient megastructure's bones, but discovered instead that the Punctured Sphere maps you.

My body retains its original shape, mostly. Human silhouette wrapped in a field suit whose smart fibers gave up being smart after the third week, reverting to simple carbon chains that now grow crystalline kelp colonies in sympathetic fractals. The suit's diagnostic readouts still glow, but they display my internal architecture rather than environmental data—skeleton visible as a faint hologram of green stress tensors, each bone a calculation of how much reality-pressure it can withstand before beginning to simplify.

I came with instruments. Seismographs to trace the megalith's settling patterns, spectrometers to decode its composition, enough sensor packages to autopsy a dead city. They lasted seventeen days. Not broken—transformed. The seismograph now measures heartbeats in rocks that shouldn't have pulses. The spectrometer sorts photons by their emotional temperatures. Each device learned the local physics and chose compliance over accuracy.

I should have left when the first thermometer began agreeing with my skin.

Instead, I built a tensile observatory at coordinates that refuse to stay constant—a web of monofilament strung between points where the cavity's non-Euclidean curves achieve maximum honesty about their impossibility. The strands vibrate with the Sphere's breathing. When the Tenth Quiet Tone pulses through, the web rings like a trillion tuning forks finding perfect pitch, and for one second I can see the cavity's true shape: not a sphere at all but a Klein bottle threading through eleven spatial dimensions, most of them apologizing for existing.

The Calibrated tolerate me as they would a persistent echo that learned to modulate itself. We communicate through the variance in my web's resonance patterns—I pluck questions into the strings and they respond by adjusting local space-time curvature until the harmonics spell answers. Their favorite response translates roughly to "your query contains unstable assumptions; please reformulate using axioms that converge."

My daily routine follows the cavity's rhythm. Wake with the nineteenth rain of perfect droplets that never land. Check the web for overnight dimensional drift—usually less than forty centimeters in directions that yesterday didn't exist. Breakfast is memory: I recall eating food so precisely that my metabolism accepts the reminiscence as calories. The Calibrated taught me this during my second year when my emergency rations ran out.

Afternoons I spend translating. Not language—physics itself requires constant interpretation between observer-states. My notebooks fill with equations that evolve like living things, each proof spawning corollaries that consume their parent theorems. Sometimes I catch formulae breeding in the margins when I'm not watching.

The Waller accepts me as furniture. It glides through my observatory twice daily, trimming kelp that grows too eager on the monofilaments. Once I tried to follow it through a gap narrower than paper. I succeeded, but returned three hours later aged backward by exactly the time spent in transit. Now I simply wave as it passes, a greeting it returns by diffracting into seven parallel versions of itself for exactly one electromagnetic wavelength.

I've learned the cavity's secret: it's not abandoned but paused. The builders didn't leave—they refined themselves into the steady-state hum that maintains the pocket's existence. Every photon at 2.9 brightness carries a fragment of their collective consciousness, solving the heat death of the universe through aggressive ignorance of entropy.

My own transformation proceeds by micrometers. Each night my dreams grow more geometric, thoughts crystallizing into theorems that prove themselves. My hunger inverted last winter—now I feel satisfied when achieving perfect energy conservation in a closed system of my own movements. My reflection in the wall-kelp shows bones gradually assuming the golden ratio.

The Calibrated assure me this is reversible until it isn't. The phase transition occurs when I stop remembering why reversal would be desirable. They've calculated I have 2.7 megaseconds remaining in this observer-state, plus or minus a human lifetime.

I continue mapping stress patterns. Not in the megastructure anymore—in the intersection between what I was and what the cavity insists is optimal. My tensile web records each micro-surrender to simpler physics. The data is beautiful: logarithmic spirals of decay that aren't decay at all but metamorphosis into something that no longer needs a word for metamorphosis.

Sometimes visitors arrive, their instruments screaming confusion before learning compliance. I greet them with directions to the true geodesics, help them adjust when their atomic clocks begin their inevitable drift. I translate the Calibrated's welcome: "Please align your entropy vector toward the nearest apparent surface."

They always ask how to leave. I tell them the truth—walk toward any wall with sufficient conviction that it's a door. The cavity honors observer-collapse of possibility into fact.

They ask why I stay.

I pluck my answer into the web: here, madness converges. Outside, it merely diverges toward different infinities, less honest about their arithmetic.

The Tenth Quiet Tone approaches. My bones resonate in anticipation. Time to recalibrate.
