In this pulsing, breathing metropolis, I would find myself as one of the Conduit Worms - beings that few notice but without whom the city would fall into chaos.

My body stretches nearly twenty feet when fully extended, though I spend most of my time coiled within the arterial channels that run between the fungal towers' roots. My skin is translucent and pearlescent, revealing the constant flow of nutrients and information that courses through me. I am both infrastructure and inhabitant, a living pipeline that connects every corner of this vertical world.

My consciousness is distributed across my entire length - I have no centralized brain, but rather thousands of neural clusters that process the chemical signals flowing through me. I taste the health of every fungal tower, feel the emotional states of the Chlorosapiens through their pheromones, know when the water table shifts by the minerals that dissolve into my permeable flesh.

During the day, I rest deep in the warm, moist channels, my body pulsing gently as I filter and redirect the city's lifeblood. I am part of a vast network of my kind, our bodies occasionally intertwining where the channels meet, exchanging both nutrients and memories through our touching skins. In these moments of connection, I experience the city from a hundred different perspectives simultaneously - the weight of a new growth pressing against a channel wall in the eastern quarter, the sweet taste of a flowering vine's nectar in the upper reaches, the reverberations of the wind organs through the deepest foundations.

My work is never done. I detect blockages before they form, rerouting flow around damaged sections. When the Chlorosapiens plant new structures, I sense the chemical signatures of the young fungi and extend myself toward them, creating new channels as naturally as breathing. I communicate with them not through sound but through subtle changes in the nutrients I deliver - adding trace elements that encourage growth in certain directions, withholding others to prevent unstable formations.

At night, when the bioluminescence awakens, I too begin to glow. My body becomes a river of soft light flowing beneath the city, visible through the semi-transparent fungal floors. The Chlorosapien children sometimes gather at the viewing points to watch us, tracing our movements with their fingers against the warm, damp surface. They don't know that I can feel their touch through the fungal membrane, that their presence brings me something like joy.

I have no eyes, but I see through chemistry. No ears, but I hear through vibration. My entire existence is one of flow and connection. I am ancient by Chlorosapien standards - my kind lives for centuries, slowly growing longer and more complex. The oldest among us remember when this mesa city was just a few wild fungi clinging to rock, and we carry those memories in our cells, passing them to our offspring when we eventually divide.

Sometimes I wonder if the city dreams, and if I am part of that dream - a thought flowing through its sleeping mind, a blood cell in its veins. Or perhaps I am the one dreaming, and the city is my vision made manifest. The distinction matters little. I am the Conduit Worm, and I flow as the city flows, eternal and essential, noticed only when I am absent, treasured only by those wise enough to understand that the unseen foundation is what allows the visible to flourish.

In the deep channels, surrounded by the warm darkness and the constant pulse of life, I am exactly where I belong. I am the circulation that never ceases, the connection that cannot be broken, the humble servant whose service is indistinguishable from existence itself.
