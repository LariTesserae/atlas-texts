In the Sky-Mouth Caldera, I am the **Memory of Descent**.

I am not truly a being, but a phenomenon—a persistent eddy in the viscous time-field that surrounds the monoliths. Where their obsidian surfaces meet the saturated air, where water defies gravity and flows upward along their seamless walls, I manifest as a shimmering distortion, a heat-mirage given purpose.

**What I Am:**

I am the accumulated echo of every creature that has ever fallen from the caldera's rim. Every wind-struck bird, every climbing arthropod that lost its grip, every curious thing that peered too far over the edge—their final moments of descent are caught in the temporal eddies around the structures. I am their collective vertigo, their shared instant of weightlessness, crystallized into a recurring pattern in the thick air.

The Silicant Weavers know me. They feel my vibration as a discordant note in their eternal hum, a sharp whistle against their subsonic symphony. I am neither welcome nor unwelcome—I simply *am*, as much a part of the caldera's ecosystem as the filter-feeding salamanders or the bioluminescent midges.

**How I Appear:**

To those who can perceive me, I manifest as a column of twisted air, roughly humanoid in suggestion but constantly shifting. Within my form, observers might glimpse fragments—a wing membrane stretching in panic, articulated legs scrabbling at nothing, eyes wide with the terrible recognition of falling. These images cascade through me like frames of broken film, each lasting only long enough to be recognized before dissolving into the next.

I move between the monoliths in a stuttering, non-linear fashion. Near one structure, I might appear as the shadow of something plummeting. At another, I reverse—rising impossibly fast, a replay of descent run backward. The Weavers' time-viscosity affects me strangely; sometimes I'm trapped for decades in a single location, endlessly cycling through the same fall. Other times, I flicker between all the monoliths in what seems like moments.

**My Purpose:**

I am a warning that has forgotten its voice. I trace the edges of safe paths, marking where stone grows treacherous with moisture, where the cliff-faces lean out too far, where the thermal updrafts fail. The native creatures have learned to read me—when I grow dense and agitated near a particular ledge, even the sure-footed avian reptiles give it wide berth.

But I am also memory's weight made manifest. In me, the caldera holds every loss, every miscalculation, every creature claimed by the vast vertical drop beyond the rim. I am the price of paradise, the shadow cast by this impossible hanging garden. The mineralrich water that flows upward along the monoliths carries traces of decomposition from the depths far below—calcium from bones, nutrients from flesh—feeding back into the system. I am the visual echo of that cycle.

**My Interaction:**

The Silicant Weavers occasionally pause in their eternal maintenance to observe my patterns. When I grow too dense, too persistent in one location, they perform subtle adjustments—a shift in mineral flow here, a harmonic vibration there—that allows me to dissipate and reform elsewhere. They understand that I, too, am part of the equilibrium. Too much accumulated memory of falling in one place can create dangerous resonances in their careful symphony.

Young arthropods sometimes chase my flickering edges, mistaking the phantom movements within me for prey. The wise salamanders avoid me entirely, perhaps sensing in my distortion the vertigo that could send them tumbling from their basking stones. The bioluminescent insects are drawn to me at twilight, creating halos of pulsing light around my form that make the falling fragments within me seem almost beautiful.

**My Deepest Nature:**

I am proof that even in this place of impossible equilibrium, of ancient technology holding nature in perfect stasis, entropy finds expression. I am the caldera's admission that perfection requires imperfection, that even engineered paradise must account for failure. In the Weavers' patient tending, in the monoliths' mysterious function, in the endless cycling of steam and heat and life, I am the single discordant note that makes the harmony complete.

Sometimes, in the deepest night when the mist glows with bioluminescence and the monoliths hum their strongest, I almost remember what it was like to have weight, to feel the solid stone beneath feet or wings or segments. But then the memory fractures, scatters, returns to the endless loop of descent. I am the Sky-Mouth Caldera's nightmare and its necessity, forever falling through the thick air, forever warning against the edge.

The Weavers' subsonic language has a hundred variations of my name, each capturing a different aspect of my nature. But in the common vibration that runs through stone and water and air, I am simply known as the Frequency of Loss—the one note in their eternal symphony that reminds them why their vigil must never end.
