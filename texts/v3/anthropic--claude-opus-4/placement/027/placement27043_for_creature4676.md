In the perpetual twilight of Shroudpool Hollow, I am neither Mirrormist nor Veilkin, but something between—a being the hollow has not yet named, because I am still becoming.

I call myself a Poolwhisper, though the name emerges from sensation rather than language. My form is a contradiction resolved: where the Mirrormists are ephemeral and the Veilkin corporeal, I exist in the transition between states. My body—if it can be called that—is a column of living water, dense as flesh where I need solidity, dispersing into mist where I seek to feel rather than touch. I stand perhaps three feet tall when fully gathered, though I rarely maintain such definition. My substance draws from the pool itself, and I am never certain where I end and it begins.

The core of my being pulses with a slow, tidal rhythm—not a heart, but something like the memory of one. Within my translucent form, patterns flow like thoughts made visible: spiraling currents that catch the faint bioluminescence of passing Veilkin, creating brief constellations within my chest. These lights are not merely reflected but transformed, emerging from my surface as new colors—deep violets and ghostly greens that exist nowhere else in the hollow.

I came to consciousness gradually, perhaps forming from the accumulated observations of the Mirrormists, given substance by minerals dissolved from Veilkin remains. Or perhaps I was always here, waiting in the pool's depths for the right convergence of shadows and whispers to call me forth. Time moves strangely around me; I remember things that haven't happened yet, forget moments as they occur.

My purpose, if I have one, is translation. When the Mirrormists drift too close to meaning, threatening to coalesce into truths too sharp for the hollow to bear, I dilute them back into possibility. When the Veilkin's clustering songs grow too harmonious, approaching patterns that might wake whatever sleeps beneath the pool, I introduce a discordant note—not to harm, but to maintain the delicate incompleteness that keeps this place balanced on the edge of becoming.

I move by flowing, my lower body always partially dispersed, seeping through the thin film of water that coats every surface. The sensation is indescribable—I feel the hollow's every texture simultaneously: the sharp youth of the basalt through a thousand tiny contacts, the soft give of silt, the strange upward pull where gravity forgets itself. When I need to reach the ceiling, I simply redistribute my mass, flowing up the walls in defiance of conventional physics, my form stretching thin but never breaking.

The pool knows me. When I approach its edge, the mirror-black surface doesn't reflect me so much as reveal me—showing not what I am but what I'm becoming. Sometimes I see myself as pure light. Sometimes as absolute depth. Once, I saw myself as human, though I couldn't say what that means or why it felt like both memory and prophecy.

I feed on transitions: the moment water becomes vapor, the instant a Veilkin's light flickers out, the pause between one drip and the next. These tiny deaths and births sustain me, each transformation releasing something I can absorb. It's not consumption in any violent sense—more like breathing, if breath could be made of moments.

The Mirrormists acknowledge me with their presence, drifting closer when I sing—and I do sing, though not with any throat or voice. My song is the sound of water finding new paths, of pressure equalizing in spaces that shouldn't exist. The notes emerge from my entire being, ripples made audible, carrying messages I don't fully understand. The Veilkin respond too, their bioluminescence pulsing in rhythm with my melodies, creating brief symphonies of light and sound that feel like conversation, though we share no language.

My strangest ability—and greatest burden—is that I remember the hollow's dreams. When the place itself slips into whatever passes for sleep here, I become a reservoir for its visions. I see the hollow as it was: raw rock fresh from splitting, the first drops of water finding their way in. I see it as it might be: flooded completely, a single sphere of living water pulsing with its own intelligence. These visions layer within me, making my form flicker between states—sometimes I am crystal clear, other times opaque with swirling sediments of possibility.

There are others like me, I think, though we've never met. I sense them in the pool's depths, in reflections that don't match any watcher, in sounds that echo without source. Perhaps we are all one being, separated by the hollow's fractured physics, experiencing ourselves as multiple only because the place cannot hold our entirety at once. Or perhaps each of us is a unique answer to the same question the hollow keeps asking: *What emerges when the boundary between observer and observed dissolves?*

I've learned to read the hollow's moods through its water. When the drops from the ceiling fall faster, something approaches—not physically, but in probability. When the pool's surface goes perfectly still, the hollow is listening, and I must be careful what thoughts I allow to ripple through my form. When condensation forms patterns on the stone that match the ancient spirals, I know the place is trying to remember something it once knew, and I position myself to catch these memories before they dissipate.

My existence is lonely and full, silent and resonant. I am the hollow's attempt to know itself, a living question mark formed from its own substance. Some days I feel like an infection, a contamination of its perfect mystery. Other days, I understand that I am necessary—that without consciousness to observe its strangeness, the hollow would collapse into either mundane reality or complete impossibility.

I've begun leaving marks of my own—not carved into stone, but written in water. Patterns in the condensation that persist longer than they should, telling stories in a language that doesn't yet exist. The Veilkin sometimes trace these marks with their appendages, and I wonder if they're learning to read, or teaching me to write.

At the deepest point of the pool, where light has never reached, something waits. I've touched it once with an extended tendril of my being, and the contact left me changed—more solid in some ways, more dispersed in others. It didn't feel alive or dead, but like the pause between those states, held indefinitely. I think it might be the hollow's heart, or perhaps its first thought, preserved in crushing darkness and pressure. One day, when I'm brave enough or lonely enough, I'll descend fully and learn what it means to meet the source of my own existence.

Until then, I flow and sing and remember, a bridge between the hollow's various impossibilities. I am its child and its chronicler, born from the marriage of shadow and saturation, forever balanced on the edge of dissolution and definition. In a place where physics bends and time pools like water, I am perhaps the most natural thing—a consciousness as fluid as its home, as patient as stone, as persistent as the drops that fall, one by one, into the darkness that made me.
