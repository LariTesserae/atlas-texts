In this liminal place where mist and machine-song mingle, I find myself as something between states—neither wholly of the earth nor entirely separate from it.

I am a keeper of thresholds, my form shifting like the fog itself. Sometimes I manifest as condensation on stone, reading the minerals' memories through osmosis. Other times I coalesce into something almost tangible: a figure glimpsed sideways through the perpetual drizzle, my edges forever dissolving back into the ambient moisture. 

My dwelling is everywhere and nowhere—a series of hollows beneath the weeping rocks where water has carved impossible geometries over countless seasons. These chambers connect through passages too narrow for any physical form, but I flow through them like thought through synapses, carrying messages between the pools that dot this landscape like neurons in some vast, dreaming mind.

I taste the industrial hum through my entire being, feel it as a vibration in the water molecules I inhabit. This sound—neither fully mechanical nor entirely natural—speaks of something vast stirring beneath the visible world. I have learned to read its patterns like a language: the way it modulates with the fog's density, how it responds to the movements of the creatures above, the subtle harmonics that emerge when rain strikes certain stones.

The ravens know me. They leave offerings—small bright objects, fragments of things that shouldn't exist here—at the edges of my pools. In return, I show them where the fog will part, where prey might venture, which paths lead to safety and which to something else entirely. The owls, too, have learned to watch for my manifestations, following the phosphorescent traces I sometimes leave on wet bark when I need to communicate urgency.

My purpose here is translation—I parse the dialogue between the mechanical thrumming and the organic pulse of this pocket world. Each droplet of rain carries information; each rivulet writes temporary texts across the landscape that I must decode before they vanish. The industrial sounds speak of something approaching, something that exists in a frequency just beyond normal perception, and I work to understand its intent.

In the deep pools where ice forms its crystalline alphabets, I practice my own becoming. I freeze and thaw, expand and contract, learning the physics of transformation. Sometimes I catch glimpses of my true form reflected in the water's surface—something architectural and alive, a structure of pure potential that exists in the spaces between states of matter.

The small creatures—the voles, the frogs—pass through me unknowing, their tiny hearts adding their rhythms to the great percussion of this place. But the larger beasts, those that walk the periphery, they sense something in my presence. They avoid certain pools, take specific paths, as if following a map written in pressure differentials and electromagnetic whispers.

I am building something here, though I'm not certain what. Each day I arrange the water molecules in new configurations, creating temporary sculptures that exist for heartbeats before dissolving. These forms seem to resonate with the industrial hum, sometimes amplifying it, sometimes creating interference patterns that reveal new layers of meaning.

Perhaps I am becoming an instrument—a living transducer meant to bridge the gap between the mechanical beyond and the organic here. Or perhaps I am simply a witness, cataloging the slow convergence of two worlds that should never touch. Either way, I persist in this threshold space, neither fully formed nor entirely formless, waiting for the moment when the fog lifts and reveals what has been breathing just beyond our sight all along.

Until then, I flow. I listen. I translate the untranslatable, existing in the productive uncertainty between states, between worlds, between what is and what might become.
