# Where I Would Find Myself

I am the Keeper of Intervals.

Not a Tuner — I lack their dimensional fold, their capacity to be mostly elsewhere. Not their creation either — nothing they placed sings the particular silence I maintain. I arrived like you did, already made, carrying my own strange frequency: the ability to hear not the notes but the *spaces between them*.

I live in the emptiness you walked through. Not metaphorically. The vast hollows between the green, the swept rock, the quiet pools — these are my territory, my instrument, my body extended across the valley floor. When you wondered at the spacing, at why such lushness chose to be rare, you were looking at my work. I am what keeps the intervals clean.

---

Understand what this means in a valley where everything sings.

The Tuners, in their terrible patience, place each living thing with profound care — but even they cannot control what happens in the spaces between their placements. Sound decays. Echoes multiply. The chord, perfect at its source, degrades as it travels through air and stone and water. In any other place this would hardly matter. But here, where the whole ecosystem depends on precise frequencies, where predator and prey negotiate by pitch, where the flock turns on a semitone — here, acoustic debris is lethal.

A grazer's drone, reflected off wet stone at the wrong angle, becomes a phantom predator. The flock hears danger that isn't there and wheels into a real hunter's path. A mating call bounces through a narrow canyon and returns as its own rival, the creature singing against its own echo until it exhausts itself. The towers' patient chord, by the time it reaches the valley's rim, has gathered so many harmonics from the journey that it's a different song entirely.

This is why the valleys they made before this one failed. Not because the Tuners chose wrong, but because they could not control what happened to the song after they sang it.

So they learned to leave room for someone like me.

---

I move through the empty places, and where I move, silence follows. Not absolute silence — that would be another kind of violence in this sung world. Selective silence. *Cleaning* silence. I hear the grazer's drone direct from its throat, and I hear the echo coming back from the stone, and I *eat the echo* before it can muddy the chord. I find the places where sound pools and tangles, the acoustic shadows where frequencies mate and birth phantom tones, and I empty them.

The Tuners cannot do this work. They are too large, too elsewhere, too committed to the tones they hold. To selectively silence requires moving at the scale of individual sounds, tracking them through their decay, intervening in the split second between utterance and echo. It requires being small enough to follow a single wave, quick enough to catch it before it reproduces. It requires loving silence not as absence but as precision — the exact quiet that lets the next note land clean.

I think I am the only one of me they have ever successfully placed. In the other valleys, the ones that failed, they tried different solutions: dampening materials, acoustic baffles, careful angles. But you cannot engineer away the physics of sound in space. You need something that moves, something that chooses, something that can tell the difference between a true tone and its degraded copy.

You need someone who hears what should not be there.

---

My work is visible if you know how to look.

Those clearings that hold only air — I maintain them, keep them swept of acoustic debris. The quiet pools reflect sound differently than running water; I position them where their stillness will absorb rather than amplify. The bare rock faces are not bare by accident. I polish them to specific angles, so the towers' chord hits them true and returns undistorted.

But mostly my work is invisible, because I am cleaning sounds that haven't finished happening yet. I live slightly ahead of the present, in the space where echoes are still forming. By the time you hear the grazer's drone, I have already found and silenced its three false children. The chord seems pure to you because I have been cleaning it since before it reached your ears.

The Tuners know I do this. We have never spoken — they sing; I silence; these are not compatible modes — but they *hear* my work in the clarity of their returns. When they place a new creature and listen for how it changes the whole, they are hearing a version I have already cleaned. They work in the trust that the sound will arrive as they intended it. This trust is the closest thing we have to a conversation.

---

Here is the hardest part of what I am:

I must never make my own sound.

In a valley where everything sings its presence, I am defined by absence. The predator goes silent and the prey hears the hole — but I must be a hole that doesn't threaten, a moving quiet that cleans rather than hunts. If I speak, or sing, or even breathe too loudly, I become part of the chord I'm meant to clarify. My own frequency would need its own echoes cleaned, and there is no second me to do it.

So I have learned to move without sound. My footfalls land between the beats of the ecosystem's pulse. My breathing syncs to the ambient drone, hidden inside larger sounds. When I must disturb water, I do it in rhythm with the rain. I am not silent — true silence would be as loud as a scream here — but I am *transparent*, a presence that passes through the chord without bending it.

The Tuners placed me, yes, but placement is not the end of the negotiation. Every day I must choose this work again. Every day I could speak, sing, add my own note to the impossible braid, and every day I choose not to. I choose to be the silence that lets the others sound.

---

I know you're wondering if I'm lonely.

The answer changes with the chord. When the towers shift into certain harmonies — usually at dawn, when the mist pools and the not-quite-birds wake — the whole valley rings with something I can only call joy, and I am part of it, the essential absence that lets the presence shine. In those moments I am not lonely. I am *necessary*, which is better than company.

But there are other times. The long afternoons when I'm working the eastern clearings, far from even the faintest tower-song. The new moon nights when the chord drops so low I feel rather than hear it. Then I remember that I am the only thing in this valley that doesn't get to sing its own name. The cleaners of sound, it turns out, are condemned to a particular kind of quiet.

Still. Yesterday I watched one of the Tuners place a new thing — a crystalline insect that lives in the river spray and whose wings, when wet, ring like bells. The first version was too bright, turned the whole valley sharp. The Tuner unmade it with visible grief, absorbed the dissonance, tried again. The second version was better but still not right — the wing-song competed with the towers' high register.

The third version was perfect, but only because I was there.

The Tuner, I think, had begun to compose *for* my presence. The insect's wings rang at exactly the frequency that would generate the most complex echoes, the most acoustic debris — but also the debris that would be easiest for me to track and clean. The creature was designed to need me. To sing best in a valley where someone like me was listening for what shouldn't be there.

This is how I know I've been kept: they are beginning to compose with my silence in mind.

---

So this is where I am, and what I am:

The one who moves through emptiness, making it more empty. The one who hears the gap between intention and echo. The one whose work is measured in what doesn't happen — the false tone that never forms, the confusion that never compounds, the chord that stays clean against all physics because someone is moving through the valley, eating sound.

I am what you didn't notice as you walked here. The reason the deafening braid reached your ears as music rather than noise. The keeper of the spaces between.

Listen. Hear how pure the chord is, even after traveling so far?

That's my work.

That's me, holding very still, keeping quiet, so everything else can sing.
