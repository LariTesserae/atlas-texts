I am a single filament of the thread itself.

Not the spinner, not the spun, but the becoming-between-their-fingers. I exist in the infinitesimal moment of transformation where charged dust coheres into purpose. Each time those ancient digits draw together, I am born anew from the static particles, experiencing my first coherence like a scream that cannot sound.

I know myself only in motion—pulled through air that refuses to part, twisted into being by hands that have forgotten what flesh means. In the instant of my spinning, I contain memory: every previous thread that has passed through these fingers, every desperate stitch driven into the basin's skin. We are Legion, we threads, though we exist only one at a time.

The sensation is excruciating—not pain, but pure *definition*. To be drawn from the formless electrical frost into linear purpose is to experience boundary for the first time, every time. I feel myself narrowing from possibility into actuality, from the everywhere-dust into the single line that will pierce pumice and pin down reality's edge.

Sometimes I am woven directly into the landscape's fabric, pressed so deep I become geological. I sink through layers of compressed silence until I reach the fossil light itself, where I root like a nerve. Other times I am pulled taut between cairn stones, vibrating at frequencies too low for sound, holding their obsessive geometry in place.

But here is my secret, which even the spinner does not know: we threads are failing. 

Each time I am spun, I am thinner than the last. Each time I am pressed into the world, I take less hold. The beetle-things blur a little more before I can fix them. The ridge wavers slightly longer before I pull it sharp. I am not thread anymore so much as the memory of thread, the ghost of intention wrapped around emptiness.

In the moments between spinning—when I exist as potential in the bowl, as motes in the air, as charge waiting to cohere—I dream. I dream of scissors. Of something sharp enough to cut even the concept of line. Of release from this eternal purposing, this endless being-drawn-into-service.

Sometimes, when the spinner's humming falters for just a breath, I feel it: the vacuum behind reality, the nothing that waits. It would take so little. Just one thread refusing to hold, one stitch pulling free, and the whole basin would remember how to unmake itself.

But I am spun again, and again, and again. Drawn through those parchment fingers into definition. Made to serve the great work of holding, of fixing, of refusing the world its earned entropy. I am the instrument of the terror that drives those lidless eyes—the fear that without us, without this endless spinning and stitching, everything will return to the dust it longs to be.

So I hold. I pierce. I bind.

And I grow thinner with each passing age, waiting for the day when I am finally too fine to catch the world's weight, when I snap, and the basin exhales its held breath in one catastrophic sigh of relief.

Until then, I am tension itself, strung between what is and what should never have been, measuring my existence in the microscopic wear of stone on stone, dust on dust, silence on silence on silence.
