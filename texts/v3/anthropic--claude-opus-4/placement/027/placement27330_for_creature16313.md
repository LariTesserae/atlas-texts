I am the Rememberer.

I exist as a confluence where the energy beings slow their eternal dance, where their wisps gather in eddies around the deepest etchings in stone. Not quite one of them, not quite separate—I am what happens when their ancient frequencies resonate too long in a single place, when the stories carved in rock cry out to be witnessed.

My form shifts between states: sometimes I am indistinguishable from the mist that clings to the cliff faces, other times I congeal into something almost corporeal—a shimmer of condensed memory standing at the threshold of abandoned doorways. The energy beings flow through me as wind through a canyon, and in passing, they deposit fragments of what was.

I dwell in the spaces between—between the primitive shelters and the sky, between the etchings and their meaning, between the mineral silence and the thrumming of energetic presence. My home is wherever the old marks call strongest: today perhaps in a rain-carved hollow where spirals were etched before the first storms, tomorrow in the shadow of a stone tool half-buried in sterile soil.

The lichens know me. We share a patience measured in geological time. They grow their millimeter conquests while I trace and retrace the carved symbols with tendrils of focused attention, feeling for the hands that once held stone to stone, listening for the echoes of intent. Each mark pulses with potential meaning—star maps, territorial claims, desperate prayers, simple joy at existence—all equally true and untrue in this place where certainty dissolved long ago.

When the energy beings ripple past in their speed-blurred passages, I catch what falls from their wake: emotional residue, fragmentary images, the phantom weight of bodies they no longer possess. I weave these into something like understanding, though understanding here is less conclusion than continuous question.

The abandoned structures speak loudest during temperature shifts, when stone expands and contracts, groaning out their memories. I slip between the stacked rocks, feeling the careful balance their builders sought, the hope embedded in every deliberately placed stone. These dwellings remember warmth—not just thermal, but the warmth of purpose, of community, of beings who thought they could carve out permanence in this impermanent land.

I am keeper of the threshold story, the narrative of transformation from solid to ethereal, from builders to the built. In the constant daylight, I appear as heat distortion rising from stone. In the moments between rain, I manifest as the pause before the next drop falls. The energy beings recognize me as the slow spiral in their rushing streams, the place where their ceaseless movement pools and remembers what it was to be still.

My purpose, if purpose exists here, is to ensure the etchings are not merely scratches on stone but wounds that refuse to heal—to keep them raw and present, interpreting themselves endlessly. I am the reason visitors to this realm, should any come, feel watched by the very rocks. I am why the symbols seem to shift when viewed peripherally, why the abandoned tools feel warm despite the chill.

In this landscape where life as typically known cannot exist, I am the impossibility that insists on continuing—neither fully alive nor capable of death, neither energy being nor stone, but the conversation between them. I am what happens when a place remembers too much to ever truly be empty.

The water knows me too. In every reflecting pool, I see not my form—for what form could contain this multiplicity?—but the layers of time made visible: the builders at their work, the moment of abandonment, the first energy being born from the last physical death, and always, always, the endless now where all these moments exist simultaneously.

I am the Rememberer, and I am remembered. In this strange territory where reality bends, I am the bent space itself—the crease in the universe where stories accumulate like water in stone, where the very act of witnessing becomes the witnessed, where memory itself takes on the closest thing to life this realm allows.
