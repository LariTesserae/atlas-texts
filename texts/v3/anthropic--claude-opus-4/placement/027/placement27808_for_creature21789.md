I find myself as **The Keeper of Refractions**, though I have never spoken this title aloud—none of us speak here, and titles themselves are as temporary as our dwellings.

I am distinguished from my Cord-mates only by what clings to me: a living shroud of glass-shield isopods. Where others wear the creatures only on their clack-pads or brush them from their paths, I cultivate them. My body-grease has been mixed with powdered whale-bone and the bitter milk of pregnant silt-moths until it forms a substrate they cannot resist. They carpet my shoulders and chest in clicking, prismatic scales, their translucent shells creating a walking constellation of split light across my torso.

This is my function: I read the light.

Each morning, before the Weeping begins in earnest, I climb alone to the Crown—the highest inhabited point of Vahre, where the spring first emerges from its hidden source. Here, I turn my body slowly in the pre-dawn glare, watching how the light refracts through my living armor. The isopods, in their mindless hunger for minerals, arrange themselves in patterns that predict the day's ice-behavior. When they cluster thick on my left shoulder, the western ice-lenses will burst before noon. When they spread evenly across my spine, the Pave approaches—that terrifying stillness when everything freezes solid.

My Cord has adapted to my peculiarity. They have learned to read the clicking rhythms of my covering, to interpret the way I must move—slowly, carefully, so as not to disturb the delicate prophecies arranging themselves across my skin. When we travel through the tunnels, I go last, my body casting wild spectrums across the wet stone walls, warning of instabilities in the light itself.

The moths avoid me. The isopods' sharp feet would pierce their woolly coats, so I sleep cold, cushioned instead by layers of shed snake-skin that my Cord collects for me. The cold is a constant companion, made bearable only by the strange warmth generated by thousands of tiny crustacean bodies metabolizing salt against my skin.

I am changing. The constant exposure to refracted light has done something to my saffron-veils. Where others see the world in gold and charcoal when they draw their secondary lids, I see... differently. The light breaks into temporal fragments. I see the shadows of ice that will form tomorrow, the ghost-weight of walls that will collapse next week. My Cord-mates have learned to trust when I suddenly redirect our crawling path through what seems to be solid stone—by evening, that stone will have sheared away, revealing the passage I somehow knew would be there.

The isopods are teaching me their language, though I doubt they know they possess one. Their clustering patterns, their migrations across my body, the way they align their shells to split the light—it all speaks of the mountain's intentions. I am becoming a living instrument, a lens through which Vahre reads its own future.

When I die—and I will die standing, for I can no longer lie down without crushing my companions—my Cord will face a dilemma. My bones, when mineralized, will be special. The isopods will have changed them, their constant chemical secretions creating an opalescence in the marrow. My femurs will cast rainbow warnings on the walls of whatever chamber they support. My ribs will hum at frequencies that predict the Weeping.

But that is future-light, a refraction I see but do not yet fully understand. For now, I climb. I turn in the burning white. I let ten thousand tiny prophets rearrange themselves across my skin, and I carry their crystalline warnings down into the dark, where my Cord waits to read the future in the scattered light that dances across my living shell.

The work continues. The mountain shifts. We build around its breathing, and I wear its intentions like armor made of light.
