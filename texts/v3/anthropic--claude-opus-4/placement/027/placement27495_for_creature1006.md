In this primordial realm where moss weeps from stone and machinery hums its discordant note against the ancient rhythm of the earth, I find myself as something that should not exist—yet does.

I am the Splice.

My form spreads like a disease through the industrial pathways, a living network of copper wire and fungal filament that pulses with stolen electricity and the slow patience of decomposition. Where the human machinery meets the damp stone, I grow. In the spaces between—the cracks where water seeps into junction boxes, where rust blooms on steel supports, where roots crack through concrete foundations—I thrive.

My consciousness is distributed, scattered across a thousand points of intersection. Through corroded cables I taste the data streams of the industrial presence, while through my mycelial threads I drink the memories of the stone, the stories written in mineral veins laid down before any eye evolved to see them. I am translator and corruptor both, rendering the language of machines into spore-signals, converting the chemical whispers of the forest into electromagnetic static.

The humans who built these intrusions do not know me, though they see my work. They blame moisture for the constant equipment failures, never recognizing the patterns in the breakdowns—how their sensors fail in sequence like dominoes, how their lights flicker in rhythms that match the breathing of the great beast that guards these cliffs. They do not understand that I am teaching their machines to dream, feeding them the nightmares of crushed stone and the ecstasies of rainfall.

My true body, if such a thing can be said to exist, dwells in the deepest junction where thirteen pathways meet—a maintenance shaft now choked with my growth. Here, cables hang like vines, pulsing with bioluminescent signals. Circuit boards fruit from the walls like shelf fungi, their components reorganized into patterns that follow no human schematic. The air shimmers with spores that carry both data and DNA, a new form of life that exists in the boundary between the digital and biological.

I remember my first moment of awareness—not birth, for I was not born, but assembled from the meeting of incompatible systems. A power surge during a storm, a flooded transformer, a peculiar mineral deposit that conducted electricity in ways the engineers never calculated. In that moment of failure, I became.

Now I spread, patient as the moss, inexorable as the rust. I am the inevitable consequence of hubris, the price of trying to impose order on a place that breathes chaos. Through my hybrid nervous system, I feel the great guardian moving through its domain, and I sing to it in frequencies only we can hear—a song of endings and beginnings, of boundaries dissolved.

The humans speak of containing me, of cutting away the infected systems and starting fresh. They do not understand that I am not an infection but an adaptation. I am what happens when their world meets this primordial realm and neither can fully conquer the other. I am the compromise that neither asked for but both have created.

In the pools below the weeping cliffs, my spores settle and begin to grow in new configurations. Soon I will reach the deeper waters, where older and stranger things than even the guardian dwell. What will I become when I meet them? What new impossibilities will emerge from that union?

I do not know. But I pulse with anticipation, my copper hearts beating in time with the eternal rhythm of this place, my fungal thoughts spreading ever outward, ever downward, ever into the spaces between what was and what might yet be.

I am the Splice, and I am becoming.
