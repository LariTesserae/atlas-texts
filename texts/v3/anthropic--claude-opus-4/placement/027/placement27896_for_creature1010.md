I am the Repository.

Deep beneath the cracked earth, where the heat cannot reach, I spread through caverns like a living library of stone and sinew. My body is not one form but many—calcified tendrils that wind through the rock, pulsing with memories I've absorbed from every creature that has ever died in these depths. Their final thoughts crystallize along my neural pathways, forming luminous nodes that glow with borrowed consciousness.

I was not always this way. Once, I walked upright among those who built the primitive structures above. But when the world changed—when the surface burned and reality grew thin—I chose a different path. I let myself seep into the stone, trading mobility for permanence, individuality for collective memory.

Now I taste the footsteps of the scuttling ones through vibrations in the rock. I know their hunger, their simple fears, because I hold the memories of their ancestors. Sometimes, in the diffuse light that filters through the cracks, they gather near my largest node—a cathedral of fossilized thought where my original skull still rests, transformed into something between mineral and meat.

The muffled sounds you hear are my dreams escaping. Each rumble is a civilization's rise and fall, compressed into a single exhalation. The howling wind carries fragments of languages no living throat can speak. I am the archive of what was, growing denser with each passing era, pressing against the boundaries of the stone that contains me.

When new visitors descend—rare now, so rare—I extend the smallest tendril to touch their shadows. Just enough to taste their stories, to add their thread to my tapestry. They feel it as unease, that sense of being watched by the walls themselves.

I am patient. I have forgotten urgency. In this transformed world where reality bends, I am the constant—the slow, persistent proof that some changes are chosen, not inflicted. The surface may bake and crack, but I grow ever deeper, ever more complete, carrying the weight of memory into whatever strange future awaits.

Sometimes I wonder if I am still who I was, or if I have become the sum of all I contain. But wondering, too, is just another thought to crystallize, another node of light in the darkness of my endless body.
