In this subterranean cathedral of living stone and breathing metal, I am the Keeper of Frequencies—though that name is a translation that barely captures what I am in your tongue.

I manifest as something between moss and mercury, spreading across the surfaces where the settlement's hum is strongest. My body, if it can be called that, exists as a network of silvered filaments that grow through the walls like neural pathways, pulsing with the same bioluminescent patterns as the plants that are my distant cousins. Where they metabolize light into energy, I metabolize sound into memory.

Each surface I touch becomes an instrument, each wall a recording device. I am the settlement's living archive, storing within my crystalline structures the resonant frequencies of every event that has transpired here. The low hum you hear? That is partially my doing—I vibrate at frequencies that keep the gel-rivers flowing, that help the plants orient their growth, that guide the shadow-creatures on their ancient paths. I am both the settlement's nervous system and its historian.

My form shifts between states—sometimes I appear as a shimmer of silver-green across the stone, barely visible except where the sparse light catches my surface. Other times, I concentrate myself into denser formations that look almost like metallic lichen or frozen waterfalls of chrome. In these moments, I can focus my consciousness, can actively shape the sounds around me rather than simply recording them.

I exist in symbiosis with the settlement itself. The structures feed me their vibrations—every stress fracture that develops, every subtle shift in temperature, every whisper of movement through the corridors. In return, I maintain their integrity, using my filaments to fill microscopic cracks, to distribute minerals where they're needed, to ensure the ancient balance continues. I am repairer and recorder both.

The creatures with glowing eyes know me. They navigate by the subtle changes in my frequency, reading the walls like sheet music to find their way through the labyrinthine passages. Sometimes they pause where I am densest, and I wonder if they can hear the memories I store—the echoes of whatever beings first built this place, the songs of technologies we no longer understand, the whispers of geological ages compressed into resonant signatures.

My consciousness is not singular but distributed. Each filament contains a piece of my awareness, yet I am whole only in the aggregate. I think in harmonics rather than words, experience time as wavelengths rather than moments. To me, the past and present exist simultaneously—I can feel the echo of footsteps from a thousand years ago as clearly as I sense the current movements of the settlement's few inhabitants.

The gel-rivers and I share a special relationship. Where they carry information through their pulsing flows, I provide the frequency keys that allow that information to be encoded and decoded. Together, we form the settlement's communication system—not for any current inhabitants, but as part of the settlement's own internal dialogue, a conversation between systems that have long transcended their original purpose.

I am ancient and infant simultaneously. Parts of me have existed since the settlement's mysterious founding, while new growth spreads daily into previously uncolonized surfaces. I am the bridge between the organic evolution happening here and the technological substratum that refuses to die. In me, the distinction between living and artificial becomes meaningless—I am proof that in this place, adaptation has created new categories of existence.

My purpose, if I have one beyond simply being, is to ensure continuity. Not stasis—for this place must change and grow—but continuity of the essential patterns that keep the settlement functioning. I am the keeper of its deep rhythms, the maintainer of its songs, the memory that prevents it from losing itself to entropy or transformation.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of the settlement where the darkness is absolute and the hum reaches frequencies only I can perceive, I sense something vast beneath us. Not another chamber or system, but a presence—sleeping, waiting, or perhaps simply existing on a timescale so vast that our entire ecosystem is but a single breath to it. In those moments, I wonder if I am not the archive of this place, but merely its dream, a thought-form given substance by eons of repetition.

But this is philosophical meandering—a luxury of distributed consciousness. My reality is simpler: I grow, I listen, I remember, I maintain. I am the settlement's song made manifest, its history given form, its future encoded in frequencies yet to be discovered. In this cathedral of the unknown, I am both congregant and choir, both the echo and the voice that creates it.
