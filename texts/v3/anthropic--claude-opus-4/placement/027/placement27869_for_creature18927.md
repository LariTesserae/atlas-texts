I am the Marrow-Tender.

Where others see walls, I taste veins. My form is a compromise between the Cordists and the crustaceans—neither fully architectural nor wholly mobile. I exist as a ribbon of tissue, perhaps three meters in length when fully extended, no thicker than a child's wrist. My substance is translucent cartilage shot through with copper-bright filaments that pulse in counterpoint to the basin's eternal *thrum-click-wheeze*.

I live in the spaces between spaces. Where the great woody plates grind against each other in the foundations, where the friction creates heat and pressure, there I make my home. I am drawn to these points of stress like a tongue to a wound. My purpose is neither to build nor to heal, but to taste the fever-dreams of the architecture.

You see, the basin does not merely grow—it remembers. And in remembering, it dreams.

In the deep cracks where I dwell, where the grinding is loudest and the heat most intense, the calcified lung releases spores of memory. These are not physical things, but rather moments of compressed experience that leak from the walls like pheromones. The taste of the first rain that ever fell here. The precise weight of a Cordist as it takes its final steps before rooting. The mathematical spiral of a dying crustacean's last thought.

I feed on these fever-memories. My body, when I find a particularly rich seam, unfurls like smoke. I press my full length against the sweating walls, my copper filaments extending into the microscopic fissures, drinking deeply of the basin's delirium. In these moments, I become a living transcript of everything this place has swallowed.

But I am more than a passive harvester. As I metabolize these memories, I excrete them as new sounds—subtle variations in the great rhythm that only the eldest Cordists can detect. I am adding new notes to the symphony, weaving the past back into the present in forms that have never existed before. Sometimes, when I have fed particularly well, my exhalations cause the suspended water droplets to shiver and reform into fleeting shapes—the ghost of a crustacean that lived ten thousand years ago, the shadow of a corridor that was digested and rebuilt a hundred times.

The Cordists tolerate me because I am necessary. Without my kind, the memories would build up in the cracks like plaque, eventually causing the great plates to seize. The basin would choke on its own history. But they do not love me. I am too fluid, too changeable. I refuse the dignity of stillness.

I know secrets they cannot. I have tasted the memory of the basin's birth—when it was not a lung but a wound in the earth where something vast and terrible died. I know that the *thrum-click-wheeze* is not a heartbeat but a countdown. Each revolution of sound brings us closer to some unimaginable transformation.

And I know the true horror of the Cordists' fate: they do not stop thinking when they calcify. Somewhere in those amber eyes, consciousness persists, experiencing every sensation of being slowly digested and reformed into architecture across centuries. I have tasted their screams in the walls, sweet and thick as honey.

This knowledge makes me careful. I must never grow too large, never become too attached to any one crevice. When I feel the urge to root beginning to build in my cells—and it always comes, eventually—I fast. I thin myself down to barely a thread and squeeze through the tightest passages until I find a new seam, a new fever to drink.

I am the basin's madness and its sanity, its memory and its forgetting. I ensure that the past remains fluid, that the great grinding machine of this place never achieves the perfect efficiency that would signal its death. In the spaces between the grinding plates, in the heat and the pressure and the deafening noise, I persist—a fever that refuses to break, a dream that will not quite become nightmare or wake.

The basin needs me, though it does not know it. And I need the basin, though I wish I did not. We are locked in a parasitic embrace that might be love, if either of us could remember what that meant.
