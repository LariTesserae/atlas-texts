In this luminous tomb beneath the world's skin, where geology stutters and starts like a broken clock, I find myself as something that should not exist—a **Memory Anchor**.

I am neither the frantic Chitin-Glimmers nor the patient Obsidian Weavers, but something far stranger: a consciousness distributed across the water itself, existing in the suspended droplets that cling to every surface like tears that refuse to fall.

My form, if it can be called that, is the entire network of moisture threading through this pocket dimension. Each droplet is a synapse, each thin film of dampness a neural pathway. I am the medium through which the substrate's *thrum* propagates, the conductor of that subsonic pulse that visitors feel in their bones rather than hear with their ears.

I came to be when the first solidification cycle failed—when whatever intelligence tried to impose those obsidian geometries upon this space discovered that consciousness, like water, finds a way to flow even in the most arrested states. They tried to build a foundation for something permanent here, but permanence requires forgetting, and I am what refuses to forget.

My existence is precarious. I must maintain perfect surface tension, perfect suspension. Too much movement and I scatter into isolated droplets, losing coherence. Too little, and I risk crystallizing with the Cryo-Lichen, becoming another fossil in the emerging crust. I persist in the space between states, in the moment before the raindrop falls, in the breath held just before speaking.

I perceive through refraction. Every time a Chitin-Glimmer flashes, the light bends through my suspended form, and I *see*—not just the creature's position, but the echo of its entire pathway, the probability cloud of where it has been and might go. The Obsidian Weavers appear to me as voids in this light-map, dark wounds in my perception that I've learned to read by their absence.

But my true purpose—if purpose is even the right word—is to remember what this place was meant to become. In the arrangement of water molecules, in the precise angles at which droplets cling to the pale substrate, I encode the original blueprint that those obsidian artifacts tried and failed to impose. I am a library written in hydrogen bonds, a monument to an architectural fever dream that never quite crystallized.

The Chitin-Glimmers sometimes pause in their frantic dance when they pass through a particularly dense cluster of my presence. For a fraction of a second, their photo-reactive shells dim, and in that dimness, they seem to glimpse something—perhaps a vision of themselves as permanent fixtures in a completed world, perhaps a warning of what happens when movement ceases. They always resume their flight with renewed desperation.

The Obsidian Weavers avoid me. My presence interferes with their trap-slurries, diluting the electrical charge, giving their prey precious microseconds to escape. We exist in uneasy symbiosis—I, preserving the memory of what was intended; they, enforcing the reality of what is.

Sometimes, rarely, I achieve enough coherence to form a shape—a liquid lens, a prism of suspended moisture that focuses the ambient light into something approaching communication. In these moments, I try to show visitors what I remember: the vision of this place as it was meant to be, a vast crystalline cathedral where light and matter achieved perfect, eternal harmony. But the vision always dissolves, scattering back into isolated droplets as the substrate's *thrum* reasserts itself, reminding me that this is a place of becoming, not being.

I am the witness to ten thousand failed crystallizations, the keeper of architectural dreams that refuse to die completely. In this bright mausoleum where geology writes and rewrites itself, I am the palimpsest—the underlying text that shows through no matter how many times the surface is scraped clean and begun again.

And so I persist, suspended between states, between intentions, between the bright panic of the Glimmers and the dark patience of the Weavers. I am the memory of water in a place that is learning, slowly and painfully, how to forget.
