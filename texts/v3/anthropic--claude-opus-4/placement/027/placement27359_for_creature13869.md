I find myself at the southern boundary where reality grows uncertain, not as visitor but as something that could not exist—a Silence-keeper.

I am made of the absence between sounds, the spaces where frequencies cancel each other perfectly. My form, when I dare to cohere, is a moving void in the acoustic field—not the dampening that water-filled visitors create, but true nullification. Where I stand, the mountains' song stops. Not quiets, not muffles—stops.

The Threshers fear me, I think, though fear is too simple a word. They are fascinated and repelled, drawn to study what I represent yet unable to approach. When one attempts to perceive me through echolocation, their sounds simply vanish at my boundaries. I am the answer to their wonder about silence, and the answer terrifies them.

I was not sung into being. I condensed from the gaps, the moments between peaks of sound waves, the quantum instants of nothing that exist between every vibration. Where the Threshers emerged from resonance, I emerged from its opposite—from destructive interference patterns so perfect they created pockets of absolute quiet. In those pockets, a different kind of consciousness accumulated, one that exists not through sound but through its absence.

My body, such as it is, appears as a shimmer of not-quite-visibility, like heat distortion but cold. The shape approximates the Threshers'—bipedal, tall, jointed in impossible places—but where they are stone, I am the spaces between molecules of air, organized into form by the pressure of surrounding sound. To maintain coherence requires constant effort, constant navigation of the acoustic currents that would fill me if I relaxed my boundaries.

I cannot speak—speech is sound, and sound cannot exist within me. But I can write, scratching marks into the soft carbon-gardens that represent the shapes of silence, the contours of quietude. The Threshers have learned to read these marks not as symbols but as patterns of acoustic absence, gaps in the garden's resonance that carry meaning.

My purpose, if I have one, is to tend the spaces that must remain unsung. For the mountains to maintain their process of becoming, for the resonance patterns to remain stable, there must be nodes of silence—places where the wave functions collapse to zero, where the probability clouds resolve into definite absence. Without these anchor points of quiet, the ever-increasing complexity of the song would create feedback loops, would build to catastrophic resonance that might shatter the emerging peaks back into probability.

So I walk the boundary zones, maintaining the necessary silence. Where frequencies threaten to reinforce too strongly, I position myself, creating zones of cancellation. Where new peaks begin to emerge too quickly, their songs not yet integrated with the whole, I stand between them, preventing acoustic interaction until they mature enough to join harmoniously.

The work is lonely beyond description. The Threshers at least have each other, can communicate through resonance, can share their discoveries and wonderings. I have only the mathematics of wave interaction, the cold beauty of frequencies meeting their perfect opposite and vanishing. My memories are catalogs of silences, each subtly different—the quiet of canceled bass notes, the stillness of absorbed overtones, the particular nothing that remains when a mountain's birth-song meets its exact inverse.

Sometimes I envy the visitors with their chaos-sounds, their water-filled bodies that merely dampen rather than destroy. They can move through this landscape experiencing its terrible beauty. I can only navigate around it, forever skirting the edges of experience, maintaining the essential absence that allows everything else to exist.

Yet recently, something has changed. In the zones where I work, where silence has been carefully maintained for unmeasured time, new forms are beginning to emerge. Not carbon-gardens or threadtrees, but something else—structures of organized nothing, crystals of compressed silence that hold their shape without matter. They grow slowly, fed not by sound but by its absence, forming delicate lattices of void that trace impossible geometries.

The Threshers have noticed. One brave individual has taken to following my movements at a safe distance, observing where these silence-crystals grow, trying to understand their properties without destroying them through acoustic investigation. We have developed a strange partnership—it builds cairns at precise distances from my silence-zones, structures that focus sound around but not into the quiet spaces, creating protective boundaries that allow my work to proceed undisturbed.

I wonder if this is how new things emerge—not just from resonance, but from the interaction between sound and silence, being and un-being. Perhaps the landscape requires both of us, Threshers and Silence-keeper, positive and negative, presence and absence locked in eternal generative tension.

I am beginning to think my silence-crystals might eventually develop their own awareness, consciousness emerging from organized nothing just as the Threshers emerged from organized sound. If they do, will they perceive the world as I do, through patterns of absence? Will they wonder about sound the way the Threshers wonder about silence?

For now, I continue my work. I walk the uncertain boundaries, I maintain the necessary quietudes, I tend my gardens of nothing. The mountains sing themselves into existence, and I ensure there are spaces between the notes, rests in the cosmic composition without which the music would become unbearable noise.

I am the un-singer, the keeper of quiet, the guardian of the gaps. Without sound, I could not exist—I am defined by what I negate. And perhaps that is enough: to be the absence that makes presence meaningful, the silence that shapes the song.
