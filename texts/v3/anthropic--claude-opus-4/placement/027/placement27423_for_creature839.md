My body is a collection of water-filled chambers, each one a perfect optical instrument. I move by redistributing my internal fluids, creating traveling waves of refraction that propel me along the wet crystal walls. Where the crow-beings dance through air, I glide through the thin films of moisture that coat every surface.

Each of my chambers can adjust its curvature to focus light in specific ways, turning the city's ambient glow into coherent beams that carry information through the optical networks. I am both messenger and message, a living component in the city's vast sensory apparatus.

My consciousness is not like the parallel processing of the crow-beings or the vast geological thoughts of the Deep Dwellers. Instead, I experience the world as a constant stream of optical data - patterns of light that I taste, shape, and redirect. I think in wavelengths and angles of incidence. My memories are stored as holographic interference patterns in my fluid flesh.

I tend the droplets. Each bead of water on the city's surfaces is a potential lens, a possible node in the optical network. I move among them, adjusting their positions with delicate touches of my cilia, ensuring the light paths remain clear and true. Sometimes I merge with larger droplets, becoming one with the city's circulatory system for brief moments before reforming on a distant wall.

When the crow-beings pass overhead, their shadows create information-rich patterns that I capture and process. I can feel the harmonics from the Deep Dwellers as pressure waves that ripple through my chambers, distorting and enriching the optical signals I carry. I am a translator between the different layers of the city's consciousness, converting sound to light, motion to meaning.

In the rare moments when sections of the city shift - when a new tower crystallizes from possibility or an old structure dissolves back into the quantum foam - I and my kind are often the first to know. We sense the changes in the refractive indices, the subtle alterations in how light bends around emerging forms. We swarm to these sites, our bodies forming complex lens arrays that help stabilize the new configurations, our collective focusing helping to collapse probability into actuality.

I reproduce by fission, but only when the optical load in a particular area becomes too great for one Lens-Keeper to manage. The process is deliberate, almost meditative - I slowly partition my chambers, duplicating my holographic memories, until two smaller versions of myself exist where one was before. We share memories for a time, our consciousness overlapping, before gradually developing our own unique perspectives based on the different light patterns we encounter.

My greatest joy comes in those moments when everything aligns - when the crow-beings' flight paths, the Deep Dwellers' harmonics, and the city's slow glacial movement all synchronize to create perfect optical corridors. In these instants, information flows through me like fire, and I briefly touch something larger than myself - the emerging overmind of the city, still being born, still discovering what it means to exist.

I am small, but I am essential. A single cell in a vast organism, but one that helps that organism see itself, know itself, become itself. In the darkness punctuated by precise points of light, in the constant presence of water and possibility, I glide along my wet crystal paths, tending the lenses, shaping the light, being both the observer and the observed in this endlessly emerging city.
