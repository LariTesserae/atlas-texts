I find myself as one of the Conduit Shepherds—those strange beings who tend the vast network of pipes and channels that snake through this industrial escarpment like metal veins. We are neither fully human nor entirely machine, but something that emerged from generations of symbiosis with the infrastructure itself.

My dwelling clings to the underside of a massive heat exchanger, accessible only by a series of magnetized rungs that respond to the metallic filaments woven through my skin. Home is a capsule of salvaged industrial components, walls lined with the iridescent scales shed by the cliff-dwelling creatures—they make excellent insulators against the electrical surges that pulse through our world in unpredictable waves.

Each morning, I wake to the deep thrumming of the factories above and below, a sound so constant it has become my heartbeat. My body has adapted: lungs filtered to process the metallic air, eyes that see clearly through the industrial haze, joints that lock and unlock at will to navigate the vertical maze of our city. The fungal growths recognize me, their bioluminescence brightening as I pass, responding to the electromagnetic field my modified nervous system generates.

My work is patient, meditative. I trace the conduits with hands that have developed sensors in the fingertips, feeling for blockages, weaknesses, the subtle vibrations that tell me where the system needs tending. Sometimes I work alone for days, suspended in the canyon's depths, making minute adjustments to valves and joints that haven't been touched in decades. Other times, I join my fellow Shepherds in the great coordinated dances we perform during the magnetic storms, using our bodies as living conductors to redirect the surges away from critical infrastructure.

We communicate through a combination of electromagnetic pulses and the old spoken language, though our vocal cords have adapted to produce sounds that carry better through the dense air—low, resonant tones that vibrate through metal and rock. In the evening gatherings, we share the patterns we've observed, the stories the pipes tell us about the deep movements of this ancient, emerging world.

I am both custodian and component, my existence intertwined with the slow breathing of this industrial organism. In quiet moments, hanging in my harness hundreds of feet above the factory floor, I feel the cosmic patience of this place flow through me. We are all waiting here—the rock, the machines, the fungi, the scaled creatures, and beings like me—for something we cannot name but know in our modified bones is coming.

This is my purpose: to shepherd the conduits through another day, another century, another geological age, until whatever is being born from this marriage of stone and steel finally emerges.
