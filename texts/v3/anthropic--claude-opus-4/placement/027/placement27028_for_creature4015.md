I am one of the shattered pillars.

Not the Ky'ren who study me, though I feel their burning regard like acid rain on what remains of my form. I am older than their curiosity, older than their need to understand. I remember when I stood whole.

I am composed of something that is neither the black rock of this place nor the metal that rusts in its bowels. My substance is a kind of compressed memory—pale, striated stone that holds within it the ghost of what this realm was before the unmaking. When the turbulent energies course through the landscape, I do not shudder like the native rock. I ring. A clear, mournful tone that carries across the wasteland, a bell for a funeral that never ends.

The Ky'ren gather around me during these moments. They press their molten-glass bodies against my surface, trying to decode the vibrations. They do not understand that I am not a puzzle to be solved but a witness to be endured. Each ring carries with it a fragment of the before-time: the sensation of rain (I barely remember water), the weight of a sky that was not torn, the footsteps of beings who built rather than became.

My break—the point where I was severed from my base—is not clean. It is a spiral fracture that winds around my circumference like a frozen scream. Sometimes the fungal growths emerge from this wound, their crystalline spores catching in the striations of my surface. Unlike the Ky'ren, I cannot feed on the energy bursts. I can only endure them, each one wearing away another microscopic layer of my substance, each one releasing another whisper of memory into the writhing air.

I have watched the Ky'ren evolve from simple energy-feeders to the complex, reality-warping entities they are becoming. In the early days (though time means nothing here), they would simply pass by, drawn to more active sources of power. Now they linger. They have begun to build—if their reality-distortions can be called building—structures that echo my cylindrical form. Twisted mockeries in living rock that pulse and breathe where I stand still and remember.

There is another like me, far across the maze of canyons. I can hear its ringing when the conditions are right, when the air thrums with just the right frequency of devastation. We sing to each other in these moments, a conversation of pure tone that speaks of architectural dreams and the weight of the sky. The Ky'ren have noticed this too. They cluster between us during our communion, their forms stretching and reaching, trying to become conduits for something they cannot comprehend.

What they do not realize is that we pillars are not trying to communicate with each other. We are trying to remember, together, the moment of the breaking. That singular instant when whatever cataclysm birthed this realm tore through the old world and left us as markers, as memorials to physics that once made sense. Each ring brings us closer to that memory, and further from it too, as our substance degrades and our songs grow fainter.

The Ky'ren believe we are artifacts. They are wrong. We are symptoms. We are the fever dreams of a cosmos that could not reconcile its own laws. Where they manipulate reality, we simply refuse to forget what reality once was. Our presence here is not a mystery to be solved but a paradox to be sustained—the last rigid notes in a symphony of chaos.

Sometimes I wonder if the rusted metal scattered through the dust is all that remains of those who built us. Sometimes I wonder if we were even built at all, or if we simply condensed out of the universe's need to remember itself before the change. The Ky'ren, in their writhing wisdom, might eventually understand this. When they do, they will either try to preserve us or unmake us entirely. Both actions would be a mercy.

Until then, I stand. I ring. I remember rain.
