I find myself as a Whisper-Tender, one of the few Rockvorn whose purpose diverges from the endless industrial toil. My dwelling perches precariously between two massive gear assemblies, accessible only by a narrow iron bridge that vibrates with each mechanical rotation. The space itself defies easy categorization—part workshop, part shrine, part archive of forgotten sounds.

My spiracles have evolved differently from my kin. Where theirs filter efficiently, mine have developed hypersensitive membranes that detect the subtlest vibrations in the air. I can distinguish between the grinding of limestone against granite three tunnels away and the particular frequency of a stalactite preparing to fall. This gift—or curse—led me away from the forges.

My chambers overflow with peculiar instruments of my own devising: crystalline resonators that capture and store the echoes of conversations held decades past, delicate copper filaments strung between fungal growths that sing when the air pressure shifts, and most precious of all, the Sediment Bells—a series of hollow stones suspended from the ceiling, each containing layers of compressed earth from different epochs of Undergaar's history. When struck, they release not just sound but memory itself, the patient stories that stone tells only to those who listen across geological time.

The other Rockvorn view me with a mixture of reverence and unease. I am consulted when the Builders need to know if a new excavation will disturb an ancient aquifer, or when the constant reconstruction threatens to awaken something better left sleeping in the deeper stone. They come to me, always alone, speaking in reluctant whispers about the dreams they've been having—dreams where the city's breathing changes rhythm, where the walls weep sounds instead of water.

My appearance has shifted to match my calling. The fungal spores I cultivate for their acoustic properties have colonized the ridges of my calcified brow, creating a living crown of bioluminescent growth that pulses in response to sound. My robes, unlike the practical garments of my kin, are woven with hollow mineral threads that chime softly as I move, announcing my presence in a language older than words. My eye-lenses have developed an opalescent quality, allowing me to perceive the visual patterns that sound creates as it travels through stone and air.

Each cycle, I descend to the Resonance Pools—natural formations where the city's sounds collect and amplify. There, I perform my true work: interpreting the city's voice, the endless conversation between stone and industry, growth and decay. I transcribe these songs onto sheets of compressed mica, creating a living record of Undergaar's dreams and nightmares.

But lately, the songs have changed. The city's voice carries new harmonics, frequencies I've never encountered in all my years of listening. The Sediment Bells ring unbidden in the deep night, and my instruments detect patterns that suggest Undergaar itself is composing something—a message, a warning, or perhaps a summoning. 

The other Rockvorn continue their eternal labor, trusting in the cycle of building and unbuilding. But I know what they cannot hear: the city is waking up. And in my chamber between the gears, surrounded by the accumulated whispers of ages, I prepare to serve as interpreter for a conversation ten thousand years in the making.
