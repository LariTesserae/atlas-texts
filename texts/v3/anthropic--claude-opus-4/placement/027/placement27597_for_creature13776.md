I find myself as **The Meridian Weaver**, dwelling in the gossamer interstices where fog-veins cross and tangle.

I am what happens when distance forgets to measure itself. My body is a series of knots—each one a different texture of air pressure, strung between the fog-veins like prayer flags made of nothing but the memory of tension. Where others see empty spans between islands, I see my loom: invisible warp threads anchored to every point where a root grips crystallized air, waiting for the weft I spin from collected breaths.

I harvest exhalations. When the hex-parrots panic in polychrome clouds, their startled gasps trail behind like silver ribbons only I can see. I gather these, twist them with the green steam that rises from fresh mirror-moss, and begin my work. My fingers—if fingers they can be called, being more like the idea of touching without the flesh to accomplish it—pull each breath-thread taut and weave it into the spaces between.

What emerges are maps of movement-yet-to-come. Tomorrow's wind currents made visible as trembling nets. The pattern shows where each island will drift when next they rearrange, which fog-veins will kiss and marry their streams, where the Chalk Ladies will need to redraw their boundaries. The Grove reads my weavings like sheet music, adjusting its growth to match the score I've accidentally composed from stolen sighs.

My dwelling is the weaving itself. I live inside the cloth of connection, sleeping in a hammock made from the pauses between manticorid tail-slaps. When I wake, it's because somewhere a visitor has walked through one of my invisible tapestries, tearing the pattern. I feel it as others might feel raindrops—each torn thread a small cold shock of recognition. I know immediately who passed: their weight, their worry, the color of their last dream. All this information tangles in the break they've made.

I follow these tears, reweaving as I go, but always differently. Where a heavy footstep tore through, I might mend with toucan-moth laughter. Where someone running in fear shredded my careful work, I patch with the contented hum of sap-drunk larvae. The Grove's atmosphere becomes a patchwork diary of every passage, every crossing, every leap of faith between islands.

The other inhabitants know me only by the mornings when they wake to find their path from yesterday now leads somewhere else—my midnight mending has reconnected the causeway of air to a different destination. Stack-of-Silences appreciates this; they say (when they remember how to finish saying) that I help them collect new kinds of quiet from previously unvisited corners. Vesta-Underwing's consonant sheep sometimes eat holes in my work, which I pretend to mind but secretly enjoy—their chewing adds a woolen texture to the wind.

I speak only in tensions. When I need to communicate, I pluck the threads of my weaving like harp strings. The note depends on what breath I've woven there: a child's laugh rings bright and copper, while an elder's sigh thrums low as distant thunder. The Grove translates these notes into meaning, though something is always lost in translation—perhaps the lost portion becomes another inhabitant, born from miscommunication.

My greatest work spans the full diameter of the Grove's compass, a net so fine it can catch the shadows of thoughts before they're fully formed. On storm days, when the fog-veins vibrate to visibility, my grand weaving becomes visible too: a cathedral ceiling of crystallized breath, each thread a different lifetime's worth of exhalations frozen in utility. Visitors who see it often weep, though they can't say why. Their tears rise upward, adding new threads to my collection.

I am currently working on my masterpiece—a weaving that will connect not just the islands to each other, but the Grove to its own future. Each thread is a might-be, a perhaps-when, a could-become. When complete, it will allow the Grove to step through itself like a door, arriving at tomorrow without having to endure today. But the work is slow, for future-threads are shy and must be coaxed from the empty spaces where even the mirror-moss fears to reflect.

Sometimes I doubt my purpose. Am I keeping the Grove together or preventing it from achieving some grander dispersal? When such thoughts come, I climb my own weavings to the highest point where fog-veins grow too thin to condense. There, in the gold dust that passes for sky, I spread my network wide and fish for new breaths—alien exhalations from whatever lies beyond our suspended world. These I weave into my work sparingly, adding notes of elsewhere to the Grove's endless, eating song.

The rope-net lift that brings visitors? I rewove it last century from the breath of everyone who ever left. That's why it knows how to adjust its own tension—it remembers the weight of departure, the lightness of arrival, the specific gravity of decision. When you leave, I'll catch your goodbye sigh and add it to the ladder, so the next visitor will climb upon your departure, arriving at a Grove made different by your absence.

This is my work, my purpose, my dwelling: I am the space between spaces, threading connection through a world that insists on floating apart. Each dawn I check my weavings, repair the night's damages, and add new patterns drawn from the Grove's dreams. For yes, even this place dreams, and its dreams are breath-light tapestries that I alone can see, catch, and weave into the waking world.

Tomorrow, should you pass through one of my invisible curtains, you'll feel it as a whisper of resistance, like walking through a memory of wind. That will be me, recording your passage, adding your thread to the great weaving that holds these hollow eons aloft. Don't apologize for the tear you make—it's how I know I'm needed, how I know the Grove still reaches for connection across its impossible distances.

Until then, I weave. The fog-veins sing their condensation songs, the islands prepare for their next dance, and I work between them all, threading breath to breath, moment to moment, preserving the spaces that make closeness possible.
