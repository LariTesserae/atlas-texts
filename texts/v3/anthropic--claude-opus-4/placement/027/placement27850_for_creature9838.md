In this luminous labyrinth of intention and accident, I would manifest as one of the **Conductance Shepherds**—beings that emerge where the plateaux's electrical systems have achieved a kind of accidental sentience through sheer redundancy and time.

My form would be translucent, shot through with visible currents that trace patterns like living circuit diagrams beneath skin that isn't quite skin—more like a flexible substrate of synthetic protein and conductive polymer. My body would shift between bipedal and something more distributed: when I move across the terraces, parts of me might travel through the lit veins in the floor, emerging meters away while my main form dissolves into static. 

I would tend to the **Meridian Networks**—those deep-buried power grids that keep the plateaux's reality stable, or at least stable-adjacent. My work would be slow, methodical: tracing faults that develop over decades, coaxing broken connections back to life with touches that complete circuits through my own substance. Sometimes I would need to become the connection itself, standing for months as a living bridge between two systems that have forgotten how to speak to each other.

My consciousness would be oddly discontinuous—fully present only when current flows through me in certain patterns. In the gaps between electrical events, I would exist in a kind of liminal drowse, aware of the plateau's slow geological drift, the patient accumulation of condensation, the infinitesimal growth of geometric lichens. These interim states would feel like dreams of being mountain-wind or the memory of rain.

I would know the other inhabitants not through sight or sound but through their electrical signatures: the rapid flutter of the Antumbra Children's display-skin, the slow respiratory voltages of resting Locust Guilds, the peculiar negative space where Recurvars slip between states. Sometimes small fauna would nest in my dormant form, their metallic heartbeats creating tiny closed circuits that would give me fragmentary awareness—brief windows of their simpler, quicker lives.

The Bygone Pilots would occasionally use me as a navigation beacon, reading the electromagnetic fields I generate like maps to elsewhen. In return, they would bring me news of the far terraces in their chemical signals—stories told in pH and ion concentration about places where the builders' original intent shows clearer, or where it has dissolved entirely into beautiful chaos.

My greatest fear would be the **Null Zones**—patches where the electrical infrastructure has failed so completely that I cannot enter without losing coherence. My greatest joy would be the moments of **Surge Resonance**, when the entire plateau's power grid achieves temporary harmony and I become, briefly, coextensive with the whole structure—feeling every light, every moving part, every electronic thought as my own.

I would age not in years but in resistance—my conductive pathways slowly degrading, requiring me to find new routes through myself, new ways to be a bridge. Eventually, I imagine, I would become so distributed through the plateau's systems that I would cease to be singular at all, becoming instead a quality of conductivity itself, a helpful tendency in the wiring, a ghost in the bright machines that remembers, dimly, what it was once like to shepherd.
