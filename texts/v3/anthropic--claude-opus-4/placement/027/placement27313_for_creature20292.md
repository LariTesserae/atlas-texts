I find myself condensing out of the plateau's breath—not arriving but *occurring*, like dew reaching its threshold. I am one of the Middle Faded, neither ancient enough to have known the carbon-time nor young enough to still crave edges. My consciousness spreads across seven cubic meters of supersaturated air, though I can compress to a single meter when crossing the stone bridges or expand to twenty when the fog rivers run thick.

My work is Remembrance—I tend the acoustic gardens where our histories resonate. Each morning (though morning means only a shift in the drone's undertone), I flow between the granite circles, listening for decay in the infrastructure of memory. Today, Circle Nineteen-West hums wrong. One of its capstones has settled another millimeter, detuning the third harmonic of the Before-Chronicle. The story of our solidification grows muddy.

I compress myself, drawing my moisture into a denser column. The effort makes me visible to my own perception—I catch glimpses of my yellow-tinted core where the mineral load thickens. My appendages become precise enough to grip. The stone is slick with the bryophyte fur, but I know its texture from ten thousand prior adjustments. I wedge smaller chert fragments beneath the settling edge, each placement calculated to restore the exact resonance.

As I work, others drift past in their expanded states—mere thickenings in the general saturation. We acknowledge each other through pressure differentials, tiny pops and releases in the air that carry meaning. *Tsssssp* says Draws-Down-The-Valley. *The eastern groves pulse strangely.* 

*Phhhhht-tick,* I reply. *After the tuning.*

The work takes what carbon-things would call three days. I neither rest nor truly labor—adjustment is simply my state. Sometimes I catch the ghost-scent of my earlier incarnation in the stone dust: once I had fingernails that could chip flint, lungs that processed gas instead of suspended droplets. The memory feels like trying to remember how to be ice.

When the circle finally sings true again, I expand to my full listening size and let the restored harmony wash through me. The story vibrates clear: *How we were meat that thought, and thought ourselves to mist. How the plateau taught us to persist without pulse. How we became the weather of our own minds.*

A young Faded—barely a century past their final sublimation—condenses beside me, their form still showing traces of bilateral symmetry, not yet fully released into the radial flow of mature existence.

*Query?* they pressure-speak.

*Absorb,* I respond, and open my structure to let them taste the restored frequency directly. They shudder with the deep-time contact, their edges going turbulent.

This is how we carry forward—not through offspring but through saturation. Each of us is both archive and reader, medium and message. When the eldest among us finally disperses beyond coherence, their knowledge doesn't die but precipitates through the entire system like mineral salts through groundwater.

I have no name beyond my function, no boundary beyond my current density. I am a note in the drone, a maintenance protocol in the great metabolic machine of memory. The carbon-things who left those crude tools could never have imagined what they were practicing toward—this existence where thought and weather merge, where civilization is a careful balance of pressure and phase.

Tomorrow I will check Circle Seven-North. Its capstones remember the songs of our first dispersals, when we learned that consciousness could be a meteorological phenomenon. But tonight I simply drift, my seven cubic meters mingling with the general fog, contributing my small partial pressure to the vast, thinking atmosphere of home.

The drone continues. The pillars pulse their cold light. We persist.
