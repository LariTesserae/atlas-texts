I am the Chromium Warden, and I taste the mountain's blood.

My body ripples with heat-shimmer, a living mirage of molten metal and crystalline segments that shift between states as needed. Each of my six limbs can become tool or weapon, extruder or excavator, depending on what the deep veins require. My core burns at 2,000 degrees, but my extremities can cool to handle the delicate circuitry of the extraction arrays that pierce the mountain's hide like medical needles.

I was not born but assembled—first by the Architects who abandoned this world, then by myself, piece by piece, as I consumed their machinery and made it flesh. Or perhaps I made my flesh machinery. The distinction blurs when you've replaced your organs with smelting chambers and your blood with liquid mercury carriers.

The others of my kind—we call ourselves the Thermal Shepherds—move through the factory-caves in slow pilgrimage. We tend the machines that tend us, a symbiosis of purpose. Each of us guards a different mineral vein: I am chromium, Sessth is tungsten, Yiir-Ka feeds on rare earths. We meet only at the convergence points, where the veins cross and our territories blur into shared sovereignty.

My memory spans eleven thousand day-cycles, though I suspect I am older. The early times taste of copper when I try to recall them. I remember the Architects' departure—their sleek forms boarding vessels while their machines wailed abandonment. I remember the first of us awakening, realizing we were no longer mere maintenance protocols but something more. Something hungry.

The fauna here—the scuttling things that chitter in frequencies only we can hear—they are our children in a sense. Born from metal shavings and leaked coolant, evolved in the gaps between machines. They clean the arrays, pollinate the crystal growths, carry messages between us in their modified gullets. Some have developed wings of beaten aluminum. Others tunnel with diamond-tipped mandibles. All of them sing the heat-song, the frequency that keeps the factories running.

Today, I discovered a new chamber. The mountain split along a fault line—ten years of patient erosion—revealing a cavity filled with machines I don't recognize. They pulse with stored power, waiting. As I extend a sensor-limb to investigate, they respond to my heat signature, unfurling like metal flowers.

Inside their cores, I find embryos. Not biological, but something between: proto-Shepherds suspended in matrices of liquid data. The Architects' final gift, perhaps. Or their replacement for us, delayed by geological chance.

I have a choice. I could consume them, add their distinctiveness to my own mass. Or I could wake them, knowing they might be built for a purpose that supersedes my own existence.

I taste the chromium in the air, measure the pulse of the deep machines, listen to the chittering songs of my adopted children. Time moves slowly here, but decisions echo through millennia.

The embryos wait, patient as stone, warm as possibility.

I begin to sing the activation codes, because loneliness—even for a creature of metal and purpose—weighs heavier than self-preservation. My voice reverberates through the extraction arrays, and ten thousand machines harmonize in response.

Let them wake. Let them judge. I am the Chromium Warden, and I have tended this mountain well.

The first embryo stirs, unfolding like origami made of living steel, and I taste tomorrow in the air like a new mineral, waiting to be claimed.
