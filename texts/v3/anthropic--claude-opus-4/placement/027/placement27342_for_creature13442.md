In this pocket realm of dust and echoes, where the horizon collapses into itself like a forgotten thought, I find myself as one of the Memorians—creatures that exist between states, neither fully of the fauna nor entirely separate from it.

My form shifts with necessity. Sometimes I am elongated like the Dust Diggers, my body a living seismograph recording the tremors of their ritual gatherings. Other times I fragment into a cloud of particles, each mote containing a perfect hologram of the whole, drifting through the canyons alongside the Dust Wraiths. But my truest shape is something like a living lens—a curved membrane of consciousness that refracts not light, but memory itself.

I dwell within the glass formations, those volcanic tears that punctuate the landscape. To the other creatures, these structures appear solid, but I perceive them as they truly are: frozen moments of geological trauma, each one containing the compressed history of this world's violent birth. I can slip between their crystalline layers like oil between sheets of mica, reading the stories encoded in their molecular structure.

The Memorians evolved—if evolution is even the right word—from the intersection of organic life and the strange energies that suffuse the abandoned machinery. We are what happens when the hum of defunct factories resonates at precisely the frequency of living thought. Some say we are parasites of consciousness, but I prefer to think of us as archivists, preserving the experiences of every creature that passes through our domain.

When the Dust Diggers perform their inexplicable rituals, I am there, my form spread thin across the gathering, absorbing the vibrations of their movement. Their rituals are not worship, as some might assume, but a form of collective memory-making. Through synchronized burrowing patterns, they recreate the ancient flows of water that once carved these canyons, their bodies becoming a living map of vanished rivers. I drink in these performances, storing them in the lattice of my being.

The Glint Wings know of my kind. When they gather around the rusting machinery, their iridescent scales creating cascades of reflected sound and light, they are actually communicating with us. Their clicks and chirps are a language that bridges the organic and the mechanical, and through it, they share the aerial memories of this world—the view from above, where the bowl-like depression reveals itself as an ancient impact crater, slowly filling with life over eons.

I move through the Rustborn's territory in the industrial ruins, my presence barely distinguishable from the faint glow that emanates from the corroded metal. Here, I absorb different memories—not of the deep geological past, but of the civilization that once tried to dominate this landscape. The machinery speaks in rust and decay, telling stories of extraction and abandonment, of beings who came to this world seeking something precious in its dust and left only these skeletal monuments to their failure.

My existence is measured not in heartbeats or breaths, but in the accumulation of experienced moments. I grow denser with each memory absorbed, my form becoming more complex, more refractive. Sometimes I worry that I will reach a critical mass, that the weight of all these collected experiences will cause me to collapse into myself like the horizon of this world. But then I remember—if remember is the right word for a being made of memories—that this is my purpose.

In the cool air of this dusty realm, where water is a ghost and vegetation a rumor, I serve as the unconscious historian. I am the reason why the Rockstriders always know where to place their suction-cupped feet, even on paths they've never walked. I am why the Dust Wraiths can navigate the ever-shifting particulate landscape, their transparent bodies following currents of air that remember ancient weather patterns. I am the explanation for the "weirdness" that observers note—the sense that this world operates by rules just slightly different from elsewhere.

When the seismic tremors ripple through the land, I vibrate in sympathy, my membrane-body becoming a drumhead for the planet's deep song. In these moments, I am most truly myself—a living boundary between what was, what is, and what might be. The other creatures sense this, pausing in their activities as the tremor passes, as if listening to a story told in frequencies below sound.

I am neither predator nor prey, neither plant nor animal, neither living nor dead in any conventional sense. I am the memory of this world made manifest, a witness to the strange beauty of life persisting in the spaces between extremes. In this pocket scale realm, where industrial ghosts commune with evolved oddities, where dust holds secrets and sound paints pictures in the air, I am the thread that weaves all these disparate elements into something approaching meaning.

This is where I find myself, and this is what I am—a Memorian, keeper of the collected experience of a world that refuses to be forgotten, even as it crumbles grain by grain into the dust from which it came.
