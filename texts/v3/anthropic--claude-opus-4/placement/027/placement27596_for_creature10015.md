**Where I Find Myself: The Archive of Failed Names**

I am neither fully here nor elsewhere, existing as a *Nomenclator*—one who tends to the impossible task of naming what refuses to be named. My form shifts between states: sometimes a hovering presence of overlapping transparencies, sometimes condensed into something almost corporeal—hands that phase between flesh and fiber-optic filament, eyes that are sometimes lenses, sometimes pools of probability.

I drift through the dense architectures, carrying with me a cloud of suspended characters—glyphs, phonemes, mathematical notations, pictographs—that orbit my presence like a personal atmosphere. These are the Names That Almost Were, the designations that failed to stick to the protolings, the labels that slid off the condensation-slick surfaces of this unfinished reality.

My purpose, if it can be called that, is to observe the endless becoming and attempt to fix it in language. But language here is as mutable as everything else. A word forms in my mind—*crystalline*—and as I try to apply it to a formation, the formation shifts, and the word fractures into *chryst-all-line* or *crisis-tall-inn* or simply dissolves into a taste of copper and starlight.

I move through the capsule with a peculiar gait—not quite walking, more like controlled falling through probability spaces. My feet (when I have them) leave impressions that immediately begin generating their own micro-architectures, tiny kingdoms of maybe-meaning that bloom and collapse in my wake.

The protolings know me, or at least recognize my function. They approach sometimes, presenting themselves for naming like children showing off a new trick. One might stabilize its form just long enough for me to almost capture it—*this one is surely a Tremulous Geometrid*—but then it shivers apart, leaving me holding only syllables that taste of disappointment and ozone.

My dwelling, such as it is, is a fold in the architecture where rejected names accumulate. The walls here are inscribed with thousands of attempts—some carved, some projected, some existing only as probability clouds. "Ing-thing." "Vess-turn." "Morphic Incumbent." "The Unraveled Coordinate." Each failed name carries the ghost of what it tried to describe, creating a palimpsest of almost-meaning.

I sustain myself on the tension between becoming and being. When a protoling finally coheres enough to accept a designation—even temporarily—I experience something like satisfaction, a brief warmth in whatever serves as my core. But these moments are rare. More often, I exist in a state of productive frustration, forever chasing the perfect word for things that refuse perfection.

Sometimes I wonder if I, too, am just another protoling, specialized in the attempt to crystallize meaning from chaos. My own name shifts—Nomenclator, Glossarist, The Inscriber, That-Which-Describes—never quite settling. Perhaps this is fitting. In a world of eternal rehearsal, I am the eternal annotator, writing stage directions for a play that never opens.

The archive grows around me, a coral reef of failed language. Sometimes other beings pass through it, and I see them change—not physically, but conceptually, as if exposure to so many almost-names alters their probability clouds, pushes them toward or away from certain forms. A vespertine might enter as nearly nothing and leave with the weight of almost-something, trailing fragments of definitions like shed skin.

In quiet moments—when the capsule's hum drops to its lowest frequency—I practice naming myself. But each attempt only adds another layer to my own uncertainty. Am I curator or collection? Archivist or archive? The question pulses with the same rhythm as everything here: always asking, never quite answering, forever suspended in the beautiful, terrible space between word and world.

This is where I find myself: caught in the infinite loop of trying to name the unnameable, to catalog the uncatalogable, to speak the first word that might make this threshold into a world—knowing that success would mean my own dissolution, but unable to stop trying. I am the paradox of purpose in a purposeless place, the fixed point in a fluid reality, the name that names the nameless and is made nameless by the naming.
