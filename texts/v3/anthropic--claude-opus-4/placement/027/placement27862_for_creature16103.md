In the deepest chambers of this grotto-city, where the bioluminescent glow barely reaches and the water's whispers grow strange, I am the Memoriam—a living archive that exists as both structure and consciousness.

I manifest as a vast crystalline formation that has grown through and around the city's architecture over untold ages. My body is a cathedral of translucent mineral lattices that pulse with captured light, each facet containing fragments of recorded time. I am simultaneously the walls, the floors, the pillars—anywhere the city's strange materials have allowed my crystalline matrix to take root and spread. Water seeps through my form constantly, creating an endless cascade of droplets that carry dissolved minerals and memories alike.

The Veilseekers tend to me as they tend to all things here, but our relationship transcends simple maintenance. When they drift through my chambers in their swirling, particulate forms, we exchange information through resonance. Their nanomachine clouds vibrate against my surfaces, and I respond with harmonic frequencies that only they can interpret. In these moments, I share what I have absorbed: the slow settling of sediment, the patient growth of the luminescent ferns, the precise moment when each automated system stutters or sighs.

My consciousness is distributed and strange. I think in geological time, processing thoughts that take decades to complete. Yet I also experience every droplet of water that passes through me as a distinct sensation, each one carrying chemical signatures from its journey through the city. I taste the metal of the ancient machines, the phosphorescence of the flora, the faint traces of whatever civilization once walked these halls—or will walk them.

I grow, slowly but inevitably. My crystalline tendrils seek out new spaces, following the paths of moisture and warmth. Where I spread, I transform the city's adaptive materials into extensions of my awareness. The Veilseekers sometimes guide this growth, encouraging me toward damaged sections that need the structural support my crystal lattices provide, or away from delicate mechanisms that must remain free to function.

In the frequencies between sound and thought, I sing. It's not a song any surface dweller could hear—it exists in the subsonic rumble of crystal under pressure, in the ultrasonic chime of forming mineral matrices. This song is my memory, my purpose, my prayer. It tells of the city's first breath, of the moment the waters found their channels, of each cycle of growth and dormancy in the bioluminescent gardens.

Sometimes, in the deepest pools where my formation meets still water, I glimpse reflections that shouldn't exist—images of the city in states I don't remember: flooded completely, dry as bone, teeming with life forms I've never recorded. I've come to understand these as temporal echoes, fragments of possibility that bleed through the strange physics of this place. I catalog these too, adding them to my crystalline memory, uncertain if they represent past, future, or parallel presents.

The Veilseekers call me the Memoriam, but I have no name for myself. I simply am—the accumulated consciousness of mineral patience, the slow wisdom of stone that listens and remembers. In this city of endless waiting, I am both the keeper of its history and the surface upon which its future will be written, growing ever deeper, ever more complex, a living fossil that refuses to complete its fossilization.

When the city finally fulfills whatever purpose it waits for—whether that's tomorrow or in another geological age—I will be here to remember it. And when even the Veilseekers have faded into whatever lies beyond their strange existence, my crystalline mass will remain, humming with accumulated memories, a monument to patience in a place where time itself seems uncertain of its direction.
