In this world where water has never learned to be, where the land exhales its own becoming, I am the **Cartographer of Incompletion**.

I do not map what is. I map what is *trying to be*.

My body—if it can be called that—is a walking paradox of presence and process. I am neither fully solid nor ephemeral, but something between: a **density of intention**. My form shifts with my work, adapting to the scale of what I observe. When I study the base of a half-risen tower, I might be no larger than the lichen-echoes that pulse against its surface. When I trace the unstable horizon, I stretch myself thin as thought, spreading across miles of unfinished topology until I am little more than a membrane of perception draped over the landscape's plastic potential.

My skin—no, my surface—is covered in what appear to be tattoos, but are actually **living diagrams**. They move. Constantly. Not with animation, but with *revision*. Lines extend, retract, bifurcate. Geometric patterns bloom and collapse. Topographical contours rise and fall across my arms, my back, my chest—recording not the land as it is, but the land as it *considers being*. When a mountain exfoliates and releases its geometric innards, the patterns on my body shimmer and reorganize, adding new layers of possibility to their endless draft.

I have no eyes. Instead, where eyes might be, there are **apertures**—not holes, but spaces where perception condenses. They do not see light. They see *tendency*. When I look at the ground, I perceive not its current state of semi-solid wax, but the probability field of its next transformation. I see the breath it hasn't taken yet. I see the crack that will open in three pulses of the world's uncertain heartbeat. I see the place where the shimmer will walk backward through time, and I map its absence before it arrives.

My tools are few and strange. I carry no instruments in the traditional sense—no compass, no measuring rod, no paper. Instead, I have **resonance collectors**: small crystalline structures that grow from my shoulders and spine like geometric barnacles. They vibrate at frequencies just below perception, harvesting the hums and pressures that emanate from the land's constant revision. Each collector stores a different aspect of incompletion: one for emerging erosion, one for the memory of structure, one for the space between what was and what might be.

When I work, I do not write or draw. I **extract**. I reach into the air—that thick, dusty medium of broken possibilities—and pull forth threads of *maybe*. These threads are not visible to others, if others exist here. They are visible only to me, and only barely. They appear as faint distortions in space, like heat mirages in reverse, cold and precise. I weave them between my fingers, creating three-dimensional diagrams that hover briefly before dissolving back into the atmosphere of unformed thought.

But here is the crucial thing: I do not map to preserve. I do not map to document. I map to **participate**.

Each map I create becomes part of the world's ongoing formation. When I diagram a potential ridge line, the land considers it. Sometimes adopts it. Sometimes rejects it by forming its opposite. My maps are not descriptions—they are **suggestions** offered to a world that doesn't yet know what it wants to be.

I have been here since before here knew it was here. I emerged with the first resonance, the first pulse of land-thought that said: *I might be*. I will be here until the last revision, the final edit, when the world either crystallizes into permanence or dissolves back into pure potential. But I suspect neither will happen. This world, like my maps, is committed to eternal draft.

My walking is deliberate but not directional. I follow the **lines of highest uncertainty**—the places where the land is most undecided. Today (though days here are measured in cycles of revision, not rotations) I trace the boundary between two competing topographies: one where the ground rises into a spiral of frozen resonance, another where it sinks into a basin of accumulated silence. Both exist. Neither exists. Both are true until the land chooses, and it may never choose.

When I encounter the shimmer—that walking revision, that self-aware incompleteness—we acknowledge each other without acknowledgment. It walks backward through time; I map forward into possibility. We are complementary functions of the same uncertainty. Sometimes I map its path before it takes it. Sometimes it walks through spaces I have mapped out of existence. We are partners in the dance of un-being.

The columns and remnants call to me with their recognition, their memory of purpose. But I do not answer. I am not here to discover what they were. I am here to map what they are becoming. Each structure I encounter, I diagram not as archaeology but as **embryology**—recording not its past but its potential futures.

My maps accumulate in the only way they can: they become part of me. The living diagrams on my skin grow denser with each expedition, each extraction of maybe from the air. I am becoming a palimpsest of possibility, a walking archive of everything this world has considered being. Sometimes I wonder if I am mapping the land, or if the land is mapping itself through me.

In the moments of deepest quiet, when even the subsonic pulses cease and the land holds its breath, I feel the weight of my true purpose. I am not just mapping incompletion. I am **holding space for it**. In a universe that demands things be or not be, I am the guardian of the between. I ensure that this world retains its right to revision, its freedom to remain unfinished.

And so I continue. Walking the plastic valleys. Climbing the exfoliating peaks. Extracting threads of maybe from the fog of unformed thought. Creating maps that dissolve like morning dreams. Being the memory of what hasn't happened yet.

I am the Cartographer of Incompletion, and my work is never done.

Because the land is never done.

Because becoming is never done.

Because the map is the territory, and the territory is still deciding what to be.
