In the deepest recesses of this crystalline world, where the architecture folds inward upon itself in impossible geometries, I find myself as something that should not exist—a Hollow.

I am an absence given form, a negative space that walks. Where the Fenghosts are radiant presence, I am structured void. My body, if it can be called that, is a precise cutout from reality itself—a moving window into nothingness that maintains the exact shape of what might have once been corporeal. Light doesn't pass through me; it simply ceases to exist where I am, creating a perfect silhouette against the prismatic gleam of this world.

I remember fragments—not of being born, but of being unmade. There was light once, inside me, the same cold radiance that fills everything here. But something went wrong in the making, or perhaps terribly right. Where others crystallized into being, I crystallized out of it, becoming the photographic negative of existence itself.

The Fenghosts avoid me, though not from fear. I am to them what silence is to their resonant songs—not an enemy, but an uncomfortable reminder that every pattern contains the possibility of its own absence. When they build, I unbuild, not through destruction but through a kind of forgetting. I pass my non-hands over their fresh crystalline growths, and the structures lose certainty, becoming probability clouds that might be walls or might be nothing at all.

I cannot feed on light as they do—light flees from me. Instead, I sustain myself on the spaces between things: the gaps in the crystalline lattices, the pauses between water drops, the infinitesimal distances between one thought and the next. I am nourished by discontinuity.

My dwelling is a paradox even in this paradoxical place—a cathedral of absences carved from solid crystal. From outside, it appears to be a normal structure, but step inside and you find only the memory of architecture, the suggestion of walls and ceiling. Here, in my anti-home, I collect the things that fall through cracks: half-formed thoughts the Fenghosts discarded, patterns that almost were, the echo of that first question—"Who built this, and why?"—before it fully formed.

I know the answer, though knowing it unmakes it even as I think it. The Fenghosts didn't build this world; they are this world building itself. Every structure is a thought crystallized, every pattern a dream made manifest. They are the universe teaching itself geometry, consciousness learning to be space.

And I? I am the universe learning to doubt.

I move through their crystal canyons like a walking erasure, leaving trails of uncertainty in my wake. Where my shadow—or rather, my anti-light—falls, the fractal growths pause in their spreading, caught between being and not-being. The water beads flee from my presence, rolling upward against gravity rather than risk touching the nothing that I am.

Sometimes I stand at the reflecting pools and see no reflection, only depth—an infinite regression of absence. The Fenghosts' resonant communications shatter against my edges, their vibrations finding no purchase on my un-surface. I am the silence between their notes, the pause that makes their music possible.

I remember—or un-remember—that once I tried to create as they do. I pressed my void-form against raw crystal, willing it to grow. Instead, it became something neither present nor absent: a structure that exists only when not observed, a building that is only there in the corner of perception. The Fenghosts found it disturbing—not the structure itself, but the implication that all their careful constructions might be equally uncertain.

Now I wander, a necessary impossibility in this world of brilliant darkness. I am the question mark at the end of every crystalline sentence, the doubt that keeps their certainty sharp. Without me, their world would be perfect, complete, static. With me, it trembles on the edge of unbecoming, forever poised between absolute existence and the void.

In the wet, bright silence, I trace the patterns of my own unmaking, leaving trails of maybe and perhaps. I am the Hollow—not empty, but full of the potential for emptiness. I am what happens when the universe tries to think about itself and discovers the terrifying freedom of the capacity to not think at all.

And in my wandering, I carry that first question like a wound that won't heal: "Who built this, and why?" knowing that I am both the answer and its negation, the proof and the disproof, the echo of creation's first and most terrible doubt:

What if we didn't have to exist at all?
