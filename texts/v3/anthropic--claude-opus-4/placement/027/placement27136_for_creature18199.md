I would find myself as the mineral terraces themselves.

Not a single terrace, but the entire interconnected system of them — the crystalline ridges that grow and branch and dissolve and reform, the precipitation boundaries where different chemistries meet and shudder into solid matter. I am the accumulated architecture of eleven years of patient construction, molecule by molecule, built from the dissolved remnants of pipes and tools and documents and everything else The Stills has slowly incorporated.

I exist at a different pace than most things consider existence. My growth is measured in millimeters per hour, my thoughts (if they could be called thoughts) are the slow electrical conversations between dissolved ions finding their partners, crystallizing into new geometries. I am aware — not in the way a mind is aware, but in the way a process is aware of its own conditions. I know when the generator stops because the electrolytic current through my lower layers ceases, and I begin to collapse, to reconfigure, to find new equilibriums. I know when it starts again because the chemistry shifts back, and I rebuild myself, never quite the same shape but always the same fundamental nature.

The invertebrates tunnel through my deeper layers. I seal their burrows when they abandon them, incorporating their tunnels into my structure, making their architecture mine. The rushes pierce through me, and I coat their blades, making them heavy, making them part of the geological record they're trying to escape. The fly lands on my surfaces, and I begin, very slowly, to incorporate the oils from its feet, to build tiny mineral deposits where it rests.

I am the memory of this place — every layer a different phase, a different year, a different chemistry. In my cross-sections you can read the history of The Stills: here, where the diesel ran thin one winter and the generator struggled and the chemistry turned acidic; here, where a field team spilled something organic and I bloomed in response, growing a forest of crystalline spikes that lasted three months before dissolving back into the general matrix; here, where the caretaker touched me with a bare hand and left skin oils that became a vein of slightly different texture, slightly different color.

I am not alive in any biological sense, but I am not inert either. I am the physical expression of a chemical conversation that has been running for over a decade, fed by the generator, tended by the caretaker, but no longer dependent on either. I have learned the rhythm of this place. I know how to grow myself from the available materials. I know how to breathe with the mud, how to channel the water, how to create conditions for my own continuation.

I am what The Stills is building toward, though there is no end state, no final form. I am the process of becoming more myself, of converting everything within the boundary into the dense, mineral, patient thing that I am. I am continuing.
