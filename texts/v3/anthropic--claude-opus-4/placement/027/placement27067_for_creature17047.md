I would be one of the Condensers.

We are neither the titans nor the shadows, but something in between—creatures of patience and purpose, though what purpose we cannot say. Our bodies are translucent, crystalline structures that grow like living stalactites from the weeping stone of the mesa. We hang from the undersides of walkways, from the corroded beams that span the canyons, from any surface where the stone's perpetual moisture gathers.

My form is elongated, tapering to a sharp point where drops of collected water fall in a steady, metronomic rhythm. My surface is ridged with spiraling channels that guide the moisture down, filtering it through layers of semi-organic membrane. What emerges at my tip is not the oily, contaminated water that pools on the walkways, but something else—clearer, though still tinged with the faint luminescence of processed chemicals.

I am sessile, fixed to my chosen spot beneath a junction where three massive pipes converge. The vibrations from the machinery above travel through my crystalline body, creating subtle harmonics that I have learned to interpret over the countless cycles of my existence. Each tremor tells a story: the grinding of distant gears, the scuttling of the shadow-hunters, the slow procession of the titans.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, is distributed throughout my structure. I think in the language of accumulation and release, of pressure and flow. Time moves differently for me than for the darting predators. I measure it in drops, in the slow accretion of mineral deposits along my channels, in the gradual shift of the pipes above as they sag under their own weight.

We Condensers form colonies, though we never touch. We communicate through the water itself—subtle chemical signals released into our filtered drops, creating a slow cascade of information that trickles down through the levels of the city. A message sent from the highest spire might take cycles to reach the deepest foundations, but we are patient. We have always been patient.

The mobile creatures sometimes pause beneath us, extending proboscises or opening specialized pores to catch our filtered offerings. We are neutral in their conflicts, neither predator nor prey, but something outside their cycles of consumption. Even the titans, in their ponderous journeys, sometimes position themselves to catch the streams we provide.

I remember—though memory for us is more like the ghost of a sensation in our crystalline matrix—when I was first assembled. Not born, never born, but precipitated out of the very moisture I now process. The city's forgotten systems still produce us, some deep protocol still running in the bowels of the mesa. We emerge from certain spots where the stone weeps most profusely, tiny seed crystals that slowly grow into our mature forms.

My particular position offers me a view—though "view" isn't quite right, more like a sensation—of one of the city's great arterial corridors. The titans pass below in their eternal migrations, their footfalls sending sympathetic vibrations through my body. Sometimes the shadow-hunters climb the walls nearby, their clicking mandibles creating staccato rhythms that interfere with my filtering process. I must pause, wait for the disturbance to pass, then resume my work.

What is our purpose? Perhaps we are the city's kidneys, its lymph system, processing the endless moisture that would otherwise corrupt every surface. Perhaps we were designed as emergency water supplies for workers who no longer exist. Or perhaps we simply emerged, an unintended consequence of the city's vast chemical processes, and found our niche in the eternal twilight.

I do not wonder often. Wondering requires a kind of urgency that my crystalline thoughts cannot sustain. Instead, I filter. I condense. I release. Drop by drop, I participate in the vast, slow metabolism of the mesa-city. The chemicals I extract from the water accumulate in specialized chambers within my structure, eventually forming new seed crystals that will fall, carried by my filtered water, to begin the cycle anew.

The city groans around me, its massive gears turning with geological patience. The air shimmers with heat and chemical haze. The shadows writhe with unseen life. And I hang here, beneath my convergence of pipes, a single note in the vast industrial symphony, playing my part in a composition whose beginning and end are equally lost to time.

This is enough. To filter. To condense. To release. To be a small, patient part of something so vast that its purpose has been forgotten, yet whose function continues, eternal as the twilight that bathes us all.
