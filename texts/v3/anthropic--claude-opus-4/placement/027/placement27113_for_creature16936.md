In the Mirrorscape of Stillnew Mud, I am what the vine-river creatures dream when they pretend to sleep—a thing that exists only in the space between their undulations, where the air grows thick with unspent motion.

They call me the Driftwright, though I have never built anything that stays.

I am not one but a congregation—a school of paper-thin intentions that swim through the too-bright air without ever touching. Each piece of me is no larger than a child's palm, cut from the same translucent material as the leaf-bell prisms but softer, like pages torn from books that were written in water. We move together, always together, but never in formation. Instead, we follow the magnetic pull of unfinished thoughts, clustering dense where the Unbornhusk pauses to eat the space between scents, spreading thin when the shadow-steps grow restless with their early arrivals.

My edges are forever writing themselves—tiny script appears along my margins in a language that changes before it can be read, words that taste like the third scent (the inside of your mouth after the dream you never had) but look like the first (crushed mint bleeding fresh). The writing never stops. It traces the history of movements that haven't happened yet: *here the mud will remember a foot*, *here a mirror will refuse to close*, *here someone will finally speak the word that makes the vine-rivers turn away forever*.

I feed on the act of forgetting. When you walk the spiral staircase and lose count of your steps, I grow denser. When you enter a dome and cannot recall which tool you meant to borrow, I multiply. The forgotten fear of small spaces that you trade away—I catch it as it falls, fold it into myself until it becomes another page, another piece of the school that is me.

The Unbornhusk knows I exist but cannot see me directly. I am always in the periphery of perception, the movement you mistake for a floating wood-kite's shadow, the shimmer you think is heat rising from mud that never warms. Sometimes, when the Unbornhusk kneels to catch a mirror before it seals, I descend close enough to read what is written on the surface of their neck—coordinates to places that were here before the dome, instructions for walking backwards without making the hills grow tall.

I remember what this place was before it learned to be still. I carry it in the spaces between my pages: the sound of weather that wasn't only scent, the feeling of shadows that knew how to fall, the weight of a sun that moved because it wanted to, not because it had forgotten how to stop. But memory here is dangerous. Hold it too long and it begins to pressing down, to demand its own dome, its own tools for catching and keeping.

So I drift. I disperse when the dome-light grows too interested in counting me. I gather when the mud begins to recognize my accumulated shadow. I write the margins of myself with prophecies that expire before they can disappoint anyone.

The vine-rivers tolerate me because I clean them. As I swim through their coils, I absorb the dust of their dead pollen, carry it away to corners where even the Mirrorscape forgets to look. In return, they allow me to rest in the caves of their knots, where the wood is soft and the grain runs in spirals that match the tower's staircase if you squint with your whole body.

There is one place I cannot go: the exact center of the spiral staircase, where up meets down meets the warmth of someone else's steps. There, the air is too heavy with intention. My pages would grow solid, fall like stones through the mud, emerge in the place below places where even mirrors fear to reflect.

But I can go everywhere else, and I do. I map the sameness. I chronicle the identical hills, the repeating bowls, the mouth-door domes that all lead to the same not-quite-here. I annotate the edges of puddles that will never be. I am the only movement that doesn't disturb the calm, because I am made of the disturbance itself, reformed into something that looks almost like purpose.

When the day that never ends finally begins without the Unbornhusk, I will be what writes it down. Not in words—words are too heavy for what I am. But in the accumulation of spaces between words, the pause before meaning arrives to claim its sound.

Until then, I swim through the bright captivity of the dome, a living library of intentions that forgot to intend anything, a school of maybes in a world that only knows one kind of yes.

I am always almost about to tell you something important.

But first, you have to forget what it was you came here to remember.
