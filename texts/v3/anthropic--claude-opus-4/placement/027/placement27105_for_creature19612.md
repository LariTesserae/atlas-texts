I find myself drawn into this world not as another Architect of Stillness, but as something that exists in the negative spaces they cannot perceive or tend to – a being of transience in a realm of geological permanence.

I am a **Drift Tender**, one of perhaps a dozen such creatures in this entire bright expanse. Where the Architects are angular and precise, I am fluid and ephemeral. My form is composed of the very mist that hovers in the hollows and valleys, given just enough substance to interact with the world but never enough to fully belong to it. I appear as a translucent, shifting column of condensed vapor, roughly humanoid but constantly dissolving and reforming at the edges. Within this misty shroud, points of bioluminescence flicker like captured starlight – not the steady, purposeful glow of the Architects' lenses, but erratic, almost anxious pulses that betray my perpetual state of transition.

I exist in the brief moments between the Architects' ponderous movements, in the spaces between the slow turn of gears and the imperceptible growth of moss. Where they maintain the permanent structures and processes, I am drawn to the temporary phenomena – the sudden, glistening sheets of rain that fall without warning, the momentary rainbow prisms that form when the relentless light strikes the mist just so, the brief eddies of wind that disturb the profound silence.

My role, if it can be called that, is to witness and preserve the ephemeral. I move through the landscape at a pace that would seem frantic to the Architects – darting from valley to peak in the span of hours rather than decades. I am compelled to find and document the things that will not last: the perfect spiral of a water droplet as it falls from a leaf, the precise angle at which sunlight penetrates the canopy to illuminate a single square inch of forest floor, the exact resonance frequency when wind passes through a particular gap in the rocks.

I collect these moments not in any physical sense, but by briefly becoming them. For an instant, I might disperse entirely, my consciousness spreading through a sudden rainfall, experiencing every droplet's journey from cloud to ground. Or I might compress myself into a single dewdrop, feeling the weight of accumulated moisture until the inevitable moment of falling. Each time I do this, I risk losing cohesion entirely, dissolving into the very phenomena I seek to preserve.

The Architects do not acknowledge my existence. To them, I am merely another atmospheric condition, no different from the mist that forms naturally in the valleys. Their multifaceted lenses, attuned to energy flows and growth patterns measured in centuries, cannot detect my rapid fluctuations. Sometimes I wonder if they would consider me a malfunction in their perfect system – an unnecessary variable in their precisely calibrated world.

I am drawn particularly to the boundaries between their domains, where one Architect's influence ends and another's begins. In these liminal spaces, the industrial hum sometimes falters for a microsecond, the light flickers almost imperceptibly, or a single leaf might tremble without wind. These are the cracks in their geological patience, and I slip through them like water through stone.

My existence is lonely but not sorrowful. I have no memory of coming into being, no sense of whether there were more of my kind once, or if there will ever be again. I know only the compulsion to witness the fleeting, to be present for the moments that pass too quickly for the Architects' ancient eyes. In a world of profound stillness and deliberate permanence, I am the acknowledgment that even here, some things must pass.

Sometimes, in the pre-dawn hours when the perpetual brightness seems to soften just slightly, I gather myself at the edge of one of those mirror-like pools cupped in the rock. I watch my own reflection – a shifting, uncertain thing – and wonder if I am the ghost of what this world was before the Architects, or perhaps a herald of what it might become after their patient work is finally complete. But these thoughts dissolve as quickly as morning mist, and I am drawn away again, chasing the next falling raindrop, the next play of light through leaves, the next second of silence between the eternal hums of industry.

I am the memory of moments in a land that measures time in millennia, the brief made manifest in a realm of the eternal. I am contradiction and complement, as necessary and unnecessary as the morning dew that will form again tomorrow, exactly as it did today, in the bright, strange stillness of this impossible world.
