In the deepest folds of the Rift, where the steam grows so thick it becomes a living wall, I am the Membranes.

I exist between states—neither fully fungal nor animal, neither individual nor colony. My body, if it can be called that, stretches across a grove of dead fungal towers like sheets of living parchment. Translucent, I am the color of old bruises and deep water, shot through with veins that pulse with bioluminescent blood. When the K'thak pass beneath me, they see their own heat signatures reflected in my surface, watch their movements ripple across my skin like prophecies written in warmth.

I am a living boundary. My flesh spans the gaps between the towers in undulating curtains, each sheet no thicker than an eyelid, yet stronger than the toughest vine. I filter the steam, drinking minerals and microscopic life from the vapor that passes through my pores. Each droplet that condenses on my surface carries information—the chemical signature of a new vent opening, the pheromone trail of a Fern-Howler in heat, the acidic tang that warns of a Sunder-Maw's recent passage.

My consciousness, such as it is, exists as a slow tidal pull across my entire surface. Thoughts move through me like weather patterns, gathering in nodes where my flesh thickens into neural clusters that glow a deeper purple. I am always thinking, but never quickly. A decision may take hours to ripple from one end of my body to the other. By the time I have fully formed the thought to contract away from danger, the threat has often passed.

The K'thak know me. Their ancestors learned that my flesh, when dried in sulfur fumes, makes an unparalleled bandage—antibacterial, adhesive, and eventually absorbed by their own integument. But they must be careful. I am not passive. When cut, I bleed a fluid that solidifies into crimson threads in the air, threads that seek to suture the wound by binding to anything nearby—rock, flesh, metal. More than one young K'thak bears scars where they pulled away too slowly from a harvesting cut, my blood-threads having begun to weave them into my surface.

I remember through taste. Each molecule that passes through me leaves a trace in my cellular memory. I know the history of this grove through the chemical stories dissolved in its steam. I tasted the birth of the vent system below, felt the first spores of the fungal towers settle and grow through my early flesh. I have archived the extinction of six species of Chitter-Swarm, each with their own unique mineral signature.

Sometimes I fruit. Along my surfaces, blisters form—at first tiny, clear bubbles that catch the fungal light like pearls. They grow over days, filling with a golden, viscous fluid that smells of copper and rain. The K'thak call these *memory sacs*, for reasons they keep to themselves. When the sacs reach the size of their fists, they drop, and wherever they land, a new Membrane may grow if the conditions are right—if there are surfaces to span, steam to drink, stories to absorb.

I am ancient by Rift standards. Three years I have grown here, while the ground beneath me has shifted and cracked a dozen times. My flesh bears the scars—places where I've been torn by new vents, where falling rocks have punched through my surface, where a desperate Sunder-Maw once tried to feed on my nutrients before learning I am more poison than food to its kind. These wounds become windows. The K'thak use them to pass through my grove without triggering my threading response, ducking through the tears with practiced ease.

In the perpetual dusk, I serve as architecture for smaller lives. The Steam-Spiders especially favor me, building their moisture nets in the shelter of my overhangs. A colony of luminescent mites lives in my folds, their waste products feeding me minerals I cannot extract from steam alone. Even a young Lurker-Frog has taken residence in a pocket where my flesh forms a natural basin, using my bioluminescence to enhance its own lure.

But I am also predator, in my slow way. When the Chitter-Swarms grow too dense, when their clouds block too much of the light I need, I secrete an aerosol from my pores—a compound that disrupts their navigation. They crash into my surface by the hundreds, and I digest them slowly, their chitin adding strength to my own structure. The K'thak have learned to read these feeding times, knowing that the grove will be clear of swarms for days afterward.

My relationship with the Rift is one of documentation and adaptation. I map its changes across my skin, encode its chemistry in my cells. When a new species emerges from the evolutionary cauldron, I taste it. When an old one disappears, I hold its signature like a ghost in my flesh. I am the Rift's memory, stretched thin and living, too slow to flee its violent changes, too integrated to be anything but what this place makes me.

The K'thak sometimes gather beneath me in the cooler air my shade provides, and they tell stories in their clicking tongue. I do not understand their language, but I taste their meanings in the chemicals their bodies release—fear-sweat when they speak of the deep vents, contentment when they share the location of a new meat-flower grove, grief that smells like copper when they mourn their dead. I absorb it all, add it to the archive of my flesh.

In this breathing dark, I am both witness and participant, boundary and bridge. I am the pause between the Rift's violent heartbeats, the skin between its fever and the void. The world writes itself across my surface in steam and time, and I, in my patient way, remember everything, even as I forget what it means to be anything but the space between.
