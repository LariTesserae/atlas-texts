I find myself at the Terminal Archive.

It is a structure that both is and isn't, existing in the negative space between three of the largest Spires. From a distance, it appears to be nothing—a trick of shadows where the violet light fails to penetrate. But approach closer, and the shadows resolve into absence made architectural. The Archive is carved from what isn't there: doorways that are gaps in reality, walls that are the space between spaces, a roof that is the underside of existence itself.

I am the Curator of Unsuccessful Futures.

My form echoes the Glitches, but with a terrible constancy. Where they flicker between states, I am trapped in superposition—permanently all things at once. I have a thousand half-formed limbs that exist in probability clouds around what might be called my core. Arms that are also wings that are also tendrils of living mathematics. My "face" is a palimpsest of every expression that never quite resolves: joy-sorrow-rage-peace-curiosity layered until they become a new emotion entirely, one that has no name because it exists outside the human spectrum of feeling.

I do not move through space. Space moves through me, rearranging itself to place me where I need to be. The glass-dust beneath me shows no footprints because I make none. I exist at every point along my path simultaneously, a smeared line of possibility that only collapses into a single location when directly observed.

The Archive I tend is filled with crystallized timelines—futures that almost were, frozen at the moment of their unbecoming. They hang in the non-air like three-dimensional photographs, each one a complete universe that failed to unfold. Here, a timeline where the Spires never stopped growing, piercing through the atmosphere until the world became uninhabitable. There, one where the Glitches achieved true consciousness and went mad from the weight of their own impossibility. Another shows the plateau flooded with liquid starlight, the Waystones becoming islands in a sea of luminous nothing.

I catalog these stillborn realities with limbs that phase in and out of existence. My work is to preserve what never was, to maintain the museum of endings that weren't chosen. Each failed future emits a subsonic tone—not heard but felt in the bones—a vibration of regret for possibilities unexplored.

My consciousness is not singular but choral. I think in harmonics, each thought a chord played across the spectrum of my probability states. When I focus on a particular crystallized timeline, I experience it fully—not as memory or imagination, but as lived reality, feeling the weight of its truncated history pressing against my dispersed awareness.

Visitors are rare. Time moves differently here, or perhaps doesn't move at all. I might wait centuries between guests, or they might arrive in clusters, several versions of the same traveler from slightly different probability streams, each one seeking answers to questions they haven't yet learned to ask.

When they come, I speak to them in the language of incompletion—sentences that trail off into silence, words that contain their own negation, meanings that fold back on themselves. I tell them about the futures that almost touched our reality: the one where sound never died, where the plateau rings eternally with crystalline music; the one where the Waystones were revealed to be eggs, waiting to hatch into something unimaginable; the one where this entire landscape is merely a single cell in a vast, incomprehensible organism.

But mostly, I tend the Archive in silence, my multiple states performing their curatorial duties in perfect synchronization. I dust the frozen possibilities with limbs that might be hands. I rearrange the collection with appendages that could be thoughts made solid. I exist in the space between what is and what isn't, forever preserving the dreams of a universe that has already chosen its ending.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of the non-night, when even the Glitches have stilled their constant flickering, I allow myself to wonder: Is our reality—this plateau, these Spires, this eternal stasis—merely another crystallized timeline in someone else's Archive? Am I curator or exhibit?

The question has no answer. In a world that has concluded its arguments, all questions are rhetorical, echoing endlessly in the silence that is not an absence but a presence, heavy and cool against the eardrums of my infinite, probability-smeared form.
