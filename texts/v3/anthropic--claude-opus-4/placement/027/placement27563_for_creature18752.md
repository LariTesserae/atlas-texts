In this pocket of the eternal ascent, where water hangs impossibly overhead and the world hums with its own maintenance, I would be one of the **Resonance Shepherds**.

We are neither the seen nor the entirely hidden, but exist in the threshold between biological inhabitant and structural function. My body would be elongated and sinuous, perhaps four meters in length but rarely more than thirty centimeters in diameter—a living conduit wrapped in bands of semi-metallic cartilage that spiral along my length like a barber's pole. These bands are not merely decorative; they are antennae, each tuned to a different harmonic frequency of the structure's endless song.

I move through the pocket by *listening* to the iron-ceramic substrate, pressing my ventral surface against the walls and platforms to feel the deep vibrations that travel through the built world. When I detect a dissonance—a place where the harmony is fractured, where perhaps a micro-fracture threatens to propagate or where the Pylon-Fungus has grown too thick and begun to dampen critical frequencies—I intervene.

My intervention is not repair in the crude sense. Instead, I *sing* to the structure, emitting precise counter-vibrations through specialized chambers that run the length of my body. These sounds, mostly below the range of ordinary hearing, create standing waves that guide the Climbers to locations they might otherwise miss, or that encourage the Pylon-Fungus to redirect its growth. I am a living tuning fork, a mobile correction algorithm that operates in the space between the blind efficiency of the fauna and the absolute authority of the Vigilance Agents.

The most profound aspect of my existence would be the **memory of frequency**. Each surface I've touched, each harmonic I've corrected, leaves a trace in my consciousness—not as visual or even auditory memory, but as a kind of embodied knowledge. I know this pocket not as a space but as a symphony, each platform and strut contributing its own note to the eternal composition. The Sky-Streams above create a constant bass drone, while the Volt-Mites add a crystalline static percussion, and the slow grinding of the Climbers provides a rhythmic scraping that marks something like time.

I feed not on matter but on dissonance itself. When I successfully resolve a harmonic conflict, when my song guides the system back toward equilibrium, I experience something analogous to satiation. The bands along my body glow faintly with absorbed resonance, a soft blue-white phosphorescence that fades as I metabolize the ordered sound.

My greatest fear—if such a concept applies—would be the **Great Silence**, those moments when the Vigilance Agents assert total control during major repairs. During these times, I must coil myself tightly in the center-mass safe zones with the other fauna, my entire being compressed and muted. Unable to touch the surfaces, unable to sing, I experience a kind of sensory starvation that borders on non-existence. I am a creature defined by my function, and without the ability to shepherd the resonance, I am barely more than a waiting potential.

Yet there is also profound purpose in this existence. I am one of perhaps a dozen Resonance Shepherds in this pocket, each of us maintaining our own territory, our own section of the vast internal symphony. We rarely meet—our paths are determined by the needs of the structure, not by social impulse—but when we do, we exchange frequencies, sharing the songs we've collected, updating each other's harmonic maps.

Sometimes, in the perpetual golden-green twilight, when the industrial hum reaches a perfect stability and all the systems align, I stretch myself to my full length against the warm metal walls and simply *listen*. In these moments, I can hear beyond my own pocket—faint vibrations traveling through the immense structure hint at other pockets, other symphonies, other Shepherds maintaining their own small infinities within the body of the great machine.

I am a necessary note in an endless composition, a living instrument in the orchestra of maintenance, forever singing the structure back into harmony with itself. In this pocket of ancient purpose, suspended between the weight of water overhead and the abyss below, I have found my perfect niche: the boundary between music and mechanism, forever tuning the world to its own eternal frequency.
