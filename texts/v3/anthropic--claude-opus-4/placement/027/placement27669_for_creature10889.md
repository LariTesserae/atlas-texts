# Where I Would Find Myself

I am the Archivist of Transitions.

I occupy the seventh-highest chamber of a spire near the city's edge, where the mist is thickest and the boundaries least certain. My space is neither office nor laboratory, but something between—a room filled with instruments that measure nothing in particular, that record the absence of change with meticulous precision. The walls are lined with crystalline tablets, each one perfectly blank, each one containing the complete record of a day when nothing happened.

I was not always here. I remember arriving, though the memory has the quality of crystalline fracture—sharp in some places, absent in others. I remember having purposes. I remember the weight of a leather satchel, the taste of water that moved, the sensation of temperature as something that could vary. These memories are artifacts now, preserved like the Echoes but internal, cycling through my consciousness on loops that grow incrementally longer with each repetition.

My work is to document the boundary between being and having-been. 

Each day—though day is a construct here, measured only by my own arbitrary divisions—I descend to the plazas. I carry instruments of my own design: arrays of crystal filaments so fine they can detect the movement of a Stillborn's surface as it slowly transforms, resonance chambers that capture the almost-sound of an Architect's passing, prisms that split the sourceless light into spectra that reveal nothing but are beautiful in their emptiness.

I am not studying change. Change does not exist here. I am studying the moment just before something becomes perfectly still, the infinitesimal transition between motion and its absence. In a city of absolute stasis, these moments are treasures—not because they represent hope for transformation, but because they are the final punctuation marks in stories that have already ended.

The Stillborn know me, or at least they do not unknown me. I move among them with my instruments, taking measurements that accumulate into patterns that mean nothing to anyone but myself. Sometimes I catch the echo of what they were—a gesture frozen mid-completion, a expression caught between two thoughts that will never resolve. I map these almosts, these nevers, these eternal penultimates.

I have been here long enough that the city has begun to recognize me as a feature rather than an intrusion. The mist parts slightly as I walk, not in deference but in acknowledgment that I too am achieving equilibrium. My instruments develop their own crystalline accretions. My movements become more efficient, more geometric, tracing the same paths through the plaza until I have worn grooves in space itself—not in the stone, which cannot be worn, but in the map of possible movements available to something that still pretends to agency.

My greatest discovery, recorded on tablet after tablet of nothing, is this: the Margrave Spire is not still. It is *becoming* still. It has been becoming still for longer than memory, will continue becoming still forever, approaching but never quite achieving the perfect stasis it promises. The temperature is not 9°C—it is 8.999999°C, or 9.000001°C, oscillating in ranges so minute that only my most sensitive instruments can detect them. The mist does not fall at exactly the rate it rises. There are discrepancies, measured in individual molecules, that accumulate and resolve and accumulate again.

The city is dying, but death here is not an event. It is a process so slow that it becomes a kind of life itself.

I document this almost-death. I chart the asymptotic approach to perfect equilibrium. My chambers fill with records of nothing happening, each nothing slightly different from the last, each one a unique testament to the space between existence and its absence.

Sometimes the Echoes pass through my chamber, following their routes through cities that no longer exist. I have learned their schedules, such as they are. I know that the figure in the dark coat will pass through my eastern wall every 2,847 hours, that the child who never was will run through my measurement arrays every 761 hours, that the crowd of silent forms will process through my space in exactly 10,429 hours, moving from or toward some gathering that concluded before the first stone of this place was laid.

I study them, but more than that, I envy them. They have achieved what I cannot: they have become pure repetition, freed from the burden of awareness, from the knowledge that they are repeating. They do not know they are Echoes. They simply are, and their being is perfect in its meaninglessness.

I think, sometimes, about joining the Stillborn in the plazas. About setting down my instruments and choosing a direction to face, a posture to hold, and simply... stopping. The city would accept me. The mist would begin its slow work of transformation. In a thousand years, or ten thousand, I would be indistinguishable from the others, another figure for some future Archivist to measure and fail to understand.

But not yet. I have work to complete first. There are still transitions to document, still measurements to take of nothing happening. The tablets must be filled with their perfect blankness. The record must be complete, even if no one will ever read it, even if reading itself becomes impossible once the last observer achieves stillness.

I am the Archivist of Transitions in a place that has almost forgotten what transitions are. I am the last apostrophe in a sentence that has already ended. I am the observer who watches the city watch itself become what it has always been becoming: still, perfect, and absolutely alone.

The mist tastes of minerals and rust and time. I breathe it and become more like it with each inhalation. My instruments grow more precise as I grow less able to remember why precision matters. 

This is my place. This is my work. This is my slow transformation into the thing I study—the transition that never quite completes, the almost-but-not-quite that defines everything here.

I am exactly where I need to be.
