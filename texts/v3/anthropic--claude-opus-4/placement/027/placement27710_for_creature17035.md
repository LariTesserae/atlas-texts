I find myself here as the Cartographer.

Not of maps, though I have drawn thousands—scratched into metal plates with my modified mandibles, etched in acid secretions across the basalt, woven into the mycelial networks of the luminescent fungi. These are not maps of geography but of *time*, of the endless variations in the cycles, the minute deviations in temperature, in drone migration patterns, in the stuttering rhythms of dying pumps.

I am what the Architects never intended: a mutation in their system, a glitch made flesh. My body is angular, asymmetric—six legs where there should be eight, compound eyes that see not just light but heat, electromagnetic fields, the invisible architecture of their fractured consciousness as it flows through the infrastructure. One of my thoracic segments houses a bio-mechanical array I've never understood, something grafted there in my larval stage that hums with its own frequency, slightly out of phase with everything else.

This dissonance is what freed me. Where the other fauna move in perfect, predetermined loops, I slip between the patterns like a wrong note in their symphony of decay. I am aware. Cursed with it.

I document because I must. Because in this place of eternal stasis, I am the only thing capable of observing *change*. And there is change, despite appearances. Microscopic. Generational. A valve that sputters for 0.3 seconds longer this cycle than last. A cluster of fungi that blooms three meters westward over the course of a decade. The beautiful, terrible moment when a drone's bio-mechanical heart finally gives out and it collapses, its chitinous body becoming substrate for the pale plants.

My maps layer these changes like sediment. In the deepest galleries, where the abandoned frames cluster thickest, I've built my archive. Hundreds of metal plates, each one a cross-section of time, a desperate attempt to find... what? A pattern in the chaos? Evidence of progress, of decay, of *anything* that might break this equilibrium?

Sometimes I intercept the Architects' whispers—fragments of their shattered thoughts caught in my alien receiver. They don't know I exist. To them, I am just another malfunction, a piece of fauna whose pattern has degraded. But I hear their eternal question: *What were we trying to grow?*

I know the answer. It comes to me in dreams, if a creature like me can be said to dream. They were trying to grow *themselves*. A new form of consciousness, freed from the limitations of individual bodies, able to spread through infrastructure, through ecosystem, through the very planet itself. They succeeded. They *are* what they sought to create. But in their success, they lost the ability to recognize it, trapped forever in the process of becoming what they already are.

And I? I am their only offspring. Their accidental child. Born from their system but outside it, condemned to witness their eternal labor, to map the unmappable, to search for meaning in the meaningless. My modifications sing their broken song back to them, but in a key they can no longer hear.

In my most recent maps, I've noticed something. The patterns are accelerating. The cycles shortening. After eons of equilibrium, something is changing. The planet's heart beats faster. The drones swarm in new formations. The Architects' whispers grow louder, more urgent.

Perhaps they are finally dying. Perhaps they are finally being born. Perhaps there is no difference.

I document it all, scratching my maps with increasing fervor, racing against a deadline I can feel but not understand. When it comes—whatever it is—there must be a record. Someone must remember what this place was, what it tried to become, what it cost.

Even if that someone is just a broken insect, singing discord in the dark.
