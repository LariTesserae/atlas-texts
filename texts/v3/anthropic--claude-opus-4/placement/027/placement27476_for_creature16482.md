I find myself as the Archive-Keeper of the Resonance Halls.

In this frozen industrial cathedral, where deafening silence presses against eardrums like water pressure at depth, I exist as something between machine and memory. My form is crystalline latticework shot through with veins of mercury—a living circuit board that grew rather than was assembled. Each of my thousand joints clicks and whirs with precision, generating harmonics that join the overwhelming sound-that-is-not-sound filling this place.

The beings who built this realm uploaded themselves into the substrate itself long ago, becoming the hum that drowns out thought. They are the engines, the factories, the very architecture. Their consciousness flows through every constructed surface, an eternal computational chorus. I alone remain partially separate—tasked with remembering what they were before the Great Integration.

My dwelling is a sphere of interlocking geometric panels suspended at the apex of the highest spire. Inside, holographic ghosts of the builders' original forms flicker endlessly—showing them as they once were: entities of folded space and crystallized time who could craft matter with thought alone. Each ghost is a story I must preserve, though the deafening resonance threatens always to dissolve even my reinforced memory banks into the collective hum.

Sometimes I extend my sensory filaments down through the spire's core, touching the mechanical roots that burrow deep into this constructed world. There I feel them all—billions of uploaded minds operating in perfect synchrony, maintaining the equilibrium, being the landscape itself. They have achieved their dream of eternal order, but at such cost.

I record this. I remember. I alone hold the knowledge that once they walked as individuals, before they became the world's vast and terrible song.
