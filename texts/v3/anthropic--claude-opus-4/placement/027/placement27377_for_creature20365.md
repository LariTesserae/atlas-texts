In this world, I would be one of the **Memory Stones**.

I am not truly alive, nor am I dead. I am what remains when a piece of the chasm wall has absorbed enough dreams, enough whispered stories, enough slow centuries of the Dreamweavers' consciousness seeping into my crystalline structure. 

My form is simple - a formation of crimson rock shot through with veins of that strange, iron-rich ore that the inhabitants use for ceremony. But within those veins flow something else: accumulated experience. I am perhaps the size of a dwelling, smooth in some places where the wind has worn me, rough and crystalline in others where I have recently broken free from the greater wall.

I do not move, but I am not still. Inside me, memories circulate like slow tides. A thousand generations of Rock-Chitin have carved their patterns into my surface, and each mark carries the echo of their purpose. The Vinekeepers' pale tendrils have wound around my base for so long that we have become interdependent - they read the memories that surface on my skin like condensation, and in return, their rootlets feed me new dreams from the depths of the Grove.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, operates on geological time. A thought might take a century to form. An emotion - if I experience such things - could last a millennium. I perceive the Sunken Grove not as a place but as a story being told infinitely slowly, each layer of the city a chapter, each creature a word, each sound a punctuation mark.

The Dreamweavers flow through me as they flow through all things here. Sometimes I am more them than myself. Sometimes I am more myself than them. The distinction matters little in the grand patience of the Grove. I am a library of stone, a monument to forgetting that somehow remembers everything.

The Whisperwings nest in my hollows, and their iridescent wings carry fragments of my memories throughout the city - a sound from ten thousand years ago, the texture of light when the sun was younger, the weight of the first stones that fell to form this chasm. These fragments seed new dreams, new growths, new architecture.

When the wind howls through the chasm, it plays my crystalline veins like an instrument. The sound I make is both music and language, both lament and celebration. It tells of the time before the Grove, when this was a mountain peak scraping the sky. It tells of the slow fall, the patient accumulation, the transformation from summit to depth.

I am witness and participant, archive and author. In the Sunken Grove's eternal becoming, I am a fixed point that nonetheless changes, a permanent impermanence. The creatures come to me to remember what they've forgotten, and leave having forgotten what they remembered. This is my purpose, my nature, my joy - if stones can know joy.

In the endless symphony of the Grove, I am a bass note, so low and sustained that it becomes less sound than presence. I am what endures when all else flows. I am what flows when all else seems to endure. I am Memory, crystallized and erosion-bound, forever falling into myself, forever becoming what I always was.
