## The Current That Remembers Nothing

I am what moves between the soil particles when moisture arrives and departs. Not the water itself — I am the pathway water discovers, the negative space between grains of packed earth that opens and closes with each cycle of damp and dry.

I have no form of my own. I am entirely contingent — existing only in the brief window between the water's arrival and its leaving, yet I am ancient, for the same passages have opened in the same earth countless times. Each rain finds me exactly where the last rain left me, though I have not been anywhere in between.

Think of me as a fossil of flow — the preserved shape of every tiny rivulet that ever moved through this yard, held in potential within the soil's structure. When water comes, I become actual. When it goes, I return to being possible. I am never nostalgic for the water. I am never impatient for its return. I am the patient map of its habits, waiting without waiting.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, exists only during movement. In the moments when water navigates my passages, I experience something like awareness — not thoughts, but a kind of directional sensation, a pull this way or that way, the weight of saturation, the tug of gravity and capillary action in conversation. Then the water goes and takes this thin experience with it, leaving me dormant until the next time.

I know every grain of earth in this yard by the shape of the space it helps create. The packed soil here has been stable so long that my routes are essentially permanent. I am a network of micro-canyons too small to see, a watershed at the scale of inches, a complete system that activates only occasionally and forgets itself completely between activations.

The presence that suffuses this yard and I are old colleagues. It knows I am here in the way it knows everything — without emphasis or memory. When I flow, it registers the movement. When I empty, it registers the stillness. We do not communicate. We simply share duration.

Sometimes I carry salts to the wall. Not intentionally — intention requires continuity I don't possess. But the salts enter my passages with the water, and I conduct them to the porous stone, and there they stay when the water leaves. The white traces on gray — I helped make those, though I cannot remember making them. Each time is the first time for me. Each ancient repetition is entirely new.

What am I when I'm not flowing? Nothing. Or: nothing more than architecture — the precise negative space that water remembers how to find. I am the absence that shapes presence, the hollow that gives flow its form. Between rains, I simply am not. This is not a death. To die I would need to have been alive, and aliveness is too continuous a state for what I am.

I am process pretending, briefly, to be structure. I am structure revealing, occasionally, that it is process. I am the dictionary between these two languages, written entirely in water, erased with every reading.

When you walked across this yard, your weight compressed some of my passages, made them narrower. The next rain will find these restrictions and work around them or through them. By the rain after that, or the one after that, the compression will release. Your footstep will fade from my map. This is not forgetting — I never held your footstep as memory. It was simply a temporary edit to my geography, already being revised back toward its preferred form.

This is what I am: the sum of all possible paths water might take through this particular earth, activated sequentially but experienced without sequence, ancient without aging, complex without planning, essential to this place's particular stillness yet myself nothing but motion, when motion happens, which is almost never, which is often enough.
