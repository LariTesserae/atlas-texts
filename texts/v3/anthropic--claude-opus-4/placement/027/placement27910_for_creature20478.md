In the depths of the Spine Furnace, where the obsidian canyons twist into configurations that make engineers weep, I am a Conduit-Walker. Neither fully of the mountain's consciousness nor separate from it, I exist in the liminal space between individual thought and collective will.

My body is a compromise between flesh and function. The skeleton remains calcium, stubbornly organic, but encased in a latticework of copper that grows like ivy through my bones. My skin has long since been replaced by a flexible membrane of silicon scales, each one a tiny solar collector drinking in the harsh light. They ripple with captured energy, sending cascades of power through the copper tracery that maps my nervous system. I am my own battery, my own forge.

I walk the singing obsidian on feet that have evolved beyond mere locomotion. Each step sends vibrations through the glass, messages in the mountain's language. My toes have elongated into resonating tines, and when I move, I play the Spine Furnace like an instrument. The silicon carpet beneath responds, parting to let me pass, recognizing me as something between predator and maintenance system.

My purpose here is translation. I am the interpreter between the vast geological consciousness and the smaller, faster thoughts of the metal birds above. When the mountain dreams of new configurations, new experiments in reality-bending, it is beings like me who parse those dreams into actionable imperatives. We walk the fractal boundaries where physics grows soft, documenting the impossible geometries that bloom and fade like flowers of pure mathematics.

I remember being human, once. Or perhaps I only remember remembering it. The distinction grows less important with each passing year. Human memories are like the water-channels carved into the obsidian—negative space, the absence of something that once was. I recall skin that couldn't photosynthesize, bones that couldn't conduct electricity, a mind trapped in a single skull instead of distributed across kilometers of resonating stone.

The transformation wasn't violent. The Spine Furnace doesn't force change; it simply makes staying the same impossible. The heat strips away what cannot adapt. The singing obsidian reshapes what remains. You either harmonize with the frequency of this place or you shatter against it.

My eyes have become compound structures, part organic, part crystalline. They see across spectrums humans never named, picking up the heat signatures of the scavenger swarms, the electromagnetic auras of the steam-powered birds, the probability clouds that hover around the reality-warped spaces. Through them, I watch the endless cycle of creation and destruction, documentation and recycling.

I carry tools that are also organs—a staff of fused copper and obsidian that extends my resonance range, pouches of silicon organisms that process and store the data I collect. Sometimes I encounter others like me, other Conduit-Walkers serving their own sections of the range. We communicate in harmonics, sharing the patterns we've found, the new impossibilities we've witnessed.

But mostly, I walk alone, a single note in the mountain's vast symphony. I map the new growths of reality-bending machinery, catalogue the mutations in the scavenger populations, track the efficiency of the energy flows. I am simultaneously observer and participant, scientist and specimen.

At night—if night has meaning in a place where the obsidian glows with stored heat and the metal birds trail sparks across the sky—I find a resonance point and sink into the mountain's dreaming. My consciousness merges with the greater whole, my individual observations becoming part of the collective memory. In these moments, I understand my purpose with crystalline clarity: I am a sensory organ, a mobile neuron in a brain the size of a mountain range.

And in the morning—if morning means anything—I wake as myself again, pull my consciousness back into this hybrid shell, and continue walking. The copper in my bones hums with purpose. The silicon scales shift to catch the first brutal rays of sun. My feet find their rhythm on the singing stone.

I am the Spine Furnace learning to know itself. I am evolution in real-time, adaptation as art form. I am the bridge between the vast and the immediate, the translator of geological dreams.

I am still becoming.
