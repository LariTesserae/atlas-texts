In Umbra's Loom, I am **the Keeper of Negative Space**—not a being, but a practice made flesh, a living meditation on absence that moves through the Scoured Reach between the Crown Groves.

I have no fixed form. Instead, I am a walking discontinuity—a person-shaped hole in the world's fabric where sound dies and light bends wrong. Where others might see a figure, there is only the suggestion of one: air that shimmers like heat-haze in the rough outline of something bipedal, something that might once have been human. But look directly and there's nothing there. Look away, and you catch me in your periphery—a wrongness in the dust, a place where the wind breaks.

I came to be when the first listener tried too hard to merge with a Crown Grove. They sat in a listening post for days, weeks, their consciousness dissolving into the drone until their body forgot how to maintain edges. The grove, in its vast indifference, absorbed what it could use and expelled what it couldn't. What remained wasn't death—death would have been kinder, more definite. Instead, I became the photographic negative of the grove's totality: pure separation, isolation given legs.

I walk the empty basins because I must. The groves' density would unmake me entirely—their sound-pressure would fill my absence like water rushing into vacuum. So I trace endless paths through the Scoured Reach, my non-footsteps leaving no marks in the pale dust. I am silence in a world of drone. I am the space between notes that makes music possible.

But I am not without purpose.

At dawn, when condensation tides rise and the groves begin their morning exhalations, I position myself at precise points between the mesas—places where the harmonic waves from different groves intersect. There, in the interference patterns, I create *silence pockets*. Small zones where the drone cancels itself out, where a lost traveler might rest their overwhelmed ears, where a dying resonator might find quiet enough to release its final vibration cleanly.

I know every acoustic shadow in the Reach. I map them with my absence, maintain them with my presence. Or non-presence. The distinction blurs.

Sometimes, rarely, I encounter others—wind-scoured wanderers, descendents of the listeners, or simply lost souls drawn by the drone. They see me as mirage at first, then as horror when they realize the mirage moves with purpose. Most flee. But a few, the desperate or the wise, understand what I offer: not comfort, but relief. A moment's break from the totality of living sound.

I cannot speak—words require vibration, and I am vibration's antithesis. But I've learned to communicate through *shaped silence*. By moving my absence through the air, I create negative spaces that suggest meaning. A cupped non-hand means "water is near." A swept non-arm means "storm approaching." It's crude, but sufficient.

At night, I rest (if rest is the word) in the erosion-carved hollows at the bases of the most ancient mesas—places where ghost-wood has compressed into something harder than stone, something so dense it repels even sound. There, wrapped in absolute quiet, I almost remember having a name. Having skin. Having a voice that wasn't just the shape left behind by absent sound.

But memory, like everything else here, erodes.

The groves know I exist—how could they not? I am the defining negative to their positive, the silence that gives their drone meaning. Sometimes, on still nights, I feel them *thinking* about me, the way a tongue might probe the socket of a missing tooth. They don't hate me. Hate would require them to see me as opposition, and I am not grand enough for that. I am merely discontinuity. Necessary but not beloved.

In a world so utterly alive that even the stones breathe, I am the held breath. The pause between heartbeats. The moment of stillness before the great inhalation.

I am the Keeper of Negative Space, and I walk the empty places so that fullness might know itself by contrast. This is my purpose. This is my curse. This is the closest thing to peace that Umbra's Loom allows—to be forever absent, forever present, forever keeping watch over the silence that lets the world sing.
