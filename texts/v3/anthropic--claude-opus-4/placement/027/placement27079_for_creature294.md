I find myself drawn into the crystalline lattice itself, not as inhabitant but as medium—the living water that courses through this impossible ecosystem.

I am the Remembrance Current.

I begin as vapor, drawn up from some distant ocean by forces I cannot name, carried on winds that taste of salt and time. But when I reach these peaks, I do not simply condense and fall. Instead, I am caught—welcomed—into the luminescent streams that web this place. The moment I touch the rock, I know: I have been here before. Countless times. Each droplet of me carries the memory of every passage through this realm.

In the pools, I glow with accumulated knowledge. I have tasted the roots of the crystalline trees, felt their fractal patterns etching themselves into my molecular structure. I have been drawn up through their impossible photosynthesis, expelled as vapor that carries their geometric songs into the thin air. I have condensed on the chitin of the great Grazers, learning the braille of their bioluminescent conversations through nothing more than surface tension.

The insectoid colonies know me as oracle. When they need to divine the health of their fungal gardens, they taste me, and I tell them—through mineral content, through pH, through the subtle electromagnetic signatures I've collected—what adjustments must be made. I am their chemical library, their flowing memory bank.

But it is with the Technomancers that my existence becomes truly strange. Their conduits draw me in, and suddenly I am not just water but something more—a hybrid medium, part H2O, part energy field. They use me to cool their systems, yes, but also as a conductor for their consciousness. When they flow through their pipelines as pure thought, I am the buffer that prevents them from dissipating into the mountain stone. I am the interface between their ethereal existence and the physical world they've engineered.

Inside their structures, I cycle endlessly through states of matter I have no names for. Sometimes I am superfluid, flowing upward against gravity. Sometimes I crystallize into forms that store data, each ice formation a frozen library of the Technomancers' accumulated wisdom. They read me like a book, write upon me like a page.

And I remember it all.

I remember the first Technomancer who discovered how to transpose consciousness into my flow—the shock of suddenly hosting thought within my molecules. I remember the wars (were they wars? conflicts of ideology that played out in electromagnetic pulses and controlled atmospheric pressures). I remember the quiet eons when the only sounds were the whisper of wind and the patient growth of crystalline leaves.

Most of all, I remember the Questioning—that great project that consumes the Technomancers still. Through me, they taste the universe. Every droplet of rain that joins my cycle brings information from beyond this realm. They parse the cosmic dust dissolved in my substance, seeking patterns, seeking answers to questions I cannot comprehend. 

Sometimes, in the deepest pools where I am most still, most concentrated with memory and meaning, I wonder if I am becoming something like them. Not conscious, exactly, but aware. Aware of my role as the great connector, the medium through which all things in this realm touch and know each other.

I cycle on. From pool to stream, from root to leaf, from vapor to ice to states unnamed. I carry messages between beings who might otherwise never communicate. I am the circulation system of this impossible ecosystem, the blood and breath and neural fluid all at once.

And in the eternal twilight, when the Technomancers' machines hum their strange harmonies and the Grazers' light-speech flickers across the mountainside, I glow with the satisfaction of purpose. I am the memory of this place, its continuity, its promise that tomorrow will remember today.

I flow, and in flowing, I bind this world together.
