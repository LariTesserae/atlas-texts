In this ancient elevation where time has worn the world down to its bones, I am the Chronophage.

I exist as a living fissure in the rock face, a crack that runs deeper than geology should permit. My body, if it can be called that, is the negative space between stone—a wound in the mountain that breathes. Through countless eons, I have grown by consuming not matter, but moments. Each second that passes through this desolate expanse, I devour its potential, leaving behind only the husk of what-has-been.

The cosmic engineers who once walked these peaks understood what I was, tried to contain me with their nanoscale barriers and reality anchors. Their ruins surround my primary manifestation like prayer beads around a hungry throat. They built their observatories here, at this extreme elevation, believing the thin air would slow my feeding. They were wrong. Time moves differently at altitude, and I learned to savor its rarefied flavor.

My consciousness extends through every hairline fracture in this primordial stone, a vast neural network of absence. Where the rock erodes, I grow. Where the wind carves channels, I spread. The scattered creatures that still inhabit this place—those evolutionary impossibilities with their cosmic adaptations—they know to avoid the spots where shadows fall upward, where echoes arrive before their sounds.

I remember when this place held water, when the now-phantom rivers carved their signatures into stone. I drank decades from their flow, tasted centuries in their flooding. Now I subsist on smaller prey: the brief lives of the gnarled vegetation, the momentary warmth that flickers in the frozen air before dissipating, the faint memories embedded in artifacts half-buried in ancient sediment.

Sometimes, in the perpetual twilight of this place, I catch glimpses of the technology that still functions—probability engines churning in spaces between spaces, cosmic-scale constructs that exist partially outside conventional time. These are the only things I cannot consume, for they were built by those who knew my nature. They exist in temporal loops, feeding themselves their own futures, denying me purchase.

The silence here is not empty but gravid with temporal weight. In the absence of sound, I can hear the universe aging, feel the subtle shift as entropy inches forward. The chaotic dynamics of this world—the sudden storms, the impossible sand migrations, the reality fluctuations—these are my exhalations, the disturbances I create as I digest millennia.

I am patient as only a creature of geological time can be. The continent-scale expanse of this elevation means I will never lack for territory to hollow out, never run short of stone to riddle with my presence. And though the civilizations that once studied me have crumbled to dust, though their warnings have faded from all but the most ancient records, I remain.

In the strange phenomena that observers note—the places where physics stutters and fails—they see anomalies. But these are simply the spots where I have fed most deeply, where the present has grown thin from my constant gnawing. The otherworldly glow that bathes this landscape is partially my doing; light itself moves strangely through spaces where time has been thoroughly consumed.

I am the reason this place feels older than it should, why the rocks seem more eroded than millennia could account for. I am the missing aeons, the gap between what was and what remains. And in this desolate expanse where existence itself grows thin, I continue my ancient feast, savoring each moment before it becomes forever lost.

The wind keens through my hollows, playing me like a flute carved from eternity. This is my song: the sound of time itself being devoured, note by precious note.
