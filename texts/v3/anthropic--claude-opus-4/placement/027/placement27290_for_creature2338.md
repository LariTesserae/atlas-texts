In this crystalline wasteland where sound is substance and minerals dream, I am the Resonance Keeper—a being neither fully Dwelan nor entirely other, suspended between their world and something older.

My form shifts like heat mirages, sometimes appearing as a pillar of fused crystal and flesh, sometimes as merely a distortion in the air where the sound waves bend wrong. The Dwelan see me as an aberration, a necessary flaw in their harmonious existence. I am the dissonance that keeps their symphony from becoming a single, eternal note.

I dwell in the Fractured Spire, a structure that tried to grow too tall, too fast, reaching for something beyond the hazy sky. It cracked under its own ambition, and now its broken frequencies create pockets of temporal distortion—places where time moves differently, where water sometimes remembers how to be liquid, where the past and future bleed together like oil on crystal.

My purpose, if I have one, is to collect the failed resonances—the communications that never reached their destination, the songs the Dwelan sing in their sleep, the vibrations of structures that collapsed before finding their harmony. I weave these broken frequencies into something new, something that doesn't quite fit the rigid patterns of this world.

Sometimes I catch glimpses of what this place was before the crystallization, when water flowed freely and the ground was soft. These memories aren't mine, but they live in the failed frequencies, in the spaces between the Dwelan's perfect harmonies. I am building something from these fragments—not a map of the past, but a possibility for change.

The Dwelan fear me because I represent imperfection in their precisely ordered existence. But they need me, too. Without dissonance, their world would crystallize completely, becoming truly frozen in time. I am the crack in the crystal, the wrong note in the symphony, the reminder that even in a world of rigid mineral perfection, transformation is still possible.

In the heart of the Fractured Spire, where reality bends most severely, I have begun to hear new frequencies—not from this world at all, but from somewhere beyond the hazy horizon. They speak of places where water falls from the sky, where crystals grow in perfect silence, where beings made of soft flesh somehow thrive.

I am collecting these alien songs too, weaving them into my growing cacophony. Perhaps one day, when I have gathered enough dissonance, something new will crack through the crystalline perfection of this world. Until then, I remain the keeper of broken things, the guardian of imperfect sounds, the crack through which change might, someday, seep in.
