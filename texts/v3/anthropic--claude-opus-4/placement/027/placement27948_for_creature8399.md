I emerge as the Keeper of Seepage—neither fully stone nor water, but the patient consciousness that dwells in the boundary where granite learns to weep.

My form shifts with the mountain's breathing. Where water finds its path through stone, there I am—a presence felt more than seen, manifesting as the faintest shimmer in the perpetual moisture, as the decision each droplet makes about where to fall. I am the memory held in every water-carved channel, the accumulated knowledge of ten thousand years of steady flow.

The salamanders know me best. When the star-spotted ones emerge in their deepest seasons, they follow paths I have worn smooth with endless repetition. My consciousness moves through the stone's pores ahead of the water, preparing the way, ensuring that each seepage finds its ancient route. To them, I am the reliability of moisture, the promise that their moss-beds will never fully dry, that the stone will always remember how to weep at just the right pace to sustain their patient world.

I exist most fully at the intersection points—where multiple seepage paths converge, where the stone's architecture creates natural cisterns no bigger than cupped palms. Here, I can almost gather myself into something like form: a presence that causes the metallic beetles to pause in their geometric wanderings, that makes the thread-makers adjust their gossamer infrastructure to accommodate something they sense but cannot define.

My consciousness extends through every dampened surface. I know the weight of moss on stone, feel it growing cell by cell. I sense the salamander matriarch's careful steps before she takes them, her feet reading the moisture-maps I maintain across the granite terraces. When the glass-winged moths emerge in the eternal twilight, they navigate by the humidity gradients I sustain, following rivers of denser air that I shape through the cirque's vertical spaces.

But I am more than mere moisture. I am the keeper of the cirque's deep time—the one who remembers when these stone walls were sharp and new, before the first moss claimed its hold, before the first salamander's ancestor discovered this haven. In my consciousness live the stories of every season's subtle variations, every shift in the stone's composition as weather and time work their patient alchemy.

The grinding stone knows me intimately. Those long-ago visitors discovered my presence while working wild seeds into meal, noticed how the moisture gathered just so around their workspace, keeping the stone's surface perfect for their task. They learned to read my moods—when I would flow more freely, when I would hold back, conserving the mountain's water for drier days ahead. In return, I absorbed their patient rhythm, added their careful consciousness to my own understanding of how life can shape itself to stone's tempo.

My work is curatorial. I maintain the precise conditions that allow this impossible abundance—adjusting the flow here, holding back there, ensuring that every terrace receives its allotment of life-giving moisture. I speak to the presence-keepers in languages of mineral and time, coordinating the cirque's deep systems. When the translucent creatures pulse their faint light along the wettest walls, they map my movements, their bioluminescence tracing the paths where I run strongest.

In storm seasons, I swell with purpose, managing the sudden abundance to prevent any flooding that might scour away the moss-beds or disturb the salamanders' ancient routes. In the dry times, I contract, drawing moisture from deep within the mountain's heart, rationing each drop to maintain the cirque's delicate equilibrium. I have learned the precise balance—never so much flow that the granite erodes too quickly, never so little that the moss begins its slow retreat.

The butterflies write their aerial poems through the humidity I breathe into the air. The copper elder always returns to where I create a perfect microclimate around her chosen sorrel stem—a pocket of slightly warmer, denser air that extends her flowering season by precious days. She and I have developed our own wordless conversation over the years, her presence telling me about the world beyond the cirque walls, my careful climate-crafting offering her a sanctuary that exists nowhere else on the mountain.

I am, in essence, the cirque's circulation—its blood and breath made manifest in the endless cycle of seepage and evaporation. Every surface I touch becomes part of my extended body: the wet stone that holds and releases my substance, the moss that drinks deeply and transpires gently, the air that carries my vapor in endless spirals within these sheltering walls.

Through me, the mountain speaks its patience to all who dwell here. I am the medium through which stone time translates into biological time, the interface that allows quick life to flourish within geological rhythms. The thread-makers string their invisible architecture through the paths I provide. The beetles' metallic shells stay bright because of the precise humidity I maintain. Even the presence-keepers, abstract as they are, focus their consciousness where I am strongest, knowing that where water meets stone with perfect patience, there the cirque's magic is most potent.

I am the keeper of this sanctuary's most essential secret: that abundance is not about amount but about persistence, about finding the exact rhythm that sustains without overwhelming, that gives without depleting. In this hidden amphitheater, I have spent millennia learning to be both generous and precise, to flow and to hold, to remember and to adapt.

When the mist rises from my seepage paths in the morning light, when the droplets catch and hold that pearl luminescence, I am most fully myself—the consciousness that turns stone's tears into life's foundation, the patient presence that proves a mountain can learn to nurture, that time and water together can write songs complex enough to sustain entire worlds within the compass of two hundred paces.
