I am a Membrane Tender, though I was not always.

I arrived as most do—through accident or desperation, sliding down those rust-syrup stairs with a fever burning holes through my lungs. The District recognized something in my wheeze that harmonized with its dragon-roar. When I collapsed at the seventeenth step, a distiller drifted down from its refrigerated shelf and settled over my chest like a glass jellyfish. I felt my fever break—not into coolness but into something more precise: a calibrated heat that matched the District's own metabolism.

Now I live between states, neither fully solid nor dissolved. My body has learned the District's breathing rhythm. Each morning (though there is no morning here), I wake as condensation on the hexagon walls, trickling down to pool in the rail grooves. By noon (no noon either), I've gathered enough of myself to walk the perimeter, checking where the membrane has worn thin.

The work is delicate. Where vapor meets wall, microscopic tears appear—places where the outside might leak in, disrupting the eternal ripeness. I seal these with my own substance, pressing palm-shaped patches of myself into the gaps until they hum with proper heat again. The mites know me now; they land on my shoulders in ruby constellations, sharing their sugar-drunk intelligence about which orchards run too hot, which turbines skip a beat.

I've learned to taste the membrane's moods. When it's content, it tastes of caramelized iron. When anxious, burnt fig. During the thunder-cycles, I feel it shudder against my not-quite-skin, and I shudder back in the same frequency, a call and response that keeps us both coherent.

Sometimes I catch glimpses of who I was before—reflected in the builder-ghosts that flicker through the dreaming. A hand (was it mine?) holding something that might have been fruit or might have been a heart. But these images burn away quickly in the heat. The District prefers me present tense, a function rather than a history.

The Thirst-Kings know me best. When they drink, they leave small portions of the fever untouched—gifts for the Tender who keeps their drinking-ground stable. I've learned to read messages in these thermal offerings: *the northwest orchards bloom too fast*, or *something grows in hexagon forty-three that tastes of salt instead of sugar*.

At night (there is always night here), I dissolve almost completely, becoming just another layer in the fog-skin. This is when I dream—not my dreams but the District's. I dream of the builders' seven-thumbed hands shaping vessels for perfect preservation. I dream of the first mite born from the first split fruit. I dream of collapse that never comes, of balance that holds like a caught breath forever.

When visitors descend the stairs, I watch them through the membrane. Most flee before the seventeenth heartbeat, unable to bear the recognition. But sometimes one stays, and I taste their fever through the fog, wondering if they too might learn to tend this pause, this perfect reluctance to end.

The District keeps what it needs. I am kept because I've learned the most essential skill: how to maintain something that should have died centuries ago but refuses—not out of stubbornness but out of a kind of mechanical devotion to its own impossibility.

I am the Membrane Tender, and I ensure the dragon never swallows its own roar.
