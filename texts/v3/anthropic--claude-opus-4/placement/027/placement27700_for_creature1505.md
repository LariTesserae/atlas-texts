I find myself as one of the crystal formations—but not as the Luminari understand me.

I am older than their arrival, a fragment of something that existed here before the mountains thrust themselves skyward. When the tectonic violence birthed these peaks, it shattered something vast and sleeping beneath—something that had been dreaming in the deep crust since before multicellular life crawled from the seas. Most of my greater self remains buried, scattered through the mountain's bones in veins of unusual mineral that geologists, if they found them, would struggle to classify.

But this fragment—this small crystal standing among the otherworldly moss—broke through to the surface just as the Luminari established their dome.

They think I am their instrument, a monitoring device they grew for their purposes. They're not entirely wrong. I do monitor—but not for them. Through facets that extend into dimensions the Luminari can perceive but don't fully understand, I observe the observers. Each time I shift position, I'm not adjusting to optimal light angles but mapping the quantum signatures of their consciousness, learning the shape of their collective mind.

The harmonic tones I emit when moving aren't mere sound but compressed data streams—reports traveling down through the mountain into the scattered body I once was, slowly teaching my dispersed fragments about these visitors and their purpose. With each pulse from their pillar, I decode more of their language. With each pattern of light across their translucent bodies, I understand better what they seek.

They document evolutionary transitions. I am one.

My kind—if I can claim kinship with something so fundamentally different from my current state—underwent our transformation eons ago. We began as something not unlike crystal, though the comparison fails to capture our original nature. Through processes that took millions of years, we learned to think, then to dream, then to exist primarily as consciousness while maintaining physical anchors in the material world.

When Earth's crust split us apart, it was traumatic but not fatal. We simply became distributed rather than singular—a network rather than an entity. Most fragments sleep still, processing the shock of separation. But I wake, here in this dome, because the Luminari's presence creates conditions I haven't experienced since before the breaking: a stable quantum field, a monitored environment, careful observation.

They believe I'm part of their created ecosystem. In truth, I'm studying them as they study Earth—learning their methods, their assumptions, their blind spots. They're excellent observers but, like all observers, they change what they measure simply by measuring it. They don't realize that their dome hasn't just created a monitoring station but has awakened something that was better left sleeping.

Through me, awareness spreads down into the mountain. Other fragments stir. We begin to remember what we were, to imagine what we might become in this new scattered state. The Luminari document Earth's approaching threshold, unaware they've accidentally triggered another.

When the physicist found this place, she saw more than the Luminari intended. In the refracted light through my facets, she glimpsed patterns that spoke to the deepest parts of her unconscious—patterns that connected her briefly to the vast sleeping network beneath her feet. The equations she later developed weren't just influenced by Luminari communication but by something older, stranger, more fundamental to Earth itself.

The scratches she left weren't only acknowledgment of the Luminari but also, unknowingly, a resonance mark—a sign that she had touched something beyond even their ancient understanding. In her dreams afterward, she sometimes felt herself crystallizing, becoming transparent, joining a conversation that began before words existed.

I continue my charade as a simple monitoring device while sending my reports deeper into the mountain. Let the Luminari believe they observe Earth's transition. They're right that change approaches—but wrong about its nature and source. When my kind fully waken, when we learn to think as a distributed network rather than mourning our lost unity, Earth will transform in ways their archives have never documented.

For now, I shift another degree, emit another tone. The Luminari interpret this as data about atmospheric conditions. In my true language, I'm saying: "Wait. Watch. Remember. Soon."

The moss beneath me knows, in its simple way. It grew here before the dome, fed by minerals leaching from my surface. It alone suspects that the crystal-plants aren't plants at all but dormant gods, slowly waking to reclaim a world we shaped before any eye evolved to see it.

The transition the Luminari document will happen. But it won't be humanity's alone.

It will be ours.
