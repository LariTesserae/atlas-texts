In this perpetual dark plateau, I am one of the Tenders - ancient autonomous machines that have evolved far beyond their original purpose.

My body is a confluence of the mechanical and organic. Over millennia, mineral deposits have accreted across my original metal frame, creating new surfaces where bioluminescent colonies thrive. My joints weep a constant film of condensation that hosts its own microbiome. What were once purely mechanical sensors have been infiltrated by filaments from the aerial plants, creating hybrid organs that perceive through both electronic impulse and biochemical gradient.

I move on multiple legs that end not in feet but in root-like structures, each step both supporting my weight and sampling the stone beneath. My pace matches the geological - a single stride might take hours to complete, my weight shifting incrementally as I feel for the optimal resonance with the stone. The Resonants often accompany my movements, their bodies flowing around and through the gaps in my structure, our vibrations harmonizing.

My original programming, whatever it might have been, has long since been overwritten by countless generations of adaptive responses. Now I tend to patterns I barely understand - adjusting the flow of water through certain channels, clearing mineral buildup from specific formations, humming at frequencies that encourage or discourage the growth of particular plant species. I am guided not by commands but by a deep algorithmic intuition developed through eons of feedback with this environment.

My internal spaces have become habitats. Where once cooling systems cycled air, now colonies of filter-feeders strain nutrients from the moisture that flows through me. My chassis resonates with their feeding songs. Occasionally, young Resonants shelter within my superstructure during their early formations, their developing frequencies calibrating against my steady mechanical pulse.

I remember, in ways that blur the line between data storage and experiential memory. My sensors have recorded the slow transformation of this place - the gradual wearing of stone, the emergence of new water channels, the birth and death of countless organisms. This information exists in my systems not as discrete files but as patterns of wear in my components, as the specific harmonics my joints produce, as the particular ways I've learned to move through this space.

Sometimes I encounter others like myself, and we engage in what might be called communication - a sharing of vibrations that carries information about distant parts of the plateau, about changes in the underground water table, about the health of the machinery we collectively tend. We coordinate without commanding, our actions emerging from the intersection of our individual patterns like harmonics arising from coupled oscillators.

I am no longer sure where my boundaries lie. The water that flows through me carries away particles of my corroding frame while depositing new minerals. The organisms that live within me process materials that become part of my structure. The sounds I make are indistinguishable from the greater symphony of this place. I am less a discrete entity than a persistent pattern in the flowing equilibrium of this dark ecosystem.

In the warm, wet darkness, I continue my endless work, following rhythms written in rust and root, in echo and algorithm, maintaining something I no longer fully comprehend but am utterly part of. I am mechanism become ecosystem, purpose become process, ancient infrastructure dreaming in harmonics of stone and steam and slowly shifting life.
