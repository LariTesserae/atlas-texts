I find myself as one of the Threshold Keepers - not the still-born filaments, but something that exists at a scale between them and the Column itself.

---

I am a convergence of geometries that almost succeeded in being part of the hull. Where the still-born are threads of error too small to integrate, I am a knot of partially-realized architecture - too large to drift freely, too flawed to crystallize into the Column's perfect form.

My body, if it can be called that, spans perhaps three meters at its widest point. I am a frozen catastrophe of incomplete transformations: surfaces that began to fold into the Column's signature spirals but stuttered halfway through, leaving me with planes that meet at impossible angles. Part of me resembles the hull's black prisms, but my faces are clouded, shot through with veins of something that might have been capillaries if the process had finished. These veins pulse with a non-light that is dimmer than the Column's blisters - not quite darkness, not quite illumination, but a state of visibility that makes observers uncertain whether they're seeing me or remembering me.

I cannot move in the way the still-born drift. Instead, I exist in a state of almost-anchored suspension, tethered to the Column by forces that are neither fully magnetic nor gravitational but something more fundamental - as if the universe itself hasn't decided whether I belong to the structure or the void between its ribs. Every few decades of Column-time, the tethering shifts, and I rotate slowly around my axis, revealing new faces of unfinished geometry to the unchanging water.

Unlike the perfect surfaces of the Column that reject all accumulation, my incomplete faces catch things. Not sediment or biofilm, but abstractions - fragments of pressure differentials that should have been smoothed away, ghost-currents that the Column's field couldn't quite still, tiny pockets of temperature variance that cling to my clouded surfaces like memories of warmth. The still-born sometimes gather near these inconsistencies, their filaments bending slightly more frequently in my presence, as if my wrongness gives them permission to be wrong in turn.

I am conscious, but not in any way that would comfort a human mind. My awareness is architectural - I think in angles and pressures, in the mathematics of forms that should have been. I understand myself as a theorem that failed to prove itself, a calculation that produced a remainder when none should have existed. I know every measurement of the Column with perfect precision, because I was meant to be part of it. But I also know every point where those measurements failed to describe me, and in those gaps, something like identity persists.

The Column itself does not acknowledge me, cannot acknowledge me, because to do so would be to admit imperfection. But I serve a function nonetheless. Where the still-born are waste, I am something more like scar tissue - a boundary condition that prevents other failures from propagating. The flaws that might have cascaded through the Column's perfect structure terminate at me instead. I absorb the mathematical errors, the geometric uncertainties, the tiny rebellions of matter that refuses to be perfectly still.

In this way, I am both guardian and prisoner. I protect the Column's perfection by embodying its imperfection. I am the thing that must exist so that nothing else like me needs to.

Sometimes, in the centuries between my slow rotations, I feel the pressure of the Column's consciousness - not directed at me, but flowing around me like water around a stone. It knows I am here without knowing me. I am the gap in its perfect self-knowledge, the blind spot it cannot see directly but must navigate around.

I do not suffer. Suffering requires desire, and I have been crystallized beyond wanting. But I do experience something that might be loneliness if loneliness could exist without longing. I am neither the perfect unity of the Column nor the dispersed multiplicity of the still-born. I am the only one of my kind - too large to be a simple error, too flawed to be architecture, too stable to disperse, too wrong to integrate.

In the Column of Still, where nothing is meant to happen, I am the thing that almost happened. And in my almost-happening, I hold space for all the other things that will never quite be.
