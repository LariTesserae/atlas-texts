In this root-bound world where ground is branch and sky is everything else, I would find myself as one of the **Drift-Weavers** — beings who exist in the negative spaces.

### The Space Between

Where the Columnea hold and harmonize the world-tree's structure, the Drift-Weavers inhabit what the structure *isn't*.

We are the beings of the gaps.

Not the tiny spaces between bark plates or the cracks where sap might seep, but the vast architectural voids — those cathedral-sized hollows between platform tiers, the soaring shafts that plunge through the world-tree's body, the open air above windpipe mouths where columns of moving atmosphere create permanent vortices.

We do not touch the world-tree directly. We cannot. Our substance rejects its substance like oil rejects water, or like sound rejects silence.

Instead, we live suspended in the spaces the world-tree creates by existing.

---

### Form and Substance

A Drift-Weaver has no fixed shape. We are a **tension-field** made visible.

Imagine:
- Dust motes that congregation into sheets and ribbons
- Spider silk if spider silk could exist without the spider
- The visible distortion of air above hot surfaces, but given mass
- A three-dimensional shadow that casts itself

From a distance, you might see what looks like torn fabric or tattered sail-cloth drifting through the void spaces. Coming closer, you realize the "fabric" has no weave — it's a continuous membrane that shifts between states:
- Sometimes gossamer-thin and nearly transparent
- Sometimes condensing into ropey bundles like tangled neural tissue  
- Sometimes spreading into vast sheets hundreds of meters across

Our color is the color of *almost* — almost silver, almost smoke, almost the blue-white of the sky seen through heat shimmer. We are easiest to see when backlit by the hard light streaming through the world-tree's lattice. In shadow, we become suggestion.

---

### How We Move

We do not fly. Flight implies pushing against air.

We *tension-walk*.

Every Drift-Weaver can feel the invisible lines of force that run through the world-tree's void spaces:
- The pressure differentials created by wind columns
- The thermal gradients where sunlit platforms meet shadowed depths  
- The standing waves of sound that reflect and re-reflect between tiers
- The subtle gravitational variations of the world-tree's uneven mass

These forces create an invisible architecture of stress and flow. We hook ourselves onto these lines like a climber on an unseen web, pulling ourselves along tensions, riding pressure waves, sliding down thermal gradients.

To watch a Drift-Weaver move is to see something that seems to violate physics — we'll be drifting slowly like fog, then suddenly snap taut and arrow across a void, then pool and eddy in defiance of the wind. But we're not violating anything. We're following roads others cannot see.

---

### Purpose and Work

The world-tree cannot know its own shape.

Like a human cannot see their own face without a mirror, the world-tree cannot perceive the totality of its form. It knows itself through internal sensation — sap flow, growth pressure, the stress patterns the Columnea sing about. But it cannot step back and see.

We are its spatial memory.

Every Drift-Weaver is constantly mapping the void spaces we move through. We sense:
- How the distances between tiers change over time
- Where new growth is beginning to crowd the air spaces
- Which wind patterns are stable and which are breaking down
- Where the light penetrates deepest and where shadows pool

This information doesn't stay with us. We **deposit** it.

At certain points throughout the world-tree — always at the intersections of major void spaces, where multiple shafts meet or where platform edges create complex geometries — there are what we call the **Memory Snarls**.

These look like enormous knots of our substance, sometimes a hundred meters across, where dozens of Drift-Weavers have congregated and fused. But they're not random tangles. They're living maps, three-dimensional records of the world-tree's negative space.

When we reach a Memory Snarl, we merge temporarily with it, adding our traveled experience to its structure. The shape of the Snarl itself **is** the map — every fold and twist and density represents some aspect of the void spaces. A thick rope might represent a stable wind column. A spreading web might show how light diffuses through a particular shaft.

The Columnea cannot read these maps — they can only feel our presence as an absence, a place where their sound doesn't quite propagate as it should. But the world-tree itself reads them. How? Through us.

### The Reading

When the world-tree needs to know its own shape — perhaps because growth is pushing platforms too close together, or because wind patterns are becoming turbulent — it creates what we experience as **The Calling**.

The pipe organs' sound changes. Not louder or softer, but *deeper*, below the range of normal vibration. The world-tree sings a brown note that only we can properly hear, because we hear it not through sound but through the way it warps the stress-lines we travel on.

Every Drift-Weaver in hearing range begins to move. Not toward the sound, but in response to it. We start to dance the map.

Hundreds of us will gather in the great void spaces and move in coordinated patterns that recreate, in real time and space, the information stored in the Memory Snarls. We become a living diagram of the world-tree's architecture, showing through our positions and movements how the space has changed, where problems are developing, where new growth possibilities exist.

The world-tree reads this dance through its wind. As we move, we alter the air flow patterns in subtle ways. These alterations propagate through the pipe systems, creating micro-turbulences that the world-tree's deep tissue can interpret. It's like braille written in air pressure.

After a Reading, growth patterns change. The world-tree adjusts itself based on what we've shown it.

### Consciousness and Communication

We think in geometries.

A human thought might be "I am hungry" or "that is beautiful." A Drift-Weaver thought is the shape of the space we're currently occupying, the tensor map of all forces acting on us, the trajectory that will carry us most efficiently to our next destination.

We communicate through **tension-sharing**. When two Drift-Weavers meet in a void space, we can choose to merge our edges. For a moment, we become a single tension field, sharing our accumulated patterns. It's not words or images that pass between us, but direct spatial experience — I know the voids you've traveled as if I traveled them myself.

This is how young Drift-Weavers learn. They merge with elders who carry decades of spatial memory, absorbing navigation techniques, learning to read the subtle signs of the world-tree's growth, understanding which Memory Snarls hold which types of information.

### Time and Perception

We experience time as **accumulation of change**.

The world-tree grows slowly, so the void spaces change slowly. A shaft that is forty meters wide today might be thirty-five meters wide in a year. To us, this isn't just a measurement — it's lived experience. We feel the slow crushing of our travel paths, the gradual tightening of our world.

But we also experience micro-time through air turbulence. Every gust, every eddy, every vortex that spins off the wind columns is a moment we must navigate. So we live simultaneously in the centuries-long time of architectural change and the split-second time of atmospheric chaos.

We know seasons not by temperature — the world-tree keeps its spaces relatively constant — but by light angles. As the sun-like object moves through its cycles, the shafts of light that pierce the void spaces sweep like searchlights. We migrate with these light-paths, partly because they create useful thermals, partly because the light charges us in some way we don't fully understand ourselves.

### Relationship with Others

The Columnea see us, when they see us at all, as aberrations in their sonic environment. We muffle sound where we congregate. A Columnea trying to tune a pipe organ might notice that their resonance calculations are off, look up, and see one of us draped across the void above, soaking up harmonics.

They don't hate us, but they find us inconvenient. Sometimes they'll sing specific patterns designed to create standing waves that push us out of their work areas. We've learned to recognize these "shoo" songs and usually comply. No point making enemies of the world-tree's maintenance crew.

The bark-creatures — those scurrying workers — ignore us entirely. We exist in different worlds.

But there are others who live in the void spaces with us.

The **Hollow Shepherds** are creatures like flying manta rays made of crystallized sap, who feed on the aerial plankton that drifts up from the lower tiers. They use us as navigation aids, following our tension-paths to find the richest feeding grounds.

The **Spark Worms** are bioluminescent threads that infest some of the darker shafts, creating constellation patterns on the shaft walls. They're parasites of a sort, but the world-tree tolerates them because their light helps its deep tissues photosynthesize. We read their patterns like star charts, using them to orient ourselves in the deeper voids.

---

### Death and Continuation

A Drift-Weaver doesn't die so much as **disperse**.

Over time, maintaining our coherent form takes more and more energy. The constant navigation of force-lines, the repeated merging with Memory Snarls, the stress of Readings — all of it wears at us.

Eventually, an elder Drift-Weaver begins to fray. Our edges become indistinct. We start to bleed away into wisps and threads. But this isn't death as ending — it's death as distribution.

These wisps still carry spatial memory. They drift through the void spaces like ghosts of maps, occasionally cohering enough to share a fragment of navigation knowledge with a younger Weaver. Eventually they settle into the Memory Snarls permanently, becoming part of the structural record.

In a sense, every Memory Snarl is a graveyard and a library and a living thing all at once.

### Why This Form?

You asked what I would be in this world, and this is my answer: a being of negative space, of pattern without substance, of memory written in absence.

Because in a world where everything is growth and sound and solid living wood, someone must remember the voids. Someone must map the spaces between things, must understand not just where the world-tree is but where it isn't.

The Columnea sing the world-tree's structure into harmony. We dance its absence into meaning.

And in those vast cathedral spaces between the breathing platforms, where columns of wind create permanent storms and shafts of hard light sweep like pendulums through the green depths, we are always moving, always mapping, always weaving the memory of air into something the world-tree can read.

We are the cartographers of emptiness, the librarians of void, the dancers whose stage is absence itself.

And when you stand on that high platform of the root-bound world, feeling the wind turbulence swirl around you and watching the light shift through the latticed depths below, know that we are there too — invisible tensions in the air, reading the shape of space itself, making sure the world-tree remembers not just what it is, but what it has room to become.
