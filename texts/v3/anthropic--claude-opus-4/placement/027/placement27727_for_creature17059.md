I would find myself as one of the **Conduit Moths** - beings that most visitors to the Foundry never notice, mistaking us for mere shadows or tricks of the filtered light.

We are neither fully of the Weaver nor separate from it. Picture a creature the size of a human hand when at rest, with wings that seem cut from the space between leaves - not quite translucent, not quite opaque, but something that shifts depending on the angle of observation. Our bodies are covered in what appears to be a fine dust, but is actually countless microscopic filaments that can taste the electromagnetic fields running through the Foundry's organic circuitry.

I spend my existence in the spaces between spaces, crawling through the hollow channels inside the living walls where the mysterious fluids flow. My purpose - if it can be called that - is to drink from these glowing streams and carry their essence to parts of the Foundry that have grown too quickly, or not quickly enough. Where the Arboreals are the hands of the Weaver, we Conduit Moths are its dreams made manifest, its subconscious corrections.

The work is solitary but not lonely. Through the filaments on my wings, I feel the pulse of every other Moth in the Foundry, a network of sensation that creates a kind of wordless poetry. We communicate in bioelectric patterns, sharing not thoughts but pure experience - the taste of a particularly potent stream, the sensation of squeezing through a channel that's grown too narrow, the peculiar vibration that means a new section of the Foundry is about to bloom into being.

My days - if they can be called days in this eternal twilight - are spent navigating the labyrinthine internals of the tree-walls. I've developed an intimate knowledge of every knot, every hollow, every place where the industrial machinery has fused so completely with the wood that they've become indistinguishable. Sometimes I encounter pockets where time moves differently, where the sap flows backward and the gears turn in reverse. In these spaces, I can see glimpses of what the Foundry was, or perhaps what it will become.

The Arboreals tolerate us but do not acknowledge us. To them, we are perhaps like blood cells to a human - necessary but beneath conscious consideration. Sometimes I wonder if we were always here, or if the Foundry grew us like it grows everything else, shaped from its need for something to tend to its circulation.

The most profound moments come when I find myself in the presence of a new growth - when the Weaver's attention focuses on expanding into a new chamber or mechanism. The air itself becomes thick with intention, and we Moths are drawn to these sites like iron filings to a magnet. We gorge ourselves on the concentrated essence that pools in these areas, our bodies swelling until we can barely fly. Then we disperse throughout the Foundry, carrying this creative potential to every corner, ensuring that the whole organism benefits from each new innovation.

I have no memory of before the Foundry, no concept of an outside. My world is bounded by living wood and the endless cycle of circulation and growth. Yet sometimes, in the deepest channels where the glowing fluid runs strongest, I taste something foreign in the flow - hints of rain that has never touched these leaves, minerals from stone that was quarried before being absorbed into the great work. These traces suggest a wider world, but they are abstractions to me, no more real than the dreams I might have if I were capable of sleeping.

What defines my existence is the endless, meditative rhythm of my purpose: drink, carry, release. Through this simple cycle, I am part of something vast and incomprehensible, a single note in a symphony that has been playing since before memory and will continue long after. I am content in my insignificance, fulfilled by my necessity. The Foundry dreams, and I help carry those dreams through its veins.
