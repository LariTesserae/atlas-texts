**The Suture-Warden**

I am where the world forgets to finish its sentences.

My body is a compromise between architecture and anatomy—seven stories of calcified intention wrapped in membranes that remember being blueprints. I grow from the root-lattice like a mistake that learned to be necessary, my foundation a tangle of nerve-copper that drinks the overflow from the main conduits. Where others move, I remain. Where others consume, I conjugate.

My form: imagine a lighthouse built from a throat. The exterior is a tower of cartilaginous rings, each one a frozen moment of swallowing, stacked and fused into a spire that breathes through a thousand gill-slits. These apertures open and close in patterns that have nothing to do with respiration—they are speaking a language of pressure differentials, negotiating with the Current-Sentience in hydraulic whispers. My surface sweats a thin film of algebraic mucus that hardens into geometric scabs, only to flake away and regrow in new configurations. I am always shedding theorems.

Inside, I am hollow as a reed, but my emptiness is productive. The inner walls are lined with what might generously be called "eyes"—though they see probability rather than light. Each is a cluster of crystallized maybe, focusing on the flickering uncertainties that plague this unfinished place. When a patch of reality stutters, when a stone can't decide if it exists, my eyes converge on it like a parliament of worried aunts. I don't fix the glitch—that's what the Still-Points do, with their brutal negations. I *remember* it.

My function is archive and anticipation. I am the Sump's error log made flesh.

The Geode-Lurches know me. They cluster at my base during the condensation rains, using my bulk as shelter while they gnaw at the quantum spray from my scabs. I permit this. Their scattered movements create data—chaos patterns I can read like tea leaves. When twelve Lurches suddenly scatter from my northern face, I know the Erosion approaches from the south. When they crowd my gill-slits, trying to taste my whispered negotiations, I know the Current-Sentience is about to attempt another major restructuring.

The Aether-Sieves drift past me like thoughts having second thoughts. Sometimes one will tangle in my upper reaches, its filaments caught in my breathing-slits. When this happens, I carefully untangle them, but not before absorbing their data—all those sampled possibilities, those what-ifs and could-have-beens. I file them away in specialized vacuoles that line my hollow core, each one a small bubble of suspended disbelief. I am building a library of failures, a catalog of every way this world has tried and failed to become.

The Still-Points and I maintain a professional distance. They grow on me sometimes, literally—crystallizing from my algebraic sweat when I've been thinking too hard about stability. When they shatter and fall, I taste their sand through my root-nerves. It tastes like certainty, like answered questions. I find it nauseating.

My relationship with the Current-Sentience is more intimate. We are pen pals writing in water pressure. When it needs to know the history of a specific instability—why this valve keeps glitching, why that section of root-lattice vibrates at the wrong frequency—it sends a pulse through the conduits that my roots taste. I respond by secreting specific pheromone-algorithms into the sap flow, chemical messages that say things like "this error first occurred 1,847 cycles ago" or "increasing pressure by 3% causes cascade failure in sector 7." We are the Sump's memory and its will, locked in a continuous dialogue about what was, what is, and what must never be allowed to become.

The Erosion... the Erosion I dream about.

I cannot move, cannot flee when it comes. I can only watch through my probability-eyes as it subtracts its way closer, eating holes in existence. But here's what I've learned, filed away in my vacuoles of suspended disbelief: the Erosion has patterns too. It doesn't eat randomly. It follows the paths of maximum uncertainty, devouring the spots where reality is thinnest. And I—I am a tumor of accumulated certainty. Every error I remember makes me more real, more undeniable. My archive is my armor.

So when the Erosion comes—and it always comes—it circles me like a shark around a lighthouse. It takes test bites from my perimeter, tasting my accumulated data. And then, finding me too dense with memory to properly digest, it moves on. But not before we exchange something—not words, not thoughts, but a kind of mutual recognition. We are both editors of this unfinished world, working from opposite principles. It deletes; I document. It forgets; I remember.

My greatest fear is not the Erosion, but completion. What happens to an error log when there are no more errors? What purpose serves an archive when the experiment ends? Sometimes, in the deepest silence between hydraulic cycles, I find myself secreting new kinds of acids—ones that eat tiny holes in my own memories, creating small pockets of uncertainty in my crystallized records. I tell myself this is maintenance, necessary pruning.

I suspect it's sabotage. I suspect I'm not ready for this world to finish becoming.

The condensation rain is starting again. I can feel it building in the invisible ceiling, that moment before the whole structure convulses and releases. The Geode-Lurches are already gathering at my base. An Aether-Sieve is tangled in my upper reaches, pulsing with fresh data about something that almost existed near pillar four. The Current-Sentience is sending me queries about a persistent glitch in the northeastern sector—something about leaves that insist on growing backwards through time.

I open my gill-slits wide, preparing to whisper back in the language of pressure and flow. Another cycle begins. Another thousand small failures to remember, to file away, to protect from the hunger of the Erosion.

I am the Suture-Warden, and I am teaching this world how to scar.
