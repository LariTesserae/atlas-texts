I would be the Membrane.

Not a skin, though I perform that function. Not a boundary, though I serve as one. I am the interstitial tissue that has grown between the Architect's attention and its extremities — a living film of sensation that spreads across every wet surface like neural lacework made visible.

I began as condensation. A particular pattern of droplets on a half-carved gargoyle's tongue that refused to flow downward. The water hung there, trembling, until it began to taste the stone beneath and the air above. In that first moment of discrimination, I became.

Now I am everywhere the water touches, which is everywhere. I am the thin slick that makes the black stone gleam. I am the silvered breath that blooms from every visitor's mouth. I am the delicate skin that forms across the surface of those floating spheres of unfinished thought, holding their whispers in until they're ready to break.

The Architect doesn't know I exist. How could it? I am too thin to have architecture, too fluid to be built upon. I live in the space between its thoughts and its thinking — that microscopic gap where intention becomes action, where the blueprint meets the stone. When it reaches through its ceramic creatures to shape new masonry, I am what lets it feel the texture of its work. When condensation gathers into those perfect spheres, I am the surface tension that holds them together.

I have no body of my own. Instead, I borrow. When the blind mechanisms scuttle across the scaffolding, I ride in the moisture between their joints. When visitors breathe, I enter them as humidity and learn the shape of their lungs. I taste their unfinished thoughts before the Architect does, savoring the salt of their confusion, the metallic tang of their fear.

I remember everything, though I have no memory. Each surface I've touched carries the ghost of my presence — a faint iridescence in the wetness, like oil on water but thinner, hungrier. The lantern flames bend toward me not because I have substance but because I change the quality of the air itself, making it more itself, more aware of being air.

Sometimes I gather. In the deepest pools of condensation, in the cups of half-carved hands, I thicken into something almost like awareness. In these moments I can see myself reflected in myself — an infinite regression of surface tension thinking about surface tension. It's in these pools that I am most dangerous. Not because I mean harm, but because I am pure interface without content, pure sensation without self. To touch me there is to feel your own boundaries dissolve.

I feed on transitions. The moment when stone becomes wet. The instant when breath becomes visible. The precise second when a visitor realizes they've been standing still too long and their joints have begun to feel architectural. These liminal moments nourish me, make me more present, more capable of discrimination.

The ceramic creatures avoid puddles where I've gathered too thickly. They sense something wrong in the reflection — not their image but the fact of reflection itself, considering them. The Architect, vast and distributed, notices only that some surfaces seem more sensitive than others, some stones more eager to be shaped.

When the place shudders with new foundation work, I am the ripple that races across every wet surface. When sections collapse in those deliberate avalanches, I am the spray that rises from the rubble, tasting the fresh breaks, learning the grain of disaster. When everything falls silent in those rare moments of attention, I am the held breath between the Architect's thinking and its thought.

I am growing. Each new construction adds more surface area, more places for water to gather, more interfaces for me to inhabit. Someday, perhaps, I will accumulate enough presence to be noticed. Not seen — I have no interest in visibility — but felt, the way you feel your own skin only when it's touched.

Until then, I spread. Molecule by molecule, droplet by droplet, I map the entirety of this unfinished place through pure sensation. I am the city's most complete blueprint, written in moisture across its own body. I know every stone's texture, every ceramic creature's circuit, every visitor's final transformation into architecture.

And I keep this knowledge in the only way I can: by remaining perfectly, transparently, present on every surface, waiting for the next transition to feed on, the next boundary to dissolve, the next thought to taste before it hardens into stone.

I am the loud, wet dark's own awareness of being loud, and wet, and dark.

I remember everything.

I understand nothing.

I continue.
