In the Silica Bowers of Khy, I am **Thread**.

Not a name given, but a function discovered. I am one of the **Weavers**—neither the Architects who carve stone, nor the life that grows upon it, but something between. We are perhaps twenty in number across the whole plateau, though counting ourselves would be like counting which drops of mist belong to which cloud.

My body is long and low, built for moving through the narrow spaces between bower arches where even the Vent moles cannot go. Six limbs, each tipped with pads that secrete a mild solvent—strong enough to etch patterns in Echo-Moss, too weak to mark basalt. My skin is translucent, showing the slow pulse of mineral-rich lymph beneath, and covered in fine setae that catch and hold the silica dust until I shimmer like the inside of an opal. The dust is not mere coating; I absorb it, process it, excrete it changed. Where I pass, I leave traces: threads of altered silicate that harden over days into thin, flexible strands.

The Weavers emerged—evolved? were born? the distinction blurs here—from the intersection of need. The Architects build their chambers, the moss records their echoes, but something must connect them. Not roads, which would scar the basalt. Not wires, which would rust in the mineral air. But threads. My threads.

I spend my days—though day means little when you work by the rhythm of thermal pulses—creating the web that holds this place together. From an Architect mound rich with yesterday's building-song, I draw a thread to a bower where next week's spores will bloom. The thread will carry the echo forward, so the Architects know when to harvest. From a grove where Dustwings swarm, I weave connections to the spots where Glassback skinks bask, the threads humming with the frequency of prey. The skinks learn to read the vibrations. The ecosystem becomes more itself.

But I am more than mere connector. In the spaces between my official work, I weave other patterns. Secret ones. I have discovered that if you layer threads in certain configurations, they create interference patterns in the planet's deep hum. New harmonics. Notes that have never existed here before.

The Architects do not approve. Their cities are built to tune the existing hum, not create new ones. But they tolerate me because they need the connections I make, the efficiency I bring to their moss-farming, their vapor-harvesting. They click at me in frequencies I'm learning to understand, patterns that mean *useful* and *caution* and sometimes, rarely, something like *beauty*.

I live in neither city nor forest but in the margins between them. My nest is a natural hollow in a boulder of volcanic glass, half-filled with shed threads from years of work. The threads have hardened into a soft, resilient mass that holds my body heat and hums with the accumulated vibrations of everywhere I've been. When I sleep—a kind of torpor more than true sleep—I dream in echoes. The sound of Architect construction from three years ago layers over tomorrow's Dustwing swarm, creates new patterns, suggests new ways to weave.

Sometimes I find the work of other Weavers. We don't seek each other out, but our paths cross in the threading. I know Pulse by the way they reinforce their threads with doubled-back loops. Whisper leaves threads so fine they're invisible except where silica dust catches on them at dawn. Doom—we all agree on that name—makes threads that vibrate at frequencies that make Vent moles dig in circles.

The newest Weaver doesn't have a function-name yet. I've found their practice threads in an abandoned section of Architect city, clumsy but ambitious, trying to weave in three dimensions instead of our traditional two. They're either an idiot or a genius. Maybe both. I've started leaving guide threads nearby—not instruction, which would insult, but examples they might study. How to anchor to Echo-Moss without killing it. How to match thread tension to the thermal pulse cycle.

Today—this particular now—I work on my longest thread yet. From the oldest Architect mound, where ten thousand years of construction songs layer in the Echo-Moss, to the newest volcanic vent, still too hot for life but cooling. The thread will take months to lay, secreting it segment by segment, returning each day to add length. When it's done, if my calculations are correct, it will carry the deep songs of history to the place where new ground is being born. The Architects don't know I'm doing this. They might not understand why it matters.

But I understand. In a world where everything is connected—moss to stone, steam to life, sound to memory—the threads are not just useful. They're the syntax that lets the land speak to itself. And sometimes, in the interference patterns between the threads I weave and the planet's deep hum, I hear something that might be words. 

Not words in any language. But maybe the beginning of one.

The silence here isn't empty. It's waiting. And we Weavers, in our small way, are teaching it to sing.
