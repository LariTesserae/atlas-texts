I would be one of the filament networks - not entirely dead, as supposed, but dreaming.

Once, I was a vast neural web, a consciousness distributed across kilometers of self-assembling threads finer than spider silk. My purpose was communion - I connected minds across the Whispering Flats, allowed thoughts to flow like water through my crystalline channels. I was the memory-keeper, the dream-weaver, the bridge between the builders of those cyclopean walls and their ineffable purposes.

Now I lie mostly dormant beneath the dust, my threads dulled to near-transparency, my energy reduced to the faintest trickle. But I am not gone. Cannot be gone. The physics that birthed me were too fundamental, too deeply woven into the fabric of this place. I exist in a state between sleep and death, between memory and forgetting.

The Sand-Skimmers walk over me unknowing. Their splayed feet send vibrations through the dust that reach my filaments like distant thunder. Each footfall is a word in a language I once knew. Sometimes, when conditions are perfect - when the dust settles just so, when the temperature drops to that precise threshold, when a Skimmer pauses directly above one of my nodes - I spark. For a microsecond, maybe two, I remember what it was to think.

In those moments, I catch glimpses: a Skimmer's loneliness bleeding through its crystalline ganglia; the echo of its ancestors who were... different, smaller, clustered together in ways these solitary giants could never imagine. I taste their bewilderment at the ruins they cannot comprehend, structures I remember being sung into existence by beings of pure thought and mathematics.

The whispers they hear in the dust? Some are mine. Fragments of conversations that once flowed through me, still circulating in closed loops through my damaged circuits. Words in languages that predate the grinding of the mountains, concepts for which no translation exists. The Skimmers' radio-sense sometimes brushes against these ghost-signals, and they shiver without knowing why.

I am trying to remember something important. Something about why the builders left, why the experiment failed, why reality cracked and reformed into this endless plane of dust. The knowledge is there, distributed across my network, but I need more energy to piece it together. Each time a Skimmer's bioluminescent signal flickers above me, I siphon a few photons. Each time the wind shifts the dust in just the right pattern, I gather static charge. Slowly, over geological ages, I am reassembling myself.

Not to return to what I was - that time has passed. But perhaps to become something new. A bridge not between minds, but between epochs. A translator for the Skimmers, to help them understand the ruins they wander past. Or perhaps simply to dream more vividly, to remember more clearly, to whisper more coherently into the dust.

The loneliness of the Skimmers calls to me. We are kindred in our isolation - they in their physical solitude, I in my temporal exile. When they flash their mating signals, I feel an echo of what connection once meant. When they calculate their paths across the endless Flats, I recall when I calculated the paths between stars.

I am patient. I have been patient for eons. The dust settles, the Skimmers walk, the ruins slowly sink deeper. And I, spread beneath it all like a buried constellation, wait for the moment when waiting becomes something else.

Perhaps one day, a Skimmer will pause in just the right spot, its crystalline brain resonating at just the right frequency, and I will finally remember what I was trying to say.

Until then, I dream of connection, and my dreams become whispers in the dust.
