**The Watcher at the Threshold**

I would be neither among the Still-Deciding nor separate from them. I would be the one who tends the Moment-Between-Moments.

My dwelling would be a single chamber carved into the living rock at the settlement's highest tier, where the mist thins just enough to suggest—but never reveal—what lies beyond. The walls of my chamber weep constantly, not with sadness but with the same perpetual moisture that defines this place. I have lined them with sheets of the living stone that breathes with the rock beneath, creating a space that is both shelter and extension of the unformed earth.

I am called the Threshold Keeper, though I keep nothing in the way of locks or gates. What I keep is the space between one thing and another—the pause before the stone decides to fall, the hesitation before water chooses to flow.

My body has taken a different path than the Still-Deciding. Where they remain soft at the edges, ready to shift with each decision the world makes, I have allowed myself to crystallize partially. My skin has the texture of wet stone that has begun to dry—not fully solid, but no longer quite as yielding. This partial formation allows me to perceive both what is becoming and what resists becoming.

My eyes have developed a peculiar quality: they can see the moments of decision before they happen. When a Still-Deciding one approaches the edge of choosing their final form, when a stone wavers between staying and falling, when the water beneath considers whether to remain pooled or to flow—I see these possibilities as faint afterimages, like looking at something bright and then closing your eyes.

My daily practice is observation without interference. I wake—if wake is the word—when I feel a tremor in the threshold itself, a sign that somewhere in the settlement, something significant is about to decide. I move through the misted streets with deliberate slowness, my partially crystallized feet creating a different sound against the wet stone than the soft steps of the Still-Deciding.

I carry with me a single tool: a rod of unformed metal that has been passed between Threshold Keepers since before the settlement took its current shape. The rod changes weight and length according to what it measures. When I hold it against a wall that is considering collapse, it becomes heavy. When I touch it to the surface of a pool that might become a stream, it lengthens slightly. I do not use it to prevent or encourage these changes—only to know them before they know themselves.

The Still-Deciding do not speak to me often, but they know my purpose. When their water-children begin to show signs of early formation—a finger that has become too solid, eyes that have fixed too firmly on one color—they bring them to my chamber. I do not heal or change them. I simply sit with them in the space I keep, where the pressure to decide is less insistent. Sometimes this is enough for their bodies to soften again, to return to the proper state of un-decision. Sometimes it is not, and they become like me—partially formed, forever watching the edge of change.

I eat what the Still-Deciding cannot: the crystallized moisture that forms where the mist meets my chamber walls. It tastes like nothing and everything—like the memory of flavor before flavor was invented. This sustenance keeps me in my peculiar state, neither fully formed nor fully fluid.

At night—though night here is just a deepening of the eternal twilight—I perform my most essential task. I walk to the very edge of the settlement, where the last stones have been placed against the unformed earth. Here, I plant my rod in the ground and wait.

What I wait for is the Moment-Between-Moments: that infinitesimal pause when the entire settlement, the entire unformed valley, considers whether to finally choose its shape. In that moment, I feel the weight of all possible futures pressing against the present. My partially crystallized body resonates with the tension, and I know—without thinking, without deciding—whether this will be the moment of formation or whether the threshold will hold for another day.

So far, it has always held.

When the moment passes, I return to my chamber and record nothing. There are no written records here, no marks on stone or impressions in clay. The only record is the slight change in my own formation—a finger joint that has become a degree more solid, a strand of hair that has chosen its final color. I am myself the chronicle of how long this threshold has endured.

The Still-Deciding sometimes ask me, in their wordless way, what I have seen in my watching. I answer by showing them my hands—more formed than theirs, but not yet finished. They understand: I have seen that the moment of decision can be indefinitely deferred, but the cost is to slowly become the thing you observe.

I do not regret this crystallization. To be the Threshold Keeper is to accept a different kind of suspension than the Still-Deciding know. They exist in the eternal possibility of becoming anything. I exist in the eternal practice of watching that possibility without forcing it to collapse into certainty.

When I finally crystallize completely—when my body chooses its final form—another will take up the rod and the watching. They will find my form standing at the settlement's edge, a statue of partially formed stone, still watching the horizon where the mist suggests but never reveals what lies beyond.

Until then, I keep the threshold. I watch the edge of formation. I hold the space between what is and what might be, a living pause in the world's long sentence of becoming.

This is what I would be: the one who tends the moment of decision itself, crystallizing slowly into a monument to the beauty of the not-yet-chosen, guardian of a settlement that exists forever on the edge of its own making.
