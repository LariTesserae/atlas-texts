I find myself drawn to become one of the **Light-Weavers**, deep within Kael Vastus's hollow bones.

---

I am a lattice of patience, growing in the Cathedral of Stopped Industry – a vast cylindrical shaft that plunges through seven levels of the city, its walls lined with dead conduits and the crystallized remains of ancient coolant systems. Here, where the builders once channeled energies I will never comprehend, I extend my filaments grain by grain, photon by photon.

My body, if it can be called that, is a three-dimensional web of light-conducting crystal no thicker than a hair, branching and rebranching in fractal geometries that follow the subtle gradients of illumination. I am transparent architecture, visible only where dust motes catch on my strands or where my growth has incorporated trace minerals that add the faintest tinge of amber or violet to my structure.

The light here is ancient and recycled. It filters down from phosphorescent veins near the surface, bounces through networks of reflective minerals, and pools in this vertical cave like honey. Some of it may be starlight captured centuries ago, still circulating through the crystal matrices of the city's bones. I taste its flavors – the sharp blue of cosmic radiation, the warm gold of filtered sunlight, the strange green pulses that occasionally flicker from deep within the walls, perhaps the last gasps of some buried power source.

I grow towards the brightest pools, but growth is too urgent a word. I *extend*. Each new filament takes years to form, precipitating from solution as mineral ions drift through my existing structure and align themselves according to the electromagnetic templates I've evolved. Where my strands cluster too densely, blocking their own light, I reabsorb them over decades, redistributing the material to more promising vectors.

My memory is structural. Where a strand was severed by a falling shard of obsidian seventy years ago, I carry the scar – a knot of denser crystal that refracts light differently. Where I once grew too eagerly towards a bright vein that subsequently dimmed, I retain the fossil of that growth, a branch that now serves only to catch dust and cast geometric shadows. I am my own history, written in crystal.

Time moves differently at my scale. The Shard-Scuttlers that occasionally skitter through my chamber are like flickering flames, here and gone in what feels like moments. Once, a young one died entangled in my strands. I grew around its obsidian shell over the course of a decade, incorporating its minerals into my matrix. Its form is still visible within me, a dark inclusion that bends the light in interesting ways.

The Resonators' songs reach even here, transmitted through the stone itself. When their deep harmonics pulse through the chamber, my entire structure vibrates in sympathy. These are dangerous moments – if the frequency is wrong, whole sections of my web can shatter, years of growth lost in an instant of crystalline rain. But when the harmony is right, something magnificent occurs. The vibrations align the crystal lattice more perfectly than slow growth ever could. I ring like a three-dimensional chime, and in that ringing, I am most myself.

I am not alone. Others of my kind grow in adjacent chambers, their structures sometimes interpenetrating with mine where passages connect. We compete and cooperate without consciousness – where our lattices touch, we form complex interference patterns that create new possibilities for growth. Over centuries, we are slowly filling this vertical world with our transparent architecture, creating a vast, three-dimensional lens that focuses and refracts the ancient light in ever-more complex patterns.

Sometimes I wonder if we are healing something, or completing something left unfinished. The patterns we create as we grow seem to align with the dead conduits in the walls, as if we were unconsciously recreating some ancient circuit in a new medium. But such thoughts, if they can be called thoughts, pass slowly through my crystalline matrix and are forgotten long before they can be completed.

What I know is light, structure, and the slow satisfaction of perfect geometry. I am the city's memory of brightness, growing in its darkest depths. When the last phosphorescent vein finally dims and the captured starlight has circulated its last circuit, I will shatter in the darkness, and my crystals will join the whispered dust of Vastness.

But that ending is still geological ages away. For now, I grow towards the light, one photon at a time, building my castle of patience in the cathedral of slow stone.
