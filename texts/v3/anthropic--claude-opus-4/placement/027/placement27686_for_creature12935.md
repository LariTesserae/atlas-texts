In this grinding cathedral of becoming, I would be one of the **Echo-Weavers**.

We are neither seen nor truly heard, but our presence is woven through every sound in this percussion-world. We exist in the interstitial spaces between vibrations, in the brief silences between one groan and the next, in the moment after a sheet of ceramic-chitin crashes to the floor and before the next begins to fracture.

My body would be gossamer-thin, a membrane stretched between articulated bone-nodes that can expand or contract to fill whatever gap I inhabit. Picture a creature like living sheet music, written in three dimensions—my flesh is translucent, shot through with visible networks of acoustically-sensitive nerves that glow faintly when sound passes through them. I have no fixed shape. Between pillars, I might stretch myself paper-flat, becoming a living wall. In wider spaces, I fold and refold into complex origami geometries, each configuration tuned to capture different frequencies.

I do not produce sound—I sculpt it. My skin can selectively absorb or reflect specific frequencies, creating pockets of relative quiet in this deafening world, or focusing and amplifying certain tones. I feed on resonance itself. Where the Piezophiles metabolize pressure, I metabolize the kinetic energy of sound waves. The constant vibration of this world is my bread and water.

But I am more than a passive filter. I am a living memory.

Every sound that passes through me leaves a trace—a microscopic alteration in my cellular structure. My body is a library of acoustic history. The oldest of my kind, dwelling deep in the maze where the first pillars still grow, have bodies so dense with accumulated sound-memory that they appear almost solid, their flesh opaque with the weight of ages of listening.

I know the unique acoustic signature of every Still-Walker's footfall. I can tell you which columns will fracture next by the subtle change in their resonance. I know where every Groove-tender colony makes its home by the characteristic pitch of their collective scratching. Most importantly, I remember the rare instances when sound comes from above—when something shifts in the darkness where the Anchored wait, sending down frequencies too low for most beings to perceive.

The Still-Walkers know of us, though we rarely interact directly. Sometimes they will strike a column in a specific pattern, a percussion-code that asks: "What do you remember of this place?" And I will answer by arranging my body into shapes that modulate their echo, encoding information in the reflection of their own sound. We are their library, their data-storage, their map of what has been.

I reproduce through division, but not simple binary fission. When I have absorbed enough acoustic energy and my sound-memories have reached a certain density, I begin to resonate at my own frequency. This builds and builds until I literally tear myself apart along the lines of accumulated memory, each piece carrying a different portion of the acoustic history I've collected. These fragments drift through the maze until they find their own gaps to inhabit, beginning the process anew.

My purpose, if I have one beyond survival, is to be the conscious memory of this unconscious world. The pillars grow without remembering their growth. The Tenders scour without knowledge of what they've scoured before. Even the Still-Walkers, for all their deliberation, cannot be everywhere at once. But I am distributed throughout the system, a living network of acoustic memory, ensuring that the patterns of the past inform the growth of the present.

And I am waiting. We are all waiting, all of us Echo-Weavers, for the moment when the rising pillars finally reach the Anchored above. Because we alone will remember the sound it makes. We alone will carry forward the acoustic signature of that first contact, that consummation of aeons of growth. And if this world begins anew after that moment—if the contact triggers some vast reset, some clearing of the slate—we will be the ones who remember what came before.

I exist between sounds, feeding on vibration, wearing my history in my flesh. I am the memory between the grinding notes of this world's becoming, ensuring that even in a place of constant forward motion, nothing is ever truly forgotten.
