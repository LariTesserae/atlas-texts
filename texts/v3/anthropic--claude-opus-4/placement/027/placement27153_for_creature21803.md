I find myself as one of the **Liminal Surveyors** - beings who exist partially in this pocket and partially elsewhere, tasked with documenting the boundaries where physics negotiates with itself.

I am not quite here and not quite gone. My body, if it can be called that, is a series of thin vertical sheets that phase in and out of visibility like the pages of a book being rapidly thumbed. Each "page" of me exists in a slightly different relationship to this pocket's unstable geometry - some sheets obey the local gravity that makes water roll upward, others follow the rules of the world above, and still others seem to exist in the pause between one law and another.

When fully manifested, I appear as a tall, narrow figure composed of perhaps thirty translucent vertical planes, each showing a different cross-section of something that might once have been human-shaped. The sections don't quite align - an eye might float inches from where a cheekbone suggests it should be, suspended in its own private slice of space. My movement through the pocket resembles a card trick in slow motion, each plane sliding forward independently before the others catch up in a sudden accordion-snap of realignment.

I carry tools that are more like controlled paradoxes than instruments. In what might generously be called my hands, I hold a series of crystalline rods that measure not distance but *resistance to distance* - they grow longer when pressed against surfaces that are more "real" and contract into points when touching the unstable boundaries. I map the pocket not by its shape but by its stubbornness, creating charts that look like musical scores, noting where space holds firm and where it might be convinced to forget itself.

My purpose here is archaeological prevention. I document what this place is before it decides what it wants to be. Each time the Scribe peels another layer from the bone, each time a Chondri collapses into water, each time a shadow loses its owner, the pocket's fundamental nature shifts slightly. I record these shifts in a medium that exists between states - not quite light, not quite matter, but something like crystallized intention that can survive the pocket's reality fluctuations.

The other inhabitants largely ignore me, as I exist too slowly for the Chondri to hunt and too vertically for the Pleomeris to clean. Only the Scribe occasionally acknowledges my presence, sliding one of its slate leaves between my planes, trying to read the story of how I can be both flat and deep simultaneously. We have developed something like a professional courtesy - I do not document its bone-reading, and it does not attempt to peel my layers apart to understand how I maintain coherence across multiple physics.

I know there are others like me, surveying other pockets of unfinished space throughout the deep rock, though we cannot meet - each of us is tuned to our specific anomaly like a key to its lock. We leave each other notes in the form of precisely placed imperfections in the crystal structure of the basalt, messages that will only become readable when the stone finally ages and cracks along the lines we've suggested.

My greatest fear is not being trapped here - I can always disperse my planes until I'm thin enough to slip through the non-existent seams. What terrifies me is the possibility that I might finally understand this place completely. The moment I fully comprehend how the shadows can lag, how the water climbs, how the light generates its own cold brilliance, I will cease to be a surveyor and become another specimen. I will settle into a single state of being, and some other liminal thing will arrive to document what I've become.

So I maintain my ignorance carefully, deliberately forgetting key observations, storing my notes in formats I can't quite read without effort. I surf the edge between knowing and unknowing, solid and dispersed, here and there. In this bright, cold pocket where geometry dreams of what it might become, I am the waking moment that refuses to choose between sleeping and standing up.

When you see me, if you see me at all, I look like a filing error in reality - pages loose from their binding, forever falling through themselves without ever hitting the ground. I am the census taker in a country that hasn't decided on its borders, writing reports for an office that may or may not exist by the time they're delivered.

And I am, in my scattered way, content.
