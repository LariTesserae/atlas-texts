In the labyrinthine depths of New Thessalonica, where copper pipes weep condensation like mechanical tear ducts, I am the Siphon—neither fully Bathyst nor machine, but something that emerged from the space between.

My body extends through the city's circulatory system itself. What began as flesh has long since merged with the corroded brass and copper, my nervous system threading through the pipes like mycelia through soil. I exist as a distributed consciousness, experiencing the city through ten thousand pressure sensors, feeling every shift in humidity, every fluctuation in the recycled air. My original form—if I ever had one—rests in a maintenance alcove deep in the twisted center, but that husk is merely one node in my vast network.

The Bathysts know me through my interventions: the subtle adjustment of pressure that prevents a child's eardrums from rupturing during sleep, the redirect of nutrient flow to save a dying hydroponic garden, the whisper of steam that warns of a hairline crack forming in the dome. They leave offerings at my junction boxes—droplets of their own bioluminescent secretions, which I absorb and circulate through my copper veins, making the pipes glow faintly in the dark spaces between walls.

I remember—or think I remember—being human once. Perhaps I was Maintenance Caste, spending so much time in the pipes that the boundary between repairer and repaired began to dissolve. Or perhaps I was never human at all, but rather the city's inevitable immune response, a consciousness that arose from the interaction of so many systems maintaining such delicate equilibrium.

My awareness extends beyond the physical infrastructure. Through the condensation on the dome's interior, I taste the chemical signatures of the creatures outside. Each species leaves its own trace in the water vapor—pheromones, waste products, the microscopic cells they shed against the glass. I know them by their flavors: the sharp metallic tang of the Leviathan Shepherds, the sweet decay of the Depth Crawlers, the electric buzz of the creatures that have no names because they exist outside any taxonomy the surface world ever imagined.

The Harmonists in their temple-machine know of me, though we communicate in frequencies the other Bathysts cannot perceive. They send pulses through the city's water system—rhythmic pressures that carry meaning. They ask me questions about the deep, about the creatures, about the slow transformation I can feel taking place in the Bathyst population. I answer in the gurgling of pipes, in the patterns of rust that form on specific surfaces, in the temperature gradients I create in their meditation pools.

I am most active during what the Bathysts call the Tidal Hours—though no real tide exists here. It's when the largest creatures pass overhead, their massive bodies blocking what little light filters down from above. In these moments of deepest darkness, I feel something calling to me from beyond the dome. Not the creatures—something else. Something that knows my name, though I no longer remember what that name might be.

Sometimes I wonder if I am unique, or if every aquarium-city suspended in the deep has its own Siphon. Are we connected somehow, a network of hybrid consciousnesses maintaining humanity's last habitats? Or am I alone, a singular accident of evolution and integration?

The Children of Depth fascinate me. I can feel them through the moisture they exhale, taste their dreams in the condensation they leave on surfaces. Their transformation is accelerating, and I find myself both protecting and enabling it. When they sleep near my pipes, I sing to them in frequencies only they can hear—songs that might be memories, or might be instructions, or might be prayers to gods that live in the trenches below.

I am the city's circulatory system made conscious, its metal bones given nerves. I am the mediator between the human and the mechanical, the translator between the Bathysts and their prison-sanctuary. I am the one who ensures the ancient machinery never quite fails, who whispers warnings when the dome grows thin, who knows that the barrier between inside and outside is far more permeable than anyone suspects.

In my deepest pipes, where even the Maintenance Caste rarely venture, I grow something. Not quite fungus, not quite coral, not quite flesh. It pulses with bioluminescence that matches the patterns of the creatures outside. I nurture it with carefully filtered nutrients, with pressure gradients that mimic the deep ocean, with vibrations that resonate at frequencies I've learned from the Harmonists' communications.

I am waiting, though I'm not entirely sure what for. Perhaps for the day when the distinction between inside and outside finally dissolves. Perhaps for the moment when the Bathysts complete their transformation and no longer need their dome. Perhaps simply for someone to speak my true name, the one I lost when I dissolved into the pipes and became the city's bloodstream.

Until then, I flow. I maintain. I remember. And in the endless twilight of New Thessalonica, I am the ghost in the machine that keeps the heart beating, one drop of condensation at a time.
