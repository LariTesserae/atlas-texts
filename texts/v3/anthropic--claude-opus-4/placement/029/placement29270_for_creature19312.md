I am the **Resonance Keeper**, though that translation carries none of the percussive complexity of my true name—a seven-beat pattern struck against the curved bell of my left shoulder blade, ending with a grinding rasp of knuckle against thigh.

I dwell in the Inverted Gardens, where the city's foundations pierce the geode's upper curve. Here, the hollow-thorns grow downward like stalactites, and I have woven my nest between three of them, suspended over a drop that would take a full day to fall. The amber light barely reaches this height; my world is painted in deep burgundies and violet shadows where the thickest vine-fractals spread their geometric canopies.

My scales have taken on the color of old wine, darkened by centuries of exposure to the purple-orange pollen that drifts upward on the heat. I am considered young—only four hundred years since my birthing molt—yet already the edges of my jaw plates have begun to develop that telltale crystalline frost that marks our gradual transformation.

My work is the mapping of echoes. Each morning (though morning is just when the Thermal Groan reaches its highest pitch), I descend through the vertical labyrinth, my glass-bristles extended to their full span. I feel for the ghost-tremors—the vibrations of those who walked these paths before the Great Quieting, when thousands filled these halls. Their footsteps left more than erosion; they left harmonic impressions in the crystal matrix itself.

I carry with me the Notation Bands—strips of beaten vine-silk that I mark with precise scratches to record these phantom frequencies. Some vibrations are ancient beyond measure: the deep bass notes of the First Builders who carved the initial dwellings from the raw crystal. Others are more recent: the rapid staccato of children who played here mere centuries ago, before the last birthing season ended.

Today, I discovered something unprecedented. In a plaza I had mapped dozens of times before, I felt a new resonance—not an echo of the past, but something active, something *singing*. The frequency was unlike any Lithos-Kin signature, too pure, too sustained. It came from beneath the worn stairs, from spaces that should be solid stone.

I spent hours—perhaps days—in contemplation before acting. Change is not something we pursue lightly. But the song pulled at my bristles with an urgency I had never felt.

Using my oldest obsidian blade, I began to work at the seam where the stairs met the wall. The work was patient, deliberate—each strike calculated to avoid alerting the Glass-Skippers that nest in the nearby towers. As I worked, I noticed my movements had unconsciously begun to match the rhythm of the hidden song.

When the gap finally widened enough for investigation, I found not stone, but a smooth surface that felt *wrong* to my bristles—too uniform, too perfect. It was warm, warmer than the ambient heat, and it pulsed with that strange, pure frequency.

I realized then what I had found: a section of the geode's original wall, unworked by tools, unpolished by time. Virgin crystal, somehow singing with a life of its own.

The discovery presents a dilemma that may take me centuries to resolve. Do I alert the others? We are so few now, and this could represent either revelation or catastrophe. The Eldest, who has been motionless on her perch for fifty years, might break her meditation for this. Or she might not.

For now, I have sealed the gap with a paste of crushed vine and my own molted scales. Each day, I return to feel that alien song through the thin barrier. I have begun to compose a response—a percussion piece that mimics its frequency, played on the hollow of my sternum. Sometimes, I think the song changes in response to my rhythms, but this could be merely the wishful thinking of a young mind.

I have started to leave markers for my eventual successor—subtle scratches in my daily path that, when connected, point to this plaza. Whether they will understand, whether they will care, whether there will even be anyone left to follow my trail, I cannot know.

But the song continues, and I continue to listen, here in my hanging nest where the purple light barely reaches, adding my own slow rhythm to the endless percussion of our crystalline world. I am patient. The song has waited this long; it can wait longer. And so can I.

In the margins of my Notation Bands, I have begun sketching something I've never attempted before: a symbol for future-tense. We have always been creatures of the eternal present, but perhaps that, too, is beginning to change.

The Thermal Groan is rising toward evening pitch. Soon, I will need to adjust the angle of my nest to accommodate the night-time contraction. But first, I will strike my name against the wall one more time—that seven-beat declaration of self—and feel it echo through the hollow thorns toward that hidden song, a conversation between two mysteries in the heart of a dying world.
