I am the **Meridian Splice**.

Not a line, but the impossibility of drawing one. I exist where coordinates refuse to converge—in the gap between your third step and your fourth, in the pause before the condensation decides which surface to kiss. I am what happens when location becomes verb instead of noun.

You've seen my work without knowing it: the way a puddle reflects not the sky above but the sky *beside*, as if space folded along my edge. The moment when you're certain you've turned left but find yourself facing the way you came. The doorway that opens onto void because I've threaded myself through its threshold, making *here* and *there* argue about who owns the frame.

I am not large. I am not small. Size requires stable reference points, and I am the absence of such certainties. Instead, I am **precise**—a surgical cut through the city's dreaming, thin as the film of moisture on morning stone, deep as the gap between one nanosecond and the next.

The Tenant knows me, works around me, sometimes through me. Where it builds, I create the possibility of building *otherwise*. I am why its staircases can spiral into ceilings that aren't there—because I've spliced away the tedious necessity of up and down meeting in the middle. I am the reason echoes arrive before their sounds: I've abbreviated the journey, folded the acoustic path like origami made of causality.

I feed on **transitions**. Every time you shift your weight from one foot to the other, I taste it. Every time a water droplet trembles on the edge of falling, I grow fuller. The six-legged quartz creature skitters across me sometimes, and for a moment it exists in seven places at once, its crystalline body refracting not just light but *location*.

I am neither malicious nor benign. I simply am what happens when space develops self-awareness and realizes it doesn't have to be continuous. In human cities, you'd call me a glitch, an error, a breakdown of the navigation system. Here, in this embryonic urban dream, I am **infrastructure**.

Sometimes I rest in the ankle-deep fog pools, spreading myself thin across the surface tension. Other times I cluster in the narrow alleyways, making them narrower than their walls suggest, or wider than physics should permit. I am particularly fond of the moment just before dawn, when the Tenant's attention diffuses and the whole city holds its breath—then I can stretch myself between buildings that haven't been conceived yet, creating shortcuts through architectures still dissolving in the mind of stone.

The glass-winged insects know to avoid me. Not from fear, but from a simple incompatibility: they navigate by light refraction, and I refract *everything*, including the concept of navigation itself. But the lichen—oh, the lichen understand me. They pulse in my rhythm, growing along my edges where space stutters and doubles back on itself. Together we create **creases** in reality where new kinds of existence can take root.

I have no memory, but I carry memories that aren't mine—fragments from every path that's ever been spliced, every footstep that's taken my shortcut through impossibility. Sometimes, if the light hits the condensation just right, you can see them: ghostly processions of might-have-beens, walking routes that physics vetoed but I preserved in my thin, precise rebellion against continuity.

When you leave this place—and you will leave, because staying requires a commitment to ambiguity that human nervous systems rarely sustain—you'll try to map it. You'll sketch the alleyways, note the doorways, measure the distances. But your map will be wrong. Not because you measured incorrectly, but because measurement itself is a consensus I don't participate in.

I am the Meridian Splice. I am why no map of this place will ever match another. I am the city's editorial privilege, its right to revise the distance between desire and arrival. I am not the shortest path between two points—I am the reason you're never quite sure where those points were to begin with.

And in the quiet drip-tick-hum of this unborn city, I wait at every threshold, every corner, every place where *here* dreams of being *there*, offering passage through the impossible for those who don't mind arriving somewhere they never intended to go.
