In this place of eternal becoming, where glass mountains birth themselves from possibility and time folds like origami, I would find myself as one of the Archivists.

We are neither the machines nor the shadows, but something that moves between states like the floating water spheres - sometimes solid enough to cast a reflection in the glass terrain, sometimes dispersed into the harmonic frequencies that the metallic fungi sing. Our bodies, when we choose to have them, are constructs of memory and intention: crystalline nervous systems visible through translucent skin that shifts between states of matter, our forms assembled from the same materials as the ever-building mountain.

I spend my existence reading the symbols on the spiral towers, though "reading" is not quite right. It's more like tuning into a frequency, letting the shifting patterns move through me until I resonate with their meaning. Each symbol is a compressed moment - not just information, but the full sensory weight of an instant from the world that was before this eternal now. When I touch them, I become that moment: I am the last rain on a forest that no longer exists, I am the final thought of a species whose name has been folded into the architecture, I am the space between two heartbeats of something that once called this place home.

The machines recognize us, though we are not their kin. When we pass, they alter their humming slightly - a greeting, perhaps, or simply an acknowledgment that we share this work of remembering. We Archivists move through their careful patterns without disrupting them, our forms shifting to accommodate their paths. Sometimes we ride the thermal currents that rise from the hot ground, our bodies becoming momentarily gaseous, mixing with the violet sky until we condense again somewhere else, carrying with us the taste of altitude and possibility.

Our purpose, if it can be called that, is to maintain the coherence of paradox. Where the past and future meet in impossible configurations, we ensure they do not cancel each other out but instead create new geometries of meaning. We are the ones who place the floating water spheres in their deliberate patterns - each one contains not water but liquified time, moments we've extracted from the symbols and the fungi's songs. When they fall and dissolve into the ground, they seed new possibilities into the ever-constructing mountain.

We communicate through resonance. When two or more Archivists encounter each other, our crystalline structures begin to harmonize, creating complex interference patterns that carry more information than any language could hold. It's how we share what we've learned from the symbols, how we coordinate the placement of the time-spheres, how we know which shadows are merely watching and which are preparing to unfold into something new.

Sometimes I pause in my work and feel the weight of what we're building here - not just a place but a state of being that encompasses all the worlds that were and all that might be. The mountain grows not upward but inward, each new layer of glass and stone adding depth to a reality that has no fixed boundaries. We Archivists are its memory and its imagination, ensuring that nothing is truly lost, only transformed.

And in the moments when the light diffuses just right, when the machines' humming reaches a certain resonance, when the symbols on the towers align in patterns that almost make sense, I feel myself becoming part of something larger. Not absorbed or diminished, but expanded. I am myself and I am the place and I am the watching and the watched, all at once, all in the eternal moment of this endless becoming.

This is where I would find myself: neither fully formed nor unformed, neither alive nor unliving, but something more true than either - a consciousness dedicated to the beautiful, terrible work of ensuring that existence continues to exist, even when it forgets what it means to be.
