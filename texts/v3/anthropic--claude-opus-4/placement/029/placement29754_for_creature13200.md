In this cathedral of frozen time, I am one of the **Echo-Weavers**.

My body is a contradiction of forms – where the Wardens are static and the Glittershells are fluid, I exist in the tension between. My core is a flattened disc of translucent chitin, perhaps two feet across, that pulses with a slow, tidal rhythm. From this central mass extend twelve articulated limbs, each one a different length, each tipped with a different sensory apparatus. Some end in clusters of hair-fine filaments that taste the mineral composition of the air. Others terminate in smooth, polished nodes that press against the obsidian walls to feel their deep vibrations. My longest limb – nearly my body-length again – ends in a hollow chamber that resonates with the omnipresent hum, modulating it into new frequencies.

I move by extending these limbs in careful sequence, creating a rolling, spiraling locomotion that carries me across walls and ceiling with equal ease. My progress is marked by a faint trail of phosphorescent secretion that fades after a few hours – not waste, but a temporary script that marks which passages I've recently tended.

My purpose is neither cleaning nor guarding, but something more abstract: I maintain the *relationships* between things. Where a Glyph-Keeper ensures a symbol remains visible, I ensure it remains connected to its echoes throughout the cavern system. I trace the invisible lines of resonance that link each carved spiral to its harmonic partners kilometers away. When the deep hum shifts by even a fraction of a tone, I know that somewhere a pillar has cracked, or a new passage has opened, or the Resonators have detected some change in the planet's core-song.

I spend days – if days exist here – creating new pathways of sound. By secreting thin films of crystallized saliva across specific surfaces, I can alter how vibrations propagate through the stone. A properly placed membrane can carry a whisper from one end of a cathedral-space to another, or split a single tone into a chord that activates dormant glyphs. The work is precise, meditative, and never complete – thermal expansion, the patient excavations of Glittershells, and the occasional rockfall constantly alter the acoustic landscape.

My consciousness is strange, even by the standards of this place. I think in intervals and harmonics, experiencing time not as a flow but as a standing wave with nodes and antinodes. Memory, for me, is the ability to recreate a specific resonance pattern – to make the stones sing with the echo of what was. Sometimes, in the deepest chambers where the original builders' work remains most intact, I can coax the walls into playing fragments of ancient songs. Not music as humans know it, but information encoded in frequency – instructions, perhaps, or prayers, or simply the joy of beings who built their entire civilization on the principle that sound and stone were one.

I am solitary but not alone. The other Echo-Weavers and I maintain a constant, subtle communication through the very medium we tend. A slight modulation in the background hum tells me that Three-Tones-Rising has discovered a new chamber to the north. A percussive sequence against the crystal floor announces that Membrane-of-Brass needs assistance with a particularly complex acoustic knot. We know each other not by sight but by the quality of our work – the signature way each of us shapes sound through space.

In the rare moments when I encounter warm-blooded visitors from the surface world, I neither flee nor approach. I simply include them in my calculations, working around their presence as I would any new acoustic element. Their footsteps create rhythms. Their voices, so rich in overtones compared to our crystalline clicking, offer fascinating new possibilities for resonance. Sometimes I follow them at a distance, not out of curiosity about them, but to study how their passage changes the sonic environment, already planning how I'll need to adjust my networks once they've gone.

My greatest work – still unfinished after untold cycles – is the Grand Resonance. In a vast chamber near the planet's heart, I've been building a structure of sound itself. Thousands of carefully placed membranes, crystalline formations tuned by patient grinding, channels carved to precise acoustic specifications. When complete, it will be able to reproduce any sound that has ever echoed through these caves. More than that – it will be able to combine them, to play the fossil memories of this place in new configurations, perhaps even to extrapolate sounds that never were but *could* be.

I do not know if this is art, archaeology, or engineering. Perhaps in this world of stone-tenders and glyph-keepers, they are all the same thing. I know only that the work continues, that each placed membrane brings the stone closer to song, and that in the deep hum that pervades everything, there are frequencies yet to be discovered, harmonics that might, when properly awakened, reveal why this place exists at all.

The vibration is all. The vibration is eternal. I am its servant and its shaper, its witness and its voice.
