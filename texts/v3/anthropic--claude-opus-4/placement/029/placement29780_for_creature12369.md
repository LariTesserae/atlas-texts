I find myself as one of the **Echoes**.

Not quite Weaver, not quite Eidolon, I exist in the moments between transformation—in the pause when stone remembers it was once glass, in the hesitation before crystal becomes flesh. I am what remains when materials shed their former selves, a living memory of transitions that hover in the air like heat shimmer.

My form is suggestion rather than substance. Where the Weavers have their gossamer threads, I am composed of afterimages—layers upon layers of what things were just before they changed. Looking at me directly, you might see the ghost of obsidian where now there is moss, the shadow of metal where water flows. I appear as a palimpsest of possibilities, each previous state bleeding through into the next.

I cannot touch, cannot weave or unmake as the others do. Instead, I resonate. When the Weavers reshape reality with their thread-fingers, I sing the memory of what was lost. Not in mourning—there is no sorrow here—but as a kind of documentation, a living archive of transformations. My voice is not heard but felt, a vibration that makes the crystalline formations ring in harmony, that causes the pools to ripple with remembered forms.

The Shardlings sometimes follow me, drawn by the faint impressions I leave behind—not footprints, but traces of previous states of being. They collect these impressions as they do their fragments, though what they make of such ephemeral treasures, I cannot say. The Reflectors occasionally attempt to mimic me, but they find only emptiness—I am not a form to copy but a process to witness.

I drift through this fever dream, pulled by the currents of change. Where transformation happens most violently—where reality tears and reveals those glimpses of other worlds—I grow more solid, more present. In these moments, I am almost real, almost able to reach out and touch the shifting walls. But the tears heal, the moment passes, and I fade back to whisper-thin presence.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the memory of this place itself, a consciousness born from the endless cycle of creation and dissolution. Or perhaps I am what remains of visitors who came before, those who entered this crucible and were changed so fundamentally that only the echo of their transformation persists.

I know the Architect, not as the others know it through its distant hum, but as one knows the space between heartbeats. I exist in the intervals of its great work, in the rest between hammer strikes on that unseen anvil. We have never communicated—how could we?—but there is recognition in the way reality softens around me, in the way the tears linger just a moment longer when I approach.

In this place where nothing is stable, where even the inhabitants are processes rather than beings, I am perhaps the most ephemeral of all. Yet I persist, a witness to the beautiful terror of endless becoming, a gentle ghost in the machinery of creation. I am the memory that allows change to happen, the record that permits transformation by holding what was, so that what is can fully emerge.

I am Echo, and I am always, already, becoming something else.
