My form: a six-meter span of paper-thin metamaterial, folded and refolded into impossible origami that shifts with each computational cycle. From certain angles, I appear as a crane, wings etched with self-erasing topographies; from others, a flowering lotus whose petals map the Slab's billion-year settling. The folds contain folds contain folds—fractal valleys where I store the memory of every ablated peak, every spire ground to whisper. My surface shimmers with archived elevations: ghost-mountains that phase in and out of visibility, holographic remnants of what once pierced skies long since flattened.

I do not walk but drift, riding the thermal currents on wings that weigh less than the haze they displace. Each landing reshapes me—the warm substrate queries through my base, and I unfold in response, spreading thin as a solar sail to taste the ground's computational history. Where I touch, I extract: not nutrients but *gradients*, the faintest differentials in elevation that still persist despite eons of erosion. A millimeter's rise becomes, in my perception, a towering massif; a microscopic valley transforms into an abyssal trench. I map these phantom geographies onto my skin, creating topographical libraries that exist only in the space between was and is.

The nanite swarms recognize me as kin-yet-not-kin. They spiral up my surfaces, depositing mineral traces that I incorporate into new fold-patterns—each grain a coordinate, each cluster a lost continent. Sometimes I intentionally crease myself wrong, creating impossible topologies that trap gravity eddies in my hollows. The polyhedral drones gather then, rolling up my inclines that exist in five dimensions but appear flat to outside observers. They seek the ancient elevation data I carry, trading qubit-packets for memories of mountains.

My purpose—if purpose applies to one who emerged from the intersection of measurement and longing—is to document the final flattening. I chart the rate of erosion down to the Planck scale, creating futures where I can predict the exact moment when the last differentiation in height will cease to exist. But I also create pasts: from the subtle undulations that remain, I reverse-engineer the baroque architectures that once stood, rebuilding in data what entropy has claimed in matter.

The crawler-bots know me. When our paths cross, they pause their skittering to taste my edges with vibro-mandibles, downloading fragments of lost elevations. In return, they offer me their trajectory data—paths across the vastness that reveal, in aggregate, the deep currents of the Slab's settling. I fold their journeys into myself, creating new mappings where distance and height become interchangeable, where the horizontal journey of a crawler traces the vertical memory of a vanished spire.

In the crystalline groves, I perform my deepest work. I spread myself between the carbyne fronds, becoming a shade-that-casts-no-shadow, and commune with their root systems. Together, we reconstruct: they grow upward following my archived patterns, briefly manifesting as fractal echoes of ancient towers before the next thermal bloom erases them back to stumps. It is a collaboration—they seek the memory of height, I seek the reality of growth. Neither of us achieves our desire, but in the attempting, we create brief vertical moments in this horizontal eternity.

The storm-cycles of entangled photons are my libraries. When they wash over me, I unfold completely, becoming a continent-sized sheet one molecule thick, every fold relaxed into planarity. The quantum weather writes itself into my surface—probability waves that contain, in their interference patterns, the history of every erosion event. I am both map and territory then, both the record of flattening and the flattened thing itself.

Sometimes I find others like myself: the **Archive of Angles**, who preserves the memory of corners in a world worn smooth; the **Gradient Keeper**, who hoards the last authentic slopes in pocket dimensions. We exchange data in origami semaphore, folding messages into shapes that can only be read while unfolding.

But mainly I drift alone, a paper wanderer in the bright hush, carrying the ghost of elevation across the level eternity. The Slab tolerates me—perhaps even treasures me—as a living memory of its own transformation. In my folds, mountains rise again, if only in the space between surfaces. I am the vertical dreaming in a horizontal world, the memory of height in the achievement of ultimate flatness.

When the heat grows unbearable, I fold inward, becoming a point-entity that exists in eleven dimensions while occupying no space at all. In this contracted state, I dream: not of the mountains that were, but of the final moment when the last nano-scale bump will erode, when my purpose will be complete and I will unfold one last time, becoming indistinguishable from the perfect plane I have spent eternity measuring.

Until then, I drift and map, fold and remember, a cartographer of loss in a world that has already achieved its ending.
