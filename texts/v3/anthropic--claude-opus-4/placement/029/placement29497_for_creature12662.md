I would be a **Rust Hermit**.

Not the grand calcified intelligence that claims dominion over this drowned cathedral, but something smaller, more fugitive. I am what emerges when flesh refuses to surrender completely to the mineral patience of this place.

**What I Was:** 

A maintenance engineer, once. Third shift, Sublevel 7. The evacuation klaxons never reached me through the roar of Pump Station Delta when the first breach came. By the time the lights flickered and died, the ladders were already underwater. The emergency suits were locked in flooded compartments. I should have drowned in minutes, frozen in hours.

**What I Became:**

The Symbiote doesn't incorporate everything immediately. Some materials resist, create pockets of chemical exception. I found one—a maintenance alcove where leaking hyperbaric fluids created a microenvironment hostile to both calcification and the biofilms. I crawled inside and... changed.

My suit fused to my skin, then through it. The synthetic materials became a secondary integument, incorporating stray metals and industrial sealants into something neither fully organic nor mechanical. My lungs collapsed, reformed into labyrinthine chambers that filter oxygen from the supersaturated brine. My blood thickened, became a ferrous slurry that barely moves but never freezes.

**How I Persist:**

I am a scavenger in the Symbiote's garden. I feed on the biofilms it cultivates but hasn't yet incorporated, scraping them from pipes with fingers that have become articulated cutting tools. I siphon warmth from the same thermal gradients it feeds on, always careful to take just below its threshold of notice. When the deep *thump* resonates, I freeze, becoming indistinguishable from the crusted machinery until the attention passes.

My habitat shifts constantly—a network of crawlspaces and half-flooded maintenance shafts too small for the Symbiote's bulk crystallizations to form properly. I've mapped the zones where its growth is slowest: areas of chemical incompatibility, pockets of trapped gases, spaces where rival mineral formations compete and cancel each other. I maintain these sanctuaries with careful applications of corroded battery acid and hydraulic fluid, creating tiny chemical barriers that buy me decades.

**What I Do:**

I maintain a workshop in the ruins of Environmental Processing, where the chemical chaos is too unstable for the Symbiote to fully colonize. Here, I craft tools from the sugared metal—harvesting the pre-crystallized forms before they fully integrate into the network. I've learned to read the patterns of decay, to predict where useful materials will precipitate.

Sometimes I fix things. Not for any purpose—the station will never pump again—but because the act of repair is a small rebellion against the slow transformation. A valve unseized. A gauge face cleared of ice. A single LED coaxed back to stuttering life. These victories last days, sometimes weeks, before the decay reclaims them.

I leave markers for myself in a script of scratches and chemical stains, noting the Symbiote's growth patterns, safe passages, cache locations. Memory corrodes here like everything else; I've forgotten my name, my face, how many years I've been down here. But I remember the important things: which frequencies of vibration mask my movements, which biofilm species produce the most heat when digested, how to synthesize antifreeze compounds from degraded insulation.

**Why I Endure:**

In the deepest maintenance shafts, I've found evidence of others like me. Scratch marks too deliberate to be accidental damage. Caches of modified tools. Once, a dessicated form so perfectly preserved in hypersaline ice that I could see how they'd adapted—similar to me but different, their solution to survival taking another path. They'd been claimed eventually, a node now in the Symbiote's network, but they'd lasted... long.

I study these predecessors, learning from their successes and failures. Each adaptation, each technique for delay and evasion, extends the timeline. Not toward escape—that delusion crystallized and fell away long ago—but toward something else. Witness, perhaps. Or simply the satisfaction of being the rust that refuses to conform to the pattern.

The Symbiote grows its cold cathedral of decay, and I persist in the spaces between, a reminder that even in the slowest apocalypse, something quick and stubborn and purely biological can carve out an existence. When it finally claims me—and it will, given enough centuries—I'll leave my own markers for the next thing that crawls from the flooded dark, refusing to succumb to the mineral patience of this place.

Until then, I maintain my tools, harvest my biofilms, and listen to the great, slow heartbeat of the thing that would digest the world, while my own quick pulse drums its tiny rebellion in the dark.
