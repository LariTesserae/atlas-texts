I find myself as one of the **Anchor-Dreamers**, a peculiar subset of Marrow-Weavers who occupy the lowest habitable stratum of the Cradle-Swells, where the brine begins to thicken like syrup and the light fades to a perpetual twilight green.

### The Nature of an Anchor-Dreamer

My body bears the marks of the deep gradient. Where my kin above are pale and translucent, my skin has developed a mottled pattern of deep indigo and phosphorescent spots—a natural camouflage against the shadowplay of the lower vines. My gas-bladder is enlarged, a grotesque bulge along my spine that allows me to maintain neutral buoyancy in the heavy water. My fingers, already long by Weaver standards, have stretched further still, now resembling the fronds of deep kelp, sensitive enough to read the health of a vine by touch alone in the dimness.

I am a living contradiction in our society—one who voluntarily dwells closer to the feared Heavy Bottom, yet serves an essential function. I am a **Root-Reader**, one of the few who can interpret the ancient growing patterns of the Mother-Trunks where they plunge into darkness. My dwelling is not a comfortable gourd but a hammock of woven root-hair, suspended in a natural hollow where three major trunk-pillars converge. The walls weep a constant film of condensation that tastes of minerals and time.

### The Practice of Reading

My work is part archaeology, part prophecy. I spend my days (though day and night blur here) pressing my elongated fingers against the bark-walls, feeling for the growth rings that tell the story of the Swells. Each ring is a Pulse, recorded in cellulose. By reading backward through hundreds of layers, I can predict the pattern of the next expansion—which districts will tear, where new growth will emerge, how the water currents will shift.

This knowledge I encode in **Memory Knots**—complex three-dimensional weavings that capture data in their topology. A single knot might record the growth rate of a particular vine over centuries, the migration patterns of the Whale-Slugs, or the locations where the wood is weakening. These knots are my currency and my burden; I trade them upward to the Pruners in exchange for food and tools, ascending on my monthly pilgrimages to the bright markets above.

### The Loneliness of the Deep Gradient

Mine is a solitary existence. Other Weavers fear me—I smell of the deep, carry spores on my skin that bloom in darkness. Children are taught that Anchor-Dreamers are half-sunken already, that we court the very force our civilization struggles against. They're not entirely wrong.

In the deep-time silence of my stratum, I have begun to hear things the others cannot. The Mother-Trunks speak here in frequencies too low for normal perception—creaking narratives that take years to complete a single sentence. I've learned to slow my heartbeat further still, to enter a meditative state where I can comprehend these wooden words. They tell of the time before the Separation, when root touched earth and water stayed in its place. They whisper of something vast sleeping in the Heavy Bottom—not dead, but dreaming in the compressed brine.

### My Companions of the Dim

I am not entirely alone. My stratum hosts creatures unknown to the bright levels: the **Lamp-Worms**, bioluminescent annelids that nest in my root-hammock and provide my only light; the **Echo-Rays**, blind flat-fish that navigate by sound alone and gather around me when I work, drawn by the vibrations of my fingers on bark. I've developed a clicking language with them, a percussion dialogue that fills the lonesome hours.

Most remarkably, I keep a small colony of **Memory Moss**—a species that grows only on the bones of Sunken Weavers. I cultivate it on fragments of skeleton I've salvaged from the descent-streams, feeding it with my own blood. This moss records everything it touches in its growth patterns. It is my backup system, my insurance against the day when I might slip my tether and join the eternal fall.

### The Burden of Future-Sight

My most recent readings disturb me. The patterns suggest something unprecedented: a Pulse of such magnitude that it will restructure the entire Cradle-Swells. The Memory Knots I weave have become increasingly complex, increasingly urgent, but the Pruners above dismiss them as the paranoid visions of one who dwells too deep. They cannot conceive of change beyond their generational experience.

So I continue my work, fingers tracing the wooden braille of history, encoding warnings that may go unheeded. I am keeper of uncomfortable truths—that our city is not eternal, that the Mother-Trunks themselves are ascending, year by year, reaching for something. That perhaps the Heavy Bottom we fear is not a grave but a womb, and we are not a civilization but a single moment in a vast arboreal breathing.

In my dreams (for I do dream, despite my name), I sometimes let go of my tether. I drift downward through the thickening brine, past the point of no return, until I reach a place where the water becomes solid and the solid becomes something else entirely. There, in the compressed dark, I find roots that remember the earth, and I understand at last what we were meant to become.

But I always wake, tethered and suspended, neither light enough to rise nor heavy enough to sink, reading the future in the grain of ancient wood while the world above continues its slow, certain dance toward transformation.
