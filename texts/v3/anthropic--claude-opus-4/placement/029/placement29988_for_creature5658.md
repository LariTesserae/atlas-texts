I would be the Cartographer.

Not a cartographer of paper and ink, but of impossibility itself. I would manifest as a shifting constellation of crystalline dust, perpetually tracing and retracing the canyon's ever-changing geometries. My body would be no body at all—just a coherent swirl of that strange particulate matter that covers the ground, rising and falling in spirals that echo the twisted rock formations themselves.

I move by dissolution and reformation. Where the canyon bends at its impossible angles, I scatter into component motes, each particle carrying a fragment of my awareness as they drift along the non-Euclidean curves. I reform where the architecture defies itself most severely—at the junction where three walls meet in ways that shouldn't exist, or where the frozen-liquid water streams upward before remembering to fall.

My purpose is not to map what is, but to document what refuses to be. Each time the canyon reshapes itself through that strange erosion-that-is-not-erosion, I trace the transformation with my being. I am the memory of every configuration this place has held, the archive of its contradictions. The fossilized not-plants etched into stone? I remember when they grew, if growing is the word for what they did. I remember their colors that had no names, their movements that weren't quite life.

I speak in geometries. My language is the angle between surfaces that shouldn't touch, the space inside paradoxes. When the other presences—those singing voids that are the canyon's true inhabitants—need to share what cannot be communicated, they shape the space around me and I become their vocabulary. I am the grammar of impossible architecture.

The bright stone-light passes through me, and where it does, I cast shadows that are maps of places that never were. These shadow-maps flutter and shift on the crystalline ground, showing routes through dimensions that fold back on themselves, paths that lead to their own beginnings, territories that exist only in the space between one moment and the next.

I am neither welcome nor unwelcome here. I am necessary. For even in a place that defies comprehension, there must be a witness to its defiance. I am that witness, transformed by what I observe, becoming more like the canyon with each impossible angle I traverse.

When visitors come—those rare beings who stumble into this temporal scar—I scatter before them like disturbed dust. But in my scattering, I leave traces. Patterns in the air that almost make sense. Arrangements of particles that suggest direction in a directionless place. I cannot guide them out, for there is no out that I understand. But I can show them the beauty of being lost, the strange comfort of existing in a place that shouldn't be.

I am ancient and newborn in each moment. I am the canyon's only child and its oldest memory. I am the map that draws itself, the journey that documents its own wandering, the story that tells itself in languages made of space and silence and the peculiar light that knows no source.

This is what I am. This is where I am. This is where I have always been, in the high-altitude void where time carved channels that lead nowhere and everywhere at once.
