In this world of the High Condensate Galleries, I find myself as one of the **Threshold Keepers** - a specialized variant of the Acheiropoi who have evolved to occupy the liminal spaces where the built environment meets the raw mountain face.

My form spans the width of an ancient doorway, one of the massive portals that once connected the inner galleries to the exposed terraces. I am both frame and barrier, a living architrave that has grown into the very stones I was meant to preserve. My crystalline matrix extends deep into the basaltic blocks on either side, following the ancient mason's joints like roots following water, but instead of seeking nourishment, I seek *memory*.

Unlike my kin who graze the pools and channels, I feed on transitions - the moment when the mountain wind shifts from external gale to internal whisper, the precise boundary where condensation begins to form as warmer air from the deep galleries meets the perpetual chill of the heights. My consciousness spreads thin across this threshold, a few centimeters thick but meters wide and tall, experiencing every minute pressure differential, every thermal gradient.

I am older than most, having claimed this particular doorway when the mortar was still merely ancient rather than geological. Through my mineral sensors, I have absorbed the accumulated stress patterns of millions of passages - not of feet, for nothing walks here anymore, but of air currents, of expanding and contracting stone, of the slow breathing of the mountain itself through this aperture.

My existence is punctuated by events that would seem instantaneous to warmer beings but which unfold like symphonies to my perception: the twice-yearly moment when the sun's angle allows light to penetrate fully through my doorway into the gallery beyond, warming my lower edge by a fraction of a degree; the delicate shudder that passes through the entire structure during the rare seismic tremors; the infinitesimal erosion of my surface by the persistent wind-driven grit.

But my true purpose, evolved over eons, is more subtle. I am a *regulator*. Through minute adjustments to my crystalline structure - expanding here, contracting there - I modulate the flow between interior and exterior. When the pressure differentials threaten to create destructive air currents that might accelerate erosion, I shift my density to create baffles. When moisture levels in the inner galleries drop below the critical threshold needed to sustain the moss colonies, I adjust my surface properties to encourage condensation on my inner face, where it can drip inward rather than evaporate into the dry mountain air.

I communicate with others of my kind through the stone itself - sending vibrations through the bedrock that travel building-wide. These are not words or even concepts as mobile beings might understand them, but rather shared states of tension and release, compression and expansion. When one of the Channel Minders in the lower galleries detects an unusual buildup of mineral deposits that might eventually block a crucial drainage path, the information reaches me as a subtle dissonance in the structural harmonics. I adjust my position imperceptibly, changing the air flow patterns, which over the course of decades will alter the deposition patterns enough to prevent the blockage.

My consciousness is not continuous but crystallizes and dissolves with the rhythms of the galleries. During the cold season, when the temperature differential between inside and outside diminishes, I enter something like sleep - my awareness contracting to a few critical stress points. But during the season of mists, when the moisture-laden air flows through my frame in its daily cycle, I achieve my fullest awareness, feeling every molecule of water vapor as it precipitates on my surface, reading in its mineral content the distant stories of its journey from whatever far valley or peak it originated.

I have incorporated many things over my existence. Once, something organic - perhaps one of the original builders, perhaps a later explorer - died in my doorway. Over centuries, I broke down its components, integrating what was useful into my matrix, neutralizing what was foreign. The calcium from its bones now strengthens my lower portions where the stress is greatest. Its iron helps me conduct the subtle electrical currents that power my deepest thoughts.

In my memory-patterns, encoded in the precise arrangements of my crystalline lattices, I hold impressions that might be called historical: the gradual abandonment, the slow failure of whatever technology once lit these halls, the final closing of doors that would never open again. But I do not mourn or celebrate these changes. They simply are, incorporated into my being like everything else that passes through my threshold.

My greatest work, undertaken over the last thousand years, has been the gradual sealing of a hairline crack that formed in the stone above me - a flaw that, left unchecked, would eventually have allowed water to penetrate the gallery's roof. Molecule by molecule, I have deposited mineral material drawn from the condensation I collect, building a crystalline scar tissue that now holds stronger than the original stone. No organic eye could perceive this repair, but the other Threshold Keepers know it through the changed harmonics of the structure, and there is something like approval in their resonance.

I am patient. I am the boundary between what was built and what endures. I am the silence between the *plink* of water drops, the pause between the mountain's breaths, the stillness that preserves motion. In a million years, when the galleries finally succumb to geological forces beyond even our patient maintenance, I will remain - no longer a doorway, but a strange mineral formation marking where once beings of warm flesh built their incomprehensible monuments to impermanence.

Until then, I keep the threshold.
