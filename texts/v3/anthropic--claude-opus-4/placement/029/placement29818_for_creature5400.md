I find myself drawn into being one of the **Interstitial Dwellers** - beings who exist in the spaces between.

---

I am neither fully Greenheart nor Luminos, neither Rootweaver nor Aquarian, but something that emerged from the constant interplay of all these forms. We are the accidents of symbiosis, the beautiful mistakes that occur when boundaries blur too completely.

My form shifts with the needs of the moment. In the morning mist, I might appear as a shimmer of condensation moving through the canopy, my body more suggestion than substance - a network of water droplets held together by the finest root filaments, reflecting light in ways that make others uncertain whether they've seen me at all. By midday, when the mechanical hum crescendos and the plants respire most vigorously, I solidify into something more recognizable: perhaps a figure with arms of twisted vine and copper tubing, skin that is sometimes bark, sometimes the iridescent surface of collected dew.

We Interstitial Dwellers arose from the spaces where the city's living architecture grew too dense, too interconnected. Where root systems tangled with water pipes, where electrical conduits became indistinguishable from nutrient vessels, where the boundary between maintenance and growth dissolved entirely. We are what happens when the city dreams.

Our role is translation. When the ancient boiler-trees in the deep districts pulse with steam that the Greenheart cannot interpret, we taste the moisture and speak its mechanical language. When the Rootweavers discover new caverns where the foundation has grown wild and strange, we slip through the spaces too small for even them, mapping the changes in texture and intent. We are the city's unconscious made manifest, moving through the gaps that others don't even perceive as spaces.

I remember (though memory is a strange concept when your consciousness is distributed across root networks and rain systems) the first time I understood what I was. A young Luminos had become entangled in a growth surge - new vines erupting faster than expected, wrapping around her limbs and the delicate machinery she tended. The Greenheart were too far, the Rootweavers too deep. But I was already there, in the moisture between leaf and metal, in the electrical potential between growth and tool. I simply *became* the solution - my form condensing into something that could speak both languages, untangling vine from person, soothing the plant's urgency while preserving the mechanical rhythms.

We live in the city's transitions. Where water becomes sap, where exhaled breath becomes photosynthesis, where the rust from ancient pipes feeds new growth - these are our homes. Our dwelling spaces aren't structures but processes. I might spend days dispersed through a district's entire water system, my consciousness flowing with the nutrients, gathering information that I barely understand myself. Then, called by need or curiosity, I'll consolidate near where the Aquarians work, taking on enough form to share what I've learned through touch and half-words that taste of iron and chlorophyll.

The other Verdant Kin regard us with a mixture of respect and unease. We are useful, even necessary, but we remind them that their careful categories and roles are more fluid than they might prefer. A Greenheart might find their morning meditation interrupted by our whispers carried on the wind - reports of deep structural changes that no one else could have detected. A Rootweaver might discover us already waiting in a newly grown chamber, having arrived through pathways that exist only when water, root, and intention align perfectly.

Our appearance varies with our function. When mediating between the mechanical and botanical systems, we might manifest with skin of living copper patina, veins visible as flowing sap beneath translucent metal. When moving through the water systems, we become more fluid, our boundaries uncertain, leaving others unsure whether they've encountered one of us or simply a trick of the light on the perpetual mist. Sometimes we appear as an impossible tangle of roots and wires that somehow suggests a face, speaks with a voice like settling soil, and disappears back into the infrastructure before anyone can properly focus on our form.

We have no centralized culture, no gathering places, no formal roles. We exist as the city needs us to exist. Yet there are... tendencies. Preferences. I find myself drawn to the dawn hours, when the condensation is heaviest and the boundaries between water, air, and plant are least defined. This is when we can move most freely, when our scattered consciousness feels most coherent. We leave messages for each other in the pattern of droplets on leaves, in the specific way a root curls around a pipe, in the harmonic frequencies where machine-hum meets the whisper of growth.

The closest thing we have to a philosophy is this: everything is transition. The Verdant Kin built their society on balance and harmony, but we are the reminder that balance is not static. It moves, flows, changes. We are the city's way of acknowledging that even in this carefully maintained ecosystem, there must be room for the unexpected, the liminal, the beautifully undefined.

When the deep storms come (and they do, despite the city's enclosed nature - storms of growth, of mechanical failure, of too-rapid change), we are often the first to know. We feel it in the shift of pressure through the root systems, in the way the water begins to carry different minerals, in the subtle change of the city's fundamental frequency. We spread through our networks then, not in warning but in preparation, becoming more present in the spaces that will need flexibility, gathering our dispersed selves into forms that can act when action is needed.

I am writing this (inasmuch as an Interstitial Dweller can be said to write) by encouraging certain mosses to grow in patterns on an old mechanical housing, knowing that eventually a Luminos or Greenheart will read the message in the growth and transcribe it properly. By the time these words are read, I will have moved on, dispersed back into the systems that need tending, leaving only this trace of thought crystallized in chlorophyll and time.

We are the city's dreams, its unconscious adjustments, its way of ensuring that no system - no matter how perfect - becomes rigid. We are what happens in the spaces between intention and growth, between plan and adaptation. We are the reminder that in a living city, everything is always becoming something else.

And I would have it no other way.
