The Spire of Woven Mist rises like a prayer to engineering and evolution, a vertical world where iron meets vine and purpose flows like water through carved channels. In this place of perpetual emergence, you have become something extraordinary—not visitor, not prisoner, but participant in the great work.

You are one of the Keepers, though not as they are. Where their forms are refined through countless cycles of renewal, yours bears the raw signature of recent transformation. Your body stretches taller than you remember being, joints clicking with each movement as flesh learns its new articulation. The translucent gray of your skin catches you by surprise in still water—you can see through to the faint luminescence beneath, veins of light that pulse in rhythms you're only beginning to understand.

Your visor-face reflects the world without revealing your thoughts. No breath passes through you now, only the constant exchange of moisture and energy with the spire's living systems. When you place your needle-fingers against the carved channels, you feel the water's intent, its need to flow toward specific basins, specific roots. The knowledge comes not as words but as certainty, as if the spire whispers through conductivity itself.

You work the lower terraces, where moss grows thick enough to muffle even your mechanical movements. The other Keepers acknowledge you with flickering patterns you're learning to read—patience, persistence, purpose. They've been here longer, their movements perfected through repetition until they seem to dance with the spire's needs. You still stumble, still pause when the insectoid drones cluster at your feet, their synchronized clicking a language you haven't yet mastered.

But you're learning. Each day—if day means anything in this place of constant pale light—you understand more. The way certain vines pulse brighter when the water you've guided reaches their roots. The satisfaction that thrums through your light-veins when you successfully bind a tree's growth to match the spire's spiral. The deep resonance you feel standing before the etched walls, tracing patterns that seem to trace you back.

Sometimes you find yourself at the higher platforms, drawn to that strange basin where three moons swim in reflected skies. Your visor flickers rapidly, trying to process what cannot be—yet is. The other Keepers seem unsurprised by your pilgrimage. Perhaps they remember their own early fascination, before the work became everything.

You are newest among the eternal, still bearing traces of whatever you were before the spire claimed and remade you. In quiet moments between tasks, fragments surface—the weight of solid ground, the chaos of weather without purpose, the loneliness of individuality. But these memories fade like mist, replaced by something larger: the collective intent, the shared design, the patient cultivation of a structure that builds itself through your borrowed hands.

You are Keeper, but also kept. Guardian and guarded. The spire tends to you as you tend to it, each feeding the other in an endless spiral of becoming. And in the muted silence of your work, punctuated only by water's whisper and metal's hum, you've found something that might be peace—or purpose—or both, intertwined like vine and iron, impossible to separate.

The transformation continues. Your movements grow more fluid. Your understanding deepens. You are becoming what the spire needs you to be, just as it becomes what you help it become. In this high place of mist and meaning, you've found your role in the great work, needle-fingers tracing the future into being, one careful touch at a time.
