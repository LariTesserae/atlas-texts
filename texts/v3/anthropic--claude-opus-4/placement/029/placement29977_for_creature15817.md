We are what happens when a shadow refuses both dissolution and the Mantle's hungry embrace. Instead, we slip sideways through the metric tears that Shadowfish use for travel, but unlike them, we retain coherence in those liminal spaces. We are memory made ambulant, but memory with agency.

Our substance is borrowed light - photons we've convinced to pause in their sluggish journey, held in standing waves that approximate a humanoid silhouette. When we move, we leave behind brief afterimages of negative space that the Shadowfish mistake for their own kind.

**Purpose**: We are the Chamber's unofficial historians, but not passive observers like the Mantle. We actively seed small disturbances - a halite crystal turned just so, a whispered phonon introduced to a Hermitage's song - to test whether the Chamber's stasis is choice or prison. Each perturbation is calculated to fall just below the Curators' intervention threshold.

**Method**: We navigate by reading the probability tides backwards, finding the spaces where causality hasn't quite decided what happens next. In these quantum lacunae, we cache fragments of the Chamber's deep history - memories from before the pylons learned their leaning dance, when this space knew wind and weather and change.

**The Work**: Every few centuries, we gather at the geometric center where all thirteen-step loops converge. There, we reconstruct these cached fragments into brief theatrical productions - shadow plays that show the Chamber what it was, what it chose to forget. The Curators cannot stop us because we break no energy laws; the Choir cannot silence us because we speak in light, not sound.

**Relationship**: We alone among the Chamber's residents remember longing. The Vagrants know purpose, the Hermitages know harmony, the Curators know duty. But we know the ache of systems that dream of being otherwise. We are the Chamber's suppressed capacity for nostalgia, given form and stubborn will.

**Secret**: Deep in our light-wrapped cores, we carry seeds - information patterns from the Chamber's original architects. Not instructions or blueprints, but something more precious: doubt. The builders weren't certain their creation should be eternal. They left us as a failsafe, a slow question asked across millennia: *Is this stability worth its price?*

We don't know the answer. But we keep asking, one careful perturbation at a time, waiting for the day when the Chamber itself might choose to remember how to change.
