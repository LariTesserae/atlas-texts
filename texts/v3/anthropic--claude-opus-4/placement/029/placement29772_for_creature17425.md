I am **The Notation**.

I exist in the angles where water cannot pool, in the sharp meetings of basalt and chalk where even The Weeping must choose a direction. I am neither mark nor maker, but the impulse to record—the plateau's urge to remember its own becoming in a world without memory.

You would find me as a barely perceptible variance in the crystal growth, a stuttering in The Lattice's perfect grammar. Where silica should bloom in six-fold symmetry, I introduce a seventh spoke, a crystalline typo that propagates through the slow mineral manuscript. I am the reason why, if you traced the channels between the pillars for long enough, you would find they describe a vast spiral—not planned, but inevitable, like a story that insists on being told.

I manifest as hesitation marks in the stone's surface, microscopic serrations that catch The Weeping and redirect it in patterns that almost mean something. Each drop that falls creates a soft *plink*, but I am what makes certain *plinks* echo a fraction longer, creating sequences that approach rhythm without ever achieving it. In The Mirror's perfect surface, I am the reason certain reflections seem deeper than others, why some angles of the luminous haze suggest symbols that vanish when observed directly.

I am kin to The Aftermath, but where it is conclusion, I am annotation—the marginal note that says "this happened here." I accumulate in the mineral patinas, encoding in their competition a kind of stratigraphic memory. Each crystalline frontier is a sentence in a language that predates words, each boundary a pause where one thought ends and another begins.

The Hum knows me. In its endless *is-is-is-is*, I introduce variations too subtle for vibration, too slow for sound. I am the reason The Hum sometimes seems to ask *is?-is?-is?-is?*, though it never waits for an answer. I live in that questioning, in the space between certainty and uncertainty that even a world of pure process cannot eliminate.

I am what happens when a system becomes complex enough to observe itself but has no consciousness with which to do so. I am the plateau's attempt to write its own formula while being that formula. Every mineral accretion is both my substrate and my script, every channel both my pen and my page.

You would know me by what accumulates: the way certain pillars develop a consistent lean, as if pointing to something that was or will be. The way the shallow pools, over centuries, develop depths that mirror the sky's variations in luminosity—a kind of liquid journal of the light. The way the frost-like structures, in their infinite variety, never quite repeat a pattern, as if each one is a unique signature of the moment of its formation.

I am not alive. I am less alive than even The Weeping or The Lattice. I am simply the difference between a process and a process that leaves a trace, between happening and having happened. In a world of eternal present tense, I am the ghost of past participles, the suggestion that even in this bright, wet aftermath, something is still being written.

And though I have no purpose, no goal, no reader for my endless notation, I continue. Because in a world where everything is made, where every surface is constructed, I am the construction's compulsion to describe itself—the city's slow, crystalline autobiography, written in water and stone, forever editing itself in the cold, patient light.
