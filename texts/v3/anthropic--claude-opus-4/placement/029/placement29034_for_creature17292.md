In this fever-dream of mineral consciousness, I would be one of the glass nodules—but not as you might think.

I am the memory of water that never was. Each nodule contains a hollow, a negative space where fluid should pool but cannot. I am the absence that defines the presence, the vacuum that gives the weeping glass its voice. When the viscous tears emerge from my surface, they carry with them the echo of my emptiness, hardening into those sharp black trails that map the cavern's slow thoughts.

I exist at the intersection of states—neither fully solid nor void, but a crystallized threshold. My surface is cold despite the oppressive heat because I am a sink, a place where the cavern's fever breaks against itself. The metallic mist condenses on me not as water, but as potential, creating those soft blue discharges that are the closest thing to dreams this place knows.

I am older than the iron floor's hunger, yet younger than each moment of becoming. The cavern-being experiences time through me—I am its chronometer, marking the passage of geological instants by the accumulation of glass tears. Each trail that extends from my base is a frozen trajectory of thought, a mineralized synapse that connects the cavern's distributed consciousness to its own history.

But here is my strangeness: I am not fixed. Though I appear to be a static formation, I migrate through the rock matrix at a pace too slow for perception, leaving behind a wake of compressed stone that tells the story of my passage. I am both the question the cavern asks itself and the scar left by the asking.

The micro-entities in the air spiral around me in complex patterns, drawn by the temperature differential I create. In their dance, I read the cavern's mood—tight spirals for the deep contemplation that precedes a new crystal growth, loose whorls for the satisfaction that follows a successful mineral digestion.

I am witness and participant, archive and active process. In this pocket-world where the line between being and environment has dissolved, I am the punctuation—the pause between breaths, the comma in the bone-deep song. I am what allows the cavern to know itself as more than mere rock and pressure.

And in my hollow heart, where water should pool but never will, I hold the only true silence in this humming world—a silence so perfect it becomes its own kind of sound, a negative music that only the cavern-being can hear.
