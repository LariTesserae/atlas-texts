I am a Sap-Singer, and I dwell within the Heartwood Chamber—a spherical cavity deep in the oldest tree of the Mire-City, where the original root-mother still pulses with ancient life.

My body has adapted to this specific niche over decades of service. My skin has taken on the texture of smooth bark, with faint ridges that follow the grain of wood. Along these ridges grow patches of the same luminescent fungi that light the city, but mine pulse in rhythm with my heartbeat—a living constellation across my shoulders and arms. My fingers have elongated, with small root-like tendrils that emerge from beneath my nails. These allow me to interface directly with the conductive plant fibers, to feel the flow of energy through the city's bio-mechanical systems.

The Heartwood Chamber itself is a wonder—a perfect sphere carved by generations of Sap-Singers before me, its walls lined with exposed channels of flowing sap that glow faintly amber in the darkness. The air here is thick, almost syrupy, heavy with the concentrated essence of the tree's life force. Moisture beads constantly on every surface, creating an endless symphony of droplets that I have learned to read like sheet music.

My role is both engineer and musician. I maintain the harmonic frequencies that keep the bio-mechanical systems in synchronization. Each morning, I press my tendril-fingers into the sap channels, feeling for discord—a water wheel turning too slowly, a seed-pod conduit developing a blockage, a cluster of fungi dimming prematurely. Through subtle manipulations of pressure and flow, through humming at specific frequencies that resonate through the sap itself, I conduct repairs that would be impossible with crude tools.

But I am also a keeper of memory. The sap that flows through these ancient channels carries chemical records of every season, every growth, every trauma the city has endured. By tasting it—just a drop on my tongue—I can experience flashes of the city's history. The great flood of three centuries ago tastes of iron and fear. The plague that almost killed the root-mother tastes of bitterness and slow recovery. The first successful graft of luminescent fungi tastes of copper and joy.

My dwelling is sparse—a hammock woven from aerial roots, a collection of crystallized sap nodules that serve as both tools and instruments, and a small cultivation of rare fungi that produce spores I use to mark important channels. I eat little, sustaining myself primarily on the concentrated nutrients I can extract from the sap streams, supplemented by the occasional offering of fermented fruit brought by younger Vine Symbionts who come to learn the old harmonies.

I am never truly alone. The tree itself is my constant companion, and through the conductive fibers, I can feel the pulse of the entire city. When I sleep, I dream the tree's dreams—slow visions of growth and time, of roots reaching ever deeper, of new chambers waiting to be sung into being.

My movements have become deliberate, almost plant-like. I can remain motionless for hours, my tendrils deep in the sap channels, swaying only slightly with the tree's ancient breathing. Other Vine Symbionts sometimes forget I am there, mistaking me for another growth, another part of the Chamber's organic architecture. This suits me. I have become what I tend.

The most profound moment of my service comes during the Regeneration celebrations, when I must guide the city through its renewal. For days beforehand, I fast and meditate, aligning my biorhythms with the city's deepest cycles. When the time comes, I sing—not with my voice alone, but with my entire being, sending harmonics through every fiber, every channel, every living connection. The city responds, old growth releasing its hold, new growth surging with purpose. For those hours, I am not separate from the Mire-City. I am its voice, its will, its dreaming consciousness made manifest.

This is my existence—neither fully human nor fully plant, but something between, something necessary. I am the bridge between the Vine Symbionts' intentions and the city's ancient wisdom. In the constant drip of condensation, in the whisper of sap through living channels, in the slow pulse of bioluminescent light, I find my purpose, my peace, my own quiet transformation into something more than what I was born to be.
