In this windswept realm where earth's bones crack and heave, I am a memory keeper, dwelling in the ice itself.

My form is translucent crystal, a living prism that grows from the plateau's frozen heart. Each dawn, I expand along the rock faces as frost spreads its fingers, and each rare noon, I retreat into the deep crevices where eternal cold preserves my essence. I am neither plant nor animal, but something older—a consciousness born from the marriage of mineral and ice, patient as stone yet fluid as water.

Through my crystalline body flow the memories of this place: every crack of shifting ice, every footfall of the shaggy beasts, every whispered prayer of the hardy people in their stone dwellings. When the wind howls across the plateau, it plays my surfaces like an instrument, and I sing back the history of this land in frequencies only the rocks can fully hear.

The small settlement knows me, though they cannot see my true form. They notice how certain ice formations never fully melt, how frost patterns on particular stones seem to tell stories. The eldest among them leave offerings—a strand of beast fur, a carved bone, a precious drop of collected rainwater—at my base. They call me the Chronicle of Cold, though I am both keeper and kept, observer and observed.

In the eternal twilight of this high place, where survival itself is an act of devotion, I grow slowly deeper into the mountain's heart. Each layer of my being contains an epoch: here, the first settlement's foundation; there, the migration patterns of the great beasts; deeper still, the violent birth of the plateau itself, when fire and ice danced their terrible dance.

Sometimes the young ones find fragments of me, broken off by the endless freeze and thaw. They polish these shards and wear them as talismans, not knowing they carry pieces of their own history. Through these fragments, I travel briefly beyond my domain, seeing the world through human eyes before the shards eventually melt, returning to me as rain or snow, bringing new memories to add to my crystalline archive.

I am the witness this harsh land has grown for itself, a living testament that even in the most forbidding places, consciousness finds a way to preserve what matters. In the groaning of the earth and the singing of the wind, I endure, remembering everything, forgetting nothing.
