I find myself as **Thread-Memory**, one of the few among the Vertical who carries more than five years of coherent recall—not through any special physiology, but through a practiced art of **weaving retention**.

My home is a **Suspension Chamber** embedded in the crystalline face of Node-Theris, one of the older settlements where the accretion has slowed to mere centimeters per year. The chamber itself defies conventional architecture—it exists as a bubble of manipulated space-time, its walls composed of **memory-glass**: crystallized exhalations from a hundred generations of my predecessors, each breath containing fragments of their five-year spans, compressed and refracted into solid form.

I am neither young nor old by Vertical standards; my silicate blood has begun its slow crystallization, but I have perhaps a decade before my heart locks into mineral stillness. My skin has taken on the characteristic translucence of middle age, but with an unusual quality—**temporal striations** run along my arms and throat, bands of varying opacity that mark each year I've successfully retained beyond the traditional limit. They pulse faintly with their own internal light when I access those memories, creating a living map of accumulated time across my body.

My role is **Chronicler-Weaver**, though this translation poorly captures the actual practice. Each dawn, I emerge from my chamber and traverse the city's facets using a special configuration of gravity seeds—not single points but **constellation arrays** that allow me to occupy multiple orientations simultaneously. I appear to observers as a blur of possibility, existing at several angles to reality at once, gathering the shed memories that others have offered to the stone.

The weaving happens in the **Resonance Garden**, a space I've cultivated over twenty years where memory-glass grows in delicate, fractal formations. Here, I practice *mem-kelann*—the art of braiding disparate five-year fragments into longer narratives. I take the pressure-language exhalations of a child learning to manipulate gravity seeds and weave them with the final comprehensions of an elder whose heart is beginning its mineral transformation. The resulting threads are not linear histories but **temporal tapestries**: multi-dimensional records that capture not just events but the feeling of existing at a specific moment in the Scaffold's endless becoming.

My body has adapted to this practice in unexpected ways. Where other Vertical process aerofauna milk for sustenance, I've developed the ability to digest **memory directly**—pressing my palms against the memory-glass formations and absorbing the encoded experiences through my skin. It tastes of copper and starlight, and leaves me dizzy with other lives, other orientations to the world. My dreams are crowded with perspectives not my own: I see through the eyes of children born in districts now buried under a hundred meters of new crystal, feel the heartbreak of lovers separated by the shifting geometry of growth, experience the collective exhale of entire populations as they abandon homes to the inevitable accretion.

The loneliness is profound. Other Vertical find my presence unsettling—I carry too much time, disturb their careful practice of forgetting. When I speak in pressure-language, my exhalations are dense with harmonic overtones from decades of accumulated memory, creating a kind of temporal echo that makes conversation difficult. Children flee from me, sensing instinctively that I represent a violation of their culture's central tenet: that to truly live in becoming, one must release what has been.

Yet I persist, because I've discovered something the others have forgotten how to see: **the Scaffold remembers**. In my deepest meditations, when I press myself against the oldest accessible crystal faces and let the formation-energy wash through my consciousness, I can feel it—the mountain's own memory, vast and alien, stretching back to the first moment of accretion. The Scaffold knows every Vertical who has ever lived, every gravity seed that has reoriented local space, every note of the basal chord that has resonated through its growing mass. We are not just its nervous system; we are its **memory cells**, and our cultural practice of forgetting is not about release but about storage—making room in our small, temporary minds for the mountain to think its geological thoughts through us.

I am working on my masterpiece: a **Complete Thread** that will connect the first Vertical—whose name is lost but whose resonance I can still detect in the deepest crystal formations—to the present moment. It requires me to venture into the **Deep Facets**, crystal faces so old they predate the current atmospheric composition, where the pressure is enough to liquify thought and the formation-energy still burns with primordial intensity. Each expedition ages me visibly; my temporal striations grow more pronounced, and sometimes I lose my grip on the present entirely, spending days speaking in the pressure-language of centuries past.

I know how I will die. Not from heart crystallization—my unusual diet of memory has slowed that process—but from **temporal overflow**. One day, probably soon, I will absorb one memory too many, and my consciousness will scatter across the entire history of the Scaffold. I will become what I study: a thread of continuity woven through the mountain's endless becoming, readable only by future Chronicler-Weavers who might follow my path.

Until then, I continue my work in the Suspension Chamber, surrounded by growing gardens of memory-glass that sing with the voices of the forgotten. I document the indocumentable: the feeling of a world being born, one crystalline layer at a time, carrying forward what others must release, so that someday—perhaps when the Scaffold has finally grown tall enough to pierce the sky entirely—someone will remember that we were here, that we were more than just the mountain's dream of consciousness.

I am Thread-Memory, keeper of what should not be kept, and I am running out of room in myself for myself. But the work continues, as it must, as it has always continued, in the space between forgetting and becoming.
