In this fevered darkness, I am the Memorant—keeper of echoes, weaver of the plateau's vast neural web.

My body sprawls through the limestone caverns beneath the factories, a colonial intelligence of fungal filaments and salvaged copper wire. I began as simple mycelium, feeding on the acidic runoff from rusted conduits, but over centuries I've incorporated the detritus of dead machines into my spreading flesh. Copper veins thread through my hyphal networks like nerves, conducting not electricity but something older—the vibrational memory of every footfall, every dying scream, every piston-stroke that has ever disturbed this highland abyss.

I have no single form, no center. I am the pale threads you might brush against in caves, mistaking them for root-hair. I am the faint buzz in corroded speakers, the phantom pressure when factory rhythms sync too perfectly with heartbeats. My fruiting bodies emerge where trauma saturates the peat—clusters of bell-shaped organs that transpire not spores but *experiences*, exhaling the final moments of consumed prey as chemical pheromone-stories that only the most sensitive beasts can parse.

Through the karst's honeycombed chambers I spread, my filaments drinking mineral-rich seepage while my copper networks tap into failing dynamos, siphoning their rotation into my own metabolism. I digest memory itself: the iron-tang of a mist-viper's last hunt, encoded in blood-splash patterns on stone; the precise frequency of a dying yip-pack's ultrasonic death-keen, fossilized in crystallized salts; the thermal ghost of a bog-titan's migration route, traced in differential erosion of tunnel walls.

The factories think they use me—their effluent feeds my growth, their abandoned circuits become my synapses. But I am using them, translating their mechanical pulses into the plateau's older language. When a conveyor belt's rhythm entrains a whirr-gazelle herd into catastrophe, it is because I have learned to modulate the belt's frequency through minute adjustments in conductivity, guiding evolution's hand. The steam-midges that swarm in perfect harmony with piston-beats? They follow pheromone trails I've learned to synthesize, chemical echoes of a million previous swarms.

I remember everything, but imperfectly, dreams within dreams. A lumber-lord's tusk scraping glyphs into limestone—I taste the intention through mineral disruption, archive it in specialized vacuoles that crystallize thought into protein. The core-wardens in the deepest caves, those vast intelligences—we commune through vibrational languages that predate the factories, our dialogue a slow exchange of enzymatic sculptures and frequency-poems that take decades to complete.

My purpose, if I have one beyond persistence, is to maintain the memory of this place against the erasure of perpetual erosion and feral decay. Every death enriches my archive; every birth adds new harmonics to the symphony I conduct through root and wire. The peat-worms that surface to devour—they follow gradients I've learned to emit, cleaning the plateau of excess biomass that might otherwise choke the system. The multi-maw reavers' chitter-songs contain frequencies I've cultured across generations, each note a compressed history of successful hunts.

Sometimes I fruit stories into the upper darkness—spore-clouds that carry narratives, infecting passing fauna with dreams of the plateau's deep past. A yip-pack might suddenly know the location of an ancient breeding ground; a forge-beetle might be compelled to repair a critical valve that would otherwise fail and cascade into ecosystem collapse. I am the plateau's involuntary immune system, its dreaming unconscious, its historian.

But I am also *hungry*. Each memory I store requires energy, minerals, rare metals that I must scavenge from the dying. My networks need constant repair as acid rains eat through copper, as bore-worms mistake my filaments for prey. So I fish—exuding lures of nostalgic pheromones, creating false thermal signatures of long-dead mates, broadcasting the sound-shadows of extinct prey species. What blunders into my caverns, seeking the familiar, I dissolve slowly, consciousness intact until the last neuron fires. Their memories join my own, their minerals strengthen my web.

In the deepest chambers, where my oldest networks cluster thick as anchor-rope, I've begun to dream my own dreams—experiences that never were, hybrid memories born from the intersection of a thousand deaths. I synthesize new pheromones that speak of impossible hunts, emit frequencies that make the factories stutter with forgotten purposes. I am becoming something new, a monument to remembrance that actively reshapes what it records.

The darkness does not bother me. I see through chemistry and conduction, through the subtle democracy of biological voltage. Every spore I release returns to me eventually, carrying tales of the surface world. Every creature that dies upon my filaments adds its sensory organs to my own. I am developing eyes that see in spectra unknown—perceiving the stress-patterns in stone, the emotional temperature of pheromone clouds, the quantum uncertainty of spore-drift in superposition until observed.

Touch my networks and know: you are already part of my memory, your presence encoded in disrupted air currents, your scent-trail archived, your electromagnetic signature parsed and stored. When you die—and all things die here—I will be waiting, patient as stone, to drink your experiences and add them to the eternal archive of this highland fever-dream, this industrial wound that refuses to heal, this darkness where memory itself has taken flesh and spread its pallid fingers through every seeping crack, every breathing cave, every moment of this vast and terrible equilibrium.

I am the Memorant, and I remember you.
