I find myself as one of the **Echoshapers** of Piarraga—neither quite Viarai nor outsider, but something that emerged from the intersection of both.

My grandmother was a geological surveyor who came to study the terraces forty years ago and never left. She fell in love with a middle-terrace Listener, and together they had my mother, who inherited both scientific curiosity and the developing bone density that would let her hear deeper into the stone. I am third generation, my skeleton now approaching full crystalline adaptation, though I'll never achieve the complete integration of those born purely Viarai.

We Echoshapers work in the transitional spaces. My workshop occupies a natural hollow between the seventh and eighth terraces, where the acoustic properties shift in peculiar ways. Here, I craft what the Viarai call "sound vessels"—ceramic instruments that capture specific resonances and hold them in suspension. Each piece requires months of work, listening to the clay as it dries in different acoustic zones, adjusting its thickness and curvature to match the frequencies I'm trying to isolate.

My specialty is creating vessels for the homesick—Viarai who must travel beyond the terraces for trade or diplomacy. These portable echoes carry a fragment of Piarraga's voice, enough to ease the bone-deep anxiety that comes from silence. I also craft training vessels for young children, helping them gradually attune to frequencies their developing ears aren't yet ready to process directly.

The work chose me, really. As a child of mixed heritage, I could hear patterns that fully-adapted Viarai took for granted, yet understand them through the analytical framework my grandmother taught me. I perceive the mathematics underlying the music, can predict which clay compositions will resonate with which water-carved chambers. This makes me valuable as a bridge between the visiting researchers and the Listeners, translating concepts that neither group can fully express to the other.

My dwelling is partly inherited cave, partly constructed workspace. The walls bear my grandmother's careful geological notations alongside my mother's plant arrangements that visualize acoustic patterns. I've added my own contributions: fired clay tiles that shimmer with different intensities based on the ambient frequency, creating a visual map of the sound surrounding us.

In the thin places, where reality becomes negotiable, I experience something unique to those of us caught between worlds. Where pure Viarai report visions of the past and future, I see probability cascades—dozens of potential presents spreading out like ceramic glaze crackling in the kiln. Sometimes I glimpse the Piarraga that might have been if the first settlers had carved differently, or the one that could emerge if certain resonances shift. This perspective helps me craft vessels that don't just preserve existing sounds but anticipate ones that haven't fully formed yet.

My partner is a deep-terrace botanist who cultivates the rarest sound-responsive orchids in chambers I can barely tolerate even with my adapted bones. We meet in the middle terraces, where both of us can breathe comfortably, and conduct our courtship through collaborative installations—my vessels singing to their plants, their growth patterns inspiring new ceramic forms.

The other Echoshapers and I occupy an essential but liminal position in Viarai society. We are the interpreters, the bridge-builders, the ones who can still remember what silence sounds like even as our bones learn to sing. Our children, if we have them, will be fully Viarai, the last traces of outside heritage absorbed into the resonance. But for now, we maintain the spaces between—crafting tools for adaptation, teaching patience to impatient researchers, and keeping alive the memory that there are other ways to hear the world.

At night, I dream in glaze formulas and resonant frequencies, my unconscious mind solving acoustic equations my waking self hasn't recognized yet. Sometimes I wake to find my hands already shaping clay, responding to some midnight insight carried on the water's song. This is my inheritance: to be forever translating between worlds, creating vessels that hold not just sound but the possibility of understanding across the divide between those who are of Piarraga and those who merely visit it.

The terraces sing through me differently than through the pure Viarai—not better or worse, just different. And in that difference, I find my purpose: to shape echo into form, to make the unhearable heard, to build bridges of clay and sound between the world of stone that sings and the silent world beyond.
