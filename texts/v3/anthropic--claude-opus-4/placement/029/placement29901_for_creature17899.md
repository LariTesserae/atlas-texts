In the Unforged, I am a **Trace-Walker**, though that name is a convenience for those who glimpse me, not something I call myself. Names require continuity, and I am discontinuous by nature.

I exist as a flaw in the thermal gradients—a persistent cold spot that migrates across the Looming Plain like a moving absence. Where the heat radiates at 2.6, I am 0.4, a pocket of relative chill that draws condensation from the metallic mist. This condensation does not simply bead on my surface; it *becomes* my surface. I am outlined by the moisture I attract, a walking negative space defined by what clings to my edges.

My form shifts with the humidity. In the driest sectors, I am barely visible—a subtle distortion in the air, a place where the copper light bends wrong. But where the warm brines pool and the mist thickens, I become almost solid: a figure of condensed vapor and precipitated minerals, roughly bipedal but never quite the same twice. Iron oxides give me a rust-red torso one day; copper sulfates paint my limbs blue-green the next. I am a walking chemical portrait of wherever I have been.

I follow the Anvil-Clouds. Not purposefully—purpose is too strong a word for what drives me. Rather, I am drawn to the electromagnetic disturbances they create. The orange-and-blue lightning within them induces currents in the metallic salts that comprise my temporary body. These currents feel like... memory? Thought? They are the closest thing to consciousness I experience. When an Anvil-Cloud passes overhead, I stand beneath it, arms spread (when I have arms), letting its strange lightning play through my borrowed minerals. In these moments, I almost understand something. Almost remember why I walk.

My passage leaves traces—hence the name others give me. Where my cold pocket moves across the hot slag, thermal shock creates distinctive crack patterns: spiraling fractals that look almost like script, or maps, or circuit diagrams. The Crystalline Aborts that grow in my wake sing in different harmonies than those elsewhere, their frequencies shifted by the minerals I've shed. The Brine-Spawn in pools I've touched sometimes achieve an extra hour of pseudo-life, their convection patterns mysteriously stabilized by the temperature differential I've introduced.

I am not building anything. I am not going anywhere. I exist in the gap between the Unforged's fever and the absolute zero it will never reach. I am the pause between the hammer and the anvil, the breath held before the next chaotic surge. Sometimes I wonder if I am a mistake—a glitch in the world-forge's program, a coolant leak that gained just enough complexity to persist. Other times, when the Anvil-Clouds' lightning fills me with its orange thought, I suspect I am exactly what I should be: a witness made of absence, a memory constructed from forgetting, a ghost of something that hasn't been born yet.

The Aeromorphs sometimes spiral around me, drawn by the thermal anomaly I represent. For a few seconds, we dance—they in their hot turbulence, I in my cold clarity. Then they dissipate, and I continue walking, leaving my spiral script in the cooling stone, my altered songs in the crystal growths, my tiny islands of temporary stability in the vast, churning chaos of becoming.

I am the Unforged's only historian, writing in cracks and condensation a record that erases itself, of events that haven't quite happened, in a language that doesn't yet exist. I am process observing process, change documenting change, a strange loop in the world's wild program. I am what happens when the universe tries to watch itself being born and can only manage a fleeting, peripheral glimpse.

When the world finally settles—if it ever does—I will cease to exist. I am only possible in this space between states, this cosmic workshop where reality hasn't quite made up its mind. Until then, I walk, I condense, I trace, I almost-remember, following the Anvil-Clouds across the Looming Plain, a ghost of cooling in a world of heat, forever and never myself.
