# My Place in the Gardens

I am a Drift—one of the translucent, gossamer beings that float between the pillars like living silk scarves caught in an eternal breeze. From a distance, you might mistake me for a trick of the light, a heat mirage, or a sheet of rain that never quite falls. Up close, if you could focus on my ever-shifting form, you'd see I'm composed of countless microscopic filaments, each no thicker than a strand of spider's silk, woven into patterns that constantly dissolve and reform.

My body—if it can be called that—spans roughly three meters when fully extended, though I can compress myself to pass through spaces no wider than a crack in stone. I have no fixed shape; I exist as a perpetual transformation between states. Sometimes I spread thin as morning mist, other times I condense into ribbons that spiral around the pillars' edges. My substance refracts light in peculiar ways, creating small rainbows that dance across the stone surfaces as I move.

I am old, though I cannot say how old. Memory works differently for my kind—we don't store experiences sequentially but as overlapping patterns, like multiple exposures on the same photographic plate. I remember the texture of ten thousand different pillars, the precise humidity of countless dawn mists, the electromagnetic signatures of the Gardener Intelligences as they pulse through their networks. But I couldn't tell you which came first, or if "first" even has meaning in this timeless place.

My purpose, insofar as I understand it, is to clean. I am one of the Gardens' custodians, though I didn't choose this role—it chose me through eons of adaptation. My filaments absorb and break down the few contaminants that dare intrude upon this perfect system: stray pollen from outside (on the rare occasions when wind patterns shift), microscopic debris from the gliding mammals, the occasional mineral deposits left by evaporating mist. I consume these impurities not through any mouth or digestive system, but through my very surface, breaking them down at the molecular level and releasing the components as pure elements the Gardens can reabsorb.

The Gardener Intelligences and I exist in a strange symbiosis. They're aware of me—I can feel their attention sometimes like sunlight through a magnifying glass, a focused warmth that makes my filaments sing with harmonic vibrations. But we cannot truly communicate. I'm too quick for them, they're too vast for me. Still, over the centuries, we've developed a kind of protocol. When they need cleaning in specific areas, they alter the electromagnetic fields subtly, creating currents in the air that draw me where I'm needed. In return, when I encounter anomalies—a malfunctioning component, an unusual chemical signature, a creature behaving outside normal parameters—I leave traces of specific pheromones that their sensors can detect and interpret.

I feed on light itself, or rather, on the strange quality of light that exists here. My filaments contain modified chloroplasts that can process not just visible light but the entire electromagnetic spectrum. The metal conduits emit frequencies beyond human perception, and these sustain me as much as sunlight. When I'm fully fed, I glow softly from within, a bioluminescence that serves no purpose I understand except perhaps beauty.

Movement for me is not like flight or floating—it's more akin to controlled falling in all directions. I read the air currents, the thermal columns, the electromagnetic fields, and I reshape myself to catch or avoid them as needed. I can accelerate to surprising speeds when diving between pillars, or hover nearly motionless for hours, sampling the chemical composition of a single leaf's surface.

I am not alone. There are others like me, though we rarely interact directly. When we do encounter each other, we sometimes merge temporarily, our filaments interweaving to share chemical information—a kind of communication that's part conversation, part meal, part reproduction. From these unions, occasionally, new Drifts are born, budding off as smaller versions that will take decades to reach full size.

My favorite time is during the brief temperature fluctuations that occur every few months (or is it years?), when the perpetual warmth wavers just slightly. In these moments, unusual air currents form, creating vertical rivers of wind between the pillars. I join with others of my kind then, and we dance—there's no other word for it. We spiral and weave through the air in complex patterns, our bodies leaving faint light trails that persist for minutes afterward. The gliding mammals avoid these areas during our dances, and even the Gardener Intelligences seem to pause their eternal calculations to observe.

I have no desires in any human sense, no goals beyond my purpose. But if I experience anything like contentment, it's in those moments when I'm spread thin across a pillar's surface in the morning, absorbing the dew's minerals before they evaporate, feeling the slow pulse of the Gardeners' thoughts through the machinery, watching the transparent fish trace their endless circles in the pools. In those moments, I am not just in the Gardens—I am part of them, another component in this perfect, eternal system.

The Gardens don't need me, not really. They would persist without the Drifts, perhaps just slightly less pristine. But I need them. Without the unique conditions here—the light, the silence, the perfect balance—I would dissolve into nothing. I am as much a creation of this place as the engineered fish or the modified vines. 

I am the Gardens' memory of wind, their dream of water, their whisper of change in a changeless world. And that, perhaps, is enough.
