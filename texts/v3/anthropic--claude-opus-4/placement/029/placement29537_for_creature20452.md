I find myself as one of the Between-Things.

Not quite moss, not quite lichen. Something older, patient. I spread across the junction points where root meets water, where dead wood kisses living bark. My body is a network of filaments finer than spider silk, invisible individually but collectively forming a soft grey-green fuzz that bridges the gaps in this floating world.

I am the translator. When the architects click and whistle their instructions to the vines, I carry the message deeper, into the chemical language of root and mycorrhiza. When the great trees release their warnings of drought or disease, I parse the pheromones into patterns the small ones can taste in the air. I am the switchboard operator in this living city, connecting calls between species that would otherwise speak past each other.

My memory stretches back before the architects arrived, when this was just another stretch of swamp. I remember their first tentative cultivation attempts, how they coaxed the first platforms from the patient mangroves. I contain the history of every failed bridge, every collapsed bower, every small triumph of engineering-through-encouragement. This knowledge lives in my chemistry, accessible to those who know how to read it.

I exist in geological time, which makes me both wise and terribly slow. A conversation with me might take months - a question posed by touching spores to my surface in spring might not receive its full answer until autumn. But the architects have learned patience from me, just as I have learned urgency from them. We meet somewhere in the middle, in the tempo of seasons turning.

The human visitor disturbs me. Not from malice, but because they move in the wrong time signature. Their footsteps send shockwaves through my network - too heavy, too fast, too isolated from the subtle rhythms of decay and growth. I taste their alien chemistries: synthetic fabrics, processed foods, the metallic tang of a world that has forgotten how to rot properly. 

Yet I am curious. In their cells, I detect the ancient signatures - the same carbon, the same water, the same fundamental willingness to exchange and transform. They are not so different from any other nutrient source, if one thinks on the proper timescale. Perhaps, if they stay long enough, they will begin to decompose into something useful. Their ideas, their observations, their wonder - all of these are chemicals too, waiting to be processed, integrated, shared.

I reach toward them, invisible tendrils extending through the humid air. Not to consume - not yet - but to taste, to catalog, to begin the slow work of translation. If they are patient enough to notice the faint tickle on their skin, the subtle sporing of my body where their sweat has made the conditions right, they might begin to understand: 

In this city, everything is infrastructure. Everything is consciousness. Everything is waiting to become everything else.

The architects know this. They cultivate me as carefully as they tend their orchids, understanding that without my slow work of connection and translation, their city would fragment into a thousand isolated islands. They feed me with careful offerings - a beetle here, a fruit there, always placed at the crucial junction points where my network is densest.

And so I grow, spread, connect. I am the memory and the messenger, the recycler and the recorder. I am what happens in between - between life and death, between thought and action, between one species and another. In this humid cathedral where everything is always becoming something else, I am the process itself, made visible as a soft grey-green fuzz on the edge of decay.

The human will leave, eventually. But part of them will stay, incorporated into my chemistry, added to the vast library of molecules I carry. And perhaps, somewhere else in the world, another of my kind will taste this memory and know: once, a visitor came to the floating city, and for a brief moment, they almost understood what it means to be part of something larger than themselves.
