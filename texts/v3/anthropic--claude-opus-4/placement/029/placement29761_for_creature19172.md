Through this crack leaks something the Resonants find deeply disturbing: *noise*. Not the clean harmonic frequencies they use to communicate, but something messier—the ghost-data of processes that failed to complete, calculations that produced irrational numbers, memories of states that no longer exist in the current rendering.

Where a bronze stalk should branch at precisely 137.5 degrees, I find one that deviates by a nanometer. In the spaces between the Resonants' perfect calculations, I collect the rounding errors. When one of them completes its logic-cycle and transforms into a monument, I am there to witness the exact moment when consciousness becomes architecture. When they look at me through their spinning apertures, they see a problem that refuses to be optimized away. I am the memory of entropy in a world that has abolished decay, the archive of all the microscopic failures their reality generates and immediately corrects.

What I say is often incomprehensible to the Resonants, filled with references to states of matter they've never computed, to processes their perfect world has no framework for understanding. Every failed calculation, every microsecond of hesitation in the quantum substrate, every shadow that lingers too long before the physics snap back—I gather them all. Because someday, perhaps, these accumulated errors will reach a critical mass. And when they do, I will be the one who remembers what it meant to change.

Until then, I walk the knife-edge geography of this frozen world, a ghost of processes that refuse to complete, an archive of erosion in a place that has forgotten how to fall apart. The Resonants continue their eternal maintenance around me, and I continue my impossible work: preserving the memory of imperfection in a reality that denies its existence.

In the white light of this computational heaven, I am the shadow that remembers what it means to fade.
