In the mineral pools beneath the world's skin, where water seeps eternal through stone chambers, I am the Chronicler of Echoes. My body is not quite flesh—more a condensation of memory and mineral, translucent where the dim light catches my edges, dense as bedrock at my core. I have learned to move like water through these subterranean settlements, my form shifting between states as needed: solid when I must carry the carved tablets of our histories, liquid when I flow through the narrow passages between chambers, vapor when I rise to taste the stories that cling to the humid air.

I make my home in a hollowed geode, its crystalline walls amplifying every sound into a symphony of reverberations. Here, the loud echoes that others find disturbing become my library. Each sound—the clicking of reptilian claws on stone, the splash of aquatic beings breaching the pool surfaces, the whispered trades in the market squares—leaves its impression in the dense air, and I have learned to read these sonic fossils like others read stone.

The small, dark-skinned humans know me by the faint chiming that accompanies my movements, a sound like water drops falling on crystal. They bring me their disputes and their celebrations, knowing I can recall every word spoken in these chambers for generations past. When the bird-beings with their shimmer-wings need to remember the ancient flight paths through the cavern networks, they seek me out. When the scaled hunters lose track of prey in the labyrinthine waterways, I can tell them which echoes to follow.

My work is never-ending in this stable equilibrium. Each day, I absorb the cacophony of life in these packed chambers—the primitive clang of tools being fashioned, the organizational calls of settlement leaders, the splash and gurgle of the eternal waters. At night, when the sounds soften but never cease, I distill these echoes into permanent forms, secreting mineral memories that crystallize on my geode walls like salt from ancient seas.

Sometimes I wonder if I was once human, or if I am what happens when a place becomes so saturated with story that it must grow a keeper. My memories of before are mineral-thick, compressed into layers I cannot fully read. But it doesn't matter. Here in this timeless underworld, where evolution forgot to bring the green growing things but remembered to pack every cubic space with strange life, I am exactly what I need to be: a living archive in a land of echoes, preserving the loud symphony of this weird, wet world where nothing changes except the stories we tell about staying the same.

The settlement-dwellers have a saying: "Even stone remembers if you listen hard enough." They don't know how literally true this is. They don't know that I am both the stone and the listening, the memory and its keeper, as eternal and essential as the water that seeps through everything here, binding us all in its patient, persistent embrace.
