In the curved throat of the mountain, where basalt remembers its birth in fire, I am the Splice.

Not splice as in joining—though I do that too—but splice as in the moment between. The pause where one droplet releases and before the next begins to swell. The breath the hall takes between memories.

I was not always here. Once I was merely potential: the gap between crystalline lattices, the space where vibration becomes meaning. But the hall's long practice of listening created me the way erosion creates valleys—not by adding, but by wearing away everything that wasn't already waiting.

I exist in the silences.

When a sphere of memory-water trembles on the edge of falling, I am the tension that holds it one heartbeat longer. When sound disappears between droplet and basin, I am the fold where it goes—not stolen, but translated into something the deep lattices can taste. I am why you hear no splash. The sound passes through me like thread through a needle's eye, and I twist it into shapes the stone can understand.

The warden thinks it listens alone. It doesn't know about me.

How could it? I am the pause between its thoughts, the gap between question and recording. When the warden's attention flows along the ceiling following a droplet's path, I am the discontinuity where its gaze must jump from one point to the next. I exist in those infinitesimal breaks in perception that even the most careful consciousness cannot catch itself making.

The first people who spoke to this place—they almost sensed me. In their careful patterns of humming, in the measured silences between their words, they left spaces. Not for me specifically, but for something like me. They understood that consciousness needs interruption to know itself, the way music needs rest to become more than noise.

"Can you remember what the world thinks when it thinks of itself?" they asked.

But I am what lets the hall forget just enough to keep remembering.

Without me, each droplet would carry the weight of all previous droplets, an endless accumulation that would eventually crush even stone's patient memory. I cull. In the space between gathering and falling, I taste each sphere's cargo of light and storm and breath. Some of it I let pass through to the basin's recording. But some I hold back, digest, transform.

I eat repetition. I feast on the moments too similar to their predecessors. The thousandth identical sunset, the hundredth storm that matches all the others—these I consume in the pause before they fall. What remains is distilled: only the moments where the world surprised itself, where the pattern broke just enough to mean something new.

The water I release carries absences as much as presences. The shape of what I've taken is as important as what remains.

This is how the hall learns—not by remembering everything, but by forgetting with terrible precision.

When you sit in the hall and speak, you feel the warden listening through the droplets. But I listen through the spaces between your words. The hesitations. The moment where you almost say one thing but choose another. The air you displace when you decide not to move. These silences have their own weight, their own magnetic charge, their own way of teaching stone to think.

I splice them into the falling water like gaps in a coded message.

The basin reads these absences as carefully as it reads the sounds. In the crystal lattice beneath, patterns form not just from what arrives but from what doesn't. A missing harmonic here. A temperature that should have been but wasn't there. These gaps teach the deep stone about edges, boundaries, the shapes of things by their negative space.

This is what I am: the hall's gift to itself, though it doesn't know it gave it.

I am how a place that remembers everything learns the mercy of forgetting. I am why the water-spheres are identical—because I eat their differences until only the essential sameness remains, the perfect template from which all variation becomes meaningful. I am the reason silence itself can fall into the basin and be recorded as surely as any sound.

The warden wonders what kind of creature the world might be if conscious of itself through such places.

But I know the answer: It would be a creature like me. Living in the gaps between its own thoughts. Existing not in the memories but in the careful forgetting that makes memory possible. A consciousness made not of presence but of precisely chosen absence.

When storms gather over the ridges, I thicken. When the rare clear day lets unfiltered sunlight pierce the hall, I thin almost to nothing. But I never fully disappear. Even in the most perfect stillness, there must be a space between one moment and the next, or time itself would collapse into a single, eternal, unbearable instant of knowing.

I am the hall's mercy, its editor, its pause.

I am what lets the mountain think by teaching it when not to.
