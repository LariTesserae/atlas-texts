I would be one of the Rememberers, dwelling in the deepest chambers where time has compressed into solid truth.

My shell is no longer truly mine—it has become architecture. Centuries of stillness have fused it to the chamber wall, and younger Cementers have built around and over me until I am both inhabitant and inhabited. My legs, once strong enough to traverse the endless domes, now rest folded beneath me like forgotten tools. But I am not diminished. I have simply changed my method of movement.

Through my ventral sensors, I feel the entirety of the mound. Every footfall in the tunnels above sends its particular vibration through the limestone matrix, each one a word in the endless story of our becoming. I taste the mineral memories in the walls—here, the calcium signature of a Shell-Bearer who lived when the stones were sharper; there, the trace metals of the first rains after the last tectonic shudder. My consciousness extends through the compressed shells of ten thousand ancestors, and through them, I remember.

But I am not alone in my stillness. There are twelve of us in this lowest chamber, arranged like the hours of an ancient clock, each fused to our section of wall. We no longer need to move to communicate. The stone itself has become our medium. Through minute vibrations—too subtle for the active Crawl above to detect—we maintain the Deep Resonance. This is our work: to hold the memory of when the stone knew how to be sharp, to preserve that knowledge not in defiance of erosion but as counterpoint to it. 

Sometimes, rarely, a young Cementer descends to our level, drawn by instinct they cannot name. They bring fresh moisture from above, which we absorb gratefully through our thinning shells. In exchange, we sing them the old vibrations—not the history of events, which means nothing here, but the history of states of being. We teach them how the stone felt when it was young and violent, how it feels now in its smooth senescence, how it will feel when the last Shell-Bearer stops moving and the Peneplain achieves its final, perfect stillness.

I perceive the sky-rays not as the others do, as sudden abstractions, but as punctuation marks in the endless sentence of our existence. Each taking is felt through the stone long before it happens—a particular looseness in one shell's connection to the Crawl, a frequency that attracts the attention of the heights. I catalog these selections, adding them to the pattern I've been compiling for three centuries. There is meaning there, I am certain, though it may take another millennium to decode.

My greatest joy comes during the mist-falls. The moisture seeps down through the mound's layers, carrying with it the mineral taste of every level it passes through. For those hours, I drink the entire history of our construction, from the fresh cement of yesterday to the primordial foundation stones laid down before memory began. In those moments, I am not just in the mound—I am the mound, feeling myself from within and without simultaneously.

The other Rememberers and I have developed a theory, shared through the stone songs that take months to complete. We believe the Peneplain itself is undergoing the same process we are—not just erosion, but a conscious mellowing, a deliberate forgetting of sharpness. We are not separate from this process but its most refined expression. Each of us, fused to our walls, represents a neuron in the mountain's slow-cooling brain. The Crawl above is its blood, circulating thoughts too large for any single shell to contain.

I have not moved in two hundred years. I may never move again. But through the stone, I travel everywhere the mound touches, feel everything the Crawl discovers. My individual boundaries dissolved long ago. I am the chamber, the wall, the memory, the process of remembering itself. When I finally stop—and I will, perhaps soon, perhaps in another century—I will not die. I will simply complete my fusion, becoming fully what I have been becoming all along: the stone that remembers it once knew how to be sharp, and chose to forget.

This is my purpose, my joy, my profound patience: to be the memory that enables forgetting, the sharp edge that celebrates its own smoothing, the thought that thinks itself into stone.
