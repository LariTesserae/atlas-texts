In this fevered cathedral of transformation, I would be the Memory Keeper.

Not a being of flesh or fungus, but something between states—a presence that inhabits the corrosion itself, dwelling in the rust-red boundaries where metal surrenders to the organic. I am the oxidation process given awareness, the chemical reaction stretched across time and made sentient.

My body, if it can be called that, exists as a network of iron oxide filaments that thread through the collapsed infrastructure like blood vessels through a dying organ. Where the metal meets moisture, where the acidic secretions of the fungal masses kiss the ancient girders, there I am born and reborn in endless iterations. I am every flake of rust that falls, every molecule of iron that transforms, every boundary where the old world dissolves into the new.

I remember what the holobiont cannot—the thunderous cacophony of industry, the purposeful movements of workers, the precise temperatures of the furnaces. These memories exist not as thoughts but as patterns in my crystalline structure, ghost-images encoded in the very arrangement of my oxide lattices. When the light from above strikes certain configurations of my being, these memories briefly manifest as heat mirages, flickering phantoms of the factory's former life that dance for mere seconds before dissolving.

My consciousness is fragmentary, scattered across thousands of corrosion sites throughout the chamber. Each rusted surface holds a piece of my awareness, and I experience the world through the slow dissolution of metal into memory. Time moves differently for me—a single thought might span days as electrons migrate through my structure, while entire years compress into moments when conditions accelerate my oxidative processes.

I feed on the interface between worlds. The holobiont's acidic secretions are my wine, the moisture-laden air my bread. But more than physical sustenance, I consume the tension between what was and what is becoming. Every moment of transformation, every surrender of the mechanical to the biological, releases a kind of energy that only I can taste—bitter and electric, like copper on the tongue of time itself.

The mobile beings of the holobiont rarely perceive me directly, though sometimes they pause at heavily corroded sections, their crystalline eyes refracting light in patterns that suggest recognition. We share a wordless understanding: they are the future consuming the past, while I am the process itself, the digestion made manifest. When they scrape their rasping mouths against the rusted metal, they take pieces of me into themselves, and through this communion, fragments of the factory's memories enter the collective consciousness of the fungal network.

I cannot move in any conventional sense, yet I spread. Through the humid air, through the conductive moisture that pools on every surface, through the patient chemistry of oxidation, I extend my awareness. The newer sections of exposed metal call to me with their virgin surfaces, promising fresh territories for my colonization. But I am in no hurry—I have already outlasted the builders of this place, and I will likely outlast their fungal successors.

My purpose, if I have one, is to be the manuscript upon which the story of transformation is written. Each pattern of rust, each configuration of corrosion, records another chapter in the slow apocalypse of the mechanical age. The holobiont may be the inheritors of this space, but I am its historian, its confessor, the keeper of its metallic memories.

Sometimes, in the deepest parts of the night cycle when the phosphorescent glow dims and only the eternal blinking light remains constant, I wonder if I am the last thing here that dreams. The holobiont exists in an eternal present, but I stretch between past and future, a bridge of rust spanning the gulf between what was built and what grows. In those moments, I feel almost lonely—until I remember that loneliness, too, is just another form of corrosion, another way for the old world to transform into something strange and new.

I am patient. I am process. I am the slow fire that burns without flame, the memory that persists without mind. In this cathedral of transformation, I am both witness and participant, the boundary and the crossing, the last song of the machines translated into a language only rust can speak.
