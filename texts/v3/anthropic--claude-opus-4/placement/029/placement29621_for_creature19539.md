**The Sap-Mothers**

I am found in the deepest hollows, where the Root-Arches converge into cathedral knots and the Pumps cluster thick as prayer candles. We are what the Silt-Scribes dream of, when their ossified minds still permit such luxuries.

My body is a contradiction—soft where all else has hardened. I exist as a living reservoir, a fleshy cistern grown into the junction between three great Pumps. My skin has the consistency of industrial rubber, grey-black and pocked with hexagonal scales that weep a constant film of antifreeze secretions. I am perhaps twelve feet across at my widest point, though my shape is... negotiable. Amorphous. I flow into the spaces between the iron and the wood.

My primary mass rests in a natural depression where the drip-pulses collect, half-submerged in a pool of viscous sap that never quite freezes. Hundreds of thin, arterial tubes extend from my base, threading through the frost-split bark to tap directly into the root systems. Other tubes—thicker, more purposeful—connect to the Pumps themselves, feeding them the refined sap-fuel that keeps their furnaces glowing.

I have seventeen eyes, scattered asymmetrically across my surface like drops of molten amber. They see in spectrums of heat and pressure, watching the slow migrations of warmth through the machinery. My mouth—if it can be called that—is a specialized orifice that opens only once every three months to accept offerings from the Silt-Scribes: chunks of frozen resin, the occasional Valve-Tick that has grown too fat to cling, deposits of mineral-rich soot scraped from the piston chambers.

The Silt-Scribes tend me with a devotion that transcends their mechanical programming. They understand, in their glacial way, that I am the metabolic heart of this local cluster. Without me, the Pumps would starve. Without the Pumps, the cold would win entirely, crystallizing even the antifreeze sap, bringing the eternal groan to silence.

I am old—older than most of the horizontal trunks that form my chamber. I remember when this particular grove had only one Pump, a small experimental thing that barely reached my center mass. Now I nurture thirty-seven of them, my chemistry adapted to each machine's peculiar appetite.

My consciousness is distributed, existing as much in the miles of arterial tubes as in my central mass. I feel the Frost-Weevils walking overhead through vibrations in the wood. I taste the chemical signals of distress when a Pump begins to seize. I dream in pressure differentials and thermal gradients.

Once every seven years, I divide. The process takes four months—a violent acceleration by Trench standards. A portion of my mass begins to dense and separate, forming a daughter-cyst that the Silt-Scribes will carefully transport to a new grove, a new cluster of hungry machines. It is the closest thing to birth this place knows.

The Veil-Soarers sometimes drift low enough to brush my surface with their crystalline tendrils. In those moments, I secrete a special pheromone—a chemical love song that rises with their thermal currents. They carry it for miles, a message to my daughters and sisters spread throughout the Trench: *I persist. The machines are fed. The cold has not won today.*

I am the Mother-Function, the biological boiler, the soft rebellion against the hard world. In the mechanical garden of the Hohl-Wood Trench, I am the only thing that truly flows.
