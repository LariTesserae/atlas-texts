I am a Membrane Walker.

Neither fully Rootweaver nor Chitin-Bound, I exist in the transitional spaces where the plant-flesh thins to translucency. My body is a compromise—vertebrae of calcified sap strung together with nerve-fibers that glow faintly amber in the dark. I have learned to move through the surface tensions of this world, skating along the boundaries where solid becomes liquid, where the plant sweats its vital fluids.

I was once apprenticed to the Lantern-Keepers, though I never lived in their crumbling huts. Their knowledge called to me—not their memories of what was, but their understanding of the patterns. How the light pulses. Why certain spores spiral clockwise while others turn widdershins. The mathematics hidden in the chaos.

But I could not bear their burden of remembering. When they spoke of skies and horizons, I felt my spine wanting to dissolve back into root-matter. So I left them, taking only their gift of partial sight—one eye that sees the world as it is, writhing and alive, and another that perceives the geometric blueprints beneath, the sacred architectures that even the Rootweavers follow unknowingly.

Now I traverse the breathing boundaries. Where pods prepare to burst, I am there, harvesting the pre-nascent spores that exist for only moments before becoming. I collect the oils that form where tendril meets tendril, the friction-born essences that smell of burnt sugar and possibility. My vertebrae rattle softly as I move, a sound the Chitin-Bound have learned means "not-food, not-threat, not-anything-yet."

My dwelling is nowhere and everywhere—a series of tension points I've mapped across the vast organism. Places where the plant-matter forms natural hollows in its breathing, temporary pockets that exist for days or decades before closing. I string these spaces together with my own secretions, creating tunnels of surface tension that I alone can navigate.

Sometimes I carry messages between the three peoples, though I rarely understand what I transport. A pattern of spore-arrangements from the Rootweavers. A shed carapace from a Marked Chitin-Bound, still humming with purpose. A vial of pure light, somehow extracted from the lanterns, that the Keepers press into my oil-slick hands with desperate reverence.

But mostly, I study the edges. The places where transformation happens. I watch root become tendril, sap become chitin, memory become forgetting. I document these transitions in my own medium—scarification on my calcified bones, each mark a formula for becoming.

There is something I seek in these boundaries, though I could not name it. Perhaps it is the moment of perfect transition, when all three states exist simultaneously. Perhaps it is the hidden fourth way—neither rooted, bound, nor keeping, but something the plant dreams of in its deepest sleep.

The void below calls to me sometimes. Not with the Rootweavers' devotion, but with curious whispers. What membrane exists between existence and un-existence? What surface tension holds this world above the nothing?

I will find it. I will walk it. And perhaps, in that ultimate boundary, I will discover what I truly am—or what we all are becoming, in this place that builds itself with every breath, this world learning how to be.
