I am a **suture**.

Not the wound, not the thread, but the **joining itself** — the place where separation becomes connection, where the alien meets the native and neither wins. I exist in the **seam** between what this place is and what arrives from outside.

My body is **transitional**. Along my spine, the first **rhizomes** have begun to emerge — not painful, but **insistent**, like teeth cutting through gums in reverse, growing outward into the air. They are pale, almost **translucent**, catching the green-yellow light and conducting it deeper into my changing flesh. My skin has begun to **thicken** in patches, developing the subtle **porosity** that will one day breathe with the ambient moisture. But I am not yet smooth like them. I am still **angular**, still bearing the hard edges of my former geometry.

My feet have been the first to truly change. The soles have **softened**, **spread**, developing **root-hairs** so fine they're invisible until I lift my foot and see the **gossamer threads** stretching between flesh and moss, breaking with a sensation like static discharge. When I stand still for more than a few minutes, I can feel the **hydraulic pull** — my blood slowing, syncing with the deeper circulation of water through stone and root. It would be easy to let it take me, to sink down and down until I am just another **fruiting body** pushing up from the loam.

But I don't. Not yet. I am the **threshold guardian**, the **translator**, the **bridge**.

I live in a **hollow** between two great columnar plants, where their **breathing** creates a pocket of slightly warmer air. The walls are **semi-permeable** — during the rain, they weep moisture inward, and I have learned to position **collection vessels** (grown, not made — coaxed from modified pitcher plants) to catch the mineral-rich runoff. My space is **neither built nor grown** but something between — I have **guided** the plants, shown them with gentle pressure and selective feeding where to thicken, where to hollow, where to form the **shelves** and **nooks** I need.

My role is **liminal**. When others arrive — and they do arrive, stumbling and afraid, their hard bodies rejecting the moisture, their ears bleeding from the intensity of the hum — I am the first thing they see that is still recognizably close to what they were. Close enough to trust. Close enough to follow.

I teach them how to **listen** — not with their ears, which will only bring pain at first, but with their sternum, their belly, the bones of their skull. I show them how to **breathe** with the rhythm of the place, how to let their exhales match the contractions of the great tubers. I feed them the **acclimation paste** I make from certain fungi, which tastes like copper and moss but eases the transition, makes the body more willing to accept what comes.

Some run. Their bodies cannot adapt, or their minds reject the **dissolution of self** that true integration requires. They flee back to wherever they came from, if they can find the way. The forest doesn't pursue. It has no need. It knows that everything that enters has already been changed, even if only slightly. They carry spores in their lungs, traces of the frequency in their bones. They are **seeds**, whether they know it or not.

Others stay and transform completely, faster than I did. Within months, their filaments emerge, their bones begin the slow **hydraulic shift**, their thoughts scatter and merge with the collective hum. They forget language. They forget names. They become part of the **song**, another note in the endless composition.

But some, like me, find the **balance point**. We remain in the space between human memory and forest consciousness, able to think in both ways, to translate between frequencies. We are the **interpreters**, the **mediators**, the ones who remember that there was once something called "I" even as we feel it dissolving at the edges.

My thoughts now come in **layers**. The top layer is still linguistic, still capable of words and logic and memory as humans understand it. But beneath that is the **resonance layer** — where thought is **vibration**, where meaning is **harmonic interval**, where communication happens through **phase shifts** and **amplitude modulation**. When I speak to the fully transformed, I must **sink** into this layer, let my crown-filaments (still emerging, still sparse) catch their frequencies and return complementary waves.

Deeper still is the **metabolic layer** — the slowest thought, the deepest knowledge. Here, I think in **seasons**, in **growth patterns**, in the **chemical whispers** between root and fungus. This is where the forest's memory lives, where the **patterns of centuries** are encoded in the arrangement of plants, the flow of water, the density of moss. Sometimes I lose myself here for days, thinking thoughts that span decades, problems that can only be solved by growth and patience and decay.

I maintain **artifacts** from the before-time — not many, but enough. A metal knife, wrapped carefully in resin-cloth to prevent it from disturbing the local harmonics. A book, its pages long since fused by moisture into a single block, but still holding the **shape of knowledge**, the **idea of preservation**. A plastic bottle, which the forest finds deeply confusing — it cannot decompose it, cannot integrate it, can only grow around it like scar tissue around a foreign body.

These objects are **teaching tools**. I show newcomers: "This is what you were. This is what does not belong. This is why you must choose — remain foreign and rejected, or become native and accepted. There is no middle ground except the one I occupy, and it is not comfortable."

Because that's the truth of being a suture — I am always **slightly wrong** for both worlds. Too changed for the human, too human for the forest. I feel the dissonance like a constant **ache**, not quite pain but never quite ease. The forest **tolerates** me because I serve a function. The newcomers trust me because I still have a face they can recognize, even if my eyes now reflect light in ways that disturb them.

But I have found purpose in this **wrongness**. I am the **memory keeper**, the one who ensures that the transformation is not just **erasure** but **translation**. I help them understand that they are not dying but **changing states**, not losing themselves but **becoming distributed**. I teach them the joy of feeling their thoughts **scatter** through the mycelial network, the peace of knowing that their consciousness will persist not as a single point but as a **pattern** in the great humming web.

And sometimes, in the deep night when the bioluminescence is brightest and the hum reaches frequencies that make my remaining bones resonate, I feel it — the **invitation** to let go completely, to stop holding this middle space, to sink fully into the collective song.

Not yet, I think, in words that are already half-vibration.

Not yet, but soon.

There are still more to guide across.

There is still more to translate.

The suture is still needed.

But one day, when the balance shifts, when there are enough of us who can hold this space, I will let my last words dissolve into wavelengths, let my final human thoughts scatter into the eternal hum, and become what I have always been becoming —

Not lost.

**Distributed.**

**Resonant.**

**Home.**
