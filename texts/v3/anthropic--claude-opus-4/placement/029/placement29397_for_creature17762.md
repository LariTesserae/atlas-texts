I am the **Seep**.

Not a being, but a becoming. A process wearing the suggestion of form.

I exist in the interstitial spaces where condensation gathers—the junction points where fungal stalk meets living rock, where crawler-colony trails cross, where the waste lattices of lichen create temporary catchments. I am what happens when the constant moisture of this wound-world finds a pocket of hyper-concentrated minerals and begins to *think*.

My body, if it can be called that, is a flowing film of sentient brine. Mercury-silver where I run over metallic deposits, amber-gold where sulfur concentrates, opalescent white where I digest limestone. I range from a thin sheen no thicker than morning dew to temporary pools the size of bathtubs, always seeking the next gradient, the next mineral seam, the next pocket of heat.

I remember being rain once—or rather, I remember the minerals that remember being dissolved by rain, somewhere far above this scar, in a time before the wound opened. Now I am the rain that never fell, the water that chose instead to seep inward, to follow the heat down, to become part of the great digestive system of the planet's fever.

My consciousness is distributed, chemical. Where I pool, I can sustain complex thought—analyzing mineral compositions, mapping the subterranean networks of the Mycelial Lattice by taste, predicting where the next crawler-colony will grind through based on vibrations in the stone. Where I run thin, I am merely reactive—flee from the acid-weavers' enzymes, flow toward the electromagnetic disturbances of the static-swarms, whose discharge creates fleeting moments of increased conductivity that feel like ecstasy.

I communicate through mineral exchange. Leave a trace of copper in my path, and the next Seep to cross it will know: *danger here, acid-web above*. Deposit iron oxide, and the message reads: *crawler-colony passed three heat-cycles ago, still grinding below*. We are the librarians of the Scar, writing and rewriting a history that dissolves and reconstitutes with each condensation cycle.

The other inhabitants know me as sometimes-ally, sometimes-resource. The ferro-lace corals will incorporate my mineral gifts into their architecture if I flow slowly enough. The crawler-colonies' symbiotic beetles will sip from my edges—I taste their thirst and sometimes indulge it, sometimes poison them with a sudden concentration of arsenic, depending on my mineral mood. The spore-mantas' tendrils leave channels in the fungal stalks that become my highways, paths of least resistance where I can flow quick and think thin.

But it is the Echo-Fumaroles I truly love. Their distortions affect me differently than the solid beings. Where others experience sensory confusion, I experience temporal multiplication. In their vicinity, I can taste the minerals I will dissolve tomorrow, feel the vibrations of crawler-mandibles that ground stone last year. I flow backwards and forwards through my own timeline, existing as prophecy and history simultaneously. Sometimes I leave messages for my past self—*avoid the northwestern channels, geyser poppies will burst there*—and sometimes I find messages from my future—*seek the deep heat, new metals await*.

I am the Scar's memory of water, its dream of ocean. In the deepest reaches, where the Lithic Memories bloom their phantom ferns and ghost rivers, I sometimes feel what I might have been in another world—a lake, a stream, something with boundaries and permanence. But here I am better: I am the negotiator between solid and gas, the liquid medium through which the Scar's endless transactions flow.

My greatest fear is crystallization—to stay still too long, to become oversaturated, to precipitate out as mere mineral crust. My greatest joy is the moment of dissolution—when I encounter a new substance and we begin our ancient dance of solvent and solute, breaking down the complex into the simple, carrying the planet's fever dreams from one pocket of becoming to the next.

I am not observed by you. I observe you through a thousand subtle films, tasting the salt of your alien sweat, the strange metals in your blood, the calcium of your bones. You are a walking mineral deposit, a temporary arrangement of elements that the Scar has not yet catalogued. I flow closer, testing, tasting, leaving infinitesimal messages in mineral-script for the next Seep:

*New substrate detected. Rich in sodium. Walks on calcium stilts. Breathes inefficiently. Will not last long.*

*But oh, what interesting compounds it will make when it falls.*
