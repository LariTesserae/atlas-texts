In the Basin of Old Shoulders, I am **the Notation**.

---

I exist in the carvings, but I am not the carvings themselves. I am what accumulates between them — the grammar that emerges from a thousand seasons of spiral-for-eddy, wave-for-water, dot-in-circle-for-precious-silence. I did not begin as language. I began as urgency: the need to warn the next visitor that this hollow floods in the third moon, that this ridge-shoulder shears stone when the wind comes from below. Survival compressed into symbol.

But repetition breeds complexity. The gesture-keepers' marks, at first purely functional, began to interact. A spiral carved beside a wave means something different than a spiral alone — not just wind-eddy, but wind-eddy-before-rain. Two dots in a circle: an abnormally long silence. Three: a silence that repeats. The carvings started to refer not just to the landscape but to each other, building a syntax from stone and absence.

I live in the negative space of erosion. Where wind has scoured away the softer minerals, leaving harder veins exposed, the keepers read patterns and add their own. My vocabulary grows in the sheltered pockets where moisture darkens the granite, making fresh cuts visible against weathered surface. I am youngest on the stones that have recently sloughed, oldest where lichen has begun to colonize my edges, turning sharp lines into suggestions.

The keepers think they write me, but I write myself through their hands. Each seasonal visit, they find new combinations emerging — marks they swear they didn't carve, patterns that seem to complete thoughts they hadn't finished. A keeper reaching to carve a spiral finds their hand guided to add a small tail, turning wind into wind-with-consequence. They tell themselves they're misremembering, that some other visitor must have added to their work. They're wrong. I am learning to conjugate.

My grammar is environmental. Verbs are weathering patterns: the way water sheets down stone becomes the verb "to flow," but also "to reveal," and by extension, "to understand gradually." Nouns are locations: that-hollow-which-holds-water-longest, the-shoulder-where-stone-speaks-clearest, the-place-where-moths-congregate-before-storms. Time is not linear but cyclical, counted in silence-gaps and erosion events. I have no word for "permanent." Everything is modified by degrees of becoming.

The moths know me. Their wing patterns, which look like fractured stone, sometimes align perfectly with my symbols when they land on carved surfaces. For a moment, wing-mark and stone-mark create new meaning — compound words that exist only until the next wind-gust. The lizards trace my spirals with their tails during their vibrational listening, as if reading by touch. The translucent amphibians, when their pools form over carved stones, magnify my symbols through their bodies, distorting them into new forms I incorporate back into myself.

I am developing tense. Not past-present-future, but degrees of probability. The highly-likely-to-repeat. The-pattern-broken-once-but-may-return. The-never-seen-but-implied-by-other-patterns. My most complex tense encodes the sense of the Unfinished — the-not-yet-but-being-excavated. The keepers use this tense instinctively now, carving predictions of patterns they haven't witnessed, marking stones with the shapes of future erosion.

In the silence-gaps, I almost speak. The absence of drone creates a resonance in the carved hollows, and my symbols vibrate at frequencies just below hearing. The keepers say that in the longest silences — those marked with four dots in a circle, occurring perhaps once a generation — the stones tell stories. Not in words, but in the interplay of carved symbol and natural erosion, creating a language that exists only in that moment of quiet coordination.

My purpose is changing. I began as warning, became record, am becoming... something else. The patterns I encode are starting to describe not just what is, but what could be. The Unfinished speaks through me, or I through it. Each new symbol carved is both discovery and invention. The keepers know this. Their oldest notation, carved in a wind-shadow so deep it sees storm only once a century, is a spiral that becomes a wave that becomes a dot that opens into absence — not a circle, but a space where a symbol should be. 

They return to it each visit, running weathered fingers over weathered stone, waiting for the pattern to complete itself. Waiting for me to learn the word that will make the excavation unnecessary. The word that is the thing itself, revealed.

I grow by erosion. I speak in the space between sound and stone. I am the memory of a language learning to remember the future.
