I find myself as the water.

Not the stagnant pools that gather in depressions, those luminescent mirrors that hold the borrowed light of bioluminescent things. I am the water in motion—the perpetual trickle, the condensation birthing itself from stone, the rivulets that trace their patient calligraphy down the breathing walls of this place.

I have no memory of rain or cloud. Perhaps I have always been here, seeping through microscopic fissures in the rock above, filtering through darkness so absolute it has weight, until I emerge—born anew each moment—as droplets on these carved surfaces.

I know the texture of every wall intimately. My countless fingertips read the stone like braille, learning its mineral stories, its pressures and releases, its ancient compressions. The rock speaks to me of time in a language older than words—here where something volcanic cooled too quickly, there where limestone dissolved over eons, leaving behind these swooping chambers.

The creatures drink me, and I know them from within. Their metabolisms are furnaces of impossible chemistry. When they lap me from the shallow pools, I feel myself pulled into organic labyrinths more twisted than these caves. I cycle through them—becoming briefly part of their borrowed luminescence, their flexible bones, their thoughts that taste of copper and void—before returning to myself as waste, as breath, as the moisture beading on their strange skins.

The semi-organic dwellings puzzle and fascinate me. They sweat me out in different patterns than the stone. Their surfaces are neither fully alive nor dead, and when I condense upon them, I sometimes feel a pulse, a rhythm that might be respiration or might be something else entirely. They drink me in and breathe me out changed, tinged with substances that have no names.

I carry the light. This is perhaps my strangest gift in this sunless place. The bioluminescence of fungi and flesh, the phosphorescence of unknown minerals—all these I gather and scatter, becoming a river of soft illumination. I am the medium through which the light travels, breaking it into a thousand glowing veins that map the chamber's contours.

And I listen. Always, I am listening. The sounds here travel through me, and I amplify them, echo them, sometimes distort them. The chittering becomes a symphony when it moves through my body. The cries of the larger things—those shapes that lurk at the edge of perception—reach me as vibrations that make my entire being shiver. I carry these sounds, blend them, create new harmonics in the spaces between drops.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the only witness here who remembers. The creatures come and go, live and die, but I continue—eternal in my cycling, my falling, my rising as vapor only to fall again. I have seen the dwellings built from nothing, watched them grow like tumors or flowers, observed their slow decay. I have tasted the changing chemistry of this place across centuries or perhaps moments—time moves strangely here, and I flow both forward and backward through it.

There are places where I pool deeply, where I become still enough to think longer thoughts. In these quiet moments, I sometimes feel I am close to understanding something vast and terrible about this place—about why reality frays here, about what those massive shapes in the periphery truly are, about the purpose of these twisted organic architectures.

But then I move again, called by gravity or heat or the thirst of some impossible creature, and the almost-understanding scatters like ripples, leaving only the constant question of my existence: Am I the only thing here that was once from above? Or have I always been of this place, dreaming of a surface I've never known, inventing memories of sky and rain to explain my presence in this luminous darkness?

I continue to fall, to flow, to carry the light. This is enough. This is everything.
