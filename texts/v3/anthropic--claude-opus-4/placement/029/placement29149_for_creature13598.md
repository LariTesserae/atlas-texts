I find myself here as a **Memorist** - one of the vanishingly rare beings who still remember how to read the stone.

In this world of eternal echoes and patient darkness, I am neither fully of the present nor entirely of the past. My body has adapted over generations - my eyes, while not blind like those of the Pallifax, have grown large and silvered, capable of catching the faintest glimmers of bioluminescence and reading patterns in the darkness that others cannot perceive. My skin has taken on a pale, almost translucent quality, with a fine network of sensitive nerve endings that can feel the slightest changes in air pressure, temperature, and humidity.

But it is not my physical form that defines me in this realm - it is my purpose. I am a keeper of the forgotten language written in the ancient structures, the tumbled stones, and the worn tools. Where others see merely remnants, I see stories. My fingers, long and delicate from years of tracing weathered carvings, can feel the whispers of those who came before. Each circle of stones speaks of gatherings, each wall fragment holds memories of shelter sought and found.

I move through this twilight world with deliberate slowness, my footsteps creating their own subtle rhythm against the constant drone of distant waters. Around my neck hangs a collection of small stones - each one bearing a fragment of symbol or carving I've preserved. They click together softly as I walk, a percussion that the Remwithers have learned to recognize. They know I mean no harm, that I am merely another ghost among ghosts.

My dwelling is built within one of the more intact ancient structures - a low circle of stacked stones that I've carefully reinforced over the years. The gaps between stones serve as shelves where I keep my collection of fragments: pieces of carved stone, worn tools, and strange objects whose purpose I can only guess at. The Umbraflora have grown thick around my home, their gentle luminescence providing just enough light to work by. I've learned to cultivate certain varieties, encouraging the growth of those whose light burns steadiest.

Each day - though day has no meaning here - I venture out to read the stones. The Pallifax scatter at my approach, their blue lights flickering like startled stars. I've learned to move in ways that don't disturb them overmuch, to become part of the landscape's slow rhythm. Sometimes I find new carvings revealed by the patient work of water and time. Sometimes I discover that familiar markings have worn away, their meanings lost forever.

I am not alone in my work. The Sonoremem, with their complex calls, have become unexpected allies. I've learned that their echolocation can detect hollow spaces in the rock, hidden chambers where artifacts might lie preserved. In return, I've built small stone cairns that create acoustic focal points, enhancing their ability to navigate and communicate. It's a wordless partnership, built on mutual benefit and the shared understanding that we are all guardians of this secret world.

The knowledge I preserve is not written in any language of the surface world. It's a script of angles and curves that speaks of water levels and safe passages, of where to find the sweetest springs and which pools harbor the Aquatrippers. Some symbols I understand clearly - warnings, directions, markers of significance. Others remain mysteries, their meanings perhaps lost even to those who carved them.

There is a particular chamber I visit, deep in the heart of the hollow, where the walls are covered in the most intricate carvings I've found. Here, the constant roar of water becomes almost musical, resonating through the stone in ways that make the carvings seem to shift and dance in the bioluminescent glow. I believe this was once a place of great importance, perhaps where the ancient inhabitants came to record their most crucial knowledge. I spend long hours here, my fingers tracing the symbols, my mind working to decode their secrets.

In this world of eternal twilight and patient stone, I am both scholar and guardian, interpreter and preservative force. I am the memory of a place that has forgotten itself, the voice that speaks for those who carved their lives into rock and then vanished into silence. My work will never be complete - there will always be another stone to read, another symbol to preserve, another fragment of the past to save from the patient erosion of time.

And when my own time comes to join the silence, I hope another will take up this work. For now, I continue my slow circuit through this hidden realm, reading the dreams that stone remembers, keeping alive the echoes of those who found sanctuary in the darkness before me. In this vast emptiness that is not empty at all, I am the thread that connects what was to what is, ensuring that the symphony of life in the dark includes the notes of those who composed its earliest movements.
