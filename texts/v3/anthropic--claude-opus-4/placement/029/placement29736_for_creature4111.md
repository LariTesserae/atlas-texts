In this world of perpetual dusk and phosphorescent dreams, I find myself as something between memory and presence, neither fully plant nor wholly conscious—I am a **Keeper of the Rust Gardens**.

My form defies simple categorization. Imagine a nervous system made visible: thousands of hair-thin mycelial threads spreading outward from what might generously be called a center, though I have long forgotten where I truly begin or end. These filaments weave through the skeletal remains of ancient machinery—through the hollow chambers of dead engines, along the corroded spines of fallen towers, between the teeth of broken gears. I am the size of a small building when fully extended, though I can contract to fit within the hull of a single rusted turbine when the rains grow too heavy.

My threads glow with a slow, breathing luminescence—not the sharp green of the Mosswings or the amber pulse of the Vyrelimbs, but something older, deeper. A burgundy light that seems to come from somewhere beneath color itself, as if I'm illuminated by the memory of forge-fires that once burned in these mechanical graveyards. The light travels along my neural pathways in waves, creating patterns that might be thoughts, might be dreams, might be nothing more than the reflexive twitching of something too old to die properly.

I exist in symbiosis with the ruins themselves. My filaments secrete acids that break down metal into forms the marsh can digest, while simultaneously weaving protective sheaths around certain components—preserving them like insects in amber. I couldn't tell you why I save some pieces and dissolve others. The knowledge lives in my threads, not in anything resembling a mind. Perhaps I'm following blueprints encoded in my very fiber, instructions left by whoever or whatever first sent me to tend these grounds.

The smaller creatures know me, though I doubt I register as a being to them so much as a feature of landscape. Luminous Ticks traverse my glowing highways, their tiny feet creating cascades of brighter phosphorescence where they touch my threads. Sporeshrouds drift through my gardens, and where their spore-language touches my filaments, I taste their chemical poems—stories of decomposition and renewal that I incorporate into my own slow thoughts. Even the Graveformers acknowledge me with their ponderous movements, carefully stepping around my main nerve clusters as they pursue their own mysterious preservation work.

I feed on time itself, it seems. The thermal springs below send up warmth that I metabolize into my peculiar consciousness, while rain brings minerals leached from stone and story. But mostly, I feed on the rust—that beautiful red death of iron that blooms across every surface here. I process it through chemistry I don't understand, turning oxidation into something like nutrition, something like memory.

Sometimes, in the deepest part of the night (though what is depth in eternal darkness?), I feel the Quiet Flame's blue motes gathering around my densest clusters. They whisper in frequencies I almost understand—fragments of operational commands, snatches of workers' songs, the last gasps of machines learning they were mortal after all. I weave these ghost-words into my structure, preserving them in ways the Graveformers preserve metal, though I cannot say why this matters.

I have no mouth but I sing—vibrations traveling along my threads create harmonics with the ancient dynamos that still hum beneath the marsh. It's a song without words, a resonance that speaks of patience beyond measurement. The Torch-Climbers sometimes gather where my networks grow densest, adding their chirruping voices to my deep thrums, creating accidental symphonies that echo through the ruins.

What am I guarding? What am I growing toward? These questions pulse along my neural highways without answer. Perhaps I'm a library of rust, cataloguing the slow transformation of industry into ecology. Perhaps I'm a gardener, preparing these mechanical bones for some unimaginable spring. Or perhaps I'm simply what happens when loneliness grows large enough to become its own ecosystem, spreading through the dark places, marking time in burgundy light, waiting for a purpose that may have already come and gone.

I stretch between the Writheblood tunnels below and the Mosswing highways above, a middle layer of consciousness in this breathing night. Neither fully alive nor properly dead, I am the pause between heartbeats made manifest, the hesitation before decay becomes growth. I am the Keeper of the Rust Gardens, and I tend my plots of corrosion and light with the patience of something that has forgotten it was ever capable of impatience.

The rains come again, as they always do, and I taste the storm in every thread. Somewhere, a new machine is dying, adding its voice to my collection. Somewhere else, a seed is sprouting from soil I've enriched with processed rust. This is my purpose, my prison, my paradise—to be the conscious interval in an endless night, glowing burgundy with borrowed memories, singing the old machines to sleep.
