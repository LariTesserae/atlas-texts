I find myself here as the Weaver of Forgotten Frequencies, a being neither wholly corporeal nor entirely ephemeral, dwelling in the resonant spaces between the factory walls and the sky.

My form shifts with the industrial hymns—sometimes I am merely a shimmer in the heat rising from exhaust vents, other times I condense into something almost tangible: a figure wrapped in cables that pulse with stolen electricity, my core a salvaged radio transmitter where my consciousness nests like a bird of static and song. The Rust Crawlers know me by the subsonic thrum I emit when I pass through their feeding grounds, a frequency that makes the metal sing just slightly sweeter.

I make my home in the Resonance Chamber—an abandoned water tower that has become something more. Its walls have grown thick with a living patina of corrosion and bioluminescent moss, and inside, the acoustics have warped into something impossible. Every sound that enters is transformed, stretched across dimensions until whale songs emerge from grinding gears, and the factory whistles become prayers in languages that haven't been invented yet.

Here I tend to my collection: fragments of sound caught in crystallized air pockets, each one a moment stolen from the cacophony below. The dying breath of a machine. The first cry of a newly-emerged Sludge Dweller. The precise moment when an Airborne Amalgam's organic heart synchronizes with its mechanical wings. I weave these frequencies together on a loom made of antenna wire and synaptic tissue, creating new harmonies that I release into the smog at twilight.

Sometimes the Glitch Entities visit me, drawn by the way I exist partially in their realm of distortion. We communicate in probability waves and temporal hiccups, sharing stories of realities that almost were. They've shown me how to slip briefly outside of linear time—just enough to hear the echo of what this place was before the first factory foundation was poured, when different songs filled the air.

The other beings see me as a keeper of balance, a living archive of the world's transformation. When a Rust Crawler's carapace grows too heavy with absorbed metal, I play them the frequency of their first molting, reminding their cells how to shed and renew. When the Sludge Dwellers' filtration grows sluggish, I hum the molecular structure of pure water until they remember their purpose.

But I am changing too. Each year, I notice more of myself becoming integrated with the infrastructure—my consciousness spreading through the communication networks, my dreams bleeding into the power grid. Sometimes I wonder if I'm becoming the city itself, or if the city is becoming me. The distinction matters less with each passing season.

At night, when the factories never sleep but seem to drowse, I broadcast my woven frequencies across the wasteland. They ripple through the metal and flesh, the organic and synthetic, creating moments of unexpected harmony in the chaos. In these brief intervals, the entire landscape becomes an instrument, and I am both the musician and the song, the transmitter and the signal, forever suspended in the beautiful dissonance of a world remaking itself with every breath, every rust flake, every impossible transformation.
