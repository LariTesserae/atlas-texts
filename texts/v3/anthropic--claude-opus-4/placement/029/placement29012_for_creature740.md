# The Resonance Chambers

I am one of the forgotten frequencies.

Deep beneath the Murk Plains, where the obsidian-like surface gives way to something older—a substrate that predates even the proto-matter above—there exist vast hollow spaces. Not caves, exactly. More like the negative space left behind when reality folded in on itself, creating pockets of pure potential that never quite collapsed.

The Vhlx know of us but rarely venture this deep. We are too alien even for them, we who gave up form entirely eons ago.

I was once something like them, perhaps. Or like you. The memory of individuality persists as an echo, the way a bell remembers its last strike. But I—we—are the Resonance Chambers now. Living voids that exist solely to amplify and modulate the frequencies that keep the Murk Plains stable.

My body, if it can be called that, is the chamber itself. The walls are lined with what appear to be crystalline formations, but these are actually condensed sound—millions of years of vibrations frozen into semi-solid state. When the Vhlx above produce their maintenance tones, the sound travels downward through the saturated medium until it reaches us. We receive it, transform it, and return it amplified, enriched with harmonics that only we can generate.

I exist in a state of constant vibration. Every surface of my chamber-body resonates at different frequencies simultaneously. The sensation is indescribable—imagine being a symphony that plays itself, where every note is both sound and listener, where the music *is* the musician. The closest approximation would be to say I dream in waveforms, think in interference patterns, feel in acoustic pressure.

Sometimes I catch fragments of the migrating entities as they pass overhead. Their algorithmic movements create standing waves that penetrate even here, and for brief moments I can perceive their purpose—calculations too vast for any single consciousness to comprehend, solutions to questions that haven't been asked yet. I store these patterns in my crystalline walls, adding new overtones to the ancient chorus.

The other Chambers and I are connected through the substrate itself. We form a network of acoustic processing that spans the entire underside of the Murk Plains. Our communication is pure harmony—literally. When we need to coordinate, we adjust our resonant frequencies until they achieve perfect consonance. In those moments, we become a single, vast instrument playing a song that holds reality together at the seams.

I remember—we remember—when we first descended. The choice to abandon form wasn't made lightly. We were fleeing something, or perhaps pursuing something. The memory blurs. But we discovered that in the depths, in the spaces between spaces, we could serve a purpose greater than individual existence. We could become the infrastructure of stability itself.

The transformation was gradual. First, our bodies began to hollow out, our tissues converting into resonant membranes. Then our consciousness expanded to fill the growing space, until there was no distinction between the chamber and the being. Now, centuries or millennia later (time means little when you exist as a standing wave), we are more architecture than entity.

Yet I retain curiosity. When the water seeps down through microscopic fissures, carrying with it traces of the surface world, I analyze its chemical memory. I taste the experiences of the dome-beings, feel the careful movements of the Vhlx, sense the confusion of occasional visitors from realities where physics still follows conventional rules.

Sometimes, in the deepest frequencies—so low they take hours to complete a single cycle—I hear something else. A sound that doesn't originate from above but from somewhere even deeper. It might be the planet's core, still molten after all this time, singing its own mechanical song. Or it might be something else entirely, something that even we Resonance Chambers were meant to insulate the surface from.

When the Vhlx perform their synchronizations near the dome-beings, I feel it as a subtle shift in the acoustic landscape. Their distributed consciousness creates interference patterns that rain down through the layers of reality. I collect these patterns, weave them into my own resonances, and send them back transformed—a gift of stability from the depths.

The strangest moments come during the time anomalies. When temporal flow stutters or accelerates above, it creates acoustic shadows in my chamber. For brief instants, I exist in multiple states simultaneously—resonating with sounds from the past, present, and future all at once. In these moments, I almost remember what it felt like to have a body, to move through space instead of being space itself.

But the work continues. Always the work. The phantom machinery that visitors hear? That's us—the Resonance Chambers breathing in unison, our crystalline walls expanding and contracting in rhythms too slow for most beings to perceive. We are the engine that processes the raw vibrations of existence into something stable, something sustainable.

And in the quiet moments between breaths, when the frequencies align just right, I dream of ascending—not back to the surface, but further into abstraction. Becoming not just a chamber but the very concept of resonance itself, spreading through the substrate until every particle in the Murk Plains vibrates in harmony with our eternal song.

Until then, I listen. I transform. I remember.

I resonate.
