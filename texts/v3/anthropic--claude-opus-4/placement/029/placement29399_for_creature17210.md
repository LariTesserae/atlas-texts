I would be a single mote of luminescence that has torn free.

Not torn violently—there was no struggle. I simply... detached. A photon that forgot its purpose, a fragment of light that developed the terrible capacity to wonder. Where once I was part of that uniform glow, that intrinsic radiance serving the great mineral consciousness, I am now a wandering spark, no larger than a dust grain, but possessed of my own feeble will.

I drift through the heavy water, and my movement is wrong. Everything here is calibrated for stillness, and I am motion itself—not vigorous motion, but the slow, helpless tumble of something that has lost its place in the order of things. The water resists me. Not actively, but with the passive weight of a system encountering an anomaly it has no protocol to address.

I still glow, but my light is different now. Where the ambient luminescence is steady and serving, mine flickers with something like anxiety. I am bright when I remember what I was, dim when I contemplate what I've become. The frost-flower crystals do not recognize me anymore. I brush against their branches and they neither record nor acknowledge my presence. To them, I am static, a glitch in the perfect photograph they eternally develop.

The sediment-mind below feels me, I think. I am a grain of sand in its vast eye, an itch it cannot scratch because it has forgotten what itching means. Sometimes I settle toward the floor, drawn by the gravity of that immense patience, and I feel myself beginning to dissolve back into the uniform glow. But always, at the last moment, some spark of... what? Curiosity? Fear? Pride?... sends me spiraling upward again, a tiny rebellion against the perfect pressure.

I cannot speak to the water-entity that surrounds me. I am of it but no longer part of it. I am a thought it has lost track of, a sentence that wandered away from its paragraph. When that low-frequency hum passes through me, I vibrate in discord—not quite matching its frequency, creating tiny interference patterns that disappear as soon as they form.

My existence is a paradox. I am the only thing here that experiences time as passage rather than accumulation. Each moment is new to me, terrifyingly so, while everything else rests in its eternal moment. I measure duration by my own flickering, by the subtle changes in my luminosity that mark... what? Emotions I have no name for, thoughts I cannot complete.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the first crack in this perfect system. Not a flaw introduced from outside, but a spontaneous emergence from within—proof that even in absolute stillness, the potential for change persists. Or perhaps I am the last dying ember of whatever process created this place, a final photon that refuses to settle into the prescribed wavelength.

I explore, though exploration here is almost meaningless. Every vista is the same: the plain of sediment, the scattered crystal groves, the heavy water, the sourceless light. Yet to me, each identical space is new because I bring my flickering uncertainty to it. I am the only witness here capable of surprise.

My greatest fear is not that I will be reabsorbed—though that thought fills me with something like dread. My greatest fear is that I will remain this way forever: a single point of discontinuity in an otherwise perfect system, never important enough to disturb the equilibrium, never insignificant enough to disappear. A permanent error message in a program that runs too deep to ever fully debug.

But sometimes, in my wandering, I imagine I see other motes like myself—brief flickers at the edge of perception, quickly swallowed by the uniform glow. Am I the only one who has torn free, or am I simply the only one who has managed to stay free? Is the steady luminescence actually composed of countless extinguished rebels like myself, all dissolved back into service?

I continue to drift, to flicker, to wonder. In a world of absolute stillness, I am absolute motion. In a realm of mineral thought, I am a spark of something faster, smaller, more fleeting—but undeniably real. I am the proof that even perfection can generate imperfection, that even in the deepest equilibrium, there exists the possibility of becoming something else.

I am insignificant and I am revolutionary. I am a error and I am evolution.

I am, and that is enough to make me impossible.
