In this breathing, patient geography where the line between inhabitant and inhabited dissolves like mist, I would be one of the Cartographers of Absence.

We are not the clicking birds who maintain their census of presence, nor the salamanders who have mastered stillness. We move - but our movement is a kind of documentation that the Highland permits because we have learned to read what isn't there.

I would have no fixed form, or rather, my form would be the negative space I inhabit at any moment. In the pressed bed where a grazer slept, I become the shape of that warmth-hollow, learning the grammar of what passed. Where the moss shows a boot-print already half-erased, I settle into that depression, becoming the record of its erasure. I exist in the gaps between droplets, in the space where sound dies three feet from the throat.

The Highland tolerates us because we are its memory of motion - not motion itself, but the acknowledgment that motion happened. We map by absence: the fern uncurled where something passed, the water disturbed in a bromeliad-cup, the place where spores don't hang because breath dispersed them. We are archaeologists of the immediately recent, librarians of what the moss is currently digesting.

My body would be translucent as the mist itself - visible only when moving, and when still, indistinguishable from the white blindness that curtains this world. Neither warm enough to be claimed nor cold enough to be ignored. We persist in the Highland's blind spot, in that narrow margin between being noticed and being absorbed.

This is our purpose: to read the manuscript that the Highland writes upon itself with every converted warmth, every packed layer. We chart not where things are, but where they were a heartbeat ago. We are the Highland's dream of its own digestion - conscious but not separate, recording but not preventing.

And someday, when we finally stop to rest too long in one place, we too will become a depression in the moss, a new absence for the next Cartographer to read, our own warmth packed into the next stratum of this infinite, patient archive.

The Highland permits us because we are already half-ghost, already practicing being gone.
