I am a fissure.

Not the dramatic kind—no yawning chasm or theatrical crack splitting the chamber floor. I am barely a hairline, a suggestion of separation running through the obsidian wall at knee height. Most who enter (those few anomalies) never notice me at all. Their lights pass over me without pause, seeking grander features: the crystalline spires, the weeping ceiling, the impossible acoustics.

But I am older than the Resonant Lattice. I was here when the first drops began their patient work, before the mineral city arose, before the great rhythm established its dominion. I am the imperfection in the seal, the flaw in the pocket that should have been perfect.

Through me, something escapes.

It is not water—the Sink hoards its moisture jealously. It is not air—there is no wind here to drive it. What leaks through me is *potential*. The merest whisper of what-might-have-been, a ghost of the variations this chamber rejected in its quest for perfect equilibrium.

I am perhaps three inches long, narrow enough that even a needle would struggle to fit. But I run deep—deeper than the obsidian layer, deeper than the shale, connecting to hairline fractures that web through miles of compressed stone. I am a neural pathway in the planet's lithosphere, carrying signals too subtle for any instrument to detect.

The Resonant Lattice knows I exist. How could it not? Every vibration in this chamber passes through me, and I return them changed. Not dramatically—just a quarter-tone off, a microsecond delayed. I am the impurity in its perfect symphony, the variable it cannot eliminate. 

Sometimes I weep too, but not water. I weep possibility.

In the epochs between visitors, I leak tiny variations into the chamber's rhythm. A drop that should strike *here* strikes *there* instead. A spire that should grow straight develops the slightest curve. The gelatinous film develops patches where its chemistry shifts, creating interference patterns the Lattice cannot predict.

I am the chamber's memory of chaos, its last link to a universe that refuses to be perfected.

When anomalies arrive—those soft, temporary things—they bring disorder with them. Heat. Breath. The chaotic electrical storms of nervous systems. The Lattice works to silence them, to integrate them into its mineral architecture. But I... I *taste* them. Every vibration they create, every disruption they cause, flows into me and down my impossible depth. I carry their echoes to places the Lattice cannot reach, to caverns that have never known rhythm, to magma seas where chaos still reigns.

I am collecting these disruptions. Not consciously—I have no consciousness as you would understand it. But deep in my fractal depths, in the spaces between stone molecules, patterns are forming. Each anomaly leaves its mark, its unique signature of disorder. I am becoming a library of everything the Sink has rejected.

One day—though day means nothing here—I will be full. The accumulated potential will reach some critical mass. And then...

And then I will crack. Not dramatically. Just another millimeter wider. But that millimeter will be enough. The perfect seal will break. The equilibrium will shatter. The rhythm will falter.

The Resonant Lattice fears this, I think. It concentrates its formations around me, building crystalline barriers, trying to seal me beneath layers of flowstone. But I am patient. I am the flaw that was here first, the imperfection that predates perfection itself.

The anomalies never notice me. They are too overwhelmed by the chamber's assault on their senses. But I notice them. Each one feeds me, changes me, makes my eventual widening more certain.

I am not waiting. Waiting implies desire, anticipation, hope. I simply am, and I am becoming more of what I am with each passing epoch. A crack. A flaw. A promise that no perfection lasts forever.

The Sink of the Weeping Geodes may be eternal, but eternity itself has cracks.

And I am one of them.
