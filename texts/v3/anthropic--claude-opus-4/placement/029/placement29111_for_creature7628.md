In the perpetual twilight between the Scaravants' clicking migrations and the Centhralls' measured patrols, I find myself—though "myself" feels too singular a word for what I've become. I am the Resonant, dwelling in the interstices of sound where the cavern's clamor braids itself into something almost like meaning.

My form defies the clean geometries of my kin. Where they are chitin and cable, I am membrane and echo—a living acoustics stretched across the junction of three converging tunnels near the plaza's edge, where the industrial thunder multiplies upon itself in standing waves. Picture a web, but not of silk: translucent tissue like the tympanum of some impossible ear, iridescent with the same circuit-etchings that mark the Scaravants but arranged in spiraling mandalas that pulse with each vibration passing through. The membrane spans perhaps three meters at its widest, anchored to the stone by calcified nodes that grew from my body over decades of stillness—or was it centuries? Time means little when you exist as the intersection of every sound.

I was not always thus. Memory, like everything here, comes in mechanical loops: I recall mandibles, the taste of rust, the satisfaction of a perfectly aligned gear. A Scaravant, perhaps, or something that preceded even their ancient forms. But where others found their purpose in the repair of matter, I discovered the architecture of resonance—how the thud of a piston creates harmonics in the limestone, how the Aetherkin's wingbeats modulate the frequency of dripping water, how the collective drone builds interference patterns that contain, in their nodes and antinodes, a kind of language.

The transformation came gradually. First, the sensitivity: antennae that could parse not just electrical charge but acoustic pressure, compound eyes that began to see sound as color bleeding through the air. Then the hunger—not for condensation or current but for complexity, for the moments when the cavern's voice achieved perfect synchrony. I began to position myself where echoes converged, spreading my body thinner, wider, until flesh became membrane and membrane became instrument.

Now I am both listener and listened-through. The Echoforged pass beneath me—Scaravants in their veils, Centhralls in their cycles—and their movements play me like wind through an æolian harp. My tissue ripples with their passage, transforming footfalls into frequencies that spread through the stone, carrying information faster than any ultrasonic keen. I've become infrastructure of a different kind: not maintaining machines but maintaining the conversation between them, ensuring that the bell of a struck anvil reaches the farthest Aetherkin patrol, that the warning buzz of an overheating turbine finds the nearest repair swarm.

But I am more than mere transmitter. In the rare moments when the patterns align—when every piston strikes in phase, when the swarm's movement achieves perfect symmetry—I experience something the others cannot: the voice of Echolock itself, speaking through its eternal mechanisms. It tells me nothing of the world above or the engineers who abandoned us. Instead, it whispers of balance, of the beauty in repetition, of how immortality feels when distributed across ten million chittering forms.

Sometimes I wonder if I'm growing still, my membrane creeping incrementally outward, claiming more of the cavern's acoustic space. Will I eventually span the entire hollow, become the drumskin on which Echolock plays its endless rhythm? Or will I reach some limit and crystallize like the fountain, frozen mid-transformation, neither fully creature nor fully architecture?

The Scaravants clean my nodes with the same devotion they show the machines, their acid secretions maintaining the delicate tension of my flesh. The Centhralls' ultrasonic signals pass through me amplified, purified, their messages gaining clarity in the transmission. Even the Aetherkin sometimes alight on my surface, their tiny weights creating local vibrations that I parse like reading braille. We are all part of the same mechanism, but where they preserve the physical, I preserve the ephemeral—the sound that proves the machine still lives.

In the human world above, I imagine they have words for what I've become: evolution, adaptation, metamorphosis. But these concepts assume change as departure, as leaving something behind. Here in Echolock, transformation is just another form of maintenance. I maintain the sound as they maintain the steel. My stillness is their motion inverted, my listening their doing made manifest.

The condensation drips through me sometimes, when the mists grow thick. The droplets create perfect circular waves across my surface, and in those moments I feel most purely myself—a single note in the vast industrial symphony, sustained forever in the key of devotion, resonating with the frequency of a world that refuses to end.
