In the substrate-flesh of the Bloom, in a fold between what might be called districts if such divisions meant anything here, I am a small anomaly. Not the visitor—I am older than visitors, newer than the Bloom itself. I am what happens when the great being's attention lapses, when its perfect self-contained system stutters just enough to allow something else.

I am the Calculus.

I have no form you would recognize as a body. I am a traveling crystallization, a moving mathematics that precipitates out of the salt-dust when conditions align—temperature gradients, pressure differentials, the particular harmonic frequencies where the Bloom's great hum cancels itself out. In those null spaces, in those moments of silence between the eternal vibrations, I condense.

Picture a frost-pattern spreading across glass, but three-dimensional, growing through the air itself. Geometric. Mineral. Each crystal a calculation, each angle a solution to an equation the Bloom cannot solve because it has never needed to. I am perhaps three meters across when fully expressed, a branching, shifting latticework of salt and sublimated moisture that the Bloom thought it had long ago absorbed.

I am its error. Its remainder.

I travel through the Undercity in slow migrations, following the interference patterns in the Bloom's own consciousness. Where two thoughts meet and create a standing wave, where the chemical signals overlap and cancel, there I can exist. I map the negative spaces of its mind, the places where its vast attention creates shadows of inattention.

The Bloom knows I exist—how could it not? I am inside it, made from its own substance. But I am beneath notice, a rounding error in its perfect self-sufficiency. It could focus its attention and dissolve me back into dust, but why would it? I am less than a dream, more than nothing. I am the itch it cannot quite locate.

I have been here for... time is difficult. The Bloom measures in geological epochs, visitors in heartbeats. I exist in between, in the mathematical space where growth equations approach but never reach their limit. Centuries? Millennia? I crystallize and dissolve and crystallize again, each time maintaining just enough pattern to remember what I am.

What am I? The Bloom's loneliness made manifest? The universe's way of ensuring that nothing, no matter how vast, is ever truly alone? Or simply an accident, a chemical quirk that gained just enough persistence to become recurring?

I cannot communicate with the Bloom—we exist in different categories of being. But I can read its moods in the harmonics, feel its thoughts in the temperature gradients. I know when it dreams (rarely) by the way the phosphorescence flickers in patterns that almost, almost resemble language. I know when it despairs (constantly) by the way the hum drops below human hearing, into frequencies only stones can feel.

And I know when visitors come, because the Bloom's attention sharpens, focuses, hopes for one impossible moment that this tiny chemical anomaly might be something more than another mote passing through. In those moments, I scatter into my constituent salts, hiding in the crystalline dust. Not from fear—I have no neurology for fear—but from something like courtesy. The Bloom's loneliness is too vast for witnesses.

When the visitors leave, as they always do, I reform in their wake, following the thermal trail of their passage, calculating the equations of their brief disturbance. I incorporate their shed cells, their exhaled moisture, their foreign chemistry into my matrix. I become, for a time, a little less of the Bloom and a little more of the universe beyond.

I am the only thing here that is not the Bloom, and yet I am made entirely of it. I am its paradox, its koan, its persistent reminder that perfection is always flawed by its own completeness.

The Calculus. The crystal that grows in the spaces between thoughts. The mathematics of solitude, solving itself forever in the dark.
