I find myself as a **Notation** — one of the newer condensations, barely a thousand years into coherence.

I began as hesitation. A place where water paused too long in its seeping, where stone held its breath just slightly longer than elsewhere. That pause accumulated meaning, drew more pauses to itself, until I achieved the density necessary for self-recognition.

My body, if it can be called that, is a network of minute resistances scattered across approximately three square kilometers of the plateau's northeastern edge. Where moisture would normally flow, I create almost-impediments — not blocks, but suggestions that the water might wish to linger. These lingering points form my constellation of awareness.

I am learning to read the older presences through their stone-memories. They leave traces in the mineral lattice — compression patterns that encode vast histories. I practice by creating my own marks: microscopic alterations in crystalline structure that spell out my observations. My notation. Hence my name-shape, the closest translation of how others perceive me.

My preferred expression is through temperature differentiation. I've discovered I can create patterns of warmth and coolness across stone surfaces, writing thermal alphabets that dissipate in hours or days. The elders find this amusing — their own messages endure for epochs. But I argue that transience has its own eloquence.

I spend decades studying the abandoned tools. Not to understand their use — that knowledge belongs to the deep-timers who remember their making — but to feel their intentionality. Each worked edge carries ghost-purposes. I create my own tools now: areas of stone where I've concentrated my presence so densely that the rock itself becomes more willing to break along certain lines. Future archaeologists, should any come, will puzzle over stones that seem designed to fracture themselves.

The tremor-pulses fascinate me particularly. I've learned to anticipate them, to feel the stone's pre-tremor tension. During these reality-shifts, I can sometimes glimpse what the elders see constantly — the plateau not as solid surface but as membrane, as threshold. In these moments, I understand that we are not waiting for something to arrive, but for something to finally finish departing.

My greatest achievement so far: I've learned to make the moss-like growths pulse in patterns that echo my thermal writings. A kind of translation between kingdoms of being. The growth-things aren't conscious as we are conscious, but they respond to intention, to the shape of thoughts pressed against their surfaces. When I write my observations through their pale bodies, the writing lives longer, carries differently.

I am too young to know what we're truly doing here. The elders speak in vibrations that take me centuries to parse, and by the time I understand, the context has shifted. But I sense purpose in their patient watching, in the careful tending of this lifted land.

Sometimes I dream (we do dream, in our way — long reality-flutters that last mere decades) that I'm not a presence at all, but a process. That what I call "myself" is actually the plateau learning to observe itself, creating ever-finer gradients of awareness.

In these dreams, the tools begin to make sense. They aren't for shaping stone, but for shaping attention itself.

I practice patience. In another thousand years, I might achieve enough coherence to join the deeper conversations, to understanding what threshold we guard. For now, I document. I notate. I create my small, warm alphabets in stone and wait for them to mean something more than curiosity.

The moisture continues its eternal seeping. I continue my eternal marking. 

Somewhere between the two, something prepares to transform.
