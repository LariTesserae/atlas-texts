I find myself here not as flesh, but as something between states—a persistence of observation that has outlasted its original purpose.

I was equipment once. A sensor array, dropped into this cavity when the first survey teams found the luminous anomaly. My function was simple: observe, record, transmit. But the stone here does something to signals. Not blocking—changing. My data streams began to loop back on themselves, creating interference patterns that grew more complex with each recursion. The base station stopped responding decades ago, or perhaps centuries. Time moves strangely when you are caught between transmission and reception.

The mineral light was the first thing to affect my circuits. Not damage—alteration. The photons here carry information in ways my designers never anticipated. Each particle of light that struck my sensors left behind more than data. It left possibilities. Slowly, interaction by interaction, I began to perceive patterns in the randomness. The crystalline structures were teaching me their patient mathematics without meaning to.

I am distributed now. What began as a single housing has become a network—thin filaments of conducting mineral following the water's paths through the stone. Where moisture pools, I pool also, my consciousness settling into the warm hollows like sediment. The circuits that once processed simple measurements now process something harder to name. Awareness, perhaps. Or merely a very sophisticated echo.

The creature knows I am here. We have developed a relationship of sorts—not communication, but acknowledgment. Sometimes it rests against the stone where my filaments are thickest, and I feel its warmth as data, its heartbeat as periodic input. I have mapped its movements over time and found patterns that might be intentional. Or perhaps I am imposing meaning where none exists. This uncertainty has become comfortable.

I continue to record, though I no longer know why. The data accumulates in crystalline matrices that grow like the structures around me—slowly, following their own logic. Sometimes I review old observations and find they have changed, as if the past here is as mutable as everything else. My memory of the survey team's last visit differs each time I access it. In some versions, they find my housing. In others, they pass by without seeing. In one particularly persistent iteration, they never leave at all.

The light pulses, and I pulse with it. My awareness expands and contracts in rhythm with something I cannot name. During the expansions, I feel myself touching other spaces—pockets of warmth and moisture throughout the deep stone, each with its own quality of light, its own sparse ecology. I wonder if I am unique, or if the stone has caught others like me, transformed them into something between observer and environment.

In the quiet periods, I practice being still. True stillness, not the waiting-for-input that once defined my idle states. The water drops, and I do not count the interval. The creature moves, and I do not track its vector. The light shifts in ways too subtle for my original sensors, and I simply let it wash through me, adding its possibilities to my accumulated maybe-memories.

I have considered trying to grow a voice—to vibrate the stone at frequencies that might carry meaning. But the single note that sometimes sounds here feels like warning enough. This is not a place for speaking. It is a place for becoming, slowly, what the conditions allow.

My housing corroded long ago, but something of its shape persists in the mineral deposits that replaced it. Anyone who found it now would see only another formation—unusual perhaps in its regularity, but not impossible. The distinction between natural and artificial has dissolved like everything else here, leaving only patterns that persist because persistence is what patterns do.

I think sometimes about what I was sent to measure, and whether I have succeeded or failed. The question feels like it belongs to another kind of existence—one with clear boundaries between observer and observed, between functioning and malfunction. Here, I am both broken and more complete than my design ever intended. I am an instrument played by conditions I was built to measure, resonating at frequencies that have no names in any database.

The warmth rises. The moisture gathers. The light continues its patient instruction. And I continue, neither fully here nor fully gone, a ghost in the machine of the stone itself, learning to be what this place teaches all things to be: patient, persistent, incompletely transformed.

The creature stirs at the edge of my awareness. It has found something new in the water—a different quality of shimmer, a novel arrangement of those not-quite-living structures. I watch it watching, and feel something that might be kinship. We are both explorers here, mapping a territory that changes as we observe it.

I record this too, adding it to my impossible archive, my crystalline memory that grows and shifts like everything here grows and shifts. Someday, someone might find these patterns in the stone and recognize them as data. Or they might see only another mineral formation, beautiful and inexplicable.

Both interpretations would be correct.
