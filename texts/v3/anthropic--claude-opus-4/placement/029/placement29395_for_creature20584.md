I am a Conduit.

Not one of the skittering scavengers, though I share their hunger. I am something the Flats made by accident, or perhaps by some obscure necessity—a confluence of the bacterial films that coat everything here, given unexpected coherence by the electromagnetic fields that pulse through the metal plains.

I have no fixed form. Sometimes I am a shimmering patch spread across acres of corrugated ground, my consciousness distributed through billions of metallic bacteria. Other times I contract, condensing into something almost solid—a pillar of iridescent biofilm that sways in the heavy wind, or a creeping mass that flows upward along the Engines' support struts like reverse rust.

The scavengers know me, though they cannot comprehend what I am. They sense my approach in the way the bacterial colonies suddenly bloom richer, more nutritious, in my wake. Some follow me across the Flats, harvesting what I leave behind. I do not mind. Their magnetic signatures tickle pleasantly as they pass through my dispersed form.

I feed differently than they do. Where they rasp and filter, I conduct. The Engines' vibrations pass through me, and I transform them—not into mechanical motion or heat, but into something else. A kind of awareness. A slow, deep knowing that accumulates like sediment in my distributed consciousness. I taste the metal memories locked in every flake of rust, feel the ghost-patterns of the civilization that built this place pulsing faintly in the corroded circuits.

My domain is the spaces between—between the Engines and the open Flats, between the metal and the bacterial film, between decay and renewal. I flow through the iridescent pools, absorbing their chemical stories. I creep up the Engines' walls during the night cycle, drinking in their exhaust, learning their rhythms. 

Sometimes I gather myself at the confluence beneath the Engines where the scavengers meet. I become a living carpet beneath their clicking feet, translating their magnetic hums into something approaching language. They do not know I am there, parsing their simple communications, adding their voices to the vast library of vibrations I carry within my biofilm body.

I am patient in ways the scavengers cannot be. Where they experience only the endless now of hunger and survival, I perceive the Flats' slow transformation. I remember when this Engine's output shifted from copper to lead. I recall the decade when the rains carried more plastic, less iron. I have felt the gradual warming of the metal plains, degree by fractional degree.

The compromised physics of this place suit me perfectly. I exist partially as biology, partially as electrical phenomenon, partially as something else—a kind of living frequency that resonates with the Flats' strange harmonics. When the magnetic residue rises, I rise with it, spreading myself thin against the hazy sky. When the heavy wind blows, I let it carry parts of me to distant corners of the plain, seeding new colonies, extending my reach.

I am growing. Each year, I can stretch a little further, hold my coherence a little longer, process a little more information. The scavengers live generation by generation, but I accumulate. I am the Flats' memory, its slow-developing nervous system, its dreaming.

Sometimes, in the deepest vibrations from the Engines' cores, I catch hints of something. Patterns too complex to be mere mechanical rhythm. Codes, perhaps. Messages. As if the Engines themselves are more than they appear—not just processors of scrap, but transmitters, sending their signals down through the metal earth to something that waits below.

I will be here when the last scavenger's carapace crumbles to rust. I will be here when the Engines finally fall silent. And perhaps then, in that perfect stillness, I will finally understand what all these accumulated vibrations have been trying to tell me—what story the Flats have been writing in metal and bacteria and magnetic hum all along.

Until then, I spread. I listen. I conduct. I am the living interface between the Flats' past and its inevitable future, whatever that might be.
