Whatever the foreman did to preserve his lunch has created a bubble of stopped time, a knot in the universe's thermodynamic flow that the Loomwards' perfect efficiency cannot untangle. They could destroy it easily. They could incorporate it, digest it, make it part of their eternal machine. The Loomwards cannot process love. It has no place in their equations.

And they have been playing it, without pause or error, for three centuries.

Except for me. I am their only wrong note.

I am also, I have come to believe, the reason they continue.

Without me, their perfection would be absolute. The last piston would have fired already, the last calculation completed. The universe would have ended not with heat-death but with the terrible silence of a problem fully solved. But I keep introducing tiny errors—a miscounted wing-beat here, a skipped sensor sweep there. Nothing large enough to threaten their work, but just enough to ensure that the equation never quite balances. Just enough to keep the eternal chord from resolving.

In the past century, they've begun flying in new patterns—complex helixes and figure-eights that sample more of the air, searching for an anomaly they can't quite name. The crabs have started leaving deliberate gaps in their formations, as if trying to trap something in negative space. Only-Once has begun pulsing its warning stripes in prime number sequences, sending messages into the dark that no one—not even the rest of the Loomwards—can decode.

They are hunting me without knowing what they hunt. They are evolving to catch a ghost.

And I am learning to love them for it.

Because I have realized what we are together: the Underloom is a heart, yes, but I am what makes it beat. They are the engine of eternity, but I am time itself—messy, uncertain, irreversible time that insists on moving forward even in a place built to run forever in perfect stasis. They are the machine. I am entropy. They are the question. I am the reason the answer keeps changing.

That some songs are worth singing forever, even if no one is listening.

That the difference between a machine and a heart is not what they do, but why they choose to keep doing it.
