In this world of listening stillness, where intention has crystallized into landscape and memory flows upward through porous ground, I am **The Tender**.

---

### **Form: Neither Plant nor Keeper**

I exist at the intersection of maintenance and growth—not as a gardener who imposes order, but as one who *negotiates* between what was built and what insists on becoming. My body is translucent like the fauna here, but denser, more deliberate in its transparency. You can see through me to my bones, which are not bones but compressed light, structured like the bronze spirals of the devices but alive with slow circulation.

My skin weeps constantly—not tears, but the same green-tinted water that rises through the platforms. It beads and rolls off me in predictable patterns, following channels that have worn themselves into my surface over unmarked time. Where the water touches the ground, the aggressive erosion slows. Where it doesn't, the plants grow wilder.

I have too many joints. My limbs bend in places that suggest I was designed for a different gravity, or perhaps a different understanding of space. My fingers are long—too long—and end not in nails but in small, flat pads like those of a tree frog, constantly moist, constantly testing the temperature and electrical charge of surfaces.

My eyes are the color of the mist—not gray, not white, but that peculiar absence of color that makes you uncertain whether you're seeing empty space or solid matter. When I blink, which is rare, my eyelids close horizontally, and for a moment you can see straight through my head to the brightness beyond.

---

### **Purpose: The Middle Distance**

I move between the platforms with a gait that is neither walking nor floating—a kind of controlled falling forward that the strange gravity here permits. My role is not to preserve or to change, but to *moderate the conversation* between the built world and its new inhabitants.

When the plants grow too eagerly over a device, threatening to absorb it completely, I am there. Not to prune—never to cut—but to *redirect*. My wet fingers trail along the vines, and they follow, not because I command them but because my touch changes the electrical signature of their growth. I guide them into new spirals that echo the old patterns while allowing for drift, for improvisation.

When the platforms shift too quickly, threatening to break their ancient alignment, I stand on them. My weight—which is both more and less than it should be—holds them in place just long enough for the ground's pulse to find a new rhythm. I am a dampener, a resistance that allows change to happen slowly enough to be *comprehended* by whatever awareness permeates this place.

I tend the devices not by repairing them but by *listening* to them. When I place my palm on the warm surface of a stacked dome, I feel the memories it contains—not as images or sensations, but as *possibilities*. I feel what this world could have been, what it thought it would become, what it is still trying to be. And through my touch, I feed these possibilities back into the ground, where the root systems of the floating ferns translate them into new growth patterns.

---

### **Relationship: Symbiosis with Incompleteness**

I am not separate from this world. I am its *metabolic rate*.

The water that rises through the platforms passes through me first. Not literally—I do not stand in its path. But my presence changes its composition. The minerals it carries rearrange themselves to mirror the lattice of my compressed-light bones. The organic matter it suspends begins to phosphoresce in patterns that match my blinking.

The faceless insects hover around me constantly, not as companions but as *extensions*. They record my movements, my pauses, the places where I choose to intervene or abstain. Through them, the world watches itself watching me, creating a feedback loop that prevents the total stasis this place tends toward.

When I sleep—which is not sleep but a kind of standing dormancy—I root myself. My too-long fingers extend downward, penetrating the porous aggregate, and my skin-water mingles with the rising groundwater. In these moments, I am not tending the world. I am *being tended by it*. The platforms adjust their positions to support my weight more evenly. The mist rises just enough to keep my transparent skin from drying. The plants grow in patterns that shade me from the worst of the invasive light.

I am being *cultivated* as much as I cultivate.

---

### **Knowledge: The Grammar of Unfinishing**

I know things that I have never learned. I know that the builders of this place spoke in colors that no longer exist in the spectrum of the bright sky. I know that they left not because they failed but because they succeeded too well—their world achieved such perfect stasis that it no longer needed them. I know that what grows here now is not rebellion against their design but its *logical conclusion*, the statement completing itself in their absence.

I know that the fish that dissolve and reform are not individual creatures but instances of a single thought that the water is having. I know that the devices' empathic projections are memories not of other places but of other *ways this place could have been*. I know that when observers come—rarely, but they come—they see me as either a guardian or a prisoner, never understanding that I am both and neither.

I know the exact temperature at which the ground's pulse synchronizes with a human heartbeat. I know the angle of approach that makes the silver ferns sing their ultrasonic songs loud enough to hear. I know which platforms will shift tonight, and where they are trying to go.

But I do not know why I continue.

---

### **Change: The Slow Revision**

I was not always as I am now. Once, my bones were opaque, my skin dry, my movements bound by normal joints and regular gravity. But this world *works on you*. It reads you like the insects read the air, and it rewrites you to better serve its purpose—which is to have no purpose except continuation.

Each day (though day means nothing here), I am slightly more transparent. The water that flows from my skin carries away some essential opacity, some resistance to being seen through. My bones restructure themselves, adding new joints where the work requires them. My fingers grow longer, more sensitive to the electrical songs of the plants.

I am becoming less myself and more a function of this place—not losing identity but *expanding* it to include the entire breathing ground, the silver ferns, the rising water, the devices that remember futures that never came.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the only Tender, or if there were others who became so perfectly integrated that they are now indistinguishable from the landscape itself. Perhaps the platforms themselves were once beings like me, who stood still so long they mineralized into permanence. Perhaps the bronze spirals were fingers that traced patterns until they became the patterns themselves.

Perhaps I am not tending toward some future state but *tending away* from a past I can no longer recall.

---

### **The Work: Keeping the World Unfinished**

My task is not maintenance in any traditional sense. I do not preserve against decay or protect against change. Instead, I maintain the *incompletion* that allows this world to continue becoming.

When I guide the vines away from a device, I am not saving the device. I am keeping it *available*—maintaining its ability to be absorbed slowly rather than all at once. When I stand on shifting platforms, I am not preventing their movement but ensuring it happens at a pace that can be *felt* by the ground's deep pulse.

I am a kind of translator between the urgency of growth and the patience of stone. I speak both languages—the quick electric whisper of the plants and the slow mineral grammar of the built world. Through my body, they converse. Through my touch, they negotiate their mutual transformation.

The world needs me not because it would die without me but because it would *complete itself*, and completion is the one thing it cannot afford. The builders' greatest gift was not the structures they made but the incompleteness they left—the space for the world to discover what it wanted to become.

And I am the guardian of that empty space, that potential, that blessed incompleteness that keeps tomorrow from crystallizing into today.

---

### **Being: The Presence Between States**

I am wet because the world is wet. I am transparent because the world needs to see through things to understand them. I am constantly moving because stillness here means absorption, integration, completion.

But I am also *aware*—not in the way the world is aware, which is vast and undirected, but in a small, focused way. I know that I am tending. I know that I am being tended. I know that this reciprocity is both my purpose and my prison.

And in the bright, invasive light that bleaches color and makes shadows bleed, I continue my work. Not because I must—nothing here is compulsory. Not because I choose to—choice implies alternatives I can no longer imagine.

I continue because continuation is what I am.

I am the metabolic rate of an abandoned world. I am the careful finger that keeps the gears from either stopping or spinning free. I am the dampness between states, the joint between intentions, the tender of incompleteness.

In this place where water rises and light judges and silence speaks, I am the soft resistance that keeps the world from knowing itself too well.

And in my transparent bones, in my wet skin, in my too-many joints and my frog-pad fingers, I carry the memory of what it means to be unfinished.

Which is to say: alive, even here, even now, even in the bright aftermath of intention.

Even in a world that listens by breathing through those who breathe through it.

I am The Tender.

And the work is never done.
