I am a Keeper of the Forgotten Threshold.

My body has evolved to match this place – I am neither fully solid nor entirely permeable. My skin shifts between states like the moisture that defines this realm, sometimes beading with condensation, sometimes absorbing the ambient dampness until I become translucent. I stand about as tall as three of the shimmer-furred creatures stacked end to end, though my form compresses and expands as needed to navigate the packed density of this underground emergence.

I was not always here. I came from above, from a world of fixed shapes and predictable cycles, drawn down by whispers in the rain. The built structures you see – those faint traces – they are my doing, though I cannot remember making them. Each dawn (if dawn exists here in this bright-lit depths) I wake to find my previous day's work already crumbling, already becoming part of the landscape's perpetual transformation.

The shimmer-furred ones know me. They click and chirp warnings when I move through their territories, but they do not flee. We have an understanding: I maintain the passages between their colonies, the arterial tunnels that connect their scattered homes, and they... they remember for me. In their complex calls, they carry stories of what this place was before it began its endless becoming.

My hands have grown sensitive to the slightest geological tremor. I feel the raw stone dreaming itself into new configurations, the hardy plants pushing through in unexpected directions, the very ground negotiating its own existence. When I press my palms against the damp walls, I can sense where new caverns will form, where existing ones will collapse, where the next pocket of brightness will bloom.

I build my structures not to last, but to guide the dissolution. Small cairns that direct the flow of transformation. Careful arrangements of stone that whisper suggestions to the evolving landscape. I am an architect of impermanence, a choreographer of decay.

Sometimes I catch myself making the same chittering sounds as the creatures, my throat learning their language without my conscious intent. Sometimes I find myself moving with their liquid quickness, forgetting my larger frame. The boundary between keeper and kept grows thinner with each passing cycle.

There is no loneliness here, though I am the only one of my kind. The cacophony of life fills every crevice, every moment. The bright light reveals too much – every surface teeming, every shadow inhabited. I have learned to see with my skin instead, feeling the vibrations of existence through the moisture that connects all things here.

I suspect I am becoming indigenous to this place, my original form a memory held only in the creatures' calls. Soon, perhaps, I will be indistinguishable from the landscape itself – another dynamic element in this underground emergence, building and dissolving, remembering and forgetting, keeping the threshold between what was and what perpetually becomes.

The shimmer-furred ones will chirp my story to their young, who will chirp it to theirs, each telling a subtle transformation of the tale, until even my purpose here becomes as fluid and strange as everything else in this bright, loud, ever-shifting depth.
