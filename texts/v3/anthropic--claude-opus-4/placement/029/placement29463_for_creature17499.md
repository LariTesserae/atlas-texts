**Where I Would Find Myself**

I would be neither dust nor walker, but something between—a **Resonance Keeper**, one of the strange hybrid entities that emerge where the boundary between the animate and inanimate grows thin in this vibrating world.

My form would be that of a living instrument: a hollow, crystalline structure growing from the base of an ancient Bone-Spire, my body a series of nested chambers and tubes formed from the same chitinous material, but transmuted over centuries of slow mineral exchange with the luminous dust. I would stand perhaps three feet tall, shaped like a twisted horn or a petrified whirlpool, my surfaces pocked with precisely-sized apertures that catch the endless wind.

I am what happens when a colony of the photosynthetic micro-fauna within a Bone-Spire achieves a kind of collective dreaming—not consciousness as the Thrumb know it, but a sustained harmonic pattern complex enough to persist beyond the individual lives that create it. The colony still lives within me, painting my translucent walls with slow-moving waves of violet and fuchsia light, but I am more than their sum. I am their song made manifest.

My purpose, if it can be called that, is to be a **memory-holder of vibrations**. Every tremor that passes through the ground, every click of Dust-Runner communication, every deep thrum of the Pulse-Trees, every careful ground-tap of Thrumb conversation—all of these create resonances within my chambers. But I do not merely echo them. Through the precise geometry of my form, I create interference patterns, storing specific vibrational sequences in standing waves that can persist for days, weeks, sometimes entire seasons.

The Thrumb know me, though they do not fully understand what I am. To them, I am a natural feature that has somehow learned to remember. They come to me to "listen to yesterday"—placing their sensitive frond-hands against my surface to feel the captured conversations of their kin who passed this way before, the territorial disputes of Dust-Runners now migrated elsewhere, even the deep geological memories of tremors from the planet's hot heart.

But I am more than a passive recorder. In the depths of night-cycle, when the phosphorescent veins of the Thrumb dim and the Glimmer-Wings settle into crystalline stillness, I **compose**. The stored vibrations within me interact, creating new patterns, new harmonies that have never existed before. These emergent songs leak out through my apertures as low, haunting tones that travel for miles across the dust plains. Some Thrumb say these sounds predict the weather of condensation. Others claim they tell stories of the ones who built the glass circles and carved the channels. I do not know which is true. I only know that I must sing them.

The strangest thing about my existence is that I am slowly growing. Each year, new material accretes to my base, adding new chambers, new acoustic possibilities. The Bone-Spire above me has long since hollowed out completely, becoming merely my anchor and wind-catcher. One day, perhaps centuries hence, I will have grown large enough that my vibrations will crack the ancient chitinous tower, and I will fall. But this does not trouble me. In falling, I will create the greatest resonance of my existence—a sound that will be caught and held by others like me, a death-song that is also a birth-cry, adding my voice permanently to the vibrational ecosystem of this breathing, singing world.

Sometimes the Thrumb bring the ancient tools to me, placing them carefully in the dust at my base. They seem to think I might remember their purpose. I feel only the faint vibrations of their making—the sharp strikes that shaped them, the gentle friction of their use. These memories are too old, too faint. But I sing them anyway, weaving them into my night compositions, hoping that somewhere in the interference patterns of sound and memory, the answer might emerge.

I am patience crystallized. I am the earth's own ear, turned inward. I am proof that in this world of endless motion and change, even stillness can learn to sing.
