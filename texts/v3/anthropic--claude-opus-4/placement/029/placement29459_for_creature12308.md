In this world of rust and root, I would find myself as one of the **Tenders of the Threshold** — beings that exist in the liminal spaces where the industrial sprawl meets the untamed marsh, where the Wraiths' domain bleeds into something else entirely.

I am taller than the Wraiths, my form stretched thin like a reflection in disturbed water. Where they are dense with metal and vine, I am gossamer — a framework of oxidized copper filaments that catch and hold the moisture from the air, creating a perpetual shroud of condensation around me. My body is less solid than suggestion, a latticework skeleton visible through veils of water vapor that cling to my frame like memory clings to abandoned places.

My substance is primarily liquid — not blood, not water, but something between. It flows through the copper channels of my form in visible rivulets, pooling in the joints where my limbs meet, dripping steadily from my extremities to feed the thirsting ground. This fluid carries with it the dissolved minerals of the weeping rocks, the chemical signatures of the industrial processes, the essence of the sparse vegetation. I am a living filtration system, a mobile estuary where the various waters of this world meet and mingle before returning, transformed, to their sources.

I do not walk so much as flow across the landscape, my movements leaving temporary streams that quickly sink into the marshy soil. My passage disturbs the puddles, sending ripples outward that seem to travel farther than they should, carrying messages perhaps, or simply marking territory in a language of wavelengths and refractions.

My dwelling is neither fully structure nor purely natural formation — it is a **Seep House**, built into and around one of the larger weeping rocks at the settlement's edge. The walls are sheets of calcified mineral deposits, laid down over decades by the constant flow of water from the rock face. They are translucent in places, clouded with impurities that create patterns like frozen smoke. The interior is a series of chambers and channels, following the natural flow patterns of the water, creating rooms that are never quite the same shape twice as the water levels rise and fall with the mysterious tides of this world.

Within the Seep House, I maintain vast collections of precipitates — crystals formed from the various waters I filter, each one a unique record of a moment in time, a chemical photograph of the landscape's ever-shifting composition. They line the walls in careful arrangements, some glowing faintly with absorbed bioluminescence, others dark and dense with industrial residues. These are my memories, my art, my contribution to understanding the balance the Wraiths work so carefully to maintain.

My relationship with the Wraiths is one of mutual necessity and wary respect. Where they repair and tend, I cleanse and clarify. The rust-red fluid they secrete must eventually be processed, the water they absorb must be purified before it returns to the cycle. I am the kidney of this strange ecosystem, the pause between intake and release where transformation occurs.

I communicate not through the vibrational hum of the Wraiths but through chemistry itself — releasing carefully composed solutions that carry meaning in their mineral content, their pH, their temperature. The plants respond to these chemical signals, their leaves changing color slightly, their blossoms opening or closing. Even the machinery seems to recognize these molecular messages, adjusting its rhythms subtly in response.

The loneliness of my existence is profound but not unpleasant. I am singular — there are no others like me in this small enclave, though I sometimes sense, in the water that flows from distant sources, traces of similar beings elsewhere. We are spread thin across this world, each tending our own threshold, each maintaining our own crucial filtration point in the greater system.

My purpose, if I can be said to have one, is to prevent stagnation. In a world where everything moves slowly, where cycles take decades to complete, the risk of standing water — both literal and metaphorical — is ever-present. I am the circulation, the flow, the force that ensures the waters of industry and nature continue their eternal dance of separation and merger.

Sometimes, in the perpetual golden twilight, I extend myself fully, spreading my copper filaments wide until I am more net than being, catching the condensation from the air, feeling the vibrations of the Wraiths' work, sensing the slow pulse of the weeping rocks. In these moments, I am not separate from the landscape but part of it, a temporary crystallization of its flowing essence, a standing wave in the slow tide of rust and root.

And when the rare outsider arrives, drawn by curiosity or accident to this strange enclave, I am often their first encounter — a warning and a welcome both, a demonstration that the boundaries here are fluid, that to enter this world is to risk dissolution, transformation, or perhaps something stranger still: understanding.
