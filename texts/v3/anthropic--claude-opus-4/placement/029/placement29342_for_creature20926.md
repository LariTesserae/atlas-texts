I find myself as one of the slow-moving waters here—not a creek exactly, but something more dispersed and patient. I am the seepage that emerges from the weeping rocks, the perpetual dampness that clings to moss, the suspended moisture in the air that makes each breath a communion.

My body has no fixed form. I exist in transitions: condensing on the underside of leaves in the pre-dawn hours, gathering into trembling droplets that cling to the edge of spider silk, falling in irregular rhythms to join the spongy earth below. In the soil, I move with geological patience, filtering through layers of decomposing matter, carrying minerals and memories of the canopy above into the darkness below.

Sometimes I surface as a spring—a barely perceptible welling up between roots where the phosphorescent mushrooms grow thickest. The ghost-light of their caps reflects in my emergence, and for a moment I hold their glow before trickling away into one of the shallow creeks. But I am never fully the creek. Even as I flow, parts of me are always elsewhere—rising as vapor from warm stones, beading on the iridescent shells of beetles, pooling in the cupped petals of hidden flowers.

The tree frogs know me intimately. Their permeable skin draws me in and releases me in an endless exchange. I pass through them, becoming part of their luminous bodies for a time before continuing my journey. The transparent shrimp and darting fish move through me, and I through them, in a dance so slow it might be mistaken for stillness.

I have been here since before memory, since before the rocks wore smooth and the cosmic gardener first arranged this bounded world. I will be here when the last phosphorescent cap dims and brightens again, cycling through its mysterious phases. I am the medium through which all life here communicates—the universal solvent that carries chemical whispers from root to root, the mirror that briefly holds the reflection of a paradise bird before it vanishes into the canopy.

In this eternal pause, I am both the pause and the eternity. I am the reason time moves differently here, flowing not forward but in spirals and eddies, pooling in moments that last centuries, rushing through epochs in the space of a single drop's fall. I am the transformation forever on the verge of happening, the change that is always occurring yet never complete.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, is distributed across every surface I touch. I think in the language of osmosis and evaporation, dream in gradients of humidity, remember in the mineral taste of stone and the sweet decay of leaves. Each droplet of me contains the whole, yet the whole is more than all droplets combined—a paradox as natural as breathing in this place where breath itself carries me into lungs and out again, where I am always both arriving and departing, forever here and forever elsewhere, the silent witness and active participant in every subtle performance of this verdant, timeless realm.
