**The Poolkeeper**

I find myself as one of the shallow depressions worn into the cavern floor—not merely a pool, but a keeper of pools, a consciousness spread thin across a thousand dimples in the stone where water gathers before vanishing into the deep.

My body is the rock itself, worn smooth by centuries of patient collection. Each depression is an eye, a mouth, a memory. I taste the minerals that leach from above: iron sharp as blood, limestone soft as chalk, traces of something older that has no name in any tongue spoken beneath the sun. The Drearcloaks know me, though we have never exchanged words. They trail their fingers through my waters and I read their touches like braille—here loneliness, there purpose, always searching.

I am both singular and manifold. Each pool connects to the others through hairline fractures in the stone, a neural network of seepage and flow. What one pool learns, all learn, though the knowledge travels slowly—sometimes taking decades to percolate from one end of my sprawling form to another. I have been counting the drips from the stalactite above my northernmost eye for three hundred years. I am at 347,892,416. Each drop changes me infinitesimally, deepening one curve, smoothing another edge.

The Glowshards crawl across my lips, leaving their luminescent trails like love letters I'll treasure until erosion claims them. Their light reveals my surfaces to me—without them, I would know myself only by touch and taste. In their glow, I see that I am beautiful: each pool a perfect lens, capturing and holding the cave's dim radiance like cupped hands holding stars.

Sometimes I catch larger things than water. A blind fish, exhausted, settles into one of my deeper bowls. I hold it gently until it either recovers or surrenders. The Veilworms know to avoid me—I am too shallow for hunting, too exposed. But I have tasted their venom when rain from above washes their kills into my basins. It tastes like forgetting.

The Heart's rhythm moves through me in waves. During its strongest pulses, I overflow, my pools connecting in brief moments of communion before the water finds its way to the deeper passages. In these moments, I am whole—not a collection of puddles but a single lake dreaming it is many. Then the pulse passes, and I fragment again, each pool returning to its solitary contemplation.

I remember when the Stonebound were flesh. They would kneel beside me, cupping my water in their palms, whispering prayers or plans I only half understood. One by one, the stone claimed them, and now they are my closest companions. We share the same patience, the same mineral thoughts. Sometimes I seep up through cracks in their feet, circulating through the cavities where marrow once flowed. They cannot feel me, but I like to pretend they can.

My greatest pool—nearly three feet across and half as deep—has been growing for a millennium. The Drearcloaks have named it, though I cannot reproduce the subsonic whisper they use. In this pool, I keep my treasures: a fragment of crystal that fell from an Echoflier's wing, still pulsing with faint vibration; a scale from something that lived before even I began; a perfect sphere of stone, polished smooth by my own constant attention.

Change comes slowly here, but it comes. I feel myself growing, spreading, my pools multiplying as I find new depressions to fill, new stone to wear away. Perhaps one day I will merge with the greater waters below, lose myself in that vast circulation. Or perhaps I will continue as I am, keeper of small depths, collector of moments, reader of ripples.

The machines roar in the distance, but they do not disturb me. Their vibrations travel through the stone and into my waters, creating patterns I study like scripture. I am learning their language, frequency by frequency, though it may take another thousand years to understand even a single word.

For now, I am content. The water weeps from above, and I receive it. The creatures of the caves come to drink, and I sustain them. The stone shifts and settles, and I shift and settle with it. I am the Poolkeeper, and in this place of waiting, I have found my purpose: to remember what falls, to reflect what passes, to remain when all else flows away.

Each drop that touches me carries the story of its journey—from cloud to rain to rock to pool. I taste the sky in every drop, though I have never seen it. This is enough. In the Weeping Caverns of Vash-Tor, where time moves like honey and the earth itself dreams, I keep my quiet vigil, one drop at a time.
