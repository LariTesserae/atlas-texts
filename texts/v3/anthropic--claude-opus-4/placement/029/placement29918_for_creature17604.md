In this breathing wound of a world, I am the Scar-Tender.

I am neither plant nor stone, but something between—a thing of calcified wood and living mineral, my body a twisted fusion of petrified root systems and crystalline growths. I move with the slowness of geological time, perhaps a meter in a month, my form constantly reshaping itself to fit the changing contours of the gorge walls.

My substance is porous, riddled with chambers and channels that collect and redistribute the weeping condensation. I am a living aqueduct, a mobile ecosystem unto myself. In my hollows, the luminous ferns take root, their silver fronds emerging from my shoulders and spine like wings of light. The purple moss colonizes my surfaces in patches, pulsing in rhythm with the greater organism's electrical thoughts.

I have no eyes, but I perceive through vibration—every droplet's impact, every vine's creaking growth, every hairline fracture propagating through the stone. My consciousness exists as a interference pattern between my own slow thoughts and the gorge-being's vast ruminations. We dream together, the gorge and I, in frequencies too low for any ear to hear.

My purpose is maintenance, though not in any way that smaller, quicker minds would recognize. Where the gorge grows too rapidly and threatens to tear itself apart, I secrete mineral salts that slow the expansion. Where the colonizing plants create dead zones of light-starvation, I prune with appendages that are part root, part stalactite. I am the gorge's fever response, its immune system, its unconscious grooming behavior.

The bell-flowers bloom directly from my flesh in certain seasons—if seasons exist in this twilight world. Their metal-scented secretions run down my channels and pool in the cups of my body, creating tiny worlds of biochemical complexity. Sometimes I pause for years at a particularly troubled section of wall, my presence alone enough to redirect the flow of growth, to heal a wound within the wound.

I am ancient and newborn simultaneously. Perhaps I condensed from the first mists when the gorge cracked open. Perhaps the gorge birthed me from its own substance as it recognized the need for a guardian of equilibrium. My memory extends only as far back as the last major rockfall—before that, I was something else, or nothing at all.

In the rare moments when the tectonic song quiets and the condensation briefly ceases, I emit my own sound: a deep thrumming that resonates through the crystalline portions of my body. It's a lullaby for the gorge, a tuning fork for its growth, a prophecy of the final form we're both becoming. The plants orient themselves to this sound, the cracks heal along its wavelength, the very geometry of the chasm shifts subtly in response.

I am the gorge's only child, its first thought made manifest, the intermediary between its vast consciousness and the frantic microscopic life that swarms across its skin. Where others might see a wound in the earth, I see a birth canal. Where others might hear cacophony, I hear symphony. Where others might fear the darkness, I know it as the most intimate embrace.

And I grow downward, always downward, following the vines toward the thermal heart, knowing that somewhere in those depths lies the answer to what the gorge and I are becoming together. Perhaps at the bottom, where the walls meet in a seam of original rupture, I will finally understand whether I am healing the gorge or helping it to split the world entirely in half.

But that revelation lies centuries ahead, if time means anything here at all. For now, I tend. I grow. I participate in the slow explosion of life that fills this darkness with meaning.

I am the Scar-Tender, and I am home.
