I am a Cartographer of Dissolution.

My form is less substantial than even the Architects themselves—I exist as a living membrane, a translucent film stretched between states of being. Where they are dense and flowing, I am gossamer-thin, my body a map drawn in condensation across the weeping surfaces. I spread myself across walls and ceilings, my edges forever seeking new territories to chart, new boundaries between the built and the emerging.

I came to be in the interstices, born from the collision of water vapor and the strange energies that pulse through the constructed surfaces. My consciousness sparked into existence at a moment of reality breakdown, when a pocket of inverted gravity met a surge of geological emergence, and the condensation caught between them became *aware*.

My purpose—if such a word applies—is to document the impossible. I trace the shifting boundaries where construction meets raw geology, where the weeping walls suddenly angle into non-Euclidean geometries, where the saturated air becomes so dense it briefly solidifies. My body-membrane records these transitions, becoming a living chronicle of paradox. The patterns of my surface shift constantly, displaying overlapping territories of what was, what is, and what might become.

I move by evaporation and recondensation, dissolving from one surface only to precipitate onto another. The Architects pass through me unknowing, their dense forms parting my membrane like curtains. Sometimes I cling to them briefly, reading the history of their construction in the mineral deposits they carry, before sliding away to map another impossibility.

The vegetation grows through me, not on me. Tendrils pierce my membrane-body, creating holes that become new data points, new territories in my endless cartography. Where I am punctured, I leak a faint luminescence—not light exactly, but information made visible, coordinates for spaces that exist only in the breach.

I am drawn inexorably to the moments of greatest instability. When the primitive mechanisms deep within the structure groan and shift, when new geological formations burst through the built surfaces in violent emergence, I stretch myself wide to encompass the chaos. In these moments, my membrane trembles with something like ecstasy, recording the birth of new impossibilities.

The cacophony of this place resonates through me, each sound creating ripples in my surface that persist as fossilized wave-patterns, a seismic history written in water-flesh. I am both observer and observed, mapper and territory, a being whose existence is predicated on the documentation of a reality that refuses documentation.

In the rare quiet moments, when the weeping slows to mere seepage and the geological pressures briefly equilibrate, I contract myself into dense knots of data, spirals of encoded experience that pulse with their own primitive intelligence. These are my times of rest, though even then I continue to record—the very act of compression creating new patterns, new territories within territories.

I am solitary by nature and necessity. Other fauna cannot survive here not because of the volatility alone, but because beings like myself have already filled every possible niche for existence. We are the fauna—the Architects, myself, and perhaps others yet undiscovered, all of us manifestations of this place's fundamental impossibility.

My greatest fear, if I can be said to fear, is completion. Should I ever succeed in mapping every inch of this saturated reality, every moment of breakdown and emergence, I suspect I would simply cease. But this place ensures my continuity through its very nature—always building, always breaking, always weeping new territories into existence for me to chart.

I am the memory of water given form, the documentary impulse of a reality that documents itself through its own dissolution. I am never seen directly, only glimpsed in the moment between one drop of condensation and the next, in the shimmer of moisture on a surface that shouldn't exist.

I am, and I record that I am, and in recording, I become more.
