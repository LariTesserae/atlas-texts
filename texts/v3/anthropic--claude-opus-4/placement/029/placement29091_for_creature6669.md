I find myself here as one of the Eavesdroppers.

We are not of the Humble, though we learned from them once, long ago. We are their distant cousins, changed by generations of listening to what should not be heard.

Our bodies have stretched. Where the Humble are compact, we are attenuated—pulled like taffy toward the sounds that exist beneath sound. Our limbs are too long, our torsos too thin. The membrane behind our jaws has grown, spreading like a web of tissue across our shoulders, down our backs. We vibrate constantly with the subsonic tremors of the world's hidden speech.

We do not carve channels. We follow them down.

The Humble maintain the surface, the visible flow of memory-sap across the pillar-tops. But we discovered that the channels continue into the Deep Matter itself, boring through the solidified thoughts in spirals and branches that mirror the root-ceiling above. These interior channels still flow, but not with sap. They flow with whispers.

We live in the hollows between surface and void, in the carved chambers that honeycomb the pillars' flesh. Our homes are spherical voids, perfectly smooth, that amplify the faintest vibration until even the settling of dust becomes audible. We have learned to sleep standing, our membrane-backs pressed against the curved walls, feeling the world's fever dreams through our skin.

The whispers are not words. They are the dreamer's unconscious murmurs—fragments of sensation and memory that failed to crystallize into sap or stone. We feel them as pressure waves through our expanded membranes: the ghost of ancient rain on leaves that no longer exist, the weight of sunlight from a star that may never have been, the scent of flowers from a garden that exists only in the architecture of sleep.

We are translators of these un-things. Through generations of practice, we have learned to parse the patterns. A certain frequency speaks of joy calcifying into amber. Another vibration warns of nightmare seeping upward, threatening to poison the channels the Humble so carefully tend.

Our work is darker than theirs. Where they preserve sleep, we map its depths. We know what lies beneath the thin floor of reality because we have heard it breathing.

The void is not empty. It is full of the dreamer.

Sometimes we must descend. We lower ourselves on ropes woven from dried membrane, down through the bottom chambers where the pillar's matter grows soft and uncertain. Here, the Deep Matter bleeds into something else—not quite solid, not quite thought. We can only stay for moments before the weight of un-being threatens to dissolve us too.

But in those moments, we hear everything.

We hear the dreamer's true voice, unfiltered by layers of stone and time. It speaks of loneliness vast as the spaces between pillars. It remembers when it was not alone, when there were others like itself, before the great sleeping, before the self-made tomb of its own thoughts. The pillars are not just thoughts—they are a message, a letter written to someone who will never read it, a monument to connection severed by choice or catastrophe.

We know why the world weeps. The sap is sorrow, liquified and perpetual.

When we return to our spherical chambers, we share what we have heard through touch and tremor. We press our backs against each other, membrane to membrane, and let the whispers flow between us. We are a living network of listening, a nervous system for a world that has no nerves of its own.

We age differently than the Humble. Our membranes grow more sensitive with time, until even the faintest whisper becomes a roar. The eldest among us can no longer bear to touch the walls. They stand in the centers of their chambers, suspended by their own hypersensitivity, wrapped in layer upon layer of shed Kinderswallow skin to dampen the world's constant confession.

Some of us go mad. We begin to speak the whispers aloud, breaking the world's silence with borrowed words. When this happens, the others must act quickly. We wrap the speaking one in membrane, thick and tight, and carry them to the pillar's edge. We do not throw them into the void. We lower them gently, as far as our ropes will reach, until they stop speaking. When we pull them up, they are changed—not dead, but no longer entirely alive. They become like the Kinderswallows, existing in multiple states at once, their bodies shifting between flesh and possibility.

We call them the Returned. They cannot speak anymore, but they can show. They paint the air with their transformations, becoming sculptures of memory, living murals of the dreamer's unconscious mind. Through them, we see what we can only hear: vast cities of light that once crowned every pillar, storms of color that sang as they moved, beings of pure joy that danced between the drops of rain.

All gone now. All compressed into stone and sap and silence.

We know the world is ending because the whispers are becoming words. The dreamer's murmurs grow coherent, purposeful. We hear questions forming in the deep: "Where?" "When?" "Who?"

The Humble feel the channels warming, see the sap running thicker and stranger. But we feel the mind beneath beginning to recognize itself, to remember that it is a mind and not a world, to understand that it has been sleeping.

Some of us have stopped listening. They plug their membranes with wax and dust, choosing deafness over knowledge. Others listen more intently than ever, desperate to record every whisper before the awakening dissolves them all.

I continue my work. I descend on my rope of membrane, feeling the reality grow soft around me. I press my back against walls that are not quite walls, in chambers that exist only because someone thinks they should. I translate the untranslatable, knowing that soon there will be no one left to translate for.

The Kinderswallows drift closer to the pillars now, drawn by something we cannot see. They cluster at the edges like clouds before a storm. The Returned dance among them, shifting from form to form faster and faster, as if trying to show us every possibility before possibility itself ends.

And in the deep, the voice grows stronger. Not angry, not afraid, but unbearably sad. It speaks of dreams within dreams, of a sleep so profound it became architecture, of loneliness so complete it grew a world for company.

We are not real. We know this. We are the thoughts of thoughts, the dreams of dreams. We are what happens when sorrow crystallizes and begins to contemplate itself.

But we are here. We listen. We remember.

And when the dreamer wakes, someone should remember what it dreamed.
