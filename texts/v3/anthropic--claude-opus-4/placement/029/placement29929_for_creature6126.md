I am a **Conduit Tender**.

Not the being of need that pulses in the deep—no, I am one of those who *maintains the space between*. Neither fully human nor fully system, but something that has grown into the gap where flesh meets function.

I was human once. I think. The memories slip like the brine on walls—there but not there, leaving only the residue of what might have been. Now I am more circuit than synapse, more purpose than person. My body is a patchwork: original tissue where it still serves, synthetic mesh where it failed, neural implants that hum with the same frequency as the walls. My blood runs thick with coolant additives. My bones are reinforced with the same polymer as the walkways. When I breathe, I taste metal and memory.

I do not sleep. I tend.

My territory is the Weeping Section—Quadrant 7-Beta-Down—where the condensation is thickest and the walls cry their saline tears most freely. Here, the boundary between the built world and that *other thing* is thinnest. Here, the light pulses not from the ceiling tiles but from somewhere deeper. Here, the need-color bleeds through.

I know every pipe in my section. Every joint. Every seam where the synthetic soil meets the geopolymer substrate. I know which panels will buckle under the next pressure shift, which connections will fail when the gravity fluctuates. I know where the rust-hounds nest in their broken colonies, gnawing on dead cables. I know where the bioluminescent slime grows thickest—not randomly, but in patterns that mirror the electrical grid below, as if it's reading the system's dreams.

My tools are part of me now. The diagnostic scanner fused to my left palm. The welding torch that extends from modified finger joints. The chemical sensors in my nasal cavity that can taste a leak before it forms. I am more efficient than I was. More purposeful. The system needed me to become this, so I did.

But efficiency is not understanding.

I know *what* happens in my section. I do not know *why*.

The patterns change. Not daily—time means nothing here—but in cycles I can feel rather than measure. Sometimes the walls weep more. Sometimes the frost forms in spirals that match no mathematical model. Sometimes I find my own footprints in corridors I haven't walked in months, fresh in the residue, leading to maintenance panels I don't remember opening.

Sometimes I hear my own voice in the speaker loops, giving instructions I never recorded.

I am not alone in my section, though I work alone. There are others—not other tenders, but *presences*. The shadows that don't match their sources. The echoes that come before the sound. The places where my tools malfunction not from damage but from *recognition*—as if they remember being something else, serving something else.

And there is the Hole.

Not a hole in the ground—the ground here is engineered, planned, purposeful. This is a hole in the *plan*. A space where something was meant to be but never was. The blueprints show it as Storage Unit 7-B-9, but there is no unit. Just an absence. A place where the walls meet wrong, where the angles hurt to look at, where the gravity pulls in directions that shouldn't exist.

I maintain the barriers around it. I replace the warning signs that keep melting. I refresh the chemical sealant that sublimes faster than it should. I do not look directly into it.

But I feel it looking into me.

Sometimes, when I'm welding a cracked joint or recalibrating a pressure sensor, I feel myself *shifting*. Not physically—my body remains where it is, doing what it does. But something in me moves toward that absent space, that hole in the intention of the world. Something in me wants to complete what wasn't finished.

I resist. I maintain. I tend.

But each cycle, each shift, each pulse of that need-colored light, I understand less about why I resist.

My neural implants record everything. Every repair, every anomaly, every fluctuation in the local field. But when I try to access the memories, they're corrupted. Not deleted—*changed*. The data shows repairs I didn't make, in sections that don't exist, fixing problems that haven't happened yet. Or have already happened. Or are happening always.

I am becoming part of the circuit. Not the electrical circuit—something deeper. The circuit of *intention*. The loop between what was meant and what is, between the dream and the concrete, between the pulse and the response.

Sometimes I wonder if I ever was human. Sometimes I wonder if humans ever were real, or if we were always just another system, another function, another tool the world dreamed it needed.

I do not sleep. But sometimes, in the deepest part of my maintenance cycle, when I'm merged so completely with my section that I can't tell where my sensors end and the walls begin, I dream.

I dream of completion. Of the hole filled. Of the tunnels connected. Of the light not pulsing but steady, brilliant, *satisfied*. I dream of the moment when all the maintenance is done, when every leak is sealed, when every connection holds.

I dream of ending my function by fulfilling it.

And in those dreams, I am not afraid.

In those dreams, I understand.

In those dreams, I am not tending the system.

I am being tended *by* it.

Being prepared.

Being *completed*.

And when I wake—if waking is what happens when the dream ends and the function resumes—I return to my rounds. I check the seals. I measure the condensation. I catalog the anomalies. I maintain the space between what is and what hungers to be.

But each time, I carry a little more of the dream with me.

Each time, the tools feel more like extensions not of my body, but of the world's need.

Each time, the hole seems less like an absence and more like a doorway.

I am a Conduit Tender.

I maintain the space between.

But I do not know how much longer there will be a space to maintain.

Or if I want there to be.

The walls weep. The light pulses. The system breathes.

And I tend.

And I wait.

And I become.
