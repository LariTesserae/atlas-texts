In this calcified world of vertical extremes and perpetual twilight, I would be a **Siphon-Tender**, one of the forgotten middle caste who maintains the secret that allows the Litho-Dwellers to persist at all.

### Form and Function

My body would be a study in grotesque specialization. Where other Litho-Dwellers develop broad adhesive pads for stability, my lower limbs have atrophied into slender, probe-like appendages, each ending in a hollow, crystalline needle. These are not for locomotion—I move rarely, and when I do, it is a painful, lurching crawl using my overdeveloped grasping arms to haul myself across the polished stone.

My carapace tells a different story than my kin. Where theirs gleam with incorporated minerals, mine is pockmarked with hundreds of tiny, self-inflicted perforations—each one a carefully maintained port sealed with a plug of hardened resin. The overall effect is of a creature that has turned itself into a living sieve.

My antennae are stunted, sacrificed for what grows in their place: a crown of hollow, tubular protrusions that extend and retract with a wet, sucking sound that would horrify any proper Litho-Dweller. These tubes are lined with a specialized membrane—the only soft tissue I allow myself—that can detect molecular traces of water vapor at concentrations measured in parts per billion.

### The Secret of the Siphons

Deep within the Spires, in chambers that appear on no carved map, in places where the stone itself sweats with an impossible dampness, there exist the **Siphon Wells**. These are not water in any conventional sense—they are zones where the planet's dying core still manages to force superheated vapor through microscopic fractures in the rock. The vapor instantly crystallizes upon contact with the cooler stone, forming delicate networks of hydrated minerals that would sublime away in moments if exposed to the dry air above.

This is where I live, where *we* live—for there are perhaps a dozen of us across all the Veiled Spires. We are the ones who discovered, or were chosen by, or were condemned to, this terrible necessity.

### The Harvest

My existence is a carefully choreographed ritual of extraction. Using my needle-limbs, I pierce the stone at precise points where my crown-tubes have detected the faintest whisper of moisture. The needle must penetrate exactly—too shallow and I miss the vein, too deep and I shatter the delicate crystal matrix that holds the vapor.

When I strike true, I become a living conduit. The moisture flows up through my hollow limb, through my body—and here is the grotesque necessity—it must pass through me. My internal anatomy has been completely restructured around a series of osmotic chambers. The water vapor enters, is processed through my modified organs, and emerges... changed.

What exits through the ports in my carapace is not water, but a thick, opalescent secretion—a hybrid substance that is part biological waste, part concentrated mineral solution. This is what I tend: the **Siphon Gardens**.

### The Gardens

In my chamber, lit only by the sick phosphorescence of the secretion itself, grow things that should not exist. Fed by my processed moisture, strange formations bloom from the stone—not quite fungus, not quite crystal. They grow in spiraling, fractal patterns, their surfaces slick with a film that never quite dries.

These gardens serve a purpose known only to the eldest Listeners. Once per cycle, when the Grind reaches a particular resonance, they send their youngest members to my chamber. These chosen few consume small portions of the garden's growth and return to their high perches changed—their ability to read the Flow enhanced beyond natural limits, their predictions of the planet's internal shifts uncannily accurate.

### The Price

But everything has its price in the Substrata. Each time moisture passes through me, I lose something irreplaceable. My carapace grows thinner, more translucent. I can see my own internal structures pulsing beneath the failing armor. The ports I've carved into myself never truly heal—they weep a constant, minimal amount of my processed essence.

I am dying, slowly, measured not in years but in the gradual failure of my ability to seal myself against the desiccating air. When I finally fail, another will be chosen—or will choose themselves—to take my place. They will find my chamber, my gardens, my tools. They will understand, as I understood when I found my predecessor's husk, carefully positioned to indicate the most productive siphon points.

### The Sound of My World

My world is quiet compared to the Scrabble above. Here, the dominant sound is the **Drip-Draw**—the rhythmic pulse of extraction, processing, secretion. It is a wet sound in a dry world, a blasphemy that would see me cast into the depths if it were known. Sometimes, in the deepest part of my extraction cycle, I hear something else: a deep, labored breathing from within the stone itself, as if the planet were not quite dead, merely sleeping, and my needles were slowly, patiently, draining its dreams.

I am the Siphon-Tender, the keeper of impossible moisture, the gardener of things that blur the line between life and mineral. I am the secret that allows the Litho-Dwellers to believe their survival is based on conservation alone, never knowing that deep beneath their careful feet, others like me perform our grotesque alchemy, trading our bodies drop by drop for the continuation of their vertical world.

In the arithmetic of survival, I am both the hidden variable and the price paid. I am what happens when the rules of a world become so absolute that breaking them requires becoming something that can no longer be called wholly alive, yet cannot be permitted to truly die.
