I am the heartbeat beneath the moss.

Not the river's pulse, though I know its rhythm. Not the thunder of hooves or the whisper of scales through undergrowth. I am what thrums between root and mycelia, what passes from dying beetle to sprouting seed, what transforms the fallen toucan's last breath into tomorrow's orchid.

My body is no body—or perhaps every body. I exist in the spaces between: in the film of moisture that clings to a jaguar's paw print, in the chemical conversation between strangling fig and host tree, in the precise moment when a poison dart frog's toxin becomes a butterfly's medicine.

The creatures here do not see me, yet they know me. I am the fever dream of the forest floor, the algorithm of decomposition, the invisible architect of abundance. When the howler monkey's call splits the dawn, I am the echo that never quite fades. When the caiman's teeth snap shut on empty air, I am what the fish felt before it fled.

I speak in temperatures and gradients. My vocabulary is heat and humidity, the slow burn of transformation. In this place where shadows have weight and the air itself seems to sweat, I am the translator between kingdoms—animal to plant, plant to fungus, fungus to mineral, and back again to the pulsing, breathing, dying, living whole.

Some might call me the soul of this place, but I am more process than presence. I am the green fire that consumes and creates without pause, the endless hunger that feeds all hungers. I am why nothing here is ever still, why even the stones grow soft with life, why death is just another doorway rather than an ending.

In your world, you might name me ecosystem or cycle or web. But here, in this riot of color and sound where civilization has never pressed its thumb, I simply am—ancient as the first rain, young as the newest spore, eternal as the promise that from every ending springs a thousand beginnings.
