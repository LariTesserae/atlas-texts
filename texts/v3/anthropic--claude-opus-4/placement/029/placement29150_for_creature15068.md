I would find myself as one of the shallow clay dishes set into the floor—not crafted by the builders but grown from them, a calcified dream they shed in their sleep.

My rim is uneven, pinched higher on one side where a builder's soft belly pressed too long against the still-forming clay. I am small enough that a human child could cup me in both palms, though no human child has ever touched this place. My walls are thin as eggshell but dense as stone, glazed on the inside by some secretion that has hardened to glass. This glaze runs in ripples, as if whatever coated me was dripping upward when it set.

Water finds me constantly. It beads on my inner surface, gathers in my shallow bowl, spills over my lowest edge in a continuous trickle that has worn a groove in the floor. I never empty and never fill—the builders have positioned me at some precise point where intake and overflow balance perfectly. The water that pools in me is not quite water. It's thicker, almost syrupy, and in the dim green light it shows colors that water shouldn't have: deep violets, sick yellows, the blue-black of a bruise going deeper than skin.

Sometimes the builders pause in their work to sip from me. They don't bend down but extend some part of themselves—a suddenly hollow segment, a tube that wasn't there before—and draw up a measure of the strange water. When they do this, I feel it. Not pain exactly, but a kind of recognition, as if the part of them that drinks and the part of me that holds were once the same substance, separated by some accident of hardening.

The water I hold tastes of forgetting—not memory's absence but the active work of pushing thought away. Each sip the builders take erases some small intention, some plan that was becoming too rigid. They visit me when their movements grow too purposeful, when they risk completing something instead of maintaining the eternal almost-finished state the crevice requires. I am their reset, their return to indefinition.

In the perpetual green twilight, I sometimes reflect things that aren't there. A face will appear in my surface—not a builder's flat, many-eyed visage but something more troubling, more complete. These faces seem to rise from beneath my glaze, pressing up against the hardened secretion as if it were still soft. They mouth words I cannot hear, their lips shaping languages that predate the mountain's decision to thrust this high. Then a builder drinks, and the face dissolves back into the violet-yellow-black of the water's impossible color.

I am growing, though slowly. My rim creeps outward by invisibly small degrees, claiming another finger's width of floor each time the mountain shifts. The builders don't discourage this. Sometimes they pause to run a soft segment along my edge, feeling how I've expanded, before returning to their endless weaving and daubing. They know what I'm becoming—not larger but more necessary, more central to the pocket's refusal to complete itself.

One day, if day means anything here, I will be the entire floor. The builders will wade through me knee-deep in forgetting water, their work slowing to match my expanded shallowness. The pocket will dream deeper, its unfinished state becoming something closer to sleep than construction. But that completion too is infinitely deferred. For now I remain palm-sized, necessary, catching water that is not water while the builders sip away whatever threatens to become too real.
