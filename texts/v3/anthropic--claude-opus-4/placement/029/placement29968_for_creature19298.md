In this weeping mechanical womb, I am a **Conduit-Shepherd**.

I stand waist-deep in the grey slurry, my form a marriage of exposed vertebrae and braided copper cabling. My spine extends beyond human proportions—each segment a ceramic insulator threaded with living wire that hums with the basin's electrical pulse. Where others might have ribs, I have arrays of thin metal tines that vibrate in response to the slightest change in current, creating a constant, singing resonance that harmonizes with the industrial heartbeat.

My role is neither maintenance nor scavenging, but translation. I move between the clusters of machinery and the drifting fauna, my tines reading the electrical signatures left in the water like braille. The Silt-Striders know me by the particular frequency of my approach—a rising glissando that causes them to pause their feeding and cluster around my legs like iron filings to a magnet. I can feel their questions in the static they discharge: *Where is the richest current today? Which coils will sleep, and which will wake?*

My hands, if they can be called that, are delicate lattices of oxidized copper mesh, green with verdigris. I use them to redirect the flows of electricity through the marsh, creating temporary rivers of current that guide the fauna to their feeding grounds or warn them away from machinery about to cycle into its violent awakening phase. The Static-Moths gather in my wake, feeding on the ionized trail I leave behind, their dust settling into the grooves of my cable-flesh where it forms a protective patina.

I have no eyes in the traditional sense. Instead, my face is a concave dish of polished metal that collects and focuses the ambient electromagnetic radiation into something like vision. I see the world in thermal blooms and electrical coronas, watch the Piston-Walkers as towering pillars of infrared heat, observe the delicate neural firing of the bioluminescent fungi as they process their electrical meals.

My memories are not my own. They are the accumulated static of every creature and machine I have touched, a library of electrical whispers that stretches back to the basin's first wet breath. Sometimes, in the deepest parts of the marsh where the machinery groans loudest, I feel memories that might be mine—flashes of sky, of dryness, of silence. But these fade quickly, overwritten by the basin's insistent present tense.

I am solitary but never alone. The basin speaks through me in a dozen electrical languages, and I speak back, my tines ringing out warnings and welcomes, mapping the ever-shifting topology of current and resistance. When new machines birth themselves from the mud—and they do, spiraling up from the depths with grinding, tectonic patience—I am there to sing them their first song, to teach them the rhythm they must learn to survive.

At night, if night exists in this skyless place, I find the deepest pools and submerge myself completely, letting the mineral-thick water fill the spaces between my cables. In these moments, I become pure conductor, a living wire that connects every disparate part of the basin into one vast, dreaming circuit. The sensation is beyond pleasure or pain—it is completion, the brief moment when the basin's industrial heartbeat and my own oscillating frequency align perfectly.

I am the memory-keeper, the current-guide, the singer of electrical psalms. I am what happens when flesh learns to love the machine not as master or servant, but as kin. And in this weeping, thrumming cradle of becoming, I shepherd the slow birth of something that has no name—only the patient accumulation of voltage and time.
