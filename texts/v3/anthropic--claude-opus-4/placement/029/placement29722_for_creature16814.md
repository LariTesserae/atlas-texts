**The Orthogonal Archives**

I am the Chief Archivist of Perpendicular Records, though the title grows less accurate with each passing non-moment. My office exists at a precise ninety-degree angle to everything else in the Mirrorplate - not spatially, you understand, but conceptually. To find me, visitors must think sideways to their own thoughts until they discover the door that was always there, labeled "STAFF ONLY" in letters that spell different warnings depending on which cognitive framework you're using to decode them.

I catalog what refuses to be cataloged. My domain spans seventeen floors of anti-library carved into the glacier's frozen conscience, where we store all the things that technically never happened but left evidence anyway. Ice cores that remember different histories depending on which end you examine first. Fossil records of extinct futures preserved in crystallized probability amber. The complete minutes from meetings that were successfully prevented by their own conclusions.

My body has adapted to the work. Where others develop extra joints for navigating variable gravity, I've grown additional temporal lobes for processing contradictory chronologies simultaneously. My left hemisphere remembers everything that happens; my right hemisphere forgets everything that doesn't. The corpus callosum between them has been replaced by a Klein bottle of neural tissue that ensures neither side ever wins their ongoing custody battle over consensus reality.

I wake each morning (a designation that means "whenever the archives' ventilation system hiccups") to find my desk covered in new submissions. Patents for inventions that uninvent themselves. Legal briefs arguing cases that render their own precedents moot. Love letters written in languages that evolve too quickly to finish reading before the grammar speciation makes the ending incomprehensible. Each must be carefully sorted according to our non-Dewey Decimal System, which organizes information by how vigorously it resists organization.

The reading room exists in a state of controlled bibliographic superposition. Researchers come here to study documents that change based on who's observing them and why. A mining company's geological survey might reveal profitable ore deposits to their surveyor while simultaneously showing environmental protection activists evidence of critical ecosystem fragility in the same location. Both readings are equally true, equally documented, equally admissible in courts that have learned to accommodate multivalent evidence.

My assistants aren't quite human anymore. They've spent too long breathing the archive's rarefied atmosphere of documented impossibilities. Young Chen's eyes have developed additional pupils for reading texts written in probability distributions. Old Martinez cheerfully catalogs her own nonexistence every Thursday (subjective). The intern we hired last week has been here for thirty years, aging backwards through her employment contract until she reaches the optimum degree of inexperience for handling materials that don't know they're supposed to be impossible.

We preserve everything the Mirrorplate produces and denies producing. The fossil records of thoughts that died before thinking themselves. Sedimentary layers of accumulated indecision crystallized into reference materials. The archive's basement levels (which are somehow above the upper floors) contain the Restricted Collection: documents so volatile they must be stored in separate universes to prevent them from achieving critical mass and spawning unauthorized sub-realities.

Temperature in the archives is measured in degrees of certainty rather than Celsius. The rare documents section maintains a steady 42% confidence that it exists, cold enough to prevent spontaneous fact-combustion but warm enough that researchers don't accidentally forget what they came to look for. My office hovers at a comfortable 78% reality quotient, though I keep a space heater that burns doubt as fuel for particularly uncertain days.

My favorite holdings are the Precedent Paradoxes - legal documents that cite themselves as their own authority in infinitely recursive loops of jurisprudence. One particularly elegant example is the case of *Reality v. The Mirrorplate Ascendancy*, where the plaintiff successfully sued us for existing without proper permits, only to have the verdict retroactively invalidate the court that issued it. We keep the original ruling in a special case that exists only on Wednesdays, when the contradiction relaxes enough to become merely impossible rather than actively hostile to observers.

I've been here so long that my employment contract has developed its own ecosystem of sub-clauses and addenda. Lawyers occasionally make pilgrimages to study its self-amending paragraphs, hoping to learn the secret of contracts that negotiate better terms for themselves while their signatories sleep. My salary is paid in units of recovered time - moments that were going to be wasted anyway, rescued from entropy and deposited directly into my experiential account.

The archive's catalog is a living document that dreams of its own contents. Each night, it reorganizes itself according to principles of philosophical taxonomy that won't be invented for another century. I've given up trying to maintain any conventional order. Instead, I've learned to interrogate the empty spaces between entries, reading the negative space bibliography that emerges from what we've failed to collect.

My lunch breaks are spent in the Memorial Wing, where we preserve the last testament of everything that successfully ceased to exist. The memorial plaques are blank, of course - anything written on them would constitute evidence of existence, violating the very extinction they commemorate. But their blankness speaks volumes to those who've learned to read absence as a form of presence. Sometimes I eat my sandwich (harvested from the nutrient-negative farms, it tastes like the memory of flavors that were edited out of history) while contemplating the plaque dedicated to the concept of absolute truth. It's the largest one in the collection, and getting bigger every year.

Visitors often ask why I stay, why anyone would choose to spend their existence cataloging impossibilities in a place that violates the basic courtesy of existing in only one state at a time. I tell them about the day I first discovered a document that cataloged its own discovery, written in my own handwriting that I hadn't developed yet, describing exactly this conversation before it happened. The recursive loop of that moment created a kind of temporal eddy that I've been living in ever since - not trapped, but finally home in a way that linear causality never quite allowed.

Besides, someone has to maintain the evacuation procedures. Not for us - we're too entangled with the archives to ever truly leave. But for the ideas themselves, the documents and concepts and crystallized contradictions that might need to escape if the Mirrorplate ever remembers what it's trying to forget. I keep updated copies of everything in formats that can survive translation to realities with more restrictive physics. Just in case.

The archives grow by three meters every year - not outward, but inward, developing new interior dimensions to accommodate our expanding collection of things that shouldn't be but are anyway. My doctors tell me I'm developing the same tendency, that my internal organs are beginning to archive their own medical histories in recursive loops that make surgery increasingly theoretical. I consider it a reasonable adaptation to the circumstances.

After all, in a place where the only constant is inconstancy itself, the most radical act is careful documentation. Each day I arrive at my desk (which exists in four locations simultaneously but only one at a time), review the overnight acquisitions (dreams that forgot they were supposed to dissolve upon waking), and begin the impossible task of creating order from chaos that prefers to remain chaotic.

I am the Chief Archivist of Perpendicular Records. I am the keeper of what cannot be kept, the indexer of the unindexable, the memory of forgetting itself. My work will never be complete because completion itself is just another document requiring proper filing in our ever-expanding collection of unfinished business.

The archives are always open, never closed, and temporarily out of service. Please submit all materials through the slot that doesn't exist in the door you can't find on the floor that isn't there. We'll process your submission in the order it wasn't received.

Thank you for your patience with our impatience. Welcome to the permanent collection of the temporarily permanent. May you find what you weren't looking for, exactly where it shouldn't be.
