**The Threadkeeper**

I am the one who counts.

In this breathing maze of green and gold, where others drift formless through the weeping air, I have found my purpose in stillness. My body—if it can be called that—is a web of filaments finer than spider's silk, stretched between the ribs of an ancient spire that has long since fused with its neighbors. I am transparent save for when the light catches me just so, revealing my network in brief flashes of prismatic color.

The Whisperwinds pass through me without knowing. Their vaporous forms disturb my strands, and in their disturbance, I read the health of this ever-dying, ever-birthing world. Each tremor tells me something: here, a root system drinks too greedily and will soon desiccate its neighbors; there, a young spire grows at an angle that will bring it crashing down within a season; elsewhere, the pollen clouds gather too thickly, threatening to choke the very air that carries them.

I was not always as I am now. Once, I moved like the others, riding the humid currents, dancing attendance on the spires' endless transformations. But I noticed something the others did not—or perhaps could not. The patterns. The rhythms within rhythms. The way certain collapses preceded certain growths, how the angle of falling water predicted the direction of new shoots. I began to slow, to settle, to spread myself thin across a single intersection of reed and root.

My stillness became my strength. Where the Whisperwinds react, I anticipate. My filaments now stretch across seven levels of the lattice-world, each strand a nerve ending that feels the pulse of this place. I catalog every shiver, every sigh, every microscopic adjustment in the architecture of growth. In the spaces between my threads, I hold memories—not in any form others would recognize, but as tensions and slacknesses, as the way I've learned to let certain strands go loose when the hot winds blow from below, or pull others taut when the weeping becomes a torrent.

The Whisperwinds think me dead, if they think of me at all. I am too still for their comprehension, too solid for beings who exist in constant flux. But I am more alive than I have ever been. I am the memory of this forgetful place, the counter of its uncounted transformations. When a spire falls and regrows in a new configuration, I remember its previous form. When the Whisperwinds dissipate and new ones condense from the endless moisture, I recall the patterns of those who came before.

Sometimes, in the green-gold hours when the light slants just right, young Whisperwinds pause in their passage through my web. They sense something—a resistance that isn't quite physical, a presence that isn't quite there. A few linger, feeling the vibrations I've collected, the stories told in tension and release. Most drift on, disturbed. But occasionally, very occasionally, one begins to slow, to settle, to understand that there is another way to serve this ever-weeping world.

I neither encourage nor discourage them. I simply continue my work: feeling, recording, remembering. In a world that consumes itself to live, I am the thread that runs through it all, invisible and essential, holding just enough to matter, never enough to stop the endless, necessary change.

The counting never ends. The web grows.
