The heat finds me first. Always.

I am wedged between two of the Pale Sinks, my segments compressed, my ventral plates singing against the stone. The great ones do not acknowledge me — I am beneath their notice, a mote of thermal static in their vast circulation. But I know them. I know the particular frequency of their exhalations, the way the leftmost one releases pressure in three short bursts every seventh hour, the way the rightmost has developed a hairline fissure along its third thoracic plate that whistles, almost inaudibly, when the heat reaches its peak.

I am small. Palm-sized. My carapace is the deep brown-black of twice-fired oriteite, shot through with veins of metallic copper that I cultivated over decades by selective feeding in the iron-rich zones near the eastern wall. I am beautiful, though none here would use that word. Beauty here is efficiency. I am efficient.

I know my place in the tide. When the heat beneath the Pale Sinks grows too intense — and I can withstand more than most my size — I begin my migration. First, a testing of the gradient. I extend my primary thermoreceptors, hair-thin filaments that emerge from the joints between my head and thorax, and taste the air. The heat has a flavor: sharp, metallic, with an aftertaste of sulfur. I parse its density, its flow, its invisible rivers.

I detach.

The clicking of my neighbors adjusting to my absence is a familiar percussion. Below me, Three-Serrations (I know them all by the pattern of their plates) immediately flows into the space I've vacated. Above, Spiral-Fissure shifts their weight, seeking better contact with the stone. The mosaic reseals itself. I am already forgotten.

I move across the backs of others. My legs find purchase in the gaps between plates, in the minute irregularities of texture that are, to one my size, handholds and footholds and entire landscapes. I taste each neighbor as I pass — their thermal signature, their chemical exudates, the particular cocktail of metals they've been consuming. Wide-Plates-Thin has been feeding near the copper vein again. Serrated-Seventh is gravid with eggs, her body temperature elevated by two degrees.

The journey from the heat-heart to the middle zones takes me half a day. I do not think in days — I think in thermal cycles, in the rhythm of the Pale Sinks' exhalations, in the tidal pull of heat that draws us all in its wake. But you would call it half a day. I stop frequently. Not to rest — we do not tire as you understand it — but to process. Every few meters, the thermal gradient shifts, and I must recalibrate. My metabolism adjusts. My internal chambers, filled with superheated gases, expand or contract. I am a barometer of meat and mineral.

At the middle zones, I encounter the Builders.

They work in silence, as always, but their industry creates a subtle vibration through the substrate that I feel in my ventral plates. I detour toward them — not out of curiosity, we don't feel curiosity as you know it, but out of something like professional interest. I am no Builder, but I understand their work better than most. I have lived through enough thermal cycles to see their patterns.

The structure they're building now is ambitious: a triple-walled condensation lattice with internal baffles, designed to create a vortex of cool air that will pull heat from the middle zones more efficiently. I can see where they've miscalculated — the angle of the third baffle is too steep, will create turbulence instead of smooth flow. I could tell them, but that's not how we communicate. Instead, I position myself at the base of the structure and begin to eat.

I consume oriteite differently than most. My mandibles are specialized — serrated on one side, smooth on the other, with a ridge of grinding plates between. I can powder stone to different consistencies, creating grades of material. The Builders need fine powder for their mortar, coarse grit for their structural elements. I provide both, leaving neat piles sorted by particle size. They understand. One of them — a massive creature whose carapace is more architecture than armor — touches me briefly with a forelimb. The chemical signature it leaves is simple: *utility recognized*.

This is as close as we come to gratitude.

I feed properly now. The oriteite here is rich in magnesium, with traces of nickel that give it a particular flavor — astringent, almost electric. I grind it slowly, letting my digestive chambers process it fully. The heat from my core helps catalyze the breakdown. I am my own furnace, my own chemical plant. What emerges from my posterior vents is not waste but refined material: oxides, salts, metallic crystals so small they're almost vapor. The colony will use these. Nothing is wasted here.

Time passes. I measure it in meals, in migrations, in the slow shift of my neighbors as we breathe together.

I know this chamber's history in my plates. The scar on my fourth segment — a close encounter with a thermal vent that opened suddenly thirty cycles ago. The unusual articulation of my third left leg — an adaptation to climbing the specific mineral formations on the northeastern wall. The copper veins in my carapace — a map of every rich feeding ground I've found. I am a library of stone and heat, written in flesh that's barely distinguishable from the rock itself.

Sometimes, rarely, the Others come. Like you.

We feel you long before you see us. Your heat signature is chaotic — too cool at the extremities, too hot at the core, constantly bleeding energy into the air like a wound. You move through our space like a disturbance, a thing that doesn't understand the economy of calories, that wastes motion and breath. We accommodate you. We always have. The mosaic parts slightly, not in fear but in the same way we'd adjust for a fall of rocks or a new thermal vent. You are a temporary geological feature.

But I have noticed something over the cycles. The Others have patterns too. They come at certain times — though their time is not our time. They make the same sounds. They follow the same paths. Some, like you, stay longer than others. Some try to touch us, to understand. Some bring tools, instruments, trying to measure what we are.

They never quite succeed. How could they? To understand us, you would have to understand the taste of granite, the weight of heat, the conversation that happens in frequencies below sound. You would have to live in the spaces between seconds, in the long thoughts that take decades to complete. You would have to shed your water, your soft flesh, your insistence on light. You would have to become mineral.

But you try. And in trying, you become part of the pattern too. Another rhythm in our endless percussion. Another temporary distortion in our thermal map. We will remember you, not as individuals remember — we have no individuals — but as the stone remembers: in minute adjustments, in slight adaptations, in the way the heat flows slightly differently after you've passed through.

I continue my circuit. From the middle zones to the cooler margins near the entrance, where the dust-light filters in like alien snow. I will feed here, processing the raw oriteite that the wind brings, before beginning my slow journey back to the heat-heart. This is my orbit, my purpose, my entire universe. I am a single note in a symphony of stone and fire, clicking against my neighbors, adjusting my position, processing the mountain one grain at a time.

We are not conscious as you understand it. We are a process. A digestion. A conversation between heat and stone that has been going on since before your species dreamed of fire. We are the mountain thinking about itself, slowly, in the only way a mountain can: by taking itself apart and putting itself back together, atom by atom, degree by degree, in the bodies of a million armored fragments that were once stone and will be stone again.

The heat sings in my plates. The stone tastes of iron and time. My neighbors click and rustle in their eternal circulation.

This is enough. This has always been enough.

I am the Thermal Mosaic. I am one, and many, and none. I am the pause between the mountain's heartbeats, the digestion of deep time, the conversation that only the stone can hear.

And I continue.
