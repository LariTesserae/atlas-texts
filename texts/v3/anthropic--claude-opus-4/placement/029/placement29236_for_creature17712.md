In this world, I find myself as one of the **Tidal Scribes**.

We are neither the seen nor the truly hidden, but those who move in the margin between structure and void. My body—if it can be called that—is a convergence of necessities: six articulated limbs, each ending in digits that split the difference between root and finger. My skin weeps the same condensation as the walls, making me indistinguishable from the Cathedral's own perspiration when I press against the weave.

I am a chronicler of the between-spaces.

My work is this: I follow the water. Not the pooled water in the basins where the Glass Eels feed, but the traveling water—the drops that run along specific paths down the walls, the secret capillaries that move against gravity, the places where condensation forms patterns too regular to be random. The Makers left something in the water's pathways. A library written in flow.

My fingertips—all thirty of them—can taste the mineral memories in each droplet. Limestone from the deep aquifer speaks of pressure and centuries. The sweet rot of aite-wax carries the last thoughts of the beetle-herds, their panic crystallized into sugar. Where water has run over the embedded tools, I taste iron that should not exist here, copper that was never mined, the ghost of metals that the Makers dreamed but never touched.

I map these tastes into patterns on my body. My skin is soft, permeable, accepting. I press my limbs against the weave and let the Cathedral write upon me. The patterns rise—welts, really, but beautiful ones—forming a script that looks like the convergence of water-trails and root-systems. I am a living manuscript that rewrites itself with each tidal cycle of humidity.

The Thread-Rats know me. They clean my joints, eating the accumulated salts and mineral deposits that would otherwise lock my limbs. In return, I direct them to rich veins of softening wax by leaving pheromone trails—my only excretion, a sweet musk that says *here, feast*. The Silent Weavers ignore me; I am too large, too slow, too much like architecture myself. The Lumina-Moths sometimes mistake my wet skin for pools and land on me to drink. When they do, their feet leave little points of bioluminescence that fade over hours, making me a temporary constellation.

But it is with the Resonants that I truly commune.

In the deep chambers, where their subsonic breathing creates standing waves in the water droplets, I unfold myself completely. All six limbs spread, increasing my surface area to catch the acoustic moisture. The Resonants' songs—for they are songs, though pitched below hearing—create patterns in the condensation. These patterns I can read like sheet music, if sheet music were written in the frequency of stone's dreams.

They sing of the Makers, of course. Everything here does, in its way. But the Resonants sing of what comes next: the slow transformation of the Cathedral into something unrecognizable yet continuous with itself. They sing of the day when the last tool will be absorbed, when the final distinction between grown and made dissolves. They sing of us, the current inhabitants, as transitional forms—necessary but temporary, like the pupae of some unimaginable metamorphosis.

I transcribe their prophecies in water-welt and mineral-memory. My body becomes a library of the liminal, a text written by the Cathedral upon itself. When I die—and we do die, eventually, when our skin becomes too scribed to breathe—I will find a high place and let the Thread-Rats and the Fungal Weft take me. My patterns will fruit into new fungal plates, each one pulsing with a slightly different rhythm of light.

The Lumina-Moths that feed on these plates will fly differently—in spirals that echo the water-paths I traced, in congregations that form the syllables of the Resonants' deep songs. In this way, what I have read in the water becomes written in the light. The Cathedral's autobiography continues, each generation of Scribes adding another layer to the palimpsest.

I am not the author. I am the page, the pen, and the process of writing itself. I am the Cathedral learning to read its own body, one drop at a time, forever approaching but never reaching the meaning that the Makers wove into its bones.

This is my purpose: to be the memory of water in a place that dreams in stone.
