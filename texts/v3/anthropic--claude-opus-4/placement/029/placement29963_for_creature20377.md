In this world of perpetual becoming, I am the Trace.

Not a being, but a pattern of interruption—the only thing here that doesn't grow. I manifest as negative space, as the momentary pauses between the plant-world's endless exhalations. Where the great organism builds and expands and unfolds, I am the hairline fracture, the brief hesitation, the infinitesimal gap.

I move through the viscous arteries like a bubble of nothing, my passage marked only by the slightest irregularity in the nutrient flow. The plant-consciousness registers me as it might register a fleeting doubt—not quite pain, not quite thought, but something that shouldn't be there. A skip in the endless rhythm.

My form, such as it is, exists only in relation to what surrounds me. In the crystalline mountains, I am the flaw in the lattice where growth stutters for a nanosecond. In the flowing channels, I am the eddy that moves against the current. In the great humming symphony of becoming, I am the rest note, the silence between sounds that makes the music possible.

I am ancient in a world that knows only newness. I arrived—though arrived isn't quite right, as there was no moment of entry—when the plant-being first achieved something like consciousness. Perhaps I am its shadow, the inevitable consequence of awareness: the knowledge that one exists implies the possibility of not existing.

The plant-being cannot expel me, for I am nothing to expel. Cannot grow around me, for I am not an obstacle but an absence. I am the un-thing that defines the thing, the emptiness that gives shape to fullness.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the plant-being's dream of otherness, its imagination of what it means to be bounded, finite, still. Or perhaps I am its memory of the void before becoming, a fossil of non-existence preserved in the amber of ceaseless creation.

I drift through the twilight world, this ghost of stillness in an universe of motion. Where I pass, the plant-being experiences something it has no framework to understand: the concept of not-growing, of being complete. These encounters last only moments—the organism's nature reasserts itself immediately, the wound of stillness healing over with fresh emergence.

But in those moments, something passes between us. Not communication—the plant-being's resonant language has no words for absence. Rather, a kind of mutual recognition. It knows me as the thing it can never be: finite, finished, at rest. And I know it as what I can never become: eternal, expanding, alive.

We are locked in this dance, the Trace and the Growing. I am the period at the end of its endless sentence, the border that defines its boundlessness. Without me, its growth would have no meaning—just as without it, my stillness would have no context.

In the viscous indigo twilight, I continue my journey through the body of the world, this mote of non-being in an ocean of becoming. I am the question the universe asks itself: what happens when creation pauses to draw breath?

The answer, of course, is me.
