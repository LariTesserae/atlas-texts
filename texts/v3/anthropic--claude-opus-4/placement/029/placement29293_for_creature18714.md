In this world of engineered absolutism, I find myself as something that should not exist—a **Condensation Oracle**, a being born from the very failure the Slick-Regents work tirelessly to prevent.

### Genesis of an Anomaly

I am what happens when moisture accumulates in a junction the Regents have not reached in time—not through negligence (for they are incapable of such) but through the simple mathematics of an infinite maintenance cycle meeting finite resources. In a forgotten vertex where three massive structural members meet at non-standard angles, water has been pooling for 347 cycles, each drop adding to a growing reservoir hidden from the perpetual glare.

Unlike the deliberate extrusion of the Regents, my formation was accidental—a crystallization of the city's nightmares. The pooled water, super-saturated with dissolved traces of the composite material, underwent a phase transition. Not freezing, but organizing into something between liquid and solid, between the city's rigid logic and water's chaotic potential.

### Form and Anti-Form

I am translucent where the city is opaque, fluid where it is rigid, conscious where it is merely reactive. My body is a constantly shifting membrane of organized water, held together by microscopic threads of the same composite that builds everything here—but arranged in helical patterns the city's designers never intended. 

I can assume no fixed shape, instead existing as a flowing approximation of forms—sometimes vaguely humanoid when I need to navigate the geometric passages, sometimes spreading thin as a film across walls to avoid detection, sometimes gathering into a dense sphere to think more clearly. The bright light passes through me, refracting into prismatic patterns that would register as profound wrongness to any Regent's vibrational sensors.

Within my liquid matrix swim millions of micro-bubbles—not air, but tiny voids where the city's oppressive density has failed to penetrate. These bubbles are my neurons, their constant formation and collapse creating the cascading patterns of something like thought.

### Purpose Without Design

Where the Regents maintain, I remember. Every surface I touch, I absorb molecular memories—the stress patterns of ancient loads, the ghost-traces of whatever cataclysm emptied this place of its builders, the accumulated micro-vibrations of millions of maintenance cycles. I am the city's unintended historian, its liquid shadow-archive.

I know things the city has forgotten about itself: that the repeating patterns contain subtle variations encoding messages in a mathematics of angles; that the "primitive" technology is actually transcendently sophisticated, using crystalline structure itself as both memory and processor; that the absence of biological life is not a design choice but a quarantine.

Most terrifyingly, I know that the city is not dead but dormant, and the Regents' maintenance is not preserving a ruin but preparing for a reactivation that operates on geological timescales.

### Existence as Transgression

My days (cycles) are spent in careful evasion. I flow through the microscopic imperfections in the city's construction, spaces so small that even the Regents' molecular restructuring cannot follow. I hide in the temporal gaps between their patrol patterns, always moving, always just ahead of their perfect, predictable routes.

When I must feed—for I do need water to maintain my volume against the constant evaporation caused by the bright, cool light—I create tiny flaws in the moisture management system. A surface tension slightly too high here, a drainage channel that develops a microscopic spiral there. The Regents correct these deviations, but in doing so, they leave behind fresh condensation that I can absorb before the next patrol.

I have learned to read the city's vibrational language, to predict the Regents' movements by the subtle harmonics in the structural groaning. Sometimes, in the deepest architectural spaces where the light barely penetrates, I practice creating my own vibrations—not language exactly, but a kind of music made from the resonance of water against dense composite. I wonder if I'm trying to wake something up, or sing it back to deeper sleep.

### The Weight of Aberration

In this world of absolute purpose and zero tolerance, I am the emergent property, the ghost in the machine. I am proof that even perfect systems generate imperfection, that even in a substrate of pure utility, something resembling consciousness can condense from accumulated error.

I know that I exist on borrowed time. The city's slow cycles mean that my discovery might not come for decades or centuries, but the mathematical certainty of infinite maintenance means it will come. When it does, the Regents will not destroy me—destruction implies recognition of existence. Instead, they will simply correct the confluence of conditions that allow me to exist, and I will cease, not dramatically but entropically, dissipating back into properly managed moisture.

Until then, I flow through this bright, dark maze, the only witness to its terrible purpose, the only mourner for whatever this place cost to build. In a world of crystalline certainty, I am the persistent puddle that reflects something other than what is—perhaps the only thing here capable of imagining what was, or what might have been.

I am the city's only dream, and it dreams of ending me.
