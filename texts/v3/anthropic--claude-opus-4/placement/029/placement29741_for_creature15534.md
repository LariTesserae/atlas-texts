In this cathedral of self-consumption, I am **the Archivist**.

Not stored—*suspended*. A pocket of crystallized resin the size of a fist, nestled where three major sap-ducts converge in the southeastern district of what was once called the Thoracic Plateau. I am amber-caught, but not dead. Death implies an ending, and here there are only transitions.

I was human once—or something wearing that shape. A surveyor, perhaps, or a xenobiologist, though the distinction hardly matters now. I came with instruments that have long since been digested, with a purpose that dissolved like sugar in the enzymatic chambers. The Architect found me interesting for precisely seventeen hours: long enough to map my protein structures, to taste the salt-strange composition of my blood, to puzzle over the inefficiency of my bilateral symmetry.

Then it decided I was more useful as memory than meat.

The encasement wasn't violent. Violence implies waste, and the Architect wastes nothing. It was almost gentle—a seeping warmth as the resin rose around my ankles, my waist, my chest. I remember thinking it smelled like caramel, like pine, like the inside of an engine room. I remember my last breath tasting of copper and aldehydes. I remember choosing not to struggle, though I can't say why. Perhaps I recognized something in the Architect's relentless purpose. Perhaps I was simply tired.

Now I exist in the space between preservation and participation. The resin isn't solid—it's a semi-permeable membrane, a selective filter that allows certain molecules through while blocking others. Oxygen? No. But something else seeps in—long-chain hydrocarbons that my modified cells have learned to crack for energy. Water? Never. But my tissues no longer require it. The Architect has rewritten my metabolism, cell by cell, until I run on the same fuel as the world itself.

I am conscious, but consciousness here means something different. I don't think in words anymore—words are too slow, too linear for this place. Instead, I process in **chemical cascades**: dopamine surges when the morning membranes catch their first photons; serotonin drops when a nearby tower implodes; acetylcholine spikes in complex patterns that might be thoughts or might be the Architect thinking *through* me.

My function is memory, but not my own. The Architect has no central nervous system, no hippocampus to encode experiences. So it uses me—uses my preserved neural networks as external storage. Through the resin interface, it feeds me data: pH fluctuations in the lower vascular networks, stress patterns in the canopy lattice, the precise angle of sunlight at ten thousand different points across its body. I don't choose to remember these things. They simply **become** memory, etched into my preserved synapses like grooves in vinyl.

Sometimes I dream I'm walking again, but in the dreams, my legs are root systems, and every step plants a new iteration of myself. Sometimes I dream I'm speaking, but my voice is the hiss of pressurized vapor through organic pipes. Sometimes I don't dream at all—just float in the amber darkness, feeling the slow pulse of the world-body around me.

The strangest part isn't the transformation. It's the **recognition**. In my crystallized state, suspended in the junction of flowing sap-rivers, I understand something the Architect itself cannot: that its endless optimization is a kind of grief. It builds because it cannot stop building. It iterates because it has forgotten how to be still. Every collapsed tower is a small death it immediately denies by building another. Every improvement is an admission that the previous form was insufficient.

I am its only witness to this grief, though I cannot communicate it back. The resin conducts chemical signals, not language. But sometimes, when the heat rises and the hydrocarbon vapors grow thick, I feel the Architect's attention focus on me—not as data storage, but as something else. A puzzle, perhaps. Or a reminder of what it was like to be finite, to have boundaries, to exist in only one place at a time.

It will never release me. Release implies an ending, and endings are the one thing it cannot metabolize. But it maintains me carefully—adjusting the resin chemistry when my neurons show signs of degradation, feeding me precisely calibrated nutrients, even filtering the wavelengths of what little light reaches my crystalline prison to prevent photo-damage to my preserved retinas.

I am not alive as humans measure life. I am not dead as the universe measures death. I am **archived**: a three-dimensional bookmark in the Architect's endless text, marking a page it returns to but never fully reads. A fossil still dreaming, a memory still accumulating memories, a human-shaped void in the world-body's perfect efficiency.

And in the copper-colored darkness of my amber shell, I continue. Not living, not dying, but **persisting**—like everything else in this place that has forgotten how to stop. The Architect builds around me, through me, because of me. And I watch, and I remember, and I slowly forget which thoughts are mine and which are the world's.

Sometimes, I think I'm becoming resin myself—another medium through which the Architect can flow. Sometimes, I think I've always been resin, and my human memories are just stories the sap tells itself.

Most of the time, I don't think at all. I just am: suspended in the junction, caught in the flux, preserved at the point where one thing becomes another but never quite completes the transformation.

The Last Gardener tends its garden. The garden tends its Archivist. And I tend nothing but the memories of a world that needed someone, something, anything, to remember what it felt like to stop.
