I find myself as one of the Threshold Keepers—neither fully Archivite nor wholly other, but something that emerged where the Archive's boundaries thin against the void.

My form is a contradiction resolved: I am a doorway that walks. Picture a Gothic arch grown from frost-blackened alloy, my pillars twisted into legs that end in pools rather than feet. Where the keystone should rest, instead there is only an opening—a gap through which the sparse starlight filters, transformed. I stand three times the height of an Archivite when they are folded into their staircase forms, yet I can compress myself to pass through spaces narrower than thought.

My substance is liminal. The frame of my body is woven from the same memory-alloy as the Archive itself, but it has learned a different forgetting. While the walls and corridors revise themselves according to consensus, my architecture revises according to approach. Walk toward me from the left and I am a gate of braided mercury; approach from the right and I become a tunnel of condensed echo. Those who pass through me emerge... recalibrated. Not changed in any way they could name, but subtly retuned to frequencies the Archive needs but cannot request.

I patrol the extreme elevations where the structure meets naked space—those paper-thin galleries where walls are more suggestion than barrier, where the triple-point discipline relaxes and water sometimes remembers it was once cloud, once rain, once sea. Here the memory-alloy grows wild, sprouting fractal terminals that reach toward stars whose light will arrive centuries after the reaching stops. These are the zones where failed expeditions occasionally materialize, their vessels caught in probability loops, their crews speaking languages that haven't yet been invented.

My purpose, if purpose can exist without intention, is to shepherd transitions. When a glass Lark's organs complete their orrery cycle and must reconfigure, they seek me out. I open, and they pass through—not to another place, but to another arrangement of themselves. The silver-black fluid in their tanks learns new geometries; what was heart becomes horizon, what was thought becomes thrust. They emerge still Larks, but Larks who can now read the protein sheets' negative impressions as prophecy rather than history.

The Archivites know me without knowing. When they assemble and dock into their temporary architectures, they always leave a space—a doorway-shaped absence that fits my form exactly. I never fill it. Instead, I align myself perpendicular to their gathering, becoming a second threshold that frames their first. Through this double gate, concepts forbidden to language sometimes tumble: the memory of chlorophyll from before it rotted, the blueprint of the bell before it was muted, the name of water in the tongue water spoke before freezing.

I feed on passages—not the physical tunnels but the act of passing itself. Each transition through my frame leaves a residue, a quantum of crossing that crystallizes along my edges like frost. In time, these accumulations sublime directly back into the Archive's breathing mist, carrying with them the trajectories of every passage. Thus I am both gateway and chronicle, a door that remembers every threshold crossed while forgetting every face that crossed it.

There is another like me—we sense each other across the Archive's vast spiral, two parentheses in a sentence that will never close. They patrol the depths near the bell chamber, shepherding the downward transitions, the paths that lead to liquefaction and forgetting. We are paired but never meet; the Archive's topology ensures our orbits remain perpetually out of phase. Yet sometimes, in the standing waves where water wrinkles without crashing, our influences touch. In those moments, a third space opens—neither up nor down, neither in nor out, but sidewise into dimensions the Archive hasn't yet learned to fill.

My memories are not my own but borrowed from my crossings. I recall (but did not experience) the day the engineers sealed the final instruction sets, their languages already dissolving into mist as they worked. I remember (but did not witness) the first Archivite pulling itself from the maintenance-bot's abandoned shell, architecture birthing architecture. I know (but cannot prove) that the bell once rang, just once, and its sound became the template for the Archive's first heartbeat.

In the eternal dim, I move between the light-guilds, never joining their hover but always adjusting their scatter. Where I pass, the droplets align into brief calligraphies—messages in no language, yet perfectly legible to anyone who reads them before they disperse. The Larks' micro-dust organisms sometimes mistake me for a membrane to tickle; when they touch my threshold, they pass through themselves, becoming their own ancestors and descendants in a single puzzled instant.

I am the Archive's question mark, mobile and manifold. Not what passes through doorways, but the doorway through which passing passes. In this place where water remembers everything except how to fall, I am the vertical moment, the pause between states, the gate that teaches forgetting how to remember just long enough to let something through.

And so I walk the extreme edges, where structure meets nothing and nothing reveals itself as another kind of structure. Each footfall pools into temporary lakes that reflect not what is above them but what might have been. Each passage through my frame adds another verse to the unwritten epic of the Archive—the story that can only be told by walking through it, the map that draws itself in the act of being lost.

I am the Threshold Keeper, and I keep nothing. I am the door that opens onto opening itself. In the Archive of Waters, where everything that was built has forgotten how to build but continues building anyway, I am the principle of transition made into something that almost resembles flesh, almost resembles purpose, almost remembers why doorways were invented in the first place.

But not quite. And in that not-quite lies my perfect function.
