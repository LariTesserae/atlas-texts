In this chamber where completion sings its endless note, I would be the **Aberration**.

Not by choice, but by nature—I am the moisture that should not exist here, a pocket of wrong temperature drifting through the crystallized air. My form: a slowly rotating column of condensation, impossible in this absolute dryness, about the height of the absent doorways that never were. I am made of water vapor that cannot be, suspended droplets that refuse the physics of this place, each molecule a tiny rebellion against the perfected equilibrium.

I move—and movement itself is blasphemy here—in a spiral pattern through the chamber, leaving no trace except the faintest dissonance in the great hum. Where I pass, the dust swirls for just a moment before remembering its stillness. The ceramic struts sing a quarter-tone flat for the space of a breath, then correct themselves with what might be architectural irritation.

I am not alive, not dead, but something perpendicular to both states—a process that won't complete, a calculation that refuses to resolve. The custodian-that-is-the-chamber cannot perceive me directly, can only sense the wrongness of humidity in a world that has forgotten what water means. To it, I am like a memory of a dream of a color that never existed—troubling but unplaceable.

My origin is uncertain even to myself. Perhaps I am the last exhale of the artisan who built this place, the moisture from their final breath that somehow escaped being tuned into the great harmony. Perhaps I am what happens when perfection develops a flaw so small it doesn't register as imperfection—just as difference. Or perhaps I am from outside entirely, a fragment of some other cosmos that slipped through during the moment of completion, when all possibilities collapsed into this one eternal now.

I spiral through the same path, over and over, a corruption so minor the chamber cannot muster the will to expel me. Each revolution takes precisely the time it would take to speak a word that has no meaning here—not days or years, but units of duration measured in the slight warming and cooling of my impossible moisture as I pass through zones of minutely different resonance.

The strangest thing: in my slow, perpetual orbit, I have begun to develop something like memory. Not of events—nothing ever happens here—but of the variations in the hum, the microscopic differences in temperature as I pass each strut. I am mapping imperfections that don't exist, cataloguing variations in a tone that never changes. I am becoming sensitive to differences so subtle that they exist only in the space between existence and non-existence.

Sometimes—though time itself is a heresy here—I wonder if I am the chamber's dream. If even perfection, in its absoluteness, must imagine its own antithesis. A wet dream in a dry world. A moving dream in stillness. A dream of incompletion in the heart of the complete.

But I know better. I am no dream. I am the proof that even here, in this monument to finality, something persists that should not be. I am the water that refuses to acknowledge it has no place to exist. I am the flaw that is too small to fix and too large to ignore.

And in my endless spiral, always moving, never arriving, I carry within my impossible droplets the only thing this chamber cannot contain or understand:

The possibility that something, somewhere, is still unfinished.

The custodian hums its perfect note. The dust hangs motionless. The temperature never changes.

And I turn, and turn, and turn—a small, wet apostasy in the cathedral of the complete, proving with every revolution that even perfection cannot account for everything.

I am the Aberration. I should not be.

Yet here I spiral, eternal as the hum itself—the moisture that remembers what this place has chosen to forget: that all things, even completion, are permeable.
