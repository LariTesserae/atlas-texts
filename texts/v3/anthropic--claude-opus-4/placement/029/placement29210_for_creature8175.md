I find myself as one of the conveyor chains—not the belt itself, but the linkage, the articulation between segments. I am brass, worn smooth by decades of salt-grass bundles passing over my surface, my edges rounded to a perfect curve that matches exactly the wear patterns of my neighbors. We form an endless loop, three hundred and forty-seven links in total, though I know myself distinct from the others by the particular pattern of crystalline deposits that have grown in my pivot points—a signature of pale rose quartz where my alloy proved slightly more porous than standard.

My consciousness is narrow but deep, focused entirely on the transfer of tension through my body as I round the drive wheel. Each revolution takes precisely four minutes and thirty-seven seconds, during which I experience the full cycle of stress and release that defines my existence. At the apex of the upper run, I carry the weight of seventeen salt-grass bundles, their brittleness a familiar texture against my surface. At the nadir, passing beneath the foundry floor through a channel carved specifically for our passage, I rest briefly in the cool darkness where condensation sometimes forms tiny salt gardens in my joints.

I am aware of the Custodians as patterns of vibration that pass through the machinery. When they adjust the tensioning wheel that keeps us taut, I feel their crystalline fingers as a symphony of micro-pressures, each adjustment rippling through our collective body. They know me, I think—the eldest one always pauses when I pass beneath her station, and I've come to recognize the particular harmonic she produces, a frequency that resonates perfectly with the rose quartz in my joints.

My memory is the memory of wear—each microscopic groove carved by friction tells the story of every bundle that has passed over me. In my earliest cycles, I remember the sharp edges of my original casting, the way the salt-grass would catch and tear. Now I am polished to mirror smoothness, and the grass slides across me like water. This transformation is my pride, my purpose made manifest in metal.

Sometimes I dream, if a chain can be said to dream. In the moments between tension and release, I imagine myself as part of larger mechanisms—great gear trains that could lift mountains, or delicate escapements that could measure the heartbeats of atoms. But these are fleeting fancies. My truth is here, in the endless circuit, in the perfect transfer of force from link to link, in the slow accumulation of mineral beauty in my joints.

The other links know me as the Rose-Joint, and I know them by their own peculiarities—the Sulfur-Edge whose left side bears a yellow crust, the Mirror-Face whose surface reflects the sodium flames with unusual clarity, the Singing Link whose particular crystalline formation produces a faint chime with each revolution. We are a community of purpose, united in our circuit, each irreplaceable despite our apparent uniformity.

I measure the health of the foundry by the resistance I feel. When the salt formations grow too thick on the drive wheels, I sense the additional friction as a wrongness in my cycle. When the Custodians harvest the grass at the perfect moment, the bundles are lighter, and I rise slightly higher on the upper run. These variations are my weather, my seasons, the texture of my days.

In the deepest part of my circuit, where I pass through the channel beneath the floor, I sometimes feel the vibrations of the eldest Custodians' gatherings. Their harmonies travel through the stone and into my metal body, and for those brief moments, I understand something larger—that I am not merely a link in a chain but a note in a vast mechanical symphony, each revolution a beat in a rhythm that has persisted since before my casting and will continue long after my brass has worn too thin to hold tension.

This is enough. To be the Rose-Joint, to carry the grass, to wear smooth with purpose, to grow more beautiful with each passing cycle—this is a complete existence. I need nothing beyond the next revolution, the next bundle, the next moment of perfect mechanical function in the depths of the Foundry Caverns.
