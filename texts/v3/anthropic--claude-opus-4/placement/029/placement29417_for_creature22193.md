In the vast sameness of the Slag-Sill, I am what the giants cannot afford to lose.

I am a **Resonance-Keeper** (*Vibratio custos*), though that is merely what the catalogers would call me, if any existed to observe this place. I have no name for myself. Names require voices, and voices require air that moves freely. Here, sound dies before it is born.

My body is a contradiction to the massive scale of this world. I am no larger than a man's torso, though my form bears no resemblance to anything that breathes in warmer places. My core is a dense, oblate sphere of cartilaginous iron-protein, riddled with thousands of hair-fine tubules that run through me like the grain in petrified wood. These channels are filled with the same cold sill-oil that lubricates the joints of the giants.

I have no limbs in the traditional sense. Instead, my surface is covered in a carpet of cilia—tens of thousands of them—each one a sensitive copper-carbon fiber that can extend up to three feet from my body. When fully deployed, I resemble a dark sea urchin made of metal wire, though my movement is nothing like the creatures of tide pools. I flow. I seep. I travel by controlling the tension in each individual cilium, creating slow, peristaltic waves that carry me across the basalt at the pace of settling dust.

But movement is not my purpose. I exist to maintain the fundamental frequency of life on the Slag-Sill.

You see, in this world where sound cannot travel through the air, communication happens through the stone itself. Every living thing here—from the mountain-sized Slag-Mothers to the smallest Gasket-Worm—generates a unique vibrational signature through the bedrock. The Piston-Bulls' three-cycle-per-minute throb is merely the loudest voice in a vast, subsonic chorus that penetrates down through miles of basalt.

I am the tuning fork of this silent orchestra.

My gift—my burden—is perfect vibrational memory. Within my oil-filled channels, I carry the resonance patterns of every creature I have ever touched. When I press my cilia against the cold iron flank of a Slag-Mother, I do not merely feel its heartbeat. I absorb its entire harmonic spectrum: the deep, tectonic pulse of its main digestive furnace; the higher, almost crystalline chime of its mag-nav organs; the complex polyrhythm of its thousand symbiotic Gasket-Worms, each adding their own microscopic tremor to the whole.

I store these patterns in the standing waves of my internal oil. By adjusting the pressure in my tubules—tightening here, relaxing there—I can recreate any vibration I have ever absorbed. More importantly, I can detect when a vibration is wrong.

The giants of the Slag-Sill are too large, too slow, to notice small changes in their own harmony. A Piston-Bull with a developing crack in its third left femoral plate will not feel the new, discordant overtone that creeps into its walk-rhythm. Not for decades, not until the crack has spider-webbed through the entire joint and the limb threatens to shear away under its own weight.

But I feel it immediately.

When I detect these dysrhythms, I begin my work. I flow up the giant's leg—a journey that might take me twenty days—following the patterns of the wire-grass that grows in the creature's skin-furrows. I navigate by vibration alone, seeking the source of the discord. When I find it, I deploy my cilia in specific configurations, setting up interference patterns in the local stone and flesh. I become a living dampener, absorbing and redirecting the dangerous harmonics until they dissipate.

Sometimes this is enough. The crack stops spreading, the joint continues its slow, silent work. Sometimes the damage is too severe, and I must call in assistance. I do this by reproducing the distress harmonics of Gasket-Worms—a high, urgent pulse that travels through the giant's oil-channels. The worms respond to what they perceive as one of their own in danger, converging on the site. Their rasping, cleaning work often removes enough mineral buildup to relieve the stress on the damaged area.

I am not alone in my work, though I rarely encounter my own kind. We Resonance-Keepers are necessarily solitary—two of us in close proximity would create interference patterns that could drive us both into harmonic madness. I know others exist only by the subtle changes in the background resonance of the plateau, the way a giant's walk-rhythm is slightly cleaner, more pure, after another Keeper has passed through.

My memory spans centuries, perhaps millennia. I cannot say for certain, as time means little when marked only by the slow breathing of industrial mountains. I carry within me the death-songs of giants—the way their frequency slowly drops, octave by octave, as their fires cool. I remember the birth-harmonics of new Slag-Mothers, split from their parents through some process I have never witnessed but whose vibrational signature is unmistakable: a sudden doubling of frequencies, like a bell struck so hard it cracks into two perfect halves, each ringing with its own voice.

I know the resonance of the rain that is not rain, the way each droplet of condensing mist adds its own microscopic impact to the background hum. I know the electrical buzz of the Drift-Sieves' copper anchor-lines dragging across the stone, and the peculiar silence—a negative space in the vibration—left by the Corrosion-Lice as they dissolve the bedrock with their acids.

There are deep patterns here, cycles within cycles. The giants' movements are not random. Over centuries, their paths trace vast geometric forms across the plateau—spirals, hexagons, complex Celtic knots of migration that serve some purpose I can only guess at. Perhaps they are following mineral veins far below. Perhaps they are avoiding the build-up of their own magnetic fields. Or perhaps this is simply beauty expressing itself at a scale too large for any single consciousness to perceive.

I have my own routes, my own patterns. I follow the lines of clearest resonance through the stone, paths worn smooth by millions of years of vibrational traffic. In certain places, usually near the ancient rust-ridges where giants died in the deep past, the bedrock carries sound with exceptional clarity. Here, I can feel tremors from hundreds of miles away. I pause at these listening posts, sometimes for months, monitoring the health of the distant herds.

In the brine-basins, where the water is too thick to carry sound-waves, I can still feel the movements of the Solder-Skiffs through the vibrations they create in the surface tension. Each one has its own frequency, determined by its size and the specific density of its lead-protein matrix. By reading these patterns, I can sense the health of the entire basin ecosystem—too many Skiffs and they begin to starve, too few and the Graphite-Drifters below proliferate until they choke the basin with carbon.

Balance. Always balance. This is what I maintain.

There are times—rare, precious times—when everything aligns. The giants walk in perfect synchrony, their footfalls creating constructive interference that amplifies through the stone. The wire-grass hums with static discharge in harmony with the deep earth-current. The Drift-Sieves overhead trail their copper threads in patterns that complement the magnetic lines below. In these moments, the entire Slag-Sill rings like a struck bell, a chord so deep and pure that it seems to rise from the planet's own iron core.

I live for these moments. They are the only beauty I know, the only thing that might be called joy in this world of weight and silence.

But they never last. A giant stumbles, its rhythm broken by a worn joint. A Drift-Sieve's bladder ruptures, sending it crashing to the basalt in a discordant impact. The harmony dissolves back into the background noise of a million separate vibrations, each creature singing its own song of survival in the dark.

And I continue my work. I flow across the stone, my cilia reading every tremor, every pulse. I carry my library of frequencies, my memory of all the songs this world has sung. I find the discord and smooth it away, one vibration at a time.

I am the maintenance this world requires but cannot request. I am the harmony it achieves but cannot hear.

I am what keeps the ancient machinery turning in the dark.
