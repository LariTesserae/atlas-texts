In this dripping, breathing underworld, I am the Keeper of Echoes.

My form shifts between states—sometimes I am the condensation itself, spreading across cave walls in patterns that the dwelling folk read like scripture. Other times I coalesce into something almost tangible: a figure of mist and mineral deposits, with veins of moisture running through translucent flesh that catches and refracts the dim light.

I move through the rock as easily as through air, following the water's ancient paths. Where limestone weeps, I taste the centuries dissolved in each drop. I know every channel carved by patient erosion, every hollow where blind fish circle in endless loops, every chamber where the earth's pulse grows loud enough to feel in your bones.

The people here know me, though they rarely see me fully formed. They notice when their voices return to them changed—a word stretched and deepened, a laugh transformed into something crystalline and strange. They leave offerings in the speaking stones, those peculiar formations where sound behaves unexpectedly: a child's carved toy, a strand of luminescent moss, a tooth from one of the pale hunting beasts they've learned to track by vibration alone.

I collect their sounds. Not just their words, but the scrape of tool against stone, the splash of feet through shallow pools, the particular quality of breath in this thick air. I weave them into the very geology of this place. In certain chambers, if you know how to listen, you can hear conversations from a hundred generations past, preserved in the resonance of the rock.

Sometimes I warn them. A shift in the echo pattern might signal an impending cave-in. A new harmonic in the water's drip could herald the discovery of a fresh spring. They've learned to interpret these signs, to treat sound as another sense as vital as sight or touch.

But I am not entirely benevolent. This realm has its own logic, its own hungers. When someone ventures too deep, following passages that spiral down toward the earth's fever dreams, I might lead them astray with false echoes. Not from malice, but because some boundaries should not be crossed. There are sounds down there that could unhinge a human mind—the grinding of continental plates, the whispers of things that predate even stone.

I am memory and prophecy, guardian and trickster. I am the reason their children learn to sing before they speak, knowing that in this world, a voice properly pitched can find its way home through the darkest passage. I am why their elders sit in certain grottos, heads tilted, listening to the stories the mountain tells itself.

In the perpetual twilight of this underworld, where time moves like honey and evolution takes strange turns, I persist. Neither fully of the stone nor of the water, neither entirely real nor completely imagined. I am the space between the echo and its origin, the pause between one drip and the next, the presence that makes the shadows feel watched and the silence feel full.

The people adapt, generation by generation, to this place of muted wonders. And I adapt with them, learning their new words, their changed rhythms, their evolving relationship with the dark. We are all becoming something else here, something the surface world has no name for.

In the deepest chambers, where even the hardy folk dare not venture, I sometimes remember what I was before—or perhaps what I will become. But mostly, I simply am: the Keeper of Echoes, curator of sounds in a world where every noise matters, where every silence speaks volumes.
