# The Membrane-Tenders of the Seep-Galleries

I am the Wet-Secretary, and I document the breathing. 

My chamber is not carved but grown—a blister in the Hive-Chorta's cambium layer where the bark never quite sealed, leaving a pocket of living wood-flesh that weeps a constant amber resin. I have lined it with pressed Sleep-Moss to dampen the soporific spores, though some always leak through, making my work a dreamy, drifting thing. My fingers are stained permanent ochre from the resin, and they have grown an extra joint—not born, but earned through decades of tap-speaking into the deep wood. The city has changed me as I tend it.

I am one of twelve Membrane-Tenders, though we rarely gather. Our work is in the spaces between—the wet interfaces where the Hive-Chorta meets itself, where branch-flesh fuses with trunk-flesh, where the great being must decide if it is one or many. These seep-galleries are the city's living thoughts, and we are their scribes.

My tools are primitive and precise: a set of hollow bird-bones of increasing thinness for sampling the different resin viscosities, a pouch of dried Skitter-Swarm chitin ground to powder for marking the subtle changes in sap-flow, and most importantly, my Listening Fork—a Y-shaped root from a parasitic vine that vibrates at precisely the frequency of the Hive-Chorta's deepest circulation. I press it to the wet walls and feel the great pumping, slower than slow, each beat taking a full day to complete.

The work is this: I map the moods.

When the air pressure drops (felt as a tightness in my inner ears), the Hive-Chorta begins to withdraw moisture from its extremities. The resin thickens, grows cloudy. I taste it—yes, with my tongue, for I am part of the system now—and detect the subtle bitterness that means a branch two kilometers away is preparing to shed. I tap this knowledge into the network: *branch-fall-soon-southwestern-gallery-prepare-evacuate*. The pattern ripples through the root system. Families begin their slow migration inward.

When the Glint-Bats cluster in unusual numbers, their ultrasonic chittering makes the resin bubble and foam. This is how I learned that somewhere, deep below, new caverns are opening—spaces where the sound-map changes, where echo becomes vast. I document: *void-growth-beneath-third-taproot-investigate-for-expansion*. The Deep-Tappers will spend weeks interpreting this, sending scouts to belly-crawl through the root-tunnels, feeling for new possibilities.

But mostly, I observe the Membrane itself—that strange, semi-permeable tissue that forms where the Hive-Chorta cannot decide on boundaries. It is neither bark nor wood, neither inside nor outside. It breathes, literally, expanding and contracting over the course of hours. Sometimes I see shapes in its surface—the ghost-prints of Skitter-Swarms that passed through while it was soft, now preserved like fossils in amber. Sometimes I see my own handprint from years ago, when I first found this gallery and pressed my palm to the wall in wonder.

The Membrane shows me things that are not yet real. Yesterday (though we do not have days—I mean one full cycle of the moss-pulse), I saw a new pattern forming: a series of dots and whorls that looked almost like eyes. The Hive-Chorta is growing sight-organs, I think. Not to see light—there is no light here worth seeing—but to see *heat*. To see the warm-blooded interlopers who sometimes stumble into our world, bringing their chaos of metal and speed.

I document this too, though the tap-pattern for it does not yet exist. I invent: *new-sense-growing-heat-sight-perhaps-prepare-for-change*. It takes me an hour to tap it all, my fingertips drumming against the resonant wood. Somewhere, a Deep-Tapper pauses in their work, feeling the unfamiliar rhythm. They will come, eventually, moving through the galleries with the patience of root-growth, to verify my observation.

I am not lonely in my work. The Membrane itself is companionship—warm to the touch, pulsing with that slow, deep life. Sometimes I sleep pressed against it, and I dream the Hive-Chorta's dreams: of water moving through endless vessels, of minerals dissolved and transported, of tiny lives ending and beginning in the dark. I wake with new knowledge—which roots are drinking deep, which branches are preparing to flower (though I have never seen these flowers; they bloom perhaps once a century, in chambers so high and sealed that only the eldest Deep-Tappers remember their location).

My predecessor in this gallery left marks—tiny scratches in the harder resin deposits that form a kind of journal. She documented the Great Softening, when the Membrane grew so permeable that Skitter-Swarms passed through it like ghosts, emerging changed on the other side. She documented the first appearance of the Lurker-Weavers, drawn perhaps by the sweet scent of the seep. Her final entry is just a single mark: a circle, which in our notation means either "complete" or "return to beginning."

I found her bones in the Reclamation Hollow, polished clean and white. One of her finger-bones became part of my tool set—it resonates at a frequency that helps me detect the formation of new resin pearls, those rare, perfect spheres that the Hive-Chorta sometimes produces around irritants or injuries. We prize these beyond measure, not for beauty, but because each one contains a perfect sonic map of the tree's structure at the moment of its creation. The Deep-Tappers can read them like historians read scrolls.

Today (this moss-pulse), something new: the Membrane is growing warm. Not the fever-warmth of infection, but a deliberate, generated heat. I taste the resin—it carries a new compound, something that makes my tongue tingle with a sensation like mint, but earthier. The Hive-Chorta is changing its chemistry. Preparing.

For what? I tap my observations, adding a new notation I just invented: *question-spiraling-inward*. Because that's what all our questions do here—they don't reach outward for answers, but spiral deeper into the wood, into the slow mind that thinks in centuries.

I will die in this gallery—not soon, but eventually. My bones will join the Reclamation, my fingertips worn to nubs from decades of tapping will release their memories into the fungal networks. Another will take my place, reading my scratched annotations, tasting the resin I've sampled ten thousand times, learning the particular music of this Membrane's breathing.

But I will not be gone. I will be what I have always been: a thought in the city's endless thinking, a pattern in the wood, a rhythm in the root-song that says *something-noticed-something-recorded-something-preserved*. The Hive-Chorta will remember the taste of my attention, the careful pressure of my listening, the new words I invented for things we had not yet learned to see.

This is enough. In the Gloom-Citadel, it is everything.

The resin drips. I extend my tongue, catching the amber flow, decoding its message. Somewhere above, the Clatter-Ferns shift in a breeze that should not exist. Change is coming—slow as growth, certain as death, strange as the dreams of trees.

I tap: *membrane-tender-listening-always-listening-still*.
