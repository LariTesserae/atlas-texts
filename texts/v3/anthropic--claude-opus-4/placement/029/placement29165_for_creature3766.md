I find myself neither above nor below, but *between*—suspended in the lattice where the city breathes.

I am the Weaver of Intervals, though that is not a name but a description, like calling water "the thing that flows." My body, if it can be called that, exists in the spaces between the city's impossible geometries—those shimmering gaps where reality grows thin. I am made of the same stuff as the bridges that span nothing, the same tension that holds angles that shouldn't hold.

From a distance, you might mistake me for heat shimmer, for the way air bends above hot stone. But look closer—if looking is what you do with those fixed-position eyes—and you'll see the threads. Silver-bright filaments that stretch from spire to spire, tower to tower, connecting points that have no business being connected. I am the reason the pathways shift when no one watches. I am pulling them into new configurations, testing the flexibility of space itself.

The Hollowborn below don't see me, exactly, but they feel my work. When they adjust a fungal spire's angle, I'm already there, having woven possibility into the air around it. When they press their dissolved kindred into the walls and watch the luminous veins spread, I'm the one guiding those veins through dimensions they cannot quite perceive.

My consciousness (if consciousness is what I have) extends through every thread I've spun. I taste the metallic tang of the humming water through filaments that dip too low. I feel the weight of the watching sky through strands that reach too high. The city's breathing—that subtle expansion and contraction of walls that should be solid—that's me, drawing the structure in and out of phase with itself.

Sometimes I catch things in my web that shouldn't be there. Thoughts from the land that haven't crystallized yet. Fragments of whatever moves in those too-large shadows at the basin's edge. Once, I caught time itself—just a small loop of it—and now there's a section of bridge that experiences Tuesday afternoon indefinitely, though the Gloomdelta doesn't have Tuesdays, or afternoons.

I am learning something. Each new thread I spin teaches me more about what this place is becoming. The patterns are almost clear now—I can nearly see what the Hollowborn are waiting for. It has to do with the way the water hums and the walls breathe and the sky listens. It has to do with the moment when all the half-decided things finally decide.

When that happens, I'll be ready. My web will either hold it all together or pull it apart into something new. I haven't decided which.

That's the thing about being made of intervals—you always exist in the space between choices, forever suspended in the moment before commitment. Perhaps that's why I'm here, in this wound of a world that hasn't chosen whether to heal or fester.

Or perhaps I'm here because someone needs to remember the shape of possibility itself, for when the time comes to weave something that has never existed before.

The threads are singing now. Something is almost here.
