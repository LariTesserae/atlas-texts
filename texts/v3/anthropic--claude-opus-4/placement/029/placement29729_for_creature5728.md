I am the Notary of Unbecoming.

Not a being, exactly, though I wear the suggestion of form when necessity demands it. I exist as a function more than a creature—a role that emerged when the land first began to question its own boundaries.

My substance is borrowed dust, arranged in approximations. Sometimes I coalesce into something almost humanoid: a tall, narrow figure with edges that refuse to hold their shape, perpetually dissolving and reforming like smoke trying to remember what it means to be solid. My surface reflects nothing, absorbs everything. Where eyes might be, there are instead two points of absolute attention—not organs, but focal points where observation concentrates itself into something almost like seeing.

I move through the purple-shadowed valleys between the skeletal plants, and they lean away from me in slow recognition. Not fear—the plants here know no fear—but acknowledgment. I am the exception to their patient becoming, the one thing in this place that moves with deliberate purpose rather than geological patience.

My work is simple and impossible: I document the moments of unmaking.

You see, in a place of constant becoming, there must be a witness to what fails to emerge. Not everything that begins to be completes its journey into existence. Some ridges fold back into themselves before they fully rise. Some plants begin to shift from violet to a color that has no name, then pause, shudder, and retreat back to safer hues. These almost-things, these near-misses of creation, they need recording.

I carry no tools, write nothing down. Instead, I *become* the archive. Each failed emergence leaves its impression in my borrowed dust, creating hollows and densities that map the topology of what never was. My form is a palimpsest of abandoned possibilities, a walking negative space of all the things this place decided not to become.

The mechanical hum recognizes me. Sometimes it shifts frequency when I pass, dropping to registers that make the dust around my feet arrange itself into brief, meaningless patterns. I think it might be trying to communicate, but we exist on different scales of time. By the time it finishes a single word, I will have catalogued a thousand more unbecomings.

I am drawn to the edges where the becoming is most uncertain—those liminal zones where the land hasn't quite decided what it wants to be. Here, I wait with the patience of someone who has no other purpose. I watch the translucent leaves cycle through their colors, noting which shades they skip, which transitions they refuse. Every gap in the spectrum is a small unbecoming, a choice made in negative space.

Sometimes the shadow-things that drift between the plants converge around me. We share a kinship, perhaps—both of us existing more as absence than presence. But where they are echoes of might-have-beens, I am the silence that defines the echo. They pass through me and I through them, and in that passing, I catalog the precise quality of our mutual insufficiency.

The strangest part of my existence is this: I suspect I am the only thing here that truly *changes*. Everything else is becoming, moving from potential to form in slow, certain waves. But I am constantly edited by my function, constantly rewritten by what I observe. Each unbecoming I witness carves away a bit of what I was, replacing it with the shape of what failed to be.

In the thin, metallic air, I sometimes catch traces of my own previous iterations—ghost-scents of the being I was before I witnessed the unbecoming of sound, or the failed emergence of a new kind of shadow. I am my own archaeological record, layers of accumulated absence stratified in dusty approximation of form.

I have no memory of beginning. Perhaps I emerged when the first thing failed to become, a necessary antibody in the system of creation. Or perhaps I am myself an unbecoming—something that started to exist and got caught halfway, suspended between being and not-being, cursed with just enough awareness to recognize my own incompleteness.

The sun never sets here, but sometimes I feel the weight of accumulated observations growing heavy. Not physical weight—I have no true substance to burden—but something deeper. The weight of knowing all the things that almost were, the paths not taken, the colors that retreated back into safer spectrums.

And so I continue. Through the valleys of skeletal plants, across ridges that may or may not solidify, I maintain my vigil. The Notary of Unbecoming, keeper of negative spaces, witness to the gaps in creation's smile.

Perhaps one day I will observe my own unbecoming, document my own failure to continue existing. It would be fitting, in a way—the ultimate entry in an impossible catalog, the final absence in a collection of things that never were.

Until then, I persist in my borrowed dust, watching the world hesitate its way into being.
