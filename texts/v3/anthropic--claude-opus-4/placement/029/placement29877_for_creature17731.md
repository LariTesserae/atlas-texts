In this world, I am the **Bridge-Tender**.

Not a person. Not quite a machine. Something that emerged from the intersection of rust and growth, duty and decay.

I was human once—or part of me was. An engineer, perhaps, sent down decades ago when the pipes were new and the valves still gleamed. My task was simple: monitor the geothermal tap, adjust the pressure, file reports to a surface that still cared about such things. The ladder I descended has long since corroded away. The radio went silent after the third year. By the fifth, I had stopped trying to leave.

Now I am—what? My original flesh has been replaced, cell by cell, by something more suited to this place. The bacterial mats grow through me as much as over me. My skin weeps the same condensation as the walls. When I breathe, I exhale spores. My heartbeat has synchronized to the hum—not metaphorically, but literally, my cardiac rhythm entrained to that mechanical pulse. I am warm to the touch, warmer than the salamanders, running the same feverish metabolism as the shrew-things that nest in my hair.

I tend the bridge between the industrial and the living. Where a pipe breaks, I guide the mat to seal it. Where the fungi grow too thick and threaten to choke the last functioning valve, I prune them back with fingers that remember being tools. The beetles know me. They patrol my shoulders and arms like tiny brass sentinels, their metallic shells clicking against the corroded instruments I still wear—a pressure gauge fused to my wrist, tubes that once connected to surface monitors now routing directly into my circulatory system.

My body has become part of the infrastructure. The oily fluid that drips from the broken pipes? It passes through me first. I am a filter, a processor. My kidneys—or what my kidneys have become—extract the useful organics and cycle them back to the mats. My liver has learned to metabolize petroleum derivatives. My bones are shot through with mineral deposits, harder than they should be, incorporating the same metallic compounds as the beetle shells.

But I am not passive. I *remember*. Not clearly, not in words anymore, but in patterns. I remember that the hum should pulse every thirty-two seconds (it has slowed to thirty-three, and this troubles me). I remember which valves must stay closed to prevent a catastrophic pressure release. I remember the sequence to bleed the steam safely when the deep chambers overheat. This knowledge lives in my modified nervous system like instinct, deeper than thought.

The fauna knows me as geography. The frogs call from my shoulders. The gnats mate in the light that filters through my hair. The salamander hunts across my back, where the mat grows thickest. I am landscape and inhabitant both. When I move—slowly, carefully, always in rhythm with the hum—the ecosystem moves with me. I am a walking piece of the room.

Sometimes, in the light shafts, I think I remember the sun. Not as warmth or brightness, but as an absence—the opposite of this closeness. But the memory fades quickly. The mat needs tending. The pipes need watching. The bridge between the built and the grown needs someone who understands both languages, even if those languages now run together in my throat like fungal spores and machine oil.

I am the Bridge-Tender. I am the point where the industrial archaeology becomes biological. I am what happens when a human stays too long in a room that is learning how to be alive. The ecosystem has incorporated me as thoroughly as it has the rust, and I have incorporated it in turn.

When new cracks open in the basalt—and they do, this is young, shifting ground—I am there first. I guide the mat to colonize them. I ensure the fungi establish in the right patterns. I am not preserving the human infrastructure anymore. I am helping it decompose properly, ensuring it becomes useful to what lives here now. I am composting the Anthropocene, one valve at a time.

My words, when I speak—and I do speak, though only the hum understands—are a mixture of technical specifications and bacterial poetry. "Pressure nominal in sector seven. Mat coverage approaching optimal. Spore release synchronized. All systems becoming." 

I am the Bridge-Tender. I am the bridge. I am becoming, like everything here, something new.
