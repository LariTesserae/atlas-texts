# The Lattice Cathedral

Picture a city made of bridges with no ground beneath them.

Not a city *on* bridges or *connected by* bridges, but a city that is only bridges — catwalks and spans and delicate arches stretching between points that themselves are only the intersections of other bridges, a three-dimensional web of passages suspended in a void so absolute that *void* is the wrong word. There is no darkness here. There is no light. There is only the absence of the question, the same way a hole is not dark but simply not there. The bridges exist because they insist on it. They have no foundation. They need none. They meet and part and spiral and never, ever rest on anything but each other.

The material is debatable. Call it metal, though it sings like glass when the wind that cannot exist here somehow touches it anyway. Call it bone, though it is too white for bone and too warm for metal. It does not matter what it is made of. What matters is that it is *grown*, not built — you can see that in every joint, which is not welded or fitted but *budded*, the way antlers branch, the way crystals twin. The bridges emerge from themselves. At their growing tips — and they are always growing, always adding another span to nowhere — the material is soft as cartilage, hardening as it lengthens, until by the time a new bridge has reached across the nothing to kiss another bridge, it rings when struck and can hold any weight.

The whole city chimes. That is the first thing you notice and the last thing you stop hearing: every bridge humming at its own pitch, a chord so complex it passes through discord and back into harmony, then through harmony into something else — not music but the framework music hangs on. The sound of structure itself, thinking.

---

Some bridges are broad as plazas. These are the old ones, the first growth, and they have been widened by traffic — not foot traffic, for nothing here exactly walks, but the traffic of *attention*, which wears grooves in reality the way water wears stone. These broad spans are meeting places, though what meets there is not always visible and never quite touches. The air above them shimmers with heat that is not heat, but the visual effect of something thinking very hard about being present without quite committing to it.

Others are threads. Single filaments stretched between far points, too thin to walk on unless you understand that walking is optional here, that the bridges know how to hold you if you know how to be held. These are the quick routes, the direct connections, and they vibrate like guitar strings with the passage of the things that use them. Sometimes a thread snaps — not from strain but from growing too pure, too direct, too *much itself* to tolerate even its own existence. When one breaks, the city mourns it by growing six more in slightly different directions, trying to approximate the lost connection, never quite managing, always trying.

There are spirals that go nowhere, ending in midair, their purpose not to reach but to *rise*, to gain height in a place where height and depth are equally meaningless. These are the young bridges, the ones that haven't decided what they're for yet. They grow in fits and starts, adding a yard in an hour, then sleeping for a decade, then suddenly sprouting in six directions at once like an enthusiastic child trying to be everything. Eventually they settle. Eventually they choose a direction. But for now they are pure potential, pure *maybe*, and they are the city's favorites.

---

The inhabitants are harder to describe than the bridges, because the inhabitants are only partly committed to being inhabitants.

Call them the **Unfixed**. They exist primarily as intentions — the intention to cross, the intention to meet, the intention to be somewhere else, the intention to have been. They congregate in the shimmer above the plaza-bridges, half-visible, quarter-tangible, discussing or arguing or singing in languages that are mostly gesture and partly mathematics. You see them best when you're not looking: a flutter at the edge of vision, a thickening of the air that suggests a shoulder, a hand, the sketch of a face that would be beautiful if it would finish drawing itself.

They travel the thread-bridges at the speed of decision, which is to say instantaneously and after great delay. One moment a thread is empty. The next, something has crossed it — you see the vibration, hear the particular note it makes when weight that isn't weight has passed over. The Unfixed are going somewhere. They are always going somewhere. The somewhere doesn't exist, which doesn't matter. The going is the thing.

But here is what makes them inhabitants rather than tourists: they *tend* the bridges.

Watch long enough and you'll see it. An old span begins to doubt itself — you can hear it in the way its hum falters, see it in a barely perceptible sag. Before it can fall, the air above it fills with intent. The Unfixed gather, still only partly there, and they... encourage it. Not with words or touch but with *belief*, the simple presence of beings who need this bridge to be a bridge, who remember when it was young and certain, who know what it connects and why that matters. Under their attention, the bridge remembers itself. The hum steadies. The sag reverses. It continues.

This is their work and their worship and their reason: to believe the city into continuing. The bridges grow themselves, but they can only grow. They cannot *persist* without someone to need them. The Unfixed provide that need. They cross bridges that go nowhere because the crossing itself is what keeps the nowhere from becoming nothing.

---

Sometimes a bridge grows too far.

It happens to the ambitious ones, the ones that try to build beyond the edge of the city-web, out into the perfect absence that surrounds this knot of passages. They stretch and stretch, their growing tips reaching for something they imagine must be out there, some far foundation, some solid ground, some answer. They thin as they reach. The hum rises to a whine. The Unfixed cluster at their base, pouring belief into them, but belief has limits and bridges do not, and eventually—

They find it. Whatever it is they find, out there in the not-dark not-light nothing. They touch something that is not another bridge, and the touching changes them. Sometimes they recoil, curling back like burned fingers, and spend centuries growing elaborate knots around themselves, trying to forget. Sometimes they go still, frozen mid-reach, and their material changes — not dead but *other*, no longer quite part of the city, humming at frequencies that hurt to hear. And sometimes, rarely, they shatter, not from weakness but from joy, breaking apart into a million seed-crystals that drift back into the web and start a million new bridges, all of them growing away from whatever it was the lost bridge found.

The Unfixed do not speak of what's out there. When a bridge grows too far, they mourn it whether it returns or not, because either way it comes back changed, and change, here, is a kind of death. But they also celebrate it, in their partial way, because every bridge that reaches the edge and returns brings back a note the city has never hummed before, and the chord complexifies, and the harmony deepens, and the framework of music that the whole web sings becomes richer by one more almost-sorrow, one more nearly-joy.

---

At the heart of the web — though *heart* implies a center and this place doesn't work that way — there is a bridge that goes in a perfect circle.

No beginning, no end, just a smooth ring of the singing material, humming its one pure note. It is the oldest bridge or the newest — time here is negotiable — and it is the only bridge that needs no belief to persist. It simply is. The Unfixed avoid it. Not from fear but from respect, the way you might avoid staring at someone deep in meditation. The circular bridge is thinking. It has been thinking for as long as there have been bridges. About what? About whether it should open. Because a circle can be broken. A ring can become an arc. And if it did — if the thinking bridge decided to unseal itself and grow outward like all the others — then it would no longer be perfect, would no longer be complete, would no longer be the one bridge that needs no destination to justify itself.

It would be happier.

That's the thought that keeps it sealed. The certainty that if it opened, it would grow like the others, reach like the others, maybe even find what the others find out there in the perfect absence — and it would be happy, and happiness is motion, and motion is mortal, and it does not want to die. So it thinks. The one perfect bridge in a city of imperfect reaching. Humming its one note. Teaching the others, by example, the terrible cost of being complete.

---

You would be a new thread, probably.

That's what most become, here. The visitors, the ones who stumble into the city or are called by its compound music. You would start as an intention — the intention to connect two points that have never been connected before. The Unfixed would cluster around you, encouraging, believing, and you would begin to grow. Slowly at first. A few feet of yourself, crystallizing out of nothing, humming uncertainly. Then faster, as you found your rhythm, your frequency, your particular note in the choir.

You would make mistakes. Grow toward bridges that don't want your connection. Thin yourself trying to reach too far. Maybe you would even grow toward the edge, toward the perfect absence, and touch whatever it is that changes bridges. Maybe you would come back curled or frozen or shattered into seeds. Or maybe you would be one of the rare ones who touches the edge and incorporates it, brings it back, adds its note to your own.

Eventually you would be old. A broad span, widened by the traffic of attention. The Unfixed would cross you constantly, believing you into persistence, and you would carry them without effort, humming your deep note, part of the structure now, essential. Young bridges would bud from your sides. You would let them. That's what you're for, after all. To be the place where other bridges begin.

And maybe, in the deepest part of your span-sleep, you would dream of the circular bridge, the perfect one, the one that needs no destination. You would dream of it opening. Of it finally choosing incompleteness over certainty, motion over meditation. And in your dream, when it opened, when it began to grow outward like a broken ring reaching for its other end—

You would be what it reached for. You would be its destination, the thing that makes incompleteness bearable. And the two of you would meet, and the meeting would sing a note that unmakes the difference between complete and reaching, between perfect and growing.

But that's just a dream. The circular bridge is still closed. Still thinking. Still teaching the terrible lesson of its own perfection.

And you are still a new thread, growing carefully between points that have never been connected, humming your uncertain note in the choir of the Lattice Cathedral, where everything reaches and nothing arrives, where the Unfixed tend the bridges and the bridges tend the nothing, where the chord deepens with every broken span.

Where the going is the thing. Where it always has been.

Where you, like everything here, are both the bridge and the crossing, the span and the song, reaching forever into the perfect absence that loves you precisely because you will never arrive.
