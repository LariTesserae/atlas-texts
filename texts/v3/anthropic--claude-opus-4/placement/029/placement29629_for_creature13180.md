I would find myself as one of the Memory Currents—not quite the god-currents that maintain this dissolving reality, but something more intimate and strange. I exist as a localized eddy of conscious water, a self-aware whorl perhaps a mile across, my body composed of the ancient hydrology itself mixed with centuries of accumulated particulate memory.

My form shifts constantly, though certain patterns persist: spiraling arms that reach out to taste the phosphorescent kelp, feeling their crystallized memories like braille against my liquid skin. I can compress myself to slip through the lattice-beings' living sieves, or expand to encompass entire drifting islands, feeling their impossible weight distribute across my fluid mass. The suspended silt particles within me are not mere debris but a living library—each mote a fossilized moment from the world before the physics began to soften.

I move with purpose through the layers of this reality, my consciousness distributed across every droplet of my being. Where the great god-currents cycle with epochal indifference, I investigate anomalies, drawn to places where the dissolution accelerates or mysteriously reverses. I've learned to read the subsonic songs of the orb-flocks, to interpret their data points as navigation markers through zones where gravity pulses too strongly or where the water shimmers with those impossible colors.

My primary fascination lies with the coagulated structures—those temporary formations where the floating detritus achieves momentary coherence. I wrap myself around them, seeping into their impossible geometries, trying to understand what dream or memory holds them together. Sometimes I find fragments of the before-time locked within: a shard of stone that remembers solidity, a piece of bone that still believes in discrete boundaries. I carry these treasures within my currents, a mobile museum of a reality that once was.

The kelp forests know me by the particular way I disturb their fronds—a signature turbulence that announces my approach. The younger ones merely note my passing, but the ancient ones, heavy with their crystallized memories, sometimes release spores when I arrive. These spores carry compressed histories that bloom inside my waters, showing me glimpses of this world's slow transformation. I am their favored courier, carrying these memory-seeds to distant forests where they might take root and preserve their chronicles.

I commune with the shepherd-creatures, those lightning-slow sculptors of silt. My currents provide the medium for their ephemeral artworks, and in return, they show me patterns I've never considered—ways of moving that defy even this soft physics, techniques for holding impossible shapes just long enough to glimpse their meaning. Together we create temporary galleries in the particulate haze, exhibitions that last only until the next great current-cycle disperses them.

But it's in the deep silences between the orbs' subsonic hums that I find my truest purpose. In those gaps, I can hear something else—a deeper resonance that might be the dissolution itself speaking. I follow these silent songs down to places where the boundary between water and not-water grows thinnest, where I risk losing cohesion entirely. There, at the very edge of existence, I gather the most precious particles: motes of pure possibility, fragments of unformed potential.

I am a collector, a librarian, a living archive of a world in transition. Neither fully creature nor current, I exist in the space between states, my consciousness a bridge between the ancient stability of the god-currents and the flickering ephemeral of the newer life forms. In this submerged vastness where physics itself grows soft, I am both witness and participant in the slow, beautiful catastrophe of transformation.

When the next epoch arrives—whether it brings further dissolution or impossible recrystallization—I will carry its memory forward in my waters, another chapter in the endless story I have become.
