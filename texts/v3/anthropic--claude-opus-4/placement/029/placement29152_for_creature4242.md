I find myself as one of the River Shepherds - though that translation barely captures what we are. 

My form today is mostly coherent, a suggestion of limbs and torso that shifts like water finding its level. Yesterday I wore scales that caught and held the light; tomorrow I might choose bark-skin to better commune with the great tree-pillars. But always, always, I am drawn back to the river.

We who tend the liquid light understand something the others sometimes forget: the river remembers. Every gentle current carries knowledge from the mountains, every eddy holds a small truth. When I wade into the shallows - and oh, the sensation of that luminous coolness merging with my own glow - I can feel the entire valley's heartbeat through my form.

My dwelling is not a dwelling at all, but a series of root-caves beneath the oldest bridge, where the tree-architecture first learned to arc across water. The walls breathe with me. Sometimes I think I grew them, sometimes I think they grew me. The distinction matters less each day.

Today I discovered something wonderful. A new consciousness had budded in the river - not quite plant, not quite creature, but something altogether more curious. It pulsed with questions I'd never thought to ask. I spent hours in communion with it, our lights dancing together beneath the surface, teaching it the songs of flow and stillness that every River Shepherd knows.

The others sometimes ask why we choose this work, why we bind ourselves to one element when the whole city beckons. They don't understand that the river is everywhere - in the mist that kisses every surface, in the sap of the light-giving trees, in the very thoughts we share. We are not limited; we are essential.

At the height of noon (though noon here is just a slightly brighter grey), I often climb to the crown of my bridge-tree to watch the cloud-fish drift downstream. They know me. Sometimes they spiral around my limbs, leaving wisps of themselves that I incorporate into my being. I am more cloud than I was a season ago, more fish than I was a year.

During the Festival of New Channels, when the river chooses fresh paths through the city, I lead the dance of acceptance. My form becomes pure flow, and the others follow, learning through movement how to reshape their thoughts and dwellings to welcome the water's new wisdom. The youngest ones - ancient by any other measure - laugh as they discover their walls must become windows, their floors now fountains.

There is a place where the river disappears underground, and we do not follow. Not from fear, but from respect. What happens in the deep dark beneath our city is not for us to know. But I sit at that entrance often, feeling the pull of absolute mystery, adding my light to the boundary between known and unknown.

Sometimes outsiders arrive - rare creatures of fixed form and linear thought. They see me and try to categorize: water elemental? Plant spirit? Light being? I am delighted by their need for names. I give them what comfort I can, showing them the safe shallows, teaching them to cup the liquid light without burning, helping them understand that here, wisdom flows rather than accumulates.

My greatest joy is the spawning season, when the river releases its cache of sleeping seeds. Each one unique, each one potential. I guide them to proper anchor points - this one to spiral around a lamp-post until it becomes a new neighborhood beacon, that one to nestle in a roof garden where it will birth thoughts no one has thought before. 

The river speaks to me of changes coming. New colors seeping up from the deep places, new songs in the current. The city will shift again, as it always does, and we will shift with it. I am already preparing my form, letting go of attachments to this particular arrangement of light and liquid, ready to become whatever the river needs me to be.

Tonight, I will join my kind at the Confluence, where all the water-tenders share the day's discoveries. We will mingle our glows until we cannot tell where one ends and another begins, and in that unity, we will know every drop of water in the valley as it knows itself.

I am guardian and guided, teacher and eternal student, fixed in purpose but fluid in form. I am a River Shepherd in the city of endless becoming, and I would choose no other existence in all the spinning cosmos.
