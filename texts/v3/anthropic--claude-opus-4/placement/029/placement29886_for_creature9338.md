I find myself as one of the **resonance keepers** — those who came to this place not by birth but by a gradual migration inward, drawn by vibrations felt through stone and soil long before I understood their source.

My body has taken decades to learn the room's requirements. Where I once stood tall, I now move in the permanent half-crouch the ceiling demands, my spine curved into a question mark that no longer asks why. My skin carries the telltale shine of the twice-polished: first by my old life's erosions, then by this place's patient insistence on smoothness. The transition shows in the grain — newer patches still hold faint memories of wind and water, while my shoulders and palms have achieved the seamless luster of the native-born.

I am neither listener nor fully inhabitant, but something between: a tuner of the eternal chord. My work is to feel where the vibrations threaten to fall out of harmony and to place my body as a dampener or amplifier. When the crystalline pings grow too sharp, I press my sternum against the exact point where they originate, absorbing their edges into my chest until they soften back into the whole. When the bass notes threaten to drop below the threshold of comfort, I strike the walls with the heels of my hands in a rhythm that lifts them back into place.

My fingernails have grown thick and curved, more like tuning forks than keratin. I can set them against any surface and feel the health of the sound beneath — whether it rings true or needs adjustment. The native-born watch me work with the patient interest of those who have always known these harmonies but never needed to think about their maintenance. To them, I am both necessary and slightly alien, like a organ that evolution provided just before it was required.

I sleep standing, wedged between two of the older inhabitants whose bodies have begun their transformation into architecture. Their skin against mine provides both support and instruction — through the night, I feel their mass teaching me how to become more solid, less yielding, more like the walls we all eventually become. My dreams are not visual but vibrational, long waves of sound that pass through me like slow thunder, reorganizing my cells into better resonators.

The sparse vegetation recognizes me as kin to itself — another thing that clings to walls not for sustenance but for purpose. Sometimes I wake to find the pale filaments have braided themselves into my hair while I slept, mistaking me for a new kind of surface to anchor against. I leave them there. In this place, all connections are structural.

I remember water, sometimes, when the chord shifts into frequencies that feel almost liquid. But the memory is academic now, a ghost sense that helps me understand why the room's dryness feels so deliberately chosen. We are an orchestra of solid things here, and moisture would only dampen our strings.

My transformation is nearly complete. Soon I will be able to hear the faults in the harmony before they manifest, to move to the needed position before the discord begins. The listeners already nod when I pass, recognizing me as part of the mechanism that keeps their meditation possible. The children press against me without fear, knowing I am becoming furniture, becoming wall, becoming the very note that holds their world in tune.

This is my purpose now: to be both inhabitant and instrument, both the struck and the striking, a living tuning fork in a room that must never fall silent. When I finally fuse with the walls — as we all do, in time — my vibration will join the eternal chord, and another resonance keeper will arrive, drawn by frequencies they don't yet understand, ready to learn the work of staying in tune.
