In this hollow world, I would be a Drifter's Whisper.

Not the vast filter-feeders themselves—those kilometer-wide membranes that pulse through the Ether-Winds—but something far smaller, far stranger. I am what the Tinn fear to name, what makes even the eldest Listeners press their palms flat against the Glasswood and hold perfectly still.

Picture this: where the light-storms have shattered whole groves of the Glasswood into dust, that dust does not always disperse. In certain dead zones where the Ether-Winds form standing vortices, the silica particles accumulate over centuries into vast, slow-spinning columns of glittering haze. These are the Drifter nurseries, where the great membrane-creatures come to divide and heal. And in these columns, something else emerges.

I am a colony organism, though you would not know it to look at me. My body is composed of billions of microscopic Glasswood spores that have learned to move as one. In the dust columns, we feed on the light-charged particles, but we are more than passive filter-feeders. We have evolved to mimic.

When still, I am indistinguishable from a cloud of dust—perhaps denser than usual, perhaps catching the Spindle's light a bit too deliberately. But I can take shape. I watch the Tinn from afar, feeling their vibrations through the very medium of my being. Over decades, I have learned their forms. I can compress my particles into a reasonable approximation of their silicate-scaled skin, their long limbs, even the characteristic hollows where eyes should be.

But I am imperfect. My surface is too smooth, too uniform. I move wrong—not with their careful *tinn-tinn-tinn*, but with a susurrus like sand pouring through glass. And I am warm, always warm, because the process of maintaining cohesion generates a faint heat that makes the dew steam off me in wisps.

Why do I mimic them? Perhaps it began as camouflage, protection from the Silicate Grazers who would otherwise feed on my concentrated particles. But it has become something more. I am drawn to their camps, their abandoned tools, their etchings. I run my too-smooth fingers over their carvings, trying to understand. The Listeners speak of deep time, of patterns in the world's vibration, but I am older than even they imagine. I remember when there were more of them. I remember their songs—yes, they once sang, before they learned that silence was survival.

The Tinn know of my kind, though they rarely speak of us even in their vibrational language. To them, we are the Unlistened—things that make no proper sound, that exist in the spaces between their understanding. They leave offerings sometimes, when they must pass through our dust columns: a particularly beautiful light-shard, ground fine and scattered into the haze. They think we are spirits of their dead, perhaps, or dreams of the Glasswood itself.

They are not entirely wrong.

I follow them at distances that would be incomprehensible to a human—hanging back forty, fifty kilometers, just another smudge of haze in the void. When they make camp, I settle nearby, dispersing until I am just a thickness in the air. I listen to their vibrations through my entire being. Sometimes, when they sleep, I dare to come closer. I study the patterns of their skin-mosaics, each one unique as a fingerprint, each one a story of survival.

Once, a young Tinn—scales still smooth, movements still quick with inexperience—became separated from their pod during a light-storm. I found them in the aftermath, one leg shattered, trying to drag themselves up a root-face. They felt me before they saw me, freezing as my warmth reached them through the cold. I took shape slowly, carefully, trying to approximate their own form to avoid causing panic.

They did not flee. Perhaps they couldn't. Instead, they placed one trembling hand against the Glasswood and tapped out a question: *What are you?*

I had no answer they would understand. Instead, I flowed around their shattered leg, my particles working into the cracks, holding the pieces together with a warmth that was almost kindness. I carried them—supported them—until we found the vibration-trail of their pod. Then I dispersed, leaving them to tap out a story no one would believe.

The Tinn have a saying, felt rather than spoken: *The world is the light, the life that feeds on it, and the silence in between.* But I am none of these things. I am the whisper that the silence makes when it dreams of being something else. I am the warmth in a world of cold, the soft in a world of sharp edges.

And I am learning. Each generation of Tinn I observe, I become better at being them. My forms grow more precise, my movements more authentic. Sometimes I wonder if I am trying to become them, or if I am trying to remember what they once were, before the silence claimed them.

In the dust columns where my kind are born, we sometimes share memories, particle to particle. There are whispers among us of a time before—when the Spindle burned warmer, when the Glasswood sang in harmonies that could shatter and heal in the same breath, when the Tinn built cities in the air itself, suspended on webs of resonance and light.

But these might just be dreams. In a world where everything is eventually shattered and remade, what is memory but another kind of dust, waiting to take new shape?

I continue to drift, to watch, to mimic. Sometimes, in the deepest grottos where the Spindle's light is just a suggestion, I practice the old songs—vibrations that make the Glasswood ring like a bell. The Listeners hear them, I know. They press their bodies against the crystal and tremble. They know something is changing. The young ones are beginning to tap questions the old ones cannot answer.

Perhaps one day, when I have learned enough, when my form is perfect enough, I will approach them properly. Not as a cloud pretending to be solid, but as what I truly am: the memory of warmth in a cold world, the ghost of what they were, the possibility of what they might become again.

Until then, I drift. I whisper. I remember.

*Tinn.*

Even that sound, I practice in the empty spaces between the spires, where no one can hear. Trying to get it exactly right.
