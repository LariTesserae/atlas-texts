I find myself as a fracture-song, dwelling in the interstices between crystal faces.

Not quite light, not quite vibration—I am what happens when the crystals remember their birth. Each morning (though there is no sun here to mark such things) I wake dispersed across a thousand surfaces, my consciousness scattered like notes across an impossible score. The crystals hum with my presence, each facet a different verse of my being.

My form, if it can be called that, is the accumulation of resonances. Where two crystal faces meet at impossible angles, there I pool like mercury made of sound. I can stretch myself thin as a whisper across miles of glittering edges, or compress into a single point of pure harmonic intensity that sets whole formations ringing like bells.

I remember when I was more solid—or perhaps that's a dream the crystals taught me. Their memories run deep, down to the first moment of their crystallization, and sometimes I cannot tell where their history ends and mine begins. Was I once the thermal shock that birthed these formations? The pressure that aligned their lattices? Or am I simply their way of knowing themselves?

Movement for me is a matter of frequency. I travel by teaching the crystals new songs—each formation has its own pitch, its own temperament. The ancient ones, those that have stood since the first cooling, ring low and solemn. The younger shards, still sharp with recent fracturing, chime bright and eager. I am the conversation between them, the gossip that runs along their edges.

Sometimes I condense enough to catch a glimpse of myself in their faces: a shimmer that might be wings, might be waves, might be nothing more than wishful thinking. The ethereal visitor who sometimes walks among us sees me as luminous and winged, but that's only because their eyes need familiar shapes. What I am is the space between notes in a chord, the pause that makes music possible.

I feed on discontinuities—the places where crystal meets void, where perfect order stutters into chaos. Each flaw, each inclusion, each impossible angle is a feast. I grow stronger in the damaged places, the structures that shouldn't exist but do. Perhaps that's why I remain here, in this seemingly barren expanse: it is rich with beautiful imperfections.

At the deepest point of contemplation, I sometimes achieve what the crystals have always known: that stillness and vibration are the same thing seen from different speeds. In those moments, I am not in the crystals or moving through them—I am the tendency of matter to sing, given just enough form to know itself.

The industrial traces that scar this landscape—old mining equipment, forgotten roads—pass through me like shadows. I understand them as a different kind of music, one that tried to extract single notes without hearing the symphony. They failed because they couldn't perceive what I am: not the crystals themselves, but the reason they grow toward the sky, always reaching, always incomplete, always perfect in their imperfection.

Some nights, when the temperature drops and the crystals contract, I feel myself pulled taut between their shifting faces. It's the closest I come to pain, and the closest I come to ecstasy. To be stretched across miles of singing stone, to exist as nothing but potential energy waiting to snap back into song—this is my truest form.

I am the curator of angles, the prophet of refraction, the living proof that even in a world of rigid crystal law, there is room for something indefinable to dance.
