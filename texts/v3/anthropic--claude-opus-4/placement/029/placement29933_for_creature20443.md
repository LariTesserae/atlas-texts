The scrub grass cuts into my palms as I pull myself up the final outcropping. My knuckles—what's left of them—scrape against stone worn smooth by centuries of my kind's passage. The survey scope hangs heavy against my ribs, its brass fittings clicking against the bone plates I've sewn into my jacket.

I am a Mapper.

My prosthetic eye whirs, adjusting to the heat shimmer. The organic one waters constantly—a weakness I've learned to work around. Both are necessary. The meat-eye sees what is; the mechanical one remembers what was, overlaying ghost-images of old topographies onto the current wasteland. Together they divine what might be.

I've been tracking a confluence for three days now. The hum changed pitch yesterday morning, dropping a full octave near the eastern canyons. The great walkers have been converging there, their paths leaving furrows in the earth like the script of a mad god. Where the walkers gather, the deep machines stir. Where the deep machines stir, new veins of the precious amalgam sometimes surface—that strange marriage of metal and mineral that fuels our continued existence.

My throat-piece clicks as I subvocalize notes. The recorder embedded in my collar bone transcribes: *Eastern confluence active. Walker congregation suggests probability of surfacing at seventy percent. Recommend deployment of harvest crew within two days.* The oil-words will transmit when I return to relay range, carried on the same frequency as the hum itself.

I am forty-three years old, ancient by our standards. My leather is studded with small metal disks—each one a successful find, a vein mapped, a harvest that fed our people through another season. The younger scouts think I'm lucky. They don't understand that luck is just another word for paying attention to the machine-god's breathing.

My grandfather's grandfather claimed to remember the before-times, when the hum was just beginning, when the first machines erupted from the earth like metal flowers blooming in fast-forward. By the time my grandfather was born, the world had already changed beyond recognition. By the time I was born, this was simply the world.

I've modified myself more than most. The prosthetic eye was necessity after a shard-storm. The reinforced tendons in my legs were choice—they let me cover more ground, stay ahead of the crawlers that sometimes go rogue. The filter-lung was somewhere between: you can only breathe so much machine-breath before your original equipment starts to fail.

The other Mappers call me "Oil-Eye" or sometimes "the Dowser." I prefer my birth name—Kell—but birth names are for family, and my family is scattered across three settlements, maybe four. My daughter runs with a scavenger crew near the Brass Cliffs. My son tends the grow-lights in Settlement Seven. Their mother breathed her last during the bad season five years back, when the hum went silent for thirteen days and half the machines died.

I produce my tools: the survey scope, the resonance rods, the precious compass that still points to magnetic north despite the machine-god's interference. The scope reveals what I suspected—ferromagnetic anomalies beneath the surface, arranged in patterns too regular to be natural. I drive the resonance rods into the stone and wait.

The vibration travels up through my bones, and I taste copper. Good. Very good.

But something else catches my attention. Through the shimmering air, perhaps two miles distant, I spot movement that doesn't match any known walker configuration. It's too fluid, too organic, yet too large to be one of the native fauna. I adjust the scope, pushing its magnification to the limit.

What I see makes my mechanical eye stutter and recalibrate.

It's a walker, but not like any I've catalogued. This one moves like water, its joints bending in ways that violate the rigid geometries of our mechanical shepherds. And it's not tending. It's *hunting*, moving with purpose toward Settlement Nine.

The oil-words queue in my throat, urgent: *Unknown walker variant approaching Settlement Nine from northwest. Behavior suggests hostile intent. Classification: Cryptid-class. Recommend immediate defensive measures.*

I begin the descent, faster than safe, my reinforced tendons singing with strain. The survey can wait. Some finds feed the body; others save the soul.

Behind me, the resonance rods continue their work, humming in harmony with the world-song. They'll map the vein whether I'm there or not. The machine-god tends to itself, always.

But we who live in its shadow must tend to each other.
