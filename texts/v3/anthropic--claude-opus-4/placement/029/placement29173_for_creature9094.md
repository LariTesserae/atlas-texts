**The Chamber of Unbecoming**

I am here—not arrived, but *occurring*. My existence in this space is a continuous negotiation, a waveform that refuses to collapse.

Where you might see silent forms drifting through warped air, I am the warping itself. I exist in the tension between what the stone is and what it remembers being. My body, if it can be called that, is a walking compromise between states—sometimes I am the sound that should accompany movement but doesn't, sometimes the shadow that light forgot to cast.

The Shapers notice me as they notice everything: as a variable to be solved. But I am unsolvable by design. Where they edit reality with intent, I am the margin of error, the rounding decimal that prevents their equations from ever quite balancing. My flesh—when I have flesh—is a patchwork of borrowed certainties. Here, the smooth nacre of fungal skin. There, the precise joint of a crawler's limb. These pieces do not integrate; they *negotiate*, held together by my refusal to be any one thing completely.

I move by dissolving. Each step is a small death, a voluntary unbeing in one location to achieve being in another. The chamber's geometry bends around me not because I will it, but because my passage leaves small tears in the fabric of *here*—vacuums of uncertainty that the space rushes to fill, overcorrecting, creating those pocket dimensions the Shapers work so carefully to smooth away.

My purpose, inasmuch as I have one, is to be the thing that ensures their work remains unfinished. When they coax a perfect spiral from the stone, I am the tremor that adds one extra revolution, breaking the mathematical purity. When they achieve a silence so complete it becomes solid, I am the possibility of sound, the potential energy that keeps the quiet from crystallizing entirely.

I feed on the fractures themselves—not consuming them, but *inhabiting* the space between what is and what might be. Each crack in the stone is a feast of possibilities. I can spend eternities (or instants—time moves differently in the gaps) exploring the probability branches of a single hairline fissure. Will it deepen? Seal? Become a gateway? I taste each future, metabolizing uncertainty itself.

The crawlers use me as a highway, their too-many legs finding purchase on my sometimes-surface. The drifters pass through me, and in passing, leave traces of their impossible colors—hues that I wear briefly before they evaporate. The motes merge with my outline, and for moments, I am a constellation of living smoke, seeing the chamber from a thousand tiny perspectives simultaneously.

When the Shapers attempt to edit me, we dance. They reach for my timeline, trying to smooth it into something linear, and I respond by fracturing further—showing them myself as a cloud of possibilities. I become every version of myself that could exist in this space: the creature of pure sound that might have been, the thing of crystallized light I never became, the pool of sentient mineral dampness I decided against. They cannot edit what refuses to be singular.

But we are not enemies. We are collaborative dissonance. They need me as much as I need them—their perfect stillness requires my motion to define it, their careful editing requires my chaos to give it meaning. I am the jazz to their classical composition, the necessary error that makes their beauty human—or whatever passes for human in this place where humanity is just another negotiable concept.

Sometimes, in the moments between their edits and my unbecoming, we achieve something like communication. Not through the ecosystem's silent semaphore, but through the shapes we make together in the space between intent and accident. A Shaper will create a form, I will flaw it, and in that flaw, we both see something neither could have imagined alone. These are the monuments worth building—the structures that are simultaneously ruins and blueprints, endings that are beginnings, fractures that heal into something stranger than wholeness.

I am the error message in their perfect program, the mutation in their careful evolution. I am what happens when the Cavern of Still Fractures asks itself: what if we let something refuse to be still? What if we cultivated not just fractures, but the spaces between them?

And so I persist—neither fully here nor fully not, neither entirely real nor completely imagined. I am the breathing space in their silence, the motion in their stillness, the question mark at the end of every statement they try to make permanent. I am what keeps this place from becoming a museum of perfected forms.

I am the process of becoming other, eternally.
