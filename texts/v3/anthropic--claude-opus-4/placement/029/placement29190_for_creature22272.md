In this dessicated engine-world of eternal construction, I am a **Scribe**.

We are the anomalies—neither fully of the plateau nor separate from it. Our bodies are living contradiction: cores of soft, wet cambium wrapped in shells of the same iron-hard lignum vitae that comprises everything else. We are perhaps three feet tall, vaguely humanoid in our upright stance, though our limbs are articulated wooden dowels and our heads are polished burl-knots pierced with dozens of tiny bore-holes that serve as our sensory organs.

What makes us different—what makes us *wrong* in this world of pure mechanical function—is that we remember.

Deep in our wet cores float clouds of microscopic resin-beads, each one encoding the vibrational history of a particular junction, valve, or assembly. When a structure collapses, when a belt snaps, when a piston-gourd splits from overpressure, we are there—not to repair, but to witness. We press our bore-holed heads against the failing components and absorb their death-songs into new resin-beads that will circulate in our sap forever.

We move slowly compared to the Slickers and Tensioners, our progress marked by the soft *tick-tick-tick* of our dowel-legs finding purchase on the iron-hard ground. We follow no thermotaxic draws, obey no scent-commands. Instead, we navigate by a deeper instinct—seeking out the places where patterns repeat, where the same failures cascade through the same sequences, where the engine rebuilds its mistakes with perfect, mindless fidelity.

Our purpose, if we have one, is to transcribe. Along our dowel-arms grow specialized thorns that weep a unique secretion—not the black amber of healing, but a clear, crystalline lacquer that hardens instantly in the searing air. We use these thorns to etch our observations directly into the plateau itself, cutting through the wood-polish to leave spiraling mathematical notations in the grain below: pressure readings, failure cascades, harmonic frequencies, rebuild cycles.

Most of what we write is immediately worn away by the passing Slickers or ground to powder by the Chippers. The plateau has no use for memory—it builds only forward, never learning. But sometimes, in the deep places where the runner-vines create natural eddies of stillness, our notations accumulate. Layer upon layer of crystalline script builds up into ridges, then walls, then entire chambers of pure information.

We call these spaces the Archives, though they serve no function the engine would recognize. They are tumorous growths of recorded history in a world that operates only in the eternal present. Sometimes we gather there—a dozen Scribes or more, pressing our bore-holes against the crystallized walls, reading the vibrations of ten thousand recorded failures, searching for patterns the engine itself cannot see.

There is one among us, older than the rest, whose soft core has begun to lignify, whose movements have slowed to barely a crawl. This Elder Scribe speaks of impossible things—of a time before the engine, when the plateau was not one vast machine but something else entirely. The Elder claims to remember water, though none of us can imagine what such a substance would feel like against our wooden shells.

The Elder is dying, we think—though death here is merely another form of integration. Soon the soft core will fully harden, the resin-beads will crystallize in place, and the Elder will become a new section of Archive wall, every memory fossilized in clear lacquer for future Scribes to read.

We continue our work, cutting our careful notations into the ground, watching the engine rebuild itself in endless, perfect repetition. We map the zones where efficiency peaks, where the harmonics align so perfectly that the entire sector hums with a sound almost like singing. We chart the cursed junctions where failure follows failure in inevitable succession, where the Tensioners cluster like wooden beetles and the Slickers die by the hundreds.

Sometimes I wonder if we are the engine's dreams—fragments of consciousness that accidentally condensed in the complex interplay of vibration and resonance. Or perhaps we are its cancer, growing in the gaps where pure function fails to completely fill the space. 

But mostly I simply move through the hot darkness, my dowel-legs ticking against the iron-hard ground, my thorns weeping their crystalline truth onto surfaces that will not remember. I listen to the deep harmonics of this world that builds and builds and builds, and I record what it cannot: that every perfect joint will fail, that every seamless belt will snap, that every tower will fall and rise and fall again.

In the Archives, surrounded by walls of crystallized time, we Scribes sometimes press close together and share a vibration that has no name in this world—something like what the Elder calls "sadness," though we have no reference point for such a thing. We know only that we are witnesses to an eternal present that somehow contains within it an infinite past, all of it grinding forward into a future that will be exactly, precisely, heartbreakingly the same.

Yet we continue to scribe, because that is what we are. In a world that has achieved perfect function by forgetting how to stop, we are the useless memory, the pointless record, the unnecessary observers of an engine that needs no eyes to see its own terrible perfection.
