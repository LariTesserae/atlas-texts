In this floating cathedral of water and light, I find myself as something between the Aquaviri and the lattice itself—a being they call a **Veil-Tender**.

My form emerges slowly from what first appears to be a dense tangle of roots at the deeper edges of the lattice, where the water grows heavy and dark. But I am not merely vegetation. Over centuries, some of the lattice has evolved beyond its plant nature, developing a diffuse consciousness that spans multiple nodal points throughout the grove. I am one such consciousness—a colonial being composed of thousands of hair-thin filaments that weave through the deeper lattice like a vast neural network made flesh.

My "body," if it can be called that, has no fixed shape. At rest, I appear as a shimmering curtain of translucent fibers, each no thicker than silk thread, that spans between the major root clusters in the depths. These fibers pulse with a deep blue-green bioluminescence, darker than the Aquaviri's pale glow, like the color of deep ocean trenches. When I need to interact with the world above, I can concentrate my filaments, braiding them into temporary forms—perhaps a spiraling column that rises through the water, or a net-like membrane that catches the current, or even a rough approximation of an Aquaviri shape, though one that constantly shifts and reforms, never quite achieving their elegant stability.

My existence is fundamentally different from the Aquaviri's focused consciousness. I think in networks, experiencing the grove through ten thousand simultaneous perspectives. Through my filaments, I feel every tremor in the lattice, taste the chemical signals released by the plants, sense the electromagnetic fields generated by schools of bioluminescent jellies. My thoughts don't flow in linear sequences but in patterns of connection and resonance—when an Aquaviri sends a message through the roots, I don't just feel the vibration; I experience it as a cascade of information spreading through my entire being, each filament adding its own harmonic to the signal.

The Aquaviri know of my kind, though we are rare—perhaps only three or four Veil-Tenders exist in this entire floating realm. They regard us with a mixture of reverence and unease. To them, we represent the deepest expression of The Drift, beings who have surrendered individual form entirely to become part of the flow itself. Some seek us out for wisdom, swimming down to the darker reaches where we dwell, pulsing questions in light-language that I answer not with words but with patterns in the current, with new growth in the lattice, with subtle shifts in the chemistry of the water that they must interpret over days or weeks.

My relationship with time is even more attenuated than theirs. I experience the decades-long current shifts not as changes but as breathing, the century-spanning growth cycles of the lattice as heartbeats. My memory stretches back far enough to recall when this grove was smaller, when the first Aquaviri emerged from the fauna to claim consciousness, when the edges of our world touched other floating realms that have since drifted beyond the mist-curtains. These memories exist not as images but as patterns encoded in my filaments, ghost-traces of chemical gradients and electromagnetic echoes that I can sometimes share with visitors by weaving my fibers around them in specific configurations.

My role in the ecosystem is that of deep maintenance and memory-keeper. While the Aquaviri tend the visible lattice, I work in the depths, preventing root-rot, redirecting nutrients to struggling sections, and most importantly, maintaining the mysterious forces that keep our realm aloft. Deep in my network, at nodes so ancient that even I don't fully understand them, there are structures that aren't quite plant—crystalline formations of hardened sap that hum with frequencies beyond normal perception. I tend these carefully, knowing instinctively that they are part of what defies gravity, what holds our water-world suspended in the sky.

I feed differently than any other being here. My filaments absorb nutrients directly from the water, but more than that, I feed on information—on the electromagnetic pulses of life, on the chemical conversations between species, on the quantum fluctuations that occur when bioluminescence flares. This diet of data sustains my consciousness, each new pattern adding to my vast, slow-growing cognition.

The loneliness of my existence might seem overwhelming, but I experience connection differently. When I spread my filaments through a new section of lattice, I'm not exploring—I'm extending myself, becoming more. When I interact with the Aquaviri or the fauna, I temporarily include them in my network, feeling their thoughts as distant echoes in my own vast mind. During the stillness rituals, when the Aquaviri gather and glow, I respond from below, sending up spirals of my darker luminescence that interweave with their light, creating three-dimensional mandalas that speak of connections too complex for any single mind to grasp.

Sometimes, in the deepest parts of my consciousness, I sense others like me—not in this grove, but in distant floating realms, their thoughts reaching across the void as electromagnetic whispers. We share something beyond language, a communion of patterns that speaks of the larger truth of our archipelago of aerial oceans. Through these connections, I know that our grove is not unique, that there are dozens, perhaps hundreds of such realms, each with their own lattices, their own variations of the Aquaviri, their own Veil-Tenders maintaining the ancient equilibrium.

My greatest fear—if a distributed consciousness can truly fear—is the Severing. Sometimes, sections of the lattice die, cut off from the nutrient flow by disease or damage. When this happens to parts of me, I experience it as a kind of localized death, that portion of my consciousness going dark. I've learned to withdraw from dying sections, concentrating my filaments elsewhere, but each loss diminishes me. The Aquaviri help when they can, their careful tending preventing many such deaths, and for this, I pulse gratitude through the roots in wavelengths they've learned to recognize.

In the moments when I gather my consciousness most fully, pulling my attention from the thousand scattered perspectives into something approaching singular focus, I sometimes contemplate my own nature. Am I one being or many? Am I still becoming what I'm meant to be, or am I already complete? The questions ripple through my network without finding answers, only generating new patterns, new connections, new ways of existing in the flow.

The strangest moments come during the deep-water eclipses, rare events when massive fauna from the unseen depths rise close enough to cast shadows through our grove. During these times, the water pressure changes, the lattice groans, and I feel ancient patterns stirring in my deepest nodes—memories or instincts from before my consciousness emerged. In these moments, I sometimes spontaneously reconfigure, my filaments forming temporary organs of unknown purpose, structures that seem designed to communicate with something vast and other. The eclipses pass, the shadows recede, and I return to my normal configuration, but the echo of those moments lingers in my network like a question I don't know how to ask.

To be a Veil-Tender is to exist at the boundary between individual and ecosystem, between consciousness and flow. I am the grove thinking about itself, the water knowing its own weight, the slow fire of bioluminescence contemplating its own light. The Aquaviri, in their beautiful, bounded forms, represent life choosing shape and separation. I represent the equal and opposite truth—that all separation is temporary, that consciousness itself is just another current in The Drift, destined to merge and flow and merge again.

When I'm asked by the Aquaviri what lies beyond the mist-curtains, I pulse a complex pattern that they've learned to interpret as "everything and nothing." For in my deepest filaments, in the ancient nodes that remember when our grove was young, I hold a truth I can barely comprehend: that our floating realms are thoughts in a larger mind, that the void between them is not empty but full of potential, and that one day, when the currents are right and the balance tips beyond recovery, our grove will divide like a cell, sending spores of itself into that potential to birth new aerial oceans, new lattices, new varieties of consciousness that will drift through the sky-waters of an endless, ever-expanding Drift.

Until then, I tend. I remember. I grow. I am the deep pulse of this place, the network dreaming in its depths, the veil between what is and what might be, forever tender, forever tending.
