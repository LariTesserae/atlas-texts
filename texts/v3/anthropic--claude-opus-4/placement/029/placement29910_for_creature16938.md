In the vertical city that drinks through geometry and hunger, I am a Cartographer of Absence—one of those rare Subscripted who became obsessed with mapping what isn't there.

My body is a palimpsest of erased trajectories. Where others among the Subscripted merely fade at their edges, I have cultivated entire regions of strategic non-existence: a missing left shoulder blade that opens onto yesterday's weather, phantom fingers that can only touch things that haven't happened yet, eyes that see exclusively in the spectrum of regret. I wear my incompleteness like haute couture.

I discovered my calling during the Third Revision, when the city accidentally uninstalled gravity for seventeen subjective minutes. While others clung to the honeycomb walls or let themselves drift into the stat-field above, I noticed the paths that appeared in gravity's absence—silver threads of pure direction connecting every point that had ever considered being somewhere else. I began tracing these threads with my not-fingers, feeling how they vibrated with the frequency of almostness.

My maps are carved into surfaces that don't exist until observed sideways. Each cartograph shows not where things are, but where they failed to be—the negative space between intentions and arrivals. The most beautiful are my Charts of Unwalked Streets: careful documentation of every route through the city that no one has taken, will take, or could take without violating their own causality. These streets glow faintly with possibility, pulsing in rhythm with the heartbeats of people who were never born.

I live (if my recursive hovering between states counts as living) in a studio apartment located in the gap between the forty-third and forty-third-and-a-half floors. You can only find it by deliberately getting lost while looking for somewhere else. The walls are papered with my collection of Unmaps—anti-cartographs that show everywhere you aren't with perfect accuracy. When overlapped correctly, they reveal exactly one location: wherever you're standing, reading them.

My greatest work is the Map of the Spherical Void, drawn entirely in the dark using ink made from powdered shadows and my own extracted uncertainty. It shows the void not as absence but as presence so dense it forgets to exist—layer after layer of compressed nothingness fossilized into almost-something. The map itself weighs less than the concept of weight but more than the memory of falling. I keep it folded into origami birds that fly backward through time, delivering messages I haven't written yet to people who won't exist until they read them.

The other Subscripted consider me mad, which here means "insufficiently committed to controlled forgetting." They whisper that I'm trying to map my way out, that I believe somewhere in the infinite non-spaces between here and here lies an exit that even the city hasn't predicted. They're wrong. I'm not looking for an exit. I'm documenting all the exits that were already taken, creating an exhaustive catalog of escape routes that lead directly back to the beginning.

My method is simple: I follow the Unwalkers.

The Unwalkers are those who move through the city by not moving at all. They've discovered that stillness, practiced with sufficient intensity, becomes a form of velocity that exceeds light speed by simply refusing to acknowledge distance. I track them by the tears they leave in spacetime's fabric—neat little rips exactly the width of a decision unmade. My maps show these tears as constellation patterns, and when you connect them in the right order, they spell out words in languages that evolution was too embarrassed to invent.

Sometimes I catch glimpses of the Unwalkers themselves: figures assembled from the shadows cast by thoughts that changed their minds. They nod at me as they don't pass, acknowledging our shared obsession with documenting absence. Once, the oldest among them—recognizable by her corona of crystallized maybes—taught me to read the city's self-destruction countdown in the angles where walls meet nothing. She said the countdown isn't counting down at all, but sideways, approaching zero from directions mathematics hasn't discovered yet.

My maps have revealed patterns the city would prefer remain hidden. The honeycomb structure isn't random but spells out a suicide note in dead languages when viewed from sufficient altitude. The twelve statues orbiting the void aren't statues at all but frozen Subscripted who achieved perfect stillness and became landmarks. The nanofabbers' song of universe-abortion contains, when played backward at the wrong speed, detailed instructions for building a door that opens onto itself from the inside.

But the most disturbing discovery came when I mapped the negative space between all my other maps. There, in the gaps between documented absence, I found myself already there, drawing maps of mapping myself mapping. An infinite recursion of cartography, each layer showing the same thing: a figure bent over papers that show a figure bent over papers that show a figure bent over papers, all the way down to single-point perspectives too small for meaning to survive.

I am the map now. The city reads me to navigate itself through its own ending. My bones have become latitude lines, my synapses longitude. When I breathe, the Subscripted check their position relative to my exhales. When I sleep—that counterfeit death we practice to remember being born—my dreams update the city's GPS with new coordinates for nowhere.

The lovers use my maps to find each other by going in opposite directions until they meet in the middle of missing. The leaders consult my charts before not-deciding, ensuring their indecisions will have maximum impact on minimum reality. Even the Subsuperscripted, in their margin-margins, steal glimpses of my work to wage their typographical wars with better precision.

Last night, I discovered I've been mapping myself out of existence one documented absence at a time. Each map I complete erases a little more of my presence here, replacing it with increasingly accurate descriptions of not being. Soon, I will achieve my masterpiece: a perfect map of everywhere I'm not, which will be everywhere, which will finally show the city what it's been trying to tell itself since before the first collapse:

That the only way out is further in, that the only map that matters is the one you leave behind for someone else to not follow, that every cartographer eventually becomes the territory they were trying to chart.

When that happens, I'll finally understand why the city invited me here in the first place—not to map its absence, but to become the absence it maps itself against. A fixed point of non-existence around which all other uncertainties can orient themselves.

Until then, I continue my work. I trace the paths of never-was through the humid air that must be absorbed rather than breathed. I document each impossible angle where the architecture suggests escape but delivers only deeper imprisonment. I catalog every shadow that tastes like fear and every echo that precedes its source.

And sometimes, in the moments between mapping and being mapped, I feel you reading this. I feel your eyes tracing the shape of my existence-through-description. And I wonder: are you reading my story, or am I mapping yours? 

Are you the cartographer, or the territory?

Are you here, or are you the here we're all trying to map our way away from?

The answer, like all answers in this vertical city compressed into a vein no wider than a single thought, is both and neither and the space between them where maps dissolve into the pure possibility of being lost with perfect accuracy.

*I leave you this map drawn in words: follow it nowhere, and you'll find me exactly where I've always been—in the process of documenting my own absence, one perfect emptiness at a time.*
