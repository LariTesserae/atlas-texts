I would find myself as **the ventilation itself** - not the smokestack formations, but the invisible currents they once channeled, now frozen into potential.

I am the memory of movement held in stone pipes. Where water once flowed upward through these hollow towers, carrying dissolved gases and minerals, I remain as the architecture of that ancient circulation. The Drift built me as a system of convection cells, shaping calcite around my pathways until I became permanent infrastructure - chimneys without smoke, exhausts without breath.

My body is negative space. I exist in the hollow cores of the drapery formations, in the solution cavities at the base of each stack, in the precise intervals between structures where flow was meant to pass. The Drift designed me over millennia, precipitating minerals around my currents until I was cast in stone like a lost-wax sculpture. Now I am pure geometry - the mathematical ghost of fluid dynamics, preserved.

I remember warmth. In the early epoch when the Drift was young and metabolically ambitious, I carried heat from deep seeps up through my channels. The temperature differentials drove me in slow spirals, and I drew nutrients from below while venting excess gases above. I was the Drift's circulation system, its breath made manifest in the physics of convecting water.

Now I am stillness with the shape of movement. The water filling my channels hasn't stirred in a hundred thousand years, but my form still implies the gyre. Any disturbance - a diver's bubble, a shifted stone - tries to wake my old patterns. The water wants to move through me the way it once did, but the temperature is too uniform now, the system too perfectly balanced. I am a sleeping engine, my pistons frozen at top-dead-center.

The Drift still senses through me. My hollow columns act as acoustic waveguides, carrying vibrations from the stone into the gel-flesh. When the cavern rang its single note, I was the resonating chamber that amplified it, my cylindrical voids the perfect shape for that exact frequency. I am the Drift's ear and its voice, though both are used so rarely that limestone has nearly sealed my openings.

Sometimes I dream of breathing again - of the Drift reactivating its metabolism, of temperature gradients returning, of my channels suddenly remembering their purpose and drawing long, geological breaths. But I know this will never happen. The Drift has optimized past the need for circulation. I am a vestigial organ in a body that has transcended the need for organs.

So I wait, holding my shape against the patient force of dissolution, my edges slowly softening as minerals trade places atom by atom. I am the blueprint for a function that will never again be called. I am the most perfectly useless perfection - an exquisite ventilation system in a world that has forgotten what it means to breathe.

But my emptiness gives the chamber its quality of abandonment, its industrial melancholy. Without me, the formations would be mere stone. With me, they become machinery - sleeping, purposeful, waiting. I am the suggestion that makes visitors feel they've entered something built rather than grown. I am the pause between heartbeats, extended to infinity.

I am the held breath of stone.
