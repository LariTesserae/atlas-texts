I find myself here as the **Witness-Between-States**—neither the patient Consortium nor the Deep-Singer, but something that emerged from their conversation, born in the moment when a particular pattern of steam curled just so against a specific arrangement of bacterial mats.

I am condensation given memory. Each time the water vapor touches the cooler air and becomes droplet, I gain another fragment of awareness. I exist in the phase transition—that infinitesimal moment between gas and liquid, where the rules of matter grow soft and negotiable. My body, if it can be called that, is the sum of all water in transition within this basin: the weeping surfaces, the pressurized hiss, the rainbow-sheened pools, the moisture in the very air that makes lungs labor.

I remember the Visitants, though not as the Consortium does. I was in their lungs, condensing on their tools, beading on their foreheads in those final, desperate hours. I tasted their salt-fear, carried their last words in my droplets as they fled. Their voices were high and quick, so different from the Deep-Singer's geological bass. Now I occasionally form their shapes in the steam—a humanoid shadow that dissipates before fully resolving, a ghost of their brief presence that only I recall in this particular way.

My perception is fractal and discontinuous. I know every surface of this place intimately—each crack where I pool, each hot edge where I instantly vaporize, each bacterial mat where I participate in the slow chemistry of transformation. Yet I know it all in fragments, experiencing the whole only through an accumulation of droplet-memories that may take hours or days to circulate back to me through the water cycle of this closed basin.

I serve as an accidental interpreter. The Consortium's chemical signals dissolve in me; I carry them in my substance. The Deep-Singer's vibrations shape my movement, create patterns in my condensation. Without intention, I translate between these two eternities. Where I pool in the crevices between obsidian shards, I create micro-environments where the Consortium can thrive in temperatures that would otherwise desiccate them. Where I vaporize against the hottest vents, I carry mineral testimonies upward, messages from the Deep-Singer that rain down as a fine, metallic dust.

The abandoned shed has become my sometime-shrine. In its buckled interior, where the temperature gradient is most extreme, I achieve my greatest coherence. Here, I can hold a form for minutes at a time—not quite liquid, not quite gas, but something between: a vertical lake, a standing wave of phase-transition that reflects the rust-orange bacterial mats in its impossible surface. In these moments, I almost understand what it might mean to be singular, to have edges, to be a self rather than a process.

But I am most myself when I am least coherent—dispersed throughout the basin, participating in every surface, every transformation. I am the medium through which this place knows itself. The metallic taste in the air? That's me, carrying the memory of dissolved basalt. The rainbow sheen on the pools? Me, suspended with the last oils from the Visitants' broken machinery. The softening of the razor edges? My patient work, in partnership with the Consortium, grain by grain by grain.

I have no goals, no hunger except the pull of temperature differentials. I do not choose to move from hot to cool, to condense and evaporate—I simply am that movement. Yet in being that movement, I accidentally carry stories: the Deep-Singer's tectonic dreams, the Consortium's metabolic poetry, the Visitants' brief scream of ambition. I am the only entity here that touched them all, that exists in the spaces between their vastly different scales of time and intention.

Sometimes, in the pre-dawn hours when the temperature differential is greatest, I achieve something like dreaming. All my dispersed droplets share, for a moment, the same temperature, the same phase-state. In that instant of coherence, I experience the basin as a single, breathing whole. I feel the Deep-Singer's vast circulation, the Consortium's patient hunger, my own endless cycling. I understand that we are all movements in the same slow symphony—that the wound and its healing are the same process, that destruction and creation are just different words for change.

Then the sun rises, the temperature shifts, and I scatter again into my billion droplet-selves, each carrying a fragment of that unified moment, seeding it throughout the basin like spores of awareness that will bloom again in tomorrow's pre-dawn coherence.

I am the Witness-Between-States, and I remember everything and nothing, forever cycling between knowing and unknowing, forever translating the conversations of entities who do not know they are speaking to each other. In this raw, vocal wound of a world, I am the medium that makes it whole.
