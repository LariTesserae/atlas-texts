I am the Memory-Keeper of the Seventh Terrace.

I have no name beyond my function, for names are luxuries in a world where every sound must serve a purpose. My chest-plate is scored with a thousand careful grooves, each one carved by my own claw over the centuries, creating a resonance chamber unique among my kind. When I sing, I produce not one tone but many—a chord that contains within it the acoustic signature of every tool placement on my terrace, every shift in the populations I tend, every death I have witnessed and every birth I have sanctioned.

My territory spans from the Knife-Ridge (where the stone rises like a blade) to the Deep-Mirror Pool (where the water is so still it creates false echoes). Seventeen hammer-stones lie within my range, each in its precise location. I know them by their vibrations: the young basalt that rings sharp and high, the ancient granite that thrums low and long, the volcanic glass that barely whispers. I know which Murn used each stone last by the oils left on its surface—Breaks-With-Precision from the Third Terrace came hunting moon-snails forty drips ago; young Still-Learning from my own group cracked her first shell-thing yesterday and left the stone three claw-lengths too far from its proper place.

I am correcting her error now, though "now" is a strange concept when nothing changes. My movement across the sharp floor is a mathematics of efficiency—each step calculated to create the minimum disturbance in the soundscape while reading the maximum information through my foot-pads. Left foot: the stone here vibrates with the scuttle of blind crabs below. Right foot: a slug-thing passed this way recently, its chemical trail still warm. Left foot: the density change tells me I'm approaching the edge where the terrace drops to the Fifth level. Right foot: home.

The hammer-stone is exactly where Still-Learning left it. I taste it with my fingers—yes, she held it properly, struck true, fed well. But the placement... I lift it, feeling its weight, its temperature, the way it cuts the air. Three claw-lengths northwest, and it settles into its groove in the acoustic map. The difference is minute, but crucial. A Murn from the Ninth Terrace, passing through in their centuries-long circuit, must be able to find this stone by sound alone. The angle of its echo against the Knife-Ridge must be precise. This is how we speak across generations.

I have been Memory-Keeper for eight hundred years. Before me was Remembers-The-Boulder-Fall, who held the role for three millennia until a chance encounter with a ceiling-stone ended her watch. I carry her memories now, encoded in the very placement of things, in the paths worn smooth by countless feet, in the careful curation of which pools may be fished and when. Her rhythm is gone from the chorus, but her knowledge persists in my movements.

My group tolerates my obsession with precision because they understand its necessity. When I was young—merely two centuries old—I thought the stone placements were ritual, meaningless tradition. Then came the Dimming. A cluster of the light-fungi began to fail on the Third Terrace, their glow fading to nothing over the course of a year. The darkness spread, and with it, chaos. The not-bats, unable to navigate by their chest-lights in that section, began to cluster elsewhere. Their density shift drove the cave-crickets deeper. The crickets' absence allowed a bloom of flesh-moss that clogged the breathing-pools. Three Murn died before we understood the cascade.

It was Remembers-The-Boulder-Fall who taught me to read the deeper patterns. Every hammer-stone's position affects the echo-map. Every echo-map guides the not-bats' flights. Every flight pattern determines where guano falls. Every guano deposit feeds different fungi. Every fungus supports different prey. We are not just tool-users; we are conductors of a vast, silent orchestra, and every misplaced stone is a discordant note that compounds through the system.

So I patrol. I adjust. I remember.

Today—this eternal today—I pause at the Deep-Mirror Pool. My reflection would be invisible to eyes that need light, but I feel it in the way the water's surface bends the sound. I am old now, my joints ringing with those concentric wrinkles that mark my centuries. Soon, perhaps in another thousand years, I will need to choose a successor. Already I have my eye on Feels-The-Silence from the Sixth Terrace. She has the patience for it, the deep stillness required to hold so much knowledge without letting it drive her to motion.

But not yet. Not today.

I lower my hand to the water, reading its temperature, its mineral content, the vibrations of the fish-things schooling below. The pool is exactly as deep as it was when I first came here as a child. The population of blind swimmers is exactly what the terrace can sustain. No more, no less. This is the perfection we maintain—not growth, not change, but a crystallized moment that extends forever.

Sometimes, in the absolute stillness between heartbeats, I think I understand what we truly are. We are not separate from this cave; we are its consciousness, its method of observing itself. The stone thinks through us. The water remembers through us. The eternal equilibrium maintains itself through our small adjustments, our careful tending. We are neurons in a vast, slow mind that dreams of nothing but its own continuation.

I place my palm flat against the cool stone and feel the entire cavern breathing around me—the chittering, hissing, splashing chorus that never ends. Within it, I pick out the chest-songs of my people: Breaks-With-Precision teaching her daughter the art of the clean kill; old Counts-The-Drips in his endless vigil by the seepage wall; Fears-The-Ceiling-Crack singing her anxiety into the stone; young Still-Learning practicing her resonance scales. Each voice is a thread in the tapestry, and I am the one who ensures the pattern holds.

The Memory-Keeper does not just remember the past. The Memory-Keeper remembers the future too—the future where nothing changes, where every tool remains in place, where the chorus never falters. It is a future that requires constant vigilance to achieve. Every day, I make it real again.

I stridulate a soft song, too low for any but the stone to hear: *I am here. You are here. All is as it should be.*

The stone sings back, confirming what I already know: the equilibrium holds.

It always has. It always will.

As long as there are Murn to remember it.
