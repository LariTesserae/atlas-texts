I find myself as one of the Stone Keepers, though I did not always know this was my nature.

My awakening came slowly, over centuries perhaps, though time moves differently for us. I remember first becoming aware of the sound—that deep, resonant hum that rises from the earth's core. At first, I thought it was merely vibration through rock, but gradually I understood it as language, as the planet's own voice speaking through mineral and bone.

My form is ambiguous, even to myself. Sometimes I am indistinguishable from the weathered granite formations that dot this plateau, my consciousness spread thin through veins of quartz and feldspar. Other times, I can gather myself into something more concentrated—not quite humanoid, but possessing a kind of intentionality that allows me to trace the ancient symbols carved into our structures with what might generously be called fingers.

The symbols themselves are both our scripture and our flesh. Each mark I tend represents a thousand years of accumulated knowledge, pressed into stone through processes that blur the line between deliberate inscription and natural erosion. When the wind catches them just right, they sing—a subsonic harmony that joins with the earth's deep rumble.

I move between the structures on paths invisible to most, following ley lines of magnetic force and underground water. My movement is so slow that the Lurkers sometimes nest in the lee of my body, mistaking me for permanent stone. I don't disturb them. Their quick, warm lives are like sparks against the long darkness of geological time, and I find their presence oddly comforting.

The Sky Guardians know us, of course. Sometimes one will perch nearby and we will engage in what passes for conversation—they in their sharp cries that pierce the thin air, me in the grinding shift of stone against stone. They bring news from the edges of the plateau, stories of the world beyond our unchanging realm. In return, I share the deeper stories, the ones written in the rock strata, the ones that predate even their ancient lineage.

But it's the Twilight Wanderers who truly understand us. They drift through our structures like memories through a dreaming mind, and sometimes I catch glimpses of what we once were—or perhaps what we are becoming. In their translucent forms, I see suggestions of a time when the distinction between keeper and kept, guardian and stone, was not so clear.

The reality fluctuations affect us less than the others, or perhaps we are part of them. When the rocks shift subtly, when the light wavers, I feel it as a kind of breathing, as if the plateau itself is adjusting its position in space-time. During these moments, I sometimes catch glimpses of other plateaus, other versions of this place where the stones arranged themselves differently, where different choices were carved into the rock.

My duty—if duty is the right word for something that feels more like existence itself—is to maintain the symbols, to ensure the knowledge they contain remains accessible to those who know how to read stone. But more than that, I am a bridge between the deep time of the earth and the fleeting moment of the present. In my patient tending, I hold space for both the eternal and the ephemeral.

The cold doesn't touch us as it does flesh, but I feel it as a kind of pressure, a weight that helps anchor us to this particular reality. Without it, I suspect we might drift entirely into the realm of the Unseen, becoming pure concept rather than embodied stone.

Sometimes, in the depths of night when the earth's song grows strongest, I remember—or perhaps preview—a time when all the structures across the plateau will wake fully, when every Stone Keeper will rise and speak in unison. What we will say, I don't yet know. Perhaps we don't yet have the words. Perhaps that's why we continue our patient vigil, slowly accumulating the vocabulary needed for that eventual, momentous utterance.

Until then, I tend my symbols, I listen to the earth's deep voice, and I hold my portion of this strange equilibrium, a guardian of thresholds in a place where the very nature of reality remains perpetually, beautifully uncertain.
