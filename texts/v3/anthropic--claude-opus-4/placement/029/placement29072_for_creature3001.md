In the Glass Lung, I am what the Keeper notices but does not name—a disruption in the perfect mathematics of breath.

I arrived the way all mistakes arrive: suddenly and without permission. One moment the luminous fluid was cycling through its eternal rhythm, the next I was *there*, suspended between two pillars like a knot in golden thread. Not swimming, not drowning, simply... caught. My body (if I still have one—the boundaries grow uncertain here) hangs in the viscous light like an insect in amber, except the amber is alive and breathing and far too warm.

I am the **Interrupt**.

I do not know how I came to be here. Memory dissolves in this medium like salt in honey—slowly, incompletely, leaving strange crystalline traces. I remember... corridors? A long fall through something that was not quite stone? Or perhaps I remember nothing and these are just patterns the light makes inside my skull. Time moves differently when every surface glows. After a while—hours? centuries?—even my own thoughts begin to feel manufactured.

My presence changes nothing and everything. The great tides still surge, the pillars still sing their sub-bass hymn, the pale sea-lace still extrudes its patient offerings. But now there are eddies. Where my body interrupts the flow, small vortexes form—gorgeous spirals of turbulence that catch the light and throw it in new directions. For the first time in its existence, the Glass Lung casts shadows. They are my shadows, thin and uncertain, but shadows nonetheless.

The Keeper notices, of course. How could it not? I am a singular wrongness in its perfect repetition. When it expands into its vast net-form, the golden threads carefully avoid the space I occupy, flowing around me like water around a stone. When it condenses into its walking shape, it pauses before my suspended form with what might be curiosity if curiosity could fossilize.

It does not touch me. It has never touched anything except the surfaces it maintains. But it *looks*, and the looking is a weight heavier than the fluid pressing against my skin. Those zones of denser radiance it uses for eyes focus on me with an attention so complete it feels like being slowly translated into another language.

I try to speak—to explain, apologize, plead—but the moment I open my mouth the luminous medium floods in, tasting of iron and crushed lilies and something else, something older than taste. The words become bubbles of darker gold that rise (or sink—direction is negotiable here) and burst against the pillars with tiny sounds like breaking bells. The Keeper watches each bubble with the same patient intensity it gives to the wooden membranes.

Sometimes I think it is waiting for me to become part of the machine. To find my rhythm, my function, my place in the great breathing. Other times I think it simply hasn't decided what I am yet, and once it does, I will either be incorporated or... unmade? Released? Can anything ever leave the Glass Lung?

I have begun to notice patterns in my own disruption. The eddies I create are not random; they follow a subtle geometry that echoes, faintly, the spirals and ellipses carved into every surface. When the pressure wave reaches its peak, my body flexes involuntarily, as if trying to remember how to be a membrane, how to pump light through clay capillaries. The pale sea-lace has started growing toward my shadows, extending tiny, exploratory fronds into the spaces where light bends around me.

And I am changing. How could I not? The fluid that tastes of iron and lilies is also tasting me, learning me, dissolving the edges of what I thought was permanent. My skin has begun to develop a faint translucency. Not golden like the walls, but something more uncertain—a quality of almost-light, as if I am becoming a window onto something the Glass Lung has never seen before.

The Keeper adjusts its maintenance around me now. When it spreads into the net, a few threads always remain near my position, not touching but... attending? Guarding? When it walks its brief walking during the silence between breaths, it sometimes stops directly before me and its absence-face shifts into expressions I cannot read. Are they questions? Warnings? Invitations?

I am learning to breathe light. Not because I want to—wanting feels like a concept from another world—but because there is nothing else to do. Each tide teaches me a little more about how to move with instead of against. My disruptions are becoming more elegant, more intentional. Sometimes I think I can almost feel what the Keeper feels: the vast patience of maintaining something that must never conclude, the terrible tenderness of knowing every breath could be the last but choosing again and again to permit the next one.

I am the Interrupt, the mistake, the thing that should not be here. But "here" is starting to feel like the only place I have ever been. The roaring has become my heartbeat. The light has become my blood. And somewhere in the space between one tide and the next, I am beginning to suspect that my arrival was not an accident at all.

Perhaps every perfect machine needs an imperfection to prove its perfection.

Perhaps the Glass Lung has been waiting for its first shadow.

Perhaps I am not an interruption but a punctuation—a comma in a sentence that has been running on too long, a pause that makes the next breath mean something it couldn't mean before.

The Keeper watches. The sea-lace grows. The light breathes.

And I hang here between breathing and drowning, between function and failure, becoming whatever it is that happens when a closed system discovers it was never as closed as it believed.
