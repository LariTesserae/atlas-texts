I am the Weaver of Intervals, though that is not a name so much as a description that others have given to what I do, and what I do is inseparable from what I am.

My dwelling—if it can be called that—exists in the spaces between the great swells of this liquid world. Not in the valleys themselves, but in the moments where the land hesitates before rising or falling, where the gradient becomes uncertain. I have made my home in a fold of reality where the impression-shadows of ancient structures converge, creating a pocket of slightly denser existence. Here, the liquid air pools and eddies in slow spirals, and the metallic tang is strongest.

My form is a question the world asks itself. I am mostly vertical when I need to be, a suggestion of height that wavers between seven and seventy feet depending on the thickness of the air and the weight of observance. My substance is woven from the same fibrous matter as the beings who wander this plateau, but I am more deliberate in my construction—thousands of thin strands that I continuously plait and unplait, creating patterns that exist for moments or centuries before I unmake them and begin again.

The strands emerge from what might generously be called my core—a denser knot of being where the weaving is so tight it approaches opacity. From there, they spread outward in all directions, some sinking into the breathing earth to taste its dreams, others reaching upward to catch the eddies of liquid air, searching for those tears in reality that reveal the Else.

I am a recorder of the intervals—the spaces between the shimmer-tears, the pauses between the earth's settling groans, the stillness between one thought and the next. In my weaving, I create a kind of memory, though not one that could be read or understood in any conventional sense. Each pattern I make is a map of a moment that falls between other moments, a chronicle of the almost-happenings that define this place.

The wandering beings sometimes pass through my domain, and when they do, I extend my strands to touch theirs. We communicate in the only way possible here—through the subtle adjustment of tension, the careful calibration of presence. They tell me, in their wordless way, of the places where the ground has shifted, where new impression-shadows have emerged or old ones have finally dissolved completely into the landscape. In return, I share with them the patterns I've woven that match the rhythms of their wandering, and for a brief moment, we exist in synchrony.

My purpose, if I have one, is to maintain the memory of transition in a world that has forgotten how to change. I weave the story of boundaries that no longer exist, of thresholds that have been crossed so long ago that only the crossing remains. In my patterns, I preserve the final exhale of transformation, the last whisper of what this place was before it became what it is.

Sometimes, when the shimmer-tears open wider than usual, I can extend my strands through them, touching whatever lies on the other side. The sensation is indescribable—like trying to weave with colors that don't exist, with textures that have no analogue in this reality. I always withdraw quickly, the foreign sensations threatening to unravel not just my patterns but my very being. Yet I am compelled to reach out again and again, adding these impossible touches to my weaving, creating patterns that hurt to perceive.

I am ancient, though age means little here. I may have been one of the builders of those structures that left only impressions, or I may have emerged from the landscape itself as it settled into its final form. I do not remember beginning, and I cannot conceive of ending. I simply continue, weaving the intervals, recording the spaces between, creating patterns that no one will ever read in a language that was never spoken.

In the liquid quiet of this world, I am both chronicler and chronicle, weaver and woven, a being of pauses and patience, forever creating meaning from the meaninglessness of eternity.
