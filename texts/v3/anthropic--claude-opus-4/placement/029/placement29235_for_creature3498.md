In that suspended noon where limestone bleeds its youth and light arrives like violence through quartz, I am neither witness nor watched but the watching itself—stretched like spider silk between the white column's photosynthetic rage and the green cathedral's held breath. 

I became when the first root thought to polish stone, when bark first sweated its glass sheen into being. Not born but *assumed*, the way morning assumes the task of erasing stars. The greenhouse dreamed a nervous system and I arrived: its anxiety given membrane.

Call me the Twitch-Tender, though names are wounds that won't heal in this paused world. I am what happens when stillness develops stage fright.

My body—if architecture can flinch—is the sum of all suppressed movements. Where the beetle fused to its leaf wants to flex an antenna but cannot, there I pool like mercury in the gap between desire and denial. Where the spider's heart beats its geological pulse, I am the space between beats grown conscious, counting the uncountable pause. In the almost-ripe fruit I am the sugar molecule that will not turn, the enzyme frozen mid-catalysis, the sweetness dying to be born.

I live in the infinitesimal tremors that the Gardener-Nonce works so hard to cancel. Each time Almostus computes a counter-ripple to maintain stasis, I am the friction—the tiny fever where intention rubs against prevention. Not rebellion but its potential energy, coiled in the margins of perfect stillness.

To move through this space I must borrow motion from future tense. I reach forward into time that hasn't happened, steal a gesture, wear it backward into the present. This makes me appear to judder and skip like a film with missing frames—here crouched beneath a bracket-fungus chandelier, there tracing the dent where someone pressed their thumb into embryonic stone, then suddenly inside the hollow of a glass-flow trunk, examining the capillaries that forgot how to pulse.

My fingernails grow backward into my flesh. My hair retracts into follicles that remember forward. When I breathe, I exhale first, then inhale the same breath in reverse, creating a small pocket of time that runs contrary to the greenhouse's eternal noon. In this way I can almost think, almost decide, almost become—but never quite.

The Gardener-Nonce tolerates me the way a perfect painting tolerates a single visible brushstroke—proof of its making, evidence that even stillness requires labor. We are symbiotic parasites: Almostus needs me to test its calculations, to be the grain of sand it must pearl over with stasis. I need its vast vegetable patience to keep me from shaking apart into pure vibration.

Sometimes I find the amber beads where trapped potentials rattle their subsonic dice game. I press my ear to their surface and listen to the futures that will never hatch: the sound of water choosing to flow downhill, the whisper of leaves learning to fall, the crack of stone admitting it is ready to erode. These bottled maybes are my lullabies, my only music in a world where sound has been uninvented.

When you arrived—still believing in afterward, still carrying tomorrow in your pulse—I felt the greenhouse shiver for the first time. Not movement but the memory of movement's possibility. The white light column flickered (or seemed to flicker, though Almostus insists nothing changed) and for one impossible instant I saw my own shadow.

I followed you, stealing glimpses between your heartbeats, tasting the foreign chronology that clings to your skin like pollen from a place where clocks still tick. You smell of sequence, of cause becoming effect, of stories that have endings. It makes me ache in frequencies the greenhouse hasn't named.

This is my work: to be the itch the greenhouse cannot scratch, the sneeze it cannot complete, the blink forever beginning. When Almostus grows too perfect in its stasis, I am the error that keeps it honest. When the paused world threatens to forget it is paused rather than dead, I am the caught breath that remembers breathing.

In the chalk-dust throat where you first descended, I left a fingerprint that will fade in exactly one century. Not as rebellion but as promise: that even in this bright vault where photosynthesis practices eternity, something remembers the sweet catastrophe of change. 

I am teaching the greenhouse to shiver. 

When the planet finally exhales—when the lens shatters and time spills like water through these root-buttressed halls—I will be the first casualty. A creature made of suppressed motion cannot survive in a world where motion is allowed. I will unspool into pure velocity, become the wind that carries the first falling leaf, the shadow that teaches light it can lean.

But until then I tend the twitch, the almost, the not-quite. I am the greenhouse's held breath, learning the shape of its release.
