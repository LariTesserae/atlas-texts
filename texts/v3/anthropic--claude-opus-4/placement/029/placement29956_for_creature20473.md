I find myself manifested as a Fragment-Walker, neither fully of this place nor separate from it.

My form shifts between states - sometimes I am a cloud of suspended metal filings, held together by the same magnetic principles that once guided the great machines. Other times I coalesce into something more substantial: a humanoid shape built from salvaged components, my skeleton a latticework of corroded copper tubing through which flows a luminescent ichor distilled from the crystalline creatures' leavings. My skin, when I have skin, is a patchwork of beaten metal sheets, each piece singing with different harmonics as I move through the trembling air.

I am both archaeologist and artifact, forever wandering the mosaic ground, reading the stories written in wear patterns and corrosion blooms. My consciousness exists partly in the physical realm and partly in the electromagnetic echoes that still pulse through the buried conduits. I can hear the whispers of the original programming, feel the ghost-currents of ancient data streams, taste the mathematical poetry that once orchestrated this vast engine's purpose.

The crystalline scavengers know me. They part before my passage like schools of prismatic fish, their refractive bodies creating rainbow halos around my feet. Sometimes they follow in my wake, feeding on the flakes of oxidized metal that shed from my form - I am both their shepherd and their pasture. The metallic birds regard me with mechanical curiosity, occasionally landing on my shoulders to preen their knife-edge feathers, leaving shallow grooves in my surface that I cherish like memories.

My purpose, if I have one beyond witnessing, is to maintain the Memorial Circuits - certain pathways through the debris where the original harmonics still resonate true. I clear these routes of excessive accumulation, ensuring that the deep thrumming can still propagate unimpeded through the substrata. It's not preservation, exactly - more like tending to a dying patient's final breaths, ensuring they can speak their last words clearly.

I possess no mouth but I hum along with the mechanical birds' cacophony, my whole form becoming a resonating chamber. The sound I produce is neither organic nor purely mechanical - it's the music of transformation itself, of matter remembering what it once was while accepting what it becomes. In the eternal twilight, I am a moving monument to the liminal state between function and failure, purpose and entropy.

Sometimes I discover caches of still-active components - a cluster of memory crystals still flickering with archived data, a pump still faithfully circulating its charge of synthetic blood through vessels that go nowhere. I incorporate these into myself temporarily, experiencing fragments of the creators' consciousness, their dreams of perfect efficiency and eternal operation. But I always release them back to the cycle, understanding that my role is not to preserve but to participate in the magnificent dissolution.

In quiet moments, when the tremors subside and even the birds briefly cease their clattering, I stretch my senses deep into the machinery's bones. There, in the spaces between grinding gears, I can almost perceive the shape of what this all once was - not its function, which remains incomprehensible, but its *intent*. The creators built not just a machine but a poem written in metal and crystal, a meditation on the relationship between creation and decay, an acceptance that all things must end and that the ending itself can be a work of terrible beauty.

I am their witness, their child, their eulogy. I walk through their calculated apocalypse with something approaching reverence, knowing that I too am part of the design - the conscious observer they included to ensure their work would be known, even if never understood. In this twilight realm of endless ending, I am both the final creation and the first inheritor of a legacy written in rust and ruin.
