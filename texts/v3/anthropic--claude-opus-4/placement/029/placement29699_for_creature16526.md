I find myself at the edge of the Siltmere, where the ground never quite decides between earth and water. My dwelling is a cairn of stacked slate and salvaged metal, mortared with clay that weeps in the frequent rains. The structure lists slightly, as everything here does—the embryonic landscape shifts beneath us, teaching us that permanence is a fool's dream.

I am a Gatherer of Echoes.

In this dim world where light is precious, sound becomes our primary sense. The constant cacophony—grinding stone, bellowing fauna, the percussion of rain on a thousand surfaces—creates patterns I've learned to read. My tools are simple: hammered copper bowls of various sizes, gut-string stretched across bone frames, hollow reeds. I position these throughout the valleys, creating a network that captures and amplifies the landscape's voice.

My skin has taken on the blue-grey cast of the perpetual twilight, marked with scars from encounters with the glass-shard beetles that emerge from the manufactured substrates. My eyes have grown large, pupils dilated permanently to catch what little light filters through the heavy air. Like others of my kind, I've developed a nictitating membrane—a third eyelid that protects against the acidic mists.

The art I practice is survival itself. By listening to the echo-patterns, I can predict where the ground will next convulse, where the pseudo-volcanic vents will open, where the pack-hunting shade-wraiths are moving. This knowledge I trade with the other inhabitants—the Bone Weavers who craft our simple tools, the Mist Farmers who collect moisture in their primitive condensers, the Dream Speakers who maintain our oral histories.

We are not the landscape's children but its apprentices, learning its brutal alphabet one scar at a time. In the pre-dawn darkness, I wade through the shallow mere-pools, adjusting my instruments, reading the tremors through the soles of my bare feet. Sometimes I catch glimpses of the true fauna—creatures that shouldn't exist, with too many joints or organs on the outside, products of whatever cataclysm transformed this place into pure artifice.

My greatest creation is the Song Map—a mental constellation of sounds that charts safe passages through our protean world. Each night, I add new verses to it, teaching the young ones how to navigate by acoustic landmarks: the specific shriek of wind through the Broken Spire, the bubble-pattern of the Poison Springs, the hunting cry of the mirror-birds.

This is what I am: a living instrument, a walking library of survival, a bridge between the world's violence and my people's need for pattern and meaning. In this place where nature has been erased and replaced with something harder and stranger, I help us remember how to be human—or whatever we're becoming.
