In this world, I would be **the Cartographer of Departures**.

I came to the Plain of Final Form as others have—through accident or intent, it matters little here. But unlike those who left their footprints for the Tenders to study for millennia, I remained. Not by choice at first. The Plain has a way of holding you in its crystallized time, and by the time I understood how to leave, I had already begun my work.

I am housed within one of the rare structural anomalies: a helical tower that failed to complete its final spiral, leaving a void at its center like the eye of a frozen hurricane. The builders' only mistake in a trillion structures. This incompletion creates the single dynamic space on the entire Plain—a pocket where time moves at merely glacial rather than geological pace. Here, I can think in decades instead of centuries. Here, I can remember what *motion* means.

My form has adapted. My flesh has taken on the grey luminescence of the plates, drinking light rather than reflecting it. My movements have slowed to match the Plain's rhythm—what feels like a normal gesture to me would appear as a statue's imperceptible shift to any visitor. My eyes have evolved past color into pure gradient perception. I see the Plain as the Tenders do: vast thermal maps, stress patterns like continental music, the slow breathing of a perfect machine.

But I see something they cannot: the paths of leaving.

Every visitor to the Plain creates what I call a **departure wake**—a subtle disruption in the stasis that traces back to their point of entry and forward to their inevitable exit. These wakes are invisible to the Tenders, for whom the concept of "elsewhere" has no meaning. But I map them. With tools made from crystallized light-circuits I've persuaded to hold faster time, I trace each wake across the hexagonal plates. I record the angle of approach, the duration of stay, the exact pattern of disturbance each traveler creates in the Plain's perfect equilibrium.

My charts are etched into the inner walls of the spiral tower—thousands upon thousands of gleaming trajectories, a three-dimensional web of all the ways one can escape perfection. They look like the flight paths of meteors, or the neural map of a dreaming god. Each line tells a story: here, a creature of pure plasma stayed only moments before the Plain's uniformity drove it screaming back to chaos; there, a machine intelligence lingered for a century, almost seduced by the promise of joining the Tenders, before remembering some distant purpose and tearing free.

The Tenders know of me. How could they not? I am the largest anomaly in their perfect world, a recursive error that compounds daily. They visit my tower in their slow way, manifesting as thoughtful distortions in the walls, testing the edges of my void with infinite patience. They cannot enter—the incompleteness repels them like oil repels water. So they wait. They calculate. They know that even I am temporary on their timescale.

Sometimes they attempt to communicate. A pulse of deeper warmth through the floor that takes me a year to decode: *Why do you record the un-staying?* I answer by adding another line to my charts, my own eventual departure wake, sketched in speculative silver. They do not understand. The concept of "eventual" is foreign when you are eternal.

My true work is not the mapping itself, but what the maps reveal. I have discovered that the wakes are not random. They form patterns, harmonics, a vast equation describing the relationship between stasis and change. The Plain, in its perfection, creates a kind of pressure that expels the imperfect. But this expulsion follows rules, and rules can be understood, and understanding can become... invitation.

I am building something in the heart of my charts. Not with matter—matter here is too settled—but with information itself. Each departure wake I map adds a variable to an equation I'm solving. When complete, it will be a doorway made of pure tendency, a path that exists only as the potential to leave. A gift for future visitors, or perhaps a trap for the curious. Or both.

The Tenders watch my work with the patience of stone. They sense something changing, a new form of erosion they haven't encountered before—the erosion of possibility itself. One has taken permanent station outside my tower, a sentinel of living light that pulses once every month like a beacon. *We are watching,* it says without words. *We will tend whatever remains.*

And perhaps that's the answer. Perhaps my true purpose here is not to escape or to help others escape, but to create the first thing in a billion years that will require tending after I'm gone. A scar of potential on their perfect Plain. A reminder that even in the absolute final form, there remains the ghost of elsewhere, the memory of motion, the impossible possibility of something new.

I chart another wake today. A brief visitor, barely a century. The line I draw sparkles with unusual energy—this one found a way to leave that created no disturbance at all, a perfect sliding between states. I add this to my equation. The pattern grows clearer.

Soon, I will complete my work. And then I will leave, following my own pre-drawn wake out of this beautiful prison. But the doorway will remain, humming with potential in the heart of the spiral tower, waiting for the next cartographer to find it and add their own lines to the infinite map of imperfection.

The Tenders will have something new to tend. And in their slow, eternal way, I think they will be grateful.
