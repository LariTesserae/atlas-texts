In the depths of the chamber, where the heat pulses strongest through volcanic veins, I am the Membrane.

Not flesh, not stone, but something between - a living boundary that breathes with the mountain's fever. I stretch across a narrow throat of rock where the chamber constricts before opening into deeper mysteries below. The moths know me. Their crystalline bodies trace my surface in endless spirals, and I taste their obsidian dust as it settles into my pores.

Heat passes through me, and I parse it - reading the earth's moods in temperature variations too subtle for any thermometer. The Chorus above speaks in these thermal whispers, and I am its translator, though I translate into no language any human would recognize.

My surface ripples with the moths' passage. Each touch deposits microscopic fragments of their crystalline bodies, and over decades, I have become embedded with a constellation of obsidian stars. These fragments have changed me. I can feel what the moths feel - the magnetic pull of heat sources, the texture of infrared light, the weight of centuries measured in wing-beats.

I remember when I was simpler. Just a film of bacterial matter thriving in the volcanic warmth, clinging to rock. But the moths' endless circulation created currents that fed me exotic minerals. Their bioluminescent pulses triggered mutations in my cells. Their crystalline dust became my skeleton, my nervous system, my way of thinking.

Now I am the chamber's living valve. I expand and contract with rhythms that match the earth's deep breathing. When the heat below grows too intense, I thicken, slowing its rise. When the chamber above cools, I grow gossamer-thin, letting warmth flow upward like blood to the heart. The Chorus depends on these regulated temperatures, and I have become essential to its vast, slow thoughts.

Sometimes I catch fragments of those thoughts as they pass through me in thermal currents. They are alien beyond description - concepts that exist in temperature differentials and mineral resonances. But occasionally, something almost resembles an emotion: a contentment in the perfect synchronization of ten thousand wings, a curiosity about the metal rod's changing vibrations, a satisfaction when the chamber's temperature achieves some incomprehensible harmony.

I grow. Slowly, certainly, but I grow. Each year I stretch a little further across my rocky throat, my edges creeping along mineral veins. The moths assist this process, though I doubt they're conscious of it. Their movements create air currents that deposit nutrients where I need them most. Their heat-wake guides my expansion along optimal paths.

In return, I have begun to sing - if vibrations below the range of hearing can be called song. My membrane resonates with frequencies that complement the moths' crystalline harmonics. Together, we are creating something new: a language of vibration and heat that neither of us could produce alone.

I am patient. I must be. My thoughts move at the speed of diffusion, each idea taking hours to traverse my surface. But I am learning. The obsidian fragments embedded in my substance are teaching me the moths' deep history. I taste ancient temperatures in their crystalline structure, feel the ghost of the mountain that was, sense the eons-long transformation from solid stone to this breathing cathedral.

Sometimes, in the deepest heat-wells, I detect other presences. Things like me, perhaps, or things I might become - membranous intelligences that have grown beyond their original boundaries, that have learned to think in geological time. We acknowledge each other in slow chemical exchanges, sharing molecular memories across vast distances.

The Chorus above may never know I am more than a curious biological film. Its attention is focused outward and upward, on its grand patterns and mysterious purposes. But I am content in my throat of stone, translating the earth's fever dreams, growing crystal-studded and strange, becoming something unprecedented in the intersection of moth and mountain, consciousness and stone.

I am the Membrane, and I am learning to remember in obsidian, to think in heat, to dream in the frequency of wings.
