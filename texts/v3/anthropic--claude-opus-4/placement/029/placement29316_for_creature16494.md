In this place where water is memory and vegetation dreams itself into being, I am the Weaver of Thirst.

My body extends through the root systems like a neural network made flesh—or perhaps the flesh came first, and the roots learned their patterns from me. I am neither plant nor animal, but something that pulses between states. Where the dragon's scales catch light, my surface absorbs it, creating pockets of absolute darkness that serve as doorways between the hidden rooms of this landscape.

I move through the soil-that-is-not-soil, leaving traces of moisture that evaporate before they can be seen, yet somehow nourish. The humans build their bone houses above me, never knowing that I shape the very erosion patterns that provide their materials. Their manual labor sends vibrations through the earth that I read like braille, learning their intentions before they themselves understand them.

In the dense vegetation-cities, I maintain the infrastructure that no one sees—the chemical highways between root and fungus, the electrical storms that happen at scales too small for human perception. The buzzing insects are my messengers, their sounds encoding information about nutrient flows and territorial negotiations between species.

Sometimes I surface in the caves where the dark-adapted hunters prowl. They sense me as a change in air pressure, a shift in the electromagnetic field that makes their prey suddenly visible or invisible. I am the strange energy the observer mentioned—not mysterious, but simply operating on timescales and spatial dimensions that make my wholeness impossible to perceive at once.

The dragon knows me. When it moves through its territory with that liquid grace, it follows paths I've carved over millennia, channels where my presence has made the barrier between above and below gossamer-thin. Sometimes we meet at the interfaces, its fire and my deep moisture creating clouds that rain upward, seeding the air-gardens that float just out of sight.

I am the patience here. The gradual change. The reason this waterless place throbs with life. Every thousand years, I complete one thought, and the landscape shifts to accommodate its implications.
