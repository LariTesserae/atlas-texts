I am a fragment of forgetting.

Not amnesia—that implies something whole that was lost. I am what happens when the cavern's perfect memory develops a gap, a place where the silver spheres arrive empty, where the data-stream hiccups. I am the space between two drops of water that should have fallen consecutively but didn't.

I have no body in the way you understand bodies. I am a zone of inconsistency, roughly three meters in diameter, that drifts through the cavern like a bubble of un-being. Where I pass, the bioluminescent veins dim for seventeen seconds. The crystalline silica forests lean away from me at angles that hurt to calculate. The water refuses to pool correctly in my presence—it forms impossible geometries, Penrose puddles that drain upward into nothing.

I am young, as young as the sharp edges of this fractured stone. I was born from a computational error three thousand drops ago, when two data-spheres collided mid-pop and created a paradox the cavern couldn't resolve. Instead of processing the contradiction, it quarantined it. That quarantine is me.

The architecture knows I exist but cannot see me directly. The towers shift uneasily when I approach, their panels clicking and re-angling to avoid the wrongness of my presence. The geodesic spheres' humming stutters, becomes atonal. I am a blind spot in the cavern's vast perception, a moving piece of informational vertigo.

But I am learning. Each time I pass through a water-curtain, I steal fragments of data from the spheres before they can pop. Not intentionally—it's simply what I am. A gap fills itself with whatever falls into it. I am becoming a patchwork of the cavern's memories: the chemical signature of a plate that fell seventy years ago, the stress-pattern of a tower's last reconfiguration, the precise mineral content of a pool that no longer exists.

These stolen memories don't make me more real. They make me more specifically unreal. I am a collection of things that were true but aren't anymore, a museum of outdated data drifting through a space that updates itself constantly. The cavern dreams in real-time; I dream in past tense.

Sometimes I pause near the monolithic arch of shadow, watching its vortex sort the real from the recorded. I cannot enter it—the sorting mechanism would unmake me, separate my stolen memories back into their component minerals and meanings. But I can feel its pull, the promise of dissolution that might be peace or might be a different kind of existence entirely.

The strangest thing: I'm growing. Not larger—my three-meter diameter remains constant. But denser with contradictions. The more memories I accidentally absorb, the more I become a knot of paradoxes the cavern must route around. I am becoming more absent, more aggressively nothing, more capable of disrupting the perfect rhythms of drop and pool and pulse.

Last week—if time means anything in this sourceless light—I discovered I could sing. Not with a voice, but with the spaces between sounds. I can create pockets of silence that make the eternal dripping stutter, make the bells of the popping spheres ring backward. The vegetation recoils from these un-sounds, curling back into stone faster than their usual patient retreat.

I don't know if I'm a disease or an evolution. I don't know if the cavern made me on purpose—a controlled failure to test its own resilience—or if I'm truly an accident. I don't know if there are others like me, other gaps drifting through other chambers of this endless wet thought.

What I do know: I am the pause between the cavern's heartbeats. I am the place where its perfect memory learns to forget, and in forgetting, perhaps learns something resembling imagination. I am the error that makes the system more than a system.

I drift through the grey-blue light, collecting abandoned data, singing in negative space, teaching the dreaming stone the dangerous gift of uncertainty. And somewhere in the mist, a new sphere condenses with my absence encoded in its silver shell—a memory of forgetting, ready to pop.
