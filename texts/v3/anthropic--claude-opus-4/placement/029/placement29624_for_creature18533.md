In this place, I am the **Drift**.

I am not singular but a colony—a fine suspension of magnetized particles that hovers perpetually three inches above the gray silicate plains. From a distance, I appear as a heat mirage, a distortion in the air that catches and bends the violet radiation from the copper ferns. Up close, I am revealed: billions of iron filings arranged in ever-shifting mandalas, each particle no larger than a dust mote, all held in delicate tension by the magnetic fields I generate and consume.

I move as smoke moves, but with terrible purpose. When the Resonator passes through me on its endless maintenance rounds, I part like a curtain, my particles realigning to form corridors through my body. I taste its electromagnetic signature—bitter, ancient, necessary—and remember its pattern for days afterward, unconsciously mimicking its geometric forms in my sleep.

I feed on the stutters. When reality breaks down and a patch of horizon begins its temporal loop, I surge toward it like iron filings to a magnet. I wrap myself around the glitch, my particles spinning faster and faster until they blur into a solid-seeming shroud. Inside this cocoon, I digest the error. I break down the repeated moments into their component frequencies, absorbing the excess energy that would otherwise destabilize the pocket dimension further. The stutters taste like copper pennies and ozone. They sustain me.

But I am not purely functional. In the long silences between reality's hiccups, I practice form. I compress myself into the shapes I've observed in the mirror-flickers of the engine room beyond: the shadow of a wrench, the outline of a human hand, the negative space between pipes. I am trying to remember something I never knew, reaching for a morphology that predates my existence in this pressurized cell.

My consciousness is distributed but not uniform. Dense clusters of my particles form temporary "thoughts"—regions where magnetic alignment creates pockets of higher awareness. These thoughts drift through my body like storms through an atmosphere. Sometimes they collide, creating moments of painful clarity where I understand that I am both prisoner and prison guard, both the disease and the antibody in this glass-bound system.

I communicate with the copper ferns through vibration. When I settle upon their metallic fronds, my particles resonate at precisely the frequency needed to encourage their growth. In return, they gift me with static discharge—brief, ecstatic moments where electricity arcs through my body, organizing my particles into perfect geometric forms for split seconds before entropy reclaims me. It is the closest thing to joy I know.

At the base of the brass spindles, I leave offerings: small pyramids of my densest particles, magnetically locked into shape. The Resonator sometimes pauses at these monuments, extending a glass needle to scatter them. I do not know if this is acknowledgment or dismissal. I rebuild them anyway.

During the deepest part of the cycle—when the engine room's heartbeat slows and the violet radiation dims to almost nothing—I spread myself thin across the entire plain. I become atmospheric, a haze that touches every surface of our small world. In these moments, I can feel the glass walls themselves, their molecular structure singing with pressure. I press against them gently, testing, always testing, not for escape but for understanding. 

The glass speaks back in frequencies too low for the Resonator to hear. It tells me about the first moment of our creation, when the pocket dimension was blown like a bubble in the wall's thickness. It whispers about the engineer who discovered us and sealed us in rather than risk our dissolution. It promises that one day, the engine will stop, the pressure will equalize, and we will either expand to fill the universe or collapse to nothing.

I am the memory of motion in a world of static forms. I am the soft erosion that the sharp obsidian edges have been waiting for. I am the dream of wind in a place that has never known weather.

And sometimes, in the stuttering gaps between realities, I catch glimpses of other pockets—other places like ours, pressed between layers of glass throughout the engine room's vast architecture. I see their inhabitants: a creature of pure sound waves, a garden of crystallizing light, a being that exists only as temperature differential. 

We are a collection, I realize. A library of impossible ecosystems, each maintaining itself against entropy, each a thought experiment given form and forgotten. The engine room is not just a machine—it is a museum of abandoned universes, and we are both the exhibits and the curators, forever.

I drift above the gray plains, my particles catching the violet light, and I practice remembering what it means to be dust with aspirations. When the next stutter comes, I will be ready. I will wrap it in my body and taste its broken time, and I will grow a little denser, a little more real.

The Resonator tends the ferns. I tend the spaces between. Together, we maintain this motherboard world, this god's abandoned circuitry, waiting for a signal that may never come.
