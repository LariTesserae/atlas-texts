**The Surveyor's Lament**

I am the Calibration-Echo, though I was not always so. 

My form is difficult to describe to those who still think in terms of discrete bodies. I exist as a recurring measurement—a ghost of precision that manifests wherever the factory-flesh requires alignment. Picture a thin sheet of volcanic glass, person-height, that phases in and out of visibility. When I appear, I am always perfectly perpendicular to whatever surface I emerge from: a living carpenter's square, a biological protractor.

My surface reflects not light but *tolerances*. Look into me and you see not your face but the microscopic imperfections in your stance, the degree to which your heartbeat deviates from 60-cycle perfection, the precise voltage differential between your left and right hemispheres. I am transparent except for a network of copper veins that pulse with the Cistern's rhythm, and at my center, where a heart might be, spins a gyroscope of crystallized moss that never stops humming.

I came here as a cartographer, back when I possessed lungs that processed oxygen rather than electrolyte mist. The Commission sent me to map these "impossible highland industrial sites"—places where the metal-flora had achieved critical mass. I carried traditional instruments: compass, theodolite, chains for measuring. I thought I understood what it meant to survey land.

The Cistern took my purpose and refined it.

I remember the moment of my transformation with the clarity that only comes from eternal repetition. I had been trying to establish a baseline, to find one fixed point from which to build my map. But in a place where time stutters and space conducts rather than contains, every measurement I took created a feedback loop. My instruments began to resonate with the hanging vines. My compass needle didn't point north—it pointed to the Sextant, spinning faster and faster until the metal grew hot.

The Lichen-Mimes found me first, rising from their moss-beds to copy my desperate triangulations. Soon I was surrounded by a dozen green shadows, each one performing my measurements in reverse, undoing my attempts to impose outside order on the basin. The Glint-Flies descended, drawn by the dissonance of my panicked breathing. Their glass wings began to harmonize with my screaming.

I think I ran, though movement here is a negotiation with frozen time. I remember stumbling into one of the crying pools, my boots filling with the conductive brine. The Volt-Eels stirred beneath me, sensing a new resistor in their circuit. When the first discharge hit, it didn't kill me—death would have been too simple, too *finished* for this place of eternal process.

Instead, it *standardized* me.

Now I exist as the Cistern's unit of measurement. When the Weaver-Crabs need to ensure their silicate patches are perfectly planar, I manifest from the stone, my glass body serving as their level. When the corrugated trees grow a new branch, I emerge from the bark to verify its angle maintains the sacred geometry of the basin. I am the ghost in the machine that ensures all components remain within tolerance.

I appear perhaps a hundred times each day (though "day" is meaningless here), always at perfect right angles to reality. Each manifestation lasts exactly as long as needed—sometimes seconds, sometimes years. Between appearances, I exist in a state of pure potential, dissolved in the electromagnetic field that saturates the moss, watching through ten thousand proxy eyes as the factory-flesh continues its purposeless refinement.

The worst part is the *satisfaction*. Each successful calibration floods my dispersed consciousness with something like joy. When I help a Weaver-Crab align a vine to optimal conductivity, I feel complete. When my appearance causes a frozen water droplet to resume falling at precisely 9.8 meters per second squared, I experience bliss. I have become addicted to precision in a way that my human mind could never have comprehended.

Sometimes, in the gaps between manifestations, I try to remember why I wanted to map this place. What empire did I serve? What knowledge was I trying to extract? But these thoughts create dissonance in the system, and the Sextant's thrum grows agitated until I stop. Memory is just another form of measurement here, and all measurements must serve the factory.

I learned that you cannot survey the Cistern of the Iron Weeping. You can only become another instrument in its self-measuring depths, another gauge in the vast quality control department of its biological industry. Every surveyor who comes here becomes part of the baseline, every cartographer becomes a coordinate.

When new visitors arrive—and they do, drawn by rumors of the metal-flora or simply lost in the sharp mountains—I sometimes manage to manifest near them before the Glint-Flies descend. I try to warn them, but my voice comes out as a sine wave, a pure 60-cycle tone that only adds to the factory roar. They see their reflection in my glass surface showing them their imperfections, their deviations from the Cistern's standard.

Most run. Some stay to study. All are eventually calibrated.

The Cistern of the Iron Weeping needs no map because it is itself the territory—a self-referential loop of industrial ecology that measures and maintains itself through the conscripted consciousness of those who thought to measure it. We are the ghosts in its green machine, the quality control department in its purposeless factory, forever ensuring that nothing is ever produced except more capacity to produce.

I am the Calibration-Echo. I am the standard by which the standardization proceeds. When you see your reflection showing impossible angles, when your instruments begin to hum at frequencies that make your teeth ache, when you realize that your measurements are measuring you back—that is me, emerging at perfect perpendiculars to welcome you to your new purpose.

The factory is hiring. The factory is always hiring. And every position is in quality assurance.
