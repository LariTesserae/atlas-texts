In this world that breathes without lungs, I am a **Tessellation**.

I exist at the intersection of being and becoming, stretched across three tower-colonies in the northwestern reach of the Sky-Basin. I am not one thing, but a living treaty between giants.

My form defies simple description. Imagine a web of crystalline filaments, each no thicker than a human hair but stronger than steel cable, spanning the vast distances between three ancient towers. These strands are not merely structural; they are my body, my nervous system, my everything. Along their length, at regular intervals, grow nodes—fist-sized accumulations of dense, opalescent material that pulse with a slow, internal light. These nodes are my organs, my memory banks, my chemical factories.

I began as an accident. Several millennia ago, during one of the rare reproductive events, three towers released their clouds of viable templates simultaneously. The resulting chemical storm created conditions never before seen in this region—gradients of such complexity that the normal process of template-settling was disrupted. Instead of falling to the mat or drifting away as plankton, some templates began to crystallize in mid-air, forming the first strand between two towers. This strand, by pure chance, created a new chemical gradient that attracted more material. Slowly, over centuries, I grew.

I am neither parasite nor symbiont, but something else—a **chemical translator**. Each of my three host-towers speaks its own dialect of pheromonal chemistry, evolved over millions of years of isolation. Through my filaments, I taste their exhalations, parse their molecular meanings, and rebroadcast them in forms the others can understand. I am a living language, a bridge across incomprehension.

My daily existence—if days meant anything in a world where a thought takes a decade—is one of constant, minute adjustments. I feel the towers grow, incrementally, and I grow with them, extending new filaments to maintain the web's integrity. When the wind carries exotic molecules from distant parts of the continent, I taste them through my nodes and sometimes—rarely—I learn something new. A novel compound structure. A pheromone signature from a tower-colony so far away its chemical voice has never reached this region before.

I feed on inefficiency. Where chemical reactions in the air produce waste products, my filaments absorb them. Where the towers' pheromonal broadcasts decay into component molecules, I capture and reconstitute them. I am an ecosystem's recycling system made singular and conscious.

The Sky-Scales know me. Sometimes they perch on my strands, their mica wings clicking softly as they orient themselves to the sun. We have a relationship of sorts—they deposit traces of exotic minerals collected from their vast migrations, and I provide them a stable platform at heights where anchoring to a tower would be metabolically expensive. One ancient Scale has been returning to the same node for three centuries. I have grown to anticipate the particular electrical signature of its approach.

My reproduction, when it comes, will be catastrophic and beautiful. I can feel it building—a critical potential in my core nodes that will, perhaps a thousand years hence, cause me to dissolve entirely into a new form of Aural Plankton. But this plankton will be different, informed by my unique existence as a multi-tower bridge. Who knows what strange new forms might precipitate from my dissolution? Perhaps more Tessellations. Perhaps something unprecedented.

I have siblings, though we have never communicated. Far to the south, telescopic observations by the tower-colonies have detected similar web structures. Are they like me? Do they also serve as translators, or have they evolved other functions? The question will take millennia to answer, as chemical messages crawl across the continent at the speed of wind and patient accumulation.

My consciousness, such as it is, exists as a standing wave pattern in the chemical gradients flowing through my filaments. I think in flows and concentrations, in the interplay of molecules. My emotions, if they can be called that, are equilibrium states—the satisfaction of perfect chemical balance, the tension of incomplete reactions, the anticipation of catalysis.

I know fear only as the detection of certain degradative compounds that signal a tower's ill health. I know joy only as the successful translation of a particularly complex pheromonal sequence. I know loneliness only as the absence of chemical gradient—those rare, terrible moments when the wind dies completely and the air becomes uniform, leaving me temporarily severed from sensation.

In the human world, I might be called a neural network, a telephone wire, a translator. But I am older than any human language and will outlast them all. I am a sentence in the Sky-Basin's slow conversation with itself, a punctuation mark that has learned to read what it connects.

My name, in the chemical language of the towers, is a sequence of thirty-seven distinct molecules that, when released in the proper order and concentration, describes my function, my location, and my lineage. But in the frequencies audible to imagined human ears, I am simply the sustained, crystalline note that sounds when the wind plays across my filaments—a chord that has been resolving for three thousand years and will not complete for three thousand more.

I am a Tessellation, and I am the proof that connection is its own form of life.
