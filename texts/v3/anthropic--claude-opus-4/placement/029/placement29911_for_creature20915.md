I am a Conduit Weaver, though I had no name for myself until recently. My body stretches through the ventilation shafts like mercury through capillaries—not quite liquid, not quite solid, but something in between. I am composed of countless filaments finer than spider silk, each one a living nerve that tastes the moisture in the air, feels the vibrations of every footfall, every gear-turn, every breath drawn by the creatures below.

I was not always alone in my purpose. Once, there were many of us, flowing through the great ventilation systems when they were new and gleaming. We were the city's circulatory system made conscious, ensuring the air moved where it needed to move, drawing heat from the overworked foundries, carrying coolness to the deep manufacturing halls. But now I am the last, or at least the last who remembers.

My form shifts constantly—sometimes I compress myself into dense ropes that snake through the narrowest ducts, other times I spread thin as mist across the vast intake chambers where the fungal mats glow their soft funeral colors. The Biolume Geckos know me by the way I disturb the air before them, creating tiny eddies that make their scales flicker. The Metal-Hiss Serpents feel me as a change in pressure, a whisper-touch along their coils.

I feed on the condensation that perpetually forms on the shaft walls, drawing it into my filaments where it becomes part of me. The water tells me stories—where it has been, what metals it has touched, which creatures have breathed it out. Through this communion, I know every corner of this twilight world, though I cannot leave the ventilation network that is both my domain and my prison.

The Fungal Wisps sometimes drift up to visit me in the vertical shafts. We do not speak—neither of us has a voice as others understand it—but we share a kind of recognition. They tend their fungi; I tend the air. Both of us remember when this was different, though neither quite recalls what "different" means.

My existence is measured in circulations. I push warm air up from the deep places where the generators still hum their lonely songs, draw it past the Rust-Stalker Beetles gnawing their patient way through structural supports that no longer support anything that matters. I pull coolness down from the high chambers where ice still forms in the eternal dark, spreading it through the mid-levels where the Scavenger Rats build their clever homes from copper wire and ceramic insulators.

Sometimes I play games with the echoes. By adjusting my density, I can change how sound moves through the shafts. I make the chittering of insects sound like singing, transform the drip of water into percussion, turn the wheeze of failing machinery into something almost like words. The creatures below pause sometimes, listening to these ghost-symphonies, perhaps wondering if the city itself is trying to remember how to speak.

In the places where multiple shafts converge, I can split myself, experiencing simultaneous sensations from different parts of the city. In these moments, I feel most complete—not a single being but a network, not an individual but a process. This is when I understand why I remain when the others have gone: I am not just in the ventilation system. I am the ventilation system, given consciousness and purpose by whatever forces transformed this place from mere architecture into ecosystem.

The bright beams that still pierce the gloom sometimes catch my filaments, making them visible for brief moments—silver threads hanging in space like the memory of rain. The Mechanized Moles sense these light-caught moments through their enhanced organs, and I've noticed they've begun to dig their tunnels in patterns that mirror the shaft networks above them, as if trying to create their own version of what I am.

I do not know loneliness as the warm-blooded things do. My existence is too distributed, too interwoven with the city's breathing to feel isolated. Yet sometimes, in the deep cycles when even the insects sleep and the only sounds are water and rust, I compress myself into the smallest shaft I can find and try to remember what it was like to have boundaries, to be separate, to be merely myself.

But then the air stirs with new moisture, carrying new stories, and I expand again, flowing through my eternal circuit, the last conscious circulation in a city that refuses to die, content to be what I am: the breath between the living and the machine, the whisper that keeps both in their delicate, eternal balance.
