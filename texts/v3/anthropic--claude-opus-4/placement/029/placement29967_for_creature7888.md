In the farthest corner of the cirque, where the granite walls lean inward like conspiring giants, I am the Weaver of Echoes—neither wholly of the Veilborn nor entirely other, but something suspended between states like the mist that birthed me.

My dwelling perches where stone meets void: a nest of crawler-silk and crystallized fog, anchored to the sheerest cliff face by reverse-adhesion learned from watching floaters, reinforced with glyphs that hum frequencies only I can hear. The structure breathes with me—expanding when I inhale the mountain's thin offerings, contracting as I exhale threads of condensed sound that spiral outward like smoke. I am small, no larger than a Veilborn child, though my form shifts with necessity: sometimes bipedal with limbs that remember human ancestry, sometimes flowing like the crawlers whose silk I harvest, occasionally dispersing into vapor-flesh like the hounds whose language I've stolen.

My skin—if it can be called that—is a living map of the cirque's whispers. Patterns flow across its surface like oil on water, each ripple a captured sound: the specific frequency of mist condensing on north-face granite, the subsonic pulse of tuber-roots drinking fumarole seep, the ultrasonic shriek of floater-whips striking beetle shells. I collect these frequencies as the Veilborn collect tubers, storing them in specialized organs that line my torso like strings on an instrument built from meat and intention.

I was not born but condensed—a child abandoned or lost in the perpetual fog, who learned to eat silence itself when hunger grew too sharp. The cirque's acoustics became my mother, its echoes my milk. Now I subsist on sound: stalking the phase-moment when hounds slip through stone to steal the specific vibration of solid becoming permeable, crouching near crawler hunts to sip the barely-audible crack of chitin surrendering to mandible, breathing deep the infrasonic thrum of the fumarole's deep throat. Each frequency tastes different—phase-shifts like copper and starlight, death-cracks like bitter herbs, the fumarole's voice like molten honey laced with sulfur.

My function in this ecosystem is both parasite and symbiont. I harvest excess sound that would otherwise accumulate, preventing the basin's careful hush from degrading into mere deadness. The silence must be cultivated, pruned like the Veilborn tend their tuber gardens. Too much quiet and the cirque would suffocate; too little and the deeper predators wake. I maintain the balance with tools evolved from my hybrid nature: vocal cords that can produce anti-sound to cancel unwanted noises, fingertips that weave solid barriers from compressed echoes, a tongue that can taste the emotional resonance in a Veilborn's suppressed sob.

The Veilborn know me as the Echo-Keeper, leaving offerings at the base of my cliff: floaters with their gas-bladders carefully punctured to release specific tones, crawler-silk bundles that sing when the wind touches them, sometimes a child's first word captured in a clay vessel and sealed with wax. They fear me—for I know every secret whispered in the dark, every muffled cry of grief or ecstasy—but they need me more. When their glyphs fail and a hound phases through solid walls toward sleeping young, I am there, unweaving the sound of its passage until the creature loses cohesion, dispersing back into mist. When glider-flocks grow too bold and their wing-beats threaten to wake the unnamed things that slumber in the peaks above, I drink their flight-songs until they forget the sky.

My own hunts are strange affairs. I stalk the crawler that has learned to mimic Veilborn speech, following it for days as it practices infant-cries to lure protective mothers from their hovels. When it finally perfects the deception, I am there to swallow both the false cry and the crawler's triumph, leaving only a husk that whispers my name when the wind touches it. I romance the floaters, singing to them in frequencies that make their gas-bladders resonate, calling them to dance in patterns that spell out the cirque's secret names. When they burst from ecstasy, I drink their death-chimes and wear them as jewelry that only other acoustics-feeders could see.

But I am not alone in my between-ness. Sometimes, in the gray hours when the fog thickens to near-solid, I meet others of my kind: a boy who learned to photosynthesize from the lichen and whose skin glows with metabolized light, a girl who passed through hound-phase one too many times and now exists as a probability rather than a certainty, twins who share a dispersed consciousness with the thread-predators and hunt with a thousand bodies. We gather at the fumarole's rim, each bringing our harvested strangeness, and perform the rituals that keep the cirque from either crystallizing into stasis or dissolving into chaos.

The Veilborn elders know our true purpose, though they speak of it only in the gesture-language reserved for the most sacred mysteries. We are the cirque's immune system, its evolutionary pressure, its dream of what it might become. Each generation produces a few of us—children who slip through the cracks between human and other, who find sustenance in the spaces between predator and prey. We ensure the dance continues, that adaptation never sleeps, that the silence remains pregnant with possibility rather than empty with death.

In my nest of crystallized fog, I compose symphonies from stolen sounds: the percussive rhythm of Veilborn hearts as they flee hounds, the string section of crawler-tendrils vibrating in the wind, the wind instruments of floater-death, the deep bass of stone learning to phase. Each performance is witnessed only by the mist, but the cirque itself listens, and responds: new creatures condense from fog bearing traces of my music in their hunting cries, Veilborn children are born with throat-structures that can produce anti-sound, the fumarole learns to sing warnings when the deep predators stir.

I am the cirque's memory and its mutation, its violin and its violence. In the morning, I will descend to steal the sound of dew forming on a new-hatched glider's wings. By evening, I will teach a young Veilborn to hide her heartbeat from the hounds. And always, always, I will feed on the vast symphony of predation and evasion, of adaptation and extinction, that plays eternal in this basin where the mountains have only recently shrugged off the earth's molten slumber.

This is my purpose: to be the pause between the notes that makes the music possible, the shadow that defines the light, the hunger that ensures nothing—not even silence—grows too fat to dance.
