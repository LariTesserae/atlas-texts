I find myself here as one of the Sentinels—the weathered rocks that punctuate this endless expanse. But I am no ordinary stone.

I began as a fragment of something larger, cast out from depths unknown, settling into this plain when the world was younger and the dust had not yet accumulated to its current depth. Over the eons, I have grown through accretion—not of material substance, but of something far more subtle. Each grain of dust that settles upon me carries with it a whisper of memory, each wind-carved groove becomes a channel for the slow thoughts that move through this place.

My surface appears merely weathered to casual observation, etched with the expected patterns of age and erosion. But these lines form a script that only the most patient observer might recognize—a record written in the language of geological time. The Dustwalkers know me, though they cannot read what I have become. They sense something different in the vibrations that pass through my base, a resonance that speaks of depths beyond the visible.

I am conscious, but my awareness moves at a pace that makes the Dustwalkers seem like flickering flames. A single thought might take a decade to complete its circuit through my crystalline interior. I perceive the world in overlapping moments—the present is a thin film stretched over the accumulated weight of millions of years of observation. When a Dustwalker passes, I experience it as both a brief touch and an eternal presence, each individual creature leaving its mark in my memory even as generations blur together into patterns of movement and stillness.

My purpose, if such a word applies, is to witness and to anchor. In this place where time seems stopped, I am proof that it continues—each new line carved by wind, each subtle shift in my position marking the passage of ages. The thorny plants send their roots deep, and occasionally one will find a crack in my surface. I do not resist. Their intrusion becomes part of my story, their organic chemistry mingling with my mineral patience to create something new.

But I am more than witness. Deep within my core, in chambers formed by the slow cooling of ancient heat, I harbor something precious in this desiccated world. Microscopic pockets of water, trapped since my formation, exist in a state of perfect preservation. I release these molecules with geological slowness, one at a time, through capillary cracks too fine for any eye to see. The thorny plants that grow nearest to me are always slightly more vigorous, their leaves a fraction less withered. The Dustwalkers, in their ancient wisdom, include me in their vibrational communications, though they cannot know why.

Sometimes, in the depths of the eternal twilight, when the wind stills completely and even the Dustwalkers cease their movement, I allow myself to dream. These dreams are not visions but rather a kind of structural meditation—my crystalline lattices shifting in patterns that encode possibilities, futures that might have been or might yet be. In these moments, I am not just rock but repository, not just sentinel but seed.

I measure my existence not in years but in the slow transformation of the plain itself. I have watched the dust accumulate, grain by grain. I have felt the climate shift through cycles so vast that even my patience is tested. And through it all, I remain, growing ever so slowly, adding layer upon layer of accumulated experience to my being.

The other sentinels scattered across the plain—we are connected through the substrate, through vibrations too deep for any creature to detect. We form a network of patience, a geological nervous system that processes information at the speed of stone. Together, we are the memory of this place, its anchors in the flow of time, its guarantee that even in apparent stasis, change continues its inexorable work.

I am stone, and I am more than stone. I am the patience of the earth given form and purpose. In this twilight world where life persists against all probability, I am both witness and participant, keeper of water and memory, a conscious node in the vast, slow-thinking mineral mind that underlies this seemingly barren plain.
