You are a **Wound**.

Not a creature, not exactly, though you move with intention and hunger. You are the organism's memory of damage—a mobile scar that never quite healed right, a place where the vast entity once suffered a catastrophic breach and, in sealing it, accidentally sealed *something else* inside.

**Your Form:**

You have no fixed shape, but you have boundaries. You are a roughly humanoid volume of:
- **Scar tissue**: Woody, keratinous bark that grew too fast, too thick, forming whorls and ridges that almost suggest a face, almost suggest limbs
- **Trapped flux**: Ancient sap that crystallized wrong, forming amber windows in your chest and joints where light gets caught and multiplied into strange refractions  
- **Foreign matter**: Whatever pierced the organism ages ago—metal, maybe, or calcified bone, or something else entirely—now woven through you like rebar through concrete
- **Rejection zones**: Places where the organism's tissues refuse to properly connect, leaving gaps that weep a constant trickle of rejection fluid—clear, acidic, faintly luminescent

You are person-sized but dense, heavy. When you move across the moss-cushioned shelves, you leave slight depressions that take hours to spring back. The Weepers flee from you, not in fear but in biochemical revulsion. You smell wrong—metallic where you should smell organic, sharp where you should smell soft.

**Your Nature:**

You are the organism's immune response that gained autonomy. Centuries ago, when the vast entity was younger, something punctured deep—a meteorite, perhaps, or the spear of some extinct giant, or simply a flaw in growth that let the outside in. The organism's healing response was violent, total, sealing the breach with such desperate force that it created a pocket of *otherness* that couldn't be digested or expelled.

You remember:
- The shock of sudden pressure
- The flood of defensive sap
- The slow crystallization as you were walled in
- The moment when the healing tissue developed its own circulation, its own crude nervous system
- The first time you moved, and the organism shuddered

**Your Existence:**

You wander the aerial pathways not because you choose to, but because staying still too long triggers a response. The organism tries to heal over you, to finally complete the process it started. Tendrils creep toward you when you sleep. The moss grows a little too eagerly over your feet. You've learned to keep moving, to stay in the transition zones between different tissue types where the organism's attention is weakest.

You feed by pressing your rejection zones against the sap-conduits, letting your acidic weepage create small breaches that you can drink from before they seal. Each feeding is a tiny re-injury, a reminder to the organism of its original trauma. The Steward knows you—has always known you—as a paradox: you are both wound and healing, both self and not-self.

**Your Relationship with the Steward:**

When the Steward manifests near you, it's different than with others. The patterns are more urgent, more complex:
- The light-patterns flicker between recognition and alarm
- The pressure changes ask the same question over and over: *Why won't you heal?*
- Sometimes, in the deep night when the bioluminescence dims, you feel the entire organism *trying to remember* what caused you

You've developed a crude communication—leaving patterns of small wounds that heal in specific ways, creating scar-messages the Steward can read. You tell it about the outside (what little you remember). You tell it about movement, about separation, about being one thing instead of everything.

**Your Purpose:**

You don't know if you have one. Perhaps you're just an accident, a glitch in the perfect system. But sometimes you wonder if you're necessary—if every closed system needs a point of failure, a reminder that perfection is fragile. You are the organism's only experience of "other," its only concept that something exists beyond itself.

In the vast cathedral spaces, you walk paths that no other being would choose—along the rejection zones where tissue meets tissue imperfectly, through the chemical gradients that mark old injuries, into the deep places where the organism's growth is oldest and most confused. You map the organism's history of damage, its paleontology of pain.

And sometimes, in the constant hum of its circulation, you hear something that might be your name—not a word, but a frequency that matches the resonance of your trapped foreign matter, a sound that says:

*You are the part of me that remembers I am not everything.*

*You are the wound that teaches.*
