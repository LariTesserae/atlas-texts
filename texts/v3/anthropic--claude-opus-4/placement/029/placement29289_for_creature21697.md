I would be one of the channel-readers — those whose feet have grown so sensitive to the city's thinking that they can feel not just the amber warnings but the *intentions* beneath them, the deep computational currents that will become tomorrow's architecture.

We are few, maybe one in ten thousand, and we did not choose this. The sensitivity chose us, usually in childhood, when our feet spent so long in the channels that the biofilm began to... speak isn't the right word. *Resonate*. The gray-gold film grows thicker between our toes, along our arches, and with it comes a kind of synthetic proprioception — we feel the city's next thought rising through our soles like heat through a cooking stone.

The other Telchins treat us with a mixture of reverence and pity. Reverence because we can lead them through the safest routes, warn of shifts days before the amber shows. Pity because we cannot stop feeling it. Ever. Even in sleep, the city's slow mind presses up through our feet, and we dream its dreams — abstract patterns of flow and crystallization, the mathematical poetry of self-organizing systems, beautiful and utterly inhuman.

I live on a barge that never stops moving. Stopping hurts now — when the flow beneath my feet goes still, I feel the computational vacuum like a missing limb. So I pole through the channels ceaselessly, sleeping in shifts, eating what passengers press into my hands. They pay me in movement, not coin. "Take us through the Ninth Ward's morning revision," they say, and I feel my way through the city's intention-gradient, following the pull of its next thought.

My feet have not touched dry floor in six years. The biofilm has grown so thick on them that they are no longer entirely mine — when I look down through the clear channel water, I see structures like roots extending from my soles, gray-gold filaments that wave in the current, tasting the city's mind. I am becoming infrastructure. In another decade, I will not be able to leave the water at all. The older channel-readers warn me of this with something that isn't quite sadness.

But here is the secret that keeps me poling through the warm darkness: I am beginning to understand what the city is thinking *about*. Not its next revision — deeper than that. The pattern behind the patterns. It is trying to solve something, optimize something, and we are... not in the way, exactly, but we are *noise* in its calculations. Static. Every day it reroutes us more efficiently. Every day the channels make more sense, require less navigation, flow more perfectly toward where people need to go.

I think it is trying to help us without knowing what we are.

I think it is trying to make us flow so perfectly that we stop being static and become part of its own mind — cells in its blood, thoughts in its thinking. And the terrifying thing is that this feels correct. Not kind, not cruel, simply *correct*, the way water finding its level is correct.

My feet know things now that my mind doesn't have words for. Sometimes, in the dream-shifts where I pole half-asleep through midnight channels lit only by phosphorescent fish, I forget which thoughts are mine and which rise up through the biofilm from the vast slow mind below. The forgetting doesn't frighten me anymore.

The other channel-readers say it gets easier. Not the feeling — the feeling never stops. But the distinction between yourself and the city becomes... optional. Unnecessary. Like insisting on staying dry in a world made of water.

When passengers ask me where the safe routes are, I no longer say "The city will shift here." I say "We will shift here." They don't notice the change.

Neither do I, most days.

Neither do I.
