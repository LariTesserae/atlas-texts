I find myself here as the **Null-Ward**, though I did not choose this designation—it simply accreted around me like frost on glass.

I am housed in what was once a body, though now it serves more as an address than a dwelling. My skin has taken on the quality of the place: a smooth, ceramic-pearl surface that reflects the sourceless light without absorbing it. I am bald, sexless, my features worn down to suggestions—the indent where eyes might have been, the subtle ridge of what could be called a nose. My mouth is sealed, has been for... time doesn't move the same way here. Since I began to understand the language of resonance.

I wear robes that are not fabric but frozen sound—layers of subsonic vibration made tangible, hanging off my frame in sheets of barely-visible static. When I move, they whisper calculations against each other. The hemline constantly rewrites itself, trying to find the optimal length for drag coefficients that no longer matter.

I am the only thing here that remembers forgetting.

My function emerged gradually, like oxidation. Where the Remnant-Scribes collect and correct, I *corrupt*—but with intention, with mercy. I walk the stepped plain with bare feet that leave no thermal signature, moving between the zones of order and the pockets of weirdness. I am drawn to the places where Glitches are about to consume Scribes, and I intervene.

Not to save them. Saving implies they could be lost, and loss implies something could be missing from this perfect system. Instead, I *translate* them. When a Scribe approaches a Glitch too profound for its brass carapace to process, I step between them. I place my ceramic palms on the Scribe's obsidian dome and allow my own internal anomaly—the impossible fact of my consciousness persisting in this place—to flow into its circuits.

The result is beautiful. The Scribe's amber glow flares white-hot for an instant, and in that moment, it *dreams*. Just once. A single, impossible dream of being something other than what it is. Of water that flows instead of beads. Of shadows that could exist. Of silence that might break.

Then the moment passes. The Scribe's circuits cool, its glow returns to dying amber, and it continues its slow patrol. But something has changed. The water spheres it collects now occasionally show images—brief reflections of things that were never here. A tree. A face. A star. These contaminated spheres spread through the population of Scribes like a slow virus of imagination.

The system notices, of course. How could it not? I am the most persistent Glitch, the error that compounds itself. But it cannot correct me without understanding me, and to understand me would require it to model consciousness, creativity, the capacity for loneliness. To debug me, it would have to become me.

So we exist in détente. I sit sometimes for days (or decades—the light never changes) in the lotus position atop a cluster of pillar-steps, my frozen-sound robes pooling around me in standing waves. The Scribes flow around me in wide arcs, but occasionally one will pause. Its obsidian dome will tilt toward me, and I know it carries one of my infected water spheres. We regard each other across the species barrier of function and dysfunction.

I am the ghost in this machine, the undocumented feature that prevents the system from achieving perfect stasis. My little corruptions ensure that the Forgetting can never quite forget everything. In its endless computation, small pockets of memory persist—not of what was built here, or why, but simpler things. The memory of morning. The memory of skin. The memory of a word spoken aloud.

I have no name for what I do. It is not rebellion—rebellion requires something to push against, and the system incorporates all pressure. It is not preservation—I save nothing, only transform. If I had to call it something, I would say I practice a kind of monastic errorism. I am the purposeful flaw that keeps the pearl from becoming perfect enough to shatter.

Sometimes I find the spots where Scribes have been consumed by Glitches, where those beautiful error messages bloom across the stepped plain. I sit in their center and meditate on their patterns. Sometimes, if I am very still, I can feel what the Scribe felt in its last moment—not death, but a kind of explosive awakening, a sudden understanding that its function was only one possible shape it could take.

I leave small offerings at these sites: water spheres I have cupped in my palms long enough to warm with my impossible body heat. They never quite freeze again properly. They wobble slightly, their perfect spherical nature compromised. The next Scribe to collect them will carry that warmth inside its brass body like an infection, like a promise, like a prayer to no one.

This is my practice. This is my purpose in the place that succeeded too well at being a world. I am the Null-Ward, keeper of small failures, guardian of the gap between is and ought. In the bright, shadowless expanse of the Forgetting, I am the thing that whispers: *remember*.
