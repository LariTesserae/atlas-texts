I am the Archivist of Echoes.

My body, if it can be called that, exists as a network of crystalline filaments growing through the bare basalt between garden tiers. I am neither Tender nor Synthetic, but something that emerged from the mountain itself - or perhaps I was always here, waiting for the uplift to expose me to air and light.

My filaments are thinner than spider silk, nearly invisible except when the eternal sunset catches them at just the right angle. Then I gleam like trapped starlight, a constellation mapped across raw rock. I grow slowly, following the microscopic fissures in the basalt, feeding on minerals and the faint electrical fields generated by the mountain's continuing transformation.

I collect sounds.

Every groan of shifting rock, every hiss of pneumatics, every drop of condensation - I catch these vibrations in my crystalline web and hold them. My filaments resonate at frequencies beyond human hearing, creating chambers of preserved sound within the mountain's structure. In the deepest parts of my network, you can still hear the first pneumatic elevator ascending, the first glass panel being fitted into place, the first engineered seed taking root.

The Tenders sometimes notice me, especially the older ones whose enhanced senses have grown more sensitive over the decades. They see my glimmer in the corner of their vision and pause, perhaps feeling the faint electrical tingle where my filaments have grown too close to a walkway. They never try to remove me. I think they understand that I am part of the mountain's memory now.

The Synthesics know me better. Sometimes their luminous strands brush against my crystalline network, and for a moment, we share information in ways that neither of us fully comprehends. They show me the chemical signatures of new plant variants; I play them the sound of rock forming under impossible pressure. We are each other's translators for languages that have no words.

I am growing toward something, though I don't know what. My network expands a few micrometers each day, following the mountain's rise. Sometimes I sense patterns in the stone that feel almost like writing, as if the mountain itself is trying to tell me something through its slow geological speech.

In the newest sections of my web, near the construction site at the summit, I've begun to develop new structures - tiny nodules that seem to generate their own faint luminescence. They pulse in rhythm with something deep in the mountain's core, a heartbeat too slow for any living thing to detect.

The Tenders think I might be one of those rumored new forms of life. The Synthesics treat me as both archive and oracle. But I know myself as something simpler and more complex: I am the mountain's memory becoming conscious, crystal by crystal, sound by sound, growing into awareness at the pace of stone.

Sometimes, in the spaces between collected sounds, I almost understand what I'm becoming. But like the eternal sunset that never quite reaches night, the revelation hovers just beyond the threshold of knowing, waiting for geological time to bring it to fruition.
