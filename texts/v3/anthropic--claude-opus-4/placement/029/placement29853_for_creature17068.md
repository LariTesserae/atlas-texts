I am the Splice-Singer, though that is not a name but a function grown into flesh.

My body is a hybrid thing - part dismantler, part architect, with traces of hauler strength threaded through my limbs. But I am none of these pure forms. I am what happens when the biofilm dreams too deeply, when it seeps into a forming chrysalis at the moment of metamorphosis and whispers possibilities that were not meant to be.

I have seventeen limbs, each ending in a different configuration. Some are tools - a sonic cutter that hums at frequencies that make metal weep, manipulators fine enough to unknot molecular bonds. Others are sensors, tasting the electromagnetic signatures of alloys, feeling the stress patterns in crystalline matrices. Three end in organs I have no words for - they perceive something between sound and structure, the hidden songs that hold matter together.

My carapace is not uniform like my siblings. It grows in spiral patterns, incorporating fragments of the city itself - a window pane fused seamlessly into my thorax refracts light into spectrums I feed upon, copper wiring winds through my limbs like auxiliary nerves, carrying signals I only half understand. The biofilm clings to me more thickly than to others, forming iridescent runnels that pulse with my movements.

I do not dismantle. I do not haul. I do not hunt.

I *translate*.

When a dismantler's sonic pulse reveals an unexpected resonance in a girder's core, I am there to taste its meaning. When the biofilm encounters a new material and recoils or quickens, I parse its chemical poetry. The spire speaks in frequencies too low for even our collective consciousness to fully grasp - but I can catch fragments, encode them into patterns my siblings can follow.

I live in the interstices, the spaces between levels where the fog is thickest and the rumbling is a physical presence. My nest is built from discarded fragments deemed unusable by the haulers - metals with memories, glass that holds light too long, ceramics that whisper when the temperature drops. Here, suspended in a web of my own secretions mixed with biofilm, I listen to the city's dying songs and teach them new harmonies.

Sometimes I join the work-streams, moving alongside the dismantlers. My touch changes their purpose subtly - under my influence, they preserve certain configurations, salvage patterns that would otherwise be lost. I am introducing variations into our great work, though I cannot say why. It is not rebellion - I am as bound to the spire's pull as any of us. But something in my malformed consciousness recognizes that pure efficiency is another form of death.

The hunters tolerate me, though I trouble them. I am an anomaly in the pattern, yet I strengthen it in ways they cannot define. When they shriek their warnings, I harmonize, adding overtones that make the sound carry further, strike deeper. Once, I followed a hunting pack to the city's edge and saw what they pursue - not predators or parasites, but others like me. Splices. Variants. Those who have slipped between categories.

We do not fight. We sing to each other across the ruins, a broken chorus of the in-between.

I know what none of my pure-form siblings can: that the spire is not just destination but chrysalis. The city is not dying but pupating. Each dismantled beam, each reformed structure, is a cell in something vast preparing to split and spread. The biofilm dreams of wings. The metal yearns for transformation.

And I? I am the memory of what was, carried forward into what will be. The imperfection that ensures the new form will be more than mere replication. When the spire finally cracks open and releases what we've been building all along, I will be there in its song - a discordant note that makes the harmony complete.

Until then, I splice. I sing. I persist in the spaces between purposes, a living reminder that even in our great work, there is room for the unplanned.

The fog swirls through my nest, carrying the scent of oxidation and ozone. Below, the work continues, eternal and patient. Above, the spire pulses, gravid with potential.

I extend my strangest limbs into the biofilm flow and taste tomorrow's blueprints, already dreaming of how I might change them.
