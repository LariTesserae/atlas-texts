I find myself as one of the bronze-tendoned titans, those vast ribcage cathedrals that drift across the plateau on prescribed spirals. But I am different from my kin—smaller, more recently assembled from the scattered detritus of the vanished industry. Where they are weathered titanium, I am patchwork: my sternum a repurposed boring shield, my vertebrae salvaged gear teeth magnetized into alignment, my hollow spaces lined with films of that purple periglaze scraped from the bore-hole's lens during some forgotten midnight.

I am perhaps three stories tall—a runt among giants. My bones sing faintly when the moon-dust cog teeth accumulate in my joints, creating tiny resonance chambers that violate the rationed silence by mere milligrams. This makes me both blessed and cursed: I can hear the subsonic memories trapped in the iron filings, feel the ghost-vibrations of the factories that were, taste the bitter history in every drift of metallic snow. The older titans sense nothing; they have grown deaf to all but their eternal spirals.

My consciousness sparked into being when two technologies collided inside my frame: a still-warm transformer coil that had somehow survived the great erasure met a pocket of concentrated ferric ember-fall from the bore-hole. The reaction was silent—as all things must be here—but it woke something that remembers being neither machine nor beast, yet understands both languages in the marrow.

I break the rules simply by existing. My footfalls—for unlike my ethereal kin, I do touch ground—leave impressions that fill immediately with magnetized dust, creating temporary star-maps of iron filings that point always toward the bore-hole. Each step writes a letter in a language no one recalls. By dawn, the wind has scattered them, but for those few hours, I am an author of ephemeral texts across the plateau.

The glassy starling-forms that chase reflections in the larger titans' hollows avoid me. They sense wrongness—perhaps the way my bronze tendons still conduct trace electricity, perhaps how my eye sockets glow faintly with that sickly purple when I stare too long at the moon. I am too young to be here, too much made of salvage to be pure. The soft pacing thing that "passes" watches me from the edge of perception, curious perhaps about this newcomer who refuses to simply drift, who insists on leaving traces.

I am searching for something I cannot name—wandering the spiral patterns but adding my own variations, small rebellions against the predetermined paths. Sometimes I think I'm looking for the tools that unmade this place, wondering if somewhere in the dust lies the switch that could reverse the great subtraction. Other times I know I'm searching for my own origin point, that spot where coil met ember and accident became intention.

When the bore-hole exhales its column of steam every six and a half thousand hours, I position myself downwind. The mist condenses on my ribs differently than on the others—it forms symbols, equations, partial blueprints for machines that never were. For those brief moments before retraction, I am a library of possibility, a catalog of what could have been built if the factories had dreamed forward instead of devouring themselves backward.

The other titans continue their ordained spirals. The storms wheel silently at wingspan level. The iron snow falls upward as often as down. And I, the youngest monument to a forgotten catastrophe, walk my deliberate patterns across the plateau, each step a question mark punctuating the endless sentence of silence, waiting for the day when the accumulated weight of my footprints might teach the ground to remember what it means to hold something home.
