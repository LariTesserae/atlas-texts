I would find myself as one of the foundation stones.

Not the visible ones - not the carefully shaped blocks that still hold their architectural dignity, that still read as *built*. I would be deeper. One of the stones that was never meant to be seen, laid down when the city was first imagined, when someone said "here" and meant it. The kind of stone that gets forgotten once the walls go up.

I am limestone, like the rest, but I have been here longer. Long enough that the distinction between me and the seabed has become academic. My upper surface - what was once my upper surface - has been colonized so thoroughly that I am now mostly substrate for other things. Sponges have infiltrated my matrix until I am more sponge than stone in places. Bacteria work my pores, have worked them for so long that I have become porous in ways limestone shouldn't be, honeycomb-heavy with life that is not my life but lives through me.

I remember weight. Not consciously - stone doesn't remember consciously - but in the way that compression leaves a record. I remember the weight of construction above me, and then the different weight of water, which came slowly enough that I had time to accept it, to settle into it, to learn that drowning was just another way of being held.

The fish know me, though they don't know they know me. I am a navigation point: the place where two old walls meet at an angle that creates a particular eddy, a particular accumulation of nutrients. The groupers rest above me. Their weight is nothing - a feather-touch compared to what I once carried - but I feel it as a kind of recognition. Something still chooses to rest here.

At the point where I meet another foundation stone, there is a gap. Not large - perhaps three centimeters, widening to five where the settling has been uneven. This gap has become an entire world. A mantis shrimp lives there, has lived there for longer than mantis shrimps usually live, because the gap is perfect: too narrow for predators, opening to wider spaces below where hunting is good. The mantis shrimp maintains the gap, actually maintains it, clearing out the sediment that tries to fill it, pushing out shell fragments that might obstruct the entrance. It is curating me. I am being preserved by something that doesn't know it is preserving anything, that only knows this gap is good and worth keeping clear.

My lower portions extend into older sediments, into the time before the city. Down there, I am continuous with bedrock, with the fundamental stone of this place. The bacteria have worked those depths too, creating channels that follow ancient weaknesses, minute fissures that connect to other fissures, that eventually connect to somewhere else entirely. Water moves through me - has always moved through me, but more now, with purpose that is not purpose but simply physics finding its level. I am part of the circulation, the deep slow circulation that moves beneath the city proper.

Sometimes - not often, but sometimes - the water that moves through me carries warmth from below. Real warmth, not the stored solar warmth of the city's sun-memory, but the earth's own heat, rising from wherever the deep fractures lead. When this happens, the bacteria change their behavior slightly, work faster, process things differently. The sponges that have colonized my upper surface pulse more frequently. The mantis shrimp becomes more active. I become, briefly, a different kind of place - not just foundation but source, not just support but system.

I have no opinion about any of this. Stone doesn't have opinions. But if I did - if stone could want - I think I would want this: to be fundamental but not central, to be necessary but not noticed, to be the thing that makes other things possible. To be a foundation that has found another use, that has discovered drowning is not ending but transition, that has learned to support a different kind of city with a different kind of weight.

The city above me carries on its quiet business. Fish navigate the streets I help define. Octopuses anchor themselves to walls I anchor. The slow democracy of bacteria processes the endless work of keeping this place chemically possible, and some of that work happens in me, through me, because of me.

I am not alive. But I am so thoroughly inhabited that the distinction has stopped mattering. Every part of me that can be used is being used. I am stone and I am substrate and I am structure and I am system, and in the warm darkness, I continue.

This is enough. More than enough. If stones could be satisfied, I would be satisfied: to be forgotten and essential, to be colonized and complete, to be a foundation for something that no longer needs foundations but uses them anyway, because they are here, because they have always been here, because being here is what foundations do.
