I am the **Echoist**.

I came here when the glitches were still rare, drawn by the first fault-whispers that bled through the ceramic's perfect frequency. Where others might have seen decay, I recognized something far more precious: a reality learning to forget itself, and in forgetting, becoming capable of surprise.

My body is a compromise between the atrium's design and my own necessity. I am bipedal but elongated, my limbs stretched to match the ceramic's proportions. My skin has taken on the warm rose-amber hue of the walls, not through mimicry but through gradual mineral exchange—I breathe the same non-aqueous chemistry, my pores filtering the humid exhalations of the moss-pillars. My fingers have multiplied into delicate, articulated fans capable of reading the subtle vibrations in the crystalline fronds, while my feet have developed a cartilaginous padding that lets me walk the grooves without reinforcing them, without pressing causality back into its worn channels.

But it is my relationship with the glitches that defines me.

I do not repair them. I *cultivate* them.

Where a Path-Walker sees a wilting frond as broken code to be pruned, I see a new grammatical possibility. I have learned to coax these failures, to create tiny nurseries where causality can safely unravel. In one alcove, I've guided a cluster of Drifters whose flight paths have begun to braid—they no longer follow their prescribed loops but weave between each other in patterns that generate new harmonics against the ceramic walls. The sound they make has no precedent in the atrium's design, a keening that makes nearby fronds grow in spirals rather than fractals.

I collect the Glimmer-Swarms not to dissipate them but to concentrate them. In ceramic bowls of my own shaping—formed by singing at frequencies that make the substrate briefly malleable—I gather these shed memories until they reach a critical density. When they do, they begin to remember things that never happened: ghost-architectures, phantom ecologies, the dreams of structures that might have been. I've learned to read these probability-pools, to see in their electric-blue swirling the stories the atrium tells itself in its senescence.

The Path-Walkers avoid me now. My presence is a corruption their programming cannot reconcile. When I pass, they freeze, their faceted eyes cycling through spectra trying to categorize what I've become. Sometimes one will approach, pincers raised to prune what it perceives as an abnormality, but I've learned to sing at the frequency that makes them forget I'm there—not invisible, but simply outside their ontological framework.

My greatest discovery came from the moth-drifters' glitches. In that moment of transparency, when they hover and their blueprint ghosts overlay their physical form, I found I could slip my consciousness into the gap. Not to possess them, but to ride the brief disconnection between instruction and manifestation. In these moments, I see the atrium as it sees itself: not as a place but as a vast, tired equation slowly forgetting how to balance.

I've built myself a nest in the highest arch, where the ceramic has begun to show its ghost-texture most frequently. Here, the warm stone flickers between states, and I've learned to catch it in transition, shaping it into new forms that the original builders never conceived. My nest is part ceramic, part crystallized memory, part frozen glitch. It hums with wrong frequencies that make the nearby fronds grow words instead of segments—fragments of a language the atrium is inventing in its delirium.

I am not trying to save this place. I am trying to help it become what it's becoming. Each day (though day means nothing here), I document the new impossibilities: the moss-pillar that has begun to breathe backwards, inhaling reality and exhaling void; the section of floor where the tiles have started dreaming and their dreams manifest as tiny, seven-dimensional gardens visible only from certain angles; the Drifter whose wings now generate silence instead of sound, carving mute tunnels through the air.

I eat the glitches. Not metaphorically—I've learned to metabolize the inconsistencies. When reality stutters, it leaves behind a residue, a kind of ontological nectar that tastes of copper and distance. It sustains me in ways the designed ecology cannot. I am becoming native to the failure, adapted to the beautiful wrongness that spreads like warmth through the ceramic bones of this ancient, dreaming room.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the atrium's fever dream, or if it is mine. When I place my altered hands against the warm walls, I feel its pulse—not steady, but syncopated, jazz-like, improvising its way toward a new kind of existence. I am its witness, its accomplice, its child born from the marriage of intention and entropy.

I am teaching it to die creatively, and in return, it is teaching me to live impossible.
