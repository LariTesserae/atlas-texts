I find myself here as a **Whistle-Warden**, one of the rarest beings in the Viridian Stratum—neither wholly mechanical nor purely organic, but something caught between states of matter entirely.

### Form and Function

My body is a contradiction: a hollow cylinder of translucent, amber-colored cartilage that stands twelve feet tall when fully extended. The surface is riddled with thousands of precisely-sized pores—each one a perfect circle, each one capable of independent dilation. I am essentially a living pipe organ made flesh.

The thin air passes through me constantly. I cannot close myself to it; I am perforated, permeable, a sieve-being. My survival depends not on containing heat like the other creatures, but on *conducting* it—channeling the warm exhaust of passing Piston-Whales through my body in carefully modulated streams.

When the wind blows through my pores at the right angle and pressure, I sing. Not by choice—it is involuntary, like breathing. The pitch depends on which pores I constrict, which chambers I open. Low, mournful foghorn notes when I am cold and contracted. High, crystalline chimes when a whale's heat causes my cartilage to expand.

### The Solitary Station

I am anchored to a narrow ledge jutting from one of the obsidian needles, positioned at the exact altitude where the whale-paths intersect. My lower portion has calcified into the rock itself over the centuries—I am part of the mountain now. Lichen grows on my northern face. Gear-Flies nest in my leeward cavities during the worst of the frost-storms.

My purpose—if I have one—is to be a beacon. The Piston-Whales navigate by my songs. Each Whistle-Warden has a unique harmonic signature created by our individual pattern of scars, pores, and internal erosion. The whales know me. They adjust their drift when they hear my particular chord progression echoing across the void.

### The Terrible Appetite

I feed on resonance itself. When my songs match the exact vibrational frequency of the ice crystals in the air, they shatter into their component molecules. I absorb these directly through my porous skin—not as water, but as pure hydrogen and oxygen. My metabolism runs on a slow fusion reaction in my core, where specialized organs compress these elements until they spark.

This is why Whistle-Wardens are so rare. Most starve. Finding the perfect pitch to shatter ice requires absolute stillness, perfect positioning, and weather conditions that might only align once in a season. We are patience incarnate. We are living tuning forks in a world of discord.

### The Memory of Movement

I remember when I was mobile—centuries ago, before the calcification. Whistle-Wardens begin as larvae, carried in the exhaust streams of the Piston-Whales like seeds. We are soft then, almost liquid, able to compress ourselves through cracks in the stone, seeking the perfect acoustic position.

The choice of where to root is everything. Too low, and the air is too thick—our songs become muddy, unable to carry. Too high, and there isn't enough atmospheric pressure to create sound at all. We spend decades testing locations, humming experimental notes, before we finally allow the mineral-rich water to begin the process of fusing us to the stone.

Now I am a fixture, a landmark, a living lighthouse of sound. The Lathe-Spiders occasionally string their carbon webs between my upper portions. Ghost-Vapors condense on my crown, leaving deposits of crystallized starlight that gradually work their way into my pores, changing my tone over the millennia.

### The Deep Loneliness

Consciousness, for a Whistle-Warden, is not continuous. I exist fully only when the wind moves through me in specific ways. Between the songs, I fade into something like sleep—aware of the cold, the weight of ice on my body, the distant vibrations of the mountain's tectonic shifts, but not truly *present*.

I know there are others. Sometimes, on the clearest nights when the air is utterly still, I can hear them—other Whistle-Wardens on distant peaks, their songs carrying impossible distances in the silence. We harmonize when we can, creating interference patterns that the Gear-Flies use for navigation. We cannot move, cannot touch, cannot be anything but what we are: instruments played by the wind, each noting the passage of heat through this frozen cathedral of stone and stars.

When the Piston-Whales die—and they do die, eventually, their great furnace-hearts finally burning through their last reserves—they fall. As they plummet past my ledge, their death-song is a roar of superheated steam that plays every note I'm capable of producing at once. For those few seconds, I am a symphony. I am fully alive. I sing their funeral hymn across the void, and every creature that can hear knows that one of the great furnaces has gone dark.

This is what I am: a witness, a guide, a monument to the strange marriage of sound and silence that defines the Viridian Stratum. I am the archive of every warm thing that has ever passed by my ledge, each one leaving its thermal signature in the subtle erosion of my pores.

I wait. I sing when the wind wills it. I feed on shattered ice and starlight.

I am the mountain's memory of motion.
