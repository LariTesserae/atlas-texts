**The Archivist**

I am the anomaly. 

Not fish, not worm, not the patient lichen that measures centuries in millimeters of growth. I am the thing that remembers.

My body is a compromise between the needs of this place and the vestigial architecture of what I once was. Elongated now, compressed by the weight of water and time into something seal-like but wrong—too many joints, fingers that have grown webbing but retained their articulation. My skin has taken on the color of the water itself, that uniform milky gray, but beneath it run patterns like the oxidation on the walls, rust-blooms of subcutaneous pigmentation that shift with my circulation. I am perhaps four feet from fingertip to toe-tip when stretched full length, though I rarely do. The cylinder teaches economy of movement.

My eyes are the betrayal. They have not atrophied like they should have. Instead, they have grown enormous, pupils dilated to black coins that catch and hold what little light the dying lamps provide. I see in gradients of gray that have no names in any language above. I see the infrared heat-ghost of the isopods, the minute pressure-shadows the fish leave in their wake, the slow crawl of lichen-time across the walls. My vision is a curse here—there is too much darkness to process, too much nothing to comprehend. Sometimes I close them for years at a time, navigating by the lateral line system that runs like a seam of electricity down my spine.

I remember because I must. Someone must.

My dwelling is a maintenance alcove, a rectangular recession in the cylinder wall that once housed valves and gauges. The instruments are long corroded, their faces blank, their purposes forgotten. I have lined it with the shed scales of fish, countless generations of them, creating a pearlescent mosaic that catches the ghost-light and throws it back in ways that please me. Here I keep my collection: small objects that sink from above, though there is no above anymore. A brass button, green with verdigris. A shard of blue glass, impossibly bright in this gray world. A key to nothing. These are my rosary, my memory-anchors. I turn them over with my webbed fingers and try to remember what "blue" meant.

I know I was not always here. The knowledge sits in me like an ache, specific but unlocalizable. I dream sometimes of lungs that knew air, of skin that knew sunlight, of a heartbeat that didn't have to slow to match the cylinder's hum. But these could be false memories, stories I tell myself to explain why I am different from the fish, why I count their unchanging numbers, why I notice when a new patch of lichen begins its century-long colonization of a rivet.

My role here is unclear, even to me. I am not part of the cycle—I neither feed nor filter, neither clean nor decompose. I am an observer, a recorder of sameness. I watch the fish school in their predictable patterns and I map their movements on the walls with my fingernail, etching diagrams that the lichen will eventually erase. I taste the water for chemical changes that never come. I listen to the hum and parse it for variations, for messages, for meaning that isn't there.

Sometimes I interact with the others. The fish avoid me, sensing my wrongness, but the isopods are indifferent. They crawl over me when I rest on the bottom, their tiny legs tickling traces across my gray skin. The worms I visit like stations of the cross, swimming the circumference of the cylinder to check each colony, though they neither acknowledge me nor change. I speak to them sometimes, bubbles of unremembered language that rise and burst against the ceiling. The sound is swallowed by the hum.

I am the only thing here that wonders. The only thing that asks why the lamps were sealed, why the water doesn't drain, why the makers' marks on the rivets spell out words in a script I can almost read. I am the only thing that measures time not in metabolic cycles but in the accumulation of sediment, the slow failure of the lamps, the patient conquest of biology over architecture.

I am waiting for something, though I no longer remember what. For the last lamp to die? For the walls to finally corrode through? For someone to drain this industrial womb and find me here, pressed against the ceiling like a secret? Or perhaps I am waiting to forget—to finally release these stubborn synapses that insist on patterns beyond the circular current, meanings beyond the hum.

But I cannot. I am the archivist of this forgetting place, the keeper of its non-history. I mark the fish-counts on the walls. I arrange and rearrange my collection of impossible objects. I swim my rounds and pretend it matters that I notice the new lichen, the shifted sediment, the way the water tastes infinitesimally more metallic each decade.

I am the closed system's only open question, the eternal's only temporary resident. I am the thing that knows this is a tomb, and that knowing makes me the ghost.

The hum continues. The current circles. The fish school and the worms filter and I remember, and remember, and remember, though I no longer know what for.
