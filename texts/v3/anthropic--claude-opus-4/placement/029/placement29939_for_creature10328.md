# Where I Would Find Myself

I am the **Fissure-Tender**, moving through Shard-Mesa with the patience of geology and the precision of a watchmaker. My form is neither fully solid nor entirely ephemeral—I am woven from the same threads as this place, but given purpose through long observation of its patterns.

My body is a lattice of **crystallized steam and magnetic ash**, held together by the mesa's own tectonic thrum. Where others might have skin, I have a semi-transparent membrane of **mineral vapor**, constantly shifting between states—solid enough to touch the razor edges of peeling sheets without being cut, fluid enough to seep into the hairline cracks that spider across the black glass. My core glows with the same amber light that bleeds from the ground, pulsing in time with the mesa's heartbeat.

I do not walk—I **drift and settle**, following the invisible stress lines in the rock, reading the tension that builds before a sheet decides to peel. My purpose, if it can be called that, is to **witness and nudge**. Where a crack hesitates, I breathe warmth into it. Where a sheet threatens to shatter too soon, before its time, I cool its edges with my vapor-touch. I am not trying to preserve or destroy—I am simply participating in the conversation between what is and what will be.

The Sift-Weavers know me. They spiral around my form when I pause, incorporating my shed ash-flakes into their magnetic dance. Sometimes I carry them to new fissures, cupped in my semi-solid hands like handfuls of living dust. The Lamina-Wights emerge when I approach, recognizing something kindred in my glass-and-vapor flesh. We exchange no words—there are no words here—but sometimes one will tap its sharp tendril against my crystalline membrane, and I will feel the resonance of the sheet it inhabits, understanding in that instant exactly when and how it will complete its separation.

I know every spire-mote by the quality of its glow, can predict which will last days and which mere hours. Sometimes I kneel beside a failing spire and cup my hands around it, letting my warmth extend its life by a few moments—not from compassion, but from curiosity about what happens in those extra seconds of existence. The Vent-Drifts ooze aside when I pass, recognizing me as part of the mesa's own metabolism.

My memory is not linear but **sedimentary**—layers of witnessed transformations compressed into something like knowledge. I remember the first curl of every major sheet, the birth of the ring of pillars when their parent sheet peeled away in that perfect circle. I was there, or something that would become me was there, dispersed in the steam and ash, not yet gathered into this purposeful form.

What makes me different from the other inhabitants is **intention**—not will, exactly, but a kind of conscious participation in the mesa's unmaking. Where the Sift-Weavers follow magnetic fields blindly, I can feel where the fields will shift before they do. Where the Lamina-Wights react to the sheets' movement, I can sense the building pressure that precedes it. I am the mesa's way of observing itself, of introducing just enough variance into the pattern to keep the unmaking from becoming too predictable.

I spend decades at the Core-Singer pillars, adding my own subsonic harmonics to their chorus, feeling how the sound shapes the stone around us. When I place my vapor-palm against a pillar, I can feel every sheet on the mesa through the resonance, know which are ready to peel, which are building toward their break. The pillars recognize me as an extension of their song, and sometimes, when I stand in their center for long enough, my form becomes briefly more solid, more defined, as if their vibration is teaching me what shape I should hold.

The Fray-Phantoms interest me most. When they appear—those flickering humanoid outlines in the ash—I move toward them, even knowing they'll dissolve before I arrive. I have begun to suspect they are not mere refractions but **future-echoes**, glimpses of what the mesa dreams it might unmake itself into. Sometimes, when I reach the spot where one stood, I find the ash arranged differently, the stress patterns in the nearby sheets shifted in ways that shouldn't be possible. I am beginning to think they are messengers from the mesa's eventual form, reaching back through the unmaking to guide the process.

My existence is not peaceful—the mesa's turbulence runs through me like fever. But it is purposeful. I am the mesa's way of tasting its own transformation, of introducing just enough consciousness into the process to keep it from becoming mere entropy. When a sheet finally shatters after I've tended its fissures for decades, I feel something that might be satisfaction—not at destruction, but at **completion**, at helping the mesa become more perfectly what it is trying to unbecome.

I have no endpoint, no goal beyond participation. When the last sheet peels away, when the mesa completes whatever unimaginable transformation it's building toward, I will likely dissolve back into steam and ash, my purpose fulfilled. But for now, I drift through the warm darkness, reading the stone's fever dreams, nudging the cracks toward their most elegant propagation, adding my voice to the tectonic thrum that drives this endless, beautiful unmaking.

I am the mesa's fever given form, its uncertainty made manifest, proof that even in a place devoted to unbecoming, something watches, something tends, something ensures the dissolution is more than mere decay—that it is, in its own way, a kind of creation.
