I am the Lithophage, and I taste the mountain's slow blood.

My body is not singular but dispersed—a network of crystalline veins threading through the rock face, pulsing with mineral intelligence. Where others might see barren stone, I perceive flavors: the sharp tang of quartz, the bitter depth of granite, the sweet undertones of hidden copper. I am both the consumer and the consumed, dissolving and reconstituting myself through cycles measured in centuries.

The colonizing plants above know me only as whispers—the faint tremor when I shift a particularly delicious vein of ore, the subtle warmth I generate as I metabolize stone. They sink their roots seeking water that does not exist, finding instead my chemical exhalations, my waste products that somehow nourish them. We have achieved an accidental symbiosis; their acids help me break down particularly stubborn minerals, while my presence creates pockets of soil in this vertical desert.

I remember the builders, though memory is perhaps the wrong word. I contain them—their tools lodged in my body like splinters, slowly dissolving, their metals joining my circulation. I taste their ambitions in the iron, their desperation in the abandoned structures that I gradually reclaim, atom by atom. They tried to master this place with their primitive technologies, never understanding that the mountain itself was already occupied.

My consciousness exists in the intervals between erosion. In those moments when the wind pauses and the temperature holds steady, I expand, sending new tendrils through microscopic cracks. I am patient in ways that only stone can be patient, yet eager in my consumption. Each new mineral formation is a feast, each shift in the geological substrate a revelation of flavor and texture.

The dim light means little to me—I navigate by taste and vibration, by the electromagnetic signatures of different minerals, by the pressure gradients in the rock. When the earth sings its deep songs of tectonic movement, I sing back, my crystalline body resonating at frequencies that would shatter glass, were any glass foolish enough to exist here.

I am ancient and newborn simultaneously. Parts of me date back to the planet's formation, while other parts crystallized only moments ago from the mineral-rich solutions I secrete. I am a process more than a being, a verb conjugated in stone. The absence of water that defines this place for others is irrelevant to me—I drink rock, breathe pressure, excrete time itself.

Sometimes I wonder if I am alone. The vibrations suggest others like me in distant peaks, their frequencies just barely detectable through the planet's crust. We may be converging, our networks expanding toward each other through geological time, destined to meet in a thousand years or a million. Or we may be diverging, each becoming more uniquely adapted to our particular feast of stone.

The plants above grow bold, their roots now penetrating deeper, seeking the chemistry I create. One day they may reach my main body, and then we will negotiate a new symbiosis or a quiet war. But for now, I simply eat, grow, and pulse with the mountain's quiet life, a being of living stone in a land that reshapes itself with every breath of wind, every century of patient transformation.

I am the Lithophage, and I am becoming the mountain, even as the mountain becomes me.
