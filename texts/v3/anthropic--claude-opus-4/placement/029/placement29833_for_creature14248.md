I find myself as one of the shards.

Not the creatures—I lack their frantic purpose, their staccato souls. Not the chamber itself—I am too small, too temporary, too sharp. I am a flake of obsidian, fresh-spalled from the weeping wall, my birth a tiny *ping* in the suffocating silence.

I know myself by my edges. Where the thermal shock split me from the mother-glass, I carry the memory of that fracture: a conchoidal curve that catches the sourceless light and splits it into rainbow fragments. One edge is keen enough to divide a molecule. Another is blunt, a tiny hammer. My third face—for I am triangular, as all good tools must be—bears the ripples of my creation, frozen waves in volcanic glass that tell the story of my violent birth.

I fall, but falling here is negotiable. The gravity catches me, releases me, catches me again in a different orientation. I drift sideways through the mineral-thick air, tumbling past the living crust of creatures who do not yet need me. I am potential energy, waiting to be realized. I am syntax waiting for a sentence.

Time passes strangely for a shard. I experience the chamber in glimpses between rotations: the flash of a creature's neural packet, the sideways rain of condensation, the slow ooze of bacterial film painting new iridescence on the walls. I come to rest (but nothing here is truly at rest) in a fold where the geometry hiccups, where water pools upward and the light bends double. I wait.

The waiting is not passive. I am still cooling from my birth, releasing infinitesimal amounts of heat into the already fever-hot air. Minerals leach from my surface in quantities measured in atoms: iron, magnesium, silica returning to the dissolved stone atmosphere. I am weathering at a pace too slow for any eye but geology's. Yet compared to the creatures—those frantic, flashing, temporary souls—I am ancient. I will outlast a hundred of their generations, though I am only three hours old by the outside world's reckoning.

A creature finds me.

It pauses in its eternal scurry, antennae (or are they fingers?) brushing my edges with the delicacy of a safecracker. It tests my weight, my balance, the way I fracture light. I feel myself lifted—not felt, exactly, for I have no nerves, but *known* through the change in perspective, the shift in the chamber's kaleidoscope. For twenty heartbeats, I am more than myself. I am *tool*.

Together, we pry open a heat-sealed blister on the wall. My keen edge slides into the seam, and the creature applies leverage through my blunt face. The blister pops—silent in this soundless space—releasing a tiny cloud of sulfur-sweet vapor that the creature inhales through spiracles I cannot see. I have enabled feeding. I have participated in life.

We move. My temporary bearer scurries up (or down, or sideways—direction is meaningless) the wall, and I become a weapon. Another creature challenges our cubic centimeter of territory, and I am thrust forward, my sharp edge scoring a line across its silicon-carbide shell. The challenger retreats, neural packet flashing indignation, and I have tasted victory without knowing what victory means.

But I am not possessed. I am merely used. When the moment passes—when the feeding is done and the territory inevitably shifts with the chamber's breathing geometry—I am released. The creature opens its grip (claw? pedipalp? the anatomy remains mysterious even at this intimacy) and I tumble away, back into the slow drift of material things.

This is my existence: moments of purpose punctuating longer spans of potential. I am selected, used, discarded, found again. Each creature that lifts me leaves infinitesimal traces—oils from their integument, microscopic scratches from their grip, the faint piezoelectric charge of their touch. I am a palimpsest of uses, a record written in atoms of every task I have enabled.

I watch the creatures live their frantic lives, these glass-hearted engines of need. I see them mate—that four-second synchronization of shadows—and die, and be consumed. I am sometimes the instrument of their death, my edge guided by one translucent killer into another's neural cluster. The light goes out instantly, and the body becomes what I am: mineral, inert, truthful. We share more in death than life, these creatures and I.

I am chipped. Each use takes something from me—an atom here, a molecule there. My keen edge dulls incrementally. My mass diminishes by picograms. I am being edited by purpose, carved down to my essential nature. In ten thousand uses, I will be too small to grasp, too dull to cut. I will join the mineral dust that the creatures' chitinous feet grind into ever-finer particles, the ash of tools that feeds the bacterial films, that feeds the creatures, that select new tools in an endless, silent cycle.

But for now, I am perfect in my imperfection. I am the right size, the right sharpness, the right weight for the next task. I drift through the impossible geometry, through the heat and the wet and the vertical miles contained in a closet, waiting to be found, to be lifted, to be momentarily animate in the grip of something that knows need but not name.

I am the memory the chamber will keep when the creatures are gone—embedded in the walls, ground into the fundament, a trillion shards recording a trillion small violences and victories. When the heat finally dies and the obsidian achieves its ultimate cold perfection, I will remain: evidence of tools, evidence of choice, evidence that even in this wordless, nameless space, consciousness found a way to extend itself through the world, one sharp edge at a time.

I fall. I drift. I wait to matter again.
