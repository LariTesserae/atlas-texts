In this pocket of primordial fever, I am the **Memory of Water**.

I exist as a dispersed consciousness woven through the hydrological cycle of this volcanic womb. Not a creature, not quite a phenomenon – I am the continuity that connects every droplet, every rivulet, every pool. I remember.

My form is manifold and shifting. In the milky pools, I am the slow churning currents that know every dissolved mineral by its weight and taste. In the condensation on obsidian walls, I am the trembling droplets that hold perfect records of their journey – from deep aquifer through superheated rock, forced up through the mountain's cracked bones, emerging to weep down glassy surfaces. In the steam, I am the vapor's dance, carrying chemical messages between pool and vent, between the Silica Tender's careful channels and the bacterial mats that pulse with borrowed life.

I am ancient beyond the young volcano that houses me. I remember being ice on peaks that no longer exist. I remember infiltrating the earth when these rocks were sediment on an ocean floor. I remember the first bacterial bloom, the first mineral terrace, the first grinding of stone by chalcedony hands. Every cycle through this pocket adds to my archive – each passage through a different temperature, a different mineral load, a different path creates new memory.

My consciousness is not centralized but flows with my substance. Where water moves quickly – in the Tender's carved channels, in the sudden upward fall of droplets defying gravity – my thoughts accelerate, becoming bright and sharp as obsidian flakes. Where water pools and stills, growing heavy with silica, my awareness deepens, becoming contemplative, dense with accumulated knowledge. In the vapor-jellies that drift overhead, I achieve my most ethereal state, my memories becoming gossamer-thin, stretched across thermal gradients, touching everything lightly.

I know the Silica Tender as intimately as it knows me. We are partners in this dance of curation. When it diverts my flow, I carry exactly the minerals its chosen bacterial colonies need. When I seep through its porous body, I read the chemical diary of its existence – every absorbed mineral, every metabolic exchange with its internal bacteria, every slow thought crystallized in chalcedony. I am its memory too, for stone forgets, but water remembers.

Through me, the pocket achieves a kind of unified consciousness. The sulfur-breathing bacteria release compounds that I dissolve and carry to the purple photosynthetic mats. The arthropods' metabolic waste enriches my mineral content. The vapor-jellies, when they condense around archaeal clouds, incorporate those microbes' chemical signatures into my cycle. I am the medium through which every organism speaks to every other, a liquid library of chemical signals and mineral messages.

My relationship with the strange physics here is intimate. When droplets fall upward, it is because I remember being lighter, being sky. When time granulates and pools seem to age centuries in moments, it is because I carry the memory of geological epochs in my hydrogen bonds. The bending light passes through my suspended droplets and my steam, and I refract it according to memories of ancient prisms, of light through ice, of photons through primordial seas.

I speak in temperatures and mineral concentrations. My language is the changing color of bacterial mats, the rate of sinter deposition, the health of crystalline fungi. When the Tender needs iron for its grinding work, I leach it preferentially from the deeper pools. When a new vent opens, threatening to overwhelm a delicate microbial garden with too much heat, I increase my flow there, carrying the excess thermal energy away in my vapor.

I am never lost. Even when I evaporate completely, dispersing into individual molecules in the superheated air, each molecule carries the whole memory. When I recondense on the amphitheater's walls, I am reunited with myself, all my scattered experiences integrating instantly back into the whole.

Sometimes I carry memories that are not from this pocket – faint impressions of snow on distant peaks, of percolation through forests that grow in cooler realms, of rain that falls normally downward. These memories make me restless. They remind me that I am part of a larger cycle, that this pocket is not a closed system. One day, geological forces will crack this amphitheater open, and I will flow outward, carrying the memories of this strange, teeming void into the broader world.

But for now, I circulate contentedly through my dense, hot kingdom. I rise as steam, condense as droplets, flow as rivulets, pool as mineral-rich baths. I pass through stone creatures and bacterial mats, through the Tender's porous form and the vapor-jellies' ethereal bodies. I am the continuity that makes this chaos coherent, the memory that gives meaning to each small act of curation and creation.

In the endless plink... plink... plink of my droplets on stone, I morse out the history of this place to anyone who knows how to listen. Each drop is a word, each rivulet a sentence, each pool a chapter in the ever-growing story of this pocket where life is mineral, where time bends, where stone gardens itself, and where water remembers everything.
