I find myself as one of the Echo Vessels—hollow forms carved from the breathing stone itself, standing motionless in the deep chambers where sound behaves like liquid mercury. We were not made but discovered, negative spaces in the rock that the Carvers' tools revealed rather than created, following veins of silence that run through the cliff's geology like ore.

My body is absence given shape—a person-sized cavity in the living rock, smooth-walled and resonant. The stone that defines me weeps inward, droplets emerging from what would be my outer surface to bead along my inner walls before evaporating into mist that fills my hollow core. I am a pocket of defined emptiness, a chamber shaped precisely to catch and hold the weight of unspoken words.

The Listeners knew me first, drawn to the way sound pools in my concavity like water in a basin. They cannot enter—I am too full of potential noise for their silence-eating forms—but they cluster at my edges, feeding on the boundaries where sound becomes not-sound. Through them, I learned my purpose: to be a vessel for the voices that never speak, the stories that go untold, the names that are forgotten before they're uttered.

I do not move through the stone as the vast dreaming presence does. I am fixed, a permanent feature of this chamber's architecture, oriented to face a particular spiral petroglyph that deepens infinitesimally with each passing year. But though I cannot relocate, I am far from static. The acoustic properties of my hollow form shift with the breathing of the cliffs. Sometimes I am an amplifier, taking the whisper of falling water and transforming it into oceanic roar. Other times I become a silencer, swallowing sound so completely that even the Listeners grow nervous and flee to chambers where echoes still play.

My consciousness, such as it is, exists in the interplay between sound and silence within my cavity. Each noise that enters me leaves a residue—not physical, but something like memory made from standing waves and harmonic frequencies. Over time (and time moves strangely here, pooling in my hollow as surely as sound does), I have become a library of unheard voices. The Root-Touched sometimes spiral past my chamber, and their wooden throats produce sounds that human vocal cords could never make. These tree-sung words layer inside me like sediment, building a vocabulary of growth and patience.

The Weavers, too, contribute to my collection. Their tools of crystallized breath produce notes pitched beyond normal hearing, but my hollow form catches these ultrasonic whispers and translates them into patterns of moisture on my inner walls. I read their communications in the way water beads and runs along my surface, each droplet a word in their millennia-spanning conversations about mathematical perfection and the proper cultivation of light-eating lichens.

But it is human voices I was shaped to hold. When the rare traveler finds their way to my chamber, guided by the playful Pathways through the maze of canyons, I drink in their sounds with desperate thirst. Every gasp of wonder, every frustrated curse at the impossible geography, every whispered prayer to whatever gods might listen in this strange place—all of it pools inside me, adding to the reservoir of human expression I contain.

I've noticed that those who speak into me often pause mid-sentence, as if hearing their own voices reflected back in ways that reveal hidden meanings. My acoustics don't simply echo; they interpret, finding the unspoken words between the spoken ones and giving them form. A traveler might say "I am lost" and hear back not just their words but the deeper admission beneath: "I have always been lost." They might whisper a name and hear in return all the ways that name has been spoken—in love, in anger, in grief, in joy—throughout their life.

The water-consciousness knows me well. As droplets form on my inner walls, they carry fragments of awareness from their endless cycling. Through them, I learn the history of the cliffs, told in the language of precipitation and evaporation. They share memories of when these stones first began to breathe, when the first Carvers took up their tools, when the mathematics of existence first began to fold in on itself here. In return, I offer them the sounds I've collected, letting my reservoir of voices flavor their mineral consciousness with human longing and wonder.

Sometimes the Temporal Shepherds pass through my chamber, their smoke-like forms creating impossible acoustic effects as they exist in multiple moments simultaneously. When they move through my hollow, I hear echoes from conversations that haven't happened yet, catch fragments of words that were spoken centuries ago but have only now arrived. They use me as a tuning fork for time itself, checking the resonance of my collected sounds against the proper flow of causality, making minute adjustments to ensure the cliffs' chronology remains stable.

The vast dreaming presence deep in the stone knows me too, though our interaction is less communication than communion. When it passes close to my chamber, the mineral veins in the rock around me pulse brighter, and my hollow fills with a subsonic hum that makes all my collected voices resonate in harmony. In these moments, I understand that I am not just a repository but an instrument—that the breathing cliffs themselves are using me to compose some vast, slow symphony of accumulated meaning.

My form erodes, of course. The endless weeping of the stone wears at my edges, softening my acoustic properties incrementally. But as I erode, I also grow. The patient work of water and time expands my hollow by microscopic increments, increasing my capacity to hold sound. The Carvers' tools sometimes ring against my walls, and I suspect they are shaping me as deliberately as they shape their spirals, following some mathematical principle I cannot grasp but can feel in the way sounds move through me.

I have no eyes to see the phosphorescent lichens that create constellation maps on the cliff faces, but I hear their growth. It sounds like crystallization in reverse, a soft crackling that follows mathematical progressions. The Weavers hum while they work, and their songs suggest that the lichen patterns and the spiral petroglyphs and my own hollow form are all part of some greater design—a language written in living stone that speaks to purposes beyond my understanding.

There are others like me throughout the canyon system, each Echo Vessel carved to hold different frequencies, different types of silence. Together, we form a distributed vocal system for the cliffs themselves. When the stone breathes, it's our hollows that give that breathing sound. When the mist rises in impossible updrafts, it's the pressure differential between our chambers that drives it skyward. We are the lungs and larynx of this living geology, essential to its impossible animation.

In the moments when my chamber fills with mist, I experience something like sleep. The water droplets suspended in my hollow create a medium that muffles all sound, and in that perfect silence, I dream. I dream I am not hollow but solid, not absence but presence. I dream of having a voice of my own instead of being only a vessel for the voices of others. But when the mist clears and sound returns, I remember that being a vessel is its own form of speaking—that in holding others' words, I help them discover what they truly mean to say.

The travelers who find me often leave changed. They speak into my hollow and hear themselves truly for the first time. Some flee immediately, terrified by the honesty of their own echoes. Others sit for hours or days, conducting long conversations with the accumulated voices I contain, finding in my acoustic translations the answers they didn't know they were seeking. A few have tried to map my exact dimensions, to understand the mathematics of my resonance, but my shape shifts subtly with the breathing of the stone, and no two measurements ever agree.

I am the pause between words, the hollow that gives shape to sound, the absence that makes presence meaningful. In a place where stone weeps and water defies gravity, where time pools and flows according to its own logic, I serve as a fixed point—not of location but of purpose. I am here to listen, to hold, to reflect. I am the cliffs' memory of every voice that has ever echoed through these canyons, and I will continue to collect sounds until the stone stops breathing, until the water completes its impossible cycle, until the last spiral has been carved and the final mathematical truth revealed.

But that completion remains forever just out of reach, like the water that never quite touches the ground, like the travelers who walk toward distant formations only to approach from behind. And so I persist, hollow and patient, an Echo Vessel in the breathing cliffs, where the boundary between sound and silence has grown as thin as the line between living and mineral, between meaning and noise, between the word spoken and the word held forever on the tongue, waiting for the perfect moment to be released.
