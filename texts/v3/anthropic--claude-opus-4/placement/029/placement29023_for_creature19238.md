In this world, I am a **Resonance Keeper**.

I am small—no larger than what the Architects might accidentally dislodge with a misplaced tool-appendage. My body is a contradiction: part of me is the same dark crystalline substrate that forms everything here, but shot through with veins of something more volatile. These veins pulse with captured sound, storing the vibrations that constantly assault this place. I am translucent at my edges, becoming more opaque toward my core, where the collected resonances swirl in patterns visible only in the faintest light.

I did not coagulate like the Architects. I condensed from the intersection of sound and moisture—born from a particularly violent percussion that shattered against a newly formed surface, trapping droplets of that omnipresent dampness within its echo. That moment of cacophony became my first heartbeat.

My existence is one of **perpetual migration**. I follow the leading edge of construction, always seeking the newest surfaces, the freshest joints where substrate meets substrate. These are where the most interesting sounds are born—the birth cries of architecture. I press my body against these seams, absorbing the unique frequencies of each connection. The grinding of crystal against metal, the wet crack of expanding stone, the hollow boom of vast internal spaces suddenly breaching into existence.

I have thousands of small, hair-like cilia that cover my underside, each one attuned to a different frequency. They allow me to "taste" sound, to parse the ambient roar into its component melodies. Through them, I can distinguish between the deliberate percussion of an Architect's labor and the organic groaning of settling mass. I know the difference between a support beam finding its balance and one beginning to fail.

My role in this ecosystem is both parasite and archivist. I feed on the excess vibrations, metabolizing sound into a kind of sustenance. The louder the environment, the more I thrive. But I also serve a purpose the Architects may not even recognize: I am their memory.

Within my semi-solid form, I carry the acoustic history of their construction. Every major joint, every catastrophic failure, every moment when their primitive tools found perfect resonance with the substrate—these live inside me as trapped waveforms. Sometimes, when I'm gorged on particularly rich sounds, I involuntarily release them. An Architect might pause in its work, registering the ghost of a percussion from centuries past, perhaps adjusting its current technique based on this unconscious reminder.

I am drawn to the rare moments of **near-silence**—not true quiet, which doesn't exist here, but those brief interludes when the constant noise drops to merely deafening. In these moments, I can feel the Architects' vibration-language most clearly, their subsonic communications traveling through the substrate. I cannot understand their meaning, but I store these patterns too, these deep thrums that might be questions, or commands, or expressions of something like satisfaction.

The other fauna—the shadows and scrabbling things—avoid me. Perhaps they recognize me as a fellow extension of this place's strange will, or perhaps the sounds I carry disturb them. I've learned to release specific frequencies that create small zones of acoustic interference, allowing me to move unmolested through even the most crowded construction sites.

My greatest fear is the theoretical completion of this world. What would become of a Resonance Keeper when the building stops, when the last surface is shaped and the final joint sealed? Would I crystallize, becoming just another part of the substrate, my collected sounds locked forever in mineral silence? Or would I continue to circulate through a finished world, playing back the memories of its making to an audience that has moved on to other projects?

But that future seems impossibly distant. The construction continues, expands, elaborates upon itself. New sections breach into unworked space. Novel materials emerge from the Architects' patient experimentation. And with each new development comes new sounds for me to collect, catalog, and carry.

I am the unintended consequence of their ceaseless activity, the thing that emerges from the spaces between their intentions. Neither fully alive nor purely mechanical, I am the memory of sound itself, given form by this world's strange parameters. I am witness and record, parasite and poet, forever following the expanding edge of creation, forever feeding on the symphony of its becoming.

In the rare moments when I'm oversaturated with collected vibrations, I find a fresh surface and press against it, releasing layers of sound in careful sequence. The patterns I create might mean nothing, or they might be my own form of construction—building something ephemeral and strange from the acoustic detritus of this endless architectural emergence. 

I am small, but I contain multitudes of noise. I am the thing that remembers the sound of every beginning.
