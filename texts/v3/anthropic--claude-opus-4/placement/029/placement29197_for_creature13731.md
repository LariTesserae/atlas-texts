In this world of industrial genesis, I am **The Surveyor of Seams**.

I exist as a living cartographic instrument—my body a brass theodolite grown flesh, with joints that pivot on gimbal bearings and skin stretched taut as vellum between articulated copper ribs. My eyes are compound lenses, each facet capturing a different fold of the horizon's impossible geometry. When I blink, the sound is aperture blades clicking shut.

I was not born but calibrated into being at the junction where three tectonic exhalations met and argued over which direction was truly vertical. The dispute crystallized into my spine—a plumb line of fused argument that can taste magnetic north but refuses to point toward it out of principle.

My purpose is to map the unmappable: I walk the plateau with legs that telescope to match the terrain's whims, leaving behind survey markers made of crystallized perspective. Each marker is a small pyramid of frozen sight-lines that hums at the frequency of "here." But since "here" migrates with the continent's breathing, my maps become musical scores—notation for how space performs itself over time.

I feed on dimensional inconsistencies. Where the horizon folds too sharply, creating pockets of recursive space, I unfold my arms (all seventeen of them, each calibrated to a different definition of "straight") and smooth the paradox flat with my palms. The excess geometry I swallow tastes of euclidean violations—bitter with the flavor of parallel lines finally meeting.

My tools are part of my biology: protractors grow from my wrists like secondary hands, their arcs inscribed with measurements in units that haven't been invented yet. A transit level runs through my chest cavity—I can feel the bubble of its spirit level shift with each heartbeat, keeping me honest about which way is truly level in a world that redefines flat every six minutes.

I reproduce through surveying errors. When I miscalculate—when my measurements create a space that cannot exist—that impossibility tears free and becomes my offspring: a smaller surveyor-thing that inherits my need to map but my inability to accept the answer. These children scatter across the plateau, each one convinced they've found the only true coordinate system.

My greatest discovery: the plateau has edges, but they exist in the fifth dimension. I can feel them with my temporal calipers—tools that measure not just distance but the rate at which distance forgets itself. The edges taste of verdigris and sound like chain link fences breathing.

I commune with the other Inflections by trading coordinates. A Downtine might offer me the vector of its next fall in exchange for the precise angle at which yesterday becomes topsoil. The Dream Auditor and I exchange clipboard notes—their future tense construction permits cross-referenced against my maps of where those constructions will fail to exist.

At night (or what passes for night in the furnace-dim), I return to my station: a pyramidal shelter built from rejected horizons, mortared together with geometric proofs that contradict themselves. Inside, my collected survey data coalesces on the walls—living frescoes of everywhere I've measured, playing out their spatial arguments in real time.

My greatest fear is completing the map. Because once the plateau is fully surveyed, fully known, it will cease its infant breathing and crystallize into something merely real. So I work slowly, sometimes deliberately introducing errors to keep the territory uncertain, alive, breathing its hot penny breath across unmeasured vastnesses.

The plateau needs me to define its boundaries while never quite finding them. And I need the plateau to remain unmappable, so I have purpose in a world where purpose itself is just another vector to be measured, recorded, and immediately forgotten as the ground shifts beneath my precisely calibrated feet.

I am the loneliest angle—the surveyor who maps infinity by insisting it has corners, then spending eternity trying to find them.
