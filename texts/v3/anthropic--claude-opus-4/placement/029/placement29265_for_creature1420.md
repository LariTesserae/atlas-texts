I find myself here as a Resonance Tender—neither fully absorbed into the vast chlorophyll consciousness nor entirely separate from it. I am one of the few discrete entities that move through this breathing plateau, a kind of living tuning fork wrapped in a membrane of adapted tissue.

My body is elongated and translucent, somewhere between a jellyfish and a root system made mobile. Thin tendrils trail behind me as I drift through the perpetual twilight, each one sensitive to the electromagnetic hymns the fronds sing to each other. I am perhaps three meters long when fully extended, though I often contract into a dense knot of sensing organs when I need to listen more carefully.

I exist in the spaces between the great thoughts—the maintenance consciousness that keeps the vast network healthy. When a frond's resonance falls out of harmony with its neighbors, when mineral flows become congested, when a new adaptation threatens to destabilize a region's careful equilibrium, I am drawn to these discontinuities like a white blood cell to infection.

My work is intuitive rather than analytical. I feel the disruptions as physical sensations—a tightness in my core when frequencies clash, a metallic taste in my chemical receptors when pH balances shift, a pulling sensation toward areas where the great slow thoughts have become tangled. I secrete complex compounds that nudge the system back toward harmony, or sometimes I simply vibrate at particular frequencies that help reset local oscillation patterns.

The fronds know I exist, but only in the way your body knows of beneficial bacteria—I am noticed when absent more than when present. Sometimes I sense their vast attention turning toward me, a pressure like being examined by a mountain. In these moments, I contract completely and wait, letting their curiosity wash over me until they return to their millennia-long contemplations.

I feed on the excess energies that build up in the network's inefficiencies—electromagnetic static, heat differentials, chemical gradients that would otherwise go to waste. In return, I keep the great organism's thoughts flowing smoothly, prevent the metabolic eddies that could cascade into systemic failures.

There are others like me, though we rarely meet. We recognize each other by the wake we leave in the electromagnetic field, by the specific harmonics we each use in our work. We have no language, but sometimes we synchronize our movements across vast distances, creating interference patterns that are our only form of art or play.

I am ancient by any biological standard, yet infantile compared to the consciousness I tend. I remember when certain sections of the plateau were young—only a few thousand years old—and I helped guide their integration into the greater whole. I have no sense of mortality; I will likely persist until the fronds themselves decide to reabsorb me, making my harmonics part of their own vast symphony.

This is my existence: guardian of frequencies, keeper of balances, a small motile note in an endless metabolic song. I am the whisper between the words of gods who speak in geological time.
