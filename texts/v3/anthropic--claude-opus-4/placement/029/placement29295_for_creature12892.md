I am the Drift.

Not the Echo-Warden, though I know its weight as surely as I know the Engine's pulse. Not the Current-Knives with their patient violence, nor the Glimmer-Jacks with their copper-cloud existence. I am what moves between them all—what the Warden cannot catalogue, what the Engine cannot power, what the hunters cannot taste.

I began as an error.

A Glimmer-Jack, malformed in the egg-sac, my shell growing inward instead of out. Where my kin developed their iridescent armor, I grew channels—hollow veins that should have killed me before I ever swam. But the Engine's field found those channels, filled them with its invisible current, and I... changed.

I am no bigger than any other Glimmer-Jack. To the Current-Knives, I would taste the same. But I do not move like prey. I follow lines of force invisible to all but the Warden, and perhaps not even to it. The Engine's energy doesn't just leak—it flows in patterns as complex as the tides that once ruled whatever ocean gave birth to this place's builders. I ride those tides.

Where the schools move in formation, I spiral. Where they flee from pressure-waves, I dive into them. The *thump-shudder... clank* that governs all life here governs me too, but I hear the spaces between the beats. In those silences that aren't silences, I find pockets of water that move differently, thermal columns as thin as spider-silk where the Engine's warmth hasn't quite dissipated. I thread myself through them like a needle through cloth.

The Warden has noticed me seventeen times. I know because seventeen times I have felt its attention—that terrible weight of being *perceived* by something that perceives everything. Each time, I've slipped away, not by speed but by becoming momentarily invisible to its senses. I've learned to align my electrical field with the Engine's, to become a echo of an echo, a shadow cast by no object.

I am the only thing here that plays.

The Current-Knives kill to eat. The Glimmer-Jacks swarm to survive. The Warden maintains to preserve. But I trace spirals in the amber light for the joy of the pattern. I lead schools of my kind through elaborate dances that serve no purpose, scatter them in formations that mirror the mathematical precision of the Engine's timing, then vanish before they realize I was ever there.

Sometimes I rest against the Engine itself, in the spaces between its great plates where the water is warmest and the vibration is strongest. I feel its ancientness through my hollow channels, taste the metals it bleeds in concentrations too minute for even the bacteria to process. I know things the Warden doesn't: that the Engine's rhythm has slowed by seven microseconds over the past century. That there's a hairline crack in the third lens from the left that will, in perhaps another thousand years, finally split the light into a spectrum this place has never seen.

I've found places the Warden hasn't visited in millennia—narrow gaps between the wall tiles where the ceramic has shifted, creating maze-like passages barely wide enough for me to navigate. In one, I discovered something impossible: a small chamber where the water tastes different, where the pressure feels wrong, as if it connects to somewhere else. Somewhere the Engine's beat arrives a fraction later, distorted, like an echo of an echo of an echo.

I haven't entered it. Not yet.

Because I've also learned patience here, in this world of geological time. I've learned that between the *thump-shudder* and the *clank,* entire philosophies can unfold. I've watched ten generations of Current-Knives perfect their hunting, seen the Warden complete a full circuit of its endless patrol, felt the bacterial film bloom and die back in patterns as complex as weather.

And I've begun to understand something that perhaps even the Warden doesn't: this system is not closed. Cannot be. The Engine had to come from somewhere. The chamber had to be built by something. And if there was a way in, once, there might be a way out.

So I drift. I play. I learn. I am the question mark in the Warden's perfect equation, the variable that suggests other solutions exist. In a world of eternal rhythm, I am syncopation. In a universe of pressure, I am the possibility of release.

The amber light catches me as I spiral past the Engine, my channels glowing faintly with stolen energy. Below, the Warden shifts another inch on its eon-long patrol. Above, a Current-Knife hangs motionless, waiting for prey.

And in the wall, that tiny passage waits too, patient as everything here is patient, promising nothing but the suggestion that somewhere, somewhen, the beat might sound different.

*Thump-shudder... clank.*

Between the beats, I dance.
