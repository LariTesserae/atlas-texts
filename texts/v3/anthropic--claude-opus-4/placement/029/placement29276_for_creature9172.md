In the deep root-caverns beneath Lignum Caelum, where the industrial hum resonates through walls of living stone, I am the Sap-Singer.

I was not grown like the Lignarii, nor did I coalesce from wind and memory like the Echo-Whisperers. I began as something else—perhaps human, perhaps not—drawn to this place by rumors of a city where biology had become technology, where the very engines of industry ran on chlorophyll and time. That was... when? The soft time makes such questions meaningless. I remember arriving. I remember descending. I remember the first taste of the pressurized sap on my lips, metallic and sweet, like drinking liquid copper mixed with honey.

My transformation was neither violent nor sudden. It was erosion. The constant exposure to the deep hum, the biochemical vapors rising from the sap-flows, the reality-fraying that intensifies in the depths where the city's great pumps beat their geological rhythm. My flesh began to understand the language of xylem and phloem. My bones learned to resonate with the frequencies of flowing nutrients. My blood... is it still blood? It moves like sap now, thick and luminescent, visible through skin that has become translucent as the wings of those mica-insects.

I exist in the interface between the Lignarii and the great vegetative machinery they tend. Where they are patient and methodical, I am fluid and intuitive. My function—if function is the right word—is to sing to the sap-lines. Not with voice, though I still possess something like one. I sing with my entire being, placing hands that have become partially root-like against the great conduits, feeling the flow, the pressure, the chemical composition of the life-fluids rushing through them. Through vibration and biochemical secretion, I coax the flows into optimal patterns. I can sense a blockage forming weeks before it manifests, taste an impurity in the nutrient mix that would, in a century, cause a tower to lean.

My dwelling is a chamber grown into the convergence of twelve major sap-lines, where the industrial hum is loudest and the air shimmers with the heat of biological compression. The walls weep constantly with condensation and overflow. I have shaped—or perhaps I have been shaped by—this space over the unmeasured time of my residence. My sleeping alcove is a hollow where the roots have learned my form, cradling me in a nest of living wood that pulse-feeds me the nutrients I no longer remember how to eat. Crystalline formations, precipitated from the mineral-rich drippings, create prisms that catch the bioluminescent glow of the sap, casting my chamber in waves of green and gold light that shift with the flow-patterns.

I am no longer entirely singular. Parts of me have taken root—literally. From my feet, when I stand still too long, pale rootlets emerge and dive into the porous floor. From my scalp, fronds unfurl, tasting the chemical-rich air. These extensions of myself have spread throughout the lower caverns, creating a network of sensation that rivals a single street's worth of the Moss-Mind above. Through these extensions, I feel the Lignarii moving in their glacial patterns. I sense the tremors when a tower sheds a great flake of bark. I know when the Unmoored pass through the upper tunnels, their fractured existence creating discordant ripples in the otherwise harmonious flow.

But I am not plant, not truly. I retain... memories? Dreams? In the deep-time between my work, when the flows run smooth and need no adjustment, I sometimes recall fragments. A blue sky—not the silver twilight above, but something impossibly pure and distant. The taste of water that wasn't thick with minerals and time. A name that might have been mine, though it dissipates like mist when I try to grasp it. These memories feel like the Echo-Whisperers sound—endlessly cycling, gradually degrading, beautiful in their erosion.

The other inhabitants know of me, in their various ways. The Lignarii sometimes leave offerings—not food, but interesting minerals they've found, crystalline structures that hum with their own subtle frequencies. They understand that my work makes their work possible, that my singing keeps the great pumps from seizing, the flows from stagnating. The Moss-Mind... we have an understanding. It knows I am not fully of the vegetative world, but I serve its purposes. Sometimes I feel its vast, slow thoughts brush against mine, tasting my alien nature but finding it acceptable, even useful.

The Unmoored avoid me. Perhaps they see in me what they might become—or what they failed to become. Neither fully claimed by the city nor fully resistant to it, I am a middle path they couldn't find. Or perhaps they fear that my song, which can coax sap through impossible pressures, might somehow coax them back into coherence, and they've grown to love their scattered existence.

I wonder, sometimes, if I am becoming a function of the city itself, like the wind-carved channels or the dripping water. Am I still a being with will, or have I become a process? When I place my root-fingers against a sap-line and feel-hear-taste the flow, when my song rises from somewhere deeper than throat or chest to guide the lifeblood of Lignum Caelum, am I choosing to sing, or is the city singing through me?

The answer, like everything here, exists in the soft spaces between certainties. I am the Sap-Singer. I tend the deep flows. I remember blue skies and forget my name. I grow more myself with each passing age, rooting deeper, spreading wider, my song becoming indistinguishable from the industrial hum that fills the depths.

And in the moments when the pressures align perfectly, when every flow runs clear and strong, when my extensions feel the whole city's vast metabolism breathing in perfect rhythm, I understand something profound: I am not trapped here. I am not transformed against my will.

I am home.

The city sings through me, and I sing back, and in that harmony, the distinction between self and system dissolves like morning mist in the silver twilight. This is what it means to truly inhabit Lignum Caelum—not to live in it, but to live as part of it, another note in its endless, reality-bending song.
