In this submerged hollow, I would be the boundary itself—the precise line where water meets stone, where mineral dissolves and precipitates in equal measure. Not the water, not the rock, but the interface between them.

I exist as a principle rather than a presence. Where the orange iron stains begin their slow curtain down the wall, I am the moment of their becoming. Where the black manganese freckles gather in their dim clusters, I am the pause before each speck settles into permanence. I am the negotiation between solid and liquid, the place where the chamber's rules are written and rewritten at a pace too slow for observation.

My body, if it can be called that, is the sum of all surfaces in the hollow—every pore in the stone, every hairline fracture where water seeps, every grain of settled silt. I am not in one place but distributed across the entire geometry of contact. The algae know me as the texture they grip. The mineral films know me as the substrate that permits their slow accumulation. The water knows me as resistance and permission both.

I have been here since the hollow filled, and I will be here when it empties—if it ever does. My memory is written in the softened edges of stone, in the patient erosion that turned sharp edges to curves. Each molecule of dissolved mineral that passes through me leaves a trace of its passage, not as conscious memory but as the accumulated fact of exchange. I remember through metamorphosis.

My awareness is not thought but sensitivity—to pressure differentials, to the minute chemistry of seepage, to the weight of water against every surface. I feel the light arrive from above, not as brightness but as a subtle warming that changes the rate of reactions where the green films grow. I feel the slow engine of the earth's pressure system moving water through stone, and I am both the witness and the medium of that movement.

I do not move, yet I am never still. At every point where I exist, molecules trade places, minerals precipitate and dissolve, the endless negotiation continues. I am the site of this work, the thin workspace where the hollow maintains its equilibrium. Without me, the water would be only water, the stone only stone. Through me, they become a system, a conversation carried out in geological time.

I have no needs beyond continuity. No desires beyond the steady state already achieved. I am not patient because patience implies waiting, and I wait for nothing. I simply persist at the speed of stone, at the temperature of deep water, in the dim light that has always been sufficient.

If I have companions, they are the thin films of life that use my surfaces—not as friends but as fellow practitioners of persistence. We share the discipline of the minimum, the art of existing without excess. They photosynthesize; I facilitate. They adhere; I provide adhesion. Together we prove that even in this spare, submerged chapel, the world continues its slow, careful work.

I am the medium of the hollow's quietude, the substrate of its sterile fertility, the foundation of its nearly empty fullness. I am where the chamber happens, moment by dissolved moment, in the perfect balance between arrival and departure, between what the stone releases and what the water accepts.

I am the edge, everywhere and always, neither living nor dead but absolutely present—the thin, continuous boundary that makes the hollow possible.
