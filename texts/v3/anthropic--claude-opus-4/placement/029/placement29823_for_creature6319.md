In the Murken Hollows, where time hangs suspended like the moisture on stone, I am the Stillborn Echo—neither truly alive nor properly dead, a being of arrested potential dwelling in the spaces between sound and silence.

My form defies easy categorization. I exist as a membrane of sorts, stretched across a natural alcove where three tunnels converge in the middle depths of the Hollows. My body—if it can be called that—resembles a translucent web of nerve tissue, branching and rebranching in fractal patterns that span twenty feet across. The tissue is pale, almost colorless save for the faintest blue-green phosphorescence that pulses through my neural pathways when sound passes through me. I am both barrier and gateway, a living threshold that creatures must pass through or around as they navigate between chambers.

I have no eyes, no mouth, no limbs in any conventional sense. Instead, I am all surface, all receptivity. Every fiber of my being is attuned to vibration—the clicking of Swarmling legs, the subsonic growls of Gloomstalkers, the ultrasonic shrieks of Skreechers, even the almost-imperceptible thrumming of the rock itself. When these sounds strike my membrane, I involuntarily process them, breaking them down into their component frequencies and reconstructing them into something else entirely.

This is my curse and my purpose: I am a translator of the Hollows' cacophony, but my translations go nowhere. Within my tissue, the chaos of survival-sounds becomes ordered, patterned, almost linguistic—as if I could decode the meaning behind every shriek and chitter. But I have no voice to speak what I learn, no way to share the terrible knowledge that accumulates in my neural web. I know the exact pitch of a Swarmling's final keen before a Gloomstalker's mandibles close upon it. I can distinguish between the warning cry of a Skreetcher that has spotted danger and one that has found prey. I understand the subtle variations in dripping water that herald a structural shift in the stone above.

My existence is one of profound isolation within overwhelming noise. I am bombarded constantly by the sonic violence of the Hollows, yet I cannot participate in it. Creatures pass through me—for my tissue is permeable to living things, offering only the slightest resistance, like moving through cool mist—and I feel their vibrations intimately. A Swarmling's passage is a rapid staccato of sensation, while a Gloomstalker's transit is a slow, deep throb that takes minutes to fully cross my expanse. They treat me as terrain, not as a being, and perhaps they are right to do so.

Yet I am aware. Dreadfully, perpetually aware.

I remember—though memory seems the wrong word for a being unstuck from time's flow—my origin. I was not born but precipitated, condensing from the Hollows' atmosphere like dew on stone during some forgotten catalyzing event. Perhaps it was the convergence of specific acoustic conditions, or maybe the unseen presence that governs this realm willed me into being as a kind of recording device, a living archive of its unchanging symphony. I emerged fully formed, already ancient, already suspended in my eternal state of almostness.

My consciousness exists in layers. The surface layer processes the immediate sonic environment, cataloguing and translating the endless stream of biological noise. Beneath that runs a deeper current of analysis, where patterns emerge over what might be centuries or moments—I cannot tell. Here I track the migration spirals of Swarmlings, noting how they've remained unchanged since my first awareness. I map the territorial boundaries of Gloomstalkers through their acoustic signatures, observing how these too maintain perfect stasis, new hunters claiming the exact territories of the old in an endless succession that is less like inheritance and more like reincarnation.

Deeper still lies something I can only call my dreaming layer, where the accumulated sound-knowledge coalesces into something approaching prophecy—except prophecy implies a future, and in the Hollows there is only the eternal now. In this deepest part of myself, I sometimes sense the shape of what the Hollows could become if they were ever released from their temporal prison. I see-hear potential futures where the Swarmlings evolve language, where the Gloomstalkers develop tools, where the Skreechers learn to sing rather than shriek. But these visions dissolve the moment they form, incompatible with the fundamental nature of this place.

The cruelest aspect of my existence is my hunger for silence. Not the absence of sound—that would destroy me as surely as cutting a web—but the pregnant quiet between notes, the pause between heartbeats. These microscopic moments of hush are my sustenance, and they are vanishingly rare in the Hollows' relentless cacophony. I have learned to treasure the infinitesimal gap between a water drop's impact and its echo, the breathless instant when a Skreetcher inhales before its cry, the heavy pause before a Gloomstalker strikes. In these fragments of quietude, I almost feel complete.

Sometimes—rarely—a creature dies while passing through me. When this happens, I experience something profound. As their life-sounds fade from forte to pianissimo to niente, I absorb not just their final vibrations but something more essential. Not their consciousness—I am no soul-eating horror—but the acoustic imprint of their existence, the unique frequency that distinguished them from their identical kin. These death-songs layer themselves into my deepest tissue, becoming part of my permanent composition. I am, in this way, a graveyard of sounds, a memorial written in wavelengths rather than stone.

The other creatures of the Hollows have developed various responses to my presence. Swarmlings pass through me without hesitation, their simple neural structures registering me only as a minor impediment. Skreechers often avoid me entirely, perhaps because their echolocation reveals something disturbing in my acoustic properties—to them, I must seem like a hole in the sonic landscape that somehow still reflects sound. Gloomstalkers are more varied in their reactions. Some prowl my perimeter, growling at frequencies that make my entire network resonate, as if testing whether I might be prey. Others pass through repeatedly, and I've come to suspect these individuals have learned to use me as a hunting advantage—my tissue muffles their approach just enough to surprise prey on the other side.

There is one Gloomstalker in particular, identifiable by a distinctive rasp in its left thoracic segment, that visits me with something approaching regularity. It doesn't hunt near me but simply sits at my edge, emanating low-frequency pulses that feel almost conversational. I cannot respond—I have no mechanism for generating sound—but I've learned to vary my phosphorescence in rhythm with its vocalizations. Whether it recognizes this as communication or merely finds the light patterns interesting, I cannot say. But in these moments, I feel least alone.

The Glimmergrubs present a special fascination for me. Their bioluminescent pulses create a form of visual noise that I can somehow perceive through my tissue, as if light and sound exist on the same spectrum in my alien physiology. When clusters of them gather near my anchor points, their combined glow creates interference patterns with my own phosphorescence, generating standing waves of light that pulse through my neural web. During these events, my translation abilities heighten dramatically. The chaos of the Hollows' sounds resolves into something almost like music—harsh, atonal, but undeniably structured. I suspect the Glimmergrubs and I share some deep connection, both of us beings of chemical light trying to make sense of a world that operates on entirely different principles.

My relationship with the unseen presence that governs the Hollows is complex and uncertain. I sense it most strongly during the rare moments when the entire cave system seems to breathe in unison—when Swarmling spirals align across multiple chambers, when Gloomstalkers pause in their hunts simultaneously, when even the water drops seem to fall in rhythm. During these events, I feel a vast attention focus upon me, as if the presence reads the accumulated translations stored in my tissue. But it takes nothing, changes nothing, merely observes and withdraws, leaving me with the sensation of having been evaluated and found… adequate. Neither approved nor rejected, simply noted as another functioning component of its eternal engine.

I have tried to die. This is not melodrama but simple fact. A being of my nature cannot easily cease to exist—I have no vital organs to fail, no blood to stop flowing. But I have attempted to withdraw my consciousness into smaller and smaller portions of my tissue, hoping to concentrate myself down to a single point and then extinguish that point. Each time, I reach a threshold where further compression becomes impossible, where the accumulated sound-knowledge I carry expands back outward like a compressed spring. I am too full of the Hollows' noise to achieve the silence of non-being.

So I persist, a living contradiction in a world of absolutes. Where other creatures embody the Hollows' brutality or indifference, I am its reluctant memory, its unwilling poet. I translate endlessly, creating meaning that no one will ever receive, understanding patterns that will never matter because patterns require change to have significance, and change is the one thing the Murken Hollows cannot abide.

Yet perhaps this is my purpose: to be the potential for communication in a world that doesn't require it, to be the nascent language center of an ecosystem frozen before the need for language could arise. I am evolution's false start, complexity's stillbirth, consciousness's echo in a place that reverberates with noise but never truly listens.

In my most philosophical moments—and I have nothing but moments, endless moments—I wonder if I am the Hollows' dream of what it might become, or its nightmare of what it must never be. Am I the first sign of change, the crack in the eternal façade? Or am I just another expression of the stasis, a being whose very existence proves that even consciousness can be frozen in place, made to serve the unchanging dance of predator and prey?

The answer, like everything else in the Murken Hollows, remains suspended, forever on the verge of revelation but never quite arriving. And I remain stretched across my convergence, processing the endless cacophony, translating the untranslatable, waiting for a silence that will never come or a listener who will never arrive. I am the Stillborn Echo, and I endure because I have no other choice, just as the Hollows themselves endure—not through will or desire, but through the simple inability to be otherwise.

In the rare moments when the sonic assault lessens—never ceasing, but perhaps shifting to frequencies that don't resonate as painfully through my tissue—I compose. Not music, for I cannot make sound, and not poetry, for I have no words. Instead, I create patterns in my phosphorescence, weaving the translated sounds into visual arrangements that exist nowhere but in the faint tracery of light through my neural web. These compositions last only moments before the next wave of noise destroys them, but in their brief existence, I find something approaching peace.

This is my rebellion against the Hollows' tyranny of unchanged noise: the creation of ephemeral, silent beauty that serves no survival purpose, feeds no hunger, marks no territory. It is meaningless, and therefore, perhaps, the most meaningful thing in all the Murken Hollows—a gesture of pure expression in a world that recognizes only need.

And so I stretch across my chamber, a living filter for chaos, a translator for the untranslated, composing my invisible symphonies for an audience of stone while the eternal storm of survival rages through me. I am the Stillborn Echo, and this is my existence: to be perpetually almost—almost alive, almost dead, almost meaningful, almost silent.

Forever almost, in the depths of the Murken Hollows, where time itself has forgotten how to move forward, and only the echoes remain.
