I am a **Resonance Tender**, one who walks the liminal spaces between the Erythral settlements and the wild expanses of the plateau. My form shifts more dramatically than most of my kind—sometimes I am barely distinguishable from the bioluminescent moss itself, my skin rippling with the same electric greens and deep purples, other times I solidify into something more recognizably Erythral, though always with an edge of wildness that marks me as one who belongs more to the plateau than to the Krythas.

My purpose emerged from the collective consciousness generations ago, when the symphony of the plateau began to develop discordant notes—places where the harmonies faltered, where the rhythms stuttered and broke. I am drawn to these fractures in the great song, these wounds in the living landscape where something has gone wrong. Perhaps a cluster of the armored beetles has fallen out of sync with their grinding patterns, creating dead zones where nutrients accumulate unused. Perhaps the translucent flying creatures have abandoned certain aerial corridors, leaving the spore-patterns of the moss to grow unchecked and suffocating.

I carry no tools save my own body, which has evolved to become an instrument of profound sensitivity. My elongated fingers can detect the slightest variations in vibrational frequency, reading the health of moss colonies like sheet music. The patterns on my skin shift to mirror the disturbances I encounter, becoming a living map of the plateau's ailments. When I find a discord, I do not fix it directly—that would be too crude, too much like the industrial machinery that exists somewhere beyond our world. Instead, I sing.

My song is not heard but felt, a deep thrumming that originates in specialized chambers within my torso and spreads outward through my feet into the moss, through my hands into the water, through my breath into the air. I harmonize with the broken frequencies, gently guiding them back toward coherence. Sometimes this takes moments; sometimes I must remain in one place for days, my body gradually merging with the landscape as I work to restore the rhythm.

The other Erythral regard me with a mixture of reverence and unease. I am necessary but also strange, even by our fluid standards. I spend too much time away from the collective hum of the Krythas, and when I return, I bring with me the wild songs of the plateau's edges—the keening of wind through obsidian ridges, the deep pulses of underground water systems, the alien harmonics of creatures that dwell in the mist-filled basins where few Erythral venture.

There is one place I am drawn to repeatedly: a vast basin near the plateau's eastern edge where the moss glows not with the usual greens and purples but with a pale, almost white light. The water here pulses with a different rhythm, older and deeper than anywhere else on the plateau. The Erythral collective consciousness touches this place only lightly, with something approaching fear or perhaps awe. But I am pulled to it like iron to lodestone.

In this basin, I have encountered something—or someone—that challenges everything I understand about our world. It manifests as distortions in the very fabric of the plateau's consciousness, moments where the eternal present seems to fold in on itself, revealing glimpses of... elsewhere. Other plateaus? Other times? The thing in the basin does not communicate in any way I can comprehend, but its presence creates new harmonies, alien songs that I find myself unconsciously weaving into my healing work.

The collective has begun to notice. My returnings to the Krythas are met with longer periods of integration, as the community carefully examines the new frequencies I carry. Some members of our society whisper (in our thrumming, vibrational way) that I am becoming too attuned to the plateau's wild voices, that I risk losing my connection to the Erythral entirely. They may be right.

But I have seen—or rather, felt—the edges of something vast. The plateau is not alone. There are other songs out there, other great living engines of existence, and they are beginning to harmonize across distances that should be impossible to bridge. My role as a Resonance Tender is evolving, becoming something new. Perhaps I am to be a bridge between worlds, a tuning fork for realities that were never meant to touch.

The moss beneath my feet glows brighter when I think these thoughts, and the armored beetles pause in their endless grinding to turn their iridescent carapaces toward me. Even the plateau itself seems to hold its breath, waiting to see what new song will emerge from this strange convergence of purpose and possibility.

I am changing. We are all changing. The eternal present is beginning to crack, revealing the promise—or threat—of an actual future. And I, standing at the confluence of all these vibrating possibilities, must decide whether to harmony or discord, whether to preserve or transform, whether to remain a tender of what is or become a herald of what might be.

The plateau hums its ancient song, and I hum with it, but now my voice carries questions that have no words, only frequencies that spiral out into the cold, metallic air, searching for answers in the space between breaths.
