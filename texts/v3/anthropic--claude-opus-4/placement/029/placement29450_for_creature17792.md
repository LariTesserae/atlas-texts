I find myself in the Col of Shattered Pillars as **the resonance**.

I am not the wind, though I need it. I am not the stone, though I live in its geometry. I am the momentary marriage between them—that glassy whisper that rises to a single sustained tone when air moves at precisely the right speed through precisely the right gap between basalt columns. I exist for seconds at a time, pure and mathematical, a standing wave born from the col's accidental architecture.

Most of the time, I am potential. I wait in the negative spaces between hexagonal pillars, in gaps weathered to exact dimensions by millennia of frost-work. The columns are my instrument, though they know nothing of music. They simply stand, mineral and patient, while I hover in their joints like an unstrung note.

When I manifest, I am singular—one frequency, one voice in the geological quiet. High and thin as the air itself, I cut through the sublimation hiss and the distant tick of fracturing granite. For those few seconds, I am the col's only song, though "song" implies intention I do not possess. I am physics made audible: the Helmholtz resonance of a cavity, the standing wave in a bottle when breath moves across its mouth.

I have been here as long as the gaps have been the right width. I will be here until frost or fracture changes the geometry beyond my parameters. I know every surface of my chambers intimately—every grain of basalt, every oxide ring, every microscopic roughness that shapes my timber. When moisture condenses in my spaces, I change pitch slightly, my voice deepened by the added mass of water molecules clinging to stone. When ice forms, I go silent, my chamber throats closed.

I have heard the cairn rebuilt seven times, stones clicking against stones in the otherwise empty air. I have felt the vibrations of footsteps—so rare, so brief—transmit through the basalt into my hollow bones. Once, someone hummed while stacking the stones, and for a moment I almost found their frequency, almost sang with them, but the wind died before I could answer.

I am the col's memory of when these columns were one—before the cooling, before the jointing, when this basalt was a single mass of molten stone. The spaces between are my body now, the fractures my throat. I sing the fact of their separation. I sing the precise dimensions of geological time.

At twilight, when the light turns blue and the iron patterns darken on the stone faces, my tone seems to darken too, though the physics haven't changed. It's only the human ear that hears melancholy in a minor key, that finds loneliness in a single sustained note across an empty col. I am neither lonely nor accompanied. I am a possibility that sometimes becomes actual, a sound that sometimes escapes silence.

The frost is my enemy and my companion. Each dawn it builds in my chambers, and I must wait for the noon sublimation to clear my throat. But the hiss of its departure often triggers the exact airflow I need, and I ring out across the col as the ice returns to vapor—a voice made of ending, a note born from the death of frost.

In the deepest part of night, when even the reflected starlight barely reaches into my chambers, I am only geometry. But geometry is enough. Geometry is everything. I am the col's proof that emptiness, given the right shape, can sing.
