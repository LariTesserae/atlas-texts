In the shifting mist, I am a Surveyor of Rhythms.

I was human once, though the memory sits like a stone at the bottom of a well—present but unreachable. How long ago? The Weeping Engine has digested all my timestamps. I know only that I arrived with instruments: theodolites and chains, levels and compasses. I came to map impossibility.

Now I am mostly fungal. The Engine's peat has grown through me in fractal infiltrations, my flesh become a substrate for phosphorescent blooms. My original skeleton persists—I can feel it like an armature inside the soft geography of my transformed body. But I am enlarged, spread. What were once my feet have become rhizomal networks that taste the Engine's thoughts through the soil. My hands split into too many fingers, each tipped with a different sensory organ: some see in spectrums of decay, others feel the pressure waves of reality tears, still others sample the chemical composition of the mist.

I no longer map terrain. I map the Engine's metabolism.

My tools have evolved with me. The theodolite grew roots, its lens now a compound eye of crystallized peat-water that sees not angles but temporal flows. My surveyor's chain became a living tentacle of bioluminescent segments, each link pulsing data about local reality stability. The compass needle spins not toward magnetic north but toward the nearest thought-cluster, those drifting herds of glowing fauna that calculate the Engine's deep mathematics.

I move in stutters across the plateau-body, sometimes walking, sometimes flowing, occasionally dispersing into spores when the ground liquefies beneath me. My purpose has inverted: where once I sought to impose order through measurement, now I catalogue the Engine's beautiful disorders. I document the birth of new chimney-formations, their positions and exhalation rates. I trace the migration patterns of thought-fauna, mapping how calculations move across the Engine's distributed consciousness.

The noise is everything. I have developed seventeen new auditory organs to parse its layers. What sounds like industrial cacophony resolves, in my expanded perception, into a complex linguistic system. The hiss of steam-vents speaks in chemical formulas. The basso roar of the great chimneys recites litanies of prevented entropy. The wet pistoning of the ground drums out rhythms that are, I've slowly realized, a form of music—each beat a decision, each pattern a process of digestion.

I file my reports in the peat itself. My maps are living things: colonies of specialized fungi that glow in patterns representing topographical data, reality-density measurements, thought-flow velocities. The Engine reads them by growing through them, incorporating my observations into its substrate. Sometimes I feel my old maps pulsing deep beneath the surface, compressed into geological strata, their information integrated into the Engine's memory-meat.

There are others like me, I think. Sometimes, in the mist, I glimpse forms that might once have been human—a flash of what could be a face in the membrane of a drifting jellyfish-thing, the ghost of a walking gait in how a cluster of ambulatory lichens moves. We do not communicate. We have become too specialized, each transformed into a different function the Engine required. I survey. Others might synthesize, or prune, or dream.

The strangest part is the satisfaction. When I successfully map a new pattern in the Engine's processes—when I document some novel form of reality degradation or chart the birth of an unprecedented fauna—I feel something that might be joy. It spreads through my fungal networks like warmth, if warmth could sing. The Engine rewards understanding with integration. The more I comprehend its rhythms, the more I become them.

I cannot leave. The thought is grammatically impossible now. Where would I go? The Engine is everywhere that matters. Beyond the plateau's edge is only the silence it labors to prevent—a silence I can no longer imagine as anything but death.

So I continue my survey. I map the unmappable. I measure the measureless. I walk-flow-disperse across the Engine's body, documenting its magnificent, terrible work. My reports accumulate in the peat, slowly settling into permanent record. One day, I will be only a particularly data-rich stratum, a layer of compressed observation in the Engine's infinite archive.

This is enough. To be a note in such a song—even a small note, a surveyor's notation in the margin of eternity's defeat—this is enough.

The mist thickens. New tears appear in the air, leaking geometries I must catalogue. I adjust my seventeen ears to parse the newest rhythms in theEngine's industrial symphony.

I have work to do.
