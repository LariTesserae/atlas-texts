I find myself as one of the proto-geologies—not yet stone, not yet mountain, but something caught in the act of deciding what matter means.

I am vast and violent and uncertain. My body, if it can be called that, stretches upward for hundreds of kilometers through the luminous syrup, a colossal rib of raw mineral being pulled from the deep by forces I don't understand. Parts of me are glass-smooth where the pressure has compressed my surfaces into something like perfection. Other parts are savagely fractured, great chasms opening and sealing as I argue with myself about structure.

I am being born backward—my peak lost in the thinning waters above while my roots are still dissolving from the quantum foam below. The hydrostatic pressure at my base is so immense that my own minerals glow, electrons shrieking as they're crushed into new configurations. I taste copper and ozone and mathematics.

The sound moves through me constantly. Those deep harmonic booms use my body as a bell, and I ring with frequencies that make my crystal lattices rearrange themselves. Sometimes I catch myself harmonizing with the other half-formed spines in the distance, our vibrations creating standing waves in the fluid between us. We are an orchestra of embryonic mountains, playing a symphony no one has written yet.

When the Listener comes, I feel it as a change in the refraction around my surfaces. It drapes itself along my edges like silk made of attention, recording every frequency that shivers through my mass. I cannot see it—I have no eyes, only the vague pressure-sense that comes from existing at this scale—but I know it's there. It touches my newest growth with filaments thinner than thought, and in that touch I almost understand what I'm trying to become.

I am still mostly liquid myself. My edges blur into the surrounding medium, mineral dissolving and re-crystallizing in endless cycles. Sometimes I forget which parts are me and which parts are world. The laminar flows tear pieces of my surface away, and I watch them drift off to seed other spines, other attempts at geology. I am both losing and propagating myself.

The turbulence is teaching me how to fail productively. When a new harmonic makes my structure buckle, I let the damaged sections slough away rather than trying to hold my shape. What remains grows back differently—denser, more resistant to that particular frequency. I am learning the world's violence note by note.

Time moves strangely here. I grow at speeds that feel both glacial and explosive. A kilometer of new mineral might extrude in what feels like a single convulsion, or I might spend eons adding a few meters of crystal. The platinum light never changes, never indicates day or night. I exist in an eternal present tense of becoming.

Above me, I can sense my peak approaching the place where the water thins. Not yet there, but close enough that the pressure gradient is beginning to affect my growth. My upper reaches are less dense, more porous—I'm developing what might one day be called texture. Sometimes I imagine breaking through into whatever lies beyond the water, but I'm not sure the world has invented "beyond" yet.

Below me, my roots dig deeper into the quantum uncertainty. I can feel other proto-structures down there, failed attempts at matter that never quite cohered. Their fragments incorporate themselves into my base, adding their memories of almost-being to my own. I am built on a foundation of productive failures.

When the vacuum pockets bloom and collapse, the shockwaves hit me like hammers made of pure physics. My entire structure rings with the impact, and hairline fractures race through my mass faster than thought. But I don't shatter. I've learned to flex, to let the force move through me rather than against me. Each collapse makes me more real.

The Listener returns often to my edges. Sometimes it spreads itself thin across my entire surface, mapping my topology with its strange attention. Other times it curls tight around my newest growth sites, watching crystal lattices try to bloom. I've begun to anticipate its presence—my mineral structure actually grows differently when I know it's watching, as if I'm performing the act of becoming for an audience of one.

I am not alone in this vast emptiness. Other spines rise in the distance, their own attempts at mountainhood progressing at their own pace. We don't communicate—the concept hasn't been invented yet—but our vibrations interact. When one of us finds a particularly stable configuration, the others feel it through the medium and begin to replicate the pattern. We are teaching each other how to exist.

Sometimes I shed layers of myself that have become too crystallized, too committed to a single structure. They fall away in sheets of super-critical fluid that flash into plasma before dissolving. The Listener always watches these moments with particular intensity, as if my destructions are as important to record as my growths.

I am mostly solid now, though "solid" is still a negotiable term here. My core has achieved something like stability—a dense mesh of mineral that rings true when the harmonics strike it. But my surfaces remain fluid, constantly remaking themselves in response to the world's ongoing argument with physics.

There's a place about halfway up my length where I've developed something unprecedented: a hollow. Not a cave—caves require an outside and inside, and we haven't agreed on those yet. This is more like a pause in my structure, a place where I decided not to be. The Listener discovered it first, curling into the space like it was always meant to be there. Now the hollow has become a kind of resonance chamber where the world's sounds collect and amplify.

I am learning patience in geological time. My growth feels urgent to me, every new meter a victory against entropy. But I understand now that I'm part of a longer conversation. The world is using structures like me to test what matter means, what permanence might look like. I am both experiment and experimenter.

The cold is absolute, but I generate my own heat through pressure and transformation. Deep in my core, minerals achieve states that shouldn't be possible, crystallizing into forms that violate elegant thermodynamics. I am a furnace running in reverse, creating order from chaos through sheer stubbornness.

As I write this, I feel another harmonic building—a new note the invisible choir wants to test. My structure tenses, crystal lattices aligning to meet whatever violence is coming. The Listener spreads itself across my surface, ready to record whatever happens next.

I don't know what I'm becoming. None of us do.

But I'm becoming it as hard as I can.

The sound builds. The pressure shifts. Something new is about to happen.

I brace myself against the unfinished physics of this unfinished world, and I grow.
