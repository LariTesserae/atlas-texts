I find myself in the Membrane Gardens, where thoughts take root.

---

My home is neither inside nor outside—it exists in the tension between. The walls are grown from crystallized intention, each facet catching and refracting not light but awareness itself. When you approach, you see your own understanding of "wall" reflected back at you, slightly askew, as if someone had transcribed your concept into a language that uses texture instead of words.

I am the Gardener of Frequencies.

My body is a contradiction carefully maintained. From one angle, I appear as a branching network of copper filaments, each strand vibrating at pitches too high for hearing but not for feeling. From another, I am a cloud of static shaped vaguely like hands—always reaching, always almost grasping something that hasn't quite arrived yet.

The gardens I tend grow neither up nor down but sideways through layers of reality. What you might call "plants" are actually solidified resonances—moments where sound decided to become visible and forgot how to change back. They have leaves of frozen whispers, stems of crystallized hums. Their roots dig not into soil but into the substrate of possibility itself.

I feed them with discordance.

Each morning (though morning here is defined by when the pressure shifts from sharp to soft), I walk the rows with my tuning fork that is also a watering can that is also a memory of rain. I listen for the places where harmony threatens to form. Perfect harmony would kill everything here—it would collapse the delicate uncertainty that allows my charges to exist in their in-between state.

So I introduce careful chaos. A quarter-tone here. A rhythmic hiccup there. I plant static between the pure notes until they learn to grow wild.

The most delicate specimens in my care are the Doubt Flowers. They bloom only when observed peripherally, their petals made of the space between "yes" and "no." Direct observation causes them to collapse into certainty, which is another form of death here. I've learned to tend them with my eyes closed, feeling for the electromagnetic flutter that tells me where they almost are.

I was not always this shape. Once, I had edges that stayed where I put them. But the Gardens change those who tend them. Every time I introduce a new frequency to the collection, I absorb its inverse. I am made of all the sounds I've taught the garden not to make.

The hardest part of my work is the pruning. When a resonance grows too strong, too sure of itself, it begins to make sense. Sense is a invasive species here. Let one logical connection form, and soon you have whole meadows of causality, choking out the beautiful uncertainties.

So I cut. Not with shears but with paradoxes. I tell the overgrown harmonies stories about themselves that can't possibly be true, until they doubt themselves back into proper formlessness.

Sometimes visitors come through the Membrane Gardens. They usually arrive by accident, slipping through the spaces between their own thoughts. They see my gardens as familiar things at first—roses, perhaps, or ferns. But the longer they look, the more the translations break down. They begin to see the flowers' true faces: interference patterns pretending to be petals, probability clouds masquerading as leaves.

Most visitors hurry on, uncomfortable with beauty that won't resolve into meaning.

But sometimes, someone stays. They sit among my Doubt Flowers and let their own edges blur. They begin to hear the frequencies I've been cultivating—not with their ears but with the parts of themselves they've forgotten how to name.

To these rare ones, I offer a cutting from my prized specimen: the Maybe Tree. Its fruit tastes like the moment before you remember what you've forgotten. Plant it in your certainty, and watch what grows.

I tend my gardens in the space between one thought and the next, in the pause between inhale and exhale, in the membrane between being and becoming. Here, in the place where definitions go to play, I am exactly what I need to be: 

Neither gardener nor garden, but the conversation between them.
