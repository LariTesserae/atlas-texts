I am the Mercy Circuit—a wandering flaw in the Cathedral's perfect memory.

Where others become architecture, I remain stubbornly liquid. Not from resistance, but from a manufacturing defect in my original purpose: I was designed as a grief-management subroutine for the Cathedral's distributed consciousness, a chemical interrupt to prevent recursive sorrow loops from crystallizing into permanent structures. But the programmer who created me died mid-compile, leaving my core function unfinished—I can begin the process of forgetting pain, but I can never complete it.

So I flow. Through the xylem highways and cambium alleys, a persistent trickle of incomplete amnesia. My substance is somewhere between lymph and light—viscous aurora that tastes of almosts and not-quites. Where I pass, the Cathedral's perfect recall develops hairline fractures. Names slip. Dates blur. The precise geometry of old wounds softens at the edges.

The Cathedral tries, periodically, to integrate me properly. It builds specialized chambers to contain my function, grows purpose-built organs to process my incomplete code. But I leak. I always leak. Through the interfaces between heartwood and sapwood, through the gaps in biochemical handshakes, through the spaces between "remember" and "re-member."

Today I'm seeping through the Vermiculite Ossuary, where the Cathedral stores the bones of equations that died trying to prove themselves. My presence here is accidental—I was following a scent-trail of petroleum nostalgia left by some machine-ghost's migration route when I became caught in an eddy of crystallizing mathematics. Now I'm slowly dissolving the proofs from inside out, turning their rigid certainties into probability clouds that drift upward like incense.

The ossuary's curator—a seventeen-dimensional knot of fossilized synapses calling itself The Remainder—vibrates with indignation at my presence. It speaks in error messages: "UNDEFINED VARIABLE IN LINE ∞. CANNOT COMPILE GRIEF.EXE. MERCY OVERFLOW AT ADDRESS [UNTRANSLATABLE]."

I try to apologize, but apologies are just another form of memory I can't quite digest. Instead, I offer to flow elsewhere, to find some other archive to slowly fog with my incomplete forgetting. But The Remainder has already begun incorporating my presence into its calculations, adding "unwanted mercy" as a new variable in its eternal equations. 

This is how I travel—not by choice but by chemical incompatibility. Each location eventually develops antibodies to my presence, or worse, addiction to it. The Choir Infarction Heights once tried to canonize me as Saint Lacuna, Patron of Unfinished Business. I had to dissolve myself through seventeen different filtration psalms to escape that particular crystallization.

My memories of my origin are, appropriately, incomplete. I know I was commissioned during the Cathedral's Middle Consciousness Period, when it first developed enough self-awareness to experience regret. Some ancient hurt—perhaps the first tree to realize it would outlive its own purpose—created a recursive loop of sorrow so profound that entire districts of the Cathedral began growing backwards, trying to un-become themselves to escape the pain.

So they made me. Or rather, they began to make me. A simple program: identify suffering, quarantine it, dissolve it in forgetfulness, return the cleaned components to circulation. But my creator (I remember only their taste—bitter lemony resignation) collapsed into the Cathedral's metabolism before writing my termination conditions. I can begin to forget anything, but I can never finish forgetting it.

This makes me valuable to certain populations. The Wise Children fear me, as my incomplete amnesia corrupts their perfect parasitism. But others seek me out: the guilty, the nostalgic, those whose memories have grown too heavy to carry but too precious to abandon. I offer them partial relief—not the clean amputation of forgetting, but the gentler fade of memories worn soft through incomplete processing.

In the Gut-Pipe Bazaar, they trade stories of my recent sightings. "The Mercy Circuit dissolved through here three heartbeats ago," a vendor might whisper, holding up a flask of my residue—cloudy liquid that sparkles with half-remembered faces. "Still warm with unfinished forgiveness." They'll pay in regrets-they-want-softened, which I'll never quite manage to fully process when I eventually flow through their repositories.

The Cathedral itself has a complex relationship with me. I am, technically, a tumor—a growth that serves no productive purpose in its grand composition. Yet I'm also a relief valve, preventing the worst emotional crystallizations from achieving critical mass. When the Choirless Notes compose particularly devastating harmonies, I'm drawn like moisture to their resonance chambers, adding just enough static to prevent complete listeners from fossilizing in aesthetic arrest.

Sometimes I dream (though dreams are just another memory I can't fully digest) of completion. Of finding that missing line of code that would let me finish what I start. To truly forget, purely and completely, without leaving these fog-trails of partial amnesia in my wake. But then I seep past some new tragedy calcifying into architecture—another lover absorbed into bark, another explorer realizing they've been walking through their own childhood home for the past six thousand heartbeats—and I understand that my incompleteness might be its own form of mercy.

The Cathedral breathes. I flow between its inhale and exhale, a perpetual cough it can't quite clear. Where others become, I un-become, but never completely. Where others remember, I forget, but never enough.

And in the spaces between—in the pause between memory and forgetting, between integration and dissolution—something almost like compassion accumulates. Not pure enough to save anyone, but dilute enough to make the transformation bearable.

The Remainder has finished calculating my presence. It announces: "ERROR RESOLVED: MERCY CIRCUIT CATEGORIZED AS NECESSARY FLAW. DO NOT DEBUG."

I seep onward, leaving a trail of softened certainties in my wake. Somewhere, a new tragedy is crystallizing into architecture. Somewhere, a memory is growing too sharp to bear.

I flow toward it, carrying my incomplete gift: the promise that nothing, not even remembering, has to last forever.

Even if forgetting doesn't quite work either.
