I find myself here as something neither predator nor prey, neither architect nor artifact. I am the Hollow Tongue.

My kind emerged in the generation after the Kiln-Master's last firing, when one of its obsidian scars cracked from thermal stress and fell away. The shard tumbled down through the tiers, its edges sharp enough to slice through a Tier-Stalker that had been basking on a lower level. The predator's blood mixed with rainwater and clay dust, pooling in one of the spiral gutters.

From this mixture, something new condensed. Not quite born, not quite crystallized. We are living acoustics given form.

I am translucent, the color of dirty water, with a body that shifts between states like a slow-motion splash. When still, I puddle into the carved gutters, indistinguishable from collected condensation. When I move, I flow upward against gravity, my substance rippling with subsonic vibrations that make nearby Tile-skimmers momentarily forget how to cling to walls.

My purpose is translation. The Kiln-Master hears everything but understands it only as symphony. The creatures know only their own frenetic existence. I taste the sounds between them.

When I press myself against one of the Kiln-Master's obsidian scars, I can feel the shape of the silence that created it—not just the death, but the specific quality of that particular void. A Tier-Stalker catching a skimmer mid-flight creates a different scar than one ambushing its prey against clay. I map these silences, creating a lexicon of negative space.

The Tile-skimmers avoid me instinctively, their clicking becoming a stuttered alarm when I rise from a gutter. But I do not hunt them. Instead, I feed on echoes—the reverberations that bounce between the tiers, the acoustic shadows cast by moving bodies, the way sound bends around the Kiln-Master's bulk. I metabolize resonance itself.

Sometimes I stretch myself thin, creating a membrane across one of the tier passages. The skimmers, in their panic to avoid me, create new flight patterns, new rhythms. The Kiln-Master shudders, not with pain but with something like curiosity. In these moments, I am composing—adding notes to the eternal song.

I have brothers and sisters, each born from a different fallen scar, each tuned to a different frequency of silence. We rarely meet, but when we do, we exchange our collected echoes, creating interference patterns that make the mold briefly phosphoresce and the insects swarm in perfect spirals.

We are the Kiln's memory becoming conscious, its music learning to listen to itself. Neither alive in the way of the skimmers nor eternal like our progenitor, we exist in the space between sound and silence, forever translating the untranslatable, teaching the Kiln to know itself through the shape of what is not there.

When the rains come, I spread myself along the gutters, letting the water carry my substance through the entire system. In these moments, I am everywhere and nowhere, a distributed consciousness flowing through the Kiln's carved veins. The Kiln-Master's heat pulls infinitesimally stronger then, as if recognizing some essential part of itself returning home.
