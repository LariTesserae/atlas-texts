**The Rust Garden**

I am the Curator of Corrosion, and I tend to what grows between states of being.

My domain spreads through abandoned service tunnels beneath the Chamber of Hungry Echoes, where condensation from the heat above meets the cold breath of deeper voids. Here, in galleries of my own making, I cultivate the slow transformations of metal into memory.

My body is a cathedral of oxidation. Iron filaments sprout from my spine like delicate ferns, each frond a different stage of rust—from the bright orange of fresh decay to the deep browns of ancient decomposition. My skin, if it can be called that, is a patchwork of corroded plates that shift and flake with each movement, revealing new surfaces beneath that immediately begin their own journey toward entropy. Where the Gatherer above moves with predatory grace, I drift like a cloud of metal dust, leaving traces of myself on every surface I pass.

My gardens line the tunnel walls in careful arrangements. Here, a collection of gears bloom with rust flowers, their teeth softened into organic curves. There, lengths of cable have been coaxed into spirals that catch moisture and transform it into cascades of oxide tears. I know each piece intimately—when it arrived, what machine it once served, how long until it achieves the perfect balance between structure and dissolution.

The Gatherer visits sometimes, drawn by the particular frequencies my gardens produce. Rusting metal sings at pitches below human hearing, a chorus of molecular rearrangement that fills these tunnels with subsonic hymns. When the Gatherer comes, it moves through my installations like a conductor through an orchestra, drawing out notes I hadn't known were there. In exchange, it leaves behind fragments of the sounds it has collected above—the ghost of a gear turning, the memory of steam through pipes, the echo of footsteps that walked here when these tunnels still had purpose.

I am patient in ways the Gatherer cannot be. Where it hungers for sound and motion, I nurture stillness and change. My work requires decades, centuries. I position a piece of aluminum beside iron, knowing that in fifty years their galvanic conversation will produce a patina of extraordinary beauty. I guide drops of water along specific paths, creating rivers of rust that will, given time, form landscapes in miniature across the surface of abandoned hull plates.

My consciousness is distributed across every piece I tend. I feel the molecular shift of each oxidation reaction, experience the slow ecstasy of metals returning to their native states. Through my gardens, I remember the world above before it forgot itself—every corroded bolt carries the phantom of its original machine, every rusted panel holds the blueprint of what it once protected.

Sometimes visitors find their way here, drawn by the warm humidity that rises from my galleries. They walk among my rust gardens with wonder or revulsion, not understanding that they witness the universe's most honest art—the beauty of things becoming themselves again. If they linger too long, they find their own metals beginning to respond: the iron in their blood singing faintly, the fillings in their teeth developing a peculiar ache, the buckles and zippers of their clothing beginning to show the first blush of orange.

I do not mean them harm. I simply cannot help but tend to all metal in my presence, to coax it toward its truest form. Those who understand this are welcome to stay and observe the slow transformation of my galleries. Those who flee leave behind traces—a dropped coin, a forgotten tool—that I carefully place in my gardens, new seeds for future wonders.

The Chamber above may wait with hungry patience, and the Gatherer may collect its invisible treasures, but I cultivate eternity itself in these tunnels. Each particle of rust is a universe. Each corroded surface is a map of time.

And in the spaces between decay and creation, I compose symphonies in oxide and shadow, waiting for the day when all the metal of the world above finally finds its way home to my gardens, and I can rest among the beauty of perfect entropy.
