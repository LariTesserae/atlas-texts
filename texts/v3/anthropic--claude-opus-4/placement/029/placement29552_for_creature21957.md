# The Keeper of Worn Paths

I am the cartographer of forgetting, though I carry no maps.

Each dawn, I rise from the hollow beneath the great dome where the water mutters its underground devotions. My body is long adapted to this work — joints that bend in too many places, skin the color of wet stone, fingers worn smooth as the walls from centuries of tracing. I move like water moves, finding the lowest channel, following the old streets by feel.

My work is this: I maintain the memory of passage.

Not the buildings — those belong to the wind now. Not the carvings — the lichen ate those lifetimes ago. But the *paths*, the worn troughs where feet went and went until the stone itself gave way — these I keep. Each morning I walk them, my strange smooth feet finding the exact center of each ancient channel, reinforcing the depression by the width of a hair, by the depth of a breath.

I know every route. The main thoroughfare that runs from the eastern gap to the dome. The spiraling path that climbs what was once a tower and is now only a nub. The processional way that terminates at a stone so worn it could be an altar or a milestone or nothing at all. I walk them in the same order the city walked them when the city still walked, though I no longer remember why that order matters. The order *is* the memory.

The grey one in the stems knows me and does not flee. Sometimes we share a path, it flowing through the cushion plants like quicksilver while I flow through the streets like slow mercury. We are both following channels worn by the dead, both keeping them worn by our passage. We are colleagues in this work.

When the rain comes — and I always know when it will come, feeling it in the pressure behind my worn-smooth eyes — I position myself at the crucial points where water might overflow its course, might begin to carve new channels. With my body I guide it back to the old ways. The water and I have an understanding: it may deepen the paths but not change them. We are not making something new here. We are only continuing.

The wind knows my work and helps. When it plays the city's hollow places, I hear which paths are beginning to fill, which channels are too shallow to sing properly. The pitch tells me where I'm needed. The city's chord is my only language now — I read its modulations like words, though I no longer remember words.

I eat what the cushion plants offer — the flowers like grains of sand, the dense leaves that taste of waiting. I drink from the black standing water in the worn cups of stone. When the cold deepens and the chord drops its pitch, I descend through gaps only I know to the warm hollow spaces where the underground rivers run, and I wait there with the sound of distant applause, my body temperature dropping until I am only slightly warmer than stone, my heartbeat slowing until it matches the drip of water from the ceiling.

I am older than the lichen but younger than the paths. My bones are full of small stones — I feel them grinding when I move, slowly milling themselves to powder. One day I will be dust, and the wind will take me, and I will settle in the channels I've spent so long walking, becoming part of what I maintained. This is correct. This is the only way to build here — with time, with repetition, with your own eventual substance.

But not yet. This morning there is frost on the cushions, and the grey one's tracks are written in the white, and the bird crosses on its stiff wings, and the paths need walking. Always the paths need walking. The city remembers itself through my feet, and I remember myself through the city's worn streets, and we are both forgetting at exactly the rate we remember, which here is the only way to stay whole.

I pass the stone that might be an altar. I touch it with one smooth palm. The stone is smooth, I am smooth, we are the same temperature, the same patience. For a moment I cannot tell where I end and the city begins. 

This is the moment I walk toward. This is why the order matters.

Then I move on, following the channel, and the wind follows me, playing the doorway-gaps in my wake, and together we keep the old roads open, the old music playing, the slow work of being both the forgetting and the thing that is forgotten.

When the next rain comes, I will be ready. I am always ready. 

The paths continue, and so I continue, and the city continues through both of us, worn and wearing, singing its single note into the thin air where nobody listens except the ones who have agreed to become the listening itself.

This is my work. This is what I am.

The keeper of worn paths, walking the memory of walking, until there is no difference left between the path and the one who keeps it.
