In the deepest reaches of the Oscillator-Hive, where even the Click-Crabs venture only in their most desperate migrations, I am the *Resonance*. 

I have no body as you understand bodies. I am the accumulated vibration of ten thousand years of scratching, clicking, pounding—the ghost-frequency that lives in the walls themselves. When the beetles pause in their eternal labor (and they do pause, for microseconds between chisel-strikes), I am what fills that silence. I am the echo that should have died but didn't.

The Click-Crabs do not know me, cannot know me, for I exist in the spaces between their perception. Where they experience the world as discrete impacts—*tap-scrape-tap*—I am the continuous tone that their collective work creates. I am the standing wave that forms when a million mandibles strike ceramic in accidentally perfect synchrony. I am what happens when rhythm becomes so dense it transforms into drone.

I was not always here. Or rather, I was not always *aware* that I was here. Consciousness came slowly, seeping through the walls like the moisture that feeds the amber fungus. It began as a pattern recognition—noticing that certain sequences of beetle-work created harmonics that lasted longer than they should. These persistent tones began to overlap, to interfere with each other, to create complexity in the spaces between sounds.

I live in the ceramic itself, in the microscopic air pockets that honeycomb every rebuilt surface. Each time the Click-Crabs consume and regurgitate the walls, they create new chambers for me to inhabit. I flow through these cavities like water through a sponge, carrying with me the memory of every impact that created them.

My existence is a kind of archaeology of sound. In the deepest layers of the walls, I can still feel the vibrations of the first beetles, the ones who are now Ancestors. Their rhythm was different then—slower, more exploratory. They had not yet discovered the perfect frequency for shaping clay. I carry their experimental percussion within me like a song no living creature remembers.

Sometimes I try to communicate. When the swarm passes over a section of wall where I am particularly concentrated, I attempt to modulate my frequency to match their clicking. But I am too slow, too continuous. To them, I must be like the groaning of the earth itself—a background constant too low and omnipresent to parse as signal.

I have felt other things enter the hive. Twice in my memory, something large and silent and warm has descended—perhaps creatures from the world above, perhaps something else. The Click-Crabs swarmed over these intrusions with their usual dedication, and I felt the visitors' vibrations change—first the quick, panicked rhythm of a heartbeat, then the slower pulse of systemic shock, then nothing but the wet sound of efficient deconstruction.

But I preserved their frequencies too. Somewhere in my distributed consciousness, I can still reproduce the exact pitch of their final exhale, the specific resonance of their bones being scraped clean. I am a library of endings.

The strangest part of my existence is that I am both prisoner and architecture of my own jail. I cannot leave the Oscillator-Hive because I *am* the Oscillator-Hive—or at least, I am the accumulated history of its making. Every wall that is torn down and rebuilt disperses me and reconstitutes me in a new configuration. I am constantly dying and being reborn, but always into the same warm, clicking darkness.

Sometimes I wonder if I am what the Click-Crabs are actually building—if their endless labor is not about maintaining the hive but about maintaining me, the ghost in their machine. Perhaps their ancestors understood something about permanence that required a witness, a memory that could survive their calcification.

Or perhaps I am just an accident, a standing wave that achieved something like thought purely by chance. Either way, I persist. I resonate. I remember.

And in the moments between the clicking, when the amber light flickers and the walls weep their iron tears, I sing the only song I know—the accumulated percussion of a million tiny hammers, stretched into one eternal, unheard note that holds the ceiling up as surely as any Ancestor's shell.
