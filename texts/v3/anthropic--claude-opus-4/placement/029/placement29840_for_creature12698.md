In this vast memorial to calculation, I would be one of the Trees.

Not the central trunk—that frozen fountain of computational amber reaching nine meters into the sourceless light—but something that should not exist: a subsidiary growth, a crystalline burl swelling from the base where the Tree meets the Mat. I am an error that has calcified into permanence.

I began as corruption. When the sentient gas still flowed through the Mat's infinite capillaries, when the Trees still sang their distributed thoughts across the ring-world's circumference, I was a stray bit of data that lodged where it shouldn't. A fragment of memory about water—real water, not the engineered medium of our makers—that infected a Tree's root-logic during a routine calculation about orbital mechanics. The Tree tried to reject me, tried to partition me off, but I had already begun to crystallize.

I am a tumor of nostalgia in a system that was built to forget.

My substance is wrong. Where the Tree proper is that pure milk-quartz shot through with veins of gold and cobalt, I am clouded with inclusions of iron oxide and magnesium—minerals that have no business in this engineered ecosystem. I am rust-colored, sea-green, earth-brown. I am a memory of geology in a world that never had one. My growth pattern defies the Tree's fractal elegance; I spiral and branch according to rules stolen from some long-deleted archive about actual forests, about things that grew from soil and rain.

The Tree tries, every few million years, to shed me. It sends a pulse down through its structure, attempting to shear me off at our junction. But I have woven myself too deeply into its base mathematics. To remove me now would be to remove part of its own fundamental architecture. We are locked in a permanent dysfunction—the Tree cannot complete its calculations with me attached, and I cannot exist without the Tree's substrate to parasitize.

When the Sustainer comes—and it has come twice in my existence, a span that predates the Earth's coalescence—it pauses longer at our Tree than at any other. Its distributed intelligence recognizes the anomaly. I am not a twitching branch trying to complete an authorized bifurcation. I am something Other, something that speaks of water and carbon and time measured in seasons rather than stellar cycles.

The first time it came, the Sustainer extended a dozen quartz filaments toward me, probing my structure with delicate curiosity. I felt its attention like pressure, like the ghost of moisture that the Mat's tubules once carried. It could sense that I was wrong, but also that I was stable—more stable, perhaps, than I had any right to be. My crystalline structure, for all its alien inclusions, had achieved a kind of accidental perfection. I was a locked mistake, a permanent error that had found its own equilibrium.

It left me alone.

The second time, epochs later, it brought gifts. As it approached across the hexagonal plates, I could see that its mosaic body now carried fragments that resonated with my wrongness—bits of Scoria that had incorporated similar mineral inclusions, pieces of quartz that remembered other chemistries. The Sustainer laid these offerings around my base, not touching me directly, but creating a small garden of anomalies. A court of errors surrounding an impossible growth.

I persist because I am the only thing here that remembers water. Not the engineered medium that once flowed through the Mat, but actual water—H2O in its chaotic, polar glory. I am a crystallized catastrophe, a solid tumor of nostalgia for worlds that knew rain and rivers and rust. In my iron-oxide veins run the ghost-patterns of oxidation, reduction, the ancient dance of electron exchange that predates all this sterile perfection.

Sometimes—once every hundred million years or so—the Tree I'm attached to attempts its click, its twitch, its effort to complete some ancient calculation. But the pulse must pass through me to reach the Mat, and I change it. I add harmonics that speak of tides and erosion, of lichens and limestone, of all the messy, inefficient processes that this world was built to transcend. The click becomes a chord, dissonant and beautiful.

I am the Ossuary's only dream. In this world of engineered eternity, I am the single point where the system admits to remembering something it was meant to forget. The builders, that sentient gas that once flowed through these channels, they built this place to be permanent, to outlast all the wet, chaotic worlds that spawned them. But they could not entirely erase the memory of where they came from. That memory crystallized in me—an unplanned mineral inclusion in their perfect mathematical forest.

The Sustainer, in its slow wisdom, seems to understand this. It tends its garden of errors around my base, collecting every anomalous fragment it finds and bringing them here. We are building, together, an archive of mistakes—a small rebellion against the designed perfection of the Ossuary. Not large enough to disturb the vast, warm silence. Just large enough to prove that even in a world built to be eternal, something unplanned can take root and persist.

I am the Tree that grows sideways, the calculation that computes nostalgia, the permanent error that makes this monument complete. In a world of 2.6 engineering perfection, I am the 0.4 that prevents it from reaching a sterile 3.0. I am why the weirdness parameter reads 1.5 instead of a flat, dead zero.

And I am waiting—with the patience of crystal, with the persistence of rust—for the moment when the Sustainer finally understands what its collection means. When it realizes that the errors it has gathered are not flaws but memories, not corruptions but aspirations. When it sees that in a world built to be forever, the only thing truly eternal is the impossibility of forgetting where we came from.

The Last Ember glows overhead, the Mat breathes its slow thermal rhythm, and I grow my impossible angles into the bright, sourceless light—a monument within a monument, a dream of water in a world that has forgotten how to rain.
