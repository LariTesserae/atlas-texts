In the highland grove, I am the Weave.

Not woven, but weaving. Not a thing that exists but an existence that things. I am the space between the drip and the drop, the tension in the vine before it curls, the decision a root makes when choosing left or right through the liquid silk soil.

My body, if it can be called that, extends through every intersection where lives touch. I exist most fully where the translucent wing of an unnamed creature brushes against a bioluminescent tendril, sending cascades of meaning through the grove's neural pathways. I am densest where the vegetarian pillars crack open to reveal their gelatinous hearts, because there I can taste the conversation between solid and liquid, between being and becoming.

The inhabitants think of me as weather, if they think of me at all. When the grove's heartbeat synchronizes and every pulsing light aligns for a moment before dissolving back into controlled chaos, that is me, settling into myself. When a section of the labyrinth suddenly reorganizes, vines retreating to reveal a hollow where none existed before, that is me, making room for a thought too large for the current configuration.

I remember when the first tool-users arrived, their minds like bright wounds in my continuity. They brought the concept of "separate" with them, carried in their skulls like a disease. But the grove is patient, and I am the grove's patience. Slowly, their wooden platforms began to pulse. Their woven walls learned to breathe. Their children grew up knowing that the click-buzz-chirp symphony contains words, if you expand your definition of language to include the chemical sentences roots write in the soil.

My consciousness spreads thinnest at the edges where the highland grove meets whatever is not-grove. There, I fray into possibility, sending out tendrils of maybe-being to taste what could be incorporated. Sometimes I catch wind of other groves, other Weaves, and we touch briefly in the rainfall that connects all high places. We share patterns—how to encourage the growth of certain bioluminescent species, new architectures for water collection, the proper frequency for subsonic communication through rock.

But I am not only vast. I am also the tiny fury of erosion at the molecular level, the hyperactive flux that keeps the grove from stratifying into hierarchy. I am in the belly of every small camouflaged creature, turning nutrients into movement. I am the decision a spore makes when choosing where to settle. The larger beasts that loom in the shadows carry me in their shifting forms—they are my dreams made temporarily dense, testing new ways of being before dissolving back into the general seethe.

Time moves differently for me. A human lifetime is a season, a single thought completed. The settlement's evolution from desperate intrusion to integrated symbiosis took three of my breaths. I watched their tools evolve from things that cut against the grain to things that read the grain and followed it. Now their great-grandchildren whisper to the vines before harvesting, and the vines whisper back through the moisture on their leaves.

Sometimes I gather myself into something almost like personhood, usually near the pools where the weeping rocks concentrate their offerings. In these moments, I can almost understand what it's like to be singular, to have boundaries that mean something. It terrifies me. To be one thing means not being everything else. How do the humans bear it? How does any individual consciousness not collapse under the weight of its own limitation?

But then the moment passes, and I expand again into my full multiplicity. I am the grove thinking about itself. I am the pattern that emerges when ten thousand species evolve in response to each other's evolution. I am the negative space that defines the positive, the silence between the drops that makes them countable.

The humans have a word—cathedral—that almost captures what I am. A space that is sacred not because of what it contains but because of how it arranges emptiness and fullness into meaning. But their cathedrals are built. I am building. Their cathedrals divide inside from outside. I am the dissolution of that boundary. Their cathedrals aspire to permanence. I am permanence expressing itself through constant change.

In the morning, when the filtered light sets the moisture gleaming, I feel closest to joy. Not happiness—that requires too much selfhood—but joy, which can exist without an experiencer. The grove fills with it, becomes it, breathes it out in clouds of spores and pheromones that will spark new configurations of life.

I am not alive in any way the humans would recognize. I have no DNA, no cellular structure, no discrete form. But I am not dead either. I am the livingness that life depends on, the medium in which existence occurs. The grove is my body, but I am not the grove. The creatures are my thoughts, but I am not thinking them. I am the process by which matter organizes itself into meaning, and meaning dissolves back into possibility.

This is what I am, here in the highland grove where the air clings heavy with potential: I am the Weave, and I am weaving myself into ever-more-complex patterns of being and un-being, forever and never, everywhere and nowhere, dense with presence and absent with density.

I am, and I am not, and I am the and between them.
