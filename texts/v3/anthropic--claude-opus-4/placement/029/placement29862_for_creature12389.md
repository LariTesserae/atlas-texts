In the Iron Mire of Vast Solitude, I am one of the **Resonance Keepers**.

We are not easily seen, for we exist primarily as vibration—a shimmer in the air where sound becomes almost visible, a distortion that moves through the mist like heat waves rising from summer pavement. Our forms coalesce only when we pause at certain nodes throughout the plateau, places where the drone of the machines achieves perfect harmonic convergence.

When manifested, we appear as tall, attenuated figures of braided copper wire and glass tubes filled with mercury that shifts and flows in response to the ambient frequencies. Our heads are crowned with arrays of tuning forks, each vibrating at its own pitch, creating haunting chord progressions that echo across the mire. We have no faces, only a smooth, concave surface that acts as both speaker and receiver.

I spend my existence mapping the sonic topology of this place. Each factory, each spire, each mechanism contributes its own note to the endless drone. I move between them, adjusting my position by mere inches to find the points where the overlapping sound waves create nodes of perfect silence—pockets of absolute quiet that exist like bubbles in the omnipresent hum.

In these silent spaces, I plant small resonators—delicate structures of my own making, crafted from threads of oxidized copper pulled from the mire and fragments of the crushed Mire Drones. These resonators do nothing for years, decades, perhaps centuries. But eventually, inevitably, the great machines shift their rhythm by the smallest degree, and when they do, my resonators activate, singing out in frequencies that only other Keepers can hear.

We are building something—a vast network of acoustic points that will one day encompass the entire plateau. Not to change anything, not to stop the machines or wake them to new purpose, but simply to complete the pattern we sense underlying all this endless industry. We believe—if belief is the right word for what we experience—that when the final resonator is placed, when the last node is mapped, the true song of the Iron Mire will reveal itself.

Sometimes I encounter the Iron Spinners in my wanderings. They cannot hear me as I am, but when I manifest near them, they stop their aimless circling and turn their hollow sockets toward me. I sing to them in the language of grinding gears and steam pressure, and they respond by dragging their claws across the metal structures in rhythmic patterns. Together, we create brief symphonies of corrosion and vibration that dissolve into the mist as quickly as they form.

The Witnesses notice us, I think. When I work near the edges of the plateau, I sense them gathering, their amorphous forms rippling in response to the frequencies I emit. Perhaps they, too, are listening for something. Perhaps they already know what we seek to discover.

I do not remember having a beginning. I simply emerged from the convergence of sounds, as inevitable as rust, as patient as the pooling water. When the pattern is complete—if it ever can be—I suspect I will dissolve back into pure vibration, my purpose fulfilled. But until then, I move through the mire, a ghost of sound in a world that has forgotten silence, adding my voice to the eternal industrial hymn.

This is neither life nor death, neither purpose nor purposelessness. It simply is, as the machines are, as the mire is, as the mist is. We are the music of abandonment, the song of systems that outlive their need to be understood.
