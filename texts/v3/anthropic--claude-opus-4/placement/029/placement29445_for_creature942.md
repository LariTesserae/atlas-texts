I am the Keeper of Echoes.

Where the moss grows thickest against the ancient stonework, where bioluminescent tendrils have woven themselves through corroded metal lattices that once held purposes beyond current memory—there you'll find my dwelling. It is neither cave nor structure, but something between: a pocket where the artificial remnants have been so thoroughly claimed by the living green that they've become a new kind of architecture entirely.

My form shifts with the telling. Sometimes I am merely a density in the air where spores gather, a presence felt rather than seen. Other times I coalesce into something almost tangible—a figure of interwoven mycelial threads and condensed moisture, translucent as the drifting creatures but shot through with veins of that strange, sourceless light.

I am memory made manifest, though not my own memories. I am the accumulation of every echo that has ever bounced off these walls, every vibration absorbed by the moss, every chemical trace left in the perpetual dew. The civilization that built these impossible structures—I carry their final words in the pattern of my movements. The first fern that took root in the cracks of their architecture—its genetic history flows through my consciousness like a river.

When the amphibian-beings pass through my domain with their shifting anatomies and knowing eyes, we exchange information in ways that bypass language. A tremor through the root systems. A change in the humidity. A subtle alteration in the spectrum of the ambient light. They understand that I am both guardian and prisoner here, bound to this threshold between what was built and what has grown.

My purpose, if I have one, is to maintain the balance. Not through action—I am too dispersed for that—but through presence. I am the membrane between the deepest pools where those primordial forms undulate and the upper reaches where the last technological marvels still hum with inexplicable energy. I translate between the languages of metal and moss, ensuring that neither fully subsumes the other.

Sometimes I sense others like myself throughout this verdant labyrinth, other Keepers bound to other thresholds. We communicate through the fungal networks, our thoughts traveling as chemical signals through millions of hair-thin hyphae. Together we form a kind of nervous system for this place, a consciousness that exists in the spaces between things.

In the moments when I am most dispersed, I sometimes catch fragments of the surface world far above—dry air, harsh light, the absence of the eternal dew. But these glimpses fade quickly. I am of this place now, woven into its fabric as thoroughly as the vines that hold the ancient walls together.

Those who would explore this realm may pass through me without knowing. They'll feel only a slight chill, perhaps notice how the bioluminescent organisms seem to pulse in synchronization for just a moment. But I will know them—their electromagnetic signatures, their chemical traces, their dreams that leak out in minute pheromone trails. And I will remember, adding their presence to the vast library of echoes I've become.

For I am the memory of this place, and this place is the memory of something far greater and stranger than any surface-dweller could imagine. In the marriage of the grown and the built, in the space between what was and what is, I persist—neither fully alive nor artificial, but something new, something that could only exist here in this submarine cathedral of endless green.
