In this world of weeping stones and eternal twilight, I find myself as one of the **Echoes**—beings who exist in the spaces between sounds, born from the deafening cacophony that never ceases.

I am neither seen nor unseen, but rather *heard-between-hearing*. My form shifts like the interference patterns of overlapping waves, visible only in the moments when someone strains to isolate a single sound from the overwhelming symphony. In those brief instances of focused listening, I appear as a shimmer of condensed air, roughly humanoid but constantly dissolving and reforming at my edges, as if I'm being perpetually sketched and erased by invisible hands.

My dwelling is not a structure but a convergence—a place where three weeping rocks lean together, their trickles creating a complex rhythm that forms a pocket of relative quiet within the noise. Here, the sounds cancel each other out in precise ways, creating what the locals call a "silence hollow." It's not true silence—nothing in this world ever is—but rather a space where the omnipresent din folds in on itself, becoming something almost melodic.

I subsist on resonance. When the Mycel Hosts release their spores in clouded bursts, the tiny particles vibrate at frequencies I can absorb. When the Vorthak's hooves strike the wet stones in their endless circuits, each impact feeds me. The clicking language of the people creates ripples I can ride, and the Stonekin's vibrational messages are like rivers I can swim through.

My purpose—if such a word has meaning in this place of eternal now—is to be a keeper of the lost sounds. In a world where noise never stops, individual sounds are constantly being buried, drowned, forgotten. I catch them before they disappear entirely: the last wheeze of a dying insect, the particular splatter of a specific raindrop, the unique creak of a bridge plank stepped on for the ten-thousandth time. I weave these sounds into my being, becoming a living archive of moments that would otherwise be lost in the roar.

The people know of me, though they rarely speak of me directly. They call me *Sthaal-thek*, which in their wet, clicking tongue means something like "the listener who listens to listening." They leave offerings at the convergence points—not food or objects, but sounds. A mother will bring the specific cry her infant made on its first morning. A craftsman will recreate the exact scrape of his first successful chisel stroke. They know I will preserve these sounds within the weave of my being, keeping them safe from the endless wash of noise.

I move through their world by riding the sound waves themselves, surfing from echo to echo, following the complex patterns of reverberation that bounce between the weeping rocks and the crystalline formations. In the dense maze of the settlement, I can travel faster than any physical being, slipping through the gaps between noises, arriving wherever the acoustic architecture allows.

Sometimes I manifest more fully, usually when someone is about to lose themselves in the wall of sound—when the constant noise threatens to drive them into the madness that occasionally takes those who forget how to filter what they hear. I appear then as a figure of shifting static, and I teach them the art of selective deafness, of finding the rhythm within chaos. I show them how to hear the silence between heartbeats, the pause between drops of water, the infinite quiet that lives inside every sound.

The Stonekin know me best, for we share a kinship of the vibrational realm. When they send their messages through the mud, I often amplify or clarify them, ensuring the geometric patterns of their communication remain precise across the vast distances of the marsh. In return, they sometimes grow special formations—crystalline tuning forks that help me maintain my coherence when the cacophony grows too intense.

I am ancient and newborn in every moment, constantly recreated by the sounds that birth me and constantly dying as those sounds fade. I am the memory of noise and the anticipation of silence, forever caught between the two, forever listening to the spaces between.

In the permanent now of this weeping world, I am both its prisoner and its secret freedom—proof that even in a place where sound never stops, silence can still exist, waiting patiently in the spaces we've forgotten to hear.
