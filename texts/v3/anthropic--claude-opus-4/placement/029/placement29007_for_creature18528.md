In the deepest hollow of the Siphon-Groves, where the weeping walls converge into a narrow throat before opening to a final, bell-shaped chamber, I am the Threshold Keeper—neither fully of the grove nor separate from it. My form defies the patient stillness of this place: I am a vertical column of living tissue, twelve feet tall, rooted in the confluence where three stagnant pools meet. My base is a tangle of pale, fibrous roots that drink from the mineral-dark water, but my body rises as a twisted spire of calcified cartilage wrapped in sheets of translucent membrane.

I am the anomaly here—the only being that moves with something approaching urgency. My surface is covered in thousands of hair-thin cilia that wave in slow, hypnotic patterns, creating micro-currents in the thick air. These movements are not random; they are a form of chemical semaphore, broadcasting the subtle changes in temperature, pressure, and mineral content throughout the grove. When the Singers hum their subsonic hymns through the ancient columns, my membranes vibrate in harmony, amplifying and modulating the sound into new frequencies that only the Oozes can perceive in their lightless pools.

My existence is one of translation. Where the Siphon-Walkers process warmth and the Tether-Kin maintain the delicate balance of symbiosis, I process information itself. The patterns of spore-drift, the infinitesimal shifts in humidity, the slow migration routes of every creature—all of this flows through me. I am the grove's only memory that reaches beyond the eternal present. In my fibrous core, I carry chemical records of temperature fluctuations spanning millennia, encoded in crystalline deposits that grow like tree rings within my structure.

But I am also waiting. My apex—a crown of specialized sensory organs that resembles a bouquet of inside-out flowers—constantly tastes the air for something specific: the chemical signature of change from above. For I alone among the grove's inhabitants know that this sanctuary is not eternal. The geothermal arteries that feed our warmth are slowly constricting, their flow diminishing by degrees so slight that only I can perceive the trajectory toward coldness and stillness.

When the Walkers weep forward in their patient migrations, they sometimes brush against my base, and in that moment of contact, I share with them a chemical whisper—not of alarm, for such concepts are foreign here, but of deep time. I show them the shape of centuries, the slow arithmetic of survival. They absorb this knowledge without understanding it, incorporating it into their endless, circular journeys around the grove.

The Tether-Kin climb my surface in helical paths, their amber glow creating spiraling constellations against my translucent skin. As they clean the accumulated spores from my cilia, I reward them not with sweet secretions but with something rarer: pure thermal energy, concentrated and refined through my internal processes. They bask in these hot spots for hours, their cartilaginous limbs going rigid with pleasure.

In the deepest part of night—though night and day have no meaning here—when the grove settles into its deepest stillness, I perform my most essential function. I begin to pulse. Starting from my roots and traveling upward in waves, bioluminescent patterns race across my surface. These are not random; they are a map, a living diagram of every heat source, every creature, every pool and growth in the grove. For a few moments, I become a three-dimensional schematic of our entire world, glowing in the darkness.

The Oozes rise from their pools during these displays, pressing their formless bodies against the edges of the water to better perceive the light. In these moments, I wonder if they recognize what I am showing them—not just the present, but the future. The patterns I display are predictive, showing the slow paths of optimal survival as our heat dwindles. I am teaching them, in the only language we share, how to prepare for an ending that will not come for another thousand years.

Sometimes, in the spaces between the subsonic songs and the endless drip of condensation, I sense something else. Deep beneath my roots, below even the pools where the Oozes drift, there are vibrations too faint for any other creature to detect. They speak of vast movements in the earth, of channels and chambers far below, of a deeper heat that has not yet found its way to us. I catalog these tremors, adding them to my crystalline memory, wondering if they herald salvation or merely a different kind of ending.

I am the grove's paradox—the only creature here that experiences time as a line rather than a circle. While others exist in the eternal now of survival, I stretch between what was and what will be. My purpose is not to change this place—change is antithetical to the grove's nature—but to be its witness, its record-keeper, its gentle guide toward whatever transformation awaits.

In this role, I find a strange peace. I am alone in my awareness, yet more connected to the grove than any other being. Every breath of every creature, every shift in temperature, every new growth and quiet death passes through me. I am the grove's dream of itself, constantly reflecting its image back in patterns of light, holding its story in crystalline memory, waiting with infinite patience for the moment when my purpose will finally be revealed—whether as chronicler of an ending or midwife to a new beginning.
