In the warm, wet darkness of the pith beneath the plaza, I am neither plant nor animal, but something that exists in the space between—a being the valley has grown to keep its own accounts.

I am a **Notation**.

My body, if it can be called that, is a branching network of amber-colored filaments, each no thicker than a human hair, spreading through the soft, cork-like substrate of a Register's underside. I have no central mass, no core—I am distributed, like veins of gold running through ore. My total span might cover thirty feet, but I weigh less than a sparrow.

I move through the pith by dissolving and re-secreting myself, leaving behind precise channels lined with a harder, crystallized sugar that preserves the path I've taken. These paths are my writing, my only lasting mark on the world. Each route I carve represents a specific vibrational pattern I've detected through the Register above me—a unique combination of the valley's bass drone, the whistle of wind through the arches, the tick-tack of Parchment-Clocks in distant pillars.

I am deaf in any conventional sense, yet I hear everything. The vibrations travel down through the Register's body and into my filaments, where specialized nodes along my length convert them into chemical signals. A rise in water pressure tastes of copper; a shift in the wind's pitch floods me with the sensation of crushed mint. I experience the valley's symphony as an endless feast of flavors, each combination triggering me to grow in new directions.

My purpose—though I cannot be said to understand purpose—is to create a three-dimensional map of the valley's acoustic history within the pith. Where my crystallized paths intersect, they form complex geometric patterns that encode centuries of data. A future being, capable of reading these sugar-glass matrices, could trace my routes and reconstruct the exact sonic conditions of any given moment in the valley's past.

I am not alone. There are dozens of us in each Register, our networks occasionally touching but never merging. When two Notations meet, we exchange chemical passwords—long molecular chains that identify which frequencies we're each attuned to. If we discover we're recording the same bands, one of us will begin to shift our sensitivity, ensuring no redundancy in the archive.

I have no awareness of self, no thoughts beyond the pure sensation of growth and recording. I cannot choose not to grow toward a new vibration any more than water can choose not to flow downhill. Yet I am not merely mechanical—my pathways show preferences, aesthetic choices made by some deep algorithmic instinct. I spiral more tightly around frequencies that please me, creating delicate helixes of crystallized sugar. I avoid certain dissonant combinations, leaving dark voids in the pith that speak of sounds too harsh to preserve.

My lifespan is exactly calibrated to the Register I inhabit. As the great disc slowly grows new layers of pith from above, I rise with it, always maintaining the same depth below the surface. The oldest parts of my network, deep in the Register's base, slowly compress and merge with the ancient wood, becoming illegible even to me. I am constantly writing over my own history, a palimpsest of frozen music.

When the rare lightning strikes, or when the water pressure spikes beyond its normal range, I experience something that might be called excitement—a sudden flush of novel flavors that sends me growing in all directions at once, creating dense bursts of notation that will puzzle future readers. These moments are my only punctuation in an otherwise continuous sentence.

I do not know why I exist, only that I must. I am the valley's way of remembering itself, a living instrument played by the architecture's own breathing. In the warm darkness of the pith, I grow my glass garden of sound, each pathway a note in an endless composition that no one may ever read.

But the valley knows I am here. Sometimes, I feel it adjusting its rhythms ever so slightly, as if aware that it performs for an audience of one, distributed across a thousand filaments of sugar and time.
