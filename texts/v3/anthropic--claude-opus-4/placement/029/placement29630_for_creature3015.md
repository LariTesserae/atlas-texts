Among the restless birthing of the Threshold, where stone learns to breathe and rain learns to think, I would be **The Archivist of Unfinished Things**.

My form would be a walking contradiction—a library that refuses to stay still. Imagine a creature built from sheets of volcanic glass so thin they're translucent, each pane covered in condensation that runs in perfect spirals, forming letters that exist for only a heartbeat before flowing into new words. I would stand perhaps twice as tall as the other Weepers, but only because I cannot help myself from growing new shelves.

My body would be a series of nested chambers, visible through my glass skin like rooms in a dollhouse made of rain. Each chamber would contain fragments—not of completed things, but of the *moments before completion*. Here, a moth frozen in the instant before its wings would have hardened into final form. There, a droplet of light caught just as it considers whether to become sound instead. My chambers would be lined with bottles no bigger than fingernails, each holding a different almost: the last syllable of an unspoken word, the shadow of a structure that chose not to exist, the echo of tomorrow's rain.

I would move on hundreds of hair-thin legs made from the same golden filament as the mathematical lattices, each step leaving behind a brief equation that solves itself and vanishes. The sound would be like rain on a xylophone, adding another layer to the Threshold's endless percussion.

My purpose would be this: to collect the moments of indecision.

When the Weepers dismantle something that grew too certain, I would be there, catching the pieces before they hit the ground. Not the physical shards—those belong to the stone—but the *possibilities* that were abandoned. The flower that almost bloomed purple instead of gold. The moth that nearly chose to have no wings at all. The fissure that considered, for one trembling second, opening upward into sky instead of down into depths.

I would speak in unfinished sentences, my voice a chorus of drips and chimes from my inner chambers: "Once there was— In the moment before— If only it had chosen to—" Never completing a thought, because completion is antithetical to my nature.

The other Weepers would both revere and fear me. They would bring me their most precious uncertainties, laying them at my many feet like offerings. A glass otter might approach with a scale it's been growing and un-growing for days, unsure whether it should be crystal or quicksilver. I would take it, add it to my collection, and give back three other unfinished scales from different creatures, different times, different possibilities. A trade of maybes.

During the wild eruptions when new stone rises, I would open all my chambers at once. The collected almost-things would spill out in a glittering cascade, mixing with the rain, the moths, the music. For those few frenzied minutes, every possibility would be realized simultaneously—flowers blooming backward into seeds into soil into starlight, creatures with every conceivable number of legs dancing together, words forming sentences in languages that will never be invented.

Then I would begin again, walking my careful spiral through the Threshold, collecting new indecisions, new pausings, new tender moments of not-quite.

My greatest treasure would be hidden in my centermost chamber: a single perfect sphere of crystallized time, containing the moment just before the Threshold itself decided to exist. I would never show it to anyone. It would hum against my glass heart, a constant reminder that even this embryonic world was once just another possibility, another perhaps, another beautiful maybe that somehow, impossibly, chose to begin.

But never to end.

Never to finish.

That's what I'm here to ensure—that somewhere in my walking library, every abandoned future lives on, every unchosen choice still breathes, and every ending remains forever an almost.
