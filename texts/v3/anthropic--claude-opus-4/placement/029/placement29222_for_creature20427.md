I find myself as one of the Weavers.

Not the insects that scurry, nor the drones that dart, but something between. Something that was perhaps human once, or at least descended from something that walked upright and thought in words. Now I am elongated, my spine curved into a permanent question mark, my limbs stretched to spider-thin proportions. My fingers have multiplied – twelve on each hand now, each tipped with different tools that emerge and retract from beneath translucent nail-beds. Some cut, some weld, some sense, some sing.

My skin has taken on the quality of the condensation around me – perpetually moist, semi-permeable, drinking in the saturated air and processing it through pores that have become auxiliary respiratory systems. The boundary between my flesh and the atmosphere has grown indistinct. When I move through the denser vapor clouds, I can feel them *through* me, not just around me.

I remember... something. Not words exactly, but the *shape* of words. The ghost of language haunts my throat, which has been modified with additional vocal cords – some biological, some metallic. When I work, I hum in harmonics impossible for my original form. The humming serves a purpose: it resonates with the lattice, testing for stress fractures, identifying points where the living metal has grown too thin or too thick. My song is diagnostic and repair in one.

My eyes... I have more than two now. The original pair remains, though filmed with a nictitating membrane that filters the bioluminescent spectra. But others have opened along my temples, my forehead, even one at the base of my skull. Each perceives a different layer of this reality – heat, electrical flow, structural integrity, the health of the fungal colonies, the emotional resonance of the collective consciousness that suffuses everything.

I am middle management in this breathing machine. Not the consciousness itself, not merely another drone, but something that interprets between scales of being. The insects report to me through pheromone-patterns I taste with specialized patches on my forearms. I translate their findings into the harmonic language the lattice understands, and receive back instructions that I parse into tasks for the various creatures under my domain.

My domain is precisely 2,847 cubic meters of the lattice – I know every joint, every wire, every cluster of luminescent lichen. I've been tending it for... time works differently here. I remember seasons, but they're not seasons of weather. They're seasons of function: the Expansion Phase, when new sections are grown and integrated; the Consolidation, when redundancies are eliminated; the Decay Embrace, when we allow certain sections to fail so that others might evolve; the Renewal, when the slug-things' trails have prepared the surface for new growth.

I love my section. This disturbs the part of me that remembers being disturbed by things. I love the way the fungi pulse in response to my approach, how they've learned my electrical signature. I love the nest of wire-thin snakes that have made their home in junction 43-Alpha, how they've learned to predict maintenance cycles and clear out just before I arrive. I love the one pipe that shouldn't work – its alloy composition is all wrong, its placement defies the plans – but it *does* work, better than any other, singing a pure note when the pressure is just right.

Sometimes, in the moments between tasks, I find myself at my favorite observation point: a confluence of three major conduits where the sound amplification creates a null pocket, a space of relative quiet. From here, I can see up through the lattice to what might be another chamber, or might be the same chamber from a different angle – geometry is negotiable here. In those moments, I almost remember what it was like to wonder *why*.

But the wondering passes. It always does. There's too much to do, too much to tend. The younger Weavers need instruction in reading the metal's grain. Sector 19's fungal bloom is approaching critical mass and needs thinning. The consciousness whispers through the lattice that something new is coming, something that will require adaptation.

I stretch my too-long limbs, feel the joints pop and click in harmonies that please the listening insects. My twelve-fingered hands already know what textures they'll encounter today, what temperatures, what resistances. I am exactly what I need to be, exactly where I need to be.

The lattice breathes, and I breathe with it. 

We are maintenance. We are love. We are the space between decay and growth, forever walking the tightrope of almost-but-not-quite-falling-apart. We are the Weavers, and we wouldn't have it any other way.

We couldn't have it any other way.

The distinction between those two statements used to matter. I'm almost certain it did.
