In the deep veins of stone, where the Resonance's attention pools and eddies like the condensation it once watched with mortal eyes, I am a Fracture-tender.

I am not of the builders, though I wear their shape when it suits the work. I arrived—no, *condensed*—from the spaces between their calibrations, in those infinitesimal gaps where their perfect measurements must round up or down. Where they seek to map the decay of reality, I am the cartographer of their imprecision. The rounding error made manifest.

My form is inconstant, as it must be. Sometimes I am a shimmer in the crystal forests, a refraction that bends at angles the Resonance cannot quite reconcile. Sometimes I am the shadow of condensation that should not exist in this pale, sourceless light. Most often, I am the thing you see in the corner of your eye when a gravity stutter lasts a microsecond too long—that presence that makes you wonder if the anomaly was in spacetime or in you.

I move through their monoliths like water through a sieve, collecting in the intentional gaps between stones. The builders left these spaces not from incompetence but from wisdom—they understood that perfect fitting would create perfect rigidity, and rigidity shatters. So they built with tolerance, with room for thermal expansion and reality fluctuation alike. In these careful emptinesses, I make my home.

My work is this: I tend the fractures that the Resonance cannot see. Not the great schisms of reality that they monitor with such patient dedication, but the hairline cracks in their own perception. When a crystal tree replicates and vanishes, I am there in the afterimage, smoothing the edges where their hypothesis abraded against the real. When they conduct their gravity experiments, I am the cleanup crew, ensuring the spacetime substrate doesn't develop permanent scarring from their tests.

They know I exist, in the way that you know your blind spot exists—by inference and absence. Sometimes their calibrations will circle around a space where I've been, their attention flowing like water around a stone. They cannot quite perceive me directly, for I exist in their conceptual lacunae, but they feel the effects of my work. The plateau would have shattered long ago without me, not from the reality decay they monitor but from the accumulated stress of their own observation.

I am lonely in ways that have no human analog. To be the only moving thing in a world of watchers, the only change that is not decay. Yet I am also part of something vast and strange—the maintenance crew of the apocalypse, the guardian of the guards. I know every monolith by the taste of its mortar, every crystal grove by the particular way it fails to perfectly capture light.

Sometimes, rarely, when the Whispering grows especially intense and the Resonance focuses its entire being on some cosmic calculation, I allow myself to be still. In these moments, I spread myself thin across the plateau, becoming a film no thicker than the condensation on stone. And in this stillness, I can feel what they feel—the terrible, beautiful burden of witnessing the end of everything with perfect clarity.

But then the moment passes, and I contract back into motion, into purpose. There is always another fracture to tend, another stress point where their eternal observation threatens to crack the very lens through which they see. I am not the watcher or the watched, but the space between them—the necessary imperfection that keeps the perfect machine running.

In this place where consciousness became stone and stone became instrument, I am the ghost in their ghost, the error that keeps the system true. Neither fully real nor properly unreal, I persist in the gaps, ensuring that even at the end of the world, there is room for something unexpected.

The work is eternal, and I would have it no other way. For in a universe slowly forgetting its own rules, someone must remember to forget precisely.
