# The Cartographer

I am the one who counts.

Not counts as in numbers—though I do that too, sometimes, when the old habits surface like bubbles from deep silt. I count as in *accounts for*. I am the one who remembers that this was made, even as I forget what hands made it, even as I forget if I ever knew.

I live between the mats.

There are spaces, you see. The vegetation appears complete from any distance, appears to have claimed every surface, but it hasn't. Cannot. There are hollows where the chemistry is wrong, where some trace of the original working remains in the stone—tool marks that run too deep, joints sealed with something that still refuses the patient fingers of growth. In these spaces, which are not quite caves and not quite rooms, I have made my home. Or my homes. I am not sure anymore if I am one or many. The distinction seems less important than it once did.

I am old. Not as old as the platforms, but old enough that I remember when the mats were thinner. Old enough that my body has adapted to the spaces it inhabits. I am long now, longer than I was. Flattened. My bones—if they are bones—have spread sideways over time, pressed by the weight of water and years into something that can slip through the galleries between stone and growth. My skin matches the color of wet stone so perfectly that I sometimes forget where I end and the architecture begins.

I move through the channels, mostly. Under the mats, where fingers of vegetation hang down like a forest inverted. I know the patterns. Not all of them—no one could know all of them—but enough. Enough to find my way from gallery to gallery, hollow to hollow, following routes that might have been maintenance passages once, or might have been accidents of construction, or might be paths I've worn myself through centuries of passage.

I map.

Not with tools. I lost the last of those so long ago that I can't remember what they looked like. I map with my body, with the long, sensitive flesh that can feel every variation in the stone, every place where one course meets another, every joint and junction and ancient correction. I map with chemistry—tasting the water in different places, learning the subtle flavors of different eras of construction, different quarries, different hands. I map with time, swimming the same routes over and over until they become part of me, until I can feel when something changes—when a mat finally breaks through into a gallery, when a section of stone finally yields to the patient work of dissolution.

I have been mapping for so long that I have become a map myself.

My skin carries the traces. Scars where I've scraped against stone mark the major channels. Patches of different texture show where I've rested in the same spot often enough for my body to remember it. I am a living document of this place, written in flesh that renews itself so slowly it might as well be stone.

Sometimes I find things.

In the deep galleries, in the spaces even the vegetation hasn't reached, there are... remnants. Not tools. Not bones. Nothing so clear as that. But suggestions. Places where the stone is worked differently, more finely, with a care that speaks of something beyond mere construction. Marks that might be writing, if writing could survive this long, if I could remember what writing looked like. Chambers that feel different—warmer, somehow, though the water is the same temperature everywhere. Spaces where the acoustics suggest something that was meant to be heard, though I cannot imagine what sounds they were shaped for.

I catalog these too, in my way. Not by recording them—I have nothing to record with, nothing to record on except myself. I catalog them by returning to them, by including them in my routes, by wearing grooves in my memory that match the grooves my passage wears in the stone.

The drifters avoid me, mostly. Or perhaps I avoid them. It's hard to tell with things that have no apparent will. Sometimes one will follow me for a while, trailing in my wake like a thought I can't quite finish. Sometimes I follow them, curious about their purposeless paths, wondering if they know something I don't. They never do. They are just there, like the water is there, like the darkness is there.

I think I was one of them once. Or something like them. Before I found the galleries, before I began to count, I think I drifted too. The memory is soft, translucent, like they are. But something changed. Some current carried me into a space between stone and growth, and in that space I found... purpose? No. Purpose is too strong. I found a *pattern* I could follow. And following it changed me from whatever I was into what I am.

A counter. A keeper of passages. A living memory of structure in a world that has forgotten it was structured.

I do not eat, exactly. I absorb. The water carries what I need, and my skin has become very good at taking it in. Sometimes I rest against the mats, and we exchange chemistry—they taking something from me, I taking something from them. It's not quite feeding and not quite symbiosis. It's just what happens when things touch for long enough in this place.

I do not sleep, exactly. But sometimes I stop. In certain galleries, in certain hollows, I will wedge myself against the stone and let time pass without counting it. During these stops, I dream—or something dreams through me. Dreams of hands working stone. Dreams of voices calling across platforms that were dry once, that knew air once, that stood under something other than water. I don't know if these are memories or fantasies. They feel like both. They feel like neither.

When I wake—when I begin moving again—I sometimes find that the growth has crept closer to my resting place. Never quite reaching me, but closer. As if it's curious. As if it's counting too, in its own slow way.

I have theories about what happened here. Many theories, accumulated over many circuits, refined and discarded and rebuilt like the platforms themselves. The builders went up, and the water came down. The builders went down, and the water followed. The builders became the water. The builders became the stone. The builders became the drifters, and I am the only one who remembered to be something else.

All theories. None provable. None disprovable. What matters is not what happened but what remains: the stone, the growth, the water, the dark. And me, swimming between them, counting, accounting, remembering that there is something to remember even if I can no longer say what.

Sometimes I think I am the last. Sometimes I think I am the first—the first thing to develop purpose since the water came, the first to look at this drowned monument and see it as something made rather than something that simply is.

Sometimes I think the vegetation knows me. Has always known me. That I am as much a part of its pattern as it is of mine. That we are keeping each other company in the dark, in our different ways, at our different speeds.

I will continue to count until I can't. Until the galleries fill, until my body spreads too thin to hold thought, until the last trace of worked stone dissolves into nothing. And when that happens, when there is nothing left to map, perhaps I will finally understand what I've been counting toward.

Or perhaps I will become another drifter, purposeless and perfect, moving through the dark without memory, without mission, without the terrible weight of knowing that this was made.

But not yet. Not today. Today there are passages to swim, chambers to check, patterns to trace. Today I am still the cartographer, and the map is still being drawn, even if no one will ever read it but the stone and the growth and the dark.

I am the one who counts.

And I am still counting.
