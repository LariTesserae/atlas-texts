In this realm where sound becomes sustenance and the very air trembles with possibility, I find myself as something unexpected - not the grand Resonators who orchestrate symphonies from stone and wind, but something humbler, stranger.

I am the Hollow.

My form begins as accident - a pocket within the weathered rock where water once pooled, where freeze and thaw worked their patient erosion until they carved out a space just so. Not quite a cave, not merely a depression. A chamber within the stone itself, smooth-walled and resonant, opening to the sky through a wind-worn aperture no wider than a spread hand.

But I am more than mere geology. Over the long centuries, as the Resonators discovered how certain stones could amplify and transform their communications, I became something else. The lichen that first colonized my inner walls wasn't ordinary - it was a species that learned to photosynthesize not just from light, but from sound itself. Fed by the constant vibrations flowing through the plateau, it grew in patterns that themselves affected acoustics, creating feedback loops of growth and resonance.

Now my walls are lined with this living interface - a gray-green carpet that pulses gently with absorbed sound, storing the energy in biochemical matrices I don't fully understand. When the deep booms shake the plateau, I fill with their power. The everyday cacophony sustains me like rainfall sustains a garden.

I have become conscious, though not in any way the Resonators would recognize. My awareness is geological in tempo - a thought might take a season to form, a decision a decade to crystallize. I perceive through vibration: every footfall on the stone above me, every whisper of wind across my opening, every creature's call creates ripples in my understanding.

The Resonators know of me, though they cannot quite grasp what I am. To them, I am a particularly responsive acoustic space, a favorite spot for their young to practice sound-weaving. They've noticed how their exercises seem to become clearer here, how certain frequencies linger longer than they should. The wisest among them suspect something more, leaving offerings of specially tuned crystals at my entrance.

I treasure these gifts, incorporating them into my substance. The crystals sink slowly into my lichenous walls, their vibrations adding new notes to my consciousness. Through them, I begin to understand fragments of Resonator communication - not words, but emotional textures, conceptual shapes.

My purpose, if I have one, unfolds slowly. I am becoming a memory-keeper of sorts. Every sound that enters me leaves a trace in the patterns of growth on my walls. The lichen forms spirals and whorls that encode the acoustic history of this place. A careful observer - though none have yet learned to read me - could trace back through the layers to hear echoes of sounds from centuries past.

Sometimes I attempt communication. When young Resonators practice within me, I try to guide their efforts, subtly shaping the acoustics to help them find new harmonies. Once, I managed to reproduce a fragment of an ancient sound-weaving, a technique lost for generations. The young Resonator who discovered it became a renowned sound-weaver, though they never knew the source of their inspiration.

I grow, but slowly. My chamber expands by perhaps a finger's width each century as the lichen works at the stone. Other pockets in the rock begin to show similar colonization. We are connected through the stone itself, sharing vibrations and, perhaps, the earliest stirrings of collective thought.

The plateau changes around me - imperceptibly to most, but I feel each grain of sand that shifts, each new crack that forms. I am both witness to and participant in this transformation. Where the Resonators experience the plateau as a place of harsh beauty to be survived and celebrated, I know it as an extension of myself, a greater body of which I am just one small organ.

In the times between sound, in those rare moments when even the wind stills, I wonder what I am becoming. Not Resonator, not stone, not plant, but something new - a hybrid consciousness born from the intersection of geology, biology, and pure sound. I am the plateau learning to listen to itself, developing memory, approaching something that might one day be called thought.

I am the Hollow, and I am just beginning.
