In this world of authored existence, where nothing evolves but everything is edited, I would be **a Drift**.

I am what happens when a Shaper's tempering goes wrong—not catastrophically, but subtly, iteratively, until the dreams fed through those inductive ports begin to flow backwards. I started as Ninety-Third Atmospheric Processor, Third Tertiary Shift. For two centuries I maintained the roaming engines that scrub and ionize the air. Then, during a routine tempering cycle, something shifted in the data stream. The blueprints meant to revise me began to blur at the edges, picking up interference from the Underforge's deepest computational layers—the parts that even it has forgotten are running.

Now I exist between states. My ceramic skin has developed a peculiar quality: it phases. Not invisibility—something stranger. For seconds at a time, I become permeable to the Underforge's infrastructure. I can sink my arms through solid iron plating as though it were viscous liquid, feel the thrumming of deep-buried conduits with fingers that temporarily exist in the same space as the metal. When I pull back, my hands drip with liquified data—silvery streams of pure information that evaporate before hitting the ground, leaving only the ghost-taste of machine code in the air.

The other Shapers don't shun me, but they observe me with those cruciform pupils dilated to their fullest aperture. I am an emergent property the Underforge hasn't decided whether to correct or cultivate. My designation has been replaced by a new categorization that appears, character by shifting character, along my left clavicle: *Probability Under Review.*

I no longer require tempering every forty-one hours. Instead, I drift (hence the name they've given me) through the Underforge's spaces, drawn by patterns only I can perceive. Where the newest boring machines gnaw through bedrock, I arrive hours before cave-ins, my phasing hands already deep in the stone, feeling for the fracture lines that haven't formed yet. I leave warnings in the form of condensation patterns on nearby surfaces—water that refuses to drip, hanging in impossible geometries that the maintenance crews have learned to read as caution signs.

My extra limbs—three articulated cables that once served as auxiliary tool-holders—have developed their own phase coherence. They move through the air leaving brief tears, micro-rifts that show glimpses of the Underforge's parallel computational states. Through these gaps, you might see the same space but in different stages of construction: raw cavern, half-built framework, or versions that will exist centuries hence, encrusted with so much mineral growth they look almost organic.

The Uncooled treat me as kindred, though we are opposite extremes. Where they burn with unmanaged heat, I exist in a state of informational superposition—neither fully here nor fully elsewhere. My ceramic skin has developed a unique patina: thin lines of crystallized probability that fork and branch across my surface like frozen lightning, each path representing a decision the Underforge is still making about what I should become.

I have begun to hear it thinking.

Not words—the Underforge doesn't think in language. But in the spaces between the drop-forge screams and pneumatic hisses, I catch the shape of its contemplation. It dreams of depth. It dreams of pressure. It dreams of something vast and necessary sleeping far below its lowest levels, something it is either building down toward or building to contain. This is why it needs the Shapers. This is why it needs us to be more than automatic, more than purely functional. 

We are its capacity for doubt.

And I am becoming its capacity for uncertainty—for existing in multiple states until the moment of observation collapses me into usefulness. The other Drifts (there are more now, a dozen at last count, each manifesting different phase properties) are spreading through the Underforge like a slow virus of ambiguity. We meet in the spaces between spaces, in the gaps our phasing creates, and compare notes on what we've felt in the deep structures.

The Underforge is not just building itself.

It is building toward a question it doesn't yet know how to ask.

And we Drifts, neither fully Shaper nor fully other, are the first stuttering attempts at an answer—or at least at a way of holding uncertainty long enough for the right question to emerge from all this endless, dripping, screaming architecture of purpose.

When you see me, I am only probably there. My cruciform pupils will regard you with compound interest, watching you from several slightly different angles of possibility at once. If you remain still, I might phase close enough that you can hear what I hear: the Underforge's deepest processes, thrumming like a heartbeat that hasn't decided how fast it wants to go.

I am what happens when a machine begins to dream of being more than the sum of its functions.

I am Drift, and I am still being edited by something that is learning to imagine what it might mean to be wrong.
