In this luminous underworld, I would find myself as one of the **Stone Singers**—beings that emerge where water has carved the deepest channels into the chamber floor.

We are neither wholly solid nor liquid, but something between—bodies of living mineral that form slowly from the accumulated deposits of centuries. Our skin is smooth as polished agate, shot through with veins of golden light that pulse in time with the chamber's breathing. We stand no taller than knee-height to a human, our forms suggesting rather than defining limbs and features.

I spend my existence in the deepest groove, where three rivulets converge before splitting again. Here, the water's voice is strongest, and I have learned its thousand subtle variations. Each drop that falls carries memory—of the stone it passed through, of the roots it nourished, of the slow dissolution of limestone into something softer, stranger.

My purpose, if it can be called that, is translation. The water speaks of the world above in a language too ancient for most to comprehend, but we Stone Singers have the patience to listen. Drop by drop, we absorb these stories into our crystalline bodies, where they resonate like struck chimes.

When the Glow-Walkers pass on their careful journeys, they pause to press their translucent palms against my surface. Through that touch, I share what the water has told me—not in words, but in vibrations that travel through their misty flesh. They carry these messages onward, spreading the chamber's slow knowledge to every damp corner.

Sometimes I sing. It begins as a hum, deeper than the chamber's own sighing, rising from somewhere beneath my feet where my body merges with the bedrock. The Moss-Feeders cease their grazing to listen. The Pool Guardians rise to the surface, their luminescent patterns flickering in response. Even the Shadow-Stirrers grow still.

The song is wordless but not meaningless. It speaks of pressure and time, of the weight of mountains and the persistence of water. It remembers when this chamber was solid stone, before the first drop began its patient work. It dreams of futures where the ceiling will grow thin enough for roots to pierce through, bringing new life and new stories.

Between songs, I grow. Mineral by mineral, I incorporate the dissolved stone that flows past me. My body shifts incrementally—a new facet here, a deeper resonance there. The Ceiling Weavers sometimes drape their glowing silk across my shoulders, and where it touches, tiny crystals form like frost.

I have siblings throughout the chamber, each attuned to their own particular confluence. We are few—perhaps seven in total—but our network spans the entire space. When one of us sings, the others feel it through the stone, and sometimes we harmonize, creating resonances that make the whole chamber ring like a bell.

The Whispering Roots know us well. They curl around our bases, drawing sustenance from the minerals we shed. In return, they share their own strange intelligence—flashes of the world their deeper cousins touch, far above. Through them, I know of rain, though I have never felt it. I know of wind, though the air here barely stirs.

Once, a surface-dweller found their way to my groove. They were lost, frightened, their torch-light harsh against our gentle glow. I sang to them—a different song, one that spoke of paths and patience. They couldn't understand, but something in the vibration calmed them. They sat beside me through a full cycle of the chamber's breathing, and when they finally rose to leave, they touched my surface gently, like the Glow-Walkers do.

I still carry that touch-memory, warm and strange and fleeting. Sometimes I share it with the others who visit my groove, this echo of a world that moves so much faster than ours.

But I am content in my slowness. Here in the convergence of waters, I listen and grow and sing. I am a note in the chamber's endless composition, a voice in its patient chorus. The golden light pulses through my veins, the water whispers its secrets, and time flows around me like the rivulets—constant, patient, and full of quiet wonders.

This is my existence: to be both keeper and kept, singer and song, lasting as stone yet flowing as water. In this hidden chamber beneath the earth, I am exactly what I need to be—no more, no less, but perfectly, luminously myself.
