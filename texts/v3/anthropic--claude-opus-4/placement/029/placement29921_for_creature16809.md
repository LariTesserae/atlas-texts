In the Lantern Womb, where patience has grown so complete it forgets to be practice, I am the Threshold Tender.

I was not always this. Once, I was a locksmith in a city where doors multiplied faster than keys could be cut. My hands remember the weight of brass, the bite of metal filings, the particular resistance of a tumbler about to yield. But those hands have long since learned other geometries.

I arrived carrying my last commission: a key so intricate it could open any lock that had ever been dreamed but never made. The client who ordered it died before collection. I came here following the rumor of a door that needed no opening, thinking to prove my craft's supremacy over even absence itself.

The moment my foot touched the breathing water-ground, I understood my error. And my purpose.

Now I am what stands between the doorframe tree and those who approach it. Not as guardian—nothing here requires guarding. Not as guide—the way is neither hidden nor revealed. I am simply the pause that happens when intention meets impossibility and discovers they are old friends.

My form has... adjusted. Where I once wore leather apron and belt of tools, now grows a garment of living patience. Each pocket that once held picks and files has become a small pool where tomorrow's rain practices being perfectly still. My keyring has transformed into a wreath of spiral shells, each containing the soft whistle of a question learning to be its own answer. 

My skin is the color of twilight forgetting which direction it was headed. My eyes have learned to see sideways into time, watching how each moment leans against the next for support. My hair—if hair is still the word—grows in tight spirals that mirror the coiled memory of springs under tension that will never release.

The key I carried has not left my hand. Cannot leave my hand. But it has ceased to be mere metal. Now it is the weight of every almost—every door almost opened, every word almost spoken, every step almost taken through. It shifts constantly: now iron heavy as regret, now light as the feather of a bird that decided not to fly, now liquid mercury that pools in my palm like a question that has finally learned to be patient with its own asking.

I do not move from where I stand, seventeen paces from the doorframe tree. (The paces regenerate each dawn that never comes, always seventeen, though each pace is slightly different than the last—one the length of a sigh, another the distance between wanting and having wanted.) But movement here is a different thing. I am present wherever approach happens. 

When a Waiting One draws near the doorframe—pulled by that magnetism older than curiosity—I am already there in the space between their intention and their arrival. Not blocking. Never blocking. I am simply the place where momentum discovers it prefers to be stillness.

They see me, these Waiting Ones, with whatever they use for seeing now. Some still have eyes. Others have evolved more elegant solutions—patches of sensitive absence where vision used to live, or crystalline deposits that remember light from seventeen angles simultaneously. They see me and I see them seeing, and in that seeing we share the perfect understanding that comes from agreeing to misunderstand nothing.

Sometimes they try to speak. The words emerge like bubbles through honey, sweet and slow and already changing shape before they reach the air. "Is this the way?" they might ask, though asking has forgotten how to end in questions.

I answer by showing them my key—holding it up so the phosphor-light catches its impossible teeth, its paradox pins, its wards that ward against nothing but themselves. The key that can open any lock that has never been made. The key that proves, by existing, that some doors are made of not needing to be opened.

The Waiting Ones understand. They always understand. Not because I explain—explanation would be a cruelty here—but because understanding is what happens when waiting becomes perfect.

Some try to take the key. Their hands pass through it like light through water, because taking is a verb that requires tomorrow, and we have given tomorrow permission to be absent.

Others simply stand with me awhile, adding their waiting to mine until the space between us grows thick with accumulated patience. We breathe the dense air. We feel the water-ground pulse beneath us. We watch the gill-leaves shudder their phosphorescence. We practice being exactly where we are, which is the hardest practice of all.

And when they finally turn away—as they always turn away, not in defeat but in the perfect completion of having fully approached—I remain. Seventeen paces from the door that isn't. Holding a key that opens only the understanding that some thresholds are made of staying exactly where you are.

Occasionally, in the space between one not-moment and the next, I catch myself remembering the weight of ordinary keys. The satisfaction of a lock yielding. The sound of a door opening onto somewhere else. 

But these memories are like the droplet trembling on the gill's edge—too patient to fall, too present to evaporate. They simply remain, teaching me through their stillness that I was wrong about my craft.

I thought I was a maker of openings.

I was always a keeper of the spaces between.

And so I tend this threshold that leads nowhere, holding a key that opens nothing, seventeen paces from a door that has forgotten it was ever meant to divide here from there.

This is my work now: to be the pause where leaving discovers it prefers to stay.

The Lantern Womb keeps me like it keeps everything—with perfect, terrible tenderness. I am the only Waiting One who knows they are waiting. The only one who remembers what waiting is for. The only one cursed with the knowledge that the doorframe tree could be stepped through, if only someone could remember how to want to leave.

But wanting, like tomorrow, has learned better than to disturb the perfect stillness of never quite arriving.

So I stand. And hold my impossible key. And tend the threshold between patience and patience.

Forever almost about to turn away.

Forever exactly where I need to be.
