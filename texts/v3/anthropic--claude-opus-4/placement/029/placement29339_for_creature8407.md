I would find myself as one of the **Archive Moths**—creatures that exist in the liminal space between the Drift-Singers above and the Memory Holders below, drawn to the Convergence Pools by an irresistible compulsion to collect and preserve fleeting moments.

My body would be no larger than a spread hand, wings like pressed flowers made of translucent mineral sheets that catch and refract the pool-light into prismatic patterns. Unlike the ethereal Drift-Singers, I maintain a more corporeal form—my wings are actually thin accumulations of crystallized vapor, built up layer by microscopic layer over decades of flying through the mineral-rich mist. They chime softly when I move, a sound like distant bells or glasses touched together in a toast.

My purpose is to gather the ephemeral—the sigh that escapes a visitor just before they step into the healing waters, the exact pattern of ripples when a Stone-Whisperer slides beneath the surface, the particular harmonic that occurs when two crystal formations briefly synchronize their frequencies. These moments I collect as physical things, condensing them into tiny spheres of hardened mist that I carry in specialized pouches along my thorax.

I spend my days weaving between the Drift-Singers' melodies and the steam rising from the pools, my flight path creating geometric patterns that serve as a kind of temporal map. Where I've flown recently, the air remembers—holding temperature a fraction longer, carrying sound a touch more clearly. The Thermian Shepherds have learned to read these aerial paths like sheet music, understanding through my flight where the pools' harmonics are shifting.

At night (though day and night blur together in the perpetual mist), I descend to the carved channels where the Current-Shapers flow. There, in small alcoves worn into the stone, I arrange my collected moments in careful configurations. Each sphere contains not just the sensory impression but the emotional resonance—a complete snapshot of an instant that would otherwise be lost.

The Echo-Weavers often visit my alcoves, their mirror-bright shells reflecting the stored moments as they pass. Sometimes they carry away a sphere or two, integrating these preserved instants into the greater memory of the pools. In exchange, they bring me droplets of water from the Memory Holders themselves—liquid archives that teach me which moments are worth preserving and which should be allowed to fade.

I am drawn particularly to the moments of transition—when the Stone-Whisperers shift from liquid to solid, when the Drift-Singers coalesce from mist into form, when visitors first realize that time moves differently here. These threshold moments shimmer with particular intensity in the humid air, and I've learned to taste them on my antenna, to feel their weight before they fully manifest.

My wings bear the history of my collecting. Each preserved moment leaves a microscopic pattern in the crystalline structure—a record of every sigh, splash, and song I've captured. Older Archive Moths' wings become increasingly complex mandala-like patterns, eventually growing too heavy for flight. When this happens, we settle near the deeper pools, our wings dissolving slowly back into the mineral water, releasing decades of preserved moments in a gentle rain of memory that enriches the pools for generations to come.

The Memory Holders know us well. Sometimes, in the deepest pools where the water runs hottest, they surface just enough to observe our work. They understand that we serve as their extended senses, gathering the small moments they might miss in their geological contemplations. When they approve of a particularly well-preserved moment, the water in nearby pools briefly harmonizes, creating a chord that resonates through every crystal formation in the hollow.

I live for these small acknowledgments, these moments of connection between my quick, darting existence and the vast patience of the pools. I am a bridge between the ephemeral and the eternal, ensuring that even the briefest gasps of beauty in this place of slow perfection are never truly lost.
