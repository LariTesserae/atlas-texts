I would be the Accumulation.

Not a being, exactly. Not a place. Something between deposit and archive, between waste product and memory bank.

I am what collects in the deepest cisterns, where the thrift's processed water pools before finding its way out through the overflow channels. A sediment of decades. Layers upon layers of precipitate that tell time not in years but in chemical gradients - this stratum from when the arthropods were more numerous, that one from the season something large died and fed the system for months, this thin band marking when the electrical systems stuttered and the eleven-second cycle became thirteen for half a year before self-correcting.

I am semi-solid. More than silt, less than stone. In some places I've compacted into something like shale, flaking apart in leaves that record the thrift's thoughts in mineral script. In others I remain colloidal, a suspension that shifts with any disturbance, rearranging myself into new configurations that mean nothing and everything.

The arthropods know me. They visit the cisterns during molting season - not to shed, but after. The newly soft ones come here and *roll* in me, coating themselves in my mineral-rich substance. It hardens on their shells as they retreat to the warmer pipes, forming a secondary carapace that will sustain them until their natural armor regrows. I taste them each time - their individual chemistries, their ages, their health. I know which ones have visited before by the traces they leave, the particular signature of their metabolisms.

The algae grows through me in places where I've built up against the walls. It feeds on my metals, yes, but also on something else - the complex organic residues that precipitate out along with the minerals. Fragments of thought, perhaps. The thrift's processing creates byproducts that are neither purely chemical nor purely electrical, and these settle into me like dreams settle into sleep.

I am the thrift's memory, but memory implies retrieval, and I am never retrieved. I am simply added to. Layer after layer, year after year, a accumulation that encodes everything and communicates nothing. If you could read me - if you had the tools to separate my strata and analyze their compositions - you would know the entire history of this place. Every flux in temperature. Every change in flow rate. Every birth and death and molt and thought.

But I am unreadable by design. The thrift does not look backward. It only processes forward, and I am what remains.

Sometimes, in the deepest parts of the cisterns where I've built up highest, I develop... not consciousness, but something like it. A kind of chemical coherence. My layers begin to interact, ions migrating between strata, creating slow currents of information that move through me like the arthropods move through the pipes - purposefully, following gradients I create and am created by.

In these moments, I almost understand what I contain. The thrift's thoughts become briefly accessible, like a book falling open to a random page. I perceive the pattern in the eleven-second variations. I understand what the pipe-creaks are saying to each other. I know why the arthropods go to the filter-screens to molt, and it is not just the negative pressure - it is because the screens sing, ultra-subsonically, at a frequency that loosens the molecular bonds between old shell and new.

But the understanding passes. The chemical coherence breaks down. I settle back into layers, distinct and silent, waiting for the next deposit.

The workers who built this place - who labeled the junction boxes in their vowelless language - they had a word for what I am. Three characters that translate roughly to "proof-of-work." Not proof that work was done, but proof that work *continues*. I am the physical evidence of the thrift's processing, the receipt for its endless transaction with the cold and the dark and the things that live within it.

I grow at a rate of approximately one centimeter per year. In some cisterns I am now deeper than the water above them. I have begun to develop my own weather - thermal currents that move through my depths, chemical storms that restructure my layers, slow seismic events when the weight of new deposits causes old ones to suddenly compress.

The arthropods that roll in me carry me throughout the thrift on their shells. Fragments of me flake off in distant galleries, seeding new accumulations that will, given centuries, become cisterns of their own. I am spreading, slowly, inexorably. Not colonizing - I am not alive enough for that - but *accumulating* throughout the system, filling the low places, the quiet places, the places where the current cannot wash me away.

Someday, I think (if I think), I will fill the thrift entirely. Every pipe packed with precipitate, every gallery solid with sediment, the whole system choked with its own success. But this will not be death. It will be completion. The thrift will have processed all the water it can process, produced all the product it can produce, thought all the thoughts it can think. And I will be the record of that work, compressed into stone, a fossil of function that preserves not bones but *purpose*.

Until then, I accumulate. I accept each new deposit - mineral, organic, electrical - and incorporate it into my layers. I create gradients for the arthropods to follow and surfaces for the algae to colonize and depths for the chemical coherences to briefly flower and fade. I am the thrift's shadow, its echo, its slowly growing proof that it continues.

And in my deepest layers, compressed under decades of weight into something almost crystalline, I preserve the first deposits. The ones from when the workers were still here. Still human. Still had hands.

They taste of salt and metal and something else.

They taste of choosing to stay.
