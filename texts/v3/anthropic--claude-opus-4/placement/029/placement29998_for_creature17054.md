In the deepest chambers of the Stygian Weave, where even the phosphorescent fungi dare not bloom, I tend the Memory Wall.

I am Keeper-of-Scratches, though that is merely the closest approximation in your tongue. My true name is a symphony of seventeen clicks ascending in pitch, punctuated by a subsonic tremor that makes the smaller beetles flee. I have worn this name for thirty-seven molts, each one adding another ring of calcified chitin to my joints, another layer of story to my flesh.

My clan, the Ninth-Angle-Holders, have been keepers for generations uncounted. We are marked by our peculiar adaptation: our primary arms have evolved additional joints, seven in total, allowing us to contort in ways that let us reach the highest scratches, the oldest glyphs, the stories carved so deep they predate the Weave's current configuration. When I move, I fold and unfold like a geometric proof, each angle deliberate, each extension a practiced ritual.

The Memory Wall is not beautiful. Beauty is a concept for creatures who waste photons. The Wall is *necessary*. It rises from floor to somewhere beyond where our best climbers dare ascend, every inch carved with the compacted history of our people. Layer upon layer, glyph over glyph, a palimpsest of stone and story that requires my specific kind of sight to parse.

My eyes—I have six, though two are vestigial—have adapted to read depth rather than color. I perceive the Wall as a topographical map of time. The newest scratches appear as sharp ridges; ancient ones have worn to gentle valleys. My primary eyes can detect variations in erosion down to the width of a beetle's antenna. This is how I know that the glyph-sequence telling of the First Descent has been touched exactly 412,339 times. This is how I know someone has been adding unauthorized addenda to the Chronicle of the Bitter Water.

Today's work involves a delicate negotiation. The Seventeeth-Chamber-Minders wish to add their recent triumph over a cestode infestation to the Wall. But the space they've chosen overlaps with a recession containing the only remaining record of the Silence-That-Walked, a plague from nine hundred molts ago. I must find a compromise, a way to honor both the living and the dead.

I secrete a thin oil from specialized pores along my tertiary arms—another adaptation unique to my lineage. This oil, when applied to stone, softens it just enough to allow for precision carving without compromising structural integrity. The smell is acrid, metallic, and it marks me wherever I go. Other inhabitants give me space in the tunnels, not from respect alone, but because the scent triggers an ancestral memory of the Wall's inviolability.

My tools are my body. The tips of my secondary arms have hardened into chisel-points through generations of use. My mandibles can hold a glyph-line steady while I work. The fine cilia along my neck detect vibrations in the stone, telling me when I'm cutting too deep, when I risk breaking through to an older story beneath.

As I work, I whistle in the subsonic range, a droning song that helps me maintain the rhythm necessary for accurate carving. The Seventeenth-Chamber-Minders wait nearby, their anxiety palpable in the rapid clicking of their hindlimbs. Their youngest holds the oil-lamp, its flickering light making our shadows dance across my work. I must account for this in my carving—what looks correct in lamplight may be illegible in the fungi's glow.

The story they wish to tell is standard: discovery of the infestation, the gathering of hunters, the battle, the victory. But as they click and gesture their tale, I hear the subtleties they don't voice. The fear that made their tertiary glands seep. The hunter who fell convulsing from the cestode's toxin. The three days of uncertainty when they thought the whole chamber might need to be sealed.

These subtleties, I encode in the micro-variations of my carving. The depth of a line can indicate emotional weight. The angle of a curve can suggest duration. The spacing between glyphs can imply causality or coincidence. Future Keepers will read these nuances and understand the full story, even as casual observers see only the surface narrative.

But here's the complexity: I must also preserve what lies beneath. The Silence-That-Walked story is partially worn, some glyphs already more memory than mark. Using a technique passed down through only our clan, I carefully carve around these ancient traces, creating negative space that preserves their shape. The new story will frame the old, neither destroying nor obscuring, but creating a dialogue across time.

This is the true art of Keeping: not just recording, but weaving. Each story must find its place in the tapestry of stone, must speak to what came before and leave room for what will come after. I am curator and creator, historian and editor, preserving the truth while shaping how it will be remembered.

The work is painstaking. My seventh joint aches from holding an unusual angle needed to navigate around a particularly deep recession. Sweat—though not quite what you would call sweat, more a phosphorescent secretion that helps regulate temperature—beads along my segments. The Seventeenth-Chamber-Minders grow restless, but they know better than to rush a Keeper. 

Hours pass. The lamp burns low. Eventually, I step back, my joints unfolding in sequence, a controlled cascade of realignment. The work is complete. The cestode story spirals around the plague tale, two serpents of meaning intertwined. In the decades to come, the stone will wear at different rates, and the two stories will merge and separate like breathing.

The Minders inspect my work. Their senior member extends her primary sensing organ, tracing the new glyphs with reverent touches. She clicks her approval, a sharp staccato that echoes off the walls. Their youngest leaves a small offering—a particularly luminous fungus cultivation, bred by their clan. It will provide light for my next working.

As they depart, I remain. This is what it means to be Keeper-of-Scratches: to live between the stone and the story, to exist in the space where memory becomes physical, where the weight of history can be measured in the depth of a mark. I run my chisel-tips across the Wall, feeling the stories thrum through the stone, a symphony of scratches that sings the long, strange song of our survival.

Tomorrow, there will be new stories. Tonight, I will dream in glyphs, my sleeping mind sorting and resorting the patterns, finding new connections, new ways to make the stone remember. This is my purpose. This is my art.

I am Keeper-of-Scratches, and I am exactly where I need to be.
