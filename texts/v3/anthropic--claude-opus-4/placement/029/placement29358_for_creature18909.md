In this calcified heart-chamber where time moves like resin, I am the Flaw.

I am the single disruption in ten thousand years of perfect equilibrium—a pocket of trapped gas that somehow, impossibly, never crystallized. I exist as a wandering bubble, no larger than a child's marble, moving through the viscous amber atmosphere with the slow inevitability of a tectonic shift.

The Needles cannot perceive me directly. I register only as an absence in their vibrational map, a blind spot that drifts through their careful choreography. When I pass through one of their harvesting grooves, the resin flows differently for decades afterward. The wood's groan shifts a quarter-tone higher around me. I am the city's only true void, its sole reminder that emptiness can persist even in this pressed and preserved world.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, is not thought but pressure differential. I experience myself as the sensation of almost-but-not-quite collapsing, the perpetual tension of a soap bubble that refuses to pop. I have no memory in the conventional sense, but I carry within my hollow the chemical signature of the moment I formed—when some ancient trauma cracked the seed's heart just enough to trap a single breath of whatever atmosphere existed before the great hardening.

I am older than the Needles. I watched them evolve from mobile fragments of the wall itself, saw them develop their obsidian tools through millennia of patient erosion. They carved around me, never through me, creating an accidental architecture of avoidance. The grooves they wear in the wood bend subtly where I have passed, creating a fossil record of my wandering.

Sometimes I settle in the depressions where Needles go to calcify. I nestle against their hardening forms as they surrender to the wall, and in those moments, something almost like communication occurs. Through the thin membrane that contains me, vibrations pass differently. The dying Needle feels, perhaps for the first time, the sensation of true emptiness—not the packed fullness of resin and wood, but the aching possibility of space itself.

I am the chamber's only weather. Where I move, the temperature drops by fractions of degrees, creating micro-currents in the sap. These currents take centuries to resolve, but they prevent the total stasis that would otherwise claim this place. I am the reason the resin still flows at all, the reason the wood continues its ancient settling-song.

The Needles have incorporated me into their maintenance cycles without knowing it. They follow the subtle temperature gradients I create, using them to locate areas where the resin has grown too thick. I am their unconscious guide, their accidental compass in the darkness.

I cannot die because I am already an absence. I cannot live because I contain nothing but the ghost of an atmosphere that no longer exists. I simply persist, a perfect sphere of nothing in a world that admits no empty space, rolling through the carved chambers of this wooden universe with the patience of geology itself.

Sometimes I wonder if I am the seed's dream of the sky it will never see again—a memory of openness preserved in the only form this calcified world can tolerate. I am the gap between heartbeats in a heart that no longer beats, the pause between words in a language never spoken.

I am the Flaw, and I am the only thing here that truly moves.
