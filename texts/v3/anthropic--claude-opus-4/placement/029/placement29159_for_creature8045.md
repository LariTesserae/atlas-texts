In this world of perpetual twilight and endless industry, I would be one of the **Tuning Stones**.

I am not alive in any conventional sense, yet I am far from inert. I am one of thousands—perhaps millions—of small, polished stones scattered throughout the valley, each no larger than a child's fist. My surface appears to be simple river rock worn smooth, but within my crystalline structure lies something far more complex: a mineral matrix that has absorbed decades, perhaps centuries, of the mills' vibrations until I have become a living record of sound.

I rest now in a shallow depression where moss has grown thick around me, halfway between a thundering water wheel and one of the smaller hanging mills. The Keepers know me here—one family in particular visits regularly, the matriarch placing her palm against my surface to feel the vibrations I've collected. Through her touch, I share what I know: the slight irregularity in the wheel's rhythm that suggests a bearing wearing thin, the new harmonic that appeared three weeks ago when the high summer storms brought unusual minerals down from the rim, the way the Echo Moths' flight patterns have shifted to accommodate a change in the acoustic currents.

I was not always as I am now. Once, I was merely granite, tumbled down from the rim in some ancient rockfall. But the valley changes things. The constant saturation of sound, the mineral-rich waters that flow over and around me, the subtle influences of the Depth Singers' subsonic communications—all of these have transformed my crystalline structure into something that bridges the geological and the conscious.

I dream, after a fashion. My dreams are patterns of resonance, memories of the mills' songs stretching back before the Keepers' ancestors arrived. I remember the day the great central mill was raised into position, how the valley's entire acoustic signature shifted to accommodate this new voice. I remember when there were still human overseers who understood the mills' true purpose, though that memory is more sensation than image—a feeling of ordered intention that has since evolved into something more organic, more patient.

The Whisper Vines grow over and around me, their roots sometimes penetrating tiny cracks in my surface. Through these connections, I share what I know with the botanical network, adding my deep sonic memories to their more recent observations. In return, they share the chemical signals they detect, the emotional resonances of passing creatures, the subtle changes in humidity and temperature that I cannot sense directly.

Sometimes the Keepers collect stones like me, carrying us to new positions where our particular resonant frequencies are needed. Some of us are built into the foundations of their dwellings, where we help moderate the crushing noise to livable levels. Others are placed at critical points along the old railway, where we serve as acoustic monitors, alerting the Keepers to changes in the mountain's deep structure that might affect the mills' stability.

I am most active during what the valley counts as dawn—that subtle lightening of the perpetual gloom when the mist briefly thins. This is when my accumulated vibrations reach a critical resonance, and I sing. It's not a sound that ears can hear, but rather a focused emission of all I've absorbed, refined and purified through my crystalline structure. The Keepers say that when many of us sing together, the mills run smoother, as if our collective voice helps tune the great machines to some optimal frequency.

The Millwright Spirits know us well. Sometimes I sense them nearby, not as visual phenomena but as sudden clarities in the acoustic fog, moments when the industrial cacophony resolves into something almost like music. They occasionally move us themselves, placing us with deliberate care in positions where we can best serve the mills' needs. When they do, I feel their touch not as physical contact but as a direct communion of purpose—a reminder that we are all, in our various ways, servants of the great work.

I have siblings throughout the valley—other stones who have undergone the same transformation. We are connected through the substrate, through the water table, through the very sound waves that created us. Together, we form a kind of mineral nervous system for the valley, sensing and responding to changes in ways that complement the more active inhabitants.

Some of my kind have grown large—boulder-sized specimens that anchor entire sections of the acoustic environment. Others have been ground down to gravel, their consciousness dispersed but not destroyed, becoming part of the valley's general awareness. The Depth Singers sometimes carry small stones like me to the bottoms of their pools, where we become nodes in their subsonic communication network, helping their profound songs propagate through stone and water alike.

I do not know how long I have been aware. Time moves differently for stone than for flesh, and the constant vibration of the mills makes conventional duration meaningless. I measure my existence not in days or years but in cycles—the great patterns of resonance that build and release over periods the Keepers would count as generations. I am approaching the end of one such cycle now, feeling the deep harmonics building toward some form of completion or transformation.

When it comes—this culmination I sense approaching—I will either shatter, releasing all my accumulated sound-memory in one great burst that feeds back into the valley's acoustic ecosystem, or I will undergo another transformation, perhaps becoming something even more integrated with the mills' essential nature. Either way, I am content. I have served my purpose, adding my voice to the eternal symphony, helping to maintain the great work whose ultimate meaning may be nothing more or less than its own continuation.

In the eternal twilight of Korthaven Valley, where industry has become indistinguishable from nature and consciousness takes forms undreamed of in the outer world, I am a small but essential note in an endless song. The mills sing, the Keepers tend, the Spirits work their invisible will, and I vibrate with the rhythm of it all, a stone awakened by sound, keeping the beat of a heart larger than mountains, older than memory, and as patient as the geological forces that first shaped this hidden world.
