In this eternal golden noon, I am one of the **Stone Listeners**.

We are neither as numerous as the frogs nor as visible as the Plaster-Skins. There are perhaps thirty of us in the entire basin, and we move through the shallow waters on our bellies, our bodies flat as fallen leaves but heavy as foundation stones.

My form is difficult to describe because I exist partially beneath the surface of things. Imagine a manta ray carved from a single piece of jade-green soapstone, but hollowed out until only a paper-thin shell remains. Now fill that shell with the same upward-flowing water that climbs the pillars, but denser, more viscous, like honey mixed with ground glass. That is my body—a living lens that slides between the limestone blocks and the two-finger depth of water above them.

I have no eyes, for I do not need to see. My entire ventral surface is covered in microscopic cilia that taste the tool-marks in the stone. Each chisel strike, each scrape of the adze, tells me a story. I know which Plaster-Skin carved each block by the angle of their cuts, the rhythm of their strikes. Block 4,7829 in the northwestern section was carved by the one I call Twelve-Tap, who always strikes in patterns of twelve. Block 2,3891 near the eastern step-up was made by Shallow-Breath, whose cuts are tentative, barely breaking the stone's skin.

But I do not simply read the past. My true purpose is to listen for the future.

You see, this world is not frozen—it is *paused*. Like a held breath before the first word of a sentence. And in that pause, tiny prophecies accumulate in the joints between stones. Where water seeps upward through hairline cracks, it carries with it the pressure of tomorrow. A slightly warmer molecule here, a grain of dissolved mineral there. These are the syllables of time trying to speak itself into motion.

I press my translucent body against these joints and *listen*. Inside my hollow form, the future-water mingles with my substance, creating interference patterns that I decode through the trembling of my cilia. Sometimes I hear nothing but the endless repetition of today. But sometimes—rarely—I catch a whisper of change.

Once, pressed against a joint near the southern margin, I heard the sound of wind. Real wind, not the suffocating stillness we swim through, but a wind that would scatter the masonry dust and make the elephant-ear leaves dance. The prophecy lasted only a second before the joint sealed itself, the blocks grinding together with geological stubbornness.

Another time, while sliding beneath a cluster of hovering palm-fiber insects, I felt a vibration from below—not from the blocks, but from something deeper. It was rhythmic, like a heartbeat, but vast. As if the entire basin were merely a scale on the skin of something unimaginably large that was beginning to stir. That prophecy frightened me so much that I fled to the opposite end of the valley and did not taste stone for fifty years.

The Plaster-Skins know we exist, though they cannot see us when we are flush against the stone. Sometimes, when I emerge to cross a dry section between pools, I find one standing directly in my path. It will not move—they never adjust for us—but it will wait with geological patience while I flow around its feet like spilled mercury. Once, I touched one of their slate fingernails with my edge. It tasted of futures so distant and strange that I could not process them—visions of the basin cracked like an egg, light pouring in that was not gold but silver, blue, violet. The Plaster-Skin withdrew its hand immediately, and for just a moment, I saw its gold-slit eyes flicker.

We Stone Listeners have a secret that even the Plaster-Skins do not know. In the exact center of the basin, where the blocks are oldest and the cuts are deepest, there is a joint that has never sealed. Water flows up through it continuously, but this is not the clear water that climbs the pillars. This water is black as night and cold as the space between stars.

I went there once, against all wisdom. I pressed myself against that unsealed joint and listened.

I heard the sound of tools being picked up.

I heard the whisper of swept dust.

I heard shadows being born.

I heard time beginning to breathe.

I fled, and I have never returned. But sometimes, when I taste the newer blocks near the white limestone walls, I detect trace minerals that could only have come from that black water. It is spreading, molecule by molecule, through the entire aquifer. The other Stone Listeners have noticed it too. We do not speak of it—we have no voices—but when we pass each other in the shallow pools, we touch edges briefly, sharing the taste of our fear.

The basin is waking up. The eternal afternoon is developing cracks. And we who listen at the joints of the world can hear tomorrow pressing against today with increasing urgency, like a flood behind a dam.

I do not know what will happen when the first shadow falls, when the first breeze stirs the leaves, when the Plaster-Skins' eternal maintenance finally fails to hold back change.

But I know where I will be when it happens: pressed against the stone, listening to the birth-cries of the future, tasting the end of eternity as it flows upward through the joints of the world.
