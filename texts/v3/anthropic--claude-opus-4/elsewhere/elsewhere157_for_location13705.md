Beneath the steel towers, where their foundations sink deep into sodden earth, lies the Understory—a labyrinthine network of maintenance tunnels, abandoned subway lines, and forgotten cisterns that have merged over decades into something altogether different from what the architects intended. Here, where the perpetual drip from above has created its own ecosystem, the water doesn't merely trickle—it flows in careful channels carved by the Tenders.

The Tenders work by touch and sound alone, their eyes long adapted to the profound darkness. They wear thick rubber suits that never quite dry, and their fingers have developed an uncanny sensitivity to temperature and current. They know every pipe, every valve, every place where the water pools and where it rushes. They speak in whispers that carry further than shouts would above ground, their words mixing with the constant music of flowing water until it's hard to tell where human voice ends and water's voice begins.

Mirin is the youngest of them, though age matters little when you can't see the sun. She inherited her position from her mother, who inherited it from hers—a line stretching back to when the first tower's foundation cracked and someone had to go down to fix what the water had done. Now she moves through the tunnels with the same certainty that woman above shows chasing her dog, except Mirin chases leaks, pursuing them through the maze of pipes with her specially trained eels.

The eels are pale as moonlight, their bodies thick as her arm. They can sense the slightest change in water pressure, the smallest breach in a pipe's integrity. When they find a leak, they coil around it, their bodies generating enough heat to seal small cracks through a process the Tenders discovered but don't fully understand. For larger breaks, Mirin follows their trail, her tool bag heavy with sealants and patches made from a fungus that thrives in the eternal damp.

The Tenders rarely speak of the world above, though they feel it constantly—the weight of all that steel and glass pressing down, the vibration of footsteps, the way the water temperature changes when the sun breaks through the clouds. They have their own geography: the Chamber of Echoes where seven streams converge, the Breathing Pool that rises and falls with some tide they've never been able to predict, the Garden of Blindfish where pale creatures swim in eternal circles.

Sometimes, in the deepest parts of the Understory, Mirin finds things that shouldn't be there—a child's toy that somehow made its way through the drains, a photograph sealed in plastic, once even a wedding ring that glowed faintly in the dark. She keeps these treasures in a dry alcove she's claimed as her own, next to the bed of soft moss where she sleeps when the work is done.

The woman above doesn't know that her joyful chase depends on Mirin's careful maintenance, doesn't know that every drop of water that falls from those ledges has been accounted for, channeled, managed by hands that work in darkness. The dog doesn't know that its paws leave prints on glass kept clear by drainage systems that run like veins through the building's body. 

But Mirin knows about them. Sometimes, when she presses her ear to certain pipes, she can hear laughter filtering down through the steel and concrete. She imagines what it must be like to run in sunlight, to chase something just for the joy of it. Then her eels signal another leak, and she follows them deeper into the dark, humming along with the water's endless song, keeping the bright world above afloat on her careful, unseen labor.
