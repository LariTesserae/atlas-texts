The Thermal Gardens

Three hundred miles north, where the ice shelf thins to mere yards and volcanic activity cracks the surface with steam, another sanctuary persists. The journey between the two places follows the Vein—a warm-water current that pulses through subterranean channels, carrying more than heat. Spores drift along its length, chemical messages dissolve into its flow, and the Listeners' songs travel faster here than through cold stone. Sometimes the Gardeners release seed-pods into the Vein, experimental offspring encoded with possibilities, trusting the current to carry them to compatible soil.

The Thermal Gardens sprawl across a series of connected calderas where geothermal energy keeps the ice at bay. Steam rises in constant pillars, creating a microclimate of fog and warmth beneath a ceiling of ice worn smooth and thin as glass. Here, the ice shelf becomes a greenhouse roof, trapping heat while letting through a stronger, whiter light than reaches the buried city. The ground is soft with volcanic soil, black and rich, veined with deposits of sulfur and copper that paint abstract patterns in yellow and green.

Where the buried city grew in patient silence, the Gardens riot with aggressive life. Plants here don't just grow—they compete, climbing over each other in desperate races for light and space. Vines thick as tree trunks spiral up basalt columns, their leaves the size of doors. Flowers bloom in explosions of color that would seem garish in sunlight but here serve as beacons in the perpetual mist. Everything moves faster here—you can actually watch some plants grow, their tendrils extending visibly as they seek new purchase.

The heat changes everything. Where the cold city preserves, the Gardens devour. Organic matter breaks down in days, feeding an soil ecosystem of terrible efficiency. Beetles the size of housecats process fallen leaves with industrial determination. Worms like translucent pythons surface at night to hunt the beetles. The very ground seethes with microbial life, breaking down anything that falls still for too long.

But this is cultivation, not chaos. The Thermalists tend these gardens with an philosophy opposite to the Undergrowth's collective patience. They are singular beings, each a walking ecosystem unto themselves. Their bodies are columnar, rising twelve feet or more, covered in a living coat of symbiotic plants that root directly into their flesh. Orchids bloom from their shoulders, ferns unfurl from their backs, moss carpets their slow-moving feet. They are mobile gardens, each one cultivating a unique collection of species that exist nowhere else.

The Thermalists compete with elaborate slowness. They wage wars of beauty and efficiency, each trying to cultivate the most spectacular, the most productive, the most impossible gardens on their own bodies. When two meet, they circle each other for hours, displaying their collections, occasionally trading specimens—a cutting exchanged for seeds, a rare orchid for a new strain of moss. These trades are serious business, negotiated through a language of growth hormones and chemical inhibitors that can encourage or sabotage the traded specimens.

Their faces, when you can find them among the vegetation, are nearly human in structure but made of something like living wood. Their eyes are compound, thousands of small photosensitive cells that can perceive spectrums of light invisible to most creatures. They see the heat signatures of their plants, the chemical communications between root systems, the quality of volcanic gases escaping from vents. Each Thermalist is ancient—they grow slowly but never stop, adding new sections to their bodies like trees adding rings, their oldest individuals towering like walking sequoias draped in hanging gardens.

The structures here are different too. Where the buried city was built and claimed by growth, the Gardens feature architecture that was grown from the start. The Thermalists cultivate building-plants: massive organisms bred to hollow themselves into livable spaces. These grow in predetermined shapes—domes, spirals, towers—their walls alive and photosynthetic, constantly filtering air and water. Inside, the walls bloom with food-producing vines and medicinal flowers. Furniture is trained from specialized fungi that harden into required shapes. Everything is alive, everything is temporary, everything can be guided into new forms as needs change.

At the Gardens' heart, where multiple hot springs converge into a lake of perfect bathwater temperature, stands the Conservatory. This is not a building but a grove of the eldest Thermalists who long ago took root permanently. They've grown together, their bodies fusing into a living cathedral of flesh and plant matter. Their combined consciousness serves as the Gardens' memory and decision-making center. Younger Thermalists come here to display their innovations, to seek judgment in disputes, to contribute genetic material to the next generation.

The Conservatory also maintains the Exchange—a series of pools connected directly to the Vein. Here, the Thermalists send their own messages downstream to the buried city. They trade heat for dreams, sending warm water full of nutrients in exchange for spore-packets of memory from the Archivists. They've learned to decode the Listeners' songs that travel upstream, translating vibrations into chemical information their plant-minds can process. Sometimes they send their own offspring—seed-pods that can survive the journey and the cold, carrying genetics pre-adapted for the darker world below.

The two places need each other in ways their inhabitants are only beginning to understand. The buried city's Network dreams of warmth and acceleration, of life that moves fast enough to innovate within seasons rather than centuries. The Thermal Gardens' competitive cultivation craves the patience and collective wisdom that comes from the Undergrowth's merged consciousness. The Vein carries more than water and messages—it carries evolutionary potential, the possibility of adaptation and change flowing both ways.

When the ice above finally weakens enough to crack through (and the Thermalists can feel it coming in the way the steam patterns change, in the subtle increase of light), both places will be ready. The Undergrowth will rise with its collective patience, its memory-storing stones, its time-shifting inhabitants. The Thermalists will bring their competitive innovation, their mobile gardens, their living architecture. 

Between them, they're creating something new—not just survival beneath the ice but a blueprint for what comes after. The seeds travel the Vein daily now, each one an experiment in fusion, in finding the balance between preservation and growth, collective and individual, patience and urgency. The world above will meet organisms that learned to thrive in impossible places, that turned burial into gestation, that discovered how to make gardens in the dark and dreams in the heat.

The connection lives in the water itself, in the constant circulation between cold and hot, dark and light. It lives in the spores that survive the journey, the songs that propagate through stone, the chemical messages that dissolve and reform. Neither place is complete without the other—they are two chambers of the same heart, beating at different rhythms but pumping the same life through hidden veins.

In the deepest pools of both places, where the Vein runs strongest, sometimes you can see them: the Ambassadors. Neither plant nor animal, neither individual nor collective, they exist as living currents, beings made of water and intention that can survive in both environments. They carry more than messages—they carry context, translating not just information but meaning between two ways of being. 

The Ambassadors remember when the ice first came, remember the panic and adaptation, the first successful mergings of flesh and flora. They've watched both civilizations develop their opposite philosophies of survival. Now they swim between them, patient as the Undergrowth, vital as the Gardens, weaving two places into one story of endurance and transformation. They know what both places sometimes forget: that they are not two sanctuaries but one, separated by distance but joined by water, dreaming different dreams of the same future where ice no longer divides the world above from the wonders growing below.
