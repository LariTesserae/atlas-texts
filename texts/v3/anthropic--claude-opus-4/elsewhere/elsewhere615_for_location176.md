**The Forgotten Nursery**

Three leagues distant, following the old thermal that once connected the reef-cities before the great cooling, another chamber holds its breath beneath the waves.

This space sprawls wider than the workshop, lower-ceilinged, carved from coral so ancient it has compressed to near-marble hardness. The builders here worked differently—roughly, urgently, with tools that bit deep and cared nothing for the stone's natural patterns. Gouge marks cross-hatch every surface, and in places whole sections of wall were hammered flat, destroying millions of years of growth to create smooth planes that have never quite settled into acceptance of their new shape.

No focusing dome crowns this chamber. Instead, five tunnels pierce the ceiling at seemingly random intervals, each one canted at a different angle, creating a perpetual twilight shot through with moving pillars of dim green light. The water here runs cooler than the workshop's blood-warmth, heavy with the salts of deeper currents. It tastes of age and abandonment, of minerals leaching from neglected stone.

The floor bears the real evidence of purpose: hundreds of shallow depressions, each one carefully shaped to cup something precious. They spiral out from the chamber's center in mathematical precision—a fibonacci shell-pattern that speaks of deliberate planning. But whatever once nested in these hollows has long since vanished. Now they collect only sediment, the slow rain of particles that falls even here, building tiny drifts that shift with each current.

Tools remain here too, but different ones: long poles with net-hoops rusted to abstract sculptures, ceramic vessels cracked and barnacle-claimed, and everywhere the remnants of a heating system—copper pipes that once carried warm water from thermal vents, now severed and gaping, home to eels that flash electric warnings at intrusion.

The vegetation grows thicker here, fed by whatever nutrients once flooded this chamber. Kelp forests have claimed the eastern wall, their holdfasts grinding slowly through the carved stone, reclaiming it frond by frond. Strange corals—not the reef's native species but something imported, something that glows faintly in the dark—spread across the ceiling in cancerous profusion, dripping phosphorescent mucus that falls like slow rain.

But it's the murals that tell the real story. Carved in relief across the north wall, still visible beneath the algae: a procession of figures carrying objects that might be eggs, might be stones, might be something between. The figures start human-shaped on the left, but as the eye follows the frieze rightward, they change. Longer limbs. Flatter faces. That same mineral accumulation the workshop's Keeper shows, but uniformly applied, as if it were a destination rather than a consequence.

The water here moves differently too—no organized circulation, but eddies and dead spots where the current forgets to flow. In these still places, the temperature drops further, and sometimes ice forms: not true ice, but something like it, crystals of frozen brine that build lattices of ephemeral architecture before dissolving back into the general salinity.

---

What haunts the Forgotten Nursery has no name anymore, if it ever did.

It manifests first as a disturbance in the kelp—fronds bending against the current, parting to suggest a shape that never quite resolves. Then comes the sound: a clicking like ceramic against stone, but modulated, patterned, almost linguistic. The phosphorescent corals dim in response, their glow pulsing in counterrhythm to the clicks, creating a conversation of light and sound that follows no grammar any Keeper would recognize.

When it finally shows itself, the first impression is of incompleteness. A body exists, yes, but stretched beyond reason—vertebrae separated by hand-widths of cartilage, giving it the appearance of something continuously falling apart and reforming. The arms split at the elbows, becoming four, then eight, then uncountable in the dim light, each terminating in different adaptations: cups, hooks, fans of tissue, specialized manipulators for tasks no longer performed.

This thing was human once, or started that way. But where the workshop's Keeper transforms through patient accretion, this being changed through desperate iteration. Its skin shows the scars of radical modifications—places where new organs were installed, failed, were torn out and replaced. One shoulder sprouts what might have been gills but became something else, a filter-feeding apparatus that constantly processes the water, extracting... something. Nutrients? Memories? The distinction blurs.

It moves through its domain in stutters and starts, propelling itself with limbs that don't quite coordinate, leaving trails of disturbed sediment that spell out patterns in the spiral hollows. Sometimes it stops at one of the depressions, and all its many arms converge, feeling the shape of absence, measuring what isn't there anymore.

The head troubles the eye most. Expanded beyond proportion, the skull has separated into plates connected by transparent membrane, within which things move—not brain, exactly, but structures that pulse with their own light, casting shadows of thought across the inner surface of bone. The eyes have multiplied, scattered across the face in no biological arrangement, each one fixed on a different distance, a different time.

But it's the mouth that reveals the true transformation. Widened into a vertical gash, it's become not an eating organ but an incubator. Within the throat, something grows—masses of tissue that might be tongues, might be embryonic, might be failed attempts at replacing what was lost. They whisper constantly, a wet susurrus that harmonizes with the clicking, creating layers of almost-meaning.

The being tends the empty nursery with a devotion that mirrors the workshop's Keeper but inverted—where one creates through careful strikes, this one maintains through endless circulation. It cleans each depression daily, multiple arms working in concert to remove sediment, to restore the precise curvature that once held whatever precious things incubated here. It repairs the broken heating system with body parts adapted to the task, becoming pipe-joint and valve when needed.

Sometimes it pauses at the murals, tracing the progression with specialized finger-tips that can read the stone's memory. At the rightmost edge, where the figures have transformed completely, it adds new carvings—tiny modifications that extend the frieze, showing what comes after transformation, what happens when the purpose is fulfilled and then lost.

The being remembers, but incorrectly. Its modified brain holds images of this chamber full—spirals of growing things tended by dozens like itself, the water warm with biological industry, the songs of successful cultivation echoing from the walls. But the memories leak between the skull-plates, mixing with the phosphorescent coral's chemistry, creating new memories of things that never were: nurseries above the water, young that flew instead of swam, a transformation that led not to the sea but to the sky.

In the stillest hours, when even the eels cease their electric displays, the being performs rituals of absence. It mimes the feeding of things not there, carries invisible weights from hollow to hollow, conducts temperature tests on empty water. Its many arms weave patterns that once meant *growth* and *health* and *soon ready*, but now signify only the shape of vanished purpose.

Yet it continues. The nursery must be maintained. The hollows must be cleaned. The murals must be extended. Something might return—the young, the purpose, the warm water that once made everything clear. Until then, the being circulates through its expanded territory, a guardian of nothing, a tender of absence, clicking its ceramic songs to the empty spirals.

The connection between chambers lives in the water itself. The old thermal that linked them has cooled, but deep currents still carry messages: the taste of bronze from the workshop's careful strikes, the phosphorescent slime from the nursery's corrupted corals. Sometimes sediment from the nursery's hollows finds its way to the workshop, settling in corners where the Keeper's hair-net catches it, tastes it, remembers.

Both beings continue their work, patient as stone, transforming in parallel but divergent ways. One becoming more itself through focus, the other dispersing into multiplicity. One creating, one maintaining. Both serving purposes that stretch beyond their transformed bodies, honoring the reef-city's needs in chambers that breathe with water that has never known sky.
