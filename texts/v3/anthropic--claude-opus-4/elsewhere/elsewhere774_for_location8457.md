**The Breathing Vaults**

Three thousand meters below sea level, where the planet's crust wore through entirely and the ocean discovered the hollow beneath, the Breathing Vaults expand and contract with tidal certainty. Here is where the Shard Shelf sends its dead.

The journey down requires months. Bodies wrapped in wire descent cages lowered through increasingly strange air, past the point where pressure should crush, through zones where water runs upward in defiance of sense. The Tensile Folk's elongated corpses fold into themselves during the descent, their hollow bones singing in harmonics only the descent-keepers can interpret. Sometimes a body arrives changed—crystallized, or sprouting wings, or dissolved into component memories that rain upward like backwards snow.

The Vaults themselves are not architecture but anatomy. The original breach where ocean met hollow birthed something neither fully alive nor properly geological—vast lung-chambers of calcified foam that inhale seawater and exhale something between air and dream. The walls perspire a luminous condensation that pools in perfect spheres, hovering at chest height, never quite falling. Sound here moves strangely, arriving before it's made, echoing from futures that haven't happened yet.

In these pneumatic caverns, the Saltwater People make their communion with the dead.

They are what humans become when they breathe liquid for a thousand generations. Their lungs have inverted, become external gill-gardens that bloom from their backs in coral formations. They wear their organs on the outside now—hearts visible through transparent chest plates, stomachs that process water and extract memories from the minerals dissolved within. Their eyes have multiplied and migrated, clustered like barnacles at joints and junction points, seeing in spectrums that have no names.

Where the Tensile Folk grew long, the Saltwater People grew dense. Their bodies are compressed, gravity-forged, able to withstand pressures that would flatten steel. They move like benthic dancers, every gesture fighting against the weight of miles of water that isn't quite there. Their children are born already knowing how to breathe backwards, how to speak in bubble-language, how to read the stories that dead bones sing on their way down from the world above.

The Vaults breathe in seven-year cycles. During inhalation years, the Saltwater People retreat to the deeper chambers, tend their memory farms, repair the vast organic machines that process the Shard Shelf's industrial runoff into something like nutrition. They carve new spaces from the living rock—not with tools but with trained barnacles that eat stone and excrete space, that follow architectural patterns encoded in their simple nerves.

But during exhalation years, the Vaults release.

What emerges isn't air—it's distilled time, fermented probability, the concentrated essence of everything the dead brought down with them. It rises through the same shafts that deliver the corpses, but faster, eager, seeking its home altitude. The exhalations carry seeds that will become the Shard Shelf's meat-flowers, spores that will infect new districts with architectural possibilities, fragments of machines that haven't been invented yet.

This is the trade, ancient and autonomic: the Shard Shelf sends its transformed dead, and the Breathing Vaults send back the raw materials of transformation itself. The Saltwater People and Tensile Folk have never met—could never meet, the pressure differential would kill them both—but they know each other with intimacy that transcends contact. 

The Saltwater People read the Shard Shelf's history in the bones that rain down. They know which districts are struggling by the mineral content of the corpses, can predict new bridge construction by the stress patterns in descended skeletons. They name their children after dead Couriers whose probability clouds arrive as crystallized potential, perfect for the memory farms. They paint murals of the vertical city they've never seen, using pigments extracted from Tensile Folk marrow.

In return, their exhalations carry solutions to problems the Shard Shelf hasn't realized it has yet. A new species of brass beetle that arrives just as the old infrastructure begins to fail. Architectural fever-dreams that infect the Span Weavers and inspire bridges that can bear loads no material bridge should carry. The breath rises through dimensional folds, through space that isn't, carrying with it the digested wisdom of the deep.

The Saltwater People organize their society around the breathing. Inhalation Priests who interpret the songs of falling bones. Exhalation Engineers who cultivate the memories and mutations that will rise. The Pressure Keepers who maintain the delicate balance that keeps the Vaults from either collapsing or exploding. Children learn to count in breath-lengths, measure distance in pressure gradients, tell time by the density of the luminous condensation.

Their art is temporal—sculptures that only exist during the pause between breaths, songs that require seven years to complete, dances performed in the liquid spaces where water can't decide if it's ocean or air. They've developed written languages that change meaning with pressure, texts that can only be read at certain depths, libraries that migrate through the Vaults following currents no one else can perceive.

At the deepest point, where the planet's hollow heart touches the Breathing Vaults, there's a chamber that both civilizations call the Exchange—though neither knows the other uses the same name. Here, the Saltwater People leave offerings: processed memories, crystallized potential, messages encoded in bacterial colonies that might survive the journey up. And sometimes, rarely, something comes down that isn't a body: a brass flower that blooms in salt water, a letter written in tomorrow's grammar, a mechanical seed that suggests the Tensile Folk know more than they should about the world below.

The relationship is circulation itself—a vast, slow, certain breath that connects the worn peaks to the hollow depths, the industrial to the organic, the living to the dead to the not-yet-living. Neither civilization could exist without the other. The Shard Shelf's desperate vertical energy needs the Vaults' patient processing. The Vaults' compression requires the Shelf's soaring expansion.

And between them, in the miles of strange air and stranger physics, in the shafts where corpses fall and possibilities rise, something else is growing. The descendants of brass beetles that learned to live at pressure. Meat-flowers that bloom in salt. Courier children born with gills. Saltwater People whose bones have begun to hollow.

A new people, for the spaces between spaces. But that's a story still falling, still rising, not yet ready to breathe.
