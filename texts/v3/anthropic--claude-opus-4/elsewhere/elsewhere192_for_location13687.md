Below the Valdad Mountains, where the roots of those stubborn trees burrow through stone and silence, lies a world turned inside out. The Sunken Courts sprawl through caverns that breathe with their own peculiar warmth, lit by phosphorescent veins that pulse through the rock like arteries of light. Here, water doesn't fall—it rises, climbing walls in defiant streams, pooling in inverted lakes that hang from ceilings like liquid mirrors.

The architecture confounds expectation. Stone stairs spiral downward only to deposit travelers on platforms suspended above their starting point. Bridges arc beneath walkways rather than over them. Chambers open into themselves through impossible angles, and doors lead both forward and back to the same threshold. It is a place where geometry has made peace with contradiction.

Where the mountains above enforce solitude, the Sunken Courts insist on congregation. Sound doesn't die here—it multiplies, gathering strength as it ricochets through crystal formations that remember every word ever spoken in their presence. A whisper becomes a chorus. A footstep becomes a drumbeat. Silence, that tyrant of the upper world, finds no purchase in these depths.

The Keepers move through this labyrinth with practiced ease, their forms draped in robes that shimmer with the same phosphorescence as the walls. They are neither fully human nor entirely other—something caught between states, as if the Court's own contradictions have shaped them. Their faces shift subtly in the changing light, never quite settling on a single expression or age. Some say they were once like those who built the abandoned structures above, but chose transformation over abandonment.

They tend to the Remembrance Pools—shallow basins carved into the living rock where memories collect like sediment. Not their own memories, but those left behind by every soul who has passed through the Valdad above. Each pool holds a different flavor of loss: here, the weight of unfinished ambitions; there, the sharp edge of words never spoken; in another, the soft ache of beauty witnessed alone. The Keepers stir these pools with long crystalline rods, keeping the memories from crystallizing, from becoming too solid to ever release.

The relationship between the two realms is one of essential tension. The mountains above press down with their silence and solitude, creating pressure that forces life into strange new shapes below. The Sunken Courts push back with their cacophony and communion, ensuring that what the mountains try to still, the depths keep in motion. Those skeletal structures abandoned in the Valdad? Their builders didn't flee—they descended, choosing transformation over defeat.

Travelers who find the hidden entrances (often revealed only when despair reaches its crescendo) discover that the Sunken Courts offer not escape but translation. Here, the desolation absorbed above can be alchemized into something else. The Keepers guide visitors to the appropriate Remembrance Pool, where they can deposit what the mountains forced them to carry. In return, they receive... not healing exactly, but perspective. The ability to see their solitude as one note in a vast symphony rather than the only sound in existence.

Some visitors stay, eventually taking up the phosphorescent robes and joining the eternal circulation through the twisted passages. They learn to read the memories in the pools, to tend them without drowning, to help others translate their mountain-silence into something that can be borne back to the world above. Others leave, but they leave different—not because they've abandoned what the Valdad taught them, but because they've learned it isn't the only truth.

The Sunken Courts pulse with a different kind of timelessness than the mountains. Where the Valdad makes hours feel like epochs, the Courts compress lifetimes into moments. A traveler might spend what feels like years learning the peculiar choreography of the Keepers, only to emerge and find the same sunset waiting that they left behind. Or they might dash through in minutes and carry decades of accumulated wisdom in their bones.

At the deepest point of the Courts, where all the impossible architectures converge, stands the Resonance Chamber. Here, the silence of the Valdad and the symphony of the Courts meet in perfect opposition. The chamber itself is small, no larger than the abandoned structures above, but it contains multitudes. In this space, a person can hear their own silenced voice speaking back to them from every pool they've contributed to, every mountain path they've walked, every impossible stair they've climbed. It is overwhelming and necessary, this confrontation with one's scattered self.

The Keepers say that long ago, before the first structures were built above, the Valdad Mountains and the Sunken Courts were one realm, neither silent nor singing, neither solitary nor crowded. Some cataclysm—perhaps nothing more dramatic than a choice—split them apart. Now they exist in necessary opposition, each preventing the other from consuming the world entirely.

And so the cycle continues: souls climb the mountains seeking something pure in isolation, find themselves scraped raw by the silence, discover the entrances to the Courts, descend into the phosphorescent chaos, deposit their burdens in the pools, and emerge... not healed, not unchanged, but somehow more spacious. Able to contain both silence and song, solitude and communion, the desolate acceptance of the peaks and the transformative turmoil of the depths.

The footprints in the Valdad tell only half the story. The other half swirls in the Remembrance Pools, tended by those who chose to stay in the spaces between.
