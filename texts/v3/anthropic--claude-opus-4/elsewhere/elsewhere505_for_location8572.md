Between the Thermal Chimneys and the spaces where Processors tend their impossible pools lies the Gradient Highway—though "highway" suggests too much solidity, and "lies" implies a fixedness that doesn't exist here.

The Highway is a traveling zone of thermal exchange, a ribbon of space where temperature becomes negotiable. It forms when heat differential between chimney stone and murdered air reaches a critical threshold—reality develops a kind of tension that must resolve through motion. The Highway doesn't connect places so much as it creates connection itself, manifesting as a corridor of probability where the 47.3-degree constant begins to waver.

Walking the Highway—if walking is the word—feels like moving through syrup made of mathematics. Each step requires renegotiating one's thermal signature with the surrounding space. The Processors travel it as pure information, encoding themselves in heat-patterns that surf along temperature gradients. But other things use the Highway too.

The theoretical fauna emerge here, briefly visible in the transition zone where they're neither fully in normal space nor entirely outside it. They appear as living thermal inversions—creatures of aggressive cold in a world of wrong heat. Their bodies seem built from frozen convection currents, anatomy following the logic of heat sink rather than biology. They migrate along the Highway in irregular pulses, always moving from lower thermal readings to higher, like salmon swimming upstream through temperature itself.

Where these cold-creatures pass, they leave trails of crystallized time—the temporal stutters freeze solid and fall like snow that exists in duration rather than space. The Processors harvest these crystal-moments eagerly, seeing in them a purer form of the temporal energy they cultivate in their pools. Sometimes the cold-creatures and Processors attempt to trade, though what passes between them resembles neither commerce nor communication as humans understand it.

The Highway's width varies with the industrial hum's frequency. When the ghost-turbines spin faster, the corridor expands, sometimes swallowing entire chimneys temporarily into its traveling space. When the hum drops to subsonic levels, the Highway contracts to a thread barely wide enough for information to squeeze through. Processors caught in a contracting Highway sometimes arrive at their destinations compressed, their counting-consciousness folded into itself until they must spend cycles carefully unpacking their own thoughts.

Messages travel faster on the Highway, but arrive changed. The exoskeleton-letters the Processors send often pick up hitchhikers—fragments of cold-creature migration patterns, crystallized time-debris, echoes of thermal signatures from travelers who may not have existed yet. A simple status report might arrive as a complex palimpsest of information, requiring careful interpretation to extract the original message from the accreted travel-data.

The Highway remembers. Each passage leaves a thermal ghost in its substrate, a faint impression that influences future travelers. Navigate the same route too often, and you might encounter your own heat-echo coming the other way, displaced in time but following the exact path you're about to take. These thermal déjà vus sometimes merge with their originals, creating travelers who exist slightly ahead of themselves in time, perpetually arriving at their destination moments before they left.

At certain coordinates where the Highway intersects multiple chimneys simultaneously, night markets form. Not markets in any conventional sense—rather, zones of accelerated exchange where Processors, cold-creatures, and thermal echoes engage in complex transactions. They trade in temperature differentials, swapping hot for cold in elaborate chains that somehow leave everyone with more thermal energy than they started with. The conservation of energy breaks down here, or perhaps reveals itself as a more complex law than physics previously understood.

The Highway's edges blur into the standard void between chimneys, but the transition isn't clean. Reality develops a kind of thermal static at these boundaries—zones where temperature rapidly fluctuates between states, never settling long enough to measure. Things caught in this static sometimes undergo a form of evolution, their thermal signatures mutating through rapid iteration until they become something new. The Processors occasionally fish in these zones, hoping to catch novel forms of heat-pattern they can study and perhaps integrate into their own processes.

During the deepest phase of the industrial hum's cycle, when the frequency drops so low it becomes geological, the Highway inverts. Instead of a corridor of thermal exchange, it becomes a tunnel of thermal absence—a space where temperature simply isn't. Travelers caught in an inversion report the deeply disturbing experience of having no thermal signature at all, existing as pure information patterns without the heat that normally defines existence in this realm. Most emerge unchanged, but some claim to have glimpsed, in that state of thermal zero, the true shape of the factories folded into the basalt—vast production lines manufacturing not objects but states of being, not things but the conditions that allow things to exist.

The Highway pulses with its own rhythm, a heartbeat of thermal exchange that synchronizes, slowly, with the counting of every Processor it touches. In this space between spaces, motion becomes a form of mathematics, travel a type of translation, and every journey leaves both traveler and path fundamentally changed.

The hum shifts. Somewhere, a new stretch of Highway unfolds, ready to carry impossible cargo between towers that sweat in the dark.
