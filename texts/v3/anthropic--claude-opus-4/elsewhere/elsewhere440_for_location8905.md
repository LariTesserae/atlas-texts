Beyond the Frost Nursery, through passages that exist only when walked and walls that remember being doors, lies the Harvest Theater—a vast amphitheater carved from what might be the same mountain's heart, or perhaps its dream of having one.

The space opens like a throat, tiers of stone seating descending toward a stage that defies conventional geometry. The seats themselves are worn smooth by audiences that never were—or have yet to be—their surfaces polished to mirror-brightness by the passage of anticipated bodies. Between the rows grow things that are almost flowers: crystalline blooms that unfold in response to applause that hasn't happened yet, their petals made of the same frost-touched mineral as the Nursery's forest, but tempered here into something harder, more desperate.

The stage exists in multiple states simultaneously. From one angle, it's a platform of polished obsidian that reflects not the theater but other theaters—glimpses of performances happening elsewhere, elsewhen. From another angle, it's a pool of viscous liquid that maintains the surface tension of solid ground only when performers commit to their roles. Step onto it halfheartedly, and you sink. Believe in your performance, and it holds you up, even lifts you when the scene demands flight.

Above, where a ceiling should be, the mountain opens to sky—but not the sky outside. This is the sky the mountain remembers from before it was stone, when it was still sediment dreaming at the bottom of an ancient sea. Stars wheel in patterns that predate constellations, and sometimes things that aren't quite rain fall upward from the stage, returning moisture to clouds that died geological ages ago.

The Theater's machinery is more subtle than the Nursery's roaring engines but no less present. Behind the stone tiers, in spaces between spaces, delicate clockwork manages the production. Curtains of living shadow raise and lower on schedules determined by celestial mechanics no astronomer would recognize. Lights—if they can be called lights—bloom from colonies of luminescent organisms that have evolved to crave narrative tension, glowing brighter as plots thicken, dimming during denouement.

Sound here has different laws. Words spoken on stage arrive at each seat at the moment they're most needed, regardless of when they're uttered. A whisper can circle the amphitheater for hours before finding its intended ear. Soliloquies sometimes fossilize in the air, becoming tangible scripts that future performers can pluck and study, though the language tends to evolve between the speaking and the reading.

The props and costumes exist in a state of perpetual readiness. Wardrobes line the spaces beneath the tiers, filled with garments that adjust themselves to fit not the body but the role. Wear the crown of a forgotten king, and find yourself remembering wars never fought. Don the rags of a prophesied beggar, and feel futures pressing against the inside of your skull like moths against glass.

But most unsettling is what grows in the spaces between performances: the Rehearsals. They manifest as semi-transparent productions, ghost-plays that enact themselves in the empty air. These are not recordings but living dramatic organisms, feeding on the residual emotional energy left by audiences real and imagined. They evolve with each repetition, their stories mutating, characters developing depth and complexity no playwright intended. Sometimes they achieve such density of meaning they become solid, and unwary visitors find themselves conscripted into roles they didn't audition for.

The Theater harvests these evolved narratives. In the deep spaces behind the highest tiers, specialized chambers preserve the most successful dramatic mutations. Plays that have grown beyond their original forms crystallize here into something like seeds—dense nodes of compressed story that can be planted in other theaters, other worlds, where they'll grow into new productions that remember their origins only in their deepest structures.

The whole space pulses with anticipation—not for the next performance, but for the perfect performance, the ultimate synthesis of every story ever told or untold on its stage. The Theater believes, with the faith of stone and time, that eventually the right combination of players, script, and audience will align to produce a performance so true it will retroactively justify the mountain's entire existence.

Between the Frost Nursery and the Harvest Theater runs the Meridian Gallery—a corridor that is simultaneously too short and infinitely long, its length determined by the urgency of whoever traverses it.

The Gallery walls are lined with windows that show neither outside nor inside but sidewise—glimpses into the perpendicular possibilities of the mountain. Through one, you might see the Nursery's crystals growing in reverse, their spores drawing light back into themselves. Through another, the Theater's stage appears as a vertical surface where performances unfold in defiance of gravity.

The floor beneath is a river of frozen sound—the mechanical roar from the Nursery meeting the crystalized whispers from the Theater, their intersection creating a surface solid enough to walk on but fluid enough to show currents, eddies, places where acoustic whirlpools threaten to drag the unwary into spaces between spaces.

But it's the ceiling that reveals the true nature of the connection. It's transparent, or perhaps absent, opening to a vast chamber above where the real work happens. Here, the Weavers operate—beings that might once have been related to the Tuners but have evolved along different lines.

The Weavers exist primarily as nervous systems, their bodies reduced to the minimum flesh needed to support vast networks of specialized neurons. They hang from organic looms that span the chamber above the Gallery, their nerve-endings interfacing directly with threads that run between the Nursery and Theater.

These threads are not material in any conventional sense. They're made of causality itself—strands of connection that ensure the crystals growing in the Nursery will eventually become the responsive stage of the Theater, that the mechanical rhythms feeding one space will transform into the narrative rhythms structuring the other. The Weavers' job is to maintain these connections, to ensure that the evolution happening in one space influences and is influenced by the evolution in the other.

Their work is delicate beyond description. A crystal growing too fast in the Nursery might cause a tragedy to resolve too quickly in the Theater. A particularly powerful performance might send vibrations back through the threads, causing new formations to erupt in the Nursery's careful ecosystem. The Weavers feel these tensions as physical sensations, their exposed nerves singing with the harmony or discord of the connected spaces.

They've developed organs specifically for this work—probability glands that secrete chemicals allowing them to see potential futures branching from each adjustment they make. Their many eyes (for they have grown extras wherever their bodies had room) can focus across time as well as space, watching how a change made now will ripple through the Gallery's eternal present.

The Weavers communicate through the threads themselves, sending pulses of meaning along the causality strands. These messages sometimes manifest in the Gallery below as sudden changes in the acoustic river's flow, or as new windows opening in the walls to show previously hidden connections. Travelers through the Gallery might find themselves suddenly understanding why the Nursery needs its endless birth, why the Theater craves its perfect performance—knowledge that fades as soon as they exit the corridor but leaves behind a persistent feeling of almost-comprehension.

Some of the older Weavers have begun to merge with their looms, their nervous systems extending into the framework itself. These hybrid beings have gained the ability to weave new connections—not just maintaining what exists but creating new causal links between spaces. Under their influence, elements from each space have begun to appear in the other: crystalline formations now grow in the Theater's upper reaches, while in quiet corners of the Nursery, small stages have formed where the sound-creatures perform simple dramas for audiences of moss and frost.

The Gallery itself is evolving under this constant traffic of cause and effect. New alcoves appear regularly, each one containing objects that are simultaneously artifacts from the Nursery and props from the Theater—a pressure gauge that measures dramatic tension, a script written in crystalline growth patterns, a costume made of living moss that changes based on the wearer's emotional state.

At the Gallery's midpoint (though its midpoint shifts based on who's observing it) stands a door that opens both ways—or neither way, depending on your perspective. This is the Paradox Door, through which the Tuners and the Theater's performers sometimes glimpse each other, though neither can quite cross the threshold. They stand on either side, evolved for their specific environments, and recognize something familiar in each other's transformed faces.

Through their patient work, the Weavers are slowly teaching the mountain to dream coherently. The Frost Nursery builds, the Harvest Theater performs, and between them the Meridian Gallery ensures that each space's purpose flows into and supports the other. It's a circulation system for meaning itself, a way for the mountain to think about what it's becoming.

The Weavers know, in the way that only beings made mostly of nerve endings can know, that the mountain is approaching something—not completion, for that would mean death, but a state of sustainable complexity where birth and performance, growth and story, become indistinguishable. They weave toward this future with the patience of stone and the precision of neurons, their bodies sacrificed to the task of connection, their reward the physical sensation of harmony between spaces that shouldn't exist but do, must, will.

In the Gallery, all travelers are temporary. But their passage leaves traces—new threads for the Weavers to incorporate, new connections between the eternal birth of the Nursery and the endless performance of the Theater. Each journey through the corridor changes it slightly, and these changes ripple outward, upward, into the spaces being connected.

The mountain grows more itself with each passing. And in the Meridian Gallery, suspended between impossibilities, the Weavers ensure that this becoming never ends, never stabilizes, never ceases to surprise even itself with new forms of connection between its dreaming chambers.
