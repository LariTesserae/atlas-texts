The Threshold Markets of Empty Percussion

The caravans arrive through tears in the air itself—long rips that open with sounds like breaking ceramic, revealing glimpses of wherever they've traveled from before sealing shut with crystalline scarring. The Markets exist in a natural bowl between ancient impact craters, where the ground is beaten brass-hard by millions of footfalls and the sky maintains a permanent twilight despite no visible source of occlusion.

This is the economy of absence. Every stall, every vendor, every traded good represents something that no longer exists somewhere else. The merchants wear robes of negative space—garments that show what's behind them but tinted with the colors of lost sunsets from worlds that burned. They speak in the past tense exclusively, their voices carrying undertones of the last words spoken in dead languages.

The Markets sprawl in concentric rings around the Fountain of Unmaking, where water that isn't water falls upward into pools that were never built. The innermost ring trades in lost sensations: the feeling of grass from worlds now desert, the taste of fruits from orchards that exist only in extinction's memory, the precise weight of gravity from planets cracked open for their cores. Moving outward, the rings grow more abstract—emotions no one feels anymore, words for concepts that died with their civilizations, the specific silence left when certain stars stop singing.

Currency here is forgetting. Merchants accept payment in the form of memories willingly surrendered, knowledge deliberately unlearned, names consciously released. The most valuable coins are those that erase something essential—the face of a loved one, the way home, the reason you came here in the first place. These transactions leave visible marks: customers develop patches of translucent skin where their erased experiences once lived, areas that show not their insides but simply nothing at all.

The Market Guards are absences themselves—uniformed gaps in reality that patrol between the stalls, their presence known only by the behavior of light around their edges and the way sound dies in their vicinity. They enforce the cardinal rule: nothing new may be created here. Every transaction must result in less than existed before. Vendors who try to sell hopes or possibilities or things that might yet be are dragged into the un-water of the fountain, where they emerge (if they emerge) speaking only in negations.

Architecture here defies construction. The stalls and permanent structures weren't built but rather represent spaces where building stopped—half-completed walls that suggest rather than enclose, doorways that frame nothing but imply everything, roofs that provide no shelter but create shadows of rain that never fell. The most successful merchants operate from locations that barely exist: a suggested corner, a theoretical alcove, a counter made from the absence of stone.

Time moves in eddies and reversals within the Markets. Customers age backwards while browsing, growing younger until they risk disappearing entirely if they linger too long. Conversations end before they begin. Goods are unsold before money unexchanges hands. The only constant temporal flow exists around the fountain, where the upward-falling water maintains a perpetual present by continuously destroying its own past.

The most prized merchandise comes from the furthest travelers—those who've witnessed endings no one else survived to remember. A vendor from the Dissolution Districts sells the last thoughts of dying AIs, crystallized into shapes like inverted snowflakes. Another offers the final symphony played as a civilization uploaded itself and failed, the music somehow both beautiful and absent, each note a small death. The Grief Merchants deal exclusively in sorrows too specific to share: the particular anguish of watching your world's three moons collide, the exact despair of discovering your history was fiction.

Barter often involves complex chains of loss. To purchase the memory of warmth from someone who knew it, you might need to trade your understanding of cold—but the Warmth Merchant only accepts concepts of cold from worlds that never knew ice, so first you must visit the Theoretical Vendors who deal in possibilities that never manifested, trading them your certainty about what did happen for their uncertainty about what didn't.

The Markets connect everywhere through their tears, but each connection costs. The caravans that arrive are always smaller than those that left their origins. Travelers speak of companions who stepped through and never emerged, of cargo that existed until the moment of transition, of entire merchant trains reduced to single confused survivors clutching inventory lists for goods that no longer exist in any reality.

At the crown of the market, furthest from the fountain, sits the Archive of Final Things—a structure that is less building than organized absence, where the last of everythings are catalogued. The last sunrise on Bethel Prime. The last word spoken in the language of the Singing Prophets. The last heartbeat of the last whale in Earth's last ocean. The Archivists, ancient beings who've traded away so much of themselves they're more gap than substance, maintain this collection through pure will, knowing that when they finally forget their purpose, the Archive itself will become part of its own collection.

Music drifts through the Markets, but it's the music of unstrung instruments, unvoiced songs, the space between notes where melody lives but cannot be caught. The Musicians of Unmaking perform at the crossroads, their instruments suggesting rather than producing sound, their audiences paying in the memory of songs they'll never hear again.

This is where the Resonance Weavers come, traveling through the fractured physics of their plateau world, to trade their excess reality for absence. They arrive as probability clouds, their crystalline forms existing in too many states simultaneously, seeking the Market's merchants who specialize in reduction. They barter overlapping existences for singular presence, trading their burden of multiplicity for the simple absence of choice.

The Markets pulse with their own rhythm—expansion and contraction, like breathing in reverse. Each exhalation brings new tears, new arrivals, new things to be unmade and sold. Each inhalation collapses another section into the pure absence that is the Market's true currency. The cycle continues, fed by the endless supply of endings the universe provides, a commerce of entropy that enriches all participants by making them less.

In the eternal twilight between the brass-hard ground and the occluded sky, fortunes in nothingness change hands. The empty-robed merchants call their wares in languages being spoken for the last time. The fountain falls upward, drops of un-water catching light that comes from nowhere, each splash a small apocalypse that feeds the market's hunger for absence.

Here, in the Threshold Markets of Empty Percussion, the universe comes to celebrate its own unmaking, and every sale is both a funeral and a birth—though of what, no one who remembers has survived to say.
