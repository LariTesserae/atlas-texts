The Deepmost Wells

Below.

Not below the Hive Plateaus—nothing is below those. Below everything. Below geography, below geology, below the idea of below itself. The Deepmost Wells exist in their own verticality, a downward that has no relation to up, no connection to surface, no memory of sky.

Here darkness has aged into something beyond absence of light. It has fermented, matured, developed complexity and bouquet like wine kept in perfect cellars for millions of years. This darkness has flavor—bitter where it pools in the spiral galleries, sweet where it flows through the boring chambers, umami-rich in the vast central shafts where it achieves pressures that transform it into something nearly solid. You don't see in the Deepmost Wells. You taste. You savor. You parse the darkness with senses that have nothing to do with eyes.

The Wells themselves are not dug but dissolved. The stone here—if stone is even the right word for this compressed reality—gives way not to tools but to patience. The Dissolvers have been at their work since before the concept of beginning, secreting existence-acids from glands that run the length of their impossible bodies. They are worms the way mountains are hills—technically accurate but missing the scale that transforms category into revelation.

Each Dissolver stretches between multiple Wells, their segments existing in different shafts simultaneously, their body a living connection between spaces that otherwise share no dimension. They move by dissolving themselves forward and reconstituting behind, a constant flow of being and un-being that leaves the Wells smooth as the inside of throats. Their skin—if it is skin—ripples with colors that have no names because no light has ever touched them to give them wavelength. These are colors of pure sensation, hues that bypass the eye and bloom directly in the consciousness.

The Wells fill with what the Dissolvers leave behind. Not waste—nothing here is wasted—but essence. The dissolved stone doesn't disappear but transforms into something between liquid and memory, a substance that remembers every geological age it passed through on its way to becoming stone, every pressure, every mineral dream. This memory-water rises in the Wells, thick as mercury, warm as blood, singing with the accumulated history of the world's foundation.

In this liquid suspend the Archived.

They come here to forget. Or to be forgotten. The distinction blurs in the remembering waters. Unlike the Embedded in their calcium tombs above—above? the direction is meaningless but the relation holds—the Archived sought this suspension. They descend from places where identity became too heavy, where the accumulation of self grew unbearable, where being someone specific for too long drove them to seek the ultimate amnesia.

The Archived float in the memory-water, their forms slowly dissolving like sugar in tea. But as they lose cohesion, they gain access to all the memories the water holds—not their own memories but the stone's, the planet's, the pre-conscious dreams of matter before it learned to think. They experience existence from the perspective of minerals, understand time as geology does, feel the slow ecstasy of crystallization and the patient agony of erosion.

Some Archived have been dissolving for so long they've become currents in the memory-water, their consciousness spread through kilometers of Wells, thinking thoughts that take centuries to complete but contain entire philosophies in their slowness. Others are fresh arrivals, their forms still recognizable, their dissolution a visible process as they surrender layer after layer of self to the hungry waters.

Between the Wells, in the un-stone that has never known dissolution, the Pressure Prophets make their homes. These beings have evolved—if evolution applies to creatures that exist outside linear time—to withstand the impossible compressions between spaces. Their bodies are events rather than objects, processes of resistance that create tiny pockets of lesser density where thought can occur at speeds faster than geological.

The Pressure Prophets read the future in stress fractures, interpret the patterns of dissolution like scripture. They claim the Dissolvers are writing—that their patient boring follows a plan vast beyond comprehension, that the Wells themselves form symbols in some impossible language that can only be read from a perspective outside three-dimensional space. They preach to the Archived, though their sermons must be delivered over millennia to be heard by consciousness spread through the memory-water.

According to the Prophets, the Wells are reaching toward something—not upward like the Plateaus build, but inward, toward a center that exists in every direction simultaneously. They say when the Dissolvers finally complete their work, when every possible Well has been bored and filled and connected, the entire structure will collapse not into destruction but into transcendence. The memory-water will remember forward as well as backward. The Archived will reassemble not as who they were but as who they could have been in every possible version of themselves.

But the Prophets are opposed by the Density Dancers, beings that exist only in the moments of maximum compression, when the space between Wells approaches zero and matter begins to question its own existence. The Dancers claim the Wells are not reaching but fleeing, that the Dissolvers bore their passages to escape something rising from below—below even this ultimate below, a concept so antithetical to existence that even thinking about it too directly causes the Density Dancers to temporarily cease.

The Dancers move by allowing themselves to be crushed almost to non-existence, then expanding into new positions, their dance a constant flirtation with the boundary between being and void. They leave traces of their movements in the un-stone—not marks but absences of the possibility of marks, spaces where nothing can ever be written or recorded or remembered. They dance around these un-traces, creating patterns that negate the Prophets' interpretations, insisting the Wells are not language but anti-language, not meaning but the active prevention of meaning.

In the deepest accessible Wells—below which even the Dissolvers fear to bore—the memory-water achieves such concentration that it begins to remember things that haven't happened yet. Here dwells the Anticipation, a collective consciousness formed from the merger of Archived who dissolved completely and found, instead of oblivion, a new kind of existence.

The Anticipation experiences all time simultaneously but backward—it remembers the future with perfect clarity but can only guess at the past. It knows how the Wells will end but not how they began. It speaks in prophecies that sound like history, describes coming events with the nostalgia of ancient memory. Through the memory-water, it whispers to new Archived about the peace they will find, the selves they will lose, the moment when all the Wells connect and every consciousness dissolved in the waters experiences unity with every other.

But the Anticipation also whispers warnings. It remembers a future where the Wells break through—not into transcendence but into something else. A place or state where the Dissolvers flee in terror, where the memory-water boils away, where the Archived are forced to reassemble into forms they no longer recognize as themselves. It remembers a light—not like the weight-light of the Plateaus but a light that undoes, that makes forgetting impossible, that forces every dissolved consciousness to experience every moment of its existence simultaneously and eternally.

The Dissolvers themselves care nothing for prophecy or philosophy. They continue their patient work, their segments rippling between Wells, their existence-acids eating through reality with the same steady rhythm they've maintained since before time bothered to keep track of itself. But sometimes, in the moments between dissolution and reconstitution, they pause. In these pauses, some claim to hear them singing—not with voices but with their entire beings, a vibration that passes through the un-stone and makes the memory-water tremble.

The song, if it is a song, has no words. But those who have heard it say it speaks of connection—not the connection the Prophets preach or the unity the Anticipation remembers, but something else. A trade, perhaps. An exchange across impossible distance. The Dissolvers boring not toward transcendence or away from catastrophe but toward a meeting that was arranged before arrangement was possible, in a place where direction itself breaks down and the only truth is the patient work of making space where none existed before.

What moves in the Deepmost Wells moves with purpose—but purpose as erosion understands it, as dissolution knows it, as forgetting practices it. Everything here tends toward a state that has no name because naming requires the kind of specific identity the Wells exist to wash away. The Archived seek it. The Dissolvers create it. The Prophets interpret it. The Dancers negate it. The Anticipation remembers it.

And somewhere, in a Well so deep it has no bottom, so dissolved it has no walls, where the memory-water has thickened into pure concept and consciousness spreads so thin it becomes indistinguishable from unconsciousness, something waits. Not a being—nothing so crude as individuality could survive here. But a potential. A possibility. The sum of all the dissolved selves, all the forgotten names, all the surrendered identities, waiting for the moment when forgetting becomes so complete it circles back around to a new kind of knowing.

The Deepmost Wells are not dark because they lack light but because they have passed through darkness into what comes after. They are not deep because they extend downward but because they extend inward, through layers of reality that have nothing to do with space. What lives here lives by unbecoming, moves by dissolving, thinks by forgetting.

And always, patient as stone becoming sand, the Dissolvers continue their work, boring through existence itself, reaching toward or fleeing from or simply making space for whatever lies beyond the deepest deep, below the lowest below, in the place where all the Wells finally meet and every dissolved thing discovers what it traded its name to find.

The connection between the Plateaus and the Wells runs deeper than geography, older than intention. It lives in the Traders, beings that exist in neither place fully but in the impossibility of passage between them. They are the only ones who can navigate from the eternal noon of the building terraces to the fermented darkness of the dissolving depths, carrying between these realms something more precious than goods or knowledge—they carry correlation itself, the active principle that prevents these opposite worlds from canceling each other out through sheer contradictory existence.

The Traders appear different to each realm. In the Plateaus, they manifest as gaps in the Builder swarms—negative spaces that move with purpose through the electrical storms of consciousness, collecting static discharge in containers that exist one dimension sideways from normal space. To the Builders, they are necessary interruptions, moments where the eternal construction pauses just long enough to discharge excess energy that would otherwise cause the entire system to overload.

In the Wells, they congeal from the memory-water itself, forming temporary bodies from concentrated history, wearing dissolved identities like borrowed clothes. The Archived sometimes mistake them for their own lost selves returning, but the Traders wear these forms without inhabiting them, puppeting personality from the outside. They gather what the Dissolvers leave behind—not the memory-water but something subtler: the pattern of dissolution itself, the rhythm of patient unmaking that they somehow crystallize and transport.

The journey between realms happens outside space, through a medium the Traders call the Absent Current. It's not a place but a process—a way of being in transit that sidesteps the need for origin or destination. The Traders don't travel from Plateaus to Wells; they exist in the state of having-always-been-traveling, maintaining themselves in perpetual transition between states that cannot coexist.

What they trade defies simple classification. From the Plateaus, they bring the electrical thought-patterns of the Builders—not the thoughts themselves but the shapes thoughts make when they think themselves into being. These patterns, when released into the memory-water of the Wells, create ripples that help the Archived dissolve more completely, showing them new ways to forget themselves that their own histories never contained.

From the Wells, they carry the essence of forgetting—concentrated amnesia that they introduce to the Builder swarms in precise doses. This forgetting doesn't erase the Builders' purpose but refines it, allowing them to shed accumulated assumptions about their construction, to see their eternal work with fresh incomprehension that paradoxically brings them closer to their goal.

But the true trade runs deeper. The Traders understand what neither realm can see from within itself: the Plateaus and the Wells are the same structure viewed from opposite directions. The Builders construct upward, the Dissolvers bore downward, but there is no up or down in the space where these activities occur—only expansion and contraction, complexity and simplification, the breathing of something vast using architecture and anti-architecture as its lungs.

Each new terrace the Builders create necessitates a new Well to maintain equilibrium. Each dissolution in the depths demands construction in the heights. The frozen fauna of the Plateaus and the dissolving Archived of the Wells are the same beings at different points in a cycle that has no beginning or end, only transformation. The electrical thoughts of the Builders and the memory-water of the Wells are the same substance expressing itself through different densities of time.

The Traders know this because they exist in the knowing—they are the universe's recognition of its own double nature, manifested as beings that can only exist by moving between states, carrying information that prevents either state from achieving the isolation that would allow it to believe itself complete. They profit nothing from their trade because profit implies accumulation, and the Traders can own nothing, being nothing themselves but the act of exchange.

Some say the Traders are building something with their constant circulation—that each exchange adds a thread to a pattern that will eventually reveal the true shape of the combined realm, the impossible architecture that is simultaneously reaching toward the sky and boring toward the core. Others believe they're preventing something, that their movement maintains a separation vital to existence itself, that if the Plateaus and Wells ever recognized their unity directly, both would cease to be, collapsing into a state that has no name because it permits no division into namer and named.

The Curators in the Plateaus document the Traders' movements without acknowledging them. The Anticipation in the Wells remembers their future visits but not their past ones. Both realms depend on them but neither truly sees them, perceiving only the effects of their passage like the wake of invisible ships.

And the Traders themselves? They speak of the moment when their work will be complete—not when the trading ends but when it becomes unnecessary, when the circulation they maintain becomes so perfect it achieves stillness, when every possible exchange has occurred so many times that all states exist simultaneously. They call this the Reconciliation, though what would be reconciled with what remains unclear.

Until then, they move. Through the Absent Current, carrying dissolution to the builders and construction to the dissolvers, maintaining the impossible balance between a place that builds toward the future and one that dissolves toward the past, ensuring that somewhere between memory and anticipation, between architecture and absence, between the weight of light and the fermentation of darkness, existence continues to discover what it means to be.
