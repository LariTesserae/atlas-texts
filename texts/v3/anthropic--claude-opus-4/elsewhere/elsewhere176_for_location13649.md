Beyond the Azure Mountains, where the Vigilant Perch keep their starlit watch, lies the Screaming Desert of Forgotten Frames.

Here, the children who built N'Terra left their first attempts—worlds half-drawn, stories begun but never finished. The sand is composed of crushed celluloid and discarded sketches, each grain a moment that almost was. When the wind blows, you can hear the whispers of dialogue that was never spoken, see the flicker of scenes that were cut before completion.

The beings who dwell here are the Unrendered.

They exist in states of partial becoming—a warrior whose sword arm dissolves into pencil strokes, a queen whose crown is still a rough sketch floating above her fully-painted face, lovers whose embrace is caught between storyboard and screen. They move through their days in loops, acting out the fragments of stories they were meant to tell, waiting for an ending that will never come.

The Unrendered speak in subtitles that hang in the air, words appearing and fading like mirages. Some have learned to edit themselves, cutting from one location to another in jump-cuts, or rewinding painful moments until they blur into abstraction. The eldest among them, the Director, is nothing but a viewfinder and a voice, roaming the desert searching for the perfect shot that would complete them all.

Where N'Terra flows with the living waters of creation, the Screaming Desert preserves every abandoned possibility. The children who built these worlds visit sometimes, walking the paths between their finished creation and their forsaken drafts. They come to remember that every world is built on the bones of the worlds that came before, that even laughter is carved from silence.

The Unrendered do not resent their makers. They understand they are the necessary incompleteness that allows N'Terra to be whole. In the early morning, when the desert is still and the screaming stops, they can see the Azure Mountains in the distance, watch the Vigilant circles overhead, and know that they too are part of the greater story—the shadow that gives the light its meaning, the rough draft that makes the final version possible.

Sometimes, very rarely, an Unrendered completes themselves through sheer force of will and walks out of the desert into N'Terra proper. The Earthborn welcome them, for they understand that all beings are, in some sense, unfinished. The rivers carry them downstream, where children might glimpse them and remember that every world they build contains within it all the worlds they didn't.
