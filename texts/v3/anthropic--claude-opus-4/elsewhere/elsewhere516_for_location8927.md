Beyond the constant moisture of the Singing Terraces, where the last fingers of seeping water dry into mineral crusts on stone, the mountain changes its nature entirely. The rock here is ancient, worn smooth by millenia of wind into flowing forms that resemble frozen drapery. This is the Thermal Veil—a vertical expanse of metamorphic stone shot through with deep channels where the planet's heat rises from below.

The Veil earns its name from the shimmer of superheated air that pours continuously from these channels, creating a curtain of distortion that makes the rock face appear to ripple and wave. The stone itself radiates warmth that can be felt from fifty paces away. Unlike the sharp, young granite of the terraces, this rock has been folded and refolded by geological forces until it forms swooping curves and hollows, each surface polished to a dull gleam by the constant flow of hot air.

No water survives here. The vegetation stops abruptly where the heat begins, leaving a margin of bare stone between the two zones that sparkles with dried mineral deposits—the ghostly remains of countless failed attempts by the green world to advance into this thermal desert. The boundary is precise: on one side, dripping moss and ferns; on the other, stone so dry it rings when struck.

The channels themselves range from hair-thin cracks that whistle with escaping air to caverns large enough to swallow a house. They breathe with a rhythm tied to deep planetary processes—exhaling scorching drafts that can singe exposed skin, then inhaling cool air from above in a cycle that takes exactly forty-seven minutes. Local wildlife has evolved to match this breathing: thermal moths that ride the uprafts during exhalation and shelter in crevices during the inhale, their wings made of scales that reflect heat like tiny mirrors.

Crystalline formations crust the channel mouths where minerals carried by the thermal currents have precipitated out over centuries. These deposits form in angular, geometric patterns: cubic formations of salt, needle-like sprays of sulfur compounds, and strange helical structures of unknown composition that spiral up the rock face like frozen smoke. The crystals grow and sublime away with the breathing cycle, some formations appearing overnight only to evaporate by noon.

The heat creates its own weather. Rising air pulls cooler drafts from the side, generating constant wind patterns that have carved the softer rock into fantastic shapes. Natural bridges span between thermal columns. Alcoves deep enough to shelter in—if one could tolerate the temperature—pocket the cliff face. The entire Veil hums with the movement of air through these formations, a deep thrumming that provides a bass note to the higher chaos of the Singing Terraces.

At night, the temperature differential becomes extreme. The rock, which glows dull red in the darkness from its retained heat, creates such powerful updrafts that small tornadoes of superheated air spiral up the cliff face. These thermal devils carry dust and small debris hundreds of feet into the air, where it cools and falls like ash on the terraces below. The boundary between the two zones becomes a wall of turbulence, impossible to cross.

The Thermal Weavers are the Chorus Weavers' nearest neighbors and most distant relatives. Where the Chorus Weavers stretched tall and thin, the Thermal Weavers compressed—their bodies low and broad, with skin that has thickened into overlapping plates like a pangolin's armor. These plates are not merely protective; they're thermal regulators, each one capable of angling independently to either trap or release heat.

Their evolution has been ruthless. Sweat glands transformed into wax-producing organs that coat their skin with a heat-resistant sheen. Their eyes, protected by transparent scales that they can blink down like inner eyelids, have adjusted to see clearly through the heat distortion that makes their world a constant shimmer. Their nasal passages lengthened and coiled to cool air before it reaches their lungs. Even their hair changed—becoming hollow tubes that circulate air for cooling, giving them the appearance of wearing elaborate copper headdresses that whistle softly in the thermal drafts.

They build not with vegetation but with the crystal deposits themselves. Through patient cultivation—adding mineral solutions at precise temperatures, seeding formations with catalytic compounds—they grow their architecture directly from the rock. Their dwellings are crystalline shells that regulate temperature through their geometric structure, channeling hot air up and out while drawing cooler air from carefully positioned intakes. The interiors gleam with salt-crystal windows that filter the harsh light into rainbows.

The Thermal Weavers' relationship with heat is intimate and practical. They cook by placing food directly on heated rock surfaces. They distill water from the air using condensation chambers that exploit the temperature differential between day and night. Their children learn to read the thermal patterns like the Chorus Weavers read sound—sensing the invisible currents of hot and cold, finding the safe paths through the breathing channels, knowing which crystals will sublime away and which will endure.

The connection between these two peoples is the Margin—that strip of neutral stone where neither water nor heat holds dominion. Here, twice each year during the seasonal transitions, when atmospheric conditions create a perfect balance, the two groups meet for the Exchange.

The Margin transforms for these occasions. The Chorus Weavers bring acoustic dampeners—woven chambers that create pockets of relative quiet. The Thermal Weavers construct temporary crystal shields that block the worst of the heat from spilling over. In this tempered zone, neither truly comfortable for either people, they trade.

The Chorus Weavers need the minerals—salts for preserving food, sulfur compounds for treating the fungal infections that plague their damp homes, and most precious of all, the helical crystals whose resonant properties allow them to create their finest sound-maps. The Thermal Weavers crave the organic materials their realm cannot produce—fibers for weaving heat-resistant cloth, oils from the acoustic beetles that lubricate their tools against the corrosive mineral dust, and the moth silk that, when treated with their wax, creates incredibly strong construction material.

But the Exchange is more than commerce. Young people from both tribes undergo the Walking—a ritual journey where Chorus adolescents must cross the Thermal Veil during its night cycle, while Thermal youth must spend three days in the cacophony of the Singing Terraces. Those who complete the journey gain profound respect and, more practically, the ability to serve as translators and mediators between the two peoples.

The two cultures have developed a hybrid language for these encounters—neither the flowing sign language of the Chorus Weavers nor the precise thermal patterns the Thermal Weavers use to communicate across the heat distortion. Instead, they use positioned stones that can be seen from both sides, arranged in patterns that convey basic concepts. Complex negotiations happen through intermediaries who have completed multiple Walkings and can survive extended periods in either realm.

Over generations, this exchange has created dependencies that run deeper than simple trade. Chorus Weavers have learned to predict weather patterns by watching the Thermal Veil's breathing cycles. Thermal Weavers time their architectural projects by the acoustic patterns that drift across from the terraces, knowing that certain beetle choruses herald the mineral-rich winds they need for crystal cultivation.

Intermarriage, though rare, does occur. The children of such unions often struggle to survive in either extreme but sometimes manifest unique adaptations—the ability to hear ultrasonic frequencies useful for navigating the heat distortion, or skin that can photosynthesize while still regulating temperature. These individuals often become permanent residents of the Margin, building hybrid structures of trained vine and cultivated crystal, serving as the living bridges between two worlds that share a mountain but might as well exist on different planets.

The mountain itself is indifferent to these divisions. Deep below, geological forces continue their slow work. The same uplift that created the Singing Terraces is gradually tilting the Thermal Veil, opening new channels while sealing others. In a thousand years, perhaps the two zones will merge, or perhaps they'll separate entirely. For now, the Chorus and Thermal Weavers continue their parallel existence—one drowning in sound and green growth, the other baking in silence and crystal precision, both claiming their piece of the vertical world, both dependent on the narrow margin where stone remembers being simply stone.
