The Crystalline Pools of the Departed

Seven days' journey through the whistling canyons, where the land rises from humid gorge to wind-scoured plateau, the stone turns from black basalt to pale limestone shot through with veins of quartz. Here, where no soil clings long enough to nurture even the hardiest shrub, where rain falls but twice a year in torrential curtains that vanish into the porous rock within hours, sprawls a different kind of settlement entirely.

The pools announce themselves first as a wrongness in the air—too still when the plateau winds should be scouring, too thick with mineral scent when nothing should grow. Then the ground drops away into a vast sinkhole, perhaps half a mile across, its walls descending in perfect terraces carved by no tool but time and water and the slow accumulation of purpose. At each level, pools of impossible clarity hold water that should not exist in this drought-land, each surface mirror-perfect, each reflecting not the sky above but something else entirely.

The inhabitants here have taken the opposite path from their fungal cousins in the gorges. Where the Night Feeders merged with the soft and breathing, these beings—who call themselves the Clarified—have embraced the mineral, the crystalline, the eternally still. They move through their city of pools like living prisms, their flesh transformed through decades of immersion into something between meat and stone.

The transformation begins at the outermost pools, where newcomers first lower themselves into water so dense with dissolved minerals it bears weight like mercury. These apprentice pools sting and burn, depositing their cargo of salts into every pore. Bathers emerge crusted white, moving stiffly as their skin learns new configurations. They must return daily, hourly, letting the minerals build in careful layers while their bodies adapt or reject the change entirely.

Deeper pools hold different treasures, different agonies. The iron pools stain their bathers rust-red and grant a terrible vitality—hearts that beat once per minute but with the force of hammers. The copper pools turn skin green-black and gift an affinity for electricity, letting their swimmers taste lightning in the air hours before storms arrive. The deepest pools, those closest to the center, contain waters so saturated with rare earths that they glow with their own light, and those who dare their embrace emerge (if they emerge at all) with bodies like living opals, every movement casting rainbow refractions.

The Clarified build nothing. The pools themselves are architecture enough—carved by patient centuries of mineral deposition into perfect geometries. Ledges of flowstone provide sleeping platforms. Curtains of deposited calcium create privacy screens translucent as aged glass. The mineral-adapted bodies need little shelter; they neither sweat nor shiver, their crystalline skin proof against temperature extremes that would kill softer flesh.

They feed on the pools themselves. Different waters carry different nutrients—sulfurs that burn the throat but grant energy, seleniums that sharpen vision until the bathers can see heat itself, calciums that strengthen already-hardening bones. The eldest have passed beyond the need for any sustenance but mineral-rich water, their metabolisms slowed to near-geological time. They sit at pool-edges for months without moving, their bodies' boundaries blurring as stone deposits claim them layer by layer.

The economy between gorge and plateau runs on water and wisdom. Twice yearly, when the brief rains come, teams of the youngest Clarified (those still flexible enough for swift movement) journey to the Hanging Gardens. They bring rain-gifts: barrels of pure water untouched by minerals, worth more than gold in the ever-humid fungal city. In exchange, they carry back dried foods, soft fibers, and most importantly, messages from the root-network translated into crystalline memory.

The Night Feeders discovered long ago that certain rare minerals, when introduced to their neural fungi, create brief windows of startling clarity—visions that pierce through time, mathematical certainties that resolve generations-old debates, moments of pure understanding that transcend their usual dream-slow thoughts. The Clarified, in turn, hunger for the organic wisdom of the root-network, the soft knowledge of growth and change that their crystallizing brains increasingly struggle to grasp.

The journey between settlements is undertaken by specialists from both sides. The Night Feeders send their Runners—those least changed, who can still tolerate direct sunlight and dry air for days at a time. They travel at night when possible, their moss-cloaks heavy with moisture, following ancient paths marked by standing stones and seasonal streams. The Clarified send their Mercuries—young ones treated with quicksilver pools who move with liquid grace despite their mineral weight, their silver-bright skin reflecting heat away.

These meetings at the borderlands between biomes have evolved their own rituals. The traders gather at neutral springs, places where clean water bubbles up uncontaminated by either fungus or mineral. They spend days in careful exchange—not just of goods but of essence. The Runners offer spore-packets that carry compressed memories from the root-network. The Mercuries provide crystal slivers that hold mathematical truths discovered in meditation. Neither fully understands what they trade, only that their home settlements crave these alien wisdoms.

More than trade goods pass between the communities. Sometimes a Night Feeder, grown weary of the soft darkness, will beg passage to the pools. Sometimes one of the Clarified, joints grinding with mineral deposits, will hobble towards the promise of yielding earth and gentle decomposition. These exchanges are honored by both sides—each death-seeking pilgrim carries messages, last gifts, and the blessing of their abandoned community.

The deep connections run stranger still. The oldest stories, crystallized in mineral memory and encoded in root-whispers, speak of a time before the split. Once, they say, there was only one people, dwelling in a middle place where water ran clear and trees grew under open sky. Some schism—philosophical, ecological, or divine—drove them apart to opposite extremes. Now they exist as opposite poles of the same yearning: for permanence, for connection, for transformation into something beyond mere human flesh.

In the deepest pool at the sinkhole's heart, where waters have concentrated minerals unknown to surface chemistry, sits a figure so transformed they seem carved from a single crystal. This is the Oracle of Clarification, who has not moved in seventy years, who speaks perhaps once per season in words that ring like struck quartz. The Oracle's eyes, when they rarely open, reflect not light but something else—visions from the root-network, messages from their fungal twin who grows at the heart of the Mother Grove, two aspects of the same divided being sending dreams across the hostile miles.

The Night Feeders know this truth through the slow wisdom of their networks. The Clarified perceive it in the mathematical precision of crystal growth. They are one people still, exploring opposite extremes of the same transformation. In the fungi, consciousness expands and softens until individual boundaries dissolve into collective dream. In the minerals, awareness compresses and clarifies until each thought rings eternal and unchanging as stone.

Between them flows water—the rain that feeds both gorge and pool, the medium of all change, the only thing that moves freely between both realms. Water carries minerals down to feed the fungal gardens. Water bears spores up to seed new pools with primitive life. Water is the messenger, the merchant, the promise that these opposite transformations are temporary stations on a longer journey.

The traders know this best. Moving between worlds, they taste both transformations without committing to either. In their travels they map a middle way—still human enough to walk under sky, already changed enough to survive both fungal dream and mineral clarity. Perhaps they are the true inheritors, these mercury-bright runners and moss-cloaked messengers, carrying synthesis in their hybrid flesh.

In certain seasons, when the rains fall heavy and the high streams run full, water courses all the way from the pools to the gardens. For a few precious weeks, the two realms touch. Mineral-rich streams feed new growths in the fungal city—crystalline fruits that grant visions of mathematical precision. Spore-thick runoff pools in limestone hollows above, seeding new colonies that filter and clarify the waters below. The worlds whisper to each other in chemical languages older than human speech.

This is how evolution works—not through single changes but through dialogue, through opposite experiments that inform each other across hostile distances. The Night Feeders sink into soft community. The Clarified rise into hard isolation. Between them, carried by water and trade and the endless exchange of transformed pilgrims, grows something new—a synthesis yet to be born, a transformation that embraces both fungal dream and crystal clarity.

The world waits, patient as stone, yielding as loam, while its divergent children slowly spiral back towards union, carrying their opposite wisdoms home.
