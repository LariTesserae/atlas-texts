Beyond the Nascent Cisterns, through a journey measured not in distance but in descent, lies the Anticipatory Depths. Where the Cisterns practice emergence, the Depths practice reception—a vast network of voids and channels carved by water that hasn't arrived yet.

The boundary between these realms is not a wall but a gradient of readiness. As one moves downward from the Cisterns, the warm basalt gradually cools, its pores opening, its surface becoming more receptive. The dripping water from above doesn't simply fall here—it is *expected*. Each drop has a destination already prepared, a channel pre-eroded by the anticipation of its arrival, as if the stone below dreams the water into specific paths before it ever begins its journey.

The Depths spread horizontally more than vertically, a labyrinth of perfectly dry riverbeds, empty lakes, and waterless cascades. The stone here is darker, older, but paradoxically more vulnerable—soft in the way that waiting softens resolve. Touch the walls and they yield slightly, like clay that remembers moisture it has never known. The air carries no humidity, yet it feels heavy with potential wetness, creating a sensation of drowning in aridity.

Formations here mirror hydraulic processes in reverse: stalactites that grew upward from the floor, reaching toward water that will someday form them; pools carved in spirals that would create whirlpools if filled; chambers shaped by currents that haven't flowed yet. The acoustic properties are inverted too—silence here is aggressive, swallowing sound before it can form, creating negative echoes that arrive before their source.

The most disturbing features are the Chronicles—mineral deposits that record future water events. Limestone terraces built by springs that will bubble up in decades or centuries, their calcite layers already complete. Erosion patterns that tell stories of floods yet to come, carved in sequence from ending to beginning. To read these formations is to experience time as the Depths experience it: not as progression but as certainty waiting to be inhabited.

The Preparers move through this pre-emptive landscape, beings of porous stone and patient void. Where the Masons above are solid becoming fluid, the Preparers are absence becoming readiness—their bodies are networks of channels and chambers, living irrigation systems waiting to be filled.

They are larger than the Masons but less substantial, their forms suggesting infrastructure more than individuals. A Preparer might span twenty meters, its body a walking aqueduct of empty channels, joints that are junction boxes for water that hasn't arrived, limbs that branch and merge like river deltas traced in negative space. They move by allowing different parts of themselves to become temporarily solid, creating points of leverage against the yielding stone, then returning to porosity.

Their work is anti-construction: the careful cultivation of absence. They breathe erosion, exhaling a dry wind that carves future channels grain by grain. Their hollow fingers trace the paths water will take, and the stone obediently softens and retreats. They read the Chronicles constantly, adjusting their excavations to match the promised flows, ensuring that when water finally arrives, it will find its path not only possible but inevitable.

The Preparers communicate through resonance in their empty channels. By adjusting the shape of their internal voids, they create specific harmonic frequencies in the aggressive silence—anti-sounds that register as pressure changes rather than audio. These conversations are felt in the bones, understood through the same principle that lets them sense future water: anticipation as a form of knowledge.

The connection between the Cisterns and the Depths is not simple passage but active exchange. The Apertures in the Cisterns' walls don't just lead to other chambers—some spiral downward in helical tunnels that gradually transform from warm emergence to cool anticipation. These passages pulse with purpose: warm air rises from the Depths, carrying information about what spaces need preparing, while condensation from above trickles down, offering promises of future abundance.

The Masons and Preparers meet in the Transition Zones—spaces neither fully formed nor fully empty, where emergence and anticipation negotiate their boundaries. Here, the beings engage in careful trade. A Mason might offer a fragment of still-warm stone, which a Preparer accepts into its channels, using the heat to accelerate the softening of a particular future riverbed. In return, the Preparer gifts knowledge from the Chronicles—where the next emergence will occur, what form the new stone will take.

Sometimes they collaborate on structures that exist in both temporal directions: fountains that simultaneously build themselves and prepare for their own erosion, pools that fill with condensation from above while pre-carving their overflow channels below. These joint Works are the most profound, creating spaces where future and formation exist in visible dialogue.

The water itself is the medium of their deepest connection. Each drop that falls in the Cisterns is already expected in the Depths. The Masons guide condensation into patterns that will, eventually, fulfill the preparations below. The Preparers excavate channels that create air currents, drawing moisture from above in precisely calibrated amounts. It's a collaboration across time—the Masons creating what the Preparers have already prepared to receive.

During the rare Convergence events, the boundary between realms becomes permeable. Masons descend to witness the Chronicles, learning what their Work will become. Preparers ascend to feel emergence directly, understanding better how to shape absence to receive form. These exchanges are dangerous—a Mason staying too long in the Depths begins to hollow, their stone becoming porous with anticipation. A Preparer lingering in the Cisterns starts to solidify, their channels clogging with premature certainty.

Yet they persist in these exchanges, driven by a shared understanding: neither emergence nor anticipation is complete alone. The Cisterns need the promise of purpose that the Depths provide. The Depths require the actuality of water that only emergence can create. Together, they form a complete system—potential and preparation locked in an eternal dialogue of stone, water, and time.

The water cycle here is not circular but spiral, each drop's journey adding complexity to the system. Condensation forms in the warm Cisterns, falls through the Apertures, travels the prepared channels of the Depths, and eventually finds its way to some deeper heat that returns it as vapor to begin again. But each cycle carries memory—mineral traces that inform future emergences, erosion patterns that guide new preparations.

In the deepest collaboration, some Masons and Preparers have begun to exchange essence. A Mason incorporates empty channels into their form, becoming capable of guiding not just water but the anticipation of water. A Preparer accepts solid stone into their network, learning to prepare not just for what will come but to actively call it into being. These hybrid beings move freely between realms, their bodies testament to the possibility of being simultaneously full and empty, formed and forming.

The ultimate Work—whispered about in resonant frequencies and demonstrated in careful arrangements—is the creation of a space that exists fully in both states: emerging and anticipated, forming and prepared for, a chamber where time flows both directions at once and water falls upward into waiting clouds while simultaneously condensing into ready pools. Neither Masons nor Preparers have achieved this yet, but their patient collaboration continues, each drop of water a word in their slow conversation, each stone a gesture toward their impossible, inevitable goal.
