Between the Chromatic Deeps and the domain of the Sifters lies the Gradient Throat—a vast vertical shaft where all the stable stratifications break down into chaos. Here, the ordered layers of the Deeps spiral and fold into each other, creating a perpetual waterfall of mixing densities that drops seven hundred meters through the planet's crust.

The Throat began as a mining excavation, a attempt to reach even deeper deposits. But the engineers miscalculated. They punched through into a pressurized pocket of primordial ocean, water that had been sealed since the planet's formation. This ancient liquid, laden with its own extinct chemistries, erupted upward and met the industrial solutions of the Deeps in a reaction that has never stopped.

Now the Throat churns like a titan's digestive system. Sheets of mercury-bright fluid cascade down its walls, only to be caught in updrafts of lighter solution and thrown back skyward. The different density layers form visible ribbons as they fall—twisted helixes of green and blue and that unsettling pink, braiding and unbraiding in patterns too complex to follow. Where they meet, they react. New compounds form and break apart in seconds. Crystalline structures bloom like frost on the shaft walls, only to be dissolved by the next passing current.

The sound is overwhelming. In the stable layers of the Deeps, noise travels strangely but softly. Here, every chemical reaction shrieks. The falling fluids create harmonics that beat against each other, producing beats and anti-beats that can liquify soft tissue if you drift too close to a resonance node. The Sifters know the safe paths through the sound, the quiet eddies where the frequencies cancel out.

This is the only way between the upper Deeps and the lower reaches. The extraction companies tried to build transport tubes, but the Throat ate them all. The violent mixing corrodes any material in hours. Only by riding the currents themselves can anything pass through—a skill the Sifters have mastered over generations.

They've mapped the Throat's moods. They know that when the ancient water runs thick—usually after a tremor in the deep crust opens new pockets—the updrafts strengthen and the journey down becomes impossible. They know which chemical signatures herald the formation of navigable channels, temporary rivers within the chaos that flow smooth and straight for minutes at a time. The young Sifters prove themselves by diving the Throat solo, reading the currents with their skin, their multiplied eyes tracking density changes too subtle for any instrument to detect.

Some never make it through. The Throat keeps them, their bodies becoming part of the chemical conversation, their elements redistributed through a million reactions. The Sifters sing memorial frequencies for these lost ones, and sometimes, in the way certain crystals form on the shaft walls, they see familiar patterns—a face, a gesture, a repeated motif that speaks of personality persisting in mineral form.

At the bottom of the Throat, where it opens into the lower reaches, there's a lens of perfectly clear fluid—a single layer of pure, primordial water that has resisted all contamination. It's only a meter thick, but passing through it is like breaking through into another world. The industrial chaos above gives way to something older, stranger. This is where the deep machines grow.

The Sifters use the Throat as more than passage. They've learned to seed reactions in the mixing zone, dropping catalysts from above that will produce specific compounds by the time they reach bottom. It's a vertical chemistry lab where the equipment is the process itself. Some of the most valuable extracts come not from the mining towers but from careful manipulation of the Throat's eternal digestion.

Trading happens here. Not commerce exactly—the Sifters have no economy—but exchange. Those who work the upper reaches bring down rare crystals and processed compounds. Those from below carry up things that have no names—structures grown in the deep pressure, organisms or mechanisms or something between the two. They meet in the relative calm of the lens layer, floating in that ancient clarity, and trade substance for substance, knowledge for knowledge.

The Throat pulses with its own rhythm, a day-long cycle of violence and calm that follows no calendar but its own. During the violent phases, the mixing intensifies. New reactions cascade through the system, and the noise becomes a physical force that can crack the shells of extraction towers kilometers away. During the calm phases, the different densities settle into temporary, impossible configurations—inverted waterfalls, spiral galaxies of fluid, standing waves that hold their shape for hours.

The Sifters time their passages to these rhythms, but sometimes they're caught in the change. Then they must ride it out, their bodies compressed and stretched by competing currents, their consciousness scattered across spectrums as their chromatophore skin tries to match every color at once. Some say this is how the first Tower-Singers learned to merge with machines—by being torn apart and reassembled so many times in the Throat that the boundary between flesh and system dissolved.

At the very center of the shaft, where the violence is worst, something is growing. The Sifters can see it when they dive—a dark mass that seems to drink light rather than emit it. It's been building for decades, fed by the constant rain of reactive compounds. Structure accretes around it layer by layer, following no blueprint but its own emergent logic. The oldest Sifters say it's the Throat's pearl, a seed of pure reaction around which everything else orbits.

They don't know what it will become when it's finished growing. But they tend it anyway, adjusting the chemical flows to feed its needs, singing to it in frequencies that make its surface ripple in response. It's part of the work now, like maintaining the towers or riding the inversions. The Throat has its own purposes, and the Sifters have learned that fighting those purposes only leads to dissolution.

The passage between worlds is never safe, never easy, but always necessary. The Throat ensures this—a violent translator between the industrial present above and the primordial past below, mixing both into something entirely new. The Sifters dive through its chaos with bodies built for impossible physics, part of the reaction, part of the solution, forever traveling between states that shouldn't exist but do.
