The Circulation Depths

Three thousand feet below the lowest terrace mill, where the water-medium thickens to the consistency of cooling glass, the Circulation Depths begin. Here, the orderly hexagons give way to something organic and ancient—vast caverns of fossilized flesh that once belonged to creatures larger than cities. The walls are ribboned with petrified muscle fiber, each strand the width of a subway tunnel, all oriented along flow-lines that channel the dense medium in planet-girdling currents.

This is where the water-medium is born. Not created—nothing here is ever created or destroyed—but transformed, reconstituted, given new purpose. The cavern walls weep with secretions from glands that still function after geological ages, adding their chemical signatures to the flow. Specialized bacteria, if they can be called that, bloom in clouds of metallic green and silver, breaking down the old medium and rebuilding it molecule by molecule.

The Depth Weavers make their home here, and they are nothing like the quicksilver Millkeepers above. Where those are liquid and responsive, the Weavers are solid and contemplative—beings of living stone shaped like starfish the size of houses, their surfaces pocked with thousands of specialized openings. They position themselves at the convergence of currents, arms spread wide to taste the chemical gradients of the entire world-ocean.

Each Weaver is a library of deep time. Their silicon-based neurons process information at the speed of erosion, thoughts taking centuries to complete. They remember when the great beasts whose bodies became these caverns still lived, swimming through younger waters under a different kind of light. They remember the first machines, crude things that nonetheless carried the seed of all that would come. Most importantly, they remember the Purpose—though none who ask them can wait long enough for the answer.

Through their bodies flow tubes of living crystal that connect to the fossilized circulatory systems of the cavern walls. The Weavers tend these connections with infinite care, ensuring that the chemical messages encoded in the water-medium reach their destinations unchanged. They are the network administrators of a system older than continents, maintaining protocols written in protein and mineral.

The Depths are never silent. The density of the medium here carries sound differently—low frequencies travel for thousands of miles, creating a constant rumble that the Weavers modulate into slow, deep songs. These songs coordinate the global circulation, telling the currents where to flow, instructing distant bacteria when to bloom, calling up minerals from the planetary core. A single note might take a year to sing and another year to be answered.

Parasites inevitably evolved in such a rich environment. The Thread Pirates are the most successful—colonies of fiber-thin organisms that tap into the crystal tubes, siphoning off chemical messages and metabolizing their information content. They reproduce the stolen data imperfectly, creating mutations in the global communications network. The Weavers hunt them with specialized symbiotes, but some Pirates always survive, adding noise to the signal, introducing chaos into the order.

More welcome are the Beacon Blooms—stationary organisms that anchor themselves to the cavern ceilings and glow with bioluminescent patterns. Each colony develops its own unique signature, creating navigation markers that remain stable for millennia. Young Weavers learn to read these constellations, using them to find their way through the labyrinthine Depths. Elder Weavers barely notice them anymore, navigating by senses that have no names in quicker languages.

The Drift Monks arrived only recently—within the last thousand years. They are refugees or pilgrims from somewhere else, beings of crystallized time who move by allowing themselves to be carried on the deep currents. Their bodies are transparent columns filled with swirling chronologies—past and future events visible as golden threads within their forms. They speak to no one, but their presence disturbs the Weavers, who sense in them an ending or a beginning that has yet to arrive.

At the deepest point of the Depths, where the pressure would crush any lesser material, stands the Heart Chamber. Here, a machine older than memory performs the Great Circulation—drawing in exhausted medium from across the world and returning it renewed. The machine is the size of a small moon, its components forged from metals that shouldn't exist, operating on principles that violate several laws of physics as understood elsewhere.

The Weavers maintain a constant vigil around the Heart Chamber. They believe—if their geological pondering can be called belief—that it is not a machine at all but a sleeping god, one whose dreams manifest as the circulation that sustains all life above and below. They fear what would happen if it woke, but fear more what would happen if it stopped dreaming.

The relationship between the Depths and the terrace mills above is one of symbiosis stretched across vertical miles. The mills process and refine what the Depths circulate. The Millkeepers' careful optimization ultimately serves the Weavers' vast patterns. Chemical signals born in the deepest caverns eventually manifest as tiny adjustments in polymer production far above. The synthetic kelp of the mills releases compounds that, years later, will reach the Depths to trigger new cycles of bacterial bloom.

But while the Millkeepers concern themselves with perfection and efficiency, the Weavers think in terms of survival and purpose. They know that the system they maintain is not eternal. The fossilized caverns are slowly collapsing, compressed by the weight of miles of water-medium above. The Heart Chamber's song grows discordant, missing notes that once rang clear. The Thread Pirates multiply faster than they can be controlled, corrupting more of each year's messages.

Most disturbing to the Weavers are the new currents they've detected—warm upwellings from depths even they cannot reach, carrying chemical signatures unlike anything in their geological memories. These currents speak of change, of transformation, of an evolution in the very nature of the water-medium itself. Some Weavers position themselves to study these flows. Others actively work to suppress them, redirecting the currents back into familiar patterns.

The youngest Weaver, barely ten thousand years old, has proposed something radical: communication with the Millkeepers above. Not the simple chemical signals that already pass between the layers, but true dialogue. It has spent centuries learning to accelerate its thoughts, compressing years of cognition into mere months. It believes the Millkeepers, with their quicksilver minds, might hold keys to understanding the changes approaching from below.

The elders consider this proposition with their customary patience, a debate that will outlast civilizations. In the meantime, the Circulation Depths continue their ancient work: processing the medium that processing the world, maintaining the channels that maintain existence, dreaming the dreams that keep the god asleep and the cosmos in motion.

And in the deepest trenches, where even the Weavers fear to anchor, something new is stirring—not life as it has been known, but life as it might become, rising on those strange warm currents toward an appointment with evolution that has been eons in the making.
