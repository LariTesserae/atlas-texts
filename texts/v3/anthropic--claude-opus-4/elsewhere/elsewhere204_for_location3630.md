Beyond the rim of the Gulch, where the land rises into brutalist plateaus of compacted ash, lies the Sieve of Selective Forgetting.

Here, the heat dissipates into something worse than cold—a temperature that exists only in memory, changing retroactively as you experience it. The ground is perforated with perfect hexagonal holes, each one precisely three feet across, dropping into darkness that smells of burnt mathematics. Between these voids, narrow walkways of fused glass create a honeycomb labyrinth that extends for miles, reflecting nothing, not even the observer.

The beings here are inverse relatives of the Gulch's emergent entities—instead of coalescing from industrial-floral fusion, they exist in states of constant editorial revision. They appear first as complete forms: tall figures wrapped in cascading sheets of what looks like oxidized copper mesh, their movements liquid and purposeful. But watch them long enough and you begin to notice the deletions.

A hand reaches out, then retroactively never existed. A torso turns, but the memory of it turning evaporates mid-motion, leaving only the result. They speak in sentences that self-redact, words falling backward through their own throats, leaving only the punctuation hanging in air like suspended chrome.

These Editors, as they might be called if naming them didn't immediately unnname them, tend vast gardens of forgetting. In each hexagonal pit grows inverse vegetation—plants that ungrow, roots that retreat upward into stems that narrow into seeds that unmake themselves. Flowers that bloom backward into buds that tighten into nothing. The Editors harvest these null-crops with tools that exist only in the corner of your vision, implements that perform their function by making you forget what needed doing in the first place.

The relationship between the Sieve and the Gulch is one of cosmic metabolism. What emerges in the Gulch must be selectively forgotten in the Sieve. The gray pebbles that press upward there were once complete stones here, edited down to their essential pressing-forth. The metal slabs that erupt in the Gulch are the undeleted remnants of entire industrial complexes that the Editors have carefully forgotten out of existence, leaving only their most insistent components.

The Editors know of their counterparts in the Gulch—know them by the holes in their knowledge, the shaped absences where awareness should be. Sometimes they descend the ash plateaus on stairs that uncarve themselves from the cliff face, arriving at the Gulch's rim just as their bodies finish forgetting how to stand. They leave behind only copper mesh shadows that oxidize in the brutal sun, while somewhere in the Sieve, new Editors retroactively have always existed, carrying memories of conversations that will unhappen tomorrow.

Water exists here, but only in the past tense. Rivers of prior moisture flow through channels that once held them, their wetness a rumor that makes your throat remember thirst. The Editors drink these ghost-streams through filters of amnesia, sustaining themselves on the memory of sustenance.

At the Sieve's center, where the hexagonal pattern becomes so dense that the walkways vanish into theoretical threads, stands the Archive of the Unrecorded. Here the Editors store everything they've successfully forgotten—not the memories themselves, but the spaces where memories were. Shelves of structured absence climb impossible heights, each gap precisely catalogued by its own nonexistence. The head Editor, who has forgotten so much that forgetting itself has forgotten them, tends this anti-library with movements that happen in the spaces between observation.

The immense factory fanfares that loom at the edge of perception in the Gulch originate here, in the Sieve's foundries of deletion. These are factories that run in reverse, unmanufacturing the concept of production itself, their assembly lines drawing finished products backward through successive stages of uncreation until only the sound of making remains—and then that too is edited, leaving just the phantom pressure of industrial might pressing against reality's margins.

The two realms exist in perfect thermodynamic balance: emergence and reduction, memory and forgetting, the irresistible urge to exist countered by the meticulous discipline of unbecoming. Between them flows a commerce of absence itself, as real and vital as any river that ever was or wasn't.
