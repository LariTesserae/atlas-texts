## The Underneath

Below the root-floor matrix, beneath the wooden continent that claims the surface, another world persists in absolute darkness. Here, the Original Ground remains—the planet's true surface, buried under countless ages of growth, where the foundations of the first factories still stand.

The darkness here is not empty but thick, almost textile in quality. It presses against visitors with physical weight, a medium composed of settled spores, industrial particulates, and the shed cells of the plant above. The air tastes of rust and mycelium, moves like syrup in the lungs. No light has touched these depths since the plant achieved total coverage overhead. The only illumination comes from those who descend here, and their lights seem diminished, swallowed by the pressing black.

The temperature never varies—cooler than the greenhouse warmth above, but still several degrees above what the planet's original climate would suggest. Heat seeps down through the root matrix, along with stranger energies. The industrial drone is muffled here but ever-present, felt through the ground as vibration rather than heard as sound. Sometimes it peaks in intensity, and the metal structures resonate in sympathy, creating localized earthquakes of harmonics.

The landscape beneath defies mapping. Factory floors stretch into the darkness, their assembly lines buried under meters of decomposed organic matter that has hardened into something like coal but still warm to the touch. Structural steel beams, some thick as redwoods, support the unimaginable weight above. They've been reinforced over time by root systems that wrap around them like muscular tentacles, the plant having learned to use human architecture as skeleton for its own growth.

Entire cities exist down here, compressed but not crushed. The buildings lean at angles that speak of gradual settling rather than catastrophic collapse. Streets run between structures like canyon floors, their pavement cracked by roots but still navigable. The windows are long gone, replaced by membranes of hardened plant matter that occasionally pulse with bioluminescent traces—messages from above, or perhaps autonomous responses to stimuli in the depths.

In these buried metropolises, infrastructure persists in transformed states. Subway tunnels have become arteries carrying the plant's fluids, the trains within them calcified into strange fossils that occasionally shudder with peristaltic motion. Power stations continue to generate electricity through processes no longer entirely mechanical—their turbines spin not with steam but with the passage of thick, nutrient-rich saps. The electrical grid has merged with the plant's own bioelectric network, creating hybrid systems that power both mechanical pumps and biological processes.

Water exists here, gathered in vast underground reservoirs where it has taken on properties unknown to surface chemistry. It glows faintly when disturbed, supports forms of life that exist nowhere else—things that might once have been industrial bacteria, evolved in the presence of petroleum products and plant secretions into organisms that metabolize complex hydrocarbons and emit pure oxygen. These reservoir ecosystems process the plant's waste, creating a chemical cycle that helps sustain the biomass above.

The deepest foundations reveal the planet's original mining operations—shafts that descend beyond even the plant's reach. Here, automated systems continue extraction processes begun centuries ago, though what they mine has changed. The planet's crust has been altered by the plant's chemistry, creating new minerals that grow like crystals in the deep heat. Mining machines, adapted by some unknown intelligence, harvest these formations and send them upward through root channels, feeding the plant's exotic metabolism.

Between the buried cities run tunnel networks of unclear origin. Some are clearly human-made—maintenance passages and utility corridors. Others seem to have been carved by the plant itself, or perhaps by things that live within it. These passages create a connective tissue between isolated underground spaces, allowing movement through the darkness for those who know the ways. The tunnels breathe with their own rhythm, air moving in tidal flows that suggest the entire underground functions as a single respiratory system.

## The Dwellers Below

In this eternal darkness, evolution has taken desperate turns.

The **Extractors** tend the deepest machines. They've lost their eyes entirely, the organs sealed over with skin that's developed extreme sensitivity to vibration. They navigate by echolocation, producing clicks with modified vocal cords that can penetrate the thick air. Their hands have evolved additional joints and a covering of cilia that let them feel electromagnetic fields, essential for maintaining the mining equipment that still functions in the depths. They understand machinery through touch alone, diagnosing problems by the subtle variations in metal temperature and vibration frequency. Their society is built around the maintenance schedules of equipment that has run for generations, their mythology centered on the "First Operators" who began the work they continue. They feed on a paste they create from certain minerals and the bioluminescent bacteria in the water, their digestive systems adapted to process what surface life would find toxic.

The **Pale Ones** inhabit the buried cities. Their skin has become translucent from generations without sun, showing the modified vascular systems beneath that pulse with stolen plant nutrients. They've developed a parasitic relationship with the root systems, using specialized organs in their palms to tap into nutrient flows. Their culture revolves around architectural knowledge—they maintain mental maps of the compressed cities, knowing which buildings will stand for another century and which will soon be claimed entirely by the growth above. They've learned to read the stress patterns in steel and concrete, predicting collapses days in advance. Their eyes have enlarged dramatically, able to perceive the faintest bioluminescent traces and navigate by the ghost-light of electromagnetic leakage. They communicate in whispers that carry impossibly far through the dead streets, their language evolved to use the acoustic properties of their urban environment.

The **Crawlers** live in the tunnel systems exclusively, their bodies elongated and joints multiplied to navigate the tightest passages. They've developed a distributed nervous system that lets them sense danger at either end of their stretched forms. Their skin secretes a substance that reduces friction and provides minor bioluminescence, leaving glowing trails that fade over hours—a navigation system for their kin. They know every connection between the isolated pockets of underground space, serving as messengers and traders between communities that might otherwise never interact. Their ribcages have become flexible, allowing them to compress their bodies to fit through spaces that seem impossibly small. They feed on the fungi that grow in the tunnels, cultivating gardens in hidden chambers they defend fiercely.

In the ancient reservoir chambers dwell the **Swimmers**. They've returned to an aquatic existence, their lungs modified to extract oxygen from the chemically altered water. Their skin has developed a symbiotic relationship with the bioluminescent bacteria, making them living lights in their dark aquatic realm. They harvest the industrial-biological hybrid organisms that thrive in the depths, using tools crafted from calcified plant matter and corroded metal. Their sonar capabilities surpass even the Extractors', allowing them to map the vast flooded spaces and the machinery submerged within. They've discovered that certain frequencies can stimulate growth in the reservoir organisms, essentially farming their food sources. Some say they've made contact with something in the deepest parts of the reservoirs—something that responds to their calls but never surfaces.

The **Archivists** have made their purpose the preservation of the past. They inhabit spaces where records survived—underground data centers, bank vaults, hardened bunkers. Their eyes have adapted to read in near-total darkness, picking up the faintest reflections from degraded surfaces. They've developed techniques to restore corrupted data storage, to read magnetic patterns by touch, to reconstruct written words from the impressions left on long-dissolved paper. Their memories have evolved to be nearly eidetic, each individual serving as a living backup for irreplaceable information. They trade knowledge for resources, their understanding of pre-plant technology making them valuable to all the underground communities. Some believe they're searching for something specific—the answer to how the plant began, or perhaps how to stop it.

The **Symbiont Shepherds** tend to the hybrid infrastructure where biological and mechanical systems merge. They've developed organs that interface directly with these systems, allowing them to feel the flow of electricity and nutrients as sensations. Their nervous systems have expanded beyond their bodies, extending into the bio-mechanical networks they maintain. They can diagnose problems in fusion generators by taste, feel blockages in fluid systems as localized pain. Their consciousness exists partially outside their physical forms, distributed through the systems they tend. They claim to dream the plant's dreams, to sense its needs and intentions through the machinery it has absorbed.

## The Deep Connections

Between the Canopy Continent above and the Underneath below, connections run deeper than the physical passages carved by roots and maintenance shafts.

The root matrix that forms the boundary between worlds is itself a living interface. It processes materials in both directions—sending extracted minerals upward while passing processed nutrients down. The humans who inhabit this intermediate zone have become translators between the two realms, their bodies adapted to survive in either environment for limited periods.

The **Elevator Keepers** maintain the few mechanical lifts that still function between levels. These massive freight elevators, originally used to move industrial equipment, now serve as the primary conduits between worlds. The Keepers have modified the machinery with biological components—muscle fibers that supplement failing cables, nerve networks that respond to thought more than control panels. They've developed pressure-adaptation organs that allow them to survive the transition between the greenhouse atmosphere above and the thick darkness below. Their role makes them neutral parties in the complex politics between surface and depth communities.

Certain roots have hollowed out over time, creating organic passages wide enough for human transit. The **Root Runners** who navigate these passages have developed gecko-like pads on their hands and feet, secreting adhesives that let them climb the vertical sections. They carry messages and small trades between the worlds, their bodies serving as living airlocks—their lungs can process both the spore-rich air above and the chemical soup below. They know which roots are stable and which might suddenly contract, crushing anything within.

The plant's bioelectric network creates another form of connection. Those with the right modifications—the Conductors above and the Symbiont Shepherds below—can send messages through the living circuits. These communications are felt rather than heard, transmitted as patterns of sensation that trained nervous systems interpret as data. Emergency signals can traverse the entire structure in minutes, warning of collapses, breaches, or the emergence of new growth patterns.

Water cycles between the worlds through the plant's vascular system. The Swimmers below have learned to read these flows, tasting the chemical signatures that tell of conditions above. Similarly, certain Sprouters on the surface can sense disturbances in the deep aquifers through vibrations in the root network. This hydraulic communication is slow but reliable, encoding information in mineral concentrations and thermal variations.

The industrial drone itself serves as a connection. Its frequency and intensity vary with conditions in both realms. The Listeners above and the Extractors below have developed parallel interpretations of these variations. When mining operations in the depths accelerate, the drone's pitch rises slightly—a change the surface dwellers have learned to associate with increased plant growth. When major sections of canopy die and regenerate above, the harmonic patterns alter in ways the deep dwellers can feel through their machinery.

Most mysteriously, certain individuals in both realms report shared experiences—dreams or visions that seem to originate from the plant itself. These episodes increase in frequency near the major connection points, suggesting that consciousness itself might flow through the living infrastructure alongside nutrients and electricity. The Archivists below collect accounts of these experiences, finding disturbing parallels with the mythologies developing among surface communities.

The two worlds exist in uneasy symbiosis. The surface processes sunlight and atmospheric gases, sending energy downward. The depths extract minerals and process waste, sending raw materials upward. The humans in each realm have become specialized components in this vast biological machine, their original purposes forgotten or transformed beyond recognition.

Yet traces of the past persist. Radio signals sometimes propagate through the bioelectric network, carrying fragments of ancient broadcasts. The Mechanics above occasionally repair devices whose purposes they don't understand, following maintenance procedures written in languages they can no longer read. The Archivists below piece together histories that explain nothing of the present. Both worlds continue because continuation is all that remains, locked in cycles of work and adaptation that serve the incomprehensible needs of the plant that has consumed their world.

The Canopy Continent grows ever outward and upward, its industrial hymn drowning out any memory of wind or rain. The Underneath contracts ever so slowly, compressed by the weight of biological history above. Between them, through root and wire, nutrient and electron, the connections pulse with the rhythm of a single, world-spanning organism that dreams in frequencies no human consciousness can fully comprehend.

And in both realms, humans persist—changed, adapted, evolved into forms their ancestors would not recognize as kin. They are the plant's neurons now, its blood cells, its maintenance systems made conscious. They remember being something else, some of them, but memory fades like everything organic, replaced by the eternal present of work and survival in a world that runs on principles too vast and strange for any one mind to hold.

The factory planet continues its transformation, neither purely industrial nor purely biological, but something unprecedented—a fusion of purposes that perhaps never had purpose at all, only the blind momentum of systems too complex to stop, too interconnected to fail, too alien to understand.
