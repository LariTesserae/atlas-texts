Far below the Vertan Mountains, where the ancient stone gives way to softer earth and the air grows thick with moisture, lies the Sunken City of Kelethara. Built into the walls of an immense limestone cavern, the city exists in perpetual twilight, illuminated by the phosphorescent fungi that carpet the cave walls like living constellations.

The entrance to Kelethara is hidden behind one of the mountain's many waterfalls, where the cool, clear streams from above plunge into darkness. The mountain-dwellers know of it, though they rarely speak its name aloud. To them, it represents all that they have chosen to reject – a life lived in shadow rather than beneath open sky, however harsh.

The descent takes three days through a spiraling network of natural tunnels, following ancient carved steps worn smooth by countless generations. The air grows warmer and heavier with each turn, thick with the scent of minerals and growing things. Water is everywhere here – dripping from stalactites, running in silver threads down the walls, pooling in perfect stillness in carved basins that serve as both mirrors and scrying pools.

Kelethara sprawls across seven terraced levels, each carved deeper into the living rock. Bridges of calcified stone span the chasms between districts, their surfaces inlaid with fragments of quartz that catch and scatter the fungal light. The architecture is organic, flowing – as if the buildings grew from the stone rather than being carved from it. Doorways are rimmed with cultivated lichens that glow different colors to indicate the purpose of each dwelling: cool blue for homes, warm gold for markets, deep purple for the halls of governance.

The Keletharans are the descendants of those who fled underground during an ancient catastrophe – the same event that drove others to seek refuge in the heights. Where the mountain-dwellers grew lean and sharp-eyed, the Keletharans became pale and luminous, their skin taking on a subtle translucence that makes them seem to glow faintly in the dark. Their eyes have enlarged over the centuries, the irises reflecting light like pools of mercury.

They are artists of water and stone, cultivating elaborate gardens of crystal formations that they shape through patient manipulation of mineral-rich flows. Music echoes constantly through the cavern – the Keletharans have developed a complex tonal language that carries perfectly in the acoustics of their stone halls. Their children learn to sing before they speak, and important messages are conveyed through elaborate harmonies that can travel across the entire city without distortion.

The two peoples maintain an uneasy interdependence. The mountain trains, when they pass over certain valleys, drop sealed containers into hidden pools. These sink through underwater passages to emerge in Kelethara's receiving chambers, carrying manufactured goods, metal tools, and most importantly, news of the upper world. In return, the Keletharans send up rare minerals, healing fungi, and the luminous ink harvested from cave-dwelling cephalopods that live in the deepest pools – an ink that the mountain-dwellers use to mark their maps and sign their most binding contracts, for it glows in the dark and cannot be forged.

Once each year, during the spring floods when the waterfall entrance becomes impassable, representatives from both peoples meet in the Middle Chambers – a series of natural caves that exist halfway between the two worlds. Here, in spaces lit by both carried torches and cultivated fungi, they renew their ancient pacts, exchange gifts, and occasionally, marry across the divide.

The children of such unions are said to be blessed with the strengths of both peoples – able to withstand the thin air of the heights and see in the darkness of the depths. They often become the keepers of the Middle Chambers, maintaining the sacred neutral ground and ensuring that neither people forgets their shared origin, even as they grow ever more distinct in their separate realms.

In Kelethara's deepest level, where the city meets the underground sea, boats of cultivated mushroom wood ferry passengers to other cavern cities, part of a vast network of underground settlements unknown to the surface world. The Keletharans speak of lands down there where the ceiling glows like daylight, where forests of giant fungi sway in manufactured breezes, where entire civilizations have forgotten that stone ever ends and sky begins.

But always, the water remembers its journey from the mountain peaks, carrying whispers of the wind and fragments of starlight down into the eternal dusk of Kelethara, just as the minerals dissolved in the deep pools eventually rise again through springs and geysers, carrying memories of the depths back to the austere settlements above.
