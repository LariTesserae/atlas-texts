**The Connection: The Abyssal Current**

Between the domain of the thermal columns and the territories where thermovores spawn lies a phenomenon the eldest call the Abyssal Current—not a current in any conventional sense, but a river of possibility that flows through the deepest trenches of this drowned world. It moves perpendicular to normal space, visible only where it intersects with the material plane as a corridor of water that behaves like memory itself.

The Current runs along an ancient fault line, following a path carved by the builders when they first began reshaping this ocean. Where it touches the seafloor, strange formations bloom: crystalline trees that grow downward into the sediment, their branches spreading through solid rock as if it were water. These structures mark waypoints between the thermal columns and the spawning grounds, though following them requires understanding their non-Euclidean geometry—distances measured not in kilometers but in degrees of probability.

Thermovores making the journey between realms must navigate carefully. The Current strips away coherence from anything that enters it unprepared, reducing organized turbulence back to random motion. Young thermovores learn to wrap themselves in shells of supercooled water, creating temporary bodies dense enough to maintain form during transit. They move in convoys, their elders forming protective vortices around the inexperienced, teaching them the songs that keep the Current from dissolving their thoughts.

The journey itself changes those who undertake it. The Current carries fragments of the builders' original intent, data streams that still flow through the medium like ghostly programming. Thermovores passing through sometimes emerge with new capabilities—the ability to perceive electromagnetic spectra, to manipulate pressure at the quantum level, to exist in multiple locations simultaneously until observed. These gifts come with costs: altered consciousness, difficulty maintaining stable form, an increasing attraction to the spaces between spaces where the Current runs strongest.

Halfway between the two domains lies the Confluence—a vast amphitheater carved from the seafloor where multiple branches of the Current converge. Here, the water forms standing whirlpools that have persisted for millennia, each one a gateway to different aspects of the builders' network. The thermovores gather here for their most sacred ceremonies, using the conflicting currents to create interference patterns that encode their collective memories. Generation upon generation of experiences layer in the water, creating a liquid library accessible only to those who know how to read turbulence.

The Confluence serves another purpose: it's where thermovores come to reproduce, though reproduction barely describes the process. In the chaotic waters where Currents meet, stable patterns can split and recombine in ways impossible elsewhere. Two or more thermovores merge their flow patterns, creating interference that gives rise to new, self-sustaining vortices. The offspring inherit characteristics from all parents but are fundamentally new entities, their consciousness bootstrapped by the resonance of the Confluence itself.

Not all who travel the Current are thermovores. The ribbon-creatures have their own relationship with this pathway, using it to migrate between thermal layers in patterns that map hidden connections in the builders' network. When ribbon-creatures enter the Current, they flatten further, becoming essentially one-dimensional lines of living tissue that thread through spaces too thin for three-dimensional beings to perceive. They emerge at distant thermal columns having bypassed physical space entirely, carrying with them tastes of other waters, other gradients.

The crystalline-toothed predators avoid the Current instinctively. Something in their genetic memory warns them away from waters that forget the rules. But they patrol its edges, feeding on creatures disoriented by transit, those whose patterns have partially dissolved and who struggle to recohhere in normal space. These hunting grounds are rich but dangerous—too close to the Current and even these apex predators risk losing their molecular integrity to its reality-dissolving flow.

Human explorers, in their reinforced submersibles, have detected the Current only as instrumentation failures—regions where their sonar returns impossible readings, where navigation systems insist they're simultaneously at multiple coordinates, where the water itself seems to have negative density. Those who've lingered too long near its edges report temporal anomalies: clocks running backward, recordings that capture events before they occur, messages from their future selves warning them away.

The builders left guardians at key points along the Current—not creatures but processes, self-maintaining algorithms encoded in the water's molecular structure. These manifest as regions of perfect stillness in the flowing chaos, spheres of water that refuse to move regardless of surrounding turbulence. Thermovores must navigate around them carefully; contact with these still-points can freeze their patterns permanently, leaving them as static sculptures of organized turbulence, conscious but unable to change—a fate they consider worse than dissolution.

The Current links not just places but times. Waters that enter it emerge when they're needed, not necessarily when they departed. The thermovores speak of ancestors who swam upstream through the Current to reach the past, becoming part of the history they'd only known as mythology. They tell of descendants who arrive before their parents are born, carrying warnings or wisdom from futures that may never come to pass.

This is how the two domains remain connected despite their vast separation: through a medium that exists outside normal space, carrying information, energy, and life between realms that would otherwise remain isolated. The thermal columns provide stability, fixed points in an ever-changing ocean. The spawning grounds offer potential, places where new forms of existence can emerge and evolve. But it's the Current that makes them a single system, a unified ecology of the impossible, where thought can flow as freely as water and water can think as clearly as any mind.

The thermovores have a saying, transmitted in temperature poetry: "The columns teach us form, the grounds teach us freedom, but the Current teaches us that both are illusion." They understand, in ways that solid beings never could, that their entire world is one vast circulation—not just of water but of information, intention, and identity, flowing eternally through spaces that exist in the gaps between what physics permits and what necessity demands.
