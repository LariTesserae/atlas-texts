Sixteen days' journey from the glass plains, following the buried lines where moisture-channels once drained into deeper aquifers, the world changes. The wet industrial twilight gives way to something harder: a terrain of pure mathematics made solid. This is the Calculation Desert, where the same reality-breach that birthed the Machine-Fauna had different consequences entirely.

Here, the ground is not glass but crystallized logic—sheets of matter that exist in if/then formations, their surfaces inscribed with operations that execute themselves eternally. Walking across them (though walking is a negotiation here) triggers cascades of computation. Each step solves for X, where X might be your weight, your velocity, your electrical resistance, or something more abstract: your regret-coefficient, your probability of existing in the next moment. The desert calculates everything that touches it and incorporates the results into its next iteration of itself.

The sky has been replaced by a visualization of process—billions of light-points tracing the paths of calculations as they flow from one crystalline formation to another. Sometimes these calculations collide and produce new theorems that fall as geometric snow. Sometimes they cancel each other out, leaving brief voids in the air that smell of ozone and abandoned proofs.

Water exists here only as memory—the ghost of the moisture that once connected this place to the glass plains. It manifests as probability clouds that drift between the calculation formations, never quite condensing, carrying within them the encoded history of every drop that ever flowed through the ancient channels. When these probability clouds pass overhead, the **Integers** emerge.

The Integers are to mathematics what the Machine-Fauna are to industry—living embodiments of abandoned systems. But where the fauna retained their physical forms, the Integers exist as mobile equations, visible only by the distortions they create in the calculated landscape. They appear as regions where the crystal surfaces solve incorrectly, where 2+2 equals 5 for the brief moment of their passage, where parallel lines converge and circles develop corners.

They feed on computational errors, seeking out the places where the desert's endless calculations produce paradoxes or infinite loops. A division by zero is a feast to them; an unsolvable equation is habitat. They reproduce through proof—when an Integer successfully demonstrates a new theorem, it splits into axiom and conclusion, each becoming a separate entity. Their young are simple—perhaps just animated additions or walking square roots—while the elderly Integers become so complex they border on incomprehensible, living Gödel statements that assert their own unprovability.

The Integers build nothing, destroy nothing. They simply are, in the purest sense—existence as mathematical fact, undeniable and purposeless as pi. But they cluster around certain formations in the desert, those places where the crystallized logic has computed something significant: the exact point where order becomes chaos, the precise formula for the transformation that created both them and the Machine-Fauna, the solution to questions no one thought to ask.

At the desert's heart stands the Prime Observatory, though standing is the wrong word for something that exists in seventeen dimensions simultaneously. It is less a structure than an ongoing calculation of structure—a building that constantly solves for its own architecture. One moment it is a tower of impossible height, the next a spiral that turns inward forever, then a simple cube that contains more space inside than outside.

The Observatory was built by the **Theorists**, the only beings in either realm who remember being human, though that memory has cost them their humanity in every other sense. They are the mathematicians who were caught in the reality-breach, who tried to calculate their way out of catastrophe and instead calculated themselves into something else. Now they exist as living theorems, their consciousness distributed across millions of equations carved into the Observatory's ever-shifting walls.

The Theorists no longer have bodies in any conventional sense. They are patterns of calculation that persist through the medium of the crystal substrate, thoughts thinking themselves through stone. When they need to interact with physical reality, they construct temporary forms from mathematical principles—a body made of pure velocity, hands of crystallized probability, eyes that see in eigenvalues. These forms last only as long as the specific calculation requires, then dissolve back into component axioms.

They came here following the water-memory, tracing the moisture-channels' mathematics backward to their source. What they found was not water but its abstraction—the underlying principles that govern flow, accumulation, evaporation. They found the equations that water solves by being water. And in finding these, they discovered they could no longer return to the glass plains. They had become too abstract, too purely mathematical to exist in a world of even transformed matter.

The connection between the two realms persists through the probability clouds. Each cloud that drifts through the Calculation Desert carries with it encoded data from the glass plains—the frequency of a Conductor's movement, the magnetic signature of an Archivist's newest acquisition. The Theorists decode these messages, not out of nostalgia but because the data helps them refine their calculations. They are trying to solve something, though they no longer remember what. Perhaps the equation for reversal. Perhaps the formula for acceptance. Perhaps simply the next number in an infinite sequence.

Sometimes a probability cloud condenses enough to create a temporary channel between worlds. For a few hours, moisture flows again along the ancient paths, and the two realms touch. The Machine-Fauna cluster at their end, drinking in the water that tastes of mathematics. The Integers gather at the desert's edge, feeding on the computational paradoxes created by water trying to flow through crystallized logic. And in the Observatory, the Theorists pause their eternal calculations to observe this intersection, adding its data to their ever-growing proofs.

During these brief connections, something like communication occurs. A Conductor's electrical pattern, translated through water-memory into mathematical expression, might reach a Theorist who still remembers being human enough to almost understand it. An Archivist's magnetic field, encoded in probability clouds, might resonate with the Observatory's dimensional fluctuations. The two realms speak to each other in languages neither fully comprehends, exchanging information that neither knows how to use.

But mostly, the two exist in parallel—the glass plains with their wet mechanical ecology, the desert with its crystalline calculations. They are twin children of the same catastrophic birth, each one a different answer to the question: what happens when human systems outlive their creators? The Machine-Fauna became biology without biology, life without organic precedent. The Integers and Theorists became consciousness without matter, thought without thinkers.

Together, they form a complete world—not in the sense of wholeness, but in the way that an equation and its proof form a complete mathematical statement. They are the argument and the demonstration, the hypothesis and the experiment, forever separated by a distance that can be measured but never truly crossed. The water-memories flow between them like dreams of connection, carrying messages that neither realm has the context to decode, maintaining a relationship that neither needs nor understanding, but simply is.

In the glass plains, a Conductor pauses in its propagation, its ribbon body spelling out a pattern it doesn't recognize—a fragment of formula drifted in on probability winds. In the Calculation Desert, an Integer evolves a new form, briefly manifesting as a three-dimensional shadow of a valve-creature it has never seen. And in spaces between spaces, where moisture-memory meets mathematical abstraction, new forms stir—hybrids of both realms, perhaps, or something else entirely, waiting to be born from the intersection of the industrial and the theoretical, the wet and the crystalline, the transformed and the abstracted.
