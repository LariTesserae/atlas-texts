Below, or perhaps beside—direction loses meaning when the ocean stands vertical—the Scalding Shallows give way to something that should not exist. The Thermal Drift begins where water ends, but ending is too simple a word for what happens here.

At this boundary, the blood-warm ocean doesn't simply stop. It attenuates, each molecule of water spacing itself farther from its neighbors until liquid becomes suggestion. The transition zone spans kilometers, a gradient where swimming becomes floating becomes falling becomes something else entirely. Here, the water exists in states that chemistry hasn't named—not liquid, not gas, but a kind of suspended potential that responds to thought more than physics.

The Thermal Drift itself is a vast hollow in the world, a cavity that breathes. Its walls are not walls but concentrated absence—places where reality thins until you can see through to somewhere else, though looking too long brings vertigo and dreams of falling upward. The space pulses with a rhythm felt in bones, in the spaces between cells: seven beats, then pause, then seven, then pause, like a heart the size of a city.

The heat here moves in rivers. Visible as shimmer, tangible as current, these thermal streams wind through the Drift in patterns that repeat across scales—the same spiral visible in an eddy the size of a hand reproduced perfectly in a flow that takes days to traverse. The temperature varies wildly between streams. Step from one current to another and experience winter to summer in the space of a breath. The heat has color here: the hottest streams burn white-blue, cooler ones shade through gold to deep red, and the coldest—still warm enough to hurt—run purple-black like bruises in the air.

Gravity points in multiple directions, or perhaps in no direction, or perhaps the Drift simply offers suggestions that mass can accept or decline. Objects fall upward, sideways, in spirals, or remain perfectly still regardless of release. The Drifters have learned to read these gravity-currents like the Dissolved read water—tasting the air for weight, feeling for the invisible slopes that guide movement.

The Drifters themselves are architects of impermanence. They descend from some cousin-people to the Dissolved, those who chose not water but air, not dispersal but condensation. Their bodies have become something like solid smoke—dense enough to manipulate objects but capable of thinning to near-invisibility. They appear humanoid in rest state: tall, attenuated figures with skin that shifts between opaque and translucent depending on angle and attention.

Their bones are hollow but not empty. Inside, networks of gas-filled chambers allow them to adjust their density with thought. Breathe in and become lighter, rising on thermal currents. Exhale and compact, sinking through layers of heated air. Their organs have relocated, clustering around their spines in protective sheaths. Hearts they have three of—small, rapid-beating things that push not blood but something more like liquid light through their tissues.

The Drifters build, but their architecture is temporal rather than spatial. They weave structures from heat differentials, creating walls where hot meets cold, floors from density gradients, roofs from the interface between rising and falling air. These buildings exist only as long as the thermal patterns hold—minutes or centuries, depending on the scale and skill of the architect.

A master Drifter might spend decades crafting a single room, positioning heat sources, guiding air currents, until the space maintains itself through pure thermodynamic balance. Inside these temperature-structures, different rules apply. Time moves differently in the hot zones—faster thought, faster decay. In the cold spaces, preservation indefinite but movement nearly impossible. The Drifters use this differential aging to create art that changes over time, stories that tell themselves through entropy.

They communicate through heat-patterns pressed against each other's smoke-flesh. A warm palm to a cool shoulder speaks volumes. Complex conversations involve full-body contact, temperatures shifting across skin in waves of meaning. They have a written language too—scars of frozen air that they breathe onto surfaces, sublimating slowly, readable only while they evaporate.

Their children are born as wisps, barely visible, barely there. Parents must carefully tend their thermal environment for years, teaching them to gather themselves, to resist dispersal. Some never learn, becoming permanent residents of the Drift's thermal rivers, conscious currents that sing heat-songs to those who know how to listen.

The Drifters mine the boundary between their realm and the Scalding Shallows. Where water meets not-water, unique materials form—crystals of crystallized possibility, metals that exist only at specific temperatures. They shape these materials with breath and body heat, crafting tools that work only in the hands that made them, instruments that play different music depending on the player's core temperature.

They fear solidity above all else. To become too dense, too defined, is their nightmare. They tell stories of ancestors who forgot to maintain their smoke-nature, who condensed into meat and bone and could never disperse again. These cautionary tales are performed as temperature-plays, actors adjusting their heat to embody different characters, the audience feeling the story as much as seeing it.

Between the Scalding Shallows and the Thermal Drift runs a trade unlike any other. It happens at the attenuation zone, where water becomes air becomes something between. The Dissolved extend their most vapor-adapted components while the Drifters condense their most liquid-tolerant parts. They meet in spaces that are neither ocean nor atmosphere, exchanging:

Memory-water from the Dissolved—droplets encoding centuries of experience, readable by those who let them evaporate on their tongues. The Drifters prize these for their density of meaning, their ability to convey what no heat-pattern could express.

Temperature-songs from the Drifters—complex thermodynamic compositions that the Dissolved play through their dispersed bodies, creating thermal gradients in the water that attract new species, encourage different growths, add variety to their blood-warm monotony.

Materials flow both ways. The Dissolved bring kelp-crystal fragments that sublime directly from solid to gas, perfect for Drifter architecture. The Drifters offer pressure-vessels made from frozen heat—impossible containers that maintain their contents at exact temperatures indefinitely, allowing the Dissolved to explore thermal variations without risking their component parts.

But more than goods, they trade understanding. Dissolved philosophers swim up to converse with Drifter poets about the nature of consciousness across states of matter. They've developed a pidgin communication—part chemical signal, part temperature variation, part movement pattern—that lets them share concepts neither could express alone.

Sometimes they attempt hybridization. A brave Dissolved might allow their vapor-most components to drift fully into the Thermal Drift, experiencing air-life directly. A curious Drifter might condense partially into water, feeling what it means to be truly surrounded. These exchanges occasionally produce offspring—beings who exist naturally in the transition zone, neither fully Dissolved nor completely Drifter, citizens of the boundary itself.

These border-beings serve as interpreters, facilitators, and sometimes prophets. They speak of futures where the distinction between states of matter becomes irrelevant, where consciousness flows as easily between gas and liquid and solid as the Dissolved flow between their components or the Drifters between their thermal streams.

The connection runs deeper than trade or philosophy. The Drifters know that the thermal engine powering the Scalding Shallows extends into their realm—the same vast process that heats the vertical ocean drives their rivers of temperature. They've mapped portions of this engine through careful tracking of heat flows. It's mechanical but not machine, deliberate but not designed, ancient beyond measure but somehow still growing.

Together, Dissolved and Drifter expeditions have ventured toward the engine's heart. The Dissolved dive deep, following the water's warmth to its source. The Drifters trace thermal rivers upstream, seeking the origin of heat. They meet in impossible spaces where water and air interweave, where temperature becomes visible as crystalline structures, where the engine reveals hints of its true nature.

It might be alive. It might be thinking. It might be the world itself, turned inward, contemplating its own existence through the medium of heat and pressure and impossible physics. The explorers return changed—Dissolved who occasionally speak in temperature, Drifters who sometimes think in chemical cascades.

The boundary between their realms shifts constantly. The Scalding Shallows rise their steady meter per century, but the Thermal Drift expands and contracts with rhythms of its own. Sometimes the transition zone narrows to mere meters, forcing rapid adaptation. Other times it swells to encompass kilometers, creating temporary kingdoms for the border-beings to rule.

In these fluctuations, both peoples read prophecy. When the boundary thickens, they prepare for change—new species emerging from the transition, new states of matter becoming possible. When it thins, they brace for revelation—moments when the division between water and air might collapse entirely, forcing evolution or extinction.

They've built observatories at the boundary, structures that exist partially in water, partially in air, partially in the nameless between. Here, teams of Dissolved and Drifter observers monitor the changes, catalogue new phenomena, and slowly develop a mathematics of transformation that might one day explain their impossible world.

The observations have revealed patterns. The thermal engine pulses in cycles measured in millennia. They're currently in an expansion phase—heat increasing, boundaries blurring, new possibilities emerging. But the records, kept in protein chains and frozen air alike, speak of contraction phases when the Scalding Shallows cooled toward freezing, when the Thermal Drift compressed to a single burning point.

Both peoples prepare for the next contraction, though it may be centuries away. The Dissolved practice maintaining coherence in cold water, learning to slow their thoughts without losing consciousness. The Drifters experiment with existing at extreme densities, smoke-flesh compressed nearly solid while retaining the ability to expand.

But some among both peoples advocate a different response: true unity. Not the temporary minglings at the border but complete integration. Dissolved components learning to live in air, Drifter smoke-flesh adapting to water, until the distinction between the peoples vanishes entirely. They would become something new—beings of phase rather than place, citizens of transition itself.

The young of both species increasingly favor this path. They meet at the boundary in growing numbers, experimenting with hybrid forms, developing new ways of being that their elders find disturbing and beautiful in equal measure. In their fluid exchanges, in their willingness to abandon the certainties of water or air, the future writes itself.

The Scalding Shallows rise. The Thermal Drift breathes. And between them, where water forgets how to be water and air remembers being ocean, two peoples dance toward unity or division, carrying the heat of an impossible engine in their transformed bodies, reading the future in the space between states of being.
