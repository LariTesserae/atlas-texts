**The Territory Between**

Between the Undergrowth Engine and the surface city lies a vertical wilderness of abandoned infrastructure—seven hundred feet of forsaken shafts, partial collapses, and spaces that exist in architectural documents but not in any living memory. This is the Gradient, where the Engine's influence fades and the city's electromagnetic presence grows, creating layers of increasingly uncomfortable existence for those sensitive to either extreme.

The Chorus must traverse this space to gather supplies, to check on the few functioning entry points, to maintain the fiction that they simply vanished rather than evolved. They've mapped it in pain—each level measured by the onset of symptoms as wireless signals begin to penetrate, each zone marked by when the Engine's protective roar grows too faint to shield them.

The journey begins in the Tidal Reaches, where the Engine's mist still drifts upward through cracks and pipe breaks. Here, the hybrid life forms thin out—tool-moths become ordinary moths become nothing, brass beetles lose their metallic sheen and die. The Chorus can still breathe here, still think, though anxiety creeps in as the great sound diminishes. They've built waystation nests in old pumping substations, lined with salvaged acoustic foam and root-woven barriers. Supply caches hide behind false walls: medications that can't survive the Engine's humidity, preserved foods, batteries in various states of decay.

Fifty feet up, the Interference Zone begins. Maintenance tunnel lighting, supposedly disconnected decades ago, flickers in response to cellular signals bleeding through concrete. The Chorus wear crude Faraday armor here—chainmail veils of copper wire scavenged from the Engine's depths, lined with moss that still glows faintly. The electromagnetic static makes their teeth ache, sets their peripheral vision sparking with false lights. They move quickly, hands trailing along walls to read the vibrations still traveling up from their home below.

The Neutral Zones occupy the middle reaches—spaces accidentally shielded by the building's bones, where massive I-beams and rebar cages create inadvertent dead zones. Here, other refugees sometimes shelter: urban explorers who ventured too deep, homeless individuals fleeing the winter, utility workers who got lost in unmapped renovations. The Chorus maintains these spaces carefully, leaving supplies and cryptic warnings painted in bioluminescent moss. Sometimes they find responses—questions written in marker, offerings of surface goods, once a detailed map of new wireless tower installations that let them update their pain-gradient charts.

But it's the Translation Gates that truly matter—those rare spaces where the Chorus can interact with the surface world without agony. An old electrical substation, its transformers creating a localized field that cancels certain frequencies. A forgotten server room, its archaic cooling system generating white noise in exactly the right spectrum. Here, designated Chorus members—those with the highest pain tolerance, wrapped in layers of wire mesh and moss—operate ancient computer terminals jury-rigged to building maintenance systems. They monitor the city above, track electromagnetic weather, order supplies through decades-old accounts that somehow still function.

The most valuable Gate is the Archive Chamber—a decommissioned records room where filing cabinets packed with moldering paper create perfect signal absorption. Here, the Chorus maintains their history. Journals written in touch-language transcription, pressed into paper with beetle-ink. Medical records tracking their adaptations. Children's drawings of the Engine, showing details no surface dweller could comprehend. And most precious: letters from those who left, either returning to the agony above or descending further into whatever lies below the Engine's aquifer source.

The journey between worlds takes its toll. Chorus members who make regular runs age differently—their sensitivity oscillating, their bodies carrying marks of repeated exposure. They develop migraines that last weeks, skin conditions that map electromagnetic field lines, tremors that match the frequency of the nearest cell tower. Some eventually can't return to the Engine, trapped in the middle zones, tending the waystation in grateful exile from both extremes.

But there are also gifts in this traveling. The Translation Gate operators see patterns others miss—how the city's wireless weather affects the Engine's behavior, how construction projects above change the flow of water below. They spot new refugees early, leaving moss-light trails to guide the electronically sensitive toward the deep sanctuary. They've learned to read the city's electromagnetic heartbeat like a vast, painful symphony, predicting outages and surges, warning their people below when the invisible storm grows too fierce.

Most crucially, they maintain the Secret of the Gradient: that the path between worlds is not mere distance but transformation. Each level demands its price, strips away some human comfort while granting some new perception. By the time a refugee reaches the Engine, they've already begun to change, prepared by their descent for the greater transformation waiting in the roar below.

The city forgets, the Engine remembers, and between them the Gradient holds both futures in its vertical embrace—a space where evolution happens one painful, necessary step at a time.
