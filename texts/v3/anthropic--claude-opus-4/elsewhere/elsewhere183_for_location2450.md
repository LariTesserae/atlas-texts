Beyond the wetlands, where the sodden earth finally yields to firmer ground, the land rises in a series of terraces carved from ancient limestone. Here, the water that dominates the marshes below has worked a different magic over millennia, hollowing out a labyrinthine network of caves that honeycomb the plateau. The limestone cliffs gleam white-gold in the blazing sun, their faces pocked with dark openings that exhale cool, mineral-scented air.

This is the Singing Caves, so named for the haunting harmonies that emerge when wind flows through the countless passages and chambers within. The caves connect to the wetlands through underground rivers and springs, creating a vast hydrological system that feeds the marshes below. During the wet season, water cascades from a hundred cave mouths, forming temporary waterfalls that thunder down the cliff faces.

Within this vertical world lives the Keeper of Echoes.

She is ancient beyond memory, though her appearance shifts with the light—sometimes a young woman with limestone dust in her hair, sometimes a crone whose skin resembles the flowstone formations that drape the cave walls. Her eyes hold the peculiar blindness-that-sees of those who have lived too long in darkness, pupils dilated to black pools that somehow perceive more than ordinary sight allows.

The Keeper moves through the caves as water moves through stone—slowly, persistently, finding passages where none seem to exist. She knows every chamber, every stalactite, every underground lake. Her bare feet have worn smooth paths in the rock over centuries of walking. She speaks rarely, and when she does, her voice carries the resonance of the caves themselves, each word accompanied by its own echo, creating layers of meaning that spiral into the darkness.

Her dwelling shifts location within the cave system according to laws only she understands. Sometimes it occupies a vast cathedral chamber where columns of stone reach from floor to ceiling like frozen waterfalls. Other times, she makes her home in intimate grottos where crystalline formations catch and amplify the faintest light. Always, her spaces are decorated with curious arrangements—stones balanced in impossible configurations, underground streams diverted to create musical fountains, colonies of blind cave fish in pools that reflect infinity despite their modest size.

The Keeper serves as guardian of the boundary between the wetland and the underworld. She maintains the ancient sluice gates carved by the same vanished people who left their mark on the marshes below. These gates regulate the flow between surface and depth, ensuring the wetlands neither flood beyond bearing nor desiccate into dust. More than this mechanical function, she preserves the memory of water—every drop that has passed through the caves carries its story, and she remembers them all.

Those who venture from the wetlands sometimes seek her out, following the underground rivers upstream into the darkness. Hunters of rare cave salamanders, collectors of mineral specimens, or simply the lost who stumble into the maze of passages. She receives them all with the same distant courtesy, offering guidance that comes in the form of riddles or silence. Many who enter the caves searching for treasures emerge instead carrying questions they never knew to ask.

The Keeper cultivates gardens of cave-adapted life. Pale fungi glow with bioluminescence in careful patterns she has encouraged over decades. Albino plants grow in patches where she has directed the faintest filtered sunlight. Colonies of bats roost in chambers she has shaped to perfect acoustics, their ultrasonic calls creating symphonies only she can fully appreciate. These cave ecosystems connect to the wetland life through mysterious channels—nutrients flowing down, messages traveling up, a circulation as vital as breath.

In the deepest chambers, where even the memory of sunlight fails, she tends to springs that have never known the surface. These waters emerge from depths so profound they carry heat from the earth's heart, creating pockets of warmth where unique species flourish—blind crustaceans like mobile jewels, bacterial mats that paint the rocks in impossible colors, fish that navigate by electrical fields. These are the nurseries of the wetland's strangest inhabitants, species that begin life in the eternal darkness before emerging into the marsh's twilight world.

The relationship between the Singing Caves and the wetlands below is one of reciprocity and time. The caves store the water that the wetlands need, releasing it according to rhythms older than human memory. The wetlands, in turn, filter and purify the water that returns to the caves through seepage and underground channels. The Keeper of Echoes stands as intermediary in this exchange, ensuring the balance holds, recording in the stone itself the endless story of water's journey between darkness and light, depth and surface, silence and song.
