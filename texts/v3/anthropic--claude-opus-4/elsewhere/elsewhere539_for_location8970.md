The hottest regions glow with their own light, a deep red that shifts to orange, yellow, and finally to a blue so pale it seems like captured starlight in the cooler zones. At the Gradient's heart lies the Core Chamber—a perfect sphere where all temperature zones meet. Here, the impossible becomes routine. Objects exist in multiple states simultaneously—ice that burns, plasma that freezes, metals that sublime directly from solid to gas and back again in endless cycles. The chamber's walls are lined with every possible crystal formation, creating a garden of mineral possibilities that grows and changes with the thermal tides.

This is not a place of life as the surface knows it, but it is far from dead. The Gradient processes energy and information on scales that dwarf biological evolution. Its crystalline matrices store patterns that persist for eons, a kind of mineral memory that records the deep history of the planet. In its flows and transformations, something like thought occurs—not consciousness as the Chorus would recognize it, but a vast, slow cognition that contemplates problems of pressure and temperature with the patience of stone.
