Beyond the salt flats' eastern edge, where the land drops away in a series of crystalline terraces, lies the Saturation Gardens. Here, water exists in such abundance that it seems like violence after the careful aridity of the Respirator District.

The Gardens sprawl across natural limestone bowls, each one containing a different depth and salinity of water. Ancient springs weep continuously from the porous rock, their flow so constant that the sound becomes a kind of silence—white noise that the mind eventually stops registering. The air hangs thick and heavy, supersaturated with moisture that condenses on every surface. Visibility rarely extends beyond fifty feet; beyond that, the world dissolves into veils of mist that shift and part without warning.

Everything here drips. The trees—if they can be called trees—are columnar masses of aerial roots that drink directly from the air. Their "leaves" are actually specialized root tips that have evolved photosynthetic capabilities, creating a canopy that exists in constant flux as roots grow, drink, die, and fall. The ground beneath is a spongy mass of decomposing root matter, so waterlogged that it ripples when walked upon. Each footstep fills immediately with seepage.

The dominant life forms are the Siphonophores—colonial organisms that exist somewhere between plant and animal. They manifest as translucent tubes ranging from finger-width to the girth of ancient trees, drawing water up from the pools and expelling it as fine mist from their crowns. Inside their transparent bodies, you can see the constant flow of fluid, carrying nutrients and microorganisms in endless circulation. Some Siphonophores have grown together into cathedral-like structures, their fused bodies creating living architecture where interior humidity reaches impossible levels—water beading from air that's somehow more than 100% saturated.

The Gardens' human inhabitants call themselves the Drowned. They move through their aqueous world wearing nothing but second skins—living films cultivated from symbiotic algae that process water and oxygen directly through the skin. Their hair grows in ropy tendrils that function as auxiliary gills, and their eyes have developed transparent nictitating membranes that let them see clearly both in air and underwater.

The Drowned have learned to navigate by taste, their tongues sensitive to minute differences in mineral content that indicate which spring system they're near. They speak in burbles and gurgles, a liquid language that carries better through water than air. Their architecture consists of woven root systems trained into dense tangles that somehow remain dry inside despite the omnipresent moisture—a trick of osmotic pressure and selective permeability that took generations to perfect.

Between these two extremes lies the Gradient Lands, a realm of constant negotiation between desiccation and drowning.

The transition begins subtly. At the eastern edge of the Respirator District, condensation appears on the pipes during the coolest part of the night cycle—just a few beads of moisture that the Grease Monks carefully collect in leather bladders. Moving eastward, the salt flats develop a different character. Here, the hexagonal plates show signs of ancient water damage, their edges rounded and surfaces etched with mineral deposits that tell stories of floods from centuries past.

The first springs appear as mere seeps, dark spots on the salt where moisture wicks up from below. Filter-mice cluster around these spots during their reproductive cycles, using the trace humidity to soften their crystalline eggs. Gradually, the seeps become trickles, cutting narrow channels through the salt. The channels host their own ecosystems: salt-tolerant mosses that can switch between dormancy and growth in minutes, and peculiar arthropods that build mobile dams from their own shed exoskeletons.

Halfway between the two settlements stands the Exchange Station—a structure that belongs fully to neither world. Built from imported stone that neither absorbs nor repels water, it serves as both physical and cultural transition point. The building's western wing maintains Respirator-level dryness through a system of desiccant chambers and air locks. Its eastern wing embraces moisture, with walls of living root filters that process the increasing humidity. At the center lies the Translation Hall, where the pressure-speech of the Breathkeepers meets the liquid language of the Drowned.

Here, the Merchant Monks facilitate trade between the two realms. They are humans who have trained their bodies to survive in both environments, though they excel in neither. They wear reversible suits—leather and oil-cloth on one side for the dry west, algae-film on the other for the humid east. Their breathing techniques allow them to function in both the thin, dry air and the thick, wet atmosphere, though they must rest in the neutral zone of the Translation Hall between transitions.

The primary trade good flowing west is water—not in its raw form, which would be catastrophic to the Respirator District's delicate balance, but processed into crystalline matrices that release moisture slowly over months. The Drowned have perfected techniques for binding water molecules into salt cages, creating "solid water" that the Breathkeepers can safely transport and store.

Flowing east goes concentrated protein from the Respirator farms, along with the refined metal oils that the Drowned prize for waterproofing their few non-biological tools. More valuable still are the filter-mice, which the Drowned have learned to breed in semi-aquatic varieties. These adapted mice help control the populations of parasitic water-worms that would otherwise clog the Siphonophores.

But it's not just goods that flow between the realms. Young people from both settlements sometimes feel the pull of the opposite extreme. Breathkeepers who dream of drowning. Drowned who fantasize about desiccation. They meet in the Gradient Lands, learning each other's languages, sharing techniques for survival.

Some never return to their birth homes. They become Gradient Dwellers, building lives in the transition zone. Their settlements are marvels of adaptive architecture: structures that expand and contract with humidity levels, roofs that collect dew on one side and channel it away from the other. They've learned to read the subtle signs that predict when the gradient will shift—when unusual weather patterns might push moisture farther west or draw the desert farther east.

The Gradient Dwellers farm the transition itself. They cultivate organisms that thrive on change: crystalline plants that grow only at specific humidity levels, creating valuable minerals as they oscillate between growth and dormancy. They harvest gradient-worms, creatures that must migrate daily between wet and dry zones to complete their life cycles, timing their movements to intercept the valuable silk these worms spin only during transition.

Climate records kept by the Gradient Dwellers show that the transition zone moves over centuries-long cycles. Ancient markers—salt-preserved root systems in the west, mineralized pipe networks in the east—tell of times when each realm extended much farther into what is now the other's territory. The current generation lives in a period of relative stability, but their grandparents remember when the Exchange Station had to be rebuilt three times in a decade as the gradient shifted.

During the rare alignment storms, when atmospheric conditions cause the gradient to oscillate wildly over hours instead of centuries, both realms send representatives to the Exchange Station. Breathkeepers and Drowned work together, performing ancient protocols to protect both settlements. They adjust pipe pressures in the west to prevent catastrophic condensation, while channeling excess moisture in the east to prevent the springs from running backward. It's during these crises that the deep connection between the two realms becomes undeniable.

For despite their radical differences, the Respirator District and the Saturation Gardens are part of a single system. The deep bore-holes that provide compressed gases to the west tap into the same geological strata that feed the springs in the east. The minerals dissolved in the Gardens' waters match perfectly with the crystalline substrates beneath the District. Maps drawn by the most far-traveling Gradient Dwellers suggest that both settlements occupy opposite edges of a vast, ancient crater—the remains of some prehistoric impact that created both the depression where water gathers and the uplift where gases escape.

In the deepest archives of both realms, stories persist of the Sundering—a time when a single civilization split itself deliberately, sending half its population to embrace absolute dryness and half to seek perfect saturation. Whether this is history or mythology, none can say. But the Gradient Dwellers claim that sometimes, in the perfect atmospheric conditions that occur perhaps once in a generation, you can stand in the exact center of the Exchange Station and hear both the pneumatic pulses of the west and the liquid whispers of the east resolve into a single, comprehensible voice—speaking words in a language no one living remembers, but everyone somehow understands.

The two realms need each other, though neither would readily admit it. The Respirator District's careful moisture recycling only works because excess humidity has somewhere to flow. The Saturation Gardens' springs maintain their pressure because of the pneumatic draws from the west. Push too hard in either direction—make one realm too dry or the other too wet—and both collapse.

And so they persist: two extremes of human adaptation, separated by a gradient that connects them, each pursuing their own vision of survival while bound in an ancient, inescapable symbiosis. The Breathkeepers ascending their copper towers, approaching some crystalline perfection of desiccation. The Drowned descending into their pools, seeking dissolution into the aqueous infinite. And between them, the Gradient Dwellers maintaining the delicate balance, ensuring that neither extreme achieves the total purity that would end them all.
