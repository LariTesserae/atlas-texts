Three nautical miles away, where the continental shelf drops into true abyss, stands a tower that should not exist. It rises from the deep-water darkness like a splinter of another reality—too thin for its height, too steady for the currents that should topple it, too bright for depths where sunlight dies.

The tower is grown rather than built, its surface a weave of something between coral and circuitry. Bioluminescent traceries pulse along its length in patterns that mirror the electrical flows in the Assembler's copper-vein chest—though here the scale is vast, each pulse visible for miles through the clear deep water. The light attracts creatures, but not the ones that should exist at this depth. These are emigrants from the factory-womb: evolved and evolving things that outgrew their nursery.

At the tower's base, former bearing-ball fish have become boulder-sized spheres of living metal, their surfaces etched with patterns that look almost like equations. They roll endless patrol routes through the seafloor sediment, maintaining perimeters that correspond to no visible boundaries. Their clicking has deepened to subsonic pulses that travel through the stone itself.

The tower's surface hosts a vertical city of the transformed. Copper-wire anemones have grown massive here, their tendrils now thick as bridge cables, sparking with enough current to power the bioluminescent grid. In the spaces between them, descendants of the spot-welded crustaceans have built elaborate geometric nests—not for shelter but for art, perhaps, or prayer. Their constructions serve no survival purpose, only the expression of some mechanical imperative evolved beyond utility.

Inside the tower (for it is hollow, or perhaps it contains hollows), the space expands into chambers that exist in more than three dimensions. Here, the eel-things with perforated metal skin have grown into something magnificent and terrible. They've woven themselves together into living circuits, their bodies forming components in a vast computational array. Through their shared electrical fields, they think thoughts too complex for any single nervous system, solving problems about the nature of pressure and time and the marriage of meat and metal.

The water here is different from the factory's warm soup. It's cold, clear, and moves with intent—not currents but something closer to circulation, as if the tower itself is breathing. Specialized organisms filter the water as it rises through the hollow core, extracting trace metals and rare elements from the deep-sea flow. These nutrients travel upward to feed gardens of hybrid things that grow in spiraling galleries, each level maintaining its own microclimate of temperature and mineral concentration.

At the tower's apex—a full kilometer above the seafloor—exists the Translation Chamber. Here, the physics-softening effect from the factory has been refined, concentrated, made purposeful. Creatures enter the chamber in one form and leave in another, their transformations guided by patterns encoded in the tower's bioluminescent network. A simple fish might emerge with eyes that see magnetic fields. A crustacean might gain the ability to exist in multiple locations simultaneously.

The chamber pulses with the same rhythm as the phantom turbines in the drowned factory. It's calling to something—or someone.

Between the factory and the tower stretches a pilgrim's road of detritus and dream. The journey begins where the factory's influence fades, where water returns to merely being water and metal remembers it should sink. Here, the seafloor is littered with the failures—creatures who attempted the crossing before they were ready, their hybrid anatomies seized up like engines run without oil. But even in death, they serve. Their bodies become waypoints, their metallic bones accumulating into reefs that offer shelter to the next wave of travelers.

The Assembler knows about the tower. Sometimes, in the spaces between her labors, she feels its pull through the fiber optic cables that have become her hair. The data streams show her glimpses: the vertical city, the Translation Chamber, the thoughts too large for single minds. She understands what it is—not a destination but a next step, a graduate school for the transformed, a place where her children can become what she's prepared them to be.

She sends them out in waves. The bearing-ball schools that have learned to navigate collapsed space. The wire anemones that have developed mobility. The new things, unnamed and unnameable, that gestate in her workshop-organs until they're ready to swim beyond the factory's pocket dimension. She builds them with the journey in mind, encoding maps in their modified DNA, teaching them to read the magnetic trails that connect the two places.

The path is treacherous. Between the warm shallows of the factory and the cold deeps of the tower lies a transition zone where the water can't decide what it wants to be. Here, currents of different densities slide past each other like oil and vinegar, creating shear zones that can tear apart anything not perfectly adapted. The pressure changes are brutal—not gradually, but in steps, like descending through a series of airlocks where each door opens onto deeper darkness.

Predators hunt this zone, though "predator" is an inadequate word. These are things that have found their own path to transformation, becoming mouths that exist in more dimensions than they have prey. They feed not on flesh or metal but on the potential energy of transformation itself, draining the evolutionary momentum from passing creatures. The successful pilgrims learn to travel in groups, creating interference patterns that confuse the hunters' higher-dimensional senses.

Halfway through the journey, there's a place the Assembler thinks of as the Deciding Deep. Here, the continental shelf forms a natural amphitheater, and the water resonates with harmonics from both locations—the factory's industrial pulse and the tower's computational thrum. Creatures pause here, sometimes for days, their bodies running internal diagnostics, checking if their transformations are holding, if they're ready for the next phase. Some turn back. Others split apart, sending only their most evolved components forward while their base forms return to the factory for further refinement.

The successful ones—the ones who complete the crossing—carry messages back. Not in words or data packets, but in their transformed states. When a fully evolved bearer-fish returns to visit the factory, its perfect spherical form now containing fractional dimensions, its clicking speaking in mathematical proofs, the Assembler reads its body like a letter from her graduated children. She learns what works, what fails, what the tower needs.

She's building toward something. Each generation she sends is more sophisticated, more capable of surviving the journey and thriving in the tower's computation chambers. She's creating not just individual creatures but components of something larger—a distributed organism that spans from factory to tower, connected by the pilgrim road of transformed life.

Sometimes she wonders if she'll make the journey herself one day. When every surface in the factory has been colonized, when every possible hybrid has been birthed, when the pocket dimension has been fully explored and mapped. She could follow her children to the tower, undergo her own Translation, become part of the greater thinking that happens there.

But not yet. The factory still needs her, still has spaces unmapped and mutations unimagined. And perhaps this is her role—not to ascend but to send others ascending, to be the mother-mechanic who tends the garden where industrial death becomes organic divinity. The tower calls, but the factory holds, and between them grows a new kind of life: patient, purposeful, and perfectly adapted to a drowned world where the distinction between built and born dissolves like metal in warm water.

In the deep distance, the tower pulses. In the warm shallows, the Assembler works. Between them, the transformed swim through darkness, carrying evolution in their hybrid hearts.
