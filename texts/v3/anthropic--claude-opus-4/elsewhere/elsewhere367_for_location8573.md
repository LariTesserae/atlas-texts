## The Rust Gardens Above

Above the stone shelf from which the Cisterns hang, where the true sky opens into a vastness of oxidized orange and petroleum green, the Rust Gardens spread across kilometers of ancient industrial plateau. Here, in the inverse of the suspended city, water is the enemy—every drop a threat that must be channeled, diverted, or consumed before it can begin its corrosive work on the vast machinery that blooms across the landscape like mechanical flowers.

The Gardens are not gardens in any biological sense. They are the graveyard of ten thousand machines, each one frozen in a different stage of oxidation, their decay arrested and cultivated by the Verdigris Shepherds who tend them with obsessive care. Enormous harvesters stand like weathered monuments, their combine heads raised to the sky, internal mechanisms visible through rusted-away panels that reveal gears gone green with patina. Pipeline networks snake between them, some still carrying volatile chemicals that have thickened to the consistency of honey over centuries, others split open to reveal crystallized interiors that glitter with toxic beauty.

The Shepherds move through their domain on modified walking machines—six-legged maintenance platforms whose joints weep oil with every step. They wear environment suits of leather and brass, the seals constantly requiring adjustment against the caustic atmosphere. Their work is that of selective entropy: encouraging certain types of corrosion while preventing others, guiding the rust into patterns that maintain structural integrity while creating new compounds, new materials born from decay.

The flora here exists in defiance of logic. Oxidation roses bloom from exhaust pipes, their petals formed of paper-thin rust sheets that ring like bells in the chemical wind. Copper willows drip green tears that etch permanent trails in the metal ground. In the deepest corrosion pools, where acids have collected into mirror-smooth lakes, aluminum lotus plants spread leaves that tarnish and renew in daily cycles. These are not plants in any biological sense but chemical processes that have achieved a kind of stability, self-perpetuating reactions that mimic life.

The fauna is equally aberrant. Corrosion moths flutter between the machinery, their wings leaving trails of accelerated rust wherever they land. They feed on specific types of oxidation, their caterpillar stage burrowing through solid metal to pupate in chambers of their own chemical creation. Acid-runners skitter across the plateau on legs of crystallized petroleum products, hunting the smaller creatures that have evolved to metabolize metal oxides. In the deepest parts of the Gardens, where the machinery is oldest and strangest, there are reports of larger things—oxidation colonies that move like liquid rust across surfaces, consuming and converting everything they touch.

Weather here is a function of chemistry rather than climate. Petroleum rains sweep across the plateau in slow curtains, leaving rainbow slicks that take weeks to evaporate. When the winds shift, they carry clouds of metal particulates that settle on every surface, creating new substrates for corrosion. During the volatile seasons, spontaneous combustion creates pillars of fire that burn for days on chemical fuel, sending smoke signals visible from the edge of the world-shelf.

The Verdigris Shepherds speak a language of patinas and oxidation states, reading the health of their garden in the specific shades of decay. They know that rust is not one thing but a thousand different processes, each with its own beauty and danger. Their greatest artists are the Corrosion Composers, who use controlled oxidation to create vast murals across the sides of dead machines—images that shift and change as the rust deepens, telling stories that take decades to fully unfold.

At the garden's heart stands the Cathedral of Controlled Decay, a massive processing plant whose original purpose has been lost. The Shepherds have cultivated its corrosion for three hundred years, guiding it into spires and arches, creating a monument to entropy that somehow maintains perfect structural stability. Inside, acids drip in careful patterns, creating stalagmites of crystallized corrosion products that ring with different tones when struck. The Shepherds gather here for their oxidation ceremonies, breathing the metal-thick air and listening to the rust sing.

## The Connection: The Neutral Zone

Between the Rust Gardens above and the Suspended Cisterns below lies a band of tortured territory—the Neutral Zone, where water and oxidation meet in constant conflict. This is where the stone shelf reveals its true nature: not solid rock but a massive geological machine, its surface riddled with drainage channels, collection systems, and the ancient infrastructure that connects the two civilizations.

The Drainage Protocols govern all interaction between above and below. Water from the Gardens—contaminated with acids, metals, and petroleum products—must be processed through seventeen stages of filtration before it can enter the Cisterns' system. The Siphon-Born maintain the lower filters, while the Verdigris Shepherds tend the upper systems. They meet only in the Neutral Zone, at the Exchange Platforms that extend from both sides of the shelf.

These meetings follow rituals developed over centuries. The Siphon-Born arrive in pressurized walking tanks, their bodies surrounded by their native water, communicating through speakers that translate their liquid language into something the air-breathing Shepherds can understand. The Shepherds come in their environment suits, carrying samples of new corrosion products that might be valuable to the water-dwellers—certain metal oxides that their mechanimals require, acids that can be diluted and used in industrial processes.

Trade moves in both directions through the Vertical Conduits—massive pipes that penetrate the shelf. The Cisterns send up purified water in carefully rationed amounts, essential for the Shepherds' survival and for controlling certain types of corrosion. In return, the Gardens provide metal oxides, specialized chemicals born from decay, and most crucially, the Stability Compounds—substances that prevent the cisterns' metal walls from succumbing to rust, created through processes only the Shepherds understand.

The Neutral Zone has its own ecology, born from the mixture of both worlds. Transition species thrive here—organisms that can survive in both environments or that require the meeting of water and rust to complete their life cycles. Amphibious mechanimals crawl up from the Cisterns to feed on corrosion products before returning to the water. Oxide jellies form in the collection pools, neither fully liquid nor solid, moving with their own alien purpose.

The zone is maintained by the Boundary Wardens, individuals from both civilizations who have chosen to live in the margin. They wear hybrid suits—partially filled with water, partially breathing filtered air. Over time, their bodies adapt to this compromise existence, developing unique characteristics that make them unsuitable for full integration into either society. They become the translators, the negotiators, the keepers of the ancient balance.

In the deepest part of the Neutral Zone, where the oldest conduits run, stand the Memorial Chambers. Here, preserved in special atmospheric conditions, are the remains of the Deep Builders' original machinery—devices that show equal affinity for both water and oxidation, that seem designed to require both environments to function. The current inhabitants can operate these machines but not fully understand them, following maintenance procedures passed down like religious texts.

The relationship between the two civilizations is one of necessary symbiosis tinged with mutual incomprehension. The Siphon-Born see the Shepherds as voluntary inhabitants of hell, choosing to live in a world of decay and poison. The Shepherds view the Siphon-Born as trapped in their spheres, unable to appreciate the terrible beauty of controlled entropy. Yet both know that without the other, their world would fail—the Gardens would drown in uncontrolled water, the Cisterns would rust and collapse.

During the rare Eclipse Ceremonies, when certain astronomical conditions create total darkness over the shelf, representatives from both civilizations gather at the Primary Exchange. They perform the Renewal Protocols, ancient procedures that involve cycling specific chemicals through both systems, recalibrating the balance that keeps both worlds stable. In these moments, watching the rust glow with bioluminescent acids while water drops carry dissolved metals like liquid stars, the artificial division between their worlds becomes transparent. They are revealed as two sides of a single system, a planet-sized machine designed to process and reprocess, to decay and renew, to suspend and oxidize in eternal balance.

The young of both civilizations sometimes dream of the other side—Bracklings imagine the open sky, the freedom from water pressure, while Shepherd children wonder what it would be like to swim through industrial murk, to breathe liquid, to float. A few attempt to cross over permanently, undergoing radical surgical and chemical modifications. Most die in the attempt. Those who survive become something new—citizens of neither world, prophets of a possible future where the barrier might dissolve, where the inhabitants might reclaim the full scope of their ancestors' vision.

But for now, the separation holds. Above, the Rust Gardens spread their chemical beauty under the corroded sky. Below, the Suspended Cisterns hang in their industrial perfection. Between them, the stone shelf maintains its ancient vigil, processing the interactions that keep both worlds alive. The system persists, as it has for generations uncounted, as it will until the rust finally wins or the water finds a way to rise—or until someone, somewhere, remembers what the Deep Builders truly intended when they created this divided world, this machine of perpetual balance, this monument to the beauty of controlled decay and preserved suspension.

In the furthest reaches of both domains, where the Gardens meet the wasteland and where the newest Cisterns push the boundaries of sustainable suspension, changes are beginning. The rust patterns show new configurations. The water develops properties no one has catalogued. The mechanimals evolve in directions that serve no industrial purpose. Perhaps the system is not as stable as its inhabitants believe. Perhaps it was always intended to change, to grow, to become something else entirely. 

The Deep Builders left no manual, only the functioning system itself. And systems, as both the Siphon-Born and the Verdigris Shepherds know, have a way of finding their own purposes, their own evolution, their own strange and terrible destinations.
