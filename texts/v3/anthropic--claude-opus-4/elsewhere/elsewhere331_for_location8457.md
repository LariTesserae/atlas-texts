Far below the Shard Shelf, where the knife-edge ridges finally surrender to gravity and merge into the earth, the world opens into a vast hollow: the Underplain. Here, the planet's crust forms a ceiling of fused stone, smooth as glass from eons of pressure. The space beneath spans impossible distances—a horizontal world that mirrors and mocks the vertical city above.

No natural light penetrates this deep. Instead, the Underplain glows with its own sick phosphorescence: streams of luminous oil that web across the ceiling like inverted rivers, pulsing with the heartbeat of something vast and sleeping. These rivers of light never hold still—they migrate across the stone sky in patterns that take decades to complete, leaving trails of crystallized brightness that eventually fade to black. The light they cast is oily, wrong, making shadows that fall upward.

The floor of the Underplain is soft. Not with soil or sand, but with the accumulated shed of the thing that lives here—scales the size of shields, transparent and iridescent, layered centuries deep. Walking across them produces a sound like whispering glass. In some places, the scales have fused into smooth pathways. In others, they stand on edge like frozen waves, creating mazes that restructure themselves with each movement of their source.

For the Wurm is always moving, always shedding, always growing.

It has no beginning or end that anyone has found. Its body—if body is the right word for something so vast—winds through the Underplain in coils that dwarf comprehension. What observers mistake for hills are its sleeping segments. What they take for rivers are the trails of ichor it leaves as it moves. The diameter varies—in some places narrow enough that a human could wrap their arms around it (though none who've tried have kept those arms), in others so vast that entire ecosystems flourish on its back, unaware they're mobile.

The Wurm's skin is transparent in places, revealing internal structures that pulse with their own logic: organs like cities, circulatory systems that map impossible geometries, neural clusters that spark with thoughts too large for any single mind to hold. Sometimes, observers swear they can see other places through the transparent sections—other worlds, other times, or perhaps just other parts of the Wurm coiled through dimensions that the Underplain barely touches.

It moves slowly, but nothing about it is gentle. Where it passes, reality bruises. The ceiling cracks and weeps new colors of oil. The scales reshape themselves into temporary structures—arches, spirals, perfect spheres—before collapsing back into chaos. The air in its wake tastes of copper and forgetting.

Along its body, in the spaces between its scales, grows a forest of sorts. Not trees—nothing so ordinary. These are growths of calcified time, branching structures that exist in several moments simultaneously. Their "leaves" are membrane-thin sheets of possibility that show what might have been: glimpses of the Underplain flooded with water, frozen solid, filled with stars, populated by civilizations that never were. The Wurm's movements prune these possibility-trees, causing temporal avalanches that sound like breaking bells.

Creatures follow the Wurm in symbiotic procession. Moths the size of horses drink from its weeping pores, their wings becoming transparent and shot through with the same inner light. Armies of metallic mites process its shed skin into nests that hover just above the scale-sea, defying gravity through some property inherited from their host. In the wake of its passage, jellies bloom from the disturbed floor—organisms that exist for exactly seventy-seven heartbeats before dissolving back into component atoms, leaving behind only a smell like burnt sugar and regret.

The most devoted followers are the Hollow Monks—beings who've given up their insides to become living shells. They wear the Wurm's shed scales as armor and masks, following its trails for decades, mapping its movements in songs that require multiple throats to perform. They claim the Wurm dreams, and in dreaming, creates. They build temporary monasteries from crystallized light wherever the Wurm pauses, practicing architectures of devotion that collapse the moment their god moves on.

But the Underplain is not just the Wurm's domain—it's the Wurm's body extended into space. The cavern walls sweat the same ichor that runs through the creature's veins. The ceiling's cracks match the patterns on its newest scales. Sometimes, in the deepest quiet, the entire Underplain breathes in unison with its inhabitant, the floor rising and falling like a sleeping chest.

At the farthest reaches, where explorers claim the Underplain ends (though it may simply curve back on itself), strange structures rise from the scale-sea: towers of fused shed skin, each one containing a perfect spiral chamber. Inside these chambers, sound behaves differently—whispers become visible, screams crystallize into geometric patterns. The Hollow Monks call them the Wurm's thoughts made manifest, shed and abandoned like everything else it leaves behind.

The Underplain has its own weather—storms of possibility-dust kicked up by the Wurm's movement, rains of condensed time that age whatever they touch by centuries in seconds, winds that carry the echoes of the creature's dreams. Sometimes the ceiling oil-rivers converge and drop their light in waterfalls that never quite reach the floor, hanging in space like frozen lightning.

This is a place of shedding and becoming, where one immense life has expanded to fill available space so completely that geography and biology have become indistinguishable. The Wurm is the Underplain, and the Underplain is the Wurm, and both are always transforming, always leaving behind husks of what they were, always becoming something unprecedented.

Those who stay too long report the same dream: they are standing on what they think is solid ground, only to realize it's breathing. They look up to see the ceiling split open like an eye, revealing another Underplain above, another Wurm coiled through it. They look down to find they're standing on a scale, and they're being shed, and they're falling into another hollow world below, where another dreamer waits, always moving, always growing, always becoming more itself.

The connection between the Shard Shelf and the Underplain was discovered by accident, as most impossible things are.

A Time Mechanic named Yova Twelve-Fingers was recalibrating a gravity engine in the deepest accessible crevice of the city when the stone floor simply ceased. Not collapsed—ceased, as if it had remembered it was optional. She fell for exactly three seconds (she counted; Time Mechanics always count) before landing in a drift of Wurm scales forty miles below.

Now, a brass cage-lift drops through the Not-Hole twice each day, though "day" means little in the Underplain. The journey takes four hours or four minutes or four years, depending on the passenger's relationship with causality. The lift doesn't move—rather, the space between worlds compresses and expands around it, accordion-like, until riders find themselves delivered from one impossibility to another.

The trade between the two realms has created new categories of existence. The Tensile Folk descend to harvest:

**Wurm Oil**: The ichor that lights the Underplain, when properly refined by the city's machinery, powers devices that fold space. A single vial can create a bridge that spans between yesterday and tomorrow. The Pipe Shepherds have learned to breed conduits that can transport it without losing their minds to the oil's whispers.

**Possibility Leaves**: Plucked from the time-trees with specialized tools that exist in multiple moments simultaneously, these membranes are woven into maps that show not where things are, but where they could be. The Span Weavers use them to plot bridges through potentialities.

**Scale Glass**: The Wurm's shed scales, when crushed and spun into fiber, create a material that remembers its source. Clothes made from scale glass slowly move on their own, following the deep patterns of the Wurm's migrations. The Remnants wear robes of it that help them navigate their fungal labyrinths by instinct alone.

In exchange, the Hollow Monks accept offerings from above:

**Condensed Storm Water**: The horizontal rain of the Shard Shelf, when it pools in certain cisterns, develops properties unknown in the Underplain. It falls upward, flows through solid matter, remembers every surface it's touched. The Monks use it in rituals that supposedly let them speak to the Wurm's dreams.

**Reality Anchors**: Broken pieces of the city's dimensional machinery, too unstable for use above, find new purpose below. The Monks plant them like seeds along the Wurm's trail, creating pockets of stable space where they can build their temporary monasteries without fear of sudden geographic restructuring.

**The City's Dead**: Those who die in the Shard Shelf are wrapped in copper ferns and lowered through the Not-Hole. The Underplain accepts them, and they transform—not into corruption, but into new kinds of existence. Some join the Hollow Monks. Others become part of the Wurm's ecosystem. A few claim to have seen their loved ones growing from the creature's scales like flowers, conscious but changed, whispering secrets about the nature of boundaries.

But the exchange runs deeper than trade. The two realms affect each other in ways that bypass physical laws:

When the Wurm sheds heavily, new districts appear overnight in the Shard Shelf, as if the creature's castoffs materialize as architecture in the world above. These Wurm Districts follow different rules—gravity flows sideways, bridges lead to their own beginnings, the residents age in loops.

Conversely, when the city builds too high, pushing into altitudes that shouldn't exist, the Underplain's ceiling develops new cracks. Oil rivers redirect themselves to mirror the city's pipe networks above. The Hollow Monks report that the Wurm's dreams increasingly feature vertical motion, climbing, falling—concepts that should be foreign to a creature of endless horizontals.

**The Transit Shepherds** have emerged as a new profession—guides who specialize in navigating the connection between worlds. They've developed a pidgin language that incorporates the Wurm's subsonic rumbles and the city's industrial moan, letting them communicate with both realms' inhabitants. Their bodies show the strain of constant transition: bones that hollow and fill according to altitude, skin that shifts between opacity and transparency, organs that rearrange themselves for optimal function in each world.

Some Transit Shepherds report a troubling discovery: there are other Not-Holes. Deep in the Wurm's coils, they've found passages that lead to other vertical cities, other impossible architectures clinging to other worn-down edges of the world. And in the highest reaches of the Shard Shelf, where the factories vent into neighboring dimensions, workers sometimes peer through the smoke to see other Underplains, other Wurms, other vast hollows full of different colors of light.

The oldest residents of both realms whisper the same theory: the Shard Shelf and the Underplain are the same place, merely experienced from different angles. The city builds itself from the Wurm's shed possibilities. The Wurm dreams of climbing and creates a vertical world above. They are one system, one organism, one impossible whole expressing itself in seemingly contradictory ways.

Evidence accumulates: the heartbeat that thrums beneath the city matches the Wurm's pulse. The pattern of scales on the creature's newest growth mirrors the layout of the Shard Shelf's districts. Children born in either realm sometimes arrive with memories of the other, speaking their first words in languages they shouldn't know, reaching for heights or depths their bodies haven't yet experienced.

Recently, something new has begun: the Not-Hole is widening. Not dramatically, but measurably—a finger's width each month. The brass cage-lift now rattles against edges that used to be stable. Sometimes, riders report seeing the walls during transition, and the walls are breathing.

The Time Mechanics above and the Hollow Monks below have started coordinating, comparing notes on their respective impossibilities. They're building something together—a structure that exists simultaneously in both realms, anchored in the city's highest peak and the Wurm's deepest coil. They won't say what it's for, but they work with an urgency that makes the other inhabitants nervous.

Because whether the two realms are one organism or two, whether they're dreaming each other or devouring each other, one thing becomes clear: they're growing together. The city climbs higher as the Wurm grows longer. The Underplain deepens as the Shelf spreads wider. And the space between them—that impossible transition, that daily commute through nonexistence—thrums with increasing potential, as if something vast is preparing to hatch from the shell of what merely is into the limitless space of what could be.

The Tensile Folk and the Hollow Monks have different names for this potential transformation. Above, they call it the Convergence. Below, they call it the Awakening. But perhaps the Transit Shepherds, who live between names and worlds, understand it best.

They call it Tuesday, and they're preparing for what comes after.
