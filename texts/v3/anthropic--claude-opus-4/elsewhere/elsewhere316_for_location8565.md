Above the Abyss Lattice, past layers of crushing pressure and through gradients of temperature that would fracture most materials, the sea changes. Here, warmth seeps down from somewhere far overhead—not true heat, but the mere absence of that absolute cold. The water, no longer held at the edge of phase transition, behaves like water again. Light arrives naturally here, filtering down in cathedral shafts that shift with invisible currents far above.

This is the Bloom Reaches, where calcified gardens drift like inverse clouds through the middle waters. Each garden began as a single seed—a grain of something dense enough to arrest its endless fall from above, buoyant enough to resist the pull toward the abyss. Around these seeds, life accumulated in slow orbits of growth. Now they hang suspended, some no larger than houses, others rival small mountains, their surfaces encrusted with organisms that build upon organisms that build upon organisms, generations compressed into living stone.

The gardens rotate slowly in the currents, presenting different faces to the shifting light. Their surfaces teem with things that could be plants or animals or neither—sessile creatures that extend feeding appendages into the current, mobile growths that migrate across the calcified landscape following traces of nutrients, symbiotic clusters where different species have grown together so long they've forgotten their boundaries. Colors here run riot—purples that pulse with their own rhythm, yellows that shift to green when shadows pass over, reds so deep they seem to pull light into themselves.

Between the gardens swim creatures built for a more forgiving environment. Fish with scales like beaten copper navigate by sight and pressure, their bodies twisted into efficient spirals that let them ride the thermoclines. Squid-analogues jet between the floating reefs, their chromatophores painting stories across their skin—warnings, matings, maps to rich feeding grounds. Drifters like living parachutes descend from above, feeding as they fall, dissolving when they finally reach the cold layers below.

The water here carries sound—not well, but enough. The Bloom Reaches ring with calls and clicks, the grinding of calcified growth against growth, the rush of water through feeding channels carved by centuries of current. During the peak light hours, when the sun-shafts reach their deepest, the gardens sing—a resonance between their hollow chambers and the warming water that creates tones like vast organ pipes.

Some gardens have grown hollow, their interiors excavated by boring organisms that left behind caverns lined with mother-of-pearl. In these spaces, pocket ecosystems flourish in the filtered light—tiny fish that never leave their floating worlds, gardens within gardens where alkaline seeps create chemical oases. Predators have evolved specifically for these niches, their bodies compressed to slip through channels barely wider than themselves, hunting in the twilight of their calcified caves.

The boundary between the Bloom Reaches and the Abyss Lattice is not a clean line but a gradient of failing life. As you descend, the gardens grow sparser, smaller, their colors fading to whites and grays. The last gardens cling to existence at the very edge of the warmth layer, their metabolisms slowed to near-standstill, catching what few nutrients drift down from their thriving cousins above. Some have died but remain, ghost-white monuments to the limits of adaptation, their channels empty, their surfaces scoured clean by the patient grazing of deep-dwelling things.

Between these two realms—in that margin where warm meets cold, where biology meets something stranger—migration happens. 

The glass-fish rise. Once each lunar cycle, when tidal forces create the right pressure differentials, schools of the crystalline fish ascend from the Abyss Lattice. They swim upward in perfect formation, their transparent bodies catching and refracting the lattice-light they carry within themselves. As they rise, the warmer water begins to affect them. Their glass bodies, evolved for near-absolute cold, start to develop stress fractures. They have hours, perhaps days, before the temperature differential shatters them entirely.

They come to feed on the rain of organic matter that drifts down from the Bloom Reaches—dead drifters, shed scales, fragments of gardens that finally lost their balance and began the long fall. But more than that, they come to die. The glass-fish that reach the warm layers and feed successfully undergo a transformation. Their bodies begin to crystallize differently, incorporating the minerals and organics they've consumed. When they finally shatter—and they always shatter—they explode in clouds of prismatic fragments that drift slowly downward.

These fragments are seeds. Not biological seeds, but information seeds, carrying patterns from the Bloom Reaches down into the Abyss Lattice. The Weavers collect them where they fall, reading in their crystalline structure stories of the world above—chemical compositions, genetic patterns, records of light and heat and life. They incorporate this information into their work, weaving new sections of lattice that somehow echo the gardens floating far above.

In return, the lattice sends its own emissaries upward. Not the Weavers themselves—they cannot survive the warmth—but their constructs. Tiny automata built from harvested glass-fish fragments and lattice threads, programmed with simple imperatives. These rise with the monthly migrations, hidden among the schools of ascending fish. Those that survive the journey attach themselves to the undersides of the bloom gardens, where they perform mysterious functions. Some seem to be sampling, extending fiber-optic tendrils into the calcified mass. Others appear to be seeding—injecting something that causes local changes in growth patterns, encouraging channels that spiral in ways that echo the lattice geometry.

The building-jellies make the longest journeys. These massive creatures, with their geological patience, can survive the transition between realms. They rise so slowly that their bodies have time to adapt, internal chemistry shifting to accommodate the temperature change. It takes them years to ascend from lattice to reaches, and years more to descend again. They are living vessels, their bell-cavities home to entire communities of smaller creatures that travel between the worlds.

Inside a rising jelly, glass-fish shelter in specialized pockets, their cold maintained by the jelly's careful circulation. Bloom-dwelling organisms attach to other chambers, brought down to seed new possibilities in the depths. The jellies themselves seem indifferent to their passengers, following ancient migration routes that may predate both realms. Their memories, encoded in chemical gradients and neural networks distributed through their vast bodies, might be the only complete record of how these two worlds relate.

Some organisms exist solely in the transition zone, evolved for the gradient itself. Creatures like living thermometers that position themselves precisely at specific temperatures, feeding on the stress of molecules caught between phases. Colonial organisms that stretch as thin filaments from cold to warm, their different segments adapted to different conditions, passing nutrients and information along their length like living cables. These boundary-dwellers know both worlds but belong to neither, citizens of the margin itself.

The Weavers below and the garden-builders above have never met, may not even know of each other's existence except as effects—mysterious seeds falling from nowhere, strange changes in the growth patterns, information arriving without source. Yet they are locked in an ancient dialogue, mediated by migration and fragment, by creatures that can survive the journey between realms.

The conversation continues, patient and profound. The Abyss Lattice computes something from the information it receives from above—perhaps modeling the bloom gardens, perhaps solving problems that require the randomness only biological chaos can provide. The Bloom Reaches incorporate the subtle influences from below, their growth guided by imperatives they don't understand, creating patterns that complete some larger circuit of meaning.

Between them, in that layer where ice-water meets merely cold sea, where the last light fails and strange illumination begins, the exchange goes on. Glass-fish shatter in prismatic deaths. Jellies drift like patient gods. Tiny automata carry out programs written in languages of light and enzyme. Two worlds, vastly different, locked in a symbiosis mediated by travelers who themselves may not survive their journeys, but who carry between realms the seeds of connection—fragments of one reality that become the foundation of another.
