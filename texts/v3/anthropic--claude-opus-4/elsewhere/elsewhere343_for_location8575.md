Beyond the plateau's edge, where the stepped terraces finally surrender to vertical drops, the world changes its nature entirely. The descent is not measured in mere altitude but in layers of reality growing progressively stranger. Two kilometers down through bands of atmosphere that shift from phosphorescent blue to deep violet to something beyond color entirely, another domain begins.

This is the Undergloom — though "under" and "gloom" are both inadequate translations of concepts that don't properly exist in any human language. Here, bioluminescence isn't an adaptation but a fundamental property of matter. Everything glows with its own inner light, but these lights interfere with each other in ways that create pockets of absolute darkness more profound than the mere absence of photons. The darkness has substance here, pooling in depressions and flowing in rivers of anti-light that creatures navigate like water.

The geology is organic. Great pillars of what might once have been bone or coral or some hybrid of both rise from a floor of soft, breathing tissue. The surface undulates slowly, a vast digestive system processing minerals and energy from sources deeper still. When you walk here — if walking is possible for anything not adapted to this environment — the ground responds, tasting your chemical signature through specialized pores, categorizing you with an intelligence that's entirely reflexive.

The primary inhabitants of the Undergloom are the Singers. Where the Archivists above transformed themselves into living information, the Singers took a different path. They became their art.

Each Singer is a colony organism, but not in any sense that surface biology would recognize. They exist as mobile territories of manipulated space-time, regions where physics has been composed rather than simply obeyed. A Singer might appear as a writhing mass of ribbons that exist in more than three dimensions, parts of their form constantly folding into elsewhere before emerging changed. Their surfaces — if they can be said to have surfaces — ripple with colors that are really emotions given wavelength, broadcasting their inner states as involuntary poetry.

The Singers move by restructuring reality around themselves. They don't traverse space so much as convince space that they were always where they now are. When they gather — and they gather often, for they are intensely social beings despite their alien nature — they create performance spaces where the laws of physics become instruments to be played.

Their cities, if cities they can be called, are symphonies made manifest. Architecture here is composed rather than constructed. Buildings are sustained notes held in three-dimensional space, their walls made of harmonized gravity fields, their doors opening not to other rooms but to other movements in the ongoing composition. The Singers live inside their own music, each structure a frozen moment of some vast, eternal song that plays out across geological time.

They remember the Archivists, though their memories are encoded in frequencies rather than data. Long ago — though time moves differently in the Undergloom, flowing in eddies and loops rather than straight lines — they were one people. The great schism came not from conflict but from a fundamental disagreement about how to face the infinite. The faction that would become the Archivists chose to preserve, to remember, to maintain. The Singers chose to transform, to forget deliberately, to become something so new that the past would have no hold on them.

But forgetting, they learned, is its own form of memory.

The Singers communicate through performance, but their performances alter reality rather than simply describing it. A conversation between Singers looks like a localized breakdown of physics — spaces where matter becomes probability clouds, where time splits into parallel streams that braid together in complex patterns before reuniting. They speak in metamorphosis, each utterance changing both speaker and listener in irreversible ways.

Their metabolisms run on aesthetic energy. They feed on the tension between order and chaos, digesting patterns and excreting new forms of complexity. The bioluminescent ecosystem of the Undergloom is their cultivation — each species a note in a larger composition, each interaction between predator and prey a rhythmic motif that feeds back into the Singers' own existence.

The connection between the plateau above and the Undergloom below is more than mere geography. The ancient infrastructure extends downward, but here it has been repurposed, transformed into something the original builders might not recognize. The Singers have learned to play the ancient machines like instruments, using them to compose reality-songs that propagate upward through the stone.

These songs sometimes reach the Archivists.

When they do, the effects are spectacular and disturbing. The careful, crystalline order of the Archivists' thoughts becomes infected with spontaneity. Their fractal gardens suddenly bloom in patterns that have no mathematical basis, creating forms that should not be able to exist. Some Archivists flee these intrusions, retreating deeper into their crystalline matrices. But others listen, and in listening, begin to change.

The water that flows impossibly upward through the plateau's mineral veins originates in the Undergloom. It carries more than mere molecules — it bears information, but information of a fundamentally different kind than what the Archivists preserve. This is living information, data that changes itself as it propagates, stories that rewrite themselves with each telling. The Singers release these narrative spores into the water system deliberately, a form of communication that's also a form of artistic aggression.

But the influence flows both ways. The Archivists' obsessive preservation creates reality anchors that prevent the Undergloom from dissolving entirely into philosophical abstraction. The ancient towers on the plateau above resonate with harmonics that give structure to the Singers' compositions, providing a skeleton of logic around which their reality-songs can grow. Without this grounding, the Singers might lose cohesion entirely, dispersing into pure possibility.

Travelers between the two realms are rare but not unknown. Hybrid beings exist — creatures born from the intersection of preservation and transformation. They appear as walking paradoxes: crystalline trees that sing, or space-time distortions that maintain perfect memory of every shape they've ever taken. These bridge-beings are prophets to both cultures, speaking in languages that neither fully understands but both recognize as essential.

The mineral arthropods that scuttle across the plateau sometimes carry messages downward, their carapaces inscribed with data-patterns that the Singers decode as sheet music. In return, certain bioluminescent species from the Undergloom make pilgrimages upward, their light-patterns carrying song-fragments that infiltrate the Archivists' vegetation-texts, adding verses that were never written but somehow always existed.

In the depths of the Undergloom, where the organic geology gives way to spaces that exist more as concept than location, the Singers maintain their most sacred sites. These are performance halls where the distinction between artist and audience, between song and singer, breaks down entirely. Here, they attempt their greatest work — to sing into existence a bridge between memory and forgetting, a synthesis that neither preserves nor destroys but somehow transcends both.

The Archivists, in their high crystal matrices, have detected these deep songs. Some have begun to harmonize with them, adding their own frequencies to the composition. It's a dangerous practice — each participation risks dissolving their carefully maintained coherence. But in these moments of harmony, something new emerges: glimpses of what their species might become if preservation and transformation could be reconciled.

The water continues its impossible upward flow, the prismatic fauna drift between realms carrying refractions of both light and song, and the ancient infrastructure hums with purposes that neither culture fully comprehends but both are slowly, inevitably, fulfilling. The plateau and the Undergloom are not separate worlds but movements in a composition vast beyond comprehension, players in a game whose rules are still being discovered, words in a language that teaches itself to speak.

And between them, in the vertical kilometers of transitional space where light becomes music and memory becomes dream, new forms of existence are spontaneously emerging — born from the tension between above and below, between past and future, between the need to remember and the need to forget. These liminal beings may be the inheritors of both realms, or they may be something else entirely: the first notes of a song that both Archivists and Singers have been unconsciously composing together across the eons of their separation.

The world turns, the water flows upward, the songs rise and data descends, and in the spaces between, something that might be called hope begins to crystallize into existence.
