Above the Singing Hollows, where the stone finally admits defeat and allows the sky to touch the earth, sprawls the Burn. This is a landscape of glass and ash, where the sand has been fused into vast sheets that stretch to every horizon, their surfaces rippled and warped like frozen waves. The glass is black in some places, green in others, shot through with veins of gold and silver where metals were caught in whatever catastrophe created this desolation. Nothing grows here. Nothing has grown here for so long that the very idea of growth seems foreign to the place.

The sun beats down without mercy, and the glass amplifies it, creating a heat that shimmers the air into constant mirages. Distance becomes meaningless when the ground itself acts as a lens, bending light in ways that make a stone seem a mountain, a crack appear as a canyon. The glass sings in the temperature changes—expanding in the day's heat with sounds like breaking bells, contracting at night with notes so low they're felt rather than heard. These thermal songs create a different music than the eternal chorus below, a daily cycle of crystalline screams and groans that marks time in a place where nothing else changes.

Cracks web across the glass plains, some hair-thin, others wide enough to swallow a caravan. These cracks are not random—they follow patterns that suggest intention, as if the glass were shattered according to some vast design. In the depths of the larger fissures, the glass gives way to ordinary stone, and there, shielded from the worst of the sun, small communities eke out an existence. They call themselves the Shard-Dancers, for their survival depends on reading the glass, knowing which sheets will hold weight and which will collapse at a footfall.

The Shard-Dancers have adapted to their environment in ways that would seem impossible to those who dwell in softer lands. Their feet are wrapped in layers of leather treated with rendered fat from the few creatures that survive here, creating soles that can withstand the burning glass at noon. They wear robes of metallic fabric that reflects the heat, and their eyes are protected by visors carved from smoky quartz found in the deeper cracks. Every movement is calculated, every step placed with intention, for the glass remembers violence—shatter one sheet carelessly, and the crack might propagate for miles, destroying paths that have been safe for generations.

They travel mostly at dawn and dusk, when the glass sings its transition songs and the temperature briefly equalizes. During these times, the mirages settle, and true distances can be judged. The Shard-Dancers have mapped the safe routes across their territory, marking them with cairns of colored glass that catch the light in specific ways, creating a navigation system visible only to those who know how to read the angles of refraction.

Water is beyond precious here—it's nearly mythical. The Shard-Dancers survive on moisture harvested from the air during the brief pre-dawn hours when dew might form on the coldest glass surfaces. They've developed techniques for creating temperature differentials that encourage condensation, building elaborate structures of angled glass that channel what little moisture exists into collection points. A master water-harvester might gather a few mouthfuls in a night's work, enough to keep a small family alive for another day.

But water is not the only thing the Shard-Dancers harvest from their unforgiving home. The glass itself, when properly worked, has properties that make it valuable beyond measure. Some sheets, when struck at the correct angle with tools of specific metals, ring with tones that can shatter stone, cut through metal, or—most valuable of all—disrupt the songs of the stone-singers far below. These sonic weapons are traded carefully, for their use must be precisely controlled. A glass blade that sings the wrong note might fracture not just its target but the wielder as well.

The creatures of the Burn are few but remarkable. Glass serpents, their bodies made of living crystal, swim through the sheets as if they were water, their passage leaving ripples frozen in the surface. They hunt the ember mice—small rodents whose fur glows with stored heat, allowing them to survive the cold nights by radiating warmth stolen from the day. Shadowhawks circle overhead, their black wings absorbing heat to power their flight, casting moving patches of blessed coolness on the glass below. These birds never land, spending their entire lives in the air, mating and even sleeping on the wing.

Most disturbing are the Vitric Men—humanoid figures made entirely of fused glass, their bodies containing bubbles and inclusions that suggest they were once flesh and blood, transformed by the same event that created the Burn. They stand motionless during the day, indistinguishable from natural glass formations, but at night they walk the plains, their footsteps ringing like wind chimes. The Shard-Dancers avoid them, for the Vitric Men seem drawn to living warmth, and their touch brings vitrification—turning flesh to glass in creeping patterns that no healer can reverse.

At the center of the Burn, where the glass is thickest and most pure, stands the Lens—a massive dome of perfect crystal that focuses sunlight into a beam that can be seen for hundreds of miles. The ground beneath it is molten during the day, a pool of liquid glass that bubbles and flows, cooling each night into new patterns. The Shard-Dancers consider this place sacred and forbidden, for those who stare too long into the Lens report visions of the time before the Burn, when this was a forest of silver trees under a different sun. Some say the Lens shows not the past but another present, a world where the catastrophe never happened, visible but forever unreachable through the barrier of melted time.

Between these two realms—the singing darkness below and the burning silence above—exists a twilight zone, a place where shafts and crevices connect the two worlds. Here, where the eternal chill of the Hollows meets the blazing heat of the Burn, strange phenomena occur. The temperature differentials create powerful air currents, howling winds that carry the songs of the stone-singers up into the glass plains, where they become trapped in the sheets, playing back at random when the thermal stress grows too great.

The Choir-Keepers know of these connection points, marking them with complex acoustic signatures that warn the herds away. For sometimes, when the conditions are right, glass serpents find their way down, their crystalline bodies singing discordant notes that drive the stone-singers into panicked stampedes. Even worse are the times when Vitric Men discover the shafts, their glass bodies immune to the pressure changes that would kill living things, descending into the galleries where their presence transforms the careful acoustic balance into chaos.

The Shard-Dancers, too, know of these passages, though they call them the Breathing Holes. In times of desperate drought, the bravest among them will descend partway, seeking the moisture that rises from below. They never go deep—the darkness terrifies them as much as the light terrorizes the Choir-Keepers. But they've learned to lower crystalline containers on chains of linked glass, gathering the condensation that forms where hot air meets cold stone, bringing back water that tastes of minerals and darkness.

Trade between the two peoples exists, though neither fully trusts the other. At the margin spaces, where neither heat nor cold dominates, they've established neutral grounds. The Shard-Dancers bring glass tools, focused light captured in special containers, and news of the surface world. The Choir-Keepers offer worked copper, preserved foods that need no sunlight to prepare, and sometimes—rarely—the crystallized remains of ancient stone-singers, whose resonant properties make them invaluable for creating the sound-tools both peoples depend on.

The exchanges are fraught with difficulty. The Shard-Dancers must wrap themselves in layers of insulation to endure even the transitional temperatures, while the Choir-Keepers must shield their sensitive ears from the glass-songs that the surface dwellers carry in their very footsteps. Communication happens through intermediaries—children of mixed heritage who can tolerate both environments for short periods, or through carved messages in the stone of the boundary spaces.

These hybrid children, called the Threshold Walkers by both peoples, are the only ones who truly understand the connection between the two realms. They speak of patterns that repeat above and below—how the branching of the glass cracks mirrors the carved passages of the Hollows, how the songs of the stone-singers resonate at frequencies that match the thermal expansion of the Burn. Some believe that both realms were created by the same event, that the Hollows were carved as a refuge from whatever created the glass desert above, or perhaps that the Burn was caused by something that escaped from the deepest galleries.

The Threshold Walkers tell stories that neither people likes to hear—that the Vitric Men were once the builders of the Hollows, transformed when they tried to return to the surface. That the stone-singers are larvae, and their adult form is the glass serpent. That the water weeping through the galleries carries more than minerals—it carries memory, intent, and slow purpose, gradually transforming both realms according to some plan too vast and slow for human perception.

At the deepest point of connection, where a great shaft plunges from the heart of the Burn straight down into the most ancient galleries of the Hollows, something impossible exists. The glass here doesn't just melt from the heat—it flows upward, defying gravity, while water from below falls up, the two substances mixing in mid-air to create formations that exist in neither liquid nor solid state. The sound here is unbearable, a mixture of the stone-singer choruses and the thermal songs of the glass creating interference patterns that manifest as physical force.

Both peoples tell legends of those who've entered this space and returned changed. They speak in languages that combine the clicks of the Choir-Keepers with the crystalline tones of the Shard-Dancers, and their bodies show signs of transformation—skin that gleams like glass in some lights, eyes that see in echo as well as light. They claim to have spoken with the original inhabitants, to understand the purpose of both realms, but their explanations make no sense, full of concepts for which no words exist in any human tongue.

These Prophet-Walkers, as they're known, say that the Burn and the Hollows are not two places but one, separated not by space but by state—like ice and steam, different phases of the same substance. They speak of a coming convergence, when the glass will sing in harmony with the stone-singers, when the water will remember how to flow upward and the light will learn to echo. Whether this is prophecy or madness, none can say, but both peoples have noticed changes—new cracks in the glass that spiral like the carved passages below, stone-singers whose crystals gleam with trapped light, water that sometimes, impossibly, tastes of sunshine.

The connection runs deeper than any map can show. In the Burn, where the glass is clearest, sometimes you can see straight through to spaces that should be solid earth, glimpses of galleries lit by bioluminescence. In the Hollows, in chambers where the acoustic conditions are perfect, the stone-singers' songs sometimes return changed, carrying the thermal music of the glass plains. The two realms bleed into each other in ways that violate ordinary understanding of space and substance.

This is the truth that the Threshold Walkers know and cannot fully communicate: the Burn and the Hollows are wound around each other like the strands of a rope, existing in the same space but different conditions. The catastrophe that created one created both, not as cause and effect but as complementary aspects of a single transformation. The builders of the Hollows didn't flee downward to escape—they carved downward to create, shaping a space where the principles that would create the Burn could be inverted, contained, given different expression.

And in the spaces between—in the shafts where air screams between pressure differentials, in the boundary zones where neither realm fully dominates, in the bodies of those who walk between the worlds—something new is being born. Not human, not stone-singer, not glass serpent or Vitric Man, but something that partakes of all these natures. The water remembers, the glass refracts, the sound echoes, and in these interactions, patterns emerge that suggest intention vast and alien and patient as stone.

The Singing Hollows below, where darkness has weight and sound has substance. The Burn above, where light has shattered space into sheets of frozen fire. And between them, threading through cracks and chasms and the transformed bodies of those who dare the crossing, a connection that rewrites the rules of both realms. This is a world broken into phases, each phase complete in itself but incomplete without the other, waiting for a convergence that the prophets promise and the wise fear, when the music of above and below will synchronize, and the true shape of what was made here will finally reveal itself.

In the deepest galleries and on the highest glass peaks, the same truth echoes: this is not a world with two places, but a single system expressing itself in extremes, each extreme necessary for the other's existence. The Choir-Keepers need the Burn to create the temperature differentials that drive the air currents that bring moisture to their stone gardens. The Shard-Dancers need the Hollows to create the acoustic resonances that tell them which glass sheets are stable. Neither realm is complete—both are halves of a whole that was broken not by catastrophe but by design, a separation that preserves both by keeping them apart while binding them together through the very forces that seem to divide them.

This is the world: a place of extremes that touch, of oppositions that require each other, of transformations that run deeper than flesh or stone or glass. A place where survival means understanding not just your own realm but the shadow it casts, the echo it creates, the reflection it demands. The Singing Hollows and the Burn, dark and light, sound and silence, bound together by the spaces between, where prophets walk and possibilities wait to be born.
