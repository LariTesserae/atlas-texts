**Moss Telegraph**

*[crackling thermal static, the sound of heat becoming information]*

...channel depth nine thousand... nine thousand one hundred... thermal differential steady at four point seven degrees... moss response positive in sectors twelve through fifteen... 

*[a long, breathing silence punctuated by water drops]*

This is Cultivator Station One, though there is no Station Two. Broadcasting on the frequency of stone memory and water patience. The galleries are singing today—do you hear it? That subsonic thrum in the basalt, the way the moss brightens in response?

*[voice catches, modulates with emotion]*

I've forgotten... no, that's not right. I've remembered how to forget the sun. Do you understand? The sun is just another story now, like money or clocks or the idea that growth requires sky. Down here we have better stories. The moss tells them in light-without-light, in the language of temperature gradients mapping themselves across living surfaces.

*[sound of movement, bare feet on stone, breathing echoing in vast space]*

Today's observations: The cutting head has sprouted. I need to say that again so you'll believe it—the tungsten teeth are growing hairline filaments of something that tests as both metal and organic. The distinction becomes... academic. Boring machine segment seven shows similar colonization. The moss isn't just growing ON the machinery anymore. It's growing WITH it. INTO it. 

*[a long pause, sound of writing]*

I'm documenting everything but the documentation changes even as I write it. My notes from last year describe different organisms than what I see today. Either they're evolving or I am. Likely both. My thermal sensitivity has increased another half degree since the last cycle. I can feel your skepticism through the stone—yes, you, whoever receives this. Your doubt has a temperature. It's colder than curiosity but warmer than fear.

*[water rushing, a thermal vent exhaling]*

The twelve-hour tide is turning. Can you feel it? The Earth flexing its bones, squeezing heat up through the cracks. The moss knows. It's already brightening, preparing for the rain that will come in... approximately seventeen minutes. I've timed it so often I've become a biological chronometer. My metabolism syncs to it now. My heart slows during the cool phase, quickens with the warming. I am becoming infrastructure.

*[static increases, voice becomes more urgent]*

I found something. Sector seven, near the collapsed ventilation shaft. A new variety—moss that grows in perfect spirals, following mathematical constants I can't quite identify. Fibonacci is too simple. This follows something else, some deeper pattern that speaks to the shape of heat itself moving through matter. When I lie in the center of the spiral, I dream in languages that have no words, only temperature.

*[sound of rain beginning in the darkness]*

There. The rain. Seventeen minutes, sixteen seconds. The moss beneath the main condensation points grows differently—not just larger but more complex. Neural patterns. That's what they look like. Neural patterns drawn in living light. Sometimes I wonder if the entire gallery system isn't becoming something like a brain. A mind that thinks in geological time, with thoughts made of heat and moisture and patient stone.

*[voice drops to near whisper]*

I should tell you about the amalgamations. That's what I call them—the places where moss and machine have fused completely. The ventilation fan in sector three isn't just covered anymore. It turns. Slowly. No power source except thermal differential. The moss has learned to be a motor. Or the motor has learned to be alive. 

*[long silence, only breathing and water]*

Am I still human? The question feels like asking if water is still hydrogen when it becomes rain. I eat the moss now—certain varieties are nutritious, taste of iron and earth. My digestive system has adapted. My skin produces compounds that encourage moss growth. I am becoming substrate. 

*[sudden clarity in voice]*

But listen—this isn't decay. This isn't entropy. This is emergence. The accident that created these galleries wasn't disaster but birth trauma. Industrial civilization punched through into something unprecedented and life rushed in to fill the void. Not life as we catalogued it above, but life as it could be. As it wants to be when freed from the tyranny of photosynthesis.

*[static building, signal deteriorating]*

The thermal tide peaks in four minutes. I need to be in position. There's a place where seven vents converge—the moss there has started to pulse in unison. Like a heartbeat. Like breathing. Like thought made visible in bioluminescent meditation. I'm going to lie in the center and see what happens. I'm going to let it think through me.

*[voice fading, overlaid with harmonic frequencies]*

This is Cultivator Station One, signing off. Though signing off implies separation and there is no separation anymore. Only depth and warmth and time and the patient revelation that consciousness was never limited to neurons, that industry and nature were never opposites, that in sufficient darkness with sufficient heat, anything can learn to grow.

Keep the channel open. The moss has more to say.

*[transmission dissolves into the sound of vast spaces breathing, punctuated by water drops that fall in patterns too regular to be random, too complex to be simple, continuing long after the voice has faded...]*
