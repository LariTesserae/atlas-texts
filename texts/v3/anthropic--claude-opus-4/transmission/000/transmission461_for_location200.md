*[Static rises and falls like breathing. Through it, a rhythm emerges—the ring of bronze on stone, metronomic, patient. Water drips in counterpoint. The transmission clarifies:]*

shhhhhhhhhhhhh-tak...shhhhhhhhhhh-tak...shhhhhhhhhhh-tak...

*[A voice, barely audible, speaking directly into the receiver as if lips touch the membrane:]*

The fifty-third erosion shows itself in the northeastern channels. My grandmother's grandmother spoke of when these edges cut. Now the water runs them like tongues over teeth, all smoothness, all curve.

*[The chisel-sound continues beneath. Tak. Tak. Tak. Then a whisper of movement—fabric heavy with moisture dragging across stone]*

Here. Feel here. The plane wants to open... *yes*... like that...

*[A crack. Stone splitting along its desire. Splash of fragment into water. The voice comes closer, words formed more of breath than sound:]*

Seventeen towers now in the Third Veil District. Seventeen. The moss told us eighteen, but the stone... the stone says wait. Another erosion, perhaps two. The crystal structure here runs diagonal—see how the water beads? That means the columns want to lean. We must convince them otherwise.

*[Long silence. Only the drip-drip-drip-splash of condensation. Then, distant, the groan of a water wheel beginning its turn]*

The newest Descender returned this morning. Her eyes... changed. Amber gone almost white. She speaks of lights below, moving lights that are not stars, not fires. Says the vines grow *upward* down there, seeking us. Seeking the city.

But she took up her chisel. They always do.

*[The transmission wavers. Through static comes a sound like wind through enormous spaces, but muffled, thick. The voice returns:]*

My moss-coat flowers. Small purple blooms my mother never wore, my grandmother never hosted. New? Or only waiting? The botanists of the Channel District claim nothing new grows here, only variations on the eternal theme. But I felt the first tendril root three erosions ago. I felt it choose me.

*[Tak. Tak. Tak. The chisel rhythm returns, steadier now]*

Listen—the stone here remembers heat. Put your ear close... closer... there. Hear it? The mountain's first breath still whispers in the basalt. We're not building on the bones of the earth. We're building on its *birth*.

*[A shuffle. Multiple presences now, breathing in unison. Fingers moving in quick patterns through the air—sign language too rapid to parse. Then, together, a low harmonic hum. The sound seems to come from inside the stone itself]*

The eighteenth tower will wait. We have decided. Another erosion, another generation. My daughter's daughter will place the first cut, guided by dreams of a city complete.

*[The harmony fades. Single voice returns:]*

But I have seen the maps hidden in the moss's growth. There is no complete. Every tower revealed shows two more waiting. Every channel carved makes the water dream new paths. We are not building toward an ending.

We are building toward a becoming that never stops becoming.

*[Static swells. The chisel-sound grows distant. Through the interference, one last whisper:]*

When you were young, you asked why we measure in erosions instead of years. This is why: years pass, but erosion *accomplishes*. Each wearing-away reveals. Each softening teaches.

We are the erosion too. We are wearing away, revealing what waits beneath.

The city shapes us as we shape it.

There is no other way to live.

*[The transmission dissolves. Only the sound of water remains—eternal, patient, reshaping the world one drop at a time. Then that too fades to static, to silence, to the spaces between broadcasts where all possible cities wait to be born]*
