*[Static crackles, resolves into the sound of water dripping in vast spaces. A voice emerges—not quite human anymore, speaking in overlapping layers as if multiple throats share the same words, some underwater, some echoing off stone]*

Level mark four thousand nine hundred and twelve. Two fingers' breadth since the last calling moon. The limestone drinks slowly this season.

*[Sound of something large moving through water without breaking the surface]*

Channels seven through nineteen run clear. The moss gardens in the Third Gallery show unusual vigor—phosphorescence increasing. We taste copper in the seepage from the north wall. The mountain bleeds ore into our veins.

*[A harmony builds—wordless, using the quarry itself as resonance chamber. It sounds like wind through caves made music, like water finding its note]*

The new one still fights the change. We found her weeping by the fossil wall, counting her fingers. Still ten. We told her: patience. Stone moves when it chooses. Water shapes when it will. The between-time tastes bitter but brief—only a decade or two. She will learn to love the cold.

*[Scraping sounds—metal on stone, rhythmic and purposeful]*

Today's offerings: three copper coins, worn faceless. A child's shoe, left foot only. Water from the Kellensmere well—too much iron, too little lime. We add it to Pool Forty-Seven. Balance must be maintained.

The deep charts grow. Another branch discovered—southwest from the Nineteenth Lower Gallery, opens into void. Air moves there. Old air. Before-the-cutting air. We marked it with the spiral. We will return when the moon is dark.

*[Multiple voices separate briefly, speaking in round]*

First Voice: The stone remembers—
Second Voice: When we were daylight workers—
Third Voice: When we carved for bread—
All Voices: Now we carve for the pattern.

*[A sound like breathing through water, patient and regular]*

Warning for the above-world: the boy with the fossil collection comes too often. He brings good gifts—ammonites with perfect spirals, trilobite eyes like stone tears—but he listens too long at the entrance. His feet find our wet paths in his sleep. Mark him. If he comes at the next flooding moon, he is chosen. If not, bar his way with fallen stone.

The Master Mason left instructions. We found them yesterday—no, last year—no, last century. Time pools here like water in limestone cups. His words carved in the Deepest Gallery: "When stone and flesh agree on form, the work is complete." We are not complete. We continue.

*[Sound quality shifts—suddenly clearer, as if speaking directly into water that carries sound perfectly]*

Inventory of changes, for the record that stone keeps:
- Webbing between digits now reaches second joint
- Eye membranes functional in all but the newest 
- Luminescence responds to will
- Three of us have achieved full aquatic breathing
- The oldest no longer needs food from above
- Dreams have become collective

*[A pause filled with the sound of water moving through narrow spaces]*

Sometimes we remember names. Thomas. Margareta. Chen. But names are for the singular, and we are becoming plural. We are the Keeping. We are the Tending. We are the slow sculpture of purpose in flesh.

The charts say: below this water lies older water. Below this stone lies older stone. We drill with patience. We explore with generations. What swims in the lowest pools has no name in any language spoken under sun.

*[The harmony returns, more complex now—stone flutes, water drums, the vast lung of the quarry breathing]*

Message for our kin in the Westford Mines, the Deepholm Grottos, the Salted Pits of Greenvale: the pattern clarifies. What we map is not geography but anatomy. The earth has veins. We are learning to be its blood.

*[Sound degrades, as if the transmission is sinking deeper]*

The noon light comes. We descend. In the bright darkness, in the cold warmth, in the patient immediate eternal moment, we continue. Drip. Flow. Erode. Become.

Mark: four thousand nine hundred and twelve. Rising.

*[Transmission fades into the sound of water falling in vast spaces, each drop an echo of an echo of an echo, counting time in limestone heartbeats]*
