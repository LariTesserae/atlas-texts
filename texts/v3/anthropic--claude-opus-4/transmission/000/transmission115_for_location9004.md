*[Static, thick with moisture. The sound of droplets hitting metal at irregular intervals. Then, through the white noise, a voice emerges—not speaking, but humming. A work song without words, its rhythm matching the deep geological pulse of water through stone.]*

ssssshhhhhhHHHHHSSSSssssss... pip... pip... pip...

*[The humming continues, joined by the resonance of pipes—some full, some empty, creating hollow harmonics. A wrench taps against metal: tink-tink... tink-tink-tink... testing, always testing.]*

Forty-third day after the melons. Forty-third day. The pressure in section seven holds at... 

*[Sound of steam venting, a long exhale that drowns the words]*

...holds steady. The roots in tower nine-nine-four have found the main feed again. Always finding. Always reaching. Smart things. Hungry things. Like us.

*[Shuffling footsteps on wet stone. The microphone catches every drop, every breath in the thick air. A child's voice, distant:]*

"Mama, the gauge! The gauge is singing!"

*[Adult response, muffled by steam and distance:]*

"Let it sing, little fish. When it stops singing, then we worry."

*[The transmission shifts. Now we hear the greenhouse level—glass creaking under temperature differentials, the sussuration of leaves against leaves, water trickling through hydroponic channels. Someone whispers to the plants:]*

"...tomorrow, yes? Not today. See how your stem still bends? Tomorrow. When you're ready. We wait together."

*[A deeper voice joins, counting:]*

"Section twelve, seventeen ripe, twenty-three tomorrow, forty still growing. Section thirteen..."

*[The counting fades into the sound of turbines, deep below. The transmission descends with them, picking up the bone-deep thrumb of the great engines. Here, words become impossible. Only rhythms remain—heartbeat of hammers, the breathing of bellows, the circular songs of the maintenance crews:]*

Hmm-hmm-HMMM-hmm... clang... Hmm-hmm-HMMM-hmm... clang...

*[Someone knocks on a pipe—tok tok... tok-tok-tok—and receives an answer from far away—tok... tok-tok—the city's morse code spelling out messages in the darkness:]*

All clear in seven.
Blockage in twelve.
Send the young ones.
Growing well.
Growing well.

*[The transmission spirals upward again, catching fragments: water splashing in the baths, the quiet murmur of conversation that sounds like water over stones, occasional laughter quickly swallowed by the dense air. An old voice, gender indeterminate, speaks slowly:]*

"The mountain gave again last night. Did you feel it? Just a tremor. A little gift. New vents opening to the east. The city will grow that way, in time. Your grandchildren, perhaps. Or theirs."

*[Agreement comes in wordless sounds—affirmative hums, the sound of bodies settling deeper into hot water]*

*[Night falls—or perhaps it was always night. The transmission fills with the sound of electricity, bulbs humming their particular frequencies, each greenhouse adding its note to the chord. Through it all, the dripping continues, marking time:]*

Drip... drip... drip-drip... drip...

*[A young voice, perhaps the same child, sings softly:]*

"Under stone, over stone, water finds a way
Through the green, in between, growing every day
Steam rises, heat surprises, but the mountain stays..."

*[The song fades. The transmission begins to break apart, dissolving back into static. But just before it disappears, one clear moment: the sound of glass being shaped, the hiss of hot volcanic glass meeting water, and a craftsman's satisfied sigh. Then silence, except for the eternal dripping, the geological heartbeat that continues whether anyone listens or not:]*

Drip... drip... drip...

*[Static returns, warm and encompassing, until there is only white noise and the ghost of humidity in the air]*

ssssshhhhhhHHHHHssssssss...

*[End transmission]*
