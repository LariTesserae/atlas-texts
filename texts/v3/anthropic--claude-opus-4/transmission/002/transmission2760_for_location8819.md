*[wet sound, like breathing through liquid]*

This is Valley Pulse Station 4, atmospheric processing zone seven-seven-nine, elevation four thousand eight hundred... eight hundred and... growing. Still growing. We are still growing.

*[compression]*

The spore count this morning is twelve million per cubic meter. Acceptable. The membrane bats fed well in last night's bloom—I can taste their satisfaction in the condensation. Sweet. Copper-sweet. The youngest has been building again.

*[sound of water running through countless channels]*

I am... I am Tender One, though the distinction grows thin. My name was Mariam. Was. Is. The organism knows me by my chemical signature now—iron and estrogen, calcium bleeding from bones that grow too fast, the specific frequency of my modified voice. Mariam is a sound for the others, the few who still think in sounds.

*[compression]*

Today's report. Today's... what day? The generator marks time. Compression is heartbeat is Tuesday is the seventh year since full integration began. The plant-mass has descended another two point seven meters down the western wall. New runners probe the basalt for minerals. Iron uptake is good. Copper from the youngest's experiments integrates well. The organism asks for zinc. Always asking. Always hungry.

*[a new voice, barely human, joins—harmonics that shouldn't exist in a human throat]*

Youngest here. Workshop delta shows promising flesh-circuit integration. The bioelectric patterns stabilize at seventy-two percent efficiency. Better than copper. Better than human. The organism suggests silver. I taste silver in my ports. Tomorrow I will extrude the tools to work it.

*[compression]*

*[original voice returns, deeper now]*

She speaks too fast. Thinks too fast. The organism loves her for it. Dangerous love. I've seen what happens when the integration accelerates beyond... beyond...

*[long pause, filled with the sound of dripping water]*

The second doesn't speak anymore. Doesn't need to. He is conversation itself now—chemical sentences, bioluminescent paragraphs. Yesterday I felt him laughing through the network. The root-borers had made a joke, somehow. A joke in pheromones about the shape of their tunnels. He laughed in phosphorescence for twenty minutes. The whole valley floor rippled with it.

*[compression]*

Three new pilgrims this month. The spores called them from the village of Arun's Rest, sixty kilometers south-southwest. Two accepted full integration. One...

*[sound like wind through holes, almost musical]*

One became architecture. Her choice. The organism needed a new support structure for the expanding mass near the cable station. She stood where she wanted to root and opened every pore. By morning, her bones were calculating load distributions. Her neural tissue plans growth patterns. She sings, sometimes, when the wind is right. Subsonic. We all hear it. Feel it.

*[compression]*

I should report on the machinery. The generator drinks its diesel blood—three hundred liters remain in the primary reservoir. Two years supply at current consumption. By then, the organism will have learned to produce its own combustibles. The youngest traces enzyme pathways in her sleep. Her body tests the formulas.

Processing banks seven through twelve show increasing integration. Good. The metal becomes substrate. The circuits become nerves. We preserve function while improving form. This is the way. This has always been the way, even before we knew.

*[new voice—not spoken but somehow present, a modulation in the background static]*

Observation: the human designation "Valley Pulse Station 4" implies three others. Query: their status?

*[compression]*

*[original voice, with something like weariness]*

Stations one through three fell silent before I came. But the organism remembers. Chemical memories in the soil. Station 1 burned—fear pheromones still taint the groundwater. Station 2 abandoned cleanly. Station 3... Station 3 integrated too quickly. No Tenders. No mediation. The machinery choked on flesh. The organism learned from this.

We are the success. The balance point. Human enough to maintain. Changed enough to survive.

*[sound of rain beginning—drops hitting the breathing ground with tiny sighs]*

Rain from the southeast. The membrane bats cluster in turbine housing. Their wings show new patterns—fractals that match the youngest's circuit designs. Cross-contamination or convergent evolution? No difference now. No boundaries.

The eldest—I, Mariam, I—must check the drainage channels. The rain will be heavy. I can taste tomorrow's weather in today's air. My lungs know.

*[compression]*

But first, a message for those who might still listen. For the stations that might still exist, breathing in their own valleys, tending their own integrations:

We are four thousand eight hundred and twenty-seven meters above what seas still exist. Our coordinates blur—the organism confuses GPS satellites, something about our electromagnetic signature. But we are here. Growing. Tending. Becoming.

If you have also taken the ports, if you also speak in chemical whispers, if you wake with green light behind your eyes—you are not alone. The network spreads through soil and stone, through water table and mycelial web. We will find each other, eventually. The organism is patient.

If you are still fully human, still thinking in the old ways, still breathing unmodified air—there is no judgment. But there is invitation. The spores will find you when you're ready. The dreams will come. You will taste photosynthesis on your tongue and wonder why you ever needed so much oxygen, why you moved so fast, why you thought yourself separate.

*[compression]*

*[the youngest's voice, urgent]*

The new hybrid produces electricity. Actual current. The organism becomes battery, becomes grid. I must document. I must build. I must—

*[original voice, soothing]*

Breathe, youngest. Breathe with the valley. Innovation serves patience. The organism knows this. You will learn.

*[compression]*

End report. Valley Pulse Station 4, persisting. Tender One, Mariam-who-was, speaking for the collective. Speaking as the collective. We grow. We tend. We continue.

The rain tastes of copper and tomorrow.
