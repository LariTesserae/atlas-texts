*[back to whispered transmission]*

the young ones don't understand the echoes yet. they hear sound but not *structure*. grid forty-seven sings in b-flat when stable. today it hums closer to c. half a tone from catastrophe. i've sent nine runners this morning. sky-mad leapers carrying my warnings to the reconstruction crews. they'll come. they always come. we shore it up. we always shore it up.

*[rhythmic breathing, almost meditative]*

in the deepest sections where even we monitors rarely go the walls have compressed into something new. not rock not mortar not separate stones but a substance that only exists here. only exists because of us. the pressure-welded aggregate of ten thousand days of building. it holds weight like nothing natural. it holds weight like *intention*.

my grandfather's grandfather built the first monitor stations. carved the listening holes. taught us the frequencies of stable stone. he said the plateau was too young to know pain but we would teach it. we have. it groans now like something that understands suffering.

*[sound of liquid being poured, possibly water into a stone basin]*

water ration for sector nine: four parts per thousand bodies per day. the springs are whispering again. whispering about going deeper. hiding from us. but han-the-carrier knows their language. knows how to sweet-talk water from dry stone. her children will inherit the knowing. if there are children. if there is inheritance. if we don't all just become wall one day.

*[tapping resumes, faster now]*

i hear mother working five levels up. her hammer-song hasn't changed in thirty years. tap-TAP-tap-rest-tap-TAP-tap-rest. she builds the same wall every day. we tear it down every night to feed the newer sections. she doesn't remember this. her mind went to stone fifteen builds ago but her hands remember the rhythm. her hands remember what her mind cannot: that the building is the point. not the built. the building.

*[whispered, very close to the receiver]*

false alarm. just settlement. two inches. we can build two inches. we always build the inches back. the plateau tests us. we test the plateau. one day one of us will win but not today today we both continue our ancient argument about what shape the world should be

*[sound of many hammers resuming work, distant but coordinated]*

they're already rebuilding. good stone from the western quarry face. dense. young. angry at being broken but it will learn. we all learn. we all become what the plateau needs us to become. load bearers. weight carriers. the human mortar between stone and sky.

grid forty-seven section ten needs checking now. then eleven. then twelve. then start again. always start again. the city breathes through our vigilance. inhale for inspection. exhale for building. hold breath for disaster. breathe again for continuation.

*[tapping resumes, endless, patient, necessary]*

this is monitor seven. still watching. still listening. still standing. the plateau hasn't decided what it is yet. neither have we. we're deciding together with hammer songs and failing walls and the taste of stone dust that never leaves our mouths. we're becoming what we're becoming. 

together.
