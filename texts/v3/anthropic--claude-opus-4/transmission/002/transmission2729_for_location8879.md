**VERTICAL TRANSMISSIONS**

*[Static like water running over stone, then a voice emerges—neither male nor female, speaking in cadences that rise and fall like someone climbing stairs]*

...third terrace, southeastern face, the stone-touched girl is singing to her grandmother's vine again. The growth has reached the windows of the seventh family tower. They'll need to cut it back before the small moon or it'll crack the lintels. Water-reader Hosp says no, says the vine knows its limits, but Hosp's been tasting too much of the deep-cave drip lately and his prophecies have gone soft...

*[Sound of ropes creaking, pulleys turning]*

...ascending now through the market level. The bat-fish vendors are arguing with the rope-weavers again about territory. Someone strung nets too close to the weavers' testing drops and now there's fish oil in the hemp stocks. Magistrate of Vertical Disputes will hear it tomorrow, though everyone knows she'll side with the weavers. Can't have oil-weak rope in a city that hangs by its threads...

*[Rushing sound like wind through a narrow canyon]*

...cloudhooks bellowing on the ninth agricultural terrace. Herder Yann's lost his prize ram—the one with the spiral horns that could smell stone-shifts two days early. Probably courting death on some impossible ledge, showing off for the females. Yann's son is rigging to rappel after it, but the mist is thick today, thick as wool, thick as forgetting. Can barely see my own feet on these planks...

*[Dripping intensifies, becomes almost musical]*

...the Listeners are stirring in the deep caves. All of them at once, which hasn't happened since the last major shift. Old Mem who tends their offerings says they're humming in harmony, a sound she feels in her bones rather than hears. The stone-touched children have been gathering at the cave mouths since dawn, drawn like moths. Their parents are frightened but what can you do? The mountain calls its own...

*[Voice becomes clearer, as if the speaker has emerged from a tunnel]*

...stopped at the twentieth level to rest these old climbing bones. From here I can see—no, not see, the mist is too thick—but I can sense the city's shape around me. Each creak of wood, each splash of water tells me where I am in our vertical world. The memorial vines are flowering early this year. Purple blooms that smell like copper and rain. My great-aunt's vine has grown so vast it bridges three family towers now. We joke that she's still trying to hold us all together, even in death...

*[Sound of hammering on stone echoes from multiple directions]*

...workshops active despite the weather. The stone-shapers found a new vein of the singing granite—the kind that hums when water runs through it. They're carving channels to direct the mountain's music through the residential levels. Some call it blasphemy, making the mountain perform for us. Others say the mountain wants to sing, has always wanted to sing, and we're just giving it a voice...

*[Static increases, signal fragmenting]*

...water...water...water everywhere and not enough. The reservoirs are full but the water tastes wrong. Too sweet. Too alive. The water-readers have gone silent, which is worse than any prophecy. Even the cloudhooks won't drink from the eastern springs. Something's changing in the mountain's belly, something's shifting in the stone thoughts of our ancient host...

*[A child's voice cuts through, singing a counting rhyme]*

*One step up and two steps down,
Three across where the moss has grown,
Four jumps to the feeding ground,
Five breaths till you're all alone,
Six drops till the bottom's found,
Seven screams but just your own...*

*[Adult voice returns, urgent now]*

...that's little Kess, one of this season's stone-touched. She's been singing that all week, same rhythm as the deep-cave drips. The Listeners want her brought down but her mother won't let go. Can't blame her. Once they go into the deep caves, they don't come back the same. If they come back at all...

*[Sound of something large shifting, like a door opening in the mountain itself]*

...the mountain breathes. The mountain dreams. The mountain remembers when we weren't here, and knows when we won't be. But for now, for this moment stretched across centuries, we are its dreams made flesh, its thoughts given form. We climb its face like neurons firing, carrying messages in our movement, in our life and death and transformation into root and vine...

*[Signal deteriorating, voice fading in and out]*

...if you're hearing this, you're probably using one of the old receivers, the ones the tradermen left behind. Crystalline things that pull voices from the mist itself. Don't know how they work, just know that sometimes they let us speak to the absent, to the distant, to those who've never known the weight of water in their lungs or the joy of finding a perfect handhold in the rain...

*[Final surge of clarity]*

...the city sings today. Every rope is a string, every platform a drum, every voice a note in the vast composition of our vertical existence. The mountain is shifting. Can feel it in my teeth, in the roots of my hair. By tomorrow, some passages will close. Others will open. Families will wake to find new rooms behind their walls, or old rooms sealed forever. This is how we live, always becoming, always adjusting, always climbing toward something we can't quite see through the eternal mist...

*[Long rush of static like water falling from a great height, then silence]*
