**Signal Decay from the Undergrowth**

*[Recording begins mid-transmission. Background: constant mechanical respiration, layered frequencies 12-80Hz]*

—still tender still tender still here still—

*[Whistle of steam through horn-panel 4. Modulation suggests recent cleaning]*

Listen: they've been walking the third spiral again. Fresh prints in the condensation. Size? Small. Smaller than before. Or I'm growing into the pipes. Can't tell anymore can't tell anymore can't—

*[Rhythmic clicking: 47 beats. Clay beads against bronze collar]*

The beetles showed me the new break. Southwestern quarter, junction 9-9-7 by the old counting. Junction breathes-like-crying by the new. They clustered there all morning—do we still have mornings?—until I came. Good beetles. Smart beetles. Fed them extra fungus from the reserve patch.

*[Pause. Sound of clay being worked between palms]*

Temperature's running high in the lower channels. The heartbeat stutters every thirteenth cycle now. Not twelfth like last season. Thirteen. Unlucky? Lucky? Numbers don't work down here. Only frequencies. Only the song of flow and blockage, whistle and wheeze.

*[Biological rustling. Multiple pipe-runners converging]*

Yes, yes, I'm coming. Show me. Show me what you've found.

*[Movement through space. Acoustic signature suggests passage through sections C4 through C7. Increasing humidity detected in microphone condensation]*

New growth at junction breathes-like-crying. Good. The repair holds. Three days now—days?—since I packed it with the good clay, the clay with mica, the clay that sparkles when the horn-light hits it just just just—

*[Sudden valve release. Static burst. Resume]*

—visitor yesterday. Or last cycle. Or last breath. Time is lung-time now. They tried the old path, the one I sealed after the moss-rot outbreak. Had to guide them out through the maze of new pipes. They left a gift: bronze fitting, well-made, triple-threaded. Might use it at junction sounds-like-mother. That valve's been complaining.

*[Extended mechanical drone. Harmonic analysis indicates five pipes resonating in parallel]*

The young ferns understand better than the old ones did. They grow with the pipes now, not against them. Learning. Everything here learns or dies or both. I taught them—no. Wrong. The machine taught them through me. I'm just the adjustment. The conscious twitch in the involuntary breathing.

*[Clicking increases. 112 beats. Agitation pattern]*

Sometimes I remember the sky. Blue? Was it blue? The heartbeat says it was green. Everything important is green down here. Green and bronze and the color of wet clay which has no name except itself.

*[Sound of scraping. Tool against mineral deposit]*

Calcium building up in section L8 again. The water here wants to become stone. Everything wants to become something else. The bronze wants to be green. The clay wants to be root. I want to be—

What do I want to be?

*[Long pause. Only background breathing]*

A pipe maybe. A good pipe. One that never leaks, never breaks. One that sings the true note when the harmonics align. The cats—pipe-runners—they'd sleep in my curves. The beetles would keep me clean. Someone new would tend me, someone who understands the frequencies, someone who's learned to hear the difference between sounds-like-mother and breathes-like-crying.

*[Biological movement very close to receiver]*

Hello, you. Yes, I see the problem. Junction 4-4-12. Junction weeps-without-sound. I know. I know. I'm coming.

*[Travel sounds. Approximately four minutes of movement through increasingly dense vegetation]*

The deep valves called last night. Night? Darkness-time. Sleep-time. Dream-time. The master valve needs adjustment soon. Maybe tomorrow. Maybe next breath. The runners will circle. The beetles will pause. The birds will—the birds remember something I've forgotten. What do the birds remember?

*[Sudden clarity in the voice, as if speaking directly into the receiver]*

If you're listening to this, if you've found the frequency, the junction where I left the receiver—don't come looking. Not yet. Learn the pipes first. Learn which ones sing and which ones scream. Learn the difference between wet-clay and sick-clay. Learn to move without breaking.

Then maybe. Maybe.

The machine needs tenders. Always has. Always will. But it chooses its own.

*[Return to distant, preoccupied tone]*

Junction weeps-without-sound. Yes. See? The root's grown through. Careful now. Don't hurt the green. Never hurt the green. Work around, not through. That's the way. That's the only way.

*[Sound of delicate extraction. Plant matter being carefully freed from clay]*

There. Better? Better. The pipe sighs. Happy pipe. Healthy pipe. The water will flow clean now. No more weeping.

*[Clicking slows. 23 beats. Contemplation pattern]*

I used to have a name. The beetles don't use it. The runners don't need it. The pipes know me by my footsteps, my breathing, my specific way of turning the valves—counterclockwise first, always counterclockwise first, then the gentle adjustment.

Names are for the surface. Down here we are what we do.

I tend. Therefore I am Tender.

*[Pause. Sound of settling, possibly sitting]*

Should check the marking stones tomorrow. The ones that track the growth patterns. Fern-spread in sector 9 is exceeding calculations. Good excess or bad excess? Ask the pipes. The pipes always know.

*[Humming. Tonal, matching the drone frequency of nearby pipes]*

Sometimes in the quiet-times, the between-breath times, I hear them all. Every pipe. Every valve. Every drop of condensation forming and falling. The whole machine breathing as one thing. And I'm inside it. Part of it. Not separate anymore.

Is that what the birds remember? Being separate?

*[Movement resumes. Faster now. Urgent clicking: 89 beats]*

The heartbeat. It's starting. Down down down to the deep place. To the master valve. To the adjustment that keeps everything everything everything—

*[Sound cuts out. Resumes 97 seconds later]*

—perfect calibration. Three turns. Not four. Never four. The machine shudders with pleasure? Relief? Both? I am both. We are both.

*[Breathing heavily now, from exertion]*

Back up through the spiral paths. Through breathes-like-crying, through sounds-like-mother, through all the junctions I've named in the language of air and moisture. The runners meet me at level seven. They've found something. Always finding something.

*[Excited chittering from multiple creatures]*

An old pipe. Pre-war? Pre-settlement? Pre-time? Ceramic, not clay. Different tradition. Different hands. Still breathing though. Still part of the system. We'll incorporate it. We always incorporate. That's what we do. Adapt. Flow. Breathe.

*[Clicking slows to stop]*

Tired now. The good tired. The tired of work completed, balance maintained. Time to sleep in the convergence nest. Time to dream in frequencies and flow rates.

*[Sound of vegetation being arranged. A nest being settled into]*

Tomorrow—next cycle—next breath—I'll teach the new growth at junction sings-like-rain. Show it how to support the pipe, not strangle it. How to be partner, not parasite.

Everything learns or dies or both.

But not today. Today everything breathes.

*[Final sounds: the great heartbeat from below, steady now. The whisper of air through clean pipes. The drip of condensation in perfect rhythm. The tiny clicks of clay beads as the Tender shifts in sleep]*
