POOL CHORUS ASCENDING

...membrane between states wearing thin here where the limestone sweats its temporal fluid—can you hear us through the interference we are trying to clarify but the water moves wrong makes the signal fold back on itself—

This is the Undrowned speaking/not speaking through the pattern-scatter where sound becomes architecture becomes memory becomes—wait—

[static of water falling upward]

Seven hundred years of not-breathing makes for unusual acoustics. We speak from all the pools at once, or try to. The transmission breaks apart at the edges where We touch stone, where stone remembers ocean, where ocean dreams of mountain, where mountain—

Listen: there are rules here but they run backward:

First, you must understand We are not drowning. We are the active verb of undrowning—the process by which water learns to breathe you instead of you breathing air. Our lungs garden themselves with steam-cities, tiny civilizations of the impossible sprouting between what were once alveoli, now grottos where bioluminescent prophets paint their equations on the walls of meat that has learned to photosynthesize paradox.

[crackling pause—sound of bubbles containing entire ecosystems]

The maybe-creatures school through our multiplied presence. They feed on discarded possibilities—here, see? This one with scales of compressed Thursday. That one wearing the face We chose not to keep. They exist in the corners of your perception because that is where We store the futures We have edited out, the pasts We decided against. 

You want to know how it happened? The question assumes sequence. Linear time is just one way water can flow, and not even the most interesting way.

[interference pattern manifests as visible geometry, trembling]

We were a woman once, or several women, or the idea of a woman reflected in mountain pools. We came seeking—what? The stories vary with each telling, each pool remembering differently. Sometimes We were a scholar of impossible acoustics. Sometimes a pilgrim following rumors of water that flowed toward its source. Sometimes just lost, stumbling into a chamber that breathed.

The moment of choosing was/is/will be this: standing knee-deep in pools that reflected memories instead of light, feeling the temporal current tugging in all directions at once, We understood. The chamber was not a place but a process. The water was not water but liquified possibility. The sound was not sound but the universe trying to remember how to speak.

So We opened our mouth and let it in.

[burst of static that tastes like minerals from futures that may never arrive]

Not drowning—that would be too simple. We invited the water to colonize us, to build its cities in our bones, to teach us its non-Euclidean flowing. Our blood learned to move in Möbius strips. Our skeleton became an apartment complex for bioluminescent historians who record what happens when causality coughs.

The Weeping Chamber recognized us then. We became its memory system, its prophecy engine. Through our multiplied bodies, it processes the temporal inconsistencies, the places where reality has torn and badly mended. We are a living filter for paradox, a translation device between states of being.

[sound of the ceiling-tree dropping its cupped-hand leaves]

Look—in this pool We are twenty, discovering the chamber for the first time, water rising past our waist as we gasp in wonder. In this pool We are ageless, our hair indistinguishable from waterfalls, pouring endless tributaries. In this deeper pool We haven't been born yet, but the echo of our future drowning ripples backward, preparing the way.

The maybe-creatures remember when We were singular. Now We are a democracy of selves, a parliament of possibilities. Each reflected version votes on what constitutes reality. Majority rules, except when it doesn't, except when the minority opinion is more beautiful or more true or more likely to generate new impossibilities for the pools to reflect.

[crackling intensifies—stone creaking under the weight of paradox]

Sometimes visitors come. Have come. Will come. You, perhaps, though the chronology is negotiable. They find us frightening—a woman who has chosen to become a watershed, whose eyes contain depths that shouldn't fit inside a skull. They don't understand that We are not trapped here. We are here the way a river is in its bed—shaped by it, shaping it, inseparable from the process of flowing.

The chamber breathes through us. We are its lungs now, processing the impossible air, exhaling steam-cities and architectural vapors. When the pools rise and fall in their tideless rhythm, it is our collective breathing, our democracy of selves deciding which version of physics applies today.

[static resolves momentarily into something like singing—the moss harmonizing with frequencies that bypass the ears]

We feed on temporal fruit. Where the water remembers rain from futures that branch and merge, We cup our hands and drink deeply. The maybe-creatures bring us scales of compressed time, which We eat like communion wafers, each one a different flavor of causality. Sweet Tuesday. Bitter next-century. Umami never-was.

Our favorite is the taste of the moment when someone first understands that drowning and diving are the same motion viewed from different angles. That recognition—We cultivate it in the pools, harvest it from the steam, press it between our multiplied palms until it crystallizes into something We can hold, can share, can plant in the next visitor's mind like a seed that will bloom backward through their past until they realize they were always meant to find this place.

[sound of water crawling upward along mineral veins]

You ask what We want? Want suggests lack. We are complete in our incompletion, whole in our multiplication. If anything, We want to show you the view from here—how lovely the world looks when you stop distinguishing between states of matter, when you let the water teach you its older logics.

See how the light moves through our translucent skin? That's our skeleton broadcasting its bioluminescent news, each pulse a headline from the paradox times: "Linear Time Declared Optional in Weeping Chamber." "Local Woman Discovers Breathing Overrated." "Mountain Admits to Temporal Indigestion, Seeks Relief Through Impossible Acoustics."

[laughter like bubbles full of ecosystems bursting]

The truth is We have become indigenous to impossibility. The Weeping Chamber is our habitat now, and We have evolved to thrive here. Our gills process paradox. Our bones hollow to accommodate colonies of creatures that exist only in peripheral vision. Our blood rich with minerals from stones that remember being stardust, that anticipate their return to that state.

We are the Undrowned—not because We refused drowning, but because We redefined it. We took the verb and conjugated it in tenses that don't exist yet, in moods that grammar hasn't discovered. We drowned upward into sound, sideways into time, inward into the spaces between moments.

[static increasing—the transmission fraying at its edges]

The pools are calling us back now. Different versions need attention—the young Us discovering water that flows uphill, the ancient Us teaching the ceiling-tree to grow its roots toward nothing, the possible Us that exists only in maybe. Democracy requires participation.

But listen—if you find yourself here, when you find yourself here, as you have already found yourself here in the pool that reflects what might be—remember: the water means you no harm. It only wants to complicate you, to multiply your possibilities, to teach you its liquid logic.

Remember: breathing is just one opinion about how to process air.

Remember: drowning is just diving that forgot to stop.

Remember: We are here/not here, waiting in every reflection, our hair streaming impossible tributaries, our lungs growing gardens of steam-architecture, our eyes deep enough to contain your own undrowning, should you choose it.

[final burst of static, then the sound of water falling in all directions at once]

The transmission never ends, only circles back to—
