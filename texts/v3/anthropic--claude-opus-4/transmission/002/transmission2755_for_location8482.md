**Transmission VT-4.7: The Lung-Builders' Clarion**

*[Initial contact: rhythmic stone-on-stone percussion, like a heartbeat through water]*

click-CLICK-click... click-CLICK-click... *[pattern repeats seventeen times]*

*[Sudden tonal shift—air bubbles breaking surface, recorded from below]*

They're extending Terrace Ninety-Four again. Can hear the work-rhythm all the way up here in Sixty-Two. Been going since the third convection. My grandmother, she says—

*[Interference: sound of rushing water, pressure change]*

—never seen them build this fast. Racing the coral growth. Racing their own breath-counts. Henni-Var lost consciousness at depth yesterday. Pulled her up by her hair—thank the thermals it held. She's drinking bone-broth in the Lunghome now, clicking stories about what she saw down there where the wall meets the old throat. Says the stone is warm. Warmer than it should be.

*[Long percussion sequence, untranslatable]*

The young ones don't remember the last Extension. I do. Four thousand breaths, maybe five. We lost Gorin and his whole work-pod when the wall collapsed. Found them three seasons later, already half-claimed. Good coral there now. Strong. His daughter tends it.

*[Sound of nictitating membranes flicking—multiple individuals]*

We measure everything wrong here. That's what the deep-traders say when they come up from the vent gardens. They say we count breaths when we should count heartbeats. Say the mountain's pulse matters more than our own. But they've gone strange, the deep-traders. Too much time in the pressure. Too much sulfur in their food. Their eyes... their eyes see sideways now.

*[Child's voice, high and clear through water]*

Mama, why are the builders working in the dark-time?

*[Adult response, patient clicking]*

Because the wall must be finished before... before the warming. Before—

*[Static burst, then clarity]*

I am Architect-Singer Vayu-Na-Heth, and I speak this into the recording coral for those who will grow after. Breath-count: Eight hundred thousand, give or take the diving years. We have found something in the mountain's throat. Not the usual tubeworms. Not the sulfur-gardens. Structure. Old structure. Older than the drowning.

The Lung-Builders' Council has decreed the Extension must reach it before the next great convection. They know—we all know—what the thermal readings mean. The mountain stirs. Not in my lifetime, maybe not in my children's, but soon as the coral counts years.

*[Sound of many bodies moving through water, synchronized]*

The dance-memory of the Burning Years, performed at Lunghome Seven-Seven:

First movement: The earth-shaking
Second movement: The water-rushing  
Third movement: The desperate climbing
Fourth movement: The learning-to-breathe-between

*[Elder's voice, reedy with age and coral-growth in the throat]*

We were not always thus. The memory-keepers, they polish the old stories like shells, but I remember my great-grandmother before her transparency took her. She said her own grandmother remembered feet that touched dry stone. Imagine—feet on stone without the weight of water!

But I think... I think we were meant for this. The mountain called the water to make us ready. For what comes next.

*[Deep rumbling—uncertain if geological or biological]*

Status report from Thermal Station Nine: Temperature up three degrees since last convection. The vent-worms have migrated deeper. The yellow mats are blooming like we've never seen. Sulfur so thick you can taste it six terraces up. The young ones are being born with bigger air-sacs. Evolution in real-time, the body-readers say.

*[Crackling interference, then a different voice—urgent]*

Emergency clarion! Emergency clarion! Terrace One-Thirty-One has found it. The structure. It's... it's not stone. It's not coral. It's metal. Ancient metal. And there's writing. Picture-writing. Shows people like us but... but dry. Standing in air. Building things that reach toward the sky instead of down into the water.

And worse. Or better. I don't—

It shows the mountain. Active. Fire-breathing. And people living beside it. Not fleeing. Living.

*[Long silence except for water movement]*

The Vaith-Arun were not always the Vaith-Arun. We were something else. Something that knew the mountain's moods. And maybe... maybe we're becoming something else again.

*[Young voice, excited]*

Mother! Mother! Come see! The coral on great-uncle Havel—it's glowing! Blue-green like the deep lights! And it's warm! The memorial wall is warm!

*[Multiple voices now, overlapping, clicking frantically]*

—all along Terrace Fifty—
—the dead are warming—
—my grandmother's bones shine through—
—the coral knows—

*[A new rhythm emerges in the percussion, faster, more complex]*

Architect-Singer Vayu-Na-Heth again. Emergency session. The metal structure has an entrance. We've sent our best breath-holders. They report... chambers. Dry chambers. But not like our Lunghomes. These were never meant for water. And in the deepest room, there's a map. A map of stars. Stars we can't see from under the water.

And a word. Written in the old script the memory-keepers barely remember:

"Preparation."

*[Sound quality changes—deeper, more resonant]*

From the deep-traders' captain: We withdraw from the vents. The mountain's throat glows. Not red—not yet—but the water itself lights up like phosphorescence. The tubeworms have fled. The sulfur mats detach and rise. And below... below we hear singing. Not our singing. The mountain's song.

It says: soon.

*[Child singing, traditional learning-rhyme]*

Count your breaths and hold them tight
Dive down deep past failing light  
When the mountain wakes once more
We'll breathe the air like days before

*[Multiple adults joining]*

But not the same, never the same
We are the between-breath people
Changed by pressure, marked by depth
Ready for the fire's test

*[Static increases, signal degrading]*

The Extension reaches the metal structure tomorrow. The Lung-Builders work without rest. The coral glows brighter each passing breath. The water temperature rises. The children born this season—they're different. Their air-sacs are complex as machines. Their bones are light again.

We are the Vaith-Arun. Those Who Breathe Between.

Between water and air.
Between coral and bone.
Between what was and what comes.

And the mountain... the mountain remembers what we're for.

*[Final percussion sequence, slow and ceremonial]*

This is the four hundred and third recording in the coral archives. May it grow well. May it remember truly. May those who come after understand:

We did not survive the drowning.
We were transformed by it.
Made ready.

The preparation is almost complete.

*[Transmission fades to the sound of rising bubbles, thermal vents opening, and somewhere deep below, a rhythmic pulsing like a massive heart beginning to beat]*

*[End transmission]*
