**Cordkeeper Station 7-Valley-Deep**

*[static resolves into rhythmic thumping, like a massive heartbeat through water]*

...tension nominal at third-twist, fourth-twist showing micro-frays, compensating with double-back weave until replacement fibers arrive from the Edge Gardens, all teams acknowledge...

*[voice cuts through—female, aged, speaking in rapid bursts between tasks]*

Seven days since last rain. The catch-basins are growing skin again, that bacterial film that means we're approaching critical moisture. I can taste it in the morning vapor—metallic, sharp. Been sending the young ones deeper for harvest but the good fiber's getting scarce down to the ninth level. Everything below that is composted to black paste.

*[sound of creaking, massive and near]*

Hold on. Big adjustment coming through sector—BRACE AND GRIP, BRACE AND GRIP—

*[thunderous groaning as tons of cordage shift, the speaker's breathing heavy with effort]*

...there. Settled. Something major happening over at the Sunrise Edge. Maybe finally connecting the new bridge to Finger-Peak-Rises. Been watching their signal fires at night, counting the smoke puffs. They're asking for more hollow-bore reed, the kind that sings in D-sharp. Must be trying for resonance matching between the anchor points.

*[background chorus of work songs, voices harmonizing through the fiber network]*

My daughter's crew is down at the fungal gardens today. Says the white-glow variety is fruiting early, pushing up through the mat like little fists of light. Good sign. Means the deep moisture is cycling properly. We'll have paste for the weavers' joints by week's end. Old Kresh-Kresh-Climbs-Without-Rest, his fingers are starting to seize in the mornings. Been working these ropes for eighty seasons, knows every strand in this section by touch. Yesterday he told me he's been dreaming the pattern—seeing how it wants to grow, where it needs reinforcement before the weak points even develop.

*[scraping sound—tools on fiber]*

The archaeological teams brought up more metal yesterday. Looks like part of a structure, all rusted angles and straight lines. Hard to imagine anyone building with such rigid stuff. No give, no flex. No wonder it all ended up buried. We'll work it into Junction Seven-Seven-Seven, let it serve as anchor bone for the new vertical highway. 

Vibration coming through from the deep workers—

*[speaker presses against the rope, subvocalizing through the network in a rapid pattern of hums and clicks]*

*[response vibrations pulse back, translated:]*

FOUND VOID AT LEVEL SEVENTEEN. BIG ONE. SENDING DIMENSIONS.

*[pause as calculations transmit]*

Blessed knots. Three hundred meters wide, depth unknown. Must be one of the original air pockets, where the first weavers built around something massive. Maybe a hill. Maybe a building. Sending a probe team with full rappelling kit and extra light-fungus. If it's stable, could become new settlement cavity. We're getting crowded in the upper hollows.

*[liquid splashing, drinking sounds]*

That's my water ration for the morning. One cup, alkaline with rope-minerals. You get used to the taste. Everything here tastes like hemp and history.

*[multiple voices approaching, speaking in the rapid overlapping pattern of work crews]*

"Need twelve-braid for the stress point—"
"—saying the Worm-Sign appeared near edge sector nine—"
"—my third-son made his first perfect bowline yesterday—"
"—harvest crew lost another to the deep fever—"

*[speaker addresses them:]*

Tell the harvest crew to pull back to level twelve until we can get some medicine worked up. The spore-bloom from last season should be dried by now. And someone needs to check the Worm-Sign report—might be a major shift coming from below.

*[voices recede, the background work-song continuing]*

The young ones don't understand patience yet. They want to weave fast, add their section and move on. But speed creates weakness. Each knot needs time to settle, to find its relationship with every other knot around it. The continent breathes, you see. Expands in the heat, contracts in the cool. If you don't build with that breath, your work tears itself apart.

*[distant singing—a deep harmonic that makes the nearby ropes hum sympathetically]*

That's the Memorizers. They're teaching the genealogy chants today. Every major rope-line going back twenty generations, which family lines maintained them, where they sourced the original fibers. History lives in the structure. You can read it if you know how—this section woven in the spiral-pattern of the Salt-Moon clan, that junction showing the distinctive triple-loop of the High-Noon-Weavers.

*[scraping intensifies, rhythmic and meditative]*

Sometimes I dream I'm the rope itself. Feeling every footstep, every added strand, every drop of water working its way down through my body. In the dreams, I can sense the whole continent at once—its weight, its balance, its slow thoughts moving at the speed of growth. Kresh-Kresh says that's how it starts. The joining. When you've worked the weave long enough, it starts working you back.

*[sudden sharp whistle—alarm pattern]*

Stripe-walker spotted. Big one, moving up from deep levels. Everyone into the hollows until it passes. Those things hunt by vibration and they're always hungry after molting.

*[scrambling sounds, voices calling positions, the speaker moving with practiced efficiency]*

...secure at Junction Nine-Nine-Two, Hollow Seven occupied, all children accounted for...

*[silence except for distant creaking]*

*[whispering now:]*

Can you feel it moving? Every step sends a wave through the weave. Probably fifty meters long, this one. They eat the boring beetles mainly, but they're not picky when they're growing new segments. Lost a good weaver last season—tried to run instead of hiding. The vibrations of running are like ringing a dinner bell.

*[tense minutes pass, marked by controlled breathing]*

*[a massive form moves overhead, its weight making the entire local structure sag and adjust—ropes groaning, water droplets shaken loose and pattering down]*

*[finally, a long, low whistle—all clear]*

Gone toward the Sunset Edge. Let the crews there know it's coming. 

Where was I? Yes—the joining. My grandmother achieved it completely in her final years. Grew tendril-roots from her spine, wrapped them into the load-bearing cables of her life's work. She's still there, part of Junction Three-Three-Three. Still alive, in a way. The young ones bring her water, pour it over her root-skin. Sometimes she speaks, but mostly she just... maintains. Adjusts tension. Guides growth. Part of something larger.

Is that where we're all heading? Sometimes I wonder.

*[work resumes—tools scraping, voices calling, the endless rhythm of construction]*

But not today. Today there's work. Always work. The Edge Crews need their fiber. The bridge builders need their calculations checked. The water teams need their schedules adjusted for the stripe-walker disruption. And somewhere far below, the probe team is descending into that vast void, their lights swallowed by darkness older than memory.

*[speaking directly into the resonance now, formal tone:]*

This is Cordkeeper Weaves-With-Iron-Rhythm, Station Seven-Valley-Deep, fourth hour past noon-zenith. All sectors stable despite fauna interruption. Deep probe proceeding. Moisture levels critical but manageable. The work continues.

*[pause, then softer, almost private:]*

The work always continues.

*[static increases, voices blending into the omnipresent symphony of dripping, creaking, singing, building—the sound of a world weaving itself larger with every passing moment—signal degrades but never quite disappears, suggesting the transmission continues, will always continue, as long as there are hands to weave and voices to carry through the endless, breathing deep]*
