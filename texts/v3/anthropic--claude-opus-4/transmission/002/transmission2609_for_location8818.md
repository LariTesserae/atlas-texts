**Chorus of the Threshold**

The water speaks first, always the water—that thin lens between stone and stem, carrying frequencies the dry world cannot hold. Listen: it begins as plant-speech, the chemical whisper of ten thousand roots in conversation, electro-ionic pulses racing through the wet networks where cell wall meets mineral solution.

*ssshhhhhhh-click-click-whisssssper-thrum*

We are the between-singers, voice of neither-nor, carriers of the margin-song. Do you hear how sound transforms crossing the surface? How each word splits into its constituent possibilities? In air: "We tend the green." In water: *click-trill-harmonic-bloom* meaning growth-partnership-patience-moisture. The same thought, doubled, refracted, made fuller by its division.

The children know this first—before their gills fold inward, before their lungs learn the harsh discipline of gas exchange. They swim in pure meaning, undifferentiated, where every thought is action and every action is thought. Listen to them: *bubble-stream-giggle-pulse,* the sound of consciousness still liquid, still unbound by the necessity of choosing.

*drip . . . drip . . . drip-drop-splash . . . rhythm building . . .*

Temperature is a language here. The stones remember deeper cold, speak it through our feet when we climb too high. The water carries warmer stories from wherever sun still touches, far above, translated down through limestone into our horizontal light. We read heat like others read words. This morning the eastern channels run two degrees warmer—somewhere above, the world turns its face to a star we remember in our cells but cannot see.

The plants sing loudest at the growing edges. Hear them: *stretch-crack-cellular-division-whisper-whisper-PUSH* as new tissue forces through old constraints. They dream of sun they've never seen, follow photons down their diving paths, grateful for echoes of echoes of brightness. We learned our patience from them, our persistence. They teach us: grow toward whatever light finds you, however bent the path, however filtered the source.

*harmonic drone underlying everything now, the frequency of chlorophyll vibrating*

Our elders recall the Dry Years (though water never left, only lowered), when the topmost walkways stood in killing air for seasons at a time. The vegetation retreated to the margins, condensing its green to threads and films, waiting. We sang the water-calling songs then, all of us, every throat both wet and dry joining the plea: *return-flow-seep-rise-return-flow-seep-rise*

And it did. It always does. The limestone weeps for us, the water table forgives our architecture its presumption. This is the covenant: we build knowing we will sink. We sink knowing we will adapt. We adapt knowing we are already changing into something else, something the next generation will remember fondly but no longer quite comprehend.

*static interlude—the sound of mineral precipitation, almost below hearing*

Questions arrive from the dry world sometimes, carried by traders who hold their breath too much, who flinch when we slip beneath the surface mid-word. They ask: "But what are you? What have you become?" As if becoming ever ends. As if we are not, each moment, choosing to remain precisely balanced on the edge of transformation.

The young ones understand better. They spend their transition years learning to fail at breathing, first one way, then the other. We watch them surface, gasping, still trying to pull water through closing gills. Watch them dive, lungs burning, forgetting they can simply open their necks and drink the oxygen dissolved around them. This is education: learning all the ways you can almost die until you find the narrow path between drownings.

*click-song begins, the formal pattern of deep discussion*

When we debate—and we debate everything, slowly, wetly, constantly—the conversation moves through mediums like a living thing seeking its level. Arguments begun in air grow subtle underwater, where gesture carries nuance that words cannot. Consensus emerges not through convincing but through saturation. We steep in each other's perspectives until the boundaries dissolve.

Yesterday (though counting days here requires noticing which shadows move and which merely drift), the family of the Third Deep proposed cultivating a new strain of surface-flowering vine. The debate moved thus:

In air: "It will require too much light pruning."
In water: *shimmer-shake-head-bubble* [meaning: the labor enriches us]
In air: "But the nutrients pulled from the deeps—"
In water: *spiral-hand-motion-down-following-up* [meaning: cycles complete themselves]

Six hours this took. By the end, we were thinking in temperatures, speaking in plant-growth, seeing through each other's gill-slits the pink truth of our interdependence. The vote was unanimous, as all true votes are—not because we agreed, but because we merged.

*the background drone shifts, incorporating new overtones*

Memory here is architectural. These stones remember their dry laying, still dream of mortar unmixed with algae. The oldest among us recall when only the first basement level kissed the water table, when these were merely damp streets rather than canals. Each generation builds higher as we sink, adding stories to compensate for those lost below. The city grows like a slow plant, reaching toward what light it can find.

But listen—beneath the architectural memory runs the deeper song. Cellular memory. Salt recollection. The trace elements in our blood still match some ancient ocean, the one we crawled from, the one we return to drop by drop. The Kithara are just one moment in this longer song, the moment when the melody pauses between verses, undecided which theme to pursue next.

*interruption: the sound of children playing, their voices doubling and redoubling between surface and depth*

"Marco!" (in air)
*Click-click-trill!* (in water)
"Polo!" (in air)
*Harmony-bloom-response!* (in water)

They've invented games we never taught them, games that only work here, in this exact depth of water, with these precise acoustic properties. The young adapt faster than the plants, which adapt faster than the stones, which adapt faster than our cherished beliefs about what we are.

What we are:

Wet carpenters of the margin. Pruners of the possible. Citizens of neither/both. Singers in the frequency of chlorophyll and limestone, translators between the vocabularies of gill and lung. We are the answer to a question nobody asked: what if you didn't choose? What if you refused the binary, insisted on the amphibian and? What if home was precisely the place that required you to keep changing?

*the water-clock in the central plaza marks another hour with falling drops*

Soon (meaning within a generation or three), the water will rise another hand's breadth. The current air-level walkways will submerge, and we'll build new ones. Our children's children will develop new organs, perhaps—something beyond gill-slits, some innovation we cannot imagine. They'll look back at us with affection and bewilderment, wondering why we clung to lungs at all.

But for now, this:

The perfect temperature of almost-too-cold. The green smell of photosynthesis in closed spaces. The architectural democracy of moss. Our voices splitting and recombining between mediums. The patience of limestone. The persistence of roots. The way light bends when it enters our world, becoming something richer, stranger, more itself.

We are the Kithara. We grow where others would drown. We breathe where others would suffocate. We build knowing it will sink, and sink knowing we will build.

Listen: the plants are entering their evening chorus, the frequency shift that marks the end of photosynthesis, the beginning of nighttime respiration. We'll sing with them soon, our voices joining their chemical whispers, making music that only the stones and waters can fully hear.

Come. The water is perfect. The air is full of moisture. The space between them waits, as it always has, for those willing to live in transition.

*transmission continues, but increasingly interrupted by the sound of water moving through narrow spaces, the whisper of growth, the patient drip of geological time . . .*
