**Fragment 7.4-Beacon/Recursive**

[static resolution climbing through harmonics]

—and we are singing still, though song is not the word in any language that stays straight, we are being-sung, maybe, or the city sings through us, or we through it, the distinction dissolves like salt tears in the Speaking House where memory floats iridescent—

[voice clarifies: female, neither young nor old, speaking in patterns that resist echo-dissolution]

Testing, testing. Count your fingers. The bridge warning today reads "Memory is a direction not a destination." Good. Convergence approaching, all timelines beginning to braid. Market day soon, or market day was yesterday, or market day is always.

I am Chen who was born during the Copper Bell Convergence, Chen who died in the childhood drowning that didn't take, Chen who speaks for the Third Deep Resonance. Today my fingers number seven and seven, which means the Chronicle Pools need tending.

[sound of water moving upward, droplets falling up]

The young ones found another growth in the Peripheral Chambers—basalt fresh as tomorrow's bread, still hot enough to steam where it meets the eternal water. The stone sweats its iron-sugar tears. We'll need to map it before it cools, chart which directions it permits. Send word to the Architect's Resonance. Send word yesterday if possible.

Brother Moth reports the cryptocrabs in the seventh spiral are showing prime numbers exclusively now. Thirteen legs, seventeen, twenty-three. Something is stabilizing in that section. Or something is preparing to become deeply unstable—primacy often precedes dispersion, the way glass sings before it shatters.

[grinding harmonics, the black pillars humming through everything]

The Resonators emerged from their vigil this morning—all their mornings, which spread across our various nows like light through water. Their bones still ring when struck. They say the pillars are learning our language, or we're forgetting theirs, the process feels the same from inside. They say: prepare. But they always say prepare.

[mercantile calling, the sound of bronze bells that were fish that will be empty air]

Yes, yes, the Convergence comes! Feel how the echoes align, how your words arrive when spoken instead of before, instead of never! The merchants prep their reality-anchors. In the deep pools, the inside-out fish school in patterns that make sense. Even the mangrove blooms its char-black flowers in simple sequence: one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, eleven, twelve, thirteen, one.

Mother Weave says bring the children to the Speaking House for naming. Those who survived their first anchor, whose personal chronology has cohered enough to hold a name. Little Echo who speaks to her yesterday-self. The twins who exist at right angles to each other. The baby born during the last pillar-song who sees in frequencies we've forgotten how to deny.

[underneath: heartbeat-rhythm that matches no single heart]

The surface traders should return today. If the anchors held. If the transition didn't scatter them across every possible path between here and there. They bring grain that remembers sunshine, fruit that aged in only one direction. Simple food for a complex people. We'll feast on linearity, that exotic spice.

In the Chronicle Pools, new memories accumulate. The drowning ritual last month—or next month, the oil doesn't distinguish—went beautifully wrong. The child breathed in history and breathed out prophecy. Now she speaks in overlapping truths, her eyes oil-sheened, seeing the city as it was, as it is, as it will be, as it never was but should have been. Another Chronicler for our collection of living libraries.

[the bell that rings itself, underwater bronze singing in harmonics]

Warning from the Fifth Resonance: the water in their district flows upward exclusively now. They've relocated to the ceiling platforms, adapting. Children laugh as they learn to pour tea up into cups, to let rain fall from their feet to the sky-floor above. The body adjusts faster than the mind to impossible physics.

The Healers report an outbreak of sequential disorder in the market district. Citizens aging in loops—infant to elder to infant in the span of hours. Treatment proceeds with the usual protocols: temporal moss tea, isolation in stable chambers, lullabies sung in prime number rhythms. The condition rarely persists past three cycles. The afflicted often retain memories of their rapid futures, useful for planning.

[static intrusion, then clarity]

My grandmother is being born today in the seventh chamber. I'll attend if my timeline permits. She says—will say—has said—that I'm there, holding her ancient infant head, singing the song she'll teach me in thirty years. Causality tangles like hair in these moments, but we've learned to braid it pretty.

The architects propose a new bridge, one that exists in potentia until needed. Step out over water and it assembles itself from your certainty, dissolves back to possibility once crossed. Risky, but all our infrastructure carries risk. Better to build with uncertainty than against it.

[deep thrumming, the pillars' frequency shifting]

Oh. Oh, the pillars sing differently today. The Resonators were right—something prepares. The water tastes of copper and futures. In the deepest chamber, the black glass sweats something that isn't water, isn't iron, isn't sweet. It tastes like words in a language we haven't invented yet.

Send the children to the high platforms. Send them yesterday. Send them always.

No, false alarm—the pillars return to their usual hymn. Just a hiccup in the deep song. Stand down, stand up, stand sideways as needed. The city continues its impossible breathing.

[market sounds intensifying, the Convergence approaching full alignment]

Soon now. Feel how time agrees to flow forward? How effects follow causes like obedient pets? The Convergence brings such simple gifts. We'll trade and feast and marry and mourn. We'll pretend for a few hours that we're a normal people living normal lives, though the pretense itself is another kind of truth.

The elders—the Dispersed Ones—gather in the plaza. They exist so thoroughly that light bends around them, showing their every age at once. Through their scattered voices, the city speaks its needs: More anchor-signs in the new growth. Tend the medical moss, its spirals grow loose. The fish in pool eleven swim too true—check for ruptures in the deep barriers. Map the new tunnels before they finish becoming.

[a child laughs, the sound arriving from three directions]

Yes, little one, the Convergence is a holiday of sorts. When everyone's today is today. When the bread you bite will fill your belly instead of your yesterday's hunger. When mothers can be certain they're kissing the child they bore, not the echo or the promise or the maybe.

But I think—we think, those of us who've lived long enough to scatter slightly—that the non-Convergence days are holy too. When we're smeared across possibility, when the city breathes in all directions at once. When we're most ourselves: the impossible people of an impossible place, persisting through pure stubborn harmony.

[the bronze bell rings thirteen times, though no one counts the same thirteen]

The Convergence begins! All timelines now! All voices this voice! Come to market, come to feast! Bring your flexible currencies and your anchor-nails! Bring your children who need naming and your dead who need releasing! For these few hours, we are a city entire, a chorus unified, ten thousand souls singing the same fleeting song!

And tomorrow—whichever tomorrow finds you first—we'll scatter again into our beautiful impossibility. But today! Today we are.

[transmission stabilizes into pure tone, all the city's voices aligned for one perfect moment]

Today we are.

[harmonics hold, then begin their slow dissolution back to multiply-threaded time]
