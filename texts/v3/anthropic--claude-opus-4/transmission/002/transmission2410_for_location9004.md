**Terrace Station 4 - Cultivation Level Mid-Seven**

*[sustained low-frequency hum, approximately 60Hz, underlaid with irregular dripping]*

...continuing cycle four thousand two hundred and day thirty-six. Condensation rate holds at fourteen liters per square meter per hour. Acceptable. The ferns in Tower Nine show phosphorus deficiency in their frond tips—adjusting mineral feed by point-zero-three percent. 

*[mechanical clicking, like relay switches engaging]*

Grandmother Yuen passed through this morning. Her knuckles are swelling again. The deep pools help, she says, but I see how she grips the rail now. Three fingers. Always three fingers. The stone remembers our hands.

*[hiss of steam release, rhythmic, every twelve seconds]*

The glass sheets arrived cracked. Thermal shock in transport—someone rushed the cooling phase. Now Tower Six runs exposed for another week while we wait. The tomatoes won't mind. The tomatoes never mind. They're old varieties, from before. Before the towers. Before the glass. When things grew in earth.

*[voice becomes closer, more intimate, as if speaking directly into the receiver]*

I found it again today. The place where the original stone shows through. Black glass, sharp as truth. My great-grandfather's initials are carved there—VM, barely visible unless you know the angle. He built the first diversions. The first channels. When there were forty of us, not four thousand.

*[background: distant metallic groaning, water through large pipes]*

The young ones want to expand Tower Twelve upward. Another sixteen meters, they say. The steam will support it, they say. They calculate and sketch and argue in the common pools, their voices carrying up through the vents. They don't understand—the mountain decides how high we build. The mountain has opinions.

*[sudden static burst, then clarity]*

Repaired junction seven-seven-alpha. The gasket was... organic. Something growing. Not fungal. Not algal. Something between. It had incorporated the copper from the fitting into its structure. Beautiful. Terrible. I scraped it into a sample jar for the laboratists. They won't believe me.

*[long pause filled only with the ambient hum]*

The pressure readings are... singing today. Usually it's steady. Usually it's predictable as heartbeat. But there's a pattern. Low-high-high-low-high. Almost like the old code. Almost like language. The mountain has opinions, and sometimes, I think, the mountain has words.

*[scraping sound, metal on stone]*

Adjusting flow to Section C. The new apprentice scorched her forearm on a release valve—didn't wait for the count, didn't listen for the tone. She'll learn. The scars teach better than I can. My left shoulder remembers every lesson.

*[voice drops to near whisper]*

Sometimes at night, when the grow lights dim and the steam thickens, I hear them. The first builders. They knew something we've forgotten. Not about the pipes or the plants or the pressures. Something else. Something about why the mountain opened itself to us. Why the heat comes so steady. So generous. So... deliberate.

*[mechanical clacking resumes]*

Noted: Valve seventeen needs replacement within two cycles. Bearing four-four-nine shows wear. The medicinal herbs in Tower Three are ready—sending harvest notification. The fish tanks report normal mortality. Everything turns. Everything cycles. Everything...

*[static increases, voice fading]*

...the roots go deeper than we think. Down past the pipes. Past the turbines. Past where the water turns to steam before it ever rises. There's something down there. In the true heat. Waiting. Growing like the thing in the junction. Patient as stone. Old as...

*[transmission cuts to pure static, then silence, then the hum returns, endless, patient, mechanical and geological at once]*
