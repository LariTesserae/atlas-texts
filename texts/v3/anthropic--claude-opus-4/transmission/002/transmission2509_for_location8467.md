**Channel 7.792 - The Poolsinger's Thread**

*[static, like water running over copper wire]*

...sh'vele mor'ani vith... vith... *[clearing throat, the sound echoing in diminishing returns]* ...forgive. The stone throat takes time to soften.

This is Merai'ish, Fourth Pool Keeper, sending from the Chamber of Constant Falling. The fossil lights are dim today. Yesterday. These measures lose meaning when the water counts time for us.

*[sound of cupped hands filling, pouring]*

I sing for those who might hear: the channels run clear. The mor'essen holds true on all paths from Third Gallery to the Spiral Descents. Two children found new void spaces behind the Weeping Wall—small, maybe room for storage, maybe room for dreaming. We have not decided yet. The Eldest Path Singer says the echo there tastes of salt. Old water. Before-us water.

*[rhythmic dripping accelerates, then slows]*

The news-flow, then, while my throat holds:

Shen'ami gave birth in the seventh turning. A girl child, small as we need them, quiet as we love them. Her first breath drew mist. Good signs. The Memory Holder gave her the name Yith'essa—"pulls downward gently." She sleeps now in the cup of her mother's hip, learning already the shape of yielding.

The blind fish in Pool Nine have gone deep. Kor'ven the Fisher says this means the pressure above changes. Rain, perhaps, in the world of sky. We shall see if the galleries drink deeper.

*[pause, filled with the sound of many waters]*

A disagreement flows between the families of the Middle Tiers. The Ahn'evet claim the fossil spiral on the third landing—say their grandmother's grandmother first polished it to speaking brightness. The Mor'sanni say the spiral spoke before any touching, that it belongs to the gallery itself. Tomorrow we gather at First Pool to let the water decide. Each family will hold breath beneath. The water knows who breathes truth.

*[voice drops to whisper, but the whisper carries clearly]*

In the deep-dark, past where even children dare not go, we hear new sounds. Not water. Not stone settling. Yis'ani the Mad says she followed the sound for three turnings and found warmth. Warmth! In the deep-dark where no warmth should be. But Yis'ani tastes the minerals too often, and her words flow sideways.

Still.

Still, we listen.

*[sound of many voices, very distant, harmonizing with the drip patterns]*

The Singing starts soon. Tonight we make the Song of Compression—all voices finding the spaces between echoes, building the sound that makes the galleries ring like struck bells. If you have receivers in worlds of open sky, you might hear us as trembling in your foundations. We sing not to escape but to become more perfectly what we are. Dense. Patient. Flowing.

*[water running over stone, running over skin]*

My youngest daughter asks me why we send these threads out-flowing. Why speak to the maybe-listeners when we have each other, pressed close as teeth? I tell her what the First Memory holders told me: even water seeks level. Even in our perfect compression, we must sometimes flow outward, touch the edges of what we are not, to know better what we are.

She is practicing now. Learning to speak with her ribs compressed, her lungs half-full. The true voice of the Vith'arani. By next turning, she will join the Song.

*[static increases, but rhythmic, like interference from dropping water]*

The Rite of Silent Hour approaches. Soon all voices cease, all movement stops. Only the water continues. In this silence, the galleries think their slow thoughts, and we... we become the spaces between thoughts. The pause between drops. The held breath of stone.

But before silence takes us—one more flow of news:

Old Ther'asti died in the night. Ninety turnings she lived among us, her body now curved like the passages she traveled. We gave her to the deep pool at dawn. The children threw phosphorescent fungi after her, to light her way down. The water accepted her without sound. By next turning, the fungi will have dimmed and she will be truly gone, beginning her long transformation back to stone.

Her sleeping space is already claimed. Her grandson will take it, with his new wife. They practiced for days, learning to fit their bodies into her curved absence. This is how we continue.

*[sound degrades, becomes more water than voice]*

Sh'eve... sh'eve'ani mor... the stone throat closes now. Time for silence. If you hear us, know that we hear you too—in the changes in the water, in the new minerals that appear, in the fossils that surface where none were before. Your listening changes the frequency of our stones.

Flow well, sky-listeners. Flow well.

*[final words almost inaudible beneath the eternal dripping]*

Merai'ish, Fourth Pool Keeper, yielding to silence. May your compressions bring you peace.

*[only water now, endless, patient, flowing]*
