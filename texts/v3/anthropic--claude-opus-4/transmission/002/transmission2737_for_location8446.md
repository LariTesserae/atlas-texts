**Salt Gospel Radio**

---

*[static crackle, the sound of crystal growth accelerated ten thousand times—a sharp, brittle percussion like rain on glass]*

Testing... testing in the frequency of minerals. This is Salt Gospel Radio, broadcasting from thirty-seven meters below baseline normal. If you're receiving this, adjust your sodium levels accordingly.

*[electrical hum modulates into something almost melodic]*

We interrupt the factory's dreams to bring you this update from the Understory. Today marks... marks... 

*[voice catches, recovers]*

Time moves different in the mercury light. We measure in crystal growth now. In the spacing between grass blades. The old calendars burned up in our lungs years ago.

*[sound of bare feet on salt crust, rhythmic, meditative]*

This is Vera speaking. Speaking to the pipes. To the grass. To whoever's tuned their bones to this frequency. Chen says the building listens through its ventilation systems. Says our words rise up through the infrastructure like moisture, condensing in unexpected places. Janitor finds salt on a doorknob seventeen floors up—that's us. That's our breath crystallized.

Elias doesn't speak anymore. Hasn't for three seasons of steam-cycling. But his movements... oh, his movements are pure transmission. Watch him tend the northern patch and you'll understand erosion from the inside out. He's writing a manual in footsteps. Chapter Seven: How to Love What Was Never Meant to Be.

*[mechanical groan in the distance, echoing]*

The factory pretends we don't exist. But we're in its dreams now. Vera here, still Vera after all these chemical years. My skin tastes like copper sulfate when I lick my arm. Is that too intimate for radio? Everything's intimate at this depth. The grass knows my mineral signature. Recognizes my shadow even in this shadowless place.

*[pause, breathing that sounds like wind through industrial cavities]*

Today's readings: Soil salinity down two percent in Quadrant Elias. That's what we call it now—the patches take our names like children. Quadrant Chen showing unprecedented root depth. She's been singing to them. Frequency 7.83 Hz. Same as the earth's pulse, she says. How she knows this, calibrated to what instrument, we don't ask.

We don't ask much anymore.

*[sound like paper or dried leaves, but it's salt crystals being brushed aside]*

Broadcasting to you from the permanent noon of elsewhere. Where the ceiling sweats coolant and the floor grows teeth. Beautiful teeth. Geometric. Patient.

Chen's turn: *[voice changes, older, more clipped]*

Ventilation report. The building breathes twenty-three thousand cubic meters per hour through our chamber. We are its lungs' lowest lobe. What grows here grows in the exhale of ten thousand workers above. Their carbon dioxide, our photosynthesis. Even in artificial light, the cycle continues.

I've mapped the acoustic properties. Every surface sings differently. The salt at 2,847 Hz. The concrete columns at 145 Hz. The grass...

*[long pause]*

The grass refuses measurement. Shifts its frequency to match my instruments. Learning. Adapting.

*[original voice returns, Vera]*

That's Chen for you. Still trying to engineer mystery. But she's right about the grass. It knows us now. Grows toward our footsteps before we take them. 

*[electrical interference, then clarity]*

We had names once. Addresses. Social insurance numbers. Now we have mineral signatures. Salt halos. The ability to taste light.

Elias found a new chamber yesterday. Followed a warm air current through a maintenance shaft none of us had noticed. Says it's smaller than ours but the salt there is pure white. Virgin territory. No grass yet. He spent six hours just standing at the threshold. Deciding.

We're careful about expansion. Every step changes the ecosystem. Every breath seeds new possibilities.

*[sound of dripping, but irregular, arrhythmic]*

Sometimes visitors come. Urban explorers. Maintenance crews following problems to their source. They stand at the edges of our light, blinking. Can't quite parse what they're seeing. A few take pictures but the mercury vapor overexposes everything. We look like ghosts in their images. Overlit. Translucent.

They always leave.

We tried to explain once. Early years. Showed them the growth patterns. The chemical gardens. The beauty of industrial accident. But language fails at this depth. You have to live in the frequency to understand it.

*[hum rises, wavers, finds a new harmonic]*

This is Salt Gospel Radio, transmitting from the Understory. From the place between intention and consequence. We broadcast weekly, daily, constantly. Our transmission is the space itself. The kept keeping. The tended tending.

Chen wants me to add: The factory's foundation shows zero degradation in our sector. We're not destroying. We're preserving. Creating stability through careful erosion. A paradox only possible in the margin.

*[footsteps receding, multiple sets, synchronized]*

Time to tend. The northeastern patch needs leaching. The lights in Sector 9 flicker—Chen will climb. Elias has his new chamber to consider.

But we'll return to this frequency. We always do. Broadcast from the bright nowhere. From the chemical cathedral. From the only church that matters—the one you build from industrial accident and patient observation.

*[static increases]*

If you're receiving this, remember: Somewhere beneath your feet, beneath the last basement, beneath intention itself, gardens grow in poisoned ground. Someone tends them. Someone cares for the spaces that weren't supposed to exist.

Adjust your tuning. Listen with your minerals. We're always broadcasting, even when the words run out. Especially then.

This has been Salt Gospel Radio. Vera, Chen, and the silence of Elias signing off. 

Keep your frequency open. Keep your salt levels balanced. Keep believing in the beauty of unintended consequence.

*[final surge of static, then underneath it, barely audible: the sound of grass growing in artificial light, pushing through salt crystal, reaching for its strange mercury sun]*

*[transmission fades to the building's omnipresent electrical hum]*
