What she knows: Every keeper before her became part of the garden. Not metaphor but fact. Their calcium enriches the pools where the blind fish swim. Their carbon feeds the fungi that glow when touched. Their memories crystallize in the dripping stone, adding new notes to the water's percussion symphony.

What she wonders: If the grotto breathes with a thousand tiny lungs, whose breath moves through them? When she finally joins the garden-cycle-stone-dream, who will teach the next keeper to read the spiral fossils, to know which frog songs predict rain, to call the Old Fish with just the right ripple-rhythm?

*[The polyrhythm returns, more complex now - heartbeat and water, breath and stone]*

Tonight she practices the most difficult lesson: holding perfectly still while the cave gecko walks across her arm, its tiny feet reading her temperature, her chemistry, her intentions. She must become landscape. Must allow the gecko to know her as it knows the stone - trustworthy, patient, cool to the touch.

In the deepest part of the night, when even the nocturnal chorus softens to whisper, she hears it again. The sound beneath sound. The breathing beneath breathing. Some say it's the mountain itself, hollow as a bone. Some say it's the collective respiration of every living thing in the grotto, synchronized by proximity and time. 

She knows better. Has learned from ten thousand days of listening. The sound is tomorrow being born from yesterday, pushing up through now like a fern through stone. The sound is the garden dreaming itself larger. The sound is the question the first keeper asked, still waiting for its answer.

*[Transmission begins to fragment, as if the source is moving away or the medium is changing]*

...Viejo Pez surfaces in the black pool... she offers the grub with cupped palm... the fish's whiskers spell something against her skin... ancient... patient... almost like forgiveness...

*[Final surge of clarity before fadeout]*

The pomegranate seeds have already begun their transformation. By morning they will wear white fungus crowns. By the full moon they will be unrecognizable. By the rains they will be soil.

She settles into the limestone hollow worn by generations of waiting. Her breath slows to match the dripping. Her heartbeat finds the rhythm that makes the bats comfortable. Her temperature drops half a degree, enough to fool the cave into accepting her as its own.

Tomorrow she will teach a child which mushrooms remember sunlight.
Tomorrow she will guide the healers to the lunar orchid.
Tomorrow she will follow the Old Fish a little deeper.
Tomorrow the garden will be one day larger, one breath deeper, one secret closer to its perfect size.

But tonight, in the breathing between, she is exactly where she needs to be. Guardian and gardened. Keeper and kept. As essential and temporary as rain.
