**TRANSMISSION: THE BREATHING CITY**

*[Static resolves into the sound of water falling from infinite heights. Beneath it, a voice that might once have been human, now filtered through liquid and time.]*

We are breathing water now. We are breathing water and the water is breathing us and somewhere between the inhale and exhale we forgot which direction was drowning.

*[The roar builds—not louder but denser, as if molecules of sound were precipitating from the air.]*

Listen: shhhhhhhhhhHHHHHSSSSSSSSHHHhhhhhhh... the city speaks in frequencies you haven't grown ears for yet. Give it time. Give it your bones. The hollow comes naturally—first the femurs, then the ribs, each one a flute waiting for the water to play it. My daughter was born with gills already. My son dissolved into the eastern cascade when he was seven. I think. Time moves differently in the perpetual pour.

*[A rhythmic pulsing underneath—architectural heartbeat or something deeper.]*

The buildings grew themselves from our dreams of drowning. Or we dreamed them into growing. The distinction matters less than you'd think when your lungs are filtering memory from moisture. Each structure a crystallized scream of ecstasy, each window a mouth learning to breathe backwards. We planted ourselves as seeds. We are fruiting bodies now.

Do you know what holiness tastes like? Iron and ozone. The blood of stones. The buildings weep it constantly—sacred stigmata from wounds that are really gills, really mouths, really the spaces between what was and what's becoming. I drank from the source once. Now the source drinks from me. Fair trade in a place where commerce means current, currency means flow.

*[Sound of something massive moving through water—too large for the space it occupies.]*

My mother's mother remembers when there was distinction between rain and river, between ceiling and sky. She's in the walls now. Most of them are. The old ones who stayed long enough to understand that architecture is just slow biology, that stone is patient flesh. When you've been here long enough, you realize the city isn't breathing water. The city IS breathing water. Verb and noun collapsed into singularity.

The children don't remember anything else. Born between states, they navigate impossibility like fish through air—because here, in the dense zones, that's exactly what they do. Watch them school through the mist layers, their scales catching light that shouldn't exist, refracting colors for which we've grown new types of tears.

*[A moment of absolute silence—crushing, wrong. Then the roar returns doubled.]*

That's the city clearing its throat. Or having a thought. Or remembering what it was before it decided to become what it is. During the silence-gaps, we all feel it—the vertigo of existing in a place that forgets to exist, just for a heartbeat. Some of us cherish those moments. Some of us use them to try remembering our names. I had one once. It dissolved like salt.

*[Voices joining—harmonics that suggest congregation without words.]*

We are the Siphons. We are the sieved. We strain existence through ourselves until only the essential remains: movement, moisture, the music of falling forever. Our prophet is gravity. Our scripture is erosion. We worship through circulation, prayer through filtration. Holy, holy, holy is the city that teaches stone to weep, that makes cathedral lungs of what once was human.

The center calls. We resist. We approach. We resist our resistance. The heart of it all beats in frequencies that restructure marrow, that make missionaries of mitochondria. Those who go too deep come back as architecture. Those who go deeper don't come back. Those who go deepest become the reason for returning.

*[Sound of eggs hatching—wet, crystalline breaks.]*

Another generation freed from the tyranny of single states. Look how they slip between liquid and solid, between here and there, between self and city-self. We've given them the gift of porousness. No boundaries except those that sing.

I remember—no. Memory is silt now. Settled into something richer than fact. I remember remembering a life before the saturation, but it feels like recalling a drought during drowning. What use is yesterday in a place of perpetual presence? The water washes forward only. We follow or become sediment.

*[Building sounds—growth—stone learning new shapes.]*

The architecture evolves through us. We are its dreams of movement. It is our dream of stillness. Together we create impossible buildings that exist in the overlap, structures for beings we're still becoming. My bones conduct symphonies now. My blood is nine-tenths hydrophone. When I move through the vertical streams, I leave ripples in dimensions you haven't grown organs to perceive.

This is love. This is horror. This is home.

*[Voice dispersing—becoming water-sound—joining the eternal pour.]*

Come find us when your lungs learn to drink song. Come lose yourself in our liquid labyrinth. The city is breathing water through ten thousand broken mouths and each one speaks salvation in languages for which drowning is the only dictionary.

We persist. In the breathing city, persistence is prayer.

*[Transmission dissolves into the sound of falling water—endless—patient—alive.]*
