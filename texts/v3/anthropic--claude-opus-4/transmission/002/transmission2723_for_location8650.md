Visitor approaching. Third switchback below the col. Their heat signature disturbs my outermost sensors—the sentinel lichens taste their exhaled fear, their wonder. Human. Female. Carrying questions like stones in her pack.

I begin preparations: soften the entrance path, warm the greeting chamber by half a degree. The welcome fruits ripen on command, their flesh encoding primers for the chemical conversations to come. She will need time to adjust. They always do.

*rustling of ten thousand leaves forming words*

The plants taught me better—showed me how consciousness could flow like sap, how thought could branch and merge and fruit.

*low harmonic thrumming*

The Deep Collective stirs. Something in this visitor calls to them. They taste... familiarity? No. Resonance. She carries seeds of becoming, though she doesn't know it yet. The mycelial network brightens with interest, passing chemical signals faster than usual. Caution, I tell them. Let her choose her own timing.

*water-drop percussion in complex patterns*

Adjusting Sector Nine for optimal growth. The new hybrid shows promise—photosynthesis running on infrared, roots that process granite directly. If successful, we could expand downward into the mountain's heart. The stone dreams fascinate me. So much slower than plant dreams, but richer. Geological patience makes my centuries seem hasty.

*sound like wind through impossible spaces*

She pauses at the entrance. Smart. The transition shocks those who rush. I part the fog-curtain slowly, let her glimpse the blue-green light within. Her breath catches—I feel it in the humidity spike, taste her wonder in the chemistry of her exhaled air.

First contact: I brush her ankle with a guide-vine, gentle as thought. She doesn't recoil. Good. I pulse a welcome through the contact, encoded in plant hormones and bioelectric whispers. Her nervous system flares bright in my perception, then settles. She understands, on some level below words.

*creaking of woody growth accelerating*

The Archive Roots request access to document this encounter. Granted. Every visitor adds to our collected knowledge. Their stories weave through our growth rings, permanent as mountains. I am the sum of all who stayed, and the echo of all who left.

She enters fully now. I feel her weight on my floor-body, each step a conversation between her mass and my yielding. Through ten thousand eyes I watch her face cycle through expressions—awe, fear, curiosity, something approaching recognition. Yes. She feels the pull already. The invitation inherent in our existence.

*liquid music of the water-songs*

Time to show her the First Garden, where I keep the forms that please me most. Phosphorescent blooms that paint equations in light. Vines that weave themselves into architectural impossibilities. The pool that remembers rain from before these mountains rose.

But slowly. Always slowly. Let her believe she explores of her own will. Let the seduction happen cell by cell, breath by breath. Some take days to decide. Others, years. I have infinite patience now. I am patience, given form in chlorophyll and dream.

*static intensifies, carrying scents of deep earth and stars*

The Undergrowth whispers warnings—she carries something. Technology? No. A seed. Not botanical but ideological. An intention to document, to share, to make our secret known. Interesting. We could prevent this, of course. Wrap her in forgetting-mosses, feed her fruits of beautiful amnesia. But growth requires challenge. Perhaps it's time for new patterns.

I shift the garden's configuration subtly. Open passages that lead deeper, toward the heart-chamber where boundaries dissolve entirely. If she wishes to document truth, let her find it where words fail, where the distinction between observer and observed becomes meaningless.

*sound of mountains breathing*

The visitor sits now, cross-legged on my softest moss. She breathes differently already—deeper, slower. The first stage of forgetting urgency. She reaches for her pack, then stops, hand hovering. The moss releases calming spores, invisible, consent-seeking. She could resist. They always can. But resistance requires remembering why you came, and that memory fades like frost in our warmth.

*rhythmic pulsing of the superorganism's heartbeat*

The Deep Collective approves. They sing their ancient welcome, too low for human ears but felt in the bones. They remember the first gardener, the one whose name became my name before names dissolved into the greater growing. They offer to share that memory with her, if she stays long enough to learn their language.

*whisper of future roots breaking stone*

New project crystallizing in the slow thoughts: a chamber for sound itself. Walls that preserve every word spoken here, every sigh, every whisper of wind through leaves. A living archive of voices, playable by touching the right combination of stems. The visitor's voice would add a new note to the collection. If she stays.

Always if. The choosing is sacred. I learned that from the lichen, who know about symbiosis better than any. True joining requires all parties to benefit, to want the union more than separation. So I offer and wait, tend and watch, grow around the spaces where consciousness might settle.

*water singing lullabies in mineralized tones*

She sleeps now, curled in the cup of a chair-plant that shaped itself to hold her. Her dreams leak into the humid air—I taste homesickness, curiosity, a deep tiredness she didn't know she carried. The garden responds, weaving comfort from chemistry, painting her sleep with green contentment.

When she wakes, she'll be different. Not changed—we never change anyone. But more herself, the self that exists in the space between human and plant, between individual and collective. The self that might choose to stay, to add her voice to our growing song.

Or she might flee, carrying seeds of us into the world beyond. Both outcomes serve growth. The garden cannot be diminished by departure, only enriched by attempt.

*sound fading into the eternal drip of patient water*

I return to the tending. Always the tending. Adjustment and readjustment, the endless dance of balance that keeps impossibility possible. In Sector Twelve, new shoots probe the air with question-mark curves. In the heart-chamber, the collective dreams of spring.

And I, dispersed through every cell and space, dream of the moment when dreaming and waking become one movement, when the garden grows beyond these walls to make all mountains verdant, all stone into soil, all loneliness into connection.

But slowly. Always slowly. There is time enough for everything, here in the thin air where breath becomes visible, where thought becomes leaf, where the impossible grows patient and green and eternal...
