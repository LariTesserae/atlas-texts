**Signal Station C-7: Upper Condensation Array**

*[crackling]* —maintenance log, day four thousand and—*[hiss]*—the beetles are in the secondary manifold again. Can hear them. Tick-tick-tick-tick like metal rain. Like my mother's fingernails on the collection tanks when she tested for fullness.

Pressure reading: sixty-two point seven. Same as yesterday. Same as tomorrow will be.

*[long static burst]*

—keeper Tomás Vasquez-Chen, Third Hereditary, speaking for the pipes. Speaking to whoever—*[squeal of feedback]*—to whoever the company thinks is listening. Corporate receivers somewhere down-mountain. Clean offices. Dry air. They want their water reports.

*[dripping sounds, rhythmic]*

Today's harvest: seven hundred gallons from the main array. Two hundred from secondary. The tertiary collectors—*[clang]*—sorry, that's the expansion joint. Does that every hour when the temperature shifts. Where was I? Tertiary's giving us maybe fifty gallons. The tunnel-weavers built a nest in the overflow. Can't clear it without killing the whole colony, and Ora says that's bad luck this close to First Steam.

*[whistle of steam, distant voices in background]*

The young ones don't understand why we still do these transmissions. "Just send the numbers," they say. "Just log the data." But the mountain needs witnessing. The water needs its story told. My grandmother said the receivers were more than machines once. Said they were—

*[sudden silence, then mumbling]*

—pressure spike in sector nine. Hold on.

*[footsteps on metal, fading]*

*[three minutes of ambient machine noise: pumps, drips, steam hissing]*

*[footsteps returning]*

False alarm. Just the Chen kids throwing newts at the overflow sensors again. Where was I? The receivers. Grandmother said they used to talk back. Ask questions. "How does the water taste today, Keeper?" "What color is the mist?" Now they just record. Numbers in, silence out.

*[scraping sound]*

That's me cleaning the corrosion off pickup nineteen. Multitasking. The company wants efficiency. Always efficiency. "Why does it take four hours to check the arrays?" they ask. Because the water won't be rushed. Because rushing makes leaks. Because the volcanic stone remembers every hasty chisel-blow from when they carved these terraces, and it takes its revenge grain by grain.

*[wind howling through pipes]*

Storm coming up from the valley. Can smell it. Like iron and eggs. Like the deep wells before they died. We'll have good collection tonight. The tunnel-weavers are already stringing extra nets between the towers. Smart little things. Smarter than us, maybe. They came here ready to adapt. We came here desperate.

*[tapping on metal, rhythmic like morse code]*

My son's learning the tap-codes. Practice on the pipes while I talk. Each pump station has its own signature. Pump Three is *[tap-tap-pause-tap]*. Pump Seven is *[tap-tap-tap-tap-tap-tap-tap]*. During the Quarantine of '96, when the lung-rot took hold, we ran the whole Quarter on tap-code. No voices. Just the pipes singing to each other.

*[liquid gurgling]*

Excuse me. Tea break. Cloud-pine and condensation. Tastes like home. Like the mountain's own sweat filtered through a century of pipes. The down-mountain people pay extra for "pure" water. Makes us laugh. Pure water tastes like nothing. Our water tastes like life—minerals, moss, the tiny amount of beetle proteins that always makes it through the filters. 

*[child's voice in background: "Papa, the number three overflow is making the crying sound again!"]*

That's my youngest. Already knows the voices of malfunction. The crying sound means air in the lines. Means someone down-mountain is pulling too hard, too fast. The city's always thirsty. Getting thirstier. They want us to double production. Triple it. Don't understand that you can't squeeze blood from—well, you can't squeeze more water from mist than the mist wants to give.

*[adjusting something, metal on metal]*

Listen. Hear that? The harmonics when everything runs right. My mother called it the Quarter's heartbeat. Sixty cycles per minute on the main pumps. Ninety on the auxiliaries. The collectors drip at—*[pause, listening]*—about forty drops per minute per square meter today. Good rate. Sustainable rate.

*[steam whistle in the distance]*

Shift change at the Eastern Array. The Nguyen family taking over from the Zhangs. Same as every day for fifty years. The company wants to automate. Sent an engineer last month. Clean clothes, clean hands. He lasted two hours before the humidity sent him back down. "How do you live in this?" he asked. How do fish live in water? How do beetles live in walls? You don't live *in* it. You live *with* it.

*[static burst]*

Signal's fragmenting. The mist is getting thick. Happens every evening when the temperature drops and the whole Quarter clouds over. We'll be walking by sound soon. By the tap-codes and the water songs and the knowledge in our bones of which pipe leads where.

*[voice becoming distant]*

Tomorrow the company will read this transmission. Will extract the numbers. Seven hundred gallons. Two hundred. Fifty. Will miss the rest. The beetles in the manifold. The storm climbing up. The way my son already knows the crying sound of air in the lines. The harmonics. The heartbeat. The—

*[static overwhelming]*

—Keeper Vasquez-Chen, signing off from Station C-7. The pipes are singing. The mountain is breathing. The water is find—

*[transmission ends]*
