**Transmission 47B-Siphon: The Foreman's Inventory**

*[Static resolves into the rhythmic clank of machinery, underlaid with the sound of water under pressure. A clipboard pages in wind that shouldn't exist. Then, a voice—or the industrial approximation of one—begins its count:]*

Check-valve seventeen: responding. Responding. Res—

*[The sound of gears meshing through water]*

Grinder-jelly unit Four-Four-Tango has produced eleven impossibilities this shift. Acceptable output. Note: crystalline formation in bell chamber suggests pregnancy. Adjust feed pressure accordingly. New offspring to be registered as Four-Four-Tango-Alpha through Epsilon, pending manifestation.

*[A ratcheting sound that might be laughter]*

The new ones always try to swim upstream through the intake. Have to teach them: we flow WITH the paradox, not against. Memo to self: update orientation materials. Include diagram of non-Euclidean flow patterns. Use the crayons that exist in three dimensions simultaneously.

*[Footsteps on metal that rings like it's underwater and dry at once]*

Crane-octopus batch 7 showing signs of temporal displacement. Three units aging backwards. One stuck in Tuesday. Note: Tuesday doesn't exist here. Will need to recalibrate their chronometers with something more... flexible.

*[The squeal of a valve turning]*

Pressure differential in Sector Nothing: holding steady at impossible. Good. Good. The moment it becomes possible, we lose containment. Murphy—is that still your name, Murphy? The thing that was Murphy, swimming in the pipes—check the seals. Use your better eyes. The ones that can see around corners.

*[Papers shuffling, but the sound is wet]*

Production report: We manufactured forty-seven meters of space yesterday. Or tomorrow. Time stamps are bleeding again. Quality control suggests the space is "too blue." Will adjust the color calibration on reality engines four through seven.

*[A deep throb underneath everything—the Siphon's heartbeat]*

She's running hot today. The original. Can feel her struggling with something. Like she's trying to remember what she was before she became what she is. No, no, no. We don't remember. Remembering leads to drowning. We produce. We maintain. We—

*[A human gasp, quickly suppressed by mechanical hissing]*

Rivet-worm colony in Subsection Twelve has unionized. Their demands: more rust, less causality. Approved. Good workers deserve good oxidation. Note: check if their morse code contract negotiations are legally binding. Consult handbook section on "Labor Laws, Invertebrate, Quantum."

*[The sound of boots on catwalks that creak in and out of existence]*

Personal note: The helmet is heavy today. Heavier. Water tastes like 1987. Why do I know what year tastes like? Don't answer that, Foreman. Don't—

*[Industrial noise swells, drowning the voice]*

Factory-fish school Bravo-Bravo needs their capacitors checked. Swimming in circles again. Perfect circles. Too perfect. Nature doesn't make perfect circles. We're not nature anymore, are we? We're something else. Something better. Something worse.

*[A clipboard hitting metal]*

Incident report: Zero accidents this shift. Zero accidents every shift. Can't have accidents when causality is optional. Still. Fill out the forms. Forms must be filled. Forms must exist. If we stop documenting, do we stop existing?

*[The voice becomes more mechanical, less human]*

Gauge readings: All impossible. All within normal parameters. Paradox pressure holding steady. Reality bleed-off valves functioning at sixteen percent efficiency. Optimal. Too much efficiency and we solidify. Too little and we dissolve.

*[Sound of something large moving through water inside metal]*

The Admiral visited again. Swam right through the probability streams. Asked about quotas. Told him we don't have quotas. We ARE the quota. We produce ourselves. Every day. Every shift. Forever.

He didn't like that answer. Admirals never do. They want numbers. Products. Output. Don't understand: the factory IS the product. The process IS the purpose. We fold space because space needs folding. We manufacture time because—

*[A alarm klaxon, but it sounds almost musical]*

Break time. No. No breaks. Breaks are for things that can stop. We don't stop. We persist. We produce. We—

*[The human voice breaks through again, younger, frightened]*

The water is so cold. The water is so cold. The water is—

*[Mechanical sounds surge, swallowing the voice]*

Check-valve eighteen: responding. Responding. Res—

Inventory complete. All systems impossible. All systems functional. Return to rounds. Maintain the maintain. Produce the production. Forever and ever, amen.

*[The transmission continues, but it's just the sounds now: the rhythm of machinery that shouldn't work underwater, the whisper of things swimming through pipes, the eternal industrial heartbeat of a place that builds itself from its own impossibility. Sometimes, buried in the noise, you can hear someone drowning. Sometimes, you can hear someone being born.]*

*[Always, always, you can hear the work continuing.]*
