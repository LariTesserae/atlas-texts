**VERTICAL CITY BEACON - CHANNEL 7.4.2**

*[Static rises and falls like breathing. Through it, a voice emerges—young, female, speaking in the clipped rhythm of someone who has learned that thin air makes every word precious.]*

Testing... testing on all standard frequencies. This is Platform Seven maintenance beacon, automated weather and conditions report follows. Current temp minus twelve Celsius. Wind gusting forty-seven kay from the northwest. Ice formation on cables seventeen through twenty-three. Seismic reading... point zero three. Within normal parameters.

*[A long hiss of static, then the voice returns, more relaxed now]*

Okay, that's the official bit done. This is Kira, third shift cable maintenance, and if anyone's actually listening to these recordings down below, well... hello from the edge of the sky.

*[The sound of wind buffeting a microphone, metal creaking]*

I'm up in Station Seven right now, the one everyone calls the Crow's Nest. Highest inhabited point on the North Ridge. My cats are here with me—Lenin and Trotsky, brothers from the same litter, both of them watching the bats-that-aren't-bats swooping past the window. They make this chittering sound... wait...

*[Faint chittering, like fingernails on glass]*

Hear that? They're hunting the moths that come to the station lights. Everything up here is about the lights, about the little pockets of warmth we make. The city glows orange from the smelters, but up here it's white LEDs and the green glow of the monitoring equipment.

*[A metronome-like clicking begins—ice forming on the cables]*

That sound means I'll need to go out soon. Steam the lines before the morning shift. But for now, it's just me and the cats and whoever might be listening. The other operators, maybe, in their own stations. We don't talk much but sometimes I hear them testing their equipment. Little blips of voice across the channels.

*[Static surge, then clearing]*

My grandmother worked the mines, back when they first broke ground. She used to say the mountain talked to her down there. Not words, but... pressures. Shifts in the air that meant the rock was thinking about moving. I get that up here sometimes. The whole city is having a conversation we're only partly invited to.

*[A deeper sound underneath—the mountain settling, a bass note felt more than heard]*

There. You hear it? That's the bedrock adjusting. The engineers say it's thermal contraction, but I think grandmother was right. The mountain is still deciding if it wants us here.

*[Wind howls, then drops to nothing]*

I've been here seven years now. Came up on a two-year contract, same as everyone. But the thing about this place... it marks you. Changes your inner ear. Down below, the flatness feels wrong now. Like something essential is missing. My mother writes asking when I'm coming home, and I don't know how to tell her that this is home. This station with its swaying and its cats and its two a.m. ice-clearing runs.

*[The click of claws on metal decking]*

That's Lenin. He's caught something. Probably one of those thumb-sized beetles that somehow live up here, eating the moss that grows on the warm pipes. Everything finds a niche. Even us.

*[A new sound—rhythmic clanging from far below]*

Night shift at the smelters. Jorge's crew, probably. They play music down there sometimes, old stuff from before the mountains rose. It carries up through the framework on quiet nights. Makes the whole city sing.

*[Static washes over the music, fragmenting it]*

I should tell you about the morning my station almost went over. Six months back. The rock face it was anchored to just... gave up. Decided it had held us long enough. I woke to the floor tilting, everything sliding toward the windows. The cats knew before I did—they were already at the door, yowling.

*[Pause. When the voice returns, it's softer]*

The engineers say I was lucky. The secondary anchors held. Gave me time to get out, ride the emergency zip line to Platform Six. But for about thirty seconds, I was falling with the building. Still attached, still inside, but falling. And the strangest thing? I wasn't scared. It felt... inevitable. Like the mountain was just correcting an error.

*[The sound of a zipper, rustling fabric]*

They rebuilt the station. Same spot, deeper anchors. I moved back in the day they certified it safe. Where else would I go? This is my watch. Someone has to keep the cables clear, feed the cats, make these recordings that nobody listens to.

*[A kettle begins to whistle in the background]*

Tea break. The only proper drug up here. We trade the good stuff like currency—real leaves, not the synthetic substitute. Mrs. Chen on Platform Four grows mint in her greenhouse. I've got a tin of it here, worth a week's wages down below.

*[Liquid pouring, the clink of metal on metal]*

The sun will be up in three hours. It'll hit the peaks first, turn them pink-gold for about twelve minutes before the clouds roll in. The morning cable car will start its run, carrying the night shift down, the day shift up. The city will creak and stretch and begin another day of being impossible.

*[Steam hissing from the kettle]*

But right now, it's just us night dwellers. The engineers watching their screens. The smelter crews feeding the furnaces. The flying things folding their wings as they settle in to sleep. And me, talking to no one, or everyone, or maybe just to the mountain itself.

*[The clicking of ice on cables intensifies]*

Time to gear up. Harness, steam pack, headlamp. The cats know the routine—they'll watch from the window, two sets of eyes glowing in the station lights. I'll be out on the lines, melting ice that would kill someone in the morning if I left it. That's the job. Keep the city moving. Keep the connections clear.

*[Sound of equipment being gathered, buckles and straps]*

Sometimes I think about the people who'll come after us. After the mountain finally shakes us off, or we leave for other impossible places. They'll find our anchors in the rock, our cables rusting in the wind. Will they understand what we were doing here? This thing we built because we could, because the mountain dared us to try?

*[Door opening, wind roaring in]*

TROTSKY, NO—

*[Scuffling, a meow, door slamming]*

Close one. He's getting bold. Wants to hunt with the real predators. Can't blame him. We all get that urge sometimes, to step off the platform and see if we've learned to fly.

*[Breathing apparatus being fitted, voice now muffled]*

Signing off. Station Seven secure, heading out for maintenance. If you're listening, stay warm. Stay anchored. Trust your equipment and don't trust the rock. And remember—we're all just guests here, clinging to the side of something bigger than we'll ever understand.

*[Footsteps on metal grating, fading]*

This is Kira, third shift. The mountain is singing tonight. Can you hear it?

*[Static swells, carrying the faint sound of cables vibrating in harmony, the city's nighttime chorus. A cat meows once, sharp and clear. Then only the wind, and the patient clicking of ice forming, and somewhere far below, the eternal rumble of industry carved into stone.]*

*[Transmission continues, wordless now—just the ambient sound of the vertical city at night, the mountain breathing, the occasional scatter of claws on metal, a distant whistle of steam. The beacon remains open, broadcasting the reality of this impossible place to anyone who might be listening in the depths below.]*
