**CARVER'S FREQUENCY**

*[static resolution into rhythmic tapping—metal on stone, steady as heartbeat]*

Below... below... the thirty-seventh deepening... 

*[voice enters: female, young-middle years, speaking in monotone whisper that carries perfectly along stone grain]*

I am Nila Copper-Eyes, apprentice to the Silent Channel. My master sleeps the stone-sleep now, three days without movement, and the work... the work must continue. This is my third solitary shift. The channel must reach the new cistern before the melt-season or the entire Seventh Quarter goes dry.

*[tapping continues underneath, occasionally syncopating with words]*

The stone here speaks limestone—you can taste it in the dust, feel it in how the chisel bites clean. Different from the granite above. More forgiving. More... alive. Each strike releases the smell of ancient seas. My great-grandmother told me this stone remembers water, holds the memory of oceans in its grain. Now it will hold our water again.

*[pause—sound of blade scraping, measuring]*

Two finger-widths since morning bell. The stone wants to split diagonal here—I can feel it in the vibration, that slight catch in the rhythm. But diagonal is wrong. Water finds its own level, must flow true. So I work against the grain, each strike a negotiation.

*[breathing audible now, slightly labored in thin air]*

My sisters say I've gone stone-drunk. Three days alone with only the echo-pulse to mark time. But they don't understand—you can't leave the work half-spoken. The channel develops its own logic, its own voice. To abandon it now would be like... like stopping mid-breath.

*[new sound: water dripping, very faint]*

There. You hear? The old channel weeps through hairline cracks. It knows its replacement comes. Everything here knows its time—the fungi that fruit once a century, the bats that return to exact roosts their grandmothers used, the stone that agrees to part along lines laid down when the world was young.

*[tapping intensifies, more purposeful]*

My master showed me the taste-map of this section before his sleep took him. Bitter here where the iron runs through. Sweet where the calcite clusters. Salt traces from the ancient waters. I navigate by tongue and fingertip now, the darkness so complete that my pupils ache from trying to open wider than wide.

*[scraping sounds—clearing debris]*

The stone mice have found me again. They follow the work, knowing fresh chips make good gnawing. One has white fur—unusual. It sits just beyond my chisel-reach, waiting. We've developed an understanding, this mouse and I. It takes only the smallest chips, the ones I'd sweep away anyway. Conservation in all things, even in our scavenging.

*[voice drops lower, almost subsonic]*

Sometimes I think I can hear them—all the carvers before me. Their rhythms still live in the stone, a thousand thousand strikes that weakened the grain just enough for my own strikes to succeed. We are all one long conversation with the mountain, each generation adding words to a sentence that won't complete until the plateau itself is hollow.

*[sudden crack—stone splitting]*

Ah! There—the grain accepts my argument. A clean break, straight as intention. The water will remember this path.

*[shuffling, repositioning]*

My knuckles bleed again. The blood makes the chisel slippery, but master says blood is just another binding agent. Everything that falls here becomes part of the work. Sweat, skin, blood—we mortar ourselves into these walls strike by strike.

*[new rhythm—faster, more confident]*

The echo-pulse says two hours until shift bell. But I won't answer it. The channel speaks so clearly now, telling me where it wants to go. My copper eyes see nothing in this darkness, but my hands... my hands see everything. The grain, the faults, the secret softnesses where water has prepared the way.

*[pause—drinking sound]*

Rationed water tastes of silver down here, filtered through seven levels of stone. One cup per shift, but the work-thirst multiplies it somehow. Each sip becomes a river.

*[tapping resumes, meditative]*

They will send someone soon. Protocol demands it—no one works alone below the twentieth deepening for more than four shifts. But I'll wedge the entrance, claim stone-deafness. This channel will not accept another's rhythm now. It knows my frequency, the particular weight of my strikes.

*[sound of fungus-fiber cloth dragging on stone]*

The white mouse comes closer. In the phosphor-gardens, white means poison—never eat the pale fruit. But here, in the carving dark, white means... what? Adaptation? Mutation? Or just the mountain's way of marking what belongs to it completely?

*[voice becoming rhythmic, matching chisel strikes]*

Strike and the stone listens
Strike and the stone speaks  
Strike and the stone remembers
Strike and the stone accepts

My master's master taught him this chant. Small words for large work. The channel grows by dust-widths, but it grows. Always it grows.

*[cracking sound—larger break]*

The breakthrough comes like birth—sudden after so much labor. I smell different air, taste the pressure change. The new cistern chamber, waiting empty like a stone womb. My channel will fill it, feed it, make it alive with the possibility of water.

*[tools being set down, exhausted breathing]*

The white mouse investigates the breakthrough, whiskers twitching. First witness to my completion. I should return now, report success, take my rest rotation. But...

*[movement sounds, settling]*

But the stone still warm from working. And my blood has marked this place as mine. I'll sleep here, just a few hours, curled in the channel like water waiting to flow. Let them come looking. Let them find me stone-drunk and dreaming, my copper eyes reflecting nothing, my hands still curved around ghost tools.

*[voice fading to whisper]*

Below the thirty-seventh deepening, I am becoming limestone. Patient. Essential. Remembering oceans while preparing for new waters. The channel is complete, but I... I am still being carved.

*[tapping resumes—but softer now, irregular, like a sleeping heartbeat against stone]*

*[static increases, swallowing sound, but the rhythm continues underneath—eternal, patient, the pulse of the mountain itself being slowly, carefully hollowed]*
