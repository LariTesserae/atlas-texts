**Frequency 7.4.12 — The Midnight Adjustment**

*[Initial carrier wave establishes itself as a low mechanical thrumming, punctuated by the distant ping of water drops on metal. The signal clarifies gradually, like someone fine-tuning through static to find the exact frequency.]*

...third gauge showing stress at point four seven, point four eight... no, wait... 

*[Sound of metal against stone, a careful tap-tap-tap]*

That's not... hold on, Yenna. The whole northeast bearing's shifted again. Two millimeters since yesterday's reading. 

*[A second voice joins, younger, breathless from climbing]*

Two millimeters? But the growth charts said we had another week before—

*[A deep grinding sound interrupts, felt more than heard through the transmission. Both voices pause. The sound of scratching pencil on paper.]*

Charts don't know this mountain like I do. Feel that? Through your feet? 

*[Silence except for the background hum of machinery, the whisper of mist]*

Like... like a cat stretching?

Exactly like that. Good. You're learning. Pass me the tension key—no, the blue-handled one. We need to loosen the entire grid before she decides to really move.

*[Metallic clicking sounds, rhythmic and practiced. The grinding sensation subsides slightly.]*

Ma'am? Why do we call the mountain 'she'?

*[A soft laugh, barely distinguishable from the sound of steam releasing]*

Same reason sailors call ships 'she,' I suppose. Anything that can kill you but keeps you alive deserves that kind of respect. Plus, have you ever known anything male to be this unpredictable and still make perfect sense?

*[The younger voice giggles. More clicking sounds, faster now.]*

My father says the mountain is male because it's always growing where it's not wanted.

Your father's a pipe-runner, not a Calibrator. We know better. Hand me that mineral lamp—I want to check something.

*[A pause. The sound of glass clinking against stone. When the first voice speaks again, it's with quiet wonder.]*

Oh. Oh, Yenna, look at this. 

What is—oh! It's beautiful! Is that... is the stone changing color?

Manganese. She's bringing up manganese deposits. That means the whole composition under Grid Seven is shifting from metamorphic to... something new. Something still deciding.

*[The excitement in both voices is palpable now, cutting through the professional calm]*

Should we wake Chief Tovar?

Let him sleep. We'll need him fresh when the day shift starts asking why we've loosened an entire grid without authorization. Besides, this is why they pay us night shift differential. We're not just maintaining—we're midwifing.

*[Sound of boots on wet stone, careful steps across what sounds like a narrow ledge]*

Midwifing stone?

Think about it. The mountain's not just growing—she's becoming. And we're here to make sure she doesn't tear the city apart while she figures out what she wants to be. Every adjustment we make, every bolt we loosen or tighten—we're helping her give birth to herself.

*[A long pause. The sound of wind through pipes creates an almost musical tone.]*

Ma'am? Do you think she knows we're here? The mountain?

*[Another pause, longer. When the answer comes, it's soft, almost lost in the mechanical background noise.]*

I've been doing this for thirty years, since before you were born. I've felt her moods through my palms, tasted her chemistry changes in the water, watched her swallow buildings and birth new cliff faces. And honestly? I think she does know. I think that's why she grows so carefully around us, why the really violent shifts always happen in the uninhabited sections. We're not just parasites to her. We're... 

Symbionts?

*[A pleased sound, like approval]*

Exactly. Symbionts. She gives us stone and steam and minerals. We give her... I don't know. Purpose? Direction? Maybe just company. Maybe even a mountain gets lonely, being born.

*[The grinding sound returns, louder now, accompanied by a new harmonic—almost like singing from deep within the stone]*

Quick now—Grid Seven, all tension points. Release pattern Delta-Three. Just like we practiced.

*[The following minutes are a symphony of mechanical adjustment—clicks, releases, the song of tensioned cables finding new equilibrium. Throughout, both voices call out numbers and confirmations in the abbreviated language of their craft. The mountain's grinding song continues, rising and falling like breath.]*

Last bolt loosened. Grid Seven fully floating.

Good. Now we wait. Come sit—there's nothing more we can do until she decides how much she wants to move.

*[Sounds of settling, the creak of leather harnesses. A thermos opening, liquid pouring.]*

Is that climbing rose tea?

My grandmother's blend. Roses from the sector twelve gardens, grown in pure condensate from the seventh tier. She always said it helped her feel what the mountain was feeling.

*[Sipping sounds. A comfortable silence except for the ever-present mechanical hum and the mountain's deep song.]*

Ma'am? What's the strangest thing you've ever seen the mountain do?

*[A long consideration]*

Five years ago, maybe six, we had a formation push up through the market square on tier four. But instead of the usual granite or schist, it was pure crystal. Quartz, but with these inclusions that looked like frozen lightning. The morning sun hit it and the whole square lit up like... like the mountain was showing off. Showing us something beautiful just because she could.

What happened to it?

*[A sad sound, part sigh, part laugh]*

It grew too fast. The crystal couldn't support its own weight once it reached about three meters. Shattered one night during the full moon. Sounded like a thousand wine glasses breaking at once. But for two weeks, we had this... this gift. This reminder that she's not just rock and minerals. She's an artist, too.

*[The grinding sound begins to fade. The harmonic singing softens to a whisper.]*

I think... I think she's done for now. Grid Seven held.

You did well, Yenna. Very well. First time managing a full grid adjustment on your own.

I had a good teacher.

*[The sound of equipment being packed, tools returning to cases]*

Same time tomorrow night?

Unless the day shift does something catastrophic. Remember to log the manganese emergence in the growth journal. And Yenna? Start bringing your own tea. My grandmother's blend is almost gone, and you'll want your own rituals for the long nights ahead.

Yes, Ma'am. Ma'am? One more question?

*[An encouraging sound]*

Do you think we'll ever fully understand her? The mountain?

*[The longest pause yet. When the answer comes, it carries the weight of years, the wisdom of countless adjustments, the patience of someone who has learned to love uncertainty.]*

I hope not. The day we fully understand her is the day she stops surprising us. And what would be the point of living on a living mountain if she became predictable? No, Yenna. We don't seek to understand her. We seek to dance with her. And tonight, we danced well.

*[The voices fade, replaced by the sound of footsteps on metal catwalks, growing distant. The mechanical hum continues, now accompanied by the soft sound of new stone cooling in the night air, the drip of condensation, the whisper of wind through industrial architecture. The transmission continues in this wordless state for several minutes—the night sounds of a city and mountain breathing together, adjusting together, becoming together.]*

*[Finally, just before the signal fades completely, a single sound: the bright ping of water finding a new path through stone, followed by what might be—if mountains could express such things—a sigh of satisfaction.]*

*[Static returns, but gentler now, like mist settling over vertical gardens in the dark.]*
