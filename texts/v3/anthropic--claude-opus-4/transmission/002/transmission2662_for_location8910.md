*[Sound of bronze on stone. Rhythmic. Purposeful.]*

Today we channel the new spring in the seventh gallery. The one the stone-borers revealed last moon. My daughter holds the measuring string while I tap tap tap the bronze into place. She hums to the stone as I've taught her, the frequency that calms the acoustic-moths. They nest too close sometimes, drawn to the vibrations of our work, and their wings—beautiful as they are—clog the channels.

She asks: why do we stay when the merchants fear this place, when the stone might swallow our hard-made homes?

I taste the water running past my feet. Copper and salt and something else, something that has no name except the-taste-of-home.

I tell her: we stay because we are learning to be rain.
