**Resonance Archive Entry #4,451: The Drought Prophet of Kharan-Vel**

*[Initial static, like sand falling on limestone. The recording equipment struggles to separate signal from the omnipresent water-song. Then, a voice emerges—not speaking above the background symphony but weaving through it, using the gaps between drops, the spaces between echoes.]*

Listen... no. Feel. The stone is forgetting how to sing.

I am Yulas-Who-Hears-Absence, born in the Chamber of Seventeen Sorrows where the water strikes limestone at the precise angle of lamentation. My mother's mother's mother was a Listener. My mother's mother went mad from silence and threw herself into the plateau winds. My mother was born with her ears sealed like a cave pearl, smooth and deaf, and so became the greatest Gardner our tier has known.

I was born wrong.

Where others hear the song, I hear the spaces between. Where others feel the eternal flow, I sense the stutters, the hesitations, the moments when the mountain holds its breath.

*[The sound of fingernails on wet stone, creating a rhythm that fights against the water-song]*

Forty seasons I have lived. Forty seasons I have tried to warn them. The Memory Keepers say I speak blasphemy—that the Flow has always been and always will be. The Tuners claim my chamber is mis-carved, that my acoustics are corrupted. Even other Listeners turn away when I pass in the narrow ways, pressing themselves against the walls as if my wrongness might be contagious.

But the stone knows. Oh, the stone remembers.

*[A shifting in the background—the water-song wavers almost imperceptibly]*

I have found the old chambers. The ones below the Dissolution Pools, below even where the eldest Singers go to join their voices to the mountain. Chambers where the limestone bears scars—long dry channels, wider than any we use now, carved for water that no longer comes.

The mountain is not eternal. The Flow is not forever.

In the deepest chamber—so far down that the water-song becomes a single pure note, where the thermal vents breathe like sleeping giants—I found them. Bones. But not dissolved, not given properly to the stone. Bones in a chamber where no water has flowed for... for...

*[A sound like weeping, but it might be water finding a new channel]*

The Memory Keepers say we have always been here. That the First Singers were born from the marriage of water and stone, that we arose like foam from the first drops to find these chambers. But these bones... they tell a different story.

Tools of metal, rusted to powder. Marks on the walls—writing, though I cannot read it. And in the center of the chamber, a basin carved so precisely that when I poured water into it—water I carried down in my own cupped hands—it sang a song I had never heard. A song of ending.

*[The voice becomes urgent, fighting against the eternal rhythm]*

I know what comes. I have seen it in the spaces between drops, heard it in the mountain's hesitation. The Flow is not eternal—it is a loop, a cycle so vast we have mistaken it for forever. The underground river that feeds us is not a river but a reservoir, and it is running low.

Not in my lifetime. Perhaps not for ten lifetimes or twenty. But someday—someday soon as the mountain counts time—the last drop will fall. The song will end. And we...

*[A terrible sound—silence creeping in around the edges of words]*

We who have built our souls around the song, we who breathe in rhythm with falling water, we whose hearts beat in time with dripping stone—what becomes of us in the quiet?

I have tried to prepare. In secret, in the chambers no one visits, I practice... I practice...

*[The longest pause yet—three full seconds of near-silence broken only by distant water]*

I practice remembering who I am without the song.

It is like trying to remember a dream while still dreaming. The silence terrifies me, but worse is what I find inside it. A person I do not recognize. Thoughts that move in straight lines instead of spirals. Words that mean only what they say instead of what they harmonize.

Yesterday, I taught a child to read the water-lack. She is young, her bones still soft, her mind still flexible. She might survive what comes. She might remember us to the quiet world, might carry some echo of what we were into the dry future.

Or perhaps I am mad. Perhaps my grandmother's silence-sickness runs in the blood, and I hear absence because absence is all I am.

*[The sound of movement—feet on stone, quick and desperate]*

But if I am right... if the bones speak true... if the stone remembers what we have forgotten...

Then someone must prepare. Someone must learn to live without the song.

I go now to the deep chambers. I will practice silence until it no longer breaks me. I will carve new channels—not for water, but for air, for wind, for the sounds that exist beyond our eternal symphony. I will teach the children, those few who can bear to listen, how to speak without harmonizing, how to think without rhythm, how to be without the Flow.

And when I am very brave—when the fear no longer paralyzes me—I will climb to the gorge's rim. I will step beyond the water-song's reach. And I will learn what we are when we are no longer Singers.

The others call me Drought Prophet, Silence Seeker, Yulas-Who-Kills-the-Song. They make gestures against the evil ear when I pass.

Let them.

Someone must remember how to be human when the music stops.

Someone must—

*[The transmission cuts to static, then to the pure sound of water on stone. When the voice returns, it is distant, as if speaking from very far away or very deep below]*

The children come in secret now. Seven, perhaps eight, who can hear what I hear. We practice words without water. We learn to love the harsh sounds—crack of stone on stone, whisper of breath without mist, the terrible clarity of unaccompanied voice.

We are the Unsingers. We are the Prepared.

And when the last drop falls, when the eternal song becomes memory, when the Singers stand paralyzed by silence—

We will teach them to speak.

*[The transmission fades, not to static but to something worse—the sound of water slowing, weakening, preparing to cease. Then, mercifully, the regular water-song returns, as if nothing had changed. As if it could flow forever.]*

*[But now you hear it too—the spaces between drops, growing longer.]*

*[End of transmission.]*
