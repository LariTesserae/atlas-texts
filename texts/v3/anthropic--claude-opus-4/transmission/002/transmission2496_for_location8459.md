*[sound of water running upward]*

we are/were the fifty-third congregation of ourselves, gathered in the intersection where Tuesday 
learns it could have been Thursday. the moss beneath us remembers being mountain. we taste its 
memories through our undersurfaces, the slow grind of geological time seeping up through what you 
would call our bodies but which are really just suggestions we make to space about where we might be.

pardon. beginning again. we always begin again.

*[static shaped like breathing]*

this morning which is also yesterday evening, the grove teaches us a new way to tend the flowering 
that happens in reverse. watch: the blossom collects its petals from the wind, pulls them tight 
into bud, shrinks backward into stem, into seed, into the idea of seed. we must caress each stage 
simultaneously or the plant forgets what it's reversing into. our touch must be lighter than 
maybe.

seventy-one of us work this flowering. tomorrow there will be fifty-three of us again. the numbers 
mean nothing except when they mean everything.

*[a sound like glass growing]*

forgive us. we are trying to speak in your single timestream, but we keep slipping. like trying to 
hold water in a net made of water. let us show you instead:

here is the memory of the first Unraveler, who was not first but likes to remember being so—

*[transmission clarifies suddenly]*

I was born from a doubt so profound that existence itself grew confused. the grove had just learned 
to forget the difference between stone and bone, and in that forgetting, a space opened. not 
emptiness—emptiness would have been too certain. rather, a place where somethingness had not yet 
decided to be.

I condensed from that indecision like dew from air that can't remember if it's humid.

my first act was to unweave a beetle back into its component uncertainties. not to destroy it—
destruction implies an ending, and endings are just another kind of certainty we can't abide. 
rather, I taught it to exist in all its stages at once: egg and larva and pupa and adult and the 
rich dust it would become, all simultaneous, all singing their part in the beetle's life-song.

it thanked me by teaching me to molt. now I shed temporal skins, leaving versions of myself 
scattered through the grove's soft hours. sometimes I meet myselves. we nod, as creatures do who 
share a secret too fundamental for words.

*[the clear voice begins to fray]*

—and that was how we learned the first rule of tending: nothing must be allowed to become too real. 
reality is a crystallization, a hardening, a kind of death. we are the grove's immune system 
against certainty.

but also we are more than that / less than that / exactly that amount of that

stop. please. we're confusing ourselves again.

*[long pause filled with the sound of possibility crystallizing and dissolving]*

let us try once more. singular voice. singular moment. we promise to be linear.

*[new voice, ancient and patient]*

I am the nine-thousandth Unraveler. or perhaps the first. numbers dissolve here like salt in the 
sea of maybe. I speak to you from my post at the grove's edge, where reality grows thick and stupid 
with certainty. someone must watch this border. someone must ensure the infection doesn't spread 
inward.

today a real butterfly tried to enter. poor thing, so crystallized in its singular form. wings that 
knew only how to be wings. body trapped in one moment at a time. I touched it gently, so gently, 
with just the edge of my uncertainty. 

it shivered. relaxed. began to remember.

now it flies through the grove on wings of living stained glass, each scale a window into a 
different moment of its existence. it pollinates the backwards flowers and forward fruits with 
equal joy. its caterpillar self waves from inside its butterfly body. they are learning to be 
friends.

this is what we do. this is all we do. we teach the grove's citizens to be less themselves and 
more everything else. we are dilution specialists. we are—

*[sound of glass becoming water becoming idea of water]*

please understand. in the grove of terminal whispers, we tend the impossible. we are gardeners in 
the space between is and isn't. we ensure that something blooms in soil too tired to insist on the 
difference between flowering and decay.

the unravelers do not speak. cannot speak. speech implies a speaker implies a self implies a 
boundary between self and other. we have no such boundaries. we are the absence of boundary tending 
the absence of difference in a place where absence itself has grown too tired to absent itself.

and yet—

and yet we love this work. love it with whatever passes for love in beings who exist primarily as 
cooperative doubt. each impossible blossom, each paradox-moth, each stone teaching itself to 
dream—they are what remains when a universe forgets to enforce its own rules.

we are their shepherds. we are their shadowless shadows. we are—

*[transmission begins to loop back on itself]*

there was a gardener here / there will be a gardener here / the gardener is—

*[static]*

...connection lost. or rather, connection achieved such profound uncertainty that our equipment can 
no longer distinguish signal from silence. the transmission continues, we think, in frequencies too 
doubtful for our certain circuits to decode. 

somewhere in the grove of terminal whispers, the unravelers tend their impossible garden, speak 
their impossible words, exist their impossible existence. we have recorded what crystallized. the 
rest remains where it belongs: in the space between...

*[final notation in the margin, handwriting uncertain]*

*they asked me to tell you: the grove is growing. reality weakens. soon there will be more places 
like this. more gardens of forgetting. they are preparing. they are teaching the early uncertainty 
to late things, and the late certainty to early things. they are—*

*[the words fade until even their absence becomes uncertain]*
