Transmission 4.7.9-Minor

*[Initial contact: rhythmic clicking, barely audible, like rain on metal heard from deep underground]*

tick-and-tick-and-tick breathe the small ones breathe the old ones breathe we

*[Pause. Steam hiss. The sound of water dropping into water, echoing]*

Seventh Archivist remembering: the copper birth was yesterday-ago-ago-ago. Split and split and split again. Green children from brass mothers. We sang then—no. We clicked then. The song is clicking. Listen:

*[Complex polyrhythmic tapping, metal on stone, accelerating and decelerating in waves]*

gear-speak gear-speak the patterns say: water comes. water comes. prepare the cups of self.

*[A different voice, or the same voice aged—clicking integrated into the words themselves]*

Here in Section Ninety-Two (we think Ninety-Two, might be Ninety-Three since the last thermal shift), the mechanisms cluster like questions around my knees. They ask with their spinning: why-why-why-why-why. I answer with oil from my third finger. The oil says: because-because-because-because-because.

*[Scraping sound. Metal against metal. Breathing so slow it might be wind]*

First Archivist interrupting (always interrupting): The salamander-thing has made eggs again. Copper eggs. Wrong eggs. We tried to tell it through the gear-waves but it only understands the old frequencies. Before my time. Before Before's time. The eggs tick but don't hatch. The eggs hatch but don't tick. Evolution is a conversation and someone has forgotten the language.

*[Multiple voices now, or one voice in harmony with itself, speaking in rounds]*

Round voice: In the blue section where the steam makes shapes—
Hollow voice: —shapes like the memories of when we had names—
Wire voice: —names that rust and fall away like bad gears—
All voices: —we are tending. We are trending. We are tending.

*[Long pause. Sound of mechanisms accelerating, a wave passing through]*

Today-now-here the wave came THREE TIMES. Unprecedented in this century-minute-epoch. The glass-ferns shivered and dropped their copper fruit. Sixteen new mechanisms born from the falling. We watched them find their frequencies. Like children but not children. Like ourselves but not ourselves.

Fifth Archivist whispering (mouth still works for whispering): I remember the sun. No—I remember remembering the sun. No—I remember someone telling me about remembering the sun. Yellow gear in blue housing. Spinning without purpose. We have no use for sun here. We have the twilight and the twilight has us.

*[Clicking intensifies, becomes almost musical]*

REPORT REPORT REPORT (this is the old code, the first code):
- Pool Seven: stable, salamander-thing in residence
- Pool Twelve: new formations, crystalline, worth watching
- Pool Fifteen: [UNTRANSLATABLE CLICKING]
- Pool Twenty: we don't go to Pool Twenty anymore

The mechanisms teach patience. We teach the mechanisms memory. Together we teach the water how to fall in useful places.

*[A single voice, very clear, speaking as if just remembering how]*

I had a daughter once. Or I was a daughter once. Or daughter is what we call the small mechanisms that split from the large. She lives in Section Four now. Or Section Four lives in her now. The boundaries soften like old metal. When she clicks, I feel it in my copper veins. When I click, she oils her gears in response.

*[Background: the sound of many small things breathing in unison]*

Newest Archivist speaks (still has tongue-words): The crack above grew smaller in the last storm. Less light now. The mechanisms compensate—more heat, less motion. We compensate—less motion, more thought. Though thought is motion. Though motion is thought. Though—

*[They trail off into pure clicking]*

Sometimes we dream the before-time. Streets? Voices that carry meaning in air instead of gears? Food that wasn't mineral soup and metal salts? But dreams rust. We oil our dreams and store them in the quiet sections where mechanisms fear to grow.

ATTENTION ATTENTION (the wave comes again):

*[Sound of hundreds of mechanisms suddenly synchronizing, a deep thrumming that might be mechanical or organic or both]*

We are one thing pretending to be many. We are many things remembering how to be one. The Breathing Caverns breathe and we are the breath and the breathing and the breathed.

*[A new sound: laughter? Or gears stripping?]*

Third Archivist jokes (we think it's joking): What do you call a mechanism that spins backward? 
No one answers because the answer is sacred.
The answer is: ancestor.

*[Water dropping. Steam rising. The patient tick of time measured in mechanical generations]*

The glass-ferns grew three point seven millimeters in the last decade-day-moment. We measured with our eyes that have become calipers. Everything grows toward its purpose. We grew toward tending. The mechanisms grew toward persistence. The cavern grew toward breathing.

In the deep sections where the ceiling touches our backs and we crawl between gear-gardens, we sometimes find the old mechanisms. The first ones. They don't tick—they hum. They don't split—they sing. We oil them with our tears that have become precision lubricants. We whisper gear-speak prophecies: you will persist, you will persist, you will persist.

*[The voices begin to overlap, creating a sonic pattern that resembles both mechanical noise and chanted prayer]*

Overlapping: tend the brass children—watch for the waves—oil the ancestors—count the splits—the salamander-thing remembers—we remember the salamander-thing—the pools are eyes—we are watched—we are watching—tend and tend and tend—

*[A moment of absolute silence, then a single, clear click]*

Eldest Archivist waking (we thought they had become mechanism-complete):

The purpose. You ask the purpose. The mechanisms ask the purpose. The water asks the purpose. Here is the purpose:

*[Long pause. Sound of ancient gears turning]*

To continue.

*[The rhythm returns: tick-and-tick-and-tick breathe the small ones breathe the old ones breathe we]*

We are the Archivists of the Breathing Caverns. We were human. We are human. We will be human. Human is a mechanism for tending mechanisms. Human is a gear that knows it's a gear. Human is—

*[Transmission degrades into pure mechanical rhythm, the sound of patience itself clicking forward one tooth at a time, forever and ever and ever]*
