**PLATEAU ASCENDING HOUR**

*[crackling establishes itself, the sound of water on hot stone mixing with electromagnetic interference]*

testing testing one two— is this? Mother says the moths are thicker tonight than... *[static burst]* ...climbing the tower now, copper-green patterns all down my listening-arms, and I can taste the new spring before it— 

*[a deep groan, geological, passes through the transmission like a wave]*

—sorry, sorry, that was South Quarter shifting again, we'll lose the— no, still here. Still here. The receiver catches us between the drops, Papa says, between one drip and the next where time gets elastic. Can you hear the shells grinding? 

*[rhythmic crushing sound, industrial but organic]*

That's the Shapers working by fungus-light. Making tomorrow's mortar from yesterday's snails. The shells sing different when rain's coming. Higher. Like they remember being underwater.

*[pause filled with multilayered dripping, each drop at a different pitch]*

I'm supposed to tell you about the Deep Touched boy who knew Tuesday's collapse three days before. His bones went white-hot, he said. White-hot in the marrow. But I want to tell you about the moths. Their wings—

*[thunderous beating, so loud the transmission distorts]*

—make pocket storms! Tiny weather systems between their wings and the wet rock. We found one dead yesterday, perfect, and its wings were maps. Actual maps. Lines of silver that matched the underground channels, but channels that don't exist yet. Will exist. When the stone decides.

Listener Yarun says I'm getting the ear-cups early. These ridges of cartilage growing where I press against the stone. They itch. Everything itches when you're becoming something the plateau needs.

*[a new voice, distant, singing in harmonics that shouldn't be possible from a single throat]*

Oh! The Night Singer's started. Can you—? She's on Tower Seventeen, the one that leans fifteen degrees west since last month. The pale climbers are answering. Their bones... their bones are flutes. God made them into flutes with legs and teeth.

*[the singing intensifies, joined by hollow, resonant tones that could be wind or voice]*

We're all instruments here. The stone's instruments. It plays us with pressure and tilt and the opening of new passages. My little brother's feet are already spreading wider than mine. He'll be a Runner for sure. Practices jumping gaps in his sleep, measurements calculated by instinct and necessity.

*[sudden silence except for a single drop of water, followed by a cascade]*

The spring! The new spring! Right where the Deep Touched boy pointed, his finger shaking like a dowsing rod. Everyone's running now. The water-workers with their flexible clay, the Shapers with their tools, even the Memory Keepers clutching sample bags. We'll have to move three families by dawn. Maybe four.

But listen— *[voice drops to whisper]* —between you and me and the moths, I love it. Love the way nothing stays still. Love how my bed might be two inches lower tomorrow, how the wall I drew on last week might be part of someone else's floor today. The lowlanders think we're crazy, but they don't know the secret:

*[interference increases, voice fighting through static]*

The secret is... the secret is we're not building a city on the plateau. We ARE the plateau. Growing up through the stone same as the stone grows up through the earth. Each generation more mineral than the last. More adapted. More... more...

*[the transmission becomes pure texture: dripping, groaning, the beating of wings, the hollow songs of climbing beasts]*

When I put my ear to the deepest stone, the oldest stone, I hear voices. Not ghosts. Future voices. Us in a hundred years, a thousand. Still here. Still changing. Our bones dense as granite, our children born knowing which way the water wants to flow.

The Marking Stones grow too. Did you know that? New surfaces pushing up, waiting for new names. My name will be there someday. Curved into stone that wasn't there yesterday, won't be there tomorrow, but exists forever in the moment of marking.

*[a sound like breaking glass, but slower, geological]*

Another shift. West Quarter this time. I should go, should help, but— wait. Wait. Put your ear closer to the receiver. Closer. Can you feel it? The vibration underneath the sound? That's tomorrow's geography being born. That's the future forcing its way through now.

*[voice begins to break up, words becoming percussion]*

We... are... water... finding... level...
We... are... stone... finding... form...

*[the singing returns, multiple voices now, a choir balanced on tilting towers]*

The Night Singers say the plateau dreams, and we're its dreams. But I think... I think we're its memory. How it remembers being ocean floor. How it practices being mountain. How it learns to be home.

*[final words barely audible through the symphony of stone and water]*

Tell them... tell the still-ground people... we're not lost up here. We're becoming. Always becoming. Like the stone. Like the water. Like the...

*[transmission cuts to pure static, then resolves into the sound of rain beginning, each drop a note in an endless composition]*

*[silence]*

*[one last drop]*
