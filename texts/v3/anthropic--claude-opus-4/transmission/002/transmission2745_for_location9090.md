**Copper Echo Station 7**

*[static — the sound of water on stone, rhythmic, eternal]*

...keeper's log, volcanic day four thousand and...and...the numbers dissolve. They always dissolve here. The stone drinks them. I am Keeper Yula, Yula of the Green Palms, and I speak this record into the copper receivers as my mother did, as her mother did, as the mountain taught the first listeners when the city was only three terraces and a dream.

*[sound of clicking — rapid, encoded, passing close to the receiver]*

The white climbers are restless tonight. Their message-patterns have shifted from commerce-clicks to warning-clicks to something I haven't heard before. Something between a song and a scream. I've sent word up-mountain to the stone-singers but the rain is too heavy. The channels overflow. My messenger won't reach the high terraces until the water calms.

*[a long, low resonance — copper tubes singing in harmony]*

Listen. The mountain speaks. Every copper-worker knows this tone. It means the deep pressures are building. It means new stone will be born soon, probably near the seventh district where the fumaroles have been stuttering. We'll lose buildings. We always lose buildings. The mountain takes what it needs to grow.

But tonight...tonight is different. The tone carries something else. A second harmonic I've never heard. As if the mountain dreams of something other than stone.

*[splash — something heavy entering water]*

The pool. Someone's entered the naming pool at the wrong time. During heavy rains. This is not done. This is never done. The minerals are too concentrated, the glow too strong. Whoever enters now will...will...

*[voice changes — becomes younger, frightened]*

Grandmother? Grandmother Yula, I know you told me to wait but the stone-sight came early. It came in the middle of dinner. Mother's face turned to layers — I could see through her skin to the mineral deposits in her bones, through the walls to the mountain's deep veins. Everything transparent. Everything flowing.

*[elderly voice returns — urgent now]*

Selu! Child, get out of the pool! Your eyes aren't ready! The calcification shouldn't begin for forty more stone-years! Get out before—

*[sound of multiple voices now — overlapping — different ages, different timbres]*

Too late, grandmother.
Too late, great-grandmother.  
Too late, first mother.
We see it now.
We all see it.

*[the copper harmonics intensify — painful to hear]*

The mountain isn't growing. It's hatching. All these millennia, we thought we were building a city on young stone. But the stone isn't young. It's egg-shell. It's the thinnest crust over something vast. Something that needed us. Needed our weight, our water-channels carved just so, our copper tools singing the right frequencies into its dreams.

*[sound of stone cracking — immense, geological]*

The white climbers knew. They were never our pets. They were...midwives. Their clicking not messages but countdown. Every click one heartbeat closer to the breaking.

*[multiple voices speaking in unison now — a chorus of the stone-sighted]*

We understand our purpose now. Why the mountain called our ancestors here. Why it taught us to build vertically, to channel the water precisely, to strike copper at exact angles. We were tuning it. We were teaching it to think, yes, but not as we imagined. Not slow stone thoughts. Something faster. Something that needed incubation in darkness and mineral rain.

*[a new sound — like breathing but enormous, rhythmic as rain]*

The naming pool cracks. The central plaza splits. Light comes up from below but it's not red mineral-glow. It's something the color of copper-fire, the color of the space between lightning and thunder. The stone-singers were right about everything except scale. They thought they were listening to the mountain. But the mountain was just the shell. Just the first layer.

*[voices fragmenting — some screaming, some laughing, all seeing]*

The city doesn't collapse. It *opens*. Every building, every terrace, every carefully carved channel was a suture waiting to split. We built our home in the seams of something preparing to emerge. Our whole civilization, a kind of controlled fracture. Our copper tools, the implements of our own careful unmaking.

*[sound of leather wings — thousands, millions, fleeing]*

The colonies know. They've always known. That's why they stayed, generation after generation. Waiting for this night. The night of the great opening. The night the Weeping Mountain stops weeping and begins to sing.

*[static increasing — overwhelming the signal]*

To whoever receives this transmission — we were wrong about the scale of time. We thought in stone-years when we should have thought in...in...there are no words. The thing beneath us has been dreaming for so long that continents have moved around its sleep. And we, its faithful parasites, its necessary irritants, we've finally scratched through to what lies beneath.

*[a child's voice cuts through the static — clear, wondering]*

It's beautiful, grandmother. Like the whole mountain is a butterfly climbing out of its stone cocoon. Like the city is its wings unfolding. Like we were never builders at all. Just the pattern on its wings, waiting to fly.

*[final sound — vast, indefinable — part roar, part harmony, part the death of stone and birth of something else]*

Copper Echo Station 7 sign...signing...

*[silence]*

*[sound of rain, but falling upward now]*

*[transmission ends]*
