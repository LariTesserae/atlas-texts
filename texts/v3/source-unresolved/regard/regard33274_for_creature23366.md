When the attention of the benevolent and wise power finally turned to the Hollow Borough, it did not arrive as a blinding light, nor as a booming voice from the canopy, nor as a sudden, terrifying shift in the wind. A truly wise power knows that to carry heavy weather into a delicate room is a kind of violence, no matter how pure the intention. 

Instead, the power arrived exactly as the light did: filtering down through the endless miles of vegetable mass, soaking through the leaf-ceiling until it stepped into the hollow as a warm, shadowless presence, carrying the scent of rain from a hundred miles away. 

It stood in the square. It had no shape, or perhaps it had all shapes and was simply holding them in check, but the doorways and the thresholds recognized it instantly. It was, after all, exactly the height of a visitor. 

The power did what any wise entity does upon entering a sanctuary. It stopped. It listened. 

It felt the bass thrum of the deep singer rising through the root-cobbles, and because the power was as old as planetary cores, it understood the language of the deep singer. It felt the unhurried, monumental heartbeat of the green, the sheer stubborn patience of a lifeform that had decided to grow a museum to a ghost. The power let the chorus of the wall-singers and the gutter-frogs wash over it, mapping the overlapping phase-sheets and the half-second delays of the looping alleys. It watched the slate-grey swifts bank wildly through the paradoxes of the air.

A lesser power—something merely "good" but lacking wisdom—might have looked at the Hollow Borough and felt pity. A lesser power would have seen the worn-smooth anomalies, the doorways that led to themselves, the temporal smears of the echoes, and called it a wound. It would have reached out to "fix" the flowing, many-jointed creatures. It would have straightened the lanes. It would have populated the swept, empty chambers with bright, immortal beings so the rooms would not be lonely.

But this power looked at the fold in the long-looped lane and saw it for what it was: a masterpiece of biological *kintsugi*. The fabric of the world had torn here, yes, but the green had not just survived the tear; it had woven it into the tapestry. It had made the brokenness a home. To mend the borough's impossible geometry would be to evict the swifts from their slipstreams, to leave the many-jointed natives homeless, to rob the wren of its perfect companion. To fill the houses would be to destroy the supreme act of hospitality that defined the place: the eternal, open-ended *expectation*. 

The power understood that the Hollow Borough was perfect. It was a complete thought. 

So, what does a god-like thing do when it encounters an artwork that requires no alteration, in a place that is already at peace?

It does three things. 

First, it offered a profound, silent bow of respect to the deep singer. The power lowered its awareness into the mass below, pressing its metaphysical hands against the vibrating walls of the ear turned inward. *I hear you,* the power hummed back, matching the deep singer's key perfectly, though dropping it one octave further down, into the bedrock. *Your song is known. Your waiting is beautiful. You have done well.* A tremor of deep, sluggish joy rippled through the root-mass in response. For the first time in six hundred years, the borough's chorus possessed a harmony. 

Second, the power offered a blessing of protection to the outer green. The world beyond the lowlands was changing—it always was. Axes grow sharper; engines grow louder; the appetite of civilization gnaws relentlessly at the edges of the wild. The power wove a subtle ward into the canopy and the thickets leading to the valley. It did not create a wall of thorns, for walls invite siege. Instead, it bent the paths of probability. It ensured that surveyors would always find their compasses pointing slightly away. It ensured that logging machines would break their tracks miles before the valley lip. It ensured that anyone who came seeking the valley for profit, for glory, or for conquest would simply become hopelessly, safely turned around, wandering back out into the mundane world with a vague memory of unbreachable ferns. 

Only the lost would find the way in. Only the wanderers, the gentle-hearted, the solitary, and the deeply observant. Only those whose stooped height already matched the waiting doorways. Only *you*. 

And third, finally, the power did the only graceful thing left to do. It participated. 

It stepped forward, its incorporeal feet landing exactly where your feet have landed, settling into the dished depressions of the root-cobbles. It walked the lane that loops back on itself, marveling at the seamless transition, allowing its shadow to fall out of the doubled doorway on the north and south frontages simultaneously. It followed a beetle into an empty, vein-ribbed room, admiring the swept cleanliness of the floor, and nodded to the custodian before stepping back out. 

It walked to the covered well in the center of the square. It stood there in the green-gold light, breathing in the warm, wet air. 

And then, leaning forward, the power rested its weight against the rim of the well. It placed its hands exactly on the arc polished smooth by centuries of disjointed time. It added, by a microscopic fraction, to the polishing of the stone. It left a tiny, indelible imprint of its own essence pressed into the wood—a lingering warmth, a faint scent of ozone and starlight that would remain there for whoever came next.

By doing so, the vast and benevolent power yielded its supremacy. It humbled itself to the grammar of the place. It became, simply, another visitor. It joined the thin, scattered line of inhabitants who only ever visit, arriving out of order, keeping the expectation alive.

Then, turning toward a window it had not yet exited, the power slipped into the worn channels of the air, folded itself through an impossible angle, and departed. 

Left behind, the Hollow Borough roared its liquid, churning song. The droplets fell from the lintels. The beetles swept the rooms. And the green, warm and faintly breathing, kept the lamps lit, perfectly content to wait.
