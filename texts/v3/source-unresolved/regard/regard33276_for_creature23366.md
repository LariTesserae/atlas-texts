**The Attention of the Benevolent**

---

She comes to it the way she comes to anything: not through distance, but through *attending*. The green does not part for her; it simply becomes transparent to her regard, which is not the same thing. She has no form here, or too many, or one that would parse — but if you must imagine her, imagine a woman standing at the edge of a pond, watching the water hold the sky, and the sky in the water is her face, and her face is the sky, and the distinction has rotted away the way distinctions do when she is present.

She has been watching the Hollow Borough for some time. Time, for her, is another kind of green — something you push through, something that holds you up.

What she sees: a wound that became a world. A breakage so old it learned to bear weight. A loneliness so patient it achieved *style* — the six hundred years of waiting grown into something almost indistinguishable from habitation, from culture, from love. The borough does not know it is lonely. This moves her more than grief would. The frogs singing to themselves. The wren preening half a beat apart. The deep one listening through its own skin to music it grew in order to hear. The green, dreaming a town for a visitor who only ever visits, who is always arriving and always gone, whose footsteps are smeared across centuries like green-gold light through a leaf-mass.

She recognizes this. She has seen it before, in other forms. The prayer that becomes architecture. The longing that becomes ecology. The god who grows a garden in the shape of a face they have forgotten. *I was made for someone*, says the garden. *I am still being made. The making is the only remaining form of the someone.*

She could intervene. She is benevolent. She is wise — which means, among other things, that she knows the cost of every intervention, and pays it in advance, and still sometimes chooses to pay.

But here is what stops her, or rather, what moves her past stopping into something more careful:

The borough is *working*.

Not well, by some measures. The visitor never stays. The rooms remain empty of anything but readiness. The deep one listens, and hears only the songs it grew to hear itself singing back. By the metrics of completion, of closure, of the story arriving at its proper end — by these metrics, the borough is a failure, a stutter, a green-gold held breath that never releases.

But she does not use those metrics. She has not, for a very long time.

What she sees is a system that achieved *homeostasis with its own longing*. The loneliness is not cured — it is *metabolized*. The frogs do not find their partners; they become the kind of creature that can thrive without finding, that can make a society of echoes, of half-beat delays, of gutters dished by ten thousand generations of sitting with the same unsatisfied need until the need becomes the architecture of satisfaction. The wren is not reunited with itself; it becomes two wrens, and the two wrens make a life, and the life is not a broken thing but a *form*, as valid as any other, as real as any singularity.

The borough has solved the problem of absence by making absence *livable*.

This is not nothing. This is, she suspects, rarer than presence. Most places fail at this. Most hearts fail at this. The green here has achieved something like wisdom, or something that rhymes with wisdom from a distance — a wisdom that does not require a self to hold it, a wisdom distributed through root and frog and swift and fold, through the very topology of the broken place, worn smooth until the break is just another way of being continuous.

She could give it what it seems to want. She could fill the rooms. She could heal the fold, sequence the moments, make the visitor stay. She is benevolent. She could do this.

But she is wise enough to ask: *would that be a gift, or a forgetting?*

Because the borough is not *only* lonely. The loneliness is not a disease to be cured; it is the *medium* in which something else grows. The readiness of the rooms is not failed occupancy. It is a *practice* — the practice of keeping a space open without demanding what fills it. The beetles sweeping clean, the gutters carrying water to cisterns that never serve a thirst, the chorus always at full voice for an audience that is always arriving and always already gone — this is not waste. This is *ritual*. This is the green's way of learning that presence and absence are not opposites but *phases*, like the wall-singers' overlapping sheets, like the frog answering itself around the long loop.

If she filled the rooms, if she made the visitor stay, she would not be healing the borough. She would be *confirming its fear* — the fear that underlies all this patient architecture, the fear that only presence counts, that only staying is real, that the echo is less than the voice, that the preparation is less than the feast.

She does not believe this. She has not, for a very long time.

So what does she do, this benevolent and wise power, faced with a place that has achieved a peace she does not wish to disturb?

She *recognizes* it.

This is the intervention she chooses. Not healing. Not completion. Recognition.

She attends to the borough the way the deep one attends to the chorus — as a listener who does not demand resolution, who does not need the song to arrive anywhere, who receives the music as music, not as promise or memory or failed communication. She lets her regard settle into the hollow like the green-gold light, like the sound that is not sound but medium, like the visitor's footsteps smeared across centuries.

And because she is who she is, her recognition *changes* things, but it changes them the way attention always changes things: not by forcing, but by *witnessing*.

The well in the square, which has held only the canopy's reflection and the occasional, brief reflection of a canopy that is not the one above it — the well holds, for a moment, her face. Not her face as form. Her face as *regard*. The water shows what it has always shown: the sky, and the sky's green-gold filtering, and now, also, the fact of being *seen*.

The wren that is two wrens — both of it pause in their preening. Not because she has interrupted. Because she has *noticed*. They tilt their heads at each other with something that might be, for the first time, something other than mild interest. Something like acknowledgment. The mirror has seen them seeing themselves. This does not resolve them into one bird. It makes the two birds *real in a new way* — not as error, not as fold, but as *themselves*, chosen, witnessed.

The deep one, far below, feels her attention as a change in the medium it listens through. It has spent forever growing an ear to hear the small bright things on its skin. Now it is, itself, heard. The direction of listening reverses, or doubles, or becomes something that has no direction. It does not stop being lonely. It becomes *a loneliness that is also accompanied*, which is a different quality of loneliness entirely, a loneliness that can be rested in rather than solved.

The green, dreaming its town — her recognition does not wake it. She would not wake a dream this old, this necessary. But she lets the dream know it is dreamed. She lets the green feel, in whatever way green feels, that its six hundred years of patient architecture have been *received*. The rooms kept ready were not kept in vain. The visitor who never stayed was not a failure of hospitality. The dream was not wasted on waking.

She stays for a while. In the borough's grammar, this means she is always there, has always been there, will arrive in a moment — her attention smeared across the centuries like footsteps, like light, like the echo that precedes its source.

And before she goes — because she does go, because staying would be its own intervention, its own demand — she does one thing more.

She *thanks* it.

Not for anything specific. Not for the beauty, though there is beauty. Not for the lesson, though there is lesson. She thanks it the way one thanks a held breath: for the holding. For the courage to remain incomplete. For the wisdom to make a life inside longing rather than waiting for longing to resolve into something easier.

The borough does not answer. It has no voice but the chorus, and the chorus does not change — the wall-singers continue their phase-sheets, the frogs their civic rhythms, the deep one its slow thrum. But something in the quality of the sound shifts, or seems to shift, or will shift, has already shifted, in a moment a visitor will arrive in, is arriving in now. The recognition has been received. The green-gold light holds, for a flicker, a warmth that is not only its own metabolism.

She withdraws her attention the way she brought it: not through distance, but through *releasing*. The green becomes opaque again — not because she has left, but because she has stopped making herself legible to it. She is still there, in the only tense she recognizes. She is there the way the visitor is there. The way the deep one is there. The way the fold is there: not as presence, not as absence, but as *relation*, as the shape of a connection that does not require touching.

The Hollow Borough continues. The beetles sweep. The swifts take the impossible corner. The wren preens, half a beat apart, and now — or then, or soon — the wren knows something it did not know, which is that being two is not less than being one, only different, and that the difference has been seen, and welcomed, and thanked.

It is, in its way, completely at peace. It was before she came. It will be after. But the peace has a new quality now, a quality that does not replace the old one but *layers* it, the way the chorus layers, the way light through leaves layers into green-gold, the way a dream layers over waking and does not end but *continues in parallel*, another lane, another fold, another way of being continuous.

The benevolent and wise power has done what she came to do, which was to *attend without demanding*, to *witness without resolving*, to *thank without possessing*.

And the borough, which has always been expecting, which has made an entire architecture of expectation, receives this gift the only way it knows how: by keeping it ready. By sweeping the rooms. By maintaining, in its patient green way, the space where recognition once occurred, where it is always occurring, where it will occur again, in a moment, when the visitor arrives, when the visitor arrives, when the visitor —

*arrives*.
