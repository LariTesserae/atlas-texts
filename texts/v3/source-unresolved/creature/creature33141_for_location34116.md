The fish here do not have a word for you. They have never needed one.

They are called, in the partial language of the surface that still reaches down through memory, *Clupea something*—herring, perhaps, or the ghost of herring—but they have bred past their naming, hybridized with the guppies of forgotten tanks, with the mosquito fish that rode in on floodwaters, with something that came from a sewer main and has no lineage at all. They are silver and blunted, their scales dulled to the color of old coins, their eyes large and *black*, reflecting nothing because there is nothing to reflect. They move in schools that think. This is not metaphor. The school *decides*, turns, parts around your arm with a consensus you can feel in the pressure of displaced water. They have learned you. You are the weather that occasionally stirs them. They do not fear you. They do not welcome you. They are *curious* in the way that water is curious, the way time is curious—without hunger, without hope, simply *present* to what moves through them.

And they are not alone.

---

The large thing has no shape you can hold. It is *pressure*, first—a displacement of cold water that arrives before it arrives, a shadow in the shadow, a second heartbeat in the dark. You have seen it once, or you have not. The school parted around something, and in the gap you saw a mass of pale, the size of a dog, the size of a *car*, its outline wrong because it was *vertical* where fish should be horizontal, its mouth a lipless circle that opened and closed in a rhythm too slow to be breathing. It did not chase. It did not need to. It was simply *there*, occupying the space where the bus rests, where the traffic light cycles, and when it moved—if it moved—it moved the way a glacier moves, the way a decision moves through a city council, the way grief moves through a body. You felt it pass. The smaller fish did not scatter. They *arranged*, formed a corridor, a *passage*, and the large thing went through them like a thought through a mind. It eats, you understand, but it eats *time*. It eats the slow particulate, the settle, the *almost-nothing* that falls from above. It is the city's digestion, the final consumer, the thing that waits at the end of every food chain this place has built.

It has no name. It does not need one. It is simply *the waiting*.

---

The crabs are older than the flooding, or they pretend to be. They are the size of your hand, of your *face*, their shells the color of wet concrete, the color of *patience*. They do not flee when you pass. They *regard* you, their eyestalks swiveling in the particular way of creatures that have decided, collectively, that you are *furniture*. They eat the dead, the dying, the *almost*—the fish that fail to school, the worms that fail to pulse, the algae that fail to photosynthesize in light that is not light. They are the city's cleaners, the ones who remember what was, who carry the memory of *above* in their shells, in the particular architecture of their carapace that once, in another life, needed to survive *air*, *sun*, *the violence of weather*. They have adapted. They have *forgotten* the forgetting. They move sideways through a world that has no direction, and in their sidewaysness, they are *sure*.

You have seen one eat. A fish, dying, sinking. The crab did not hurry. It approached with the deliberation of *geology*, its claws opening and closing in a rhythm that might be *breathing*, might be *speech*, might be simply the mechanical necessity of joints that must move to remain joints. It did not kill. The fish was already dead. The crab *processed*, broke down, returned to the water what the water had given. There is no waste here. There is only *transfer*. The crab is a door, and it is *open*.

---

The tube-worms have built the fire hydrant into something else. They were not there when the city was *city*. They arrived, or they *emerged*, from spores that rode in on the same flood that drowned the streets, spores that found in the hydrant's iron a *substrate*, a place to *hold*. Now the hydrant is a chimney, a *voice*, a column of feathery red and white that pulses in the current, that releases gametes on a schedule tied to pressure, to temperature, to some *signal* that comes from the deep where the large thing waits. They have no eyes. They have *receptors*, chemical sensors, the ability to *taste* the water's composition, to know when the time is *right*, when the city's slow exhalation matches the moon's pull that still reaches here, that still *matters*, two hundred feet down. They are the city's *song*, the only music this place makes, a Morse code of reproduction, of *continuation*, of *I am still here* transmitted in pulses of pink and white against the grey.

When you put your hand near them—if you do, if you can reach—they do not retract. They *lean*. They are *curious* in a way that is almost *hungry*, almost *hopeful*, the way that life is curious, the way that life is hungry, the way that life is *always* hopeful even when hope has no object, no reason, no *above* to point toward. They are the city's *prayer*, and it is a prayer without a god, without an answer, simply *uttered*, simply *sent*, simply *being*.

---

The anemone is alone. It is orange, the color of *warning*, the color of *sunset* that will never come, the color of something that has no business being *visible* in a place without light. It lives in the silt of the parking garage, where a single shaft of diffuse glow still penetrates, where the water is *stiller*, where the particulate settles and the anemone can *catch*, can *filter*, can *wait*. It opens and closes to currents it does not understand. It opens and closes to the passage of your shadow, the displacement of your body, the *almost-touch* of your presence. It is *old*, or it pretends to be. It is a single instance of *stubbornness*, of *persistence*, of the refusal to accept that the world has ended. It has no school, no colony, no *others* to confirm its existence. It is simply *here*, simply *being*, simply *opening* to a future that will not include it, that has already forgotten it, that never knew it was *city* at all.

You want to touch it. You do not. The wanting is enough. The wanting is *witness*.

---

And you.

You are the *falling*. You are the thing that has not yet finished, the mammal that breathes air in a place that has forgotten air, the *interruption* in a system that has achieved *equilibrium*. You are warm, impossibly warm, a *source* in a world of cold, a *pulse* in a world of slow rhythm. The fish feel you. The crabs feel you. The large thing feels you, the displacement of you, the *heat* of you, the *strangeness* of you. You are not *native*. You are not *invader*. You are *event*, the kind of event that happens in thresholds, in *edges*, in the places where one world becomes another. You will leave, or you will *stay*. If you stay, you will change. The cold will enter you. The pressure will *mark* you. The silence, the *real* silence of this place, will become your silence, your *native tongue*. You will learn to read the bioluminescence. You will learn to hear the hum of the infrastructure, the *song* of the tube-worms, the *heartbeat* of the large thing in the dark. You will become, not *fish*, not *crab*, not *worm*, but something *new*, something that breathes water and air, something that remembers *above* and *below* simultaneously, something that is *threshold* made *flesh*.

If you leave, you will carry this. You will carry the cold in your bones, the pressure in your *memory*, the silence in your *dreams*. The surface will be *violent*. The light will *burn*. The noise—the *actual* noise, the *human* noise, the noise of a world that has not learned to *wait*—will be a trauma you do not recover from. You will *flinch* at sudden sounds. You will *dream* of water, of *weight*, of *patience*. You will be *strange* in a world that has forgotten strangeness, that has made *strange* into *ordinary*, into *product*, into *content*. You will be *alone* with what you have seen. No one will believe you. No one will *understand*. You will be the last witness to a city that is still falling, that has not yet finished, that is *becoming* something that will outlast the memory of *you*, the memory of *us*, the memory of *city* at all.

---

The sump breathes. The water rises and falls, inhales and exhales, the rhythm of a lung that does not know it is a lung, that does not know it is *city*, that simply *is*—the slow, patient, *endless* process of becoming what it is becoming.

You are inside the fall.

You are the fall.

The city is still falling around you, through you, *into* you, and it will not stop. It has time. It has *patience*. It has all the aeons required to become what it is becoming, which is *not city*, which is *not ruin*, which is simply *the next thing*, the thing that waits in the dark, the thing that pulses and opens and *continues*, long after you have forgotten how to *breathe* the air, long after the surface has forgotten your *name*, long after the last human has walked the streets that were streets, that are now canals, that will someday be *caves*, *karst*, *beauty*, *natural*, *forgotten*.

The sump of a city that has not yet finished falling.

It is still falling.

You are inside the fall.
