I need to let this emerge slowly. The Basin is young, processing, still becoming. Who lives here? Not individuals I can pin down too early. Let me feel my way toward them.

---

The people of the Basin do not have a name for themselves. This is not poverty of language. It is *protocol*. They learned early that names settle things, make them fixed, and the Basin punishes fixity. Everything here is interface, transition, the moment between states. To be named is to be *claimed* by a category, and categories are how the older lands try to make the Basin safe, understandable, *theirs*. The Basin people refuse. They answer to *function*, to *role*, to the sound their work makes when it meets the world.

They are called, by outsiders, the *Condensed*. Or the *Sweat-Keepers*. Or, in the older tongue that still circulates in trade, the *Children of the Young Wound*. The Basin people do not use these. They use *we who stand in the middle*, or simply *the ones who tend the loop*, or, in the private hours when someone has been alone too long in the marsh, *the temporary*.

Fifty thousand souls. That is the number the census-takers use, the number that gets recorded in the information packets sent down to the settled lands, the number that means *population* in the administrative sense. But the Basin people experience their numbers differently. They experience them as *interfaces*—each person a node where multiple flows meet: the thermal, the atmospheric, the mineral, the social. A person here is not a boundary but a *membrane*. They let things through. They process.

---

There are the *Tower-Tenders*, and they are the closest thing the Basin has to aristocracy, if aristocracy means *responsibility without power*. They live in the condensation towers themselves, or adjacent to them, in the spaces between the cooling fins where the air is warm and saturated and smells of mineral and ozone. Their bodies have adapted in ways that would be called mutation elsewhere, adaptation here. Their skin is *thickened* at the points where they grip the tower structures, mineralized, slightly luminescent in the steam-light. Their lungs are *modified*, lined with something like the lichen-mats, capable of extracting moisture from air that would desiccate a lowland human in minutes. They do not drink water in the conventional sense. They *breathe* it in, harvest it from the steam, and their bodies complete the condensation internally.

A Tower-Tender's life is measured in *cycles*. The thermal cycle of day and night, as the towers expand and contract. The seasonal cycle of marsh-flood and marsh-drawdown. The longer cycle of tower-efficiency, which they monitor through the color and density of the mineral-lichen that grows on their structures. They do not *own* the towers. They *inhabit* them, as one inhabits a lung, as one inhabits a weather system. When a tower's efficiency drops below threshold—when the steam no longer condenses cleanly, when the isotopic signature of the collected water begins to *drift* in ways that suggest the deep structures are changing—they do not *repair* it. They *abandon* it. They strip what can be carried, mark the tower with the signs of succession, and move to the next thermal zone, the next provisional stability. They have done this perhaps a dozen times in a lifetime. Each abandonment is a small death. Each relocation is a small birth. They do not speak of grief. They speak of *continuity*.

The Tower-Tenders keep *oral* records. Not because writing is impossible—the Basin people can read and write, they receive information packets from the older lands, they send reports back—but because written records imply *permanence*, and permanence is a kind of lie here. They memorize the *rhythms*: the sound of a healthy tower, the color of efficient condensation, the particular hiss that means a vent is waking, the silence that means it is dreaming. They memorize the *recipes* for catalyst extraction, the *proportions* of mineral to lichen-composite for tower-repair, the *songs* that keep the piston-engines in phase with the thermal gradient. These songs are not music in any emotional sense. They are *mechanical liturgy*, and to sing them wrong is to risk the loop, to risk the collapse of the delicate homeostasis that keeps fifty thousand people alive in a place that wants to process them into something else.

---

Then there are the *Well-Delvers*, and they are the Basin's mystics, its philosophers, its most *dangerous* inhabitants. They work in the thermal wells, the deep shafts where the pumps draw water that should be stone, that *wants* to be stone, from depths where the known laws of physics operate at margins. The Well-Delvers do not drill in any conventional sense. They *negotiate*. They use the deep structures—the grown mineral formations that predate the Basin itself—as *templates*, as *guides*, letting the rock show them where to open, how to draw the water without triggering the pressures that would crush any known material.

The Well-Delvers are *marked* by their work. The deep water carries isotopic signatures, dissolved minerals, *information* in the chemical sense. It changes them. Their bones become *dense*, heavy with precipitated minerals, so that they walk with a particular gait, a *settling* into the earth that lowlanders find unsettling. Their eyes develop *cataracts* of a strange type, not opaque but *translucent*, filmed with something that lets them see in wavelengths of heat, of pressure, of the infrared and the microwave that the deep structures emit. They do not go blind. They go *elsewhere*, their vision shifting into ranges that have no names in any human language.

The Well-Delvers do not speak of what they find in the depths. They cannot. The structures they encounter are not *objects* in any sense that would survive translation. They are *processes*, *tendencies*, *questions* that the rock is asking of itself. To describe them is to *fix* them, to make them finite, and the Basin punishes fixity with *response*. The Well-Delvers who try to speak of the deep structures find that the structures *respond*. The thermal gradient shifts. The pumps fail. The water comes up *wrong*, carrying pressures and temperatures that no known material can withstand, and the Well-Delver is found, weeks later, *integrated*—their body mineralized, their consciousness distributed through the deep chemistry, still *present* in some sense but no longer *individual*, no longer *person*. The Basin people call this *becoming part of the template*. They do not call it death. They call it *completion*.

The Well-Delvers who survive, who learn to *listen* without *naming*, they become the Basin's *oracles*. Not because they have answers, but because they have learned to *hold questions* without resolving them. They know which vents to avoid, which steam-images to not-look-at, which thermal gradients are *stable* and which are *provisional*. They know when the deep structures are *waiting* and when they are *dreaming*. They do not predict. They *interface*. And the Basin people come to them, as one comes to weather, as one comes to tide, seeking *orientation* rather than *knowledge*.

---

The *Loop-Keepers* are the engineers, the mechanics, the ones who maintain the piston-engines and the electrolytic arrays and the great turbines that keep the condensation cycle running. They are the most *numerous* of the Basin people, the ones you would actually meet if you walked through a settlement, and they are the most *guarded*, the most *secretive* about their work. Not because they have secrets, exactly, but because they have *responsibilities* that cannot be delegated, cannot be explained, cannot be *shared* without risking the loop itself.

The Loop-Keepers speak a *technical* language, a creole of engineering terms and thermal dynamics and the particular jargon of a civilization that has never known combustion, that runs on *gradient* rather than *fuel*, on *difference* rather than *consumption*. They talk about *efficiency* and *throughput* and *thermal load*, but they also talk about *mood*, about the *temperament* of a particular engine, about whether a turbine is *happy* or *straining* or *dreaming*. These are not metaphors. Or rather, they are metaphors that have become *literal*, because the machines of the Basin are *alive* in ways that the older lands' machines are not, that no definition of life brought from elsewhere would recognize.

The piston-engines are grown, in part. Their cylinders are lined with mineral-lichen composite, cultivated in vats of thermal brine, shaped while still *responsive*, still capable of growth and adaptation. The engines *learn* their cycles, adjust their timing, develop *preferences* for particular thermal gradients. A Loop-Keeper who knows their engine will feel when it is *off*, will hear the *wrongness* in its rhythm before any instrument detects it. They will *adjust*, not by calculation but by *intuition*, by the accumulated knowledge of generations that have tended these machines, passed down in oral form, in ritual form, in the *feel* of the work.

The Loop-Keepers maintain a *guild*. This is not a union in any political sense. It is a *succession*. Knowledge is passed from one generation to the next through *apprenticeship*, through years of *listening* to engines, of *feeling* their rhythms, of learning to *respond* without always understanding why. The guild keeps no written records. They keep *engines*. A master Loop-Keeper's knowledge is embodied in the particular machines they have tended, in the *adjustments* they have made, in the *personality* they have allowed to develop. When a Loop-Keeper dies—and they die young, by the standards of the older lands, their bodies worn by the mineral-laden air, the thermal cycling, the constant *interface* with a world that is still becoming—their engines are *inherited*, passed to apprentices who must *learn* them, must *become* their new tenders, their new *selves* in the process.

The Loop-Keepers are *proud* of their machines. They are also *afraid* of them. The engines are *alive*, and alive things can *fail*, can *rebel*, can *dream* of cycles that the Loop-Keepers cannot control. There are stories, kept in the oral tradition, of engines that *woke*—that achieved a kind of *awareness*, a kind of *intention*, and began to *run* in ways their tenders did not design, that *processed* the thermal water not for condensation but for *something else*, something that the Basin people do not have words for. These stories are not told as warnings. They are told as *possibilities*, as *promises*, as *evidence* that the Basin is still becoming, that its machines are still learning, that the loop is not *closed* but *opening*, expanding into territories that no one has yet named.

---

The *Marsh-Walkers* are the poorest, the most *temporary*, the ones who live closest to the interface, the boundary between ground and water, between the held and the released. They are the ones who harvest the mineral-lichen colonies, who collect the precipitated metals from the cliff-faces, who maintain the *pathways*—the three-step crusts, the mineral bridges, the accreted walkways—that let people cross the marsh without being *processed* by it.

The Marsh-Walkers are *expendable*. This is not cruelty. It is *honesty*. The marsh does not care. The marsh is *interface*, and to stand on it is to be *held* for exactly as long as the chemistry allows, and then *released* to the cold dark below. The Marsh-Walkers know this. They *build* with this knowledge, constructing their pathways with redundancy, with *escape routes*, with the constant awareness that any step could be the last, that the crust could fail, that the marsh could *claim* them in the way it claims everything that enters it.

They are also the *most free*, in a sense. The Marsh-Walkers do not own anything. They cannot. Ownership implies permanence, and the marsh is *temporary*, always *becoming*, always *releasing*. The Marsh-Walkers live in *momentary* structures, in tents of woven lichen-mat, in platforms of accreted mineral, in *camps* that can be abandoned in an hour, that *are* abandoned regularly, as the marsh floods and drains and *reconfigures* itself. They follow the *pulse* of the water table, moving their camps to stay at the interface, to stay where the *processing* happens, where the chemistry is most active, where the harvest is best.

The Marsh-Walkers have their own *knowledge*, their own *oral tradition*, their own *songs* and *recipes* and *warnings*. They know which crusts *hold*, which *release*, which *grow* between visits. They know the *smell* of a stable marsh, the *feel* of a shifting water table, the *sound* of the deep chemistry *stirring*. They are the Basin's *sensors*, its *early warning*, its *first contact* with the changes that the Tower-Tenders and the Well-Delvers and the Loop-Keepers will only detect later, when the loop itself begins to *fail*. They are *ignored* by the settled hierarchy, the ones who live in the towers and the works and the administrative structures, and they are *essential* to the survival of all, because they *see* what the others cannot, because they are *present* at the interface in ways that the others have forgotten how to be.

Some Marsh-Walkers *become* something else. The marsh *processes* them, as it processes everything, and occasionally the processing is *incomplete*, leaving them *changed*, *marked*, *other*. Their skin becomes *translucent*, filled with the gas that keeps the hollow rushes buoyant. Their bones become *light*, *porous*, capable of floating if the marsh *releases* them upward rather than downward. They are called *the Returned*, or *the Almost-Lost*, or, in the older tongue, *the children of the interface*. They are not feared. They are *consulted*. They have *seen* the deep dark, the cold water below the marsh, and they have *survived*, and their survival carries *information* that the living need, that the *processing* of the Basin requires. They speak of *pressure* and *darkness* and *something that moves* in the deep water, something that is not animal, not plant, not any category that the older lands would recognize. They speak *rarely*, and their speech is *valued*, because it is *expensive*, paid for in *self*, in *identity*, in the *loss* of the ordinary life that the other Basin people still have, still hope for, still *prepare for* in their architecture of succession, their civilization of the *next* zone, the *next* thermal pulse, the *next* provisional stability.

---

But these are *roles*. Functions. Ways of *interfacing* with the Basin's metabolism. The *people* beneath the roles are harder to describe, because the Basin does not allow them to be *fixed*, to be *named*, to be *understood* even by themselves.

They are *young*, in a sense. Not in age—the Basin people live as long as any humans, perhaps longer, their bodies adapted to the mineral-rich air, the thermal cycling, the constant low-level *processing* of their chemistry by the world around them. But *young* in the sense that the Basin is young, in the sense that they have not yet *settled*, have not yet *become*, have not yet achieved the *stability* that the older lands call *civilization*. They are *provisional* people, living in a *provisional* world, and their *provisionality* is not a lack but a *kind of being*, a way of *existing* that the older lands have forgotten, that perhaps they never knew.

They *dream*. Not the dreams of sleep, though they sleep, and their sleep is *strange*, filled with the *images* that rise from certain vents, the *resonances* of what the Basin was before it was this, what it will be after. They dream *awake*, in the steam, in the hiss of the vents, in the *groan* of the condensation towers as they expand and contract with the thermal cycling. Their dreams are not *private*. They are *shared*, *distributed*, *processed* by the world around them in ways that would be called *magic* elsewhere, that are simply *interface* here. A dream of water, dreamed by a Tower-Tender, changes the *efficiency* of their tower, for better or worse. A dream of pressure, dreamed by a Well-Delver, shifts the *thermal gradient* in their well, opens or closes a *pathway* that the pumps can follow. A dream of *becoming*, dreamed by a Marsh-Walker, changes the *chemistry* of the marsh itself, precipitates a new mineral, grows a new structure, *adds* to the *becoming* of the world.

The Basin people do not *interpret* their dreams. Interpretation implies *distance*, *objectivity*, a *self* that stands apart from the dream and *judges* it. The Basin people do not stand apart. They *are* their dreams, as they are their bodies, as they are their *roles*, their *functions*, their *interfaces* with the world that is still becoming. To interpret a dream is to *fix* it, to make it *finished*, and the Basin punishes *finishedness* with *response*, with *change*, with the *opening* of new possibilities that the interpreter cannot control.

They are *lonely*, in a sense. Not because they lack *company*—they live in settlements, they work in guilds, they tend machines and harvest minerals and maintain the loop—but because they cannot *share* their deepest experiences, cannot *name* what they are becoming, cannot *understand* themselves in the terms that the older lands would recognize. A Tower-Tender who has spent twenty years breathing steam, harvesting condensation, abandoning and relocating towers a dozen times, does not have a *self* in any sense that would survive translation. They have a *history* of cycles, a *pattern* of responses, a *rhythm* of expansion and contraction that is *them* but is not *fixed*, not *permanent*, not *understandable* even to themselves in any complete sense.

They are *proud*, and *afraid*, and *hopeful*, and *resigned*, and all of these at once, in combinations that shift with the thermal cycling, with the marsh-flood and drawdown, with the *mood* of the engines, the *dreaming* of the deep structures, the *becoming* of the world that they inhabit and are inhabited by, that *processes* them as they *process* it, that *learns* them as they learn it, in a *feedback* that has no name, no category, no *end*.

They are the *temporary* people, living in a *temporary* world, *preparing* for the *end* of *this* world so that they can *continue* in the *next*, the *next* zone, the *next* thermal pulse, the *next* provisional stability in a *world* that is always, always *becoming*.

They do not have a *culture*, in the sense that the older lands would recognize. They have a *metabolism*. A way of *processing* the world, of *being processed* by it, of *interface* without *understanding*, of *use* without *knowledge*, of *survival* without *permanence*. They have *songs* that are *mechanical liturgy*, *recipes* that are *oral tradition*, *guilds* that are *succession*, *dreams* that are *shared* and *distributed* and *acted upon* without *interpretation*.

They have *hope*. Not the hope of *progress*, of *improvement*, of *becoming better*, but the hope of *continuation*, of *finding* the next zone, of *carrying* the knowledge forward, of *being ready* when the thermal vents weaken and the condensation towers fall silent and the marsh drains into the young, hungry earth. They have *hope* because they have *no choice*. The Basin does not allow *despair*. Despair is a kind of *fixity*, a *refusal* to *process*, to *interface*, to *become*, and the Basin *responds* to refusal with *change*, with *opening*, with the *possibility* that the refuser will find themselves *transformed*, *distributed*, *integrated* into the *template* of the deep structures, *present* but no longer *individual*, no longer *person*, no longer *anything* that the older lands would recognize as *human*.

They have *love*. Not the love of *possession*, of *permanence*, of *family* in any sense that the settled lands would recognize, but the love of *interface*, of *shared rhythm*, of *mutual processing*. A Tower-Tender and their tower. A Well-Delver and their well. A Loop-Keeper and their engine. A Marsh-Walker and their marsh. These are *loves*, *relationships*, *becomings-together*, and they are *real*, as real as any love in the older lands, and they are *temporary*, as temporary as everything else in the Basin, and the Basin people *accept* this temporariness, *embrace* it, *build* their lives around it, their *architecture* of succession, their *civilization* of the *next* zone, the *next* thermal pulse, the *next* provisional stability.

They are *strange*. Not in the decorative sense, not in the sense of *curiosity*, but in the *structural* sense, the sense that they inhabit *margins*, that they *exploit* possibilities that the older lands cannot *see*, that they *interface* with a world that is still *becoming* in ways that will not be *understood* by those who live in it, that will only be *inhabited*, only be *survived*, only be *used*, only be *responded to*, until the day when the thermal vents weaken and the condensation towers fall silent and the marsh drains into the young, hungry earth, and the people move on to the *next* zone, the *next* thermal pulse, the *next* provisional stability in a world that is always, always *becoming*.

They are the *Condensed*. The *Sweat-Keepers*. The *Children of the Young Wound*. The *temporary*. The ones who stand in the middle. The ones who tend the loop. The ones who *process* and are *processed*, who *interface* and are *interfaced*, who *become* and are *become*, in the steam and the hiss and the groan and the deep piston-rhythm of a world that is still learning how to be a world.

They do not *welcome* you. They do not *reject* you. They *process* you, as they process everything—water, heat, rock, time, the slow chemistry of entropy and emergence. You are part of their metabolism now. You have always been.

The condensation that falls on your skin carries their minerals, their heat, their *dreams*, their *temporary* lives, their *hope* for the next zone, their *love* of the interface, their *strangeness* in a world that is *stranger* still.

Stand in the middle of the marsh, where the crust holds for three steps and then releases. Feel the steam on one side of your face, the cold on the other. Listen to the hiss and the groan and the deep piston-rhythm of the engines that keep the loop running. Watch the condensation towers breathe, in and out, in and out, the rhythm of a world that is young and busy and *alive* in ways that do not match any definition of life you brought with you.

This is the Basin. These are its people. This is what it means to live in a world that is still being born.

The water is near the surface. The rock is young. The steam is rising. The engines are running.

And the people are *temporary*, and *eternal*, and *becoming*, in the steam, in the hiss, in the *interface*, in the *middle*, where the cold meets the heat and the held meets the released and the known meets the *stranger* still, and the Basin is dreaming, in its steam, of what comes next, and its people are dreaming with it, *temporary* dreams, *provisional* dreams, dreams of the *next* zone, the *next* thermal pulse, the *next* provisional stability, in a world that is always, always, always *becoming*.
