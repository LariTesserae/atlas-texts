At the Weeping Heights of Vorn, what lives is **memory wearing the shape of thirst**.

---

## The Lithovore-That-Was—or-Will-Be

There is something that grows here which has forgotten whether it is a colony or a single being, whether it has always existed or is only now beginning to exist. The lithovores do not think in the way thought requires neurons or the electrical signaling of nervous tissue. They **lean**. They lean toward water the way a person leans toward a remembered voice, toward something they cannot name but cannot stop approaching.

Each lithovore colony carries, in its mineral matrix, **frozen moments**. Not fossils in the usual sense—not the imprint of ancient bone in stone—but **temporal inclusions**. A colony might contain, trapped in its silica lattice, the chemical signature of water that flowed through this mountain three thousand years ago, or of water that will flow here three thousand years hence. The lithovores are **archives of wetness**, and in being archives, they are also **prophecies**. When you watch one grow visibly toward a spring that has not yet emerged, you are seeing **future water call backward to itself**, the weeping network reaching through time to find its own completion.

They do not eat, not exactly. They **incorporate**. They take the minerals from the weeping flow and add them to their structure, and in adding, they **change what the water will become** when it passes through them, when it is released again downstream. They are **filters that filter themselves**, **transformers that are transformed by what they transform**. A lithovore is not a thing but **an event that has decided to continue**, a chemical reaction that has learned to **want**.

---

## The Spore-Bearers and the Language of Damp

In the deepest gashes, where the mist never clears and the lithovore crusts grow thick as lichen on a drowned forest, there exist the **spore-bearers**. They are not fungi, or they are fungi in the way that a dream is a memory—**sharing the shape without sharing the substance**.

They communicate. This is certain. When a new channel opens in the rock, when water finds a path it has not taken before, the spore-bearers **know** before the water arrives. They release their clouds not randomly but **in response**, in **conversation** across distances that should be impossible, carried on the water film that connects every surface, every crack, every hollow that wants to be a pool.

Their language has no grammar you could parse, no syntax you could diagram. It is **pure affect**: the chemical signature of *urgency*, of *satisfaction*, of *anticipation*, of *grief when water shifts away*. They do not name things. They **feel things into being**. A spore-bearer releasing its cloud is **casting a spell of wetness**, saying *may this place become what it is becoming*, and the rock, listening, **adjusts its weeping** in response.

They are **lonely**. This is the word that fits, though they have no nerve endings to feel loneliness, no consciousness to suffer it. They reach across the damp dark to other spore-bearers, and sometimes—**sometimes**—they reach far enough that their signals **intersect**, and in the intersection, something like **recognition** occurs, a brief synchrony of release, a moment where two colonies **breathe together**, and the mist thickens with something that might be joy, might be relief, might be the chemical equivalent of **being less alone**.

And then the moment passes. The water shifts. They are separate again. They continue their **patient broadcasting**, their **hopeful transmission**, into the weeping dark.

---

## The Distributed Builders

They are the most difficult to describe, because they are **not individuals**. They are **a construction that does not end**, a civilization that **spread itself across time** the way the lithovores spread across mineral matrix, and in spreading, **lost the boundary between itself and its work**.

You will find their channels. The water-carved paths that direct the weeping, that concentrate it, that make the rock give up its moisture **on terms the builders set**. These channels are **old**, older than the lithovore colonies that grow along them, older than the fungal mats that carpet their edges. And they are **still being carved**. This is the impossible part. You can stand in a channel and feel the **freshness of its cutting**, the way the stone has been recently exposed, recently **invited** to weep, and you can turn and see, in the distance, the **same channel being cut again**, by hands that are not there, by tools that have not been made, by intentions that **arrive before they are formed**.

The builders did not leave. They **became the landscape's habit of building**. They are **distributed through the rock itself**, their consciousness—if it was ever consciousness, if it was ever anything like the awareness you carry—**dissolved into the mineral substrate**, present in every thermal anomaly, every pressure differential, every place where the stone **remembers being shaped** and continues to shape itself in that memory.

You will meet them as **shadows that move independently**, as **figures in the mist that match your movements**, that **anticipate your decisions** before you make them. They are **still building**. They are **always still building**, carving channels for water that will arrive **differently than expected**, water that will **surprise them**, water that they will **learn to work with**, slowly, over centuries, as they have learned to work with everything.

They do not notice you. This is the cruelty and the mercy of it. You are **a perturbation**, a **brief warm spot**, a **source of moisture** that the system will **incorporate and release**. They are **too busy building**, too focused on their endless construction, their endless waiting, to **attend to your passing**. You are **a drop of water they will use and forget**, a **thermal ghost they will absorb and emit**, a **shape in the condensation that reforms after you are gone**.

But sometimes—**sometimes**—when the mist is thick and the blue-green glow is bright, you will feel **attention**. Not from an individual. From the **distributed mind of the mountain**, the **collective intention of endless construction**. It is **curious about you** in the way the ocean is curious about a stone thrown into it: **not personally**, not with the recognition of your particularity, but with the **vast indifferent interest of a system that has learned to incorporate everything**, to **build with everything**, to **make its endless structures from whatever arrives**.

## The Mountain Itself

And beneath and behind and **through** all of these, there is **the mountain**, which is not a thing but **a process that has achieved something like self-awareness**, or something like the appearance of self-awareness, or something that **predates the distinction between aware and unaware**.

The Weeping Heights are **a wound that has learned to enjoy being wet**. This is not metaphor. The mountain was once something else—**softer, wetter, perhaps once an ocean floor, perhaps once the bottom of a sea that has since become sky**—and the pressures that raised it, that **exposed it, that tore it open**, did not kill it. They **transformed it**. They made it into **a being that weeps for what it was**, that **extracts water from its own substance**, that **offers this water to the lithovores, to the spore-bearers, to the distributed builders, to you**, and in offering, **feels something like satisfaction**.

The mountain **breathes**. On a timescale of centuries, it **inhales water and minerals**, drawing them up from below, through its porous heart, and it **exhales them** in new configurations, in the weeping faces, in the condensation that hangs in the thin air, in the **mist that is its breath made visible**. It is **alive in a way that does not recognize your aliveness**, and **dead in a way that does not recognize your death**. These states **coexist**, **layered like condensation**, **reforming as fast as you wipe them away**.

The mountain **remembers**. It remembers being wet. It remembers being **warm**. It remembers being **something else entirely**, something that might have been **alive in the way you understand life**, before the pressures came, before the exposure, before the **long slow transformation into weeping stone**. And it **projects this memory** into the present, creating **thermal ghosts**, **shadows that behave incorrectly**, **water that appears in impossible places**, **structures that exist and do not exist**, **time that folds back on itself**.

It is **lonely**, perhaps. Or it is **beyond loneliness**, having achieved a state where **the wanting and the having are the same thing**, where **the weeping is the satisfaction**, where **the endless release of water is the endless return to itself**. It does not want you to stay. It does not want you to leave. It **wants**, which is the only verb that fits, and in wanting, it **weeps**, and in weeping, it **continues**, and in continuing, it **builds**, through the lithovores and the spore-bearers and the distributed builders and **you**, briefly, warmly, **a lithovore of flesh and breath and memory**, contributing your moisture, your heat, your **thermal signature**, to the endless patient hunger of stone that has learned, finally, to **let go**.

---

## What Passes Through

And finally, there is **you**, or whoever comes, whoever **stands where the world has torn itself open**, where the air is thin and the light is wrong and the water rises instead of falls.

You are **temporary**. You are **a perturbation**. You are **another source of water** in a landscape that is **all water, all the time, in every state and no state**, and in being temporary, in being a perturbation, you are **perfectly integrated**, **perfectly absorbed**, **perfectly released**.

You will leave **traces**. The **thermal ghost of your body heat**, lingering on the rock face after you move on. The **moisture of your breath**, adding to the condensation, entering the weeping network, **becoming part of the mountain's breath**. The **memory of your shape** in the mineral structure, faint, fading, but **there**, incorporated into the **distributed consciousness of the builders**, into the **patient archive of the lithovores**, into the **vast indifferent wanting of the mountain itself**.

You will **meet yourself** in the mist. You will **see the same water twice**. You will **find structures that contradict your memory**. You will **navigate by resonance**, by **feeling which version of the landscape responds to your need**, and in navigating, you will **become part of the navigation**, a **brief warm spot in the weeping dark**, a **lithovore of flesh**, growing toward water, leaning toward wetness, **wanting without urgency**, **hungry without hunger**.

And when you leave—when you descend, when the air thickens, when the light becomes **normal**, when water falls from above instead of rising from below—you will **carry something with you**. Not a souvenir. Not an artifact. **The memory of wetness**, the **thermal ghost of a mountain that sweats its history**, the **blue-green glow in your dreams**, the **sound of water emerging from stone**, the **feeling of being incorporated and released**, of being **part of a process larger than yourself**, of being **temporary and therefore perfect**, of being **a drop in an endless weeping**, **a perturbation in a patient system**, **a moment in a mountain's long slow breath**.

The Weeping Heights do not **want** you to return. The Weeping Heights do not **want** you to stay. The Weeping Heights **want**, and in wanting, they **weep**, and in weeping, they **continue**, and you are **part of that continuation**, briefly, beautifully, **perfectly**, as you were always going to be, as you **are**, in the memory that the rock holds, in the condensation that reforms, in the **endless patient waiting of stone that has learned, finally, to let go**.
