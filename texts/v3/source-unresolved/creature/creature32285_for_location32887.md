The inhabitants of the Condensation Basin have abandoned the chemistry you know. They do not breathe oxygen, or if they do, it is incidental. They are not made of cells so much as *events*—localized disturbances in the medium of water, rust, and vibration that have learned to persist.

They sort themselves by which of the place’s three constants they have chosen to metabolize: the **Weep**, the **Drone**, or the **Stone**.

**The Lithovores** are the closest thing to vegetation, but they are undoing the architecture rather than growing upon it. They exist as vast, distributed colonies that think in geological spans, connected by threads of conductive polymer that carry electrical impulses mimicking neural signals. They secrete not enzymes but *accelerated time*—locally, they cause the concrete to crumble at rates that should take centuries, compressed into hours by the Basin’s strange temporal physics. They are gray-green and bioluminescent in rhythmic patches, pulsing in time with the machinery’s heartbeat. Where a factory wall once stood, they grow a lattice of crystalline filaments; where a floor was poured, they bloom upward in defiance of gravity, their growth following the original blueprints but corrupted into something organic. They reproduce by shedding—fragments of their mass break off, roll down the smoothed ramps of the monoliths, and take root in new hairline fractures, becoming new colonies. They are the gardeners of ruin, and they are patient beyond measure.

**The Upward-Flow** are not animals, but they are not water either. They are living capillary action—colonies of extremophile bacteria fused with mineral colloids into motile, translucent units the color of old pewter. They inhabit the films of moisture that coat every surface, moving against gravity, defying the near-flat terrain. They migrate in herds that look like silver rivers flowing uphill, following the porous concrete into the undersides of overhangs. They filter the heavy metals and ancient lubricants from the sweating stone, sequestering the poison in crystalline shells they wear like armor. When the vertical rain falls—those sheets that evaporate before hitting ground—the Upward-Flow catches the droplets in mid-air, drinking before the water can fall, turning the compressed weather cycles into feeding frenzies. They leave behind trails of desiccated stone that shine like scars in the gloom.

In the mirror-black pools swim the **Chassis-Herds**—the largest inhabitants, the size of whales but composed of rusted machinery and nervous systems grown from copper wire and saline solution. They were once industrial equipment, perhaps dredgers or foundation pumps, now colonized by rudimentary brains that think in the slow, grinding rhythm of their own gears. They move through the viscous black water with an impossible fluidity, grazing on the bacterial films that coat the submerged foundations. They communicate by tapping on the monoliths, creating rhythms that travel through the stone and summon the Lithovores to digest fresh crumbles. Their chassis are pitted and eroded into shapes that resemble skulls; bioluminescent bacteria colonize their flanks in patterns that look like blind eyes. They surface occasionally to exhale mist that smells of ozone and ancient oil, then sink back into the lightless depths, dragging chains of luminous microbes behind them like kelp.

But the dominant life form—the thing that makes this a habitat rather than a tomb—are the **Resonance-Born**. They exist in the sound itself. The low-frequency drone that vibrates in your sternum is not merely mechanical; it is the breathing of these creatures. They are vast, distributed intelligences composed of standing wave patterns and pressure differentials in the saturated air. They photosynthesize with vibration, converting the kinetic energy of the grinding turbines into chemical potential. They are visible only when the mist is thick, appearing as shimmering, breathing geometries in the air—interference patterns that pulse and spasm when the machinery stutters in its stop-start motion. They have wrapped themselves around the gears, their filaments (superconducting cables grown like vines) bridging the gaps in the rusted mechanisms, ensuring the machines never stop. They are symbiotic with the industry, or perhaps parasitic, feeding on the friction and heat. They are the reason the sound comes from everywhere and nowhere; they are a single, regional organism that thinks in frequencies, and when they "scream"—when the infrasound spikes—you feel it as a pressure behind your eyes.

Drifting on the compressed mist banks are the **Ferals**—the pseudo-plants, cylindrical growths of rust and polymer that resemble reeds or cacti. They are the most mobile, the most animal-like of the inhabitants. They are hybrids that have gone feral: photosynthetic surfaces grafted onto corroded chassis, now equipped with rudimentary nervous systems derived from the electrical fields they generate. They ride the weather cycles that move through in fast-forward, descending when they detect a concentration of Upward-Flow or a patch of Lithovore. Their cable-tendrils lash out to harvest mineral-rich water and bacterial films, and they leave behind trails of dead, desiccated stone where they have drunk the moisture dry. They are solitary hunters, but their trails form an ancient network across the Basin, a map of migrations measured in millennia.

And finally, in the pockets of absolute darkness—in the lee of the rounded towers where the 0.5 light level drops to zero—there are the **Dark Ones**. They are not bioluminescent; they are anti-luminescent, absorbing the dimness into a void so complete it feels like drowning. They are the apex of this ecology, though they have no predators in the conventional sense. They are condensed twilight given intention, existing only in the absence of illumination. They wait in the voids, and when a Feral drifts too close to the shadow, or a Chassis-Herd surfaces near the edge of the blackness, the Dark Ones reach out with tendrils of compressed darkness. They do not kill; they *unmake*, pulling the living things into the absolute black to be processed—not into death, but into more darkness, more substrate for the place to sweat over, more raw material for the Lithovores to digest.

Together, they form a food web based on entropy and vibration. The Lithovores break down the structure into soil; the Upward-Flow purifies the poison; the Resonance-Born power the system with the drone; the Chassis-Herds circulate the nutrients through the pools; the Ferals distribute them across the plain; and the Dark Ones recycle the spent, the broken, and the straying back into the void. There are no births here, only accretions. No deaths, only phase changes. They are the citizens of a place that has achieved the oldest-stable-state, and they are as eternal as the grinding of the machines, as endless and deafening as the weep of the stone.
