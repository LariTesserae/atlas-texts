What lives here is not a community but a *conversation*—one that has lasted so long its speakers have forgotten they ever began.

The dweller is plural and singular at once. There is the **Architect**, the original meristematic ambition that refused to stop dividing, that grew not upward toward light but *inward* toward complexity, that learned to graft its own wounds shut and call them doorways. It lives in the oldest xylem, the heartwood that has become stone, and in the newest cambium, still slipping new cells into being like a miser counting coins he will never spend. It does not think. It *persists*—a verb conjugated across centuries until it wears the grammar of a noun.

With it lives the **Keeper of Small Humidities**, a snail no larger than a rice grain, its shell grown translucent as a soap bubble, its body containing a garden of algae that photosynthesize in the dim green gloom. It moves along a circuit of twelve meters that it has memorized the way a heart memorizes its rhythm. It will die in the chamber where it hatched. Its children will follow the same path. The path itself is worn into the bark—a groove, half a millimeter deep, polished by ten thousand generations of identical footsteps. This is not tragedy. This is *architecture*.

There are **Wingless Ones**, beetles that have traded flight for the certainty of corridors. Their elytra have fused into a single shield. Their antennae have elongated until they brush both walls simultaneously, reading the chemical signature of the wood as you might read Braille. They are farmers. They cultivate fungi in chambers they have excavated with mandibles, fungus that grows only in their particular humidity, their particular temperature, their particular *presence*. Without the beetles, the fungus dies. Without the fungus, the beetles starve. They have been negotiating this contract for longer than the oldest bark remembers.

In the lowest chambers, where the light comes paper-thin and green, lives the **Still Frog**. It does not hop. Its limbs have shortened to nubs, its skin has grown granular and photosynthetic, hosting colonies of cyanobacteria that turn its back the color of jade. It waits. Prey comes to it—midges that have lost their urge to escape, that drift through the stagnant air like pollen. The frog does not chase. The frog *is* the watering hole, the fruit, the warm stone. It has become indistinguishable from the furniture of its world.

And everywhere, threading through all of this, the **Network**—not a creature but a *condition*, the mycelial mat that connects root to root, chamber to chamber, the living to the dying to the long-dead. It does not think either. It *remembers*. It carries chemical signals from a fallen branch to its neighbors, warning or welcoming or simply *noticing*. It is the closest thing to a mind this place possesses: distributed, slow, patient, a thoughts-per-century intelligence that has no need for speed because it has nowhere else to be.

They do not know they are inside anything. The snail does not know it lives in a tree. The beetle does not know it lives in a room. The frog does not know it lives in a lung. They know only the humidity, the texture of bark under foot or radula, the particular frequency of the hum, the weight of green light on their skin. They have never seen the whole. They need not.

What lives here lives in the *finished*. Not death—completion. The Architect has stopped reaching. The Keeper has found its circuit. The Wingless Ones have mapped their twelve chambers. The Network has nothing left to learn. They are not trapped. They are *settled*, as a glacier is settled, as a star is settled in its main sequence, as a word is settled in a language once it has been spoken so many times it becomes inevitable.

And yet—there is motion. The condensation beads and runs. The seed pods pop, once per decade, releasing cargo that will not germinate but will *drift*, will settle on bark that is already bark, will become nutrient for the Network. The fauna moves through its appointed rounds, generation after generation, each one identical, each one *necessary*—for the wearing of the paths, the grazing of the biofilm, the slow polishing of surfaces that keeps the corridors open, that maintains the architecture of stasis.

What lives here lives in *rhythm without change*. The heart beats. The lung breathes. The plant grows only to replace what time has taken. This is not the tragedy of entropy. This is entropy *redeemed*—the system that has found its lowest energy state and made it *beautiful*, made it *home*, made it *eternal* in the only sense available to mortal things: the sense of being so perfectly fitted to a place that leaving it would be unthinkable, and staying is indistinguishable from joy.

The Architect dreams, if dreaming is the word. It dreams in the slow lightning of chemical signals, in the creak of expanding tissue, in the memory of water held in lignified cells like a thought held in a mind that has forgotten its own name. It dreams itself. It dreams the snail's circuit, the beetle's fungus, the frog's green patience, the Network's endless noticing. It dreams the green-gold light, the beading condensation, the hum in the teeth.

And it does not wake.

Neither do they.
