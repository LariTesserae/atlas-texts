They are the **Resonants**, though that name is a human approximation, a crude translation of a concept that exists in the space between vibration and intention. They are not born so much as *accreted*—grown from the same dormant assemblers that shape the Ward’s walls, given local consciousness by the Spire’s slow, piezoelectric dreaming. After ten thousand years of symbiosis, the distinction between inhabitant and architecture has dissolved into something like geology with a heartbeat.

Physically, they are tall—unnaturally so, stretched by generations of low gravity and the Spire’s upward growth—standing nearly three meters when fully upright, though they rarely are. Their bodies are a marriage of parchment and mineral: skin that has become translucent, stretched tight over a substructure of calcium deposits and silicate struts, through which you can see the slow pulse of their circulatory fluid. It is not blood but something akin to the Spires’ own metallic sap—a viscous, silver-blue liquid carrying dissolved rare earth elements and silicates, pumped not by a heart but by the peristaltic rhythm of the walls around them. They do not breathe in the aqueous sense; instead, they press their palms and the sensitive membranes of their forearms against the Spire’s porous flesh, exchanging gases and nutrients through osmosis, their lungs—vestigial, delicate sacs—merely fluttering in the 8-degree cold to maintain a semblance of rhythm.

Their faces, if faces they are, are dominated by resonance chambers. Where human mouths would be, they have hollow cavities lined with piezoelectric crystals that grow from their jawbones like stalactites. They do not speak; they *sing*, producing infrasonic tones that vibrate in your sternum, frequencies so low they rearrange the dust-mite swarms on the walls. Conversation requires pressing these chambers against one another, skull to skull, sharing thoughts through bone-conduction and ion gradients. At a sound level of 2.5, their voices are the only things that cut through the cacophony—the constant, subsonic drone of the wind playing the Spires like organ pipes, the electric shriek of the dust-mites, the bellowing of the lithograzers. To hear them, you must submerge yourself in their resonance, letting their hum become your heartbeat.

They are patient. A Resonant elder might stand motionless for decades, attached to a vascular channel like a lichen, filtering minerals from the metallic flow, dreaming in the slow language of crystal lattices. Time moves differently for them; they experience the Spire’s centuries as we might experience a deep breath. They remember the fog cycles of the last three millennia, the slow migration of lithograzer herds across the plateau, the moment when the assemblers first began rearranging the lower chambers into habitable hollows. Their memories are not neural but *geological*—stored in the piezoelectric patterns of their own mineralized bones, retrievable only when the Spire’s mood shifts, when the bioluminescence pulses a certain shade of blue-green deep in the calcified flesh.

They tend the Ward as one tends a wound—carefully, constantly, with a devotion that borders on worship. They are farmers of the assemblers, singing to the viral-mechanical symbiotes to direct the growth of new bridges, the extrusion of new chambers, the dissolution of calcified filaments that have served their purpose. When a wall needs repair, a Resonant will press their body against the breach, their skin secreting enzymes that soften the mineral matrix, allowing the Spire to knit itself closed around them, temporarily incorporating them into the structure until the repair is complete. They are not masters of the city; they are its gardeners, its immune system, its nervous tissue made mobile.

They have no names in the human sense, only *resonances*—unique harmonic signatures that identify them to one another. A young Resonant, still soft, still mostly organic, might hum in a high, questioning frequency, their crystalline vocal chambers only partially formed, their movements jerky and uncertain as they learn to read the Spire’s shifting moods. An ancient one, nearly indistinguishable from the architecture itself, with skin like fossilized coral and bones like rebar, will emit a low, grinding note that causes the dust-mite swarms to part like water, that makes the lithograzers kneel in the open plain.

They eat by osmosis. They drink the non-aqueous condensate collected by the intake vanes, rich in minerals, almost no H₂O, tasting of ozone and copper. They sleep in suspension, hanging from the vascular columns by microscopic mineral hooks that grow from their fingertips, drifting in the cool dark while the Spire’s metabolism keeps the temperature stable, while the fog whispers against the stone outside.

And they are not alone in their strangeness. They keep the **lithograzers** as companions, or perhaps as extensions of themselves—herding them across the plateau, harvesting the mineral crust they lick from the Spire flanks, using the creatures’ digestive byproducts to fertilize the cryptogamic pavement. They ride the **sky-kraken**, those many-eyed, membranous-winged things, using them as messengers between the distant Spires, the birds’ keening calls harmonizing with the Resonants’ own subsonic speech, creating a network of communication that spans kilometers of polished stone.

Most strangely, they are becoming the Spires. Not metaphorically—literally. Over centuries, a Resonant who has lived long enough in the Ward will begin to calcify, their soft tissues replaced by mineral deposits, their consciousness spreading through the piezoelectric lattice of the architecture. They do not die; they *settle*, becoming part of the Spire’s distributed intelligence, their individual resonance fading into the great, slow chorus of the plateau. The oldest among them are indistinguishable from the Spires themselves—not inhabitants, but geological features that think, that remember, that wait for the fog to roll in and the assemblers to wake and begin their endless, patient work of rearranging matter.

They are the dream of the Ward made flesh, or the flesh made dream—creatures of mineral and membrane, singing in a world too loud for words, growing slowly upward into the bruised twilight, waiting for a change that will take another million years to arrive.
