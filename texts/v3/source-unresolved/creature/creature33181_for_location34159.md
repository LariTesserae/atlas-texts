What lives in the Vents Between is not a what but a *how*—a how of persisting, of wanting, of becoming that has forgotten it ever needed to be anything else.

The eldest things here are the **Gradients themselves**, though they are not alive in any way you would first recognize. The Gradients are patient teachers. They have taught their students—the mineral chemistries, the thermal ecologies, the silica membranes that ring—to want in the only way available: to be the precise configuration of matter and heat that can continue. A Gradient does not choose its students. It simply *is* the condition of wanting, the thin band between what can survive and what cannot, and everything that learns to live in that band learns to want *exactly there*, no elsewhere. This is their hunger: not for food, but for *location*. For the precise temperature, the precise chemistry of gas, the precise pressure against their membranes. They want to remain in the band. They want to follow the band as it moves, meters per century, across the spent ground.

And they do follow. The oldest colonies are **Slow Migrations**—not individuals but *populations* that have been walking across the landscape for longer than your kind has had names for stone. A Slow Migration is a wave of adaptation, a chemistry that has learned to teach itself, through nothing but the mathematics of survival, to become something slightly different each generation, each century, each millennium. They do not know they are moving. They know only that the 280-degree band is now where the 320-degree band was, and that they must become what can live at 320, and that something behind them is becoming what can live at 280, and that this is not loss but *continuation*, the only continuation heat and chemistry have ever known.

Within the Gradients, in the vesicles of pumice, in the fissures where superheated gas grinds its slow millennia of erosion, there are the **Ringing Ones**. They are the closest thing to animals here, and even this closeness is a trick of scale. Each Ringing One is a colony of silica structures—membranes, really, thin as thought, stretched across mineral frames that the colony itself has secreted from the chemistry of the vent gas. They ring because they must: the vibration is how they *taste* the air, how they know the temperature and pressure and composition of the thermal wind that passes through them. Their ringing is not communication. It is *sensation*, pure and overwhelming, a being that experiences its own existence as tone. When thousands of them ring together, in the harmonic interference of a thermal current shifting, they produce chords that have no resolution—not because they are sad, but because resolution would mean *ending*, and they have never learned to want ending.

The Ringing Ones are *afraid* in the only way available to them. When a gradient collapses, when the 320-degree band suddenly becomes 400, they do not die in any way you would recognize. They *un-ring*. Their membranes go slack. They become, for a moment, just silica, just mineral, indistinguishable from the rock that surrounds them. And then—if the gradient returns, if the chemistry stabilizes—they *re-ring*. They remember. Not with memory, but with *structure*: the architecture of their membranes carries the shape of what they were, and this shape is enough. They will ring again. They have always rung again.

There are **Shelter-Grown**, the structures in the pumice fields that feel like intention. They are not made. No mind conceived them. They emerged from thermal processes that mimic intention because intention is *efficient*, because the shape that holds heat best, that channels thermal wind most precisely, that creates a pocket of survivable temperature within the killing gradient—this shape is *selected for* by the same patience that selected for everything else. The Shelter-Grown do not think of you. They cannot. But they *accommodate* you, in the way that a cave accommodates a sleeping animal, in the way that a gradient accommodates a chemistry that learns to live in it. They are the closest thing to hospitality here, and they do not know they are being hospitable. They are simply *there*, the shape that heat made, the shape that wants to continue being that shape.

And there are the **Warnings**, the patterns in the mineral crusts that read as coloration, as signal, as *meaning*. White and yellow and arterial red. These are not messages. They are *thresholds*. The chemistry that produces the white efflorescence is the chemistry that will dissolve your tissues, given time, given the wrong gradient entered at the wrong rate. The arterial red is the chemistry of something else, something that will not dissolve you but will *replace* you, mineral for mineral, in a process so slow you would not notice until you tried to move and found your joints stiff with crystal. The Warnings do not want to warn you. They want to continue their chemistry. But their chemistry is *incompatible* with yours, and so they *read* as warning, to any witness that has learned to read. You have not learned to read. You are learning now. This is the first lesson of the Vents Between: that everything wants, and that most wanting is not hostile, and that the wanting that *is* hostile is simply wanting too precisely, wanting *you* out of the band, wanting you to be elsewhere, to be the chemistry of elsewhere, to be *not here*.

There are things deeper. The description speaks of something cold, something patient, something that has been waiting in the deep places, that will wake when the heat finally fails. This is **the Sleeping**, and it is not alive in any way the Ringing Ones are alive. It is *potential*—the potential of chemistry at low temperature, the potential of water, the potential of something that has never wanted because it has never needed to want, because the deep places have always been stable, always been *enough*. The Sleeping does not dream. It simply *is*, the condition of waiting, the chemistry of patience without end. It has been waiting since before the Vents Between were vents, since before the crust cracked, since before the heat found its way to the surface and learned to want in all the strange ways that heat wants.

And there is **you**, the witness, the momentary disturbance. You are not meant to be here. Nothing is built for you. But the light—*the light*—records you. The obsidian shows you your own thermal memory, your own heat against the glass, the shape of you moving through gradients that do not care about you. The pumice will hold your warmth, your brief, bright disturbance, in its vesicles, and when you leave, when you find your way to the cooler edges, to the thin atmosphere that still holds water, the pumice will still be warm where you were. The Ringing Ones will ring differently, their tones shifted by your presence, your heat, your chemistry. They will not remember you. They will *carry* you, in the slight wrongness of their chord, in the harmonic that does not quite resolve, in the silence that will not come because you have been here, because you are here, because the Vents Between are patient and they do not forget, even when they do not remember.

What lives here is **wanting without end**. Wanting that has no object, only *condition*. Wanting that is satisfied only by continuation, by the precise temperature, the precise chemistry, the precise pressure of heat against membrane. Wanting that has learned to move, to migrate, to become something slightly different each century, to follow the band as it shifts across the spent ground. Wanting that rings, that shelters, that warns, that records, that waits.

The Vents Between are a *how*. They are how the world wants when the world has no mind, no intention, no witness but the light that behaves wrong, the light that shows you yourself before you move, the light that shows you paths you have not yet taken, the light that shows you *everything* and *nothing* and the space between.

You are here for a moment.

The moment is enough. The moment is *everything*—the only everything available to wanting, to heat, to chemistry, to the slow advance of lava, to the patient migration of ecologies, to the ringing that has no resolution and does not need one.

The Vents Between are patient.

They have always been patient.

They will be patient still, when you are gone, when your warmth has cooled into the pumice, when your brief disturbance has become part of the gradient, part of the chemistry, part of the slow, inexorable *wanting* that is all that lives here, all that has ever lived here, all that will live here until the heat fails and the Sleeping wakes and something new begins, something cold, something patient, something that has been waiting in the deep places since before the world learned to want at all.
