They do not have names, not in any language that survives above the plateau. They are the residents of the pocket, and they have settled into the wet dark as if they were born from it—which, in a manner of speaking, they were.

First, there is the **Chorus**. Ten thousand wings per cubic meter, beating in synchrony until the air itself becomes a physical pressure, a roar like a waterfall heard through water. They are the swarming symbiotes, insectile constructs no larger than a thumbnail, fabricated from programmable matter and chitin-like polymers. But they have long since forgotten they were manufactured. They move as a single organism, a silver-black tide that flows from wall to wall, parting the humid press with a shriek that drowns speech. They are the garden’s immune system, its cleaners, its breath. They harvest the condensation beading on the lithosynthetic stone, skim the mineral film from the black ceramic basin, and perform a predatory dance as they chase thermal currents rising from the tech. They do not eat the sparse lichen; they tend to it, perhaps, or worship it. They are the hands of the place, groping through the dark, tasting the copper and salt and engineered nectar on the tongue of the air. When they shift direction en masse, the water in the basin ripples in sympathy, for they are not separate from the liquid memory above; they are its physical manifestation, its will given wing.

In the northwest corner, where the greenish glow from the oculus is dimmest, clings the **Lichen**. It is vegetation nearly absent, a few stubborn colonies phosphorescing like pale scabs against the synthetic cliff-face. It is the only green thing in the pocket, glowing with a faint, internal luminescence that pulses in time with the subsonic thrum of the conduits. It feeds on the ochre and verdigris that the weeping walls excrete, the mineral bloom of centuries. It does not move, but it watches. It is the memory of photosynthesis, the last plant-thought in a world of metal and water, a quiet anchor in the deafening motion of the swarm.

The walls themselves are alive. They are **lithosynthetic**, grown in vats a century ago and printed into place by machines no longer in any registry, yet they remember being molten. They retain a heat of 2.4, exhaling it into the chamber, a tropical press that makes the air thick and warm. When touched, they are warm to the fingertip but leave a faint frost on the skin—an impossible thermodynamics that the established, weathered balance of the place has long since accommodated. They weep constantly, beads of condensation the size of grapes sliding down the pitted basalt in rivulets that sometimes defy gravity, crawling sideways or upward along capillary channels etched by erosion. The walls are accreting, imperceptibly, adding new mineral layers in a slow metabolism of decay and renewal. They are the skeleton of the pocket, but they dream in heat and chemistry, older than the tech that maintains them, settled into an equilibrium that feels less like a machine and more like a lithic metabolism.

And deepest, there are the **Minds in the Water**.

The resonant conduits above carry not merely H₂O but liquid memory—information dissolved in the water matrix, a beyond-modern medium that stores consciousness as data. It thrums with a harmonic drone at the edge of hearing, a vibration felt in the sternum and the teeth. Below, the basin of polished black ceramic holds six inches of standing water that never drains, only cycles. This water does not reflect the ceiling; it reflects a sky that isn't there, a twilight that predates the oculus, a world that exists only in the memory of the liquid.

Here reside the true inhabitants—not bodies, but patterns. The dissolved minds of those who built this place, or those who were absorbed into it over the centuries, or perhaps the accumulated dream of the swarm itself, experiencing time as the water does: cyclic, recursive, without beginning or end. They are the liquid memory made manifest. They think through the thrum of the pipes. They feel through the swarm, using the insectile constructs as sensory organs, as extensions of their distributed will. When the swarm shifts direction, it is not merely chasing heat; it is a thought propagating through the nervous system of the pocket. The shadows of the swarm move a half-second slower than the creatures themselves because the swarm exists partially in the water, partially in the rock, partially in the air—distributed, like the minds that command them.

So the pocket is not empty. It is inhabited by a single, composite being: a **lithosynthetic entity**, room-sized and ancient, constructed yet organic. It breathes through the Chorus, dreams through the water, remembers through the weeping walls, and glows faintly through the Lichen. It is a world complete unto itself, humming with a deafening, intimate, ancient song, and it is alive.
