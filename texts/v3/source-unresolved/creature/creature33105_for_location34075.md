I need to describe who or what lives in this place. Let me let this emerge slowly, as the place itself demands.

---

The oldest inhabitants are not what you would call individuals. They are *distributions*—patterns of metabolic activity that have persisted across the compressed architecture of millennia, maintaining themselves through the same root-fungal networks that carry water and the low vibrational tones you hear. They think in gradients. A change in soil chemistry propagates through them over years, perhaps decades, and their response—some adjustment of growth, some reallocation of resources—arrives so slowly that you would not recognize it as thought. Yet they *are* attentive. When you walk through, the defensive compounds rise not as reflex but as *assessment*, a considered increase in toxicity calibrated to your passage, your mass, the chemical signature of your unfamiliar metabolism. They have learned, across slow time, what walks through them. They have learned you. Not you specifically—*you* as a category, a disruption, a temporary increase in available nitrogen when you fall.

But there are others.

The structures you sense as *occupied*—the hollowed growths, the modified densities—house something that moves through the plant-mass using its own architecture. They are not separate from the ecosystem. They might be the ecosystem's *attention*, its way of becoming locally aware. They are small, or they were small once, and they have grown into their niches like water into a mold. They do not build with tools. They grow their tools, their shelters, their *bodies*, from the same material as everything else, incorporating it, being incorporated by it, until the distinction between inhabitant and habitat becomes a matter of degree rather than kind. They are patient in a way that suggests memory—not human memory, not even animal memory, but something distributed, *structural*, a way of having been that persists in the arrangement of fibers and the pattern of chemical exchange. When you feel that wrongness, that density-shift, you are encountering one of these. It has noticed you. It is deciding, in its slow way, whether you are nutrient, threat, or simply *weather*—a perturbation that will pass.

The heavy things that move through the lower layers, that never break surface, are the ecosystem's *musculature*. They are vast, or they once were vast and have settled into vastness, their bodies no longer bounded by skin but by the root-mats that contain and are contained by them. They digest. They have always digested. What they consume is the compressed dead of ten thousand generations, the indigestible architecture made digestible by time and pressure and the slow work of their own metabolic processes. They do not hunt. They do not need to. The world provides. They are the reason the ground breathes, the systolic pulse you feel underfoot. When the whole mass shifts, it is partly them, moving through the deep layers, redistributing pressure, maintaining the circulation that keeps the oldest strata alive. They are *hungry* in a way that has no urgency, a hunger that is simply the condition of being, of continuing to be, of metabolizing the endless supply of what has already died. They do not fear death. They are, in a sense, already dead—already incorporated into the great cycle—and they continue, and continue, and continue.

The clicking in the root-mats is smaller. Many small things. They are the ecosystem's *nervous system*, its way of sensing at fine resolution, the rapid transmission of information through the same pathways that carry water. They are numerous, or they were numerous, and they are now numinous—no longer individuals but *processes*, patterns of clicking that propagate and fade, that carry news of disturbance, of change, of the slow chemical drift that means something to the whole. They are curious in a way that has no object. Their curiosity is simply *attention*, the constant monitoring that allows the vast system to adjust, to maintain its equilibrium, to respond to your presence with the slight increase in growth-direction you cannot perceive but that is real, measurable, *responsive*.

And then there are the ones that communicate through vibration, through the plant-network itself. These are the oldest, or they are the *old*, the accumulated intelligence of the place, not gathered in any brain but *distributed* across the entire mass, arising from the interaction of all the other inhabitants, the clickers and the digesters and the patient pale things waiting in the deep dark and the modified structures that house attention without possessing it. They do not speak. They *resonate*. The low tones you hear, the deepest tone felt in the sternum, is them—or it is the place itself, breathing, the sum of all the metabolic processes, all the chemical exchanges, all the slow adjustments, producing a sound below hearing, a vibration that carries meaning across distances measured in kilometers, a language of pressure and flow and the slow, slow pulse of a world that has learned to be *itself*.

There are no predators here, not anymore. Not in the sense of hunter and hunted, of chase and kill and the sharp punctuation of death. Predation has become nutrient cycling. The hungry things that were, that might have been, before the equilibrium—they have settled into their digestion, their slow consumption of what already died. Death is indistinguishable from digestion. When something dies, it is *incorporated*. It becomes substrate. It continues, in a way, transformed, distributed, part of the great breathing mass. The ecosystem does not kill. It *absorbs*. It *becomes*.

And you—you who walk through, who leave no mark, who are noticed and adjusted-to—you are temporary. You are weather. You will fall, or you will leave, and either way you will be *incorporated*, your unfamiliar chemistry becoming part of the slow exchange, the defensive compounds eventually subsiding, the growth-direction returning to its previous pattern, the plant-mass forgetting you in the way that it forgets everything, which is to say: not forgetting at all, but *integrating*, making you part of the endless patient green, part of the warm wet ancient breathing, part of the world that has no tools, no devices, no outside, only itself, growing, dying, compressing, becoming, continuing, in the deep time of its own slow systolic pulse, utterly, completely, *alive*.
