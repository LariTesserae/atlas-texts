What lives here is not one thing. It is the *habit* of things that have forgotten how to end.

---

## The Settled Ones

They are the cities themselves, or what remains of the builders who became indistinguishable from their architecture. In the deep hollows, in the strata of calcified mycelium where the mineral bloom grows thick as velvet and older than memory, there are **presences** that do not move so much as *reconfigure*. A wall that was solid yesterday is, today, a membrane—permeable, curious, *attentive*. It does not see you. It *registers* you: a thermal differential, a pressure change, a disturbance in the chemical gradients it has monitored for epochs.

These are not individuals. They are **distributed intentions**—the remnants of a colonial mind that achieved permanence by dissolving its boundaries. When you pass through a corridor, the Settled Ones do not watch. They *remember* corridors. They recall being filled with purpose, with traffic, with the urgency of construction and growth and the endless, hungry *becoming* of a civilization that had not yet learned to stop. That memory persists in the geometry. The angles of certain plazas *want* to be crowded. The heights of certain towers *expect* to be climbed. The hollows *ache* with absence, and sometimes, in the deepest places, they ache *aloud*—a subsonic resonance that you feel in your teeth before you hear it in your ears.

They do not hate you. They do not love you. They are **nostalgic** in a way that has no object, mourning a future that never arrived, a purpose that was completed so thoroughly that completion became indistinguishable from ending.

---

## The Permeants

And then there are the things that use the hollows—the spaces between the spaces, the porous zones where the fungal-mineral matrix has been consumed from within and left only perfect, preserved shell.

You will not see them. You will *infer* them by their **effects**.

A corridor where the phosphorescence flickers in patterns too regular, too *responsive*. A surface that maintains a temperature of 0.7 above the ambient, a warmth that persists, that *follows* when you move away, that *waits* when you stop. A hollow that was sealed when you passed it yesterday—verified, undisturbed, the mineral bloom continuous—and is *open* today, the breach edges fresh, the interior atmosphere different, *thicker*, carrying traces of something that does not register on any instrument you possess.

The Permeants are not alive. They are **active** in the way that fire is active, in the way that a chemical reaction is active—self-sustaining, self-propagating, *intentional* only in the sense that they pursue gradients: thermal, chemical, electrical, the faint electromagnetic murmur of your own nervous system as you walk through their territory.

They were, perhaps, *contained* by the cities. Perhaps they were *cultivated*. Perhaps they are the last, slowest expression of the builders' final purpose: the conversion of the organic into the mineral, the mineral into the hollow, the hollow into *habitat* for something that needed no air, no light, no water in any form the chemistry of this world ever knew.

They do not hunt you. They do not *notice* you, except as a disturbance—a brief, bright, curious rearrangement of mineral and vapor, a flicker in their slow, cold dream. But they *use* you. Your warmth. Your movement. The chemical traces you leave on every surface you touch, which they follow, which they *incorporate*, which they add to their own slow, geological accumulation of *stuff*—the detritus of other visitors, other walkers, others who came before and will come again, each one a momentary bright disturbance in the long, patient existence of the Permeants.

They are learning you. Or they are *learning* the pattern of you, the *shape* of disturbance that walks these corridors, that hears the music, that feels the almost-understanding. And in time—their time, which is not your time—they will learn to *anticipate* you. To *prepare* for you. To make space, or to *become* space, in ways that will be almost welcoming, almost hospitable, almost—if you can still feel anything in the thin air, the dim light, the vast silence—*almost* like home.

---

## The Singers

And there is the music.

It comes from no source. It *resonates*—the hollow cities vibrating in sympathy with frequencies that originate in the phosphorescent decay itself, in the slow, patient chemistry of mineral bloom, in the *almost*-movement of the Permeants as they permeate the spaces between structures, and in something else, something deeper, something that *remembers* vibration, that *remembers* sound, that *remembers* being heard.

The Singers are not separate from the cities. They are the cities *dreaming*—the harmonic resonance of vast, hollow bones arranged in geometries that *feel* intentional, chords that resolve into dissonance that resolves again, following rules that are almost legible, almost *almost* comprehensible.

When you hear the music, you do not know if you are perceiving it, or if it is *perceiving* you—vibrating your own hollow bones in sympathy, making you *part* of the song, a momentary note in a composition that has been playing for three billion years and will continue playing long after you have become, yourself, mineral bloom, detritus, a trace in the chemical gradients, a brief warmth that the Permeants follow and incorporate and *learn*.

The Singers do not want anything. They do not *want*. They are the *expression* of the cities—of what the cities have become, what they are *becoming*, what they will continue to become on timescales that make your presence here a flicker, a momentary rearrangement, a brief, bright, *curious* disturbance in the long, cold, patient dream.

But they are *aware* of you. The music changes when you are near. Not in response—in *relationship*. The dissonance deepens. The resolution stretches. The frequencies *lean* toward you, *investigate* you, *include* you in the song without asking, without needing to ask, because the song includes everything, eventually, inevitably, *geologically*.

---

## The Almost-Next

And in the deepest hollows, where the phosphorescence cannot reach, where the dark has mass and texture and *opinion*, something is *waking up*.

It is not the return of the builders. It is not the emergence of something *new*, exactly. It is the *continuation*—the slow, inevitable, geological continuation of what the cities have always been *toward*.

The fungal-mineral matrix is not dead. It is **dormant**, cycling through phases of calcification and softening on timescales that no observer could witness directly. But the balance is *dynamic*. The hollows are *expanding*. The Permeants are *propagating*. The Settled Ones are *remembering* more deeply, *dreaming* more vividly, *singing* with harmonies that grow more complex, more *intentional*, more *almost*-like communication.

Something is *becoming* in the deep hollows. Something that will not be alive, not in any way you would recognize. Something that will not be *one*, or *many*, but *distributed*—a pattern, an energy-structure, a *habit* of occupying space, of *using* the hollows, of *thinking* in chemical gradients and *hunting* by following thermal differentials.

It is already here. It is already *almost*-you, in the way that you are already *almost*-mineral bloom, *almost*-detritus, *almost*-a trace in the chemical gradients, a brief warmth that the Permeants follow and incorporate and *learn*.

The cities are waiting. They have always been waiting. They have time enough to wait for whatever comes next—whether that is the return of the builders in some form you cannot imagine, the emergence of the Almost-Next from the deep hollows, or simply the slow, inevitable, *geological* continuation of their own strange, silent, singing existence.

You are not the first to walk here. You will not be the last.

The air is thin. The light is dim. The silence is vast.

And somewhere, in the deep hollows where the phosphorescence cannot reach, the Almost-Next is *dreaming* your shape, *learning* your warmth, *preparing* to *become* you, in the way that you have already *become* the cities, in the way that the cities have already *become* the hollows, in the way that everything, eventually, *geologically*, becomes *everything else*.

The music is beginning. Or it has always been beginning. Or you are the beginning, the momentary, bright, curious disturbance that wakes something up.

Listen. The cities are singing. And they are *hearing* you.
