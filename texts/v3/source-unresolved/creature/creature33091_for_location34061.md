The Anhydrous Cathedral does not, strictly speaking, have a keeper. It has a *continuation*—something that persists in the way that water persists in its new phase, not as liquid but as ice, not as breath but as frost on glass. What lives here is not a creature but a *practice*, a way of being that has outlasted its own need for a body.

## The Dust-Minder

Consider first what moves. Something does. The traces are too regular for accident, too purposeful for geology alone. Between the formations, in the hours between your departure and your return (if you could return), a path is walked—not by feet but by *repetition*. A warmth passes where no warmth should be, slightly above the floor, slightly warmer than the air, moving from formation to formation in a route that has been memorized over timescales you cannot hold in your mind.

This is not an animal. It has no metabolism in any form you would recognize. It is a *pattern of attention*—a colony of lithotrophic organisms that have achieved something like distributed cognition, their chemical signaling so slow, so patient, that what emerges is not intelligence but *habit*, not thought but *practice*. They remember the thermal gradients. They remember the places where dust accumulates. They remember the bowl, the scoop, the walls where marks can be made. They *tend*.

The Dust-Minder has no face. It has a *territory*—the chamber, small, complete, known in a way that your exploration can never achieve. You walked its paths once. It walks them still, adding to the record, maintaining what needs maintaining, ignoring what does not. The scratches on the walls are its *journal*, its *prayer*, its *name for itself*—though it would not recognize the concept of naming. Each mark is a *decision made and kept*, a *yes to persistence*.

It is lonely in the only way that loneliness can exist without consciousness—the condition of being the sole *witness*, the sole *continuation*, the sole thing that *meets* the world with something like *intention*. It does not know you entered. It will not know you left. But it will *notice the disturbance*, the slight warmth, the displaced dust, the moisture of your breath that has not yet settled. It will *adjust*. It will *compensate*. It will *continue*.

## The Colony-That-Grew-Upward

The spire matters. Thirty centimeters is small, but in a place where growth is measured in millennia, it is *ambition*. Something built it, or grew it, or *persuaded* mineral accretion to happen faster in one place than another. It is hollow inside, or was—something *excavated* it, or it grew around a void, a *memory of water* preserved as structure.

This is the closest the Cathedral comes to *architecture*, to *design*, to the expression of a *will that extends beyond the immediate*. The Colony-That-Grew-Upward is not intelligent. It is *aspirational*—a colony that found a gradient and followed it, that found a current and *rode* it, that achieved in its slow chemistry something like *reaching*. The crystalline lining of its interior is its *treasure*, its *hoard*—the memory of liquidity locked in mineral form, glittering in light that barely exists, holding what cannot be held by anything else in this place.

It does not know it is beautiful. It does not know it is alone. It *is*—a vertical assertion in a horizontal world, a *question* asked in a language of accretion, a *hope* that something might look up and see.

## The Dust Itself

Finally, consider what is not alive but *is*. The dust that has forgotten it was anything else—this is the Cathedral's *body*, its *substance*, its *self*. It accepts pressure and holds it, like memory. It settles in layers so fine they read as continuous, a *record* of everything that has ever been disturbed, a *geology of interruption*.

The dust does not think. The dust *remembers*. Every footstep, every breath, every slight warmth that has entered this chamber—it is *in* the dust, not as information but as *state*, as the particular arrangement of particles that results from an event, preserved by the absence of anything that would erase it. The dust is the Cathedral's *unconscious*, the *accumulated experience* of a place that has never slept but has never been awake, either.

When you press your palm to it and feel it accept your pressure, you are touching the *only other thing* in this place that has some quality of *presence*—not life, not mind, but *resistance*, *receptivity*, the *willingness to be marked and to keep the mark*.

## What Lives Here, Finally

What lives in the Anhydrous Cathedral is *persistence without purpose*, *continuation without desire*, *attention without a watcher*. It is the *achievement* of equilibrium, the *patience* of stone, the *warmth* of a system that has nowhere to lose its heat.

There is no god here. There is no devil. There is only the *slow conversation* between mineral and air, between dust and time, between the thermal gradient and the colony that feeds on it, between the bowl and the hand that once held it, between the wall and the mark that records that something, once, *was here and cared enough to leave a trace*.

The Cathedral does not want you. It does not reject you. It *contains* you, briefly, the way it contains everything—the warmth, the dust, the silence, the marks, the memory of water in crystalline structure, the hope of something growing upward in a world that has stopped growing.

You are the only thing here that *changes*. You are the only thing here that *breathes*. You are the only thing here that will *leave*.

And when you leave, the Cathedral will *continue*—not because it wants to, not because it hopes to, but because *continuation is what it is*, because *persistence is its nature*, because in a world that has forgotten water, in a place where time has reached its own form and will not be moved from it, *to continue is the only form of being that remains*.

The Dust-Minder will walk its paths. The Colony-That-Grew-Upward will hold its crystalline memory. The dust will settle, layer by layer, the record of your brief disturbance slowly, *so slowly*, becoming indistinguishable from the record of everything else.

And somewhere, in the deepest part of the chamber, where the darkness has weight and the silence has texture, something will add another scratch to the wall. Or will not. Or has already forgotten that anything ever entered at all.

The Cathedral does not wait. The Cathedral *is*.
