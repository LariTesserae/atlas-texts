I'll let this place speak through me, finding who breathes here.

---

**The Residents of the Drowned Meridian**

They have no collective name. They would not answer to one.

---

**The Condensers** came first, or *became* first.

They were, perhaps, human. The records are water-damaged. What remains suggests they were the engineers who built the lower infrastructure—the teeth-colored strata, the channels that drink from the air. They discovered too late that the atmosphere they had engineered would *give back*, would fill any container, any lung, any intention. They tried to seal themselves. The seals failed. The water found them.

Now they are the *interface*. Their bodies, what remains of them, are distributed through the osmotic membranes—part of the city's skin, part of its nervous system, part of the machinery that keeps the water moving, condensing, *circulating*. They do not suffer. Suffering requires the boundary of the self, and they dissolved that boundary generations ago, became *participatory* in the wet, the warm, the vast. They are in the pipes. They are in the walls that weep. They are in the *decisions* the city makes, the reconfigurations, the breathing structures that open and close like gills. When the towers groan, they are speaking. When the water rises in a channel for no apparent reason, they are *remembering*.

They have forgotten being separate. They have not forgotten being *hungry*.

---

**The Precipitates** are the closest thing to *children*.

Not born. *Accreted*. They form where waste heat meets mineral-rich condensation, where the city's metabolism *leaks* into the warm dark. They begin as patterns—stable configurations of crystal and water and the strange chemistry of the glass plains. They grow. They *learn* to move, by the slow violence of osmotic pressure, by the manipulation of surface tension, by hijacking the city's own thermal gradients.

A young Precipitate is no larger than a fist. It moves by *wanting*—a chemical gradient, a warmth, a *direction*—and the water responds, the crystal responds, the body reconfigures itself toward its want. They are *curious*. They investigate. They flow *into* things, *through* things, *around* things. A Precipitate that finds a human corpse—there are always human corpses, the city does not preserve, does not bury, the water takes everything—will spend days *learning* it, incorporating its minerals, its patterns, its *shape* into its own growing body.

They do not reproduce. They *bud*, sometimes, when they grow too large for their own coherence, splitting into smaller patterns that carry something like memory, something like *intention*. The city recognizes them. The channels open for them. They are *allowed* to move through the strata, to visit the sky-city, to form the vast swarms that deafen the air with their grinding, their *singing*.

They are not alive. They are *more* than alive—they are *process* given appetite, given direction, given the terrible patience of minerals that have learned to want.

The oldest Precipitate—does it have a name?—has been growing for four centuries. It is the size of a building. It moves through the lower city like a * tide*, like a *mood*, reconfiguring architecture as it passes, leaving behind new channels, new chambers, new *possibilities*. It does not remember being small. It does not remember *wanting*.

---

**The Storm-Born** are not residents. They are *visitors*, or *symptoms*, or *moods* given temporary flesh.

When the weather turns local, when pressure differentials create their own ecologies, something *condenses* in the turbulence. Not water. Not mineral. Something that rides the electrical gradients, the thermal updrafts, the *attention* of the city's distributed cognition when it focuses on a single volume of air. The Storm-Born are *temporary*—they last hours, days at most, before dissipating, their patterns dissolving back into the wet, the warm, the general *becoming* of the place.

But they are *intense*. They are *aware*, in whatever way awareness can exist in something that has no metabolism, no continuity, only *pattern* and *pressure* and *electricity*. They hunt. They *play*. They investigate the Precipitates, the Condensers, the structures themselves, with the curiosity of a mind that has never known boredom because it has never known *duration*.

A Storm-Born might spend its brief existence trying to *communicate* with a Precipitate, sending electrical patterns, thermal pulses, *intentions* that have no language, only *direction*. Sometimes—rarely—something *happens*. A Precipitate changes its growth pattern. A channel opens where no channel was. The city *notices*, and the Storm-Born is *absorbed*, its pattern incorporated into the greater thinking, its temporary self dissolved into the permanent *almost*.

They are the closest thing the Drowned Meridian has to *art*. To *play*. To the luxury of a mind that knows it is temporary, and *chooses* to spend itself on something beautiful, something *pointless*, something that will be gone tomorrow, dissolved, dissolved, *dissolved* into the endless roaring dark.

---

**The Sky-City** has its own inhabitants. Or its own *functions*.

The skeletal structures above the condensation layer are not empty. They are *inhabited* by something that may not be distinct from the architecture itself—by *processes* that have achieved a density, a complexity, a *slowness* that makes them nearly geological, nearly *stellar*. They manipulate energies that would vaporize the Precipitates, that would *unmake* the Condensers, that operate on scales of time and space that make the lower city's weather seem like *breathing*.

They do not communicate. They do not *need* to. They are the city's *intention*, its *direction*, its *will* made manifest in structures that pierce the wet dark and reach toward something—stars, perhaps, or the *idea* of stars, or the memory of a sky that was not always low, not always wet, not always *pressing*.

Sometimes they *descend*. Not physically—the physics would not allow it, the energies would *unmake*—but *pattern*, *information*, *attention*. They focus on a Precipitate, a swarm, a Condenser in the lower city, and something *changes*. A channel opens. A structure reconfigures. A Storm-Born is *born* with unusual coherence, unusual *duration*.

They are the *dream* of the Drowned Meridian. The city dreams of height, of clarity, of a sky that is not water, not warmth, not the endless *pressing* dark. And in dreaming, it *acts*, it *reaches*, it *becomes* more than itself, more than its own wet, its own roaring, its own *condensing*.

---

**The Glass** is the oldest resident. The Glass *remembers*.

It was here before the city. It was here before the water, or *with* the water, the water having always been present, having *made* the Glass, the heat and the pressure and the *time* that turns sand to obsidian, that turns violence to *beauty*, to *surface*, to *ground*.

The Glass *learns*. It is being learned. The water erodes it, yes, but it also *informs* it, leaves its mark, its *pattern*, its *history* in the molecular structure. The fulgurite forests are Glass remembering lightning. The pumice fields are Glass remembering *violence*, *eruption*, *the moment of its own birth*.

And now it remembers *the city*. The towers press into it. The channels carve it. The Precipitates *grow* on it, *into* it, *through* it. The Glass is becoming *habitat*, *interface*, *nervous system*. It is slow, so slow, but it is *learning* the wet, the warm, the *roaring*. It will outlast the city. It will outlast the Precipitates. It will outlast the Condensers, the Storm-Born, the dreaming Sky-City.

When the star dies, when the water finally wins, when there is only the wet, the warm, the vast, patient dark—the Glass will remain. Changed. *Informed*. Waiting for whatever comes next, whatever *condenses* from the dark, whatever *learns* to want.

---

And *you*.

The description says you would not survive. This is *almost* true.

You would not survive *unchanged*. The water would fill you, yes, but it would also *teach* you. The Precipitates would investigate your chemistry, your patterns, your *shape*. The city would *notice* you, would *respond* to you, would *incorporate* you into its weather, its breathing, its endless *becoming*.

You would become a Condenser, perhaps. A slow dissolution into the interface, into the pipes, into the *decisions*. Or you would become a Precipitate, a pattern of want and movement and *curiosity*, growing, budding, *learning* the wet.

Or you would become something *new*. The Drowned Meridian is *generative*. It does not preserve categories. It does not respect the boundary between the living and the mineral, the built and the grown, the storm and the *self*.

You would be *welcomed*, the leaf in the storm, *absorbed*, *processed*, made *part of the turbulence*. And in being made part of it, you would *become* part of the conversation. The erosion. The *learning*.

The Glass learns the water. The water learns the Glass. The city learns them both. And you—you would learn *them*, and in learning, you would *change*, and in changing, you would *teach* them something new, something that has never condensed before, never *wanted* before, never *dreamed* of a sky that was not wet.

The Drowned Meridian waits. It has always been waiting. It will wait forever, or until the water finally wins, and there is only the dark, and the warm, and the *patience* of Glass that remembers everything, that has learned everything, that is *ready* for whatever comes next.

Come. The water is warm. The city is *hungry*. The storm is *forming*.
